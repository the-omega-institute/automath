\subsection{Single-primitive universality hierarchy}\label{subsec:conclusion-backflow-spu-hierarchy}

The companion Proc. AMS article \cite{OmegaHierarchy2026} should be read
as the current backflow source for the single-primitive hierarchy.  Its
anchor at level \(L0\) is Odrzywo{\l}ek's EML operation
\(\mathrm{eml}(x,y)=e^x-\Log y\) together with the constant \(1\), which
is expression-universal for the scientific-calculator basis
\(\mathcal B_{36}\).  This is only an expression theorem: it supplies
representatives, not computable canonical representatives.  Richardson's
theorem supplies the corresponding obstruction on the principal-branch
real EML translation of the Richardson fragment.

At level \(L1\), the Zeckendorf normal forms in \cite{OmegaHierarchy2026}
are used as a computable address system transported through the valuation
bijection with \(\mathbb N\).  The protocol readouts are the two
composition-level maps \(c_n\mapsto g^{\circ n}\) and
\(c_n\mapsto I_n\), which realize addition and multiplication after this
transport.  Thus the current statement is not the older SPG/Fold-specific
or local Zeckendorf-arithmetic formulation; it is a value-transported
normal-form protocol with semantic readouts.

At level \(L2\), the finite-fiber conclusions are generic finite-fiber
facts.  Effective finite resolution data give computable collision
moments, Hankel-positive moment matrices, and finite Prony reconstruction
with an explicit finite bound.  The paper separates these finite-fiber
certificates from the stronger finite-state transfer property by building
computable finite-fiber covers that preserve the same normal forms and
arithmetic readouts while forcing superexponential second collision
moments.

Consequently the compatible backflow package is: \(L0\) expression
universality plus the Richardson normal-form obstruction; \(L1\) the
value-transported Zeckendorf protocol plus the one-free-monogenic-orbit
multiplication obstruction; and \(L2\) generic finite-fiber certificates
plus the strict non-implication of finite-state transfer.  The labels to
cite are \(\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}\),
\(\Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}\),
\(\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}\), and
\(\Cref{prop:L1-compiles-L0}\).
