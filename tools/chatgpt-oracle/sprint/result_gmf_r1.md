1. Verdict
Reject.
The single strongest reason is that the advertised cross-resolution compatibility theorem does not establish cross-resolution compatibility of the folded observations. The paper defines
ρ​m+1,m​=πm+1→m​∘Foldm+1​
and then proves
Foldm​∘ρ​m+1,m​=ρ​m+1,m​.
But this follows merely because ρ​m+1,m​ was defined to take values in Xm​, and Foldm​ fixes Xm​. The proof has no substantive cross-level content. 
The actual compatibility square one needs,
Foldm​∘τm+1,m​=?πm+1→m​∘Foldm+1​,
is false. The paper itself supplies the counterexample 011:
Fold2​(01)=01,π3→2​Fold3​(011)=00.

Either “cross-resolution compatibility” has its ordinary projective meaning, in which case the central claim is false, or it means only the displayed fixed-point identity, in which case the theorem is vacuous and cannot support a research paper.
2. Significance threshold
It does not clear the significance threshold of a good specialist journal in symbolic dynamics.
This is not merely a sound paper aimed one tier too high. It is incorrect in one advertised structural conclusion, and after that conclusion is removed, the remaining mathematics is too small:


The normal-form theorem is obtained by choosing a finite section sm​ and composing it with reduction modulo Fm+2​; idempotence and surjectivity are then immediate from the definition. 


The addressing theorem says that a sigma-algebra generated by events already measurable in G≤L​ is contained in G≤L​. 


The clarity section is atomwise Bayes classification followed by a boundary-mass factorization.


The sofic presentation is the standard de Bruijn graph followed by subset determinization and Perron–Frobenius theory; no new structural result about the resulting shifts Ym​, their entropy as m→∞, their Fischer covers, periodic points, or measures is obtained. 


The manuscript has only two references, Lind–Marcus and Parry, despite presenting Zeckendorf normalization, discrepancy estimates, Markov concentration, entropy continuity, Bayesian approximation, and finite-state constructions as part of its contribution. 
After a radical reduction and the addition of a genuinely nontrivial theorem about the fold fibers or their asymptotics, The Fibonacci Quarterly would be a plausible ceiling. In its present form, there is no right-sized research journal.
3. Correctness audit
The central structural proof fails
The paper repeatedly upgrades Theorem 6.1 into the statement that the construction has “cross-resolution compatibility.” The abstract makes that claim,  and Corollary 9.7 explicitly says that compatibility follows from Theorem 6.1 and Proposition 6.3. 
That inference is invalid.
Proposition 6.3 proves only that the already legal codomains
X1​←X2​←X3​←⋯
form an inverse system under prefix deletion.  It says nothing about whether the stable types assigned by
Foldm​(ω1​⋯ωm​)
are compatible when the observed window is enlarged. The proof is therefore carried out on a different object from the one needed by the advertised conclusion. This is exactly the kind of structural mismatch described in the question.
There is also a population-level counterexample, even for the fairest possible source. Under IID fair Bernoulli input,
p2fib​(00,01,10)=(21​,41​,41​).
At depth three, the fold fibers give
p3fib​(000,001,010,100,101)=(41​,41​,81​,41​,81​).
Pushing the latter forward under stable prefix restriction gives
(π3→2​)#​p3fib​=(21​,81​,83​),
so
DTV​(p2fib​,(π3→2​)#​p3fib​)=81​.
Thus the folded laws are not even projectively consistent for IID fair input. Appendix D quietly acknowledges that this residual need not vanish and warns the reader not to assume it does.  That warning is incompatible with the front matter’s unqualified presentation of cross-resolution compatibility.
Appendix C contains a false discrepancy bound
The rotation audit asserts that when Az(m)​ is a union of at most Bm​(z) intervals,
​πm,Nrot​(z)−πmrot​(z)​≤Bm​(z)DN∗​(α,x0​).

This is false for ordinary one-dimensional star discrepancy. A general interval contributes the difference of two anchored discrepancies, so the generic bound is 2DN∗​, not DN∗​; for B intervals it is 2BDN∗​.
Here is a counterexample inside the manuscript’s own rotation model. Put
δ=10−72​,α=103​+δ,β=10089​,x0​=54​,
and take m=3, N=4. Then α is irrational. For z=101, one has
Fold3−1​(101)={101},
and direct calculation gives
A101(3)​=[107​−δ,10081​−δ),
a single interval, so B3​(101)=1, with Lebesgue measure 11/100.
The four orbit points, in increasing order, are
101​+δ,52​+2δ,107​+3δ,54​.
Two of them lie in A101(3)​, hence
π3,4rot​(101)=21​,​π3,4rot​(101)−π3rot​(101)​=10039​.
Their star discrepancy is
D4∗​(α,x0​)=51​+3δ<0.201.
Consequently
10039​>B3​(101)D4∗​(α,x0​),
contradicting the stated certificate by almost a factor of two.
Every rotation-source certificate that uses this bound is therefore unsound as written.
The reproducibility theorem is under-hypothesized
Theorem E.1 claims that deterministic outputs reproduce the same canonical hashes once scripts, tolerances, and serialization conventions are declared. Its proof says canonical serialization removes representation ambiguity. 
That does not follow. Canonical serialization makes equal numerical values serialize equally; it does not force two floating-point implementations, eigensolvers, transcendental libraries, or parallel reductions to produce equal numerical values. A tolerance can make two runs both pass while their serialized decimals and SHA256 hashes differ. Bitwise hash reproducibility requires exact arithmetic or a completely fixed arithmetic implementation and rounding protocol, not merely declared tolerances. This theorem needs substantially stronger hypotheses.
4. Abstract and introduction versus theorem hypotheses
For the clarity exponent theorem, the front matter is unusually careful: it carries the eventual nonemptiness assumption, the uniform two-sided cylinder-volume bounds, the exact exponential boundary-count hypothesis, and the posterior-thinning condition.  The contribution list repeats those hypotheses.  The addressing claim likewise states its decisive measurability assumption. 
The front matter nevertheless contains two serious overclaims.
First, it says the paper proves “cross-resolution compatibility” without explaining that the proved statement is only the fixed-point identity for a map defined by normalizing at the larger resolution.  The contribution list repeats that the “folded type spaces are compatible across adjacent resolutions.”  Under the natural meaning involving the actual depth-m folded observation, this is false by 011.
Second, the claim hierarchy says that level C1 includes “folding rewrites, termination, confluence,” and then says the main theorems cover C0+C1+C2.  But Appendix A does not specify a rewrite system or prove termination, local confluence, or global confluence. It merely lists six properties that a future “full formal audit” should verify.  The scope section therefore claims results the paper does not contain.
So the exact-rate theorem does not suffer from dropped hypotheses. The central structural contribution suffers from something worse: the front matter states a stronger conclusion than the theorem proves.
5. Length
The length is not justified. Scale is plainly manufactured by naming elementary observations, definitions, and software procedures as theorems.
Examples include:


Theorem 3.1: finite intersections of measurable sets are measurable, and a finite family generates a finite sigma-algebra. 


Theorem 7.3: events measurable in G≤L​ generate a sub-sigma-algebra of G≤L​. 


Theorem 8.1: a binary Bayes decision is optimized atom by atom. 


Theorem 8.7: after defining ϑm​=εm​/bm​, the paper records the resulting factorization and subtracts exponential rates. 


Theorem D.1: the entropy and relative-entropy chain rules for a deterministic finite map. 


Theorems E.2 and E.3: deterministic identities do not fail because of sampling, and statistics computed from saved tables are functions of those tables. 


Appendix A is a checklist rather than a normalization proof. Appendix B is standard automata material. Appendix C is an audit protocol, including at least one false certificate. Appendix D is mostly finite-fiber bookkeeping and entropy chain rules. Appendix E devotes roughly ten pages to directory names, CSV schemas, hashes, manifests, script ordering, and pass/fail labels. That belongs in a repository README, not as a sequence of mathematical theorems.
A competent rewrite would be under ten pages. Even at that length, it would still need a genuinely new theorem before it became publishable research.
