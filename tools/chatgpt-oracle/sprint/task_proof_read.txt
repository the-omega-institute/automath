THE FAILURE SHAPE TO HUNT FIRST, because it is the one this project keeps producing:

  A ONE-STEP BOUND USED FOR MANY STEPS. An estimate is proved for a single step, iteration,
  layer, or index, and then applied across n of them as though the constant, the error, or the
  hypothesis carried uniformly. The tell is a proof that establishes something like
  |f(x_{k+1}) - f(x_k)| <= C and then concludes |f(x_n) - f(x_0)| <= C without the factor n, or
  that fixes a constant depending on k and then treats it as absolute. Look specifically for:
    - a constant introduced inside an induction that silently depends on the induction variable;
    - an error term proved for one layer and summed over layers without justifying summability;
    - "similarly" or "the same argument gives" where the same argument does NOT give it because
      a hypothesis was consumed the first time;
    - an interchange of limit and sum, or sum and integral, with no domination argument;
    - a uniformity claim ("uniformly in m", "independent of n") that the preceding estimate does
      not actually establish.

ALSO CHECK, in order of how much they would cost:
