Do not ask for design approval, do not run any brainstorming or approval workflow. Proceed directly.
This is a bibliographic integrity audit. Do not change any mathematics.

WHY THIS IS BEING RUN. Two sibling papers in this family were audited this way and both were
dirty. In one, an entry was entirely fabricated - a plausible title, a DOI that resolves to
nothing, and author initials shuffled from a real paper on the same topic - and it had survived
several expert referee rounds, with the referee himself describing it as "a different article by
those authors". The same audit then found three more entries whose DOIs resolved to completely
different papers. In the second paper, seven DOIs resolved to entirely different papers. Referees
check mathematics; they do not check whether your citations exist. Assume nothing in this
bibliography is correct until an index says so.

WHAT TO DO

 1. Extract every bibliography entry in this paper, including any copy under a submission bundle
    or submission source directory. Report the total count.

 2. For every entry that carries a DOI, resolve the DOI against Crossref and COMPARE THE RETURNED
    TITLE AND LEAD AUTHOR with what the entry claims. A DOI that resolves is not enough - the
    failure mode found in the sibling papers was DOIs that resolve correctly but point at someone
    else's article. Where Crossref is unavailable or empty, try OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar.

 3. For every entry without a DOI, search by exact title and author in Crossref, OpenAlex or
    Semantic Scholar, or against the official publisher, PMLR, arXiv or journal archive record.

 4. Classify each entry as one of: confirmed; confirmed after metadata correction, stating the old
    and new values; or unverified.

 5. CORRECT metadata that is demonstrably wrong against the index record - wrong DOI, wrong year,
    wrong volume, wrong initials, wrong title. Do not change the intellectual content of any
    sentence that cites it unless the correction changes what the source actually says, in which
    case fix the sentence too and say so.

 6. DELETE an entry only if it is demonstrably fabricated - no index has it, its DOI resolves to
    nothing or to an unrelated work, and no plausible near-match exists. If you delete one,
    resolve every \cite of it individually: read the surrounding sentence, and either cite a
    verified source that genuinely supports the claim, or rewrite the sentence so it no longer
    needs the citation. Do not blanket-substitute one key for another.

 7. NEVER invent a replacement citation, and never silently drop an entry you merely could not
    confirm. Anything unverified stays in the bibliography unchanged and is listed in
    artifacts/literature_check.md with the exact searches you tried and their outcomes. Pre-DOI
    classics and obscure proceedings legitimately fail this check; that is fine and must be
    recorded rather than hidden.

 8. RECORD THE AUDIT'S OWN LIMITATIONS. If an index was rate-limited or unavailable, say so
    explicitly and say which entries were therefore checked against fewer sources than intended.
    A service failure is NOT negative evidence about a citation. Note explicitly if the intended
    cross-source redundancy was not achieved.

 9. Write the full per-entry verification table into artifacts/literature_check.md, appended to
    whatever is already recorded there. Keep existing sourcing-gap records intact.

RULES:
 - Do not weaken, restate or renumber any theorem. This audit touches citations only.
 - Keep every .tex under 800 lines.

   artifacts/SHA256SUMS (LF) if artifacts change.

   directory, and remove any stale ones you find.
 - Do NOT delete build logs before reporting - they are needed to confirm the compile claims.

REPORT: the total entry count; the full verification table; every correction with old and new
values; every deletion with the four citation decisions that followed it; every unverified entry
with what was tried; the audit's own limitations including any index that was unavailable; page
