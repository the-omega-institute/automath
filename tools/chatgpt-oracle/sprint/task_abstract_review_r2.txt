METHOD, per paper. Read the abstract. For EVERY mathematical claim in it, find the theorem,
proposition or lemma in the body that is supposed to support it, and compare:

  - Quantifiers. Does the abstract say "every" or "all" where the body says "for n >= n_0", or
    "for all sufficiently large", or restricts to a subfamily?
  - Side conditions. Model assumptions, admissibility, irreducibility, primality, coprimality,
    non-degeneracy, choice of convention, working hypotheses.
  - Scope words. "composite", "generic", "any", "arbitrary" are the usual carriers of an
    over-claim. Check each against the exact condition proved.
  - Proved versus computed. If the abstract states something flatly that the body establishes by
    an audited or numerical computation, that is a finding. Several of these papers correctly
    hedge with "computationally certified" or "conditional on the audited identification"; a
    claim that lost its hedge on the way into the abstract is exactly what to catch.
  - Attribution. If a result is imported from the literature, does the abstract make it sound
    like the authors' own?
