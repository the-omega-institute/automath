THE OBJECT. Fix m at least 6. For 0 <= n < 2^m let Fold_m(n) be the digits of the greedy
Zeckendorf expansion of n in positions F_2 through F_{m+1}, everything above discarded, and let
R_m(n) be its numerical value. Regard the integers below 2^m as vertices of the hypercube,
adjacent when their binary representations differ in one bit. The coloured-star signature is

    Phi_m(a) = ( R_m(a), the multiset of R_m(a XOR 2^i) for 0 <= i < m ).

The open lemma is that every fibre of Phi_m has at most two elements.

WHAT IS ALREADY SETTLED, all of it verified by me independently, so please do not redo it.
The tail numbers, meaning those whose expansions vanish in the retained positions, are exactly
T(r) = F_{m+1} r + F_m floor((r+1)/phi), consecutive gaps lie in {F_{m+1}, F_{m+2}}, and the
fold is the sawtooth remainder R_m(T(r) + y) = y. An edge changes the fold by an element of an
explicit at most eight element set, and pairwise disjointness of those sets implies injectivity;
that disjointness holds for every m from 13 to 5000 by exact integer computation, and holds for
all sufficiently large m by the p-adic Subspace Theorem, though with an ineffective cutoff.
Combined with direct computation for small m, the lemma is verified for 6 <= m <= 5000.

THE REFORMULATION, which is yours from last round and which I checked completely. For a vertex
n and a colour y put

    E_y(n) = { (n XOR 2^i) - y : R_m(n XOR 2^i) = y },

a set of breakpoints. If a < b share a Phi_m value and D = b - a, then a colour preserving
matching in which every u_i is 0 or -D exists if and only if for every colour y there is a
disjoint decomposition E_y(a) = C_y + L_y with E_y(b) = C_y + (L_y + D). When that holds, the
elementary hypercube sum forces sum |C_y| = 2 and sum |L_y| = m - 2, hence exactly two u_i
equal -D, hence D is a difference of two powers of two; with the Bugeaud, Cipu and Mignotte
classification of Fibonacci numbers with two binary digits this closes the lemma for all m.

I verified all of this on every one of the 227 collisions at m = 6 through 10: residue
conservation, the chain criterion, the counts, and that the forced construction yields a
genuinely valid matching with exactly two u_i = -D. I also ran the discriminating control:
the criterion is applied to pairs that share a fold value but have different stars, and it
rejects all 5193 of them, so it is not vacuous.

THE ONLY THING LEFT. Prove that the transport always exists. Concretely, in the chain form:
fix a colour y and a residue c modulo D, set A_k = 1 if c + kD is in E_y(a) and B_k = 1 if
c + kD is in E_y(b), and let S_k be the prefix sum of A_j - B_j for j <= k. The claim to prove
is

    S_k is 0 or 1 for every k, and S_k <= A_k for every k, and S_k returns to 0.

You called this a D-chain interlacing lemma. It is strictly stronger than the two aggregate
identities from round one, and my counterexample with the cancelling plus and minus 34 pair is
exactly why those identities cannot give it.

MY QUESTIONS.

One. Can you prove the interlacing? The structure that ought to drive it is that E_y(a) and
E_y(b) are sets of Zeckendorf tail numbers, so they are Sturmian in nature - the gaps take only
two values F_{m+1} and F_{m+2} in the golden-ratio pattern - and D is small compared with the
gaps. A three-distance or Ostrowski argument on the breakpoint sequence seems the natural tool.

Two. If you cannot, is the interlacing perhaps false, with the lemma still true for another
reason? I would rather learn that now. A counterexample to interlacing that is not a
counterexample to the lemma would be just as valuable as a proof.

Three. Independently: can the ineffective Subspace cutoff be replaced by an effective bound?
The relevant Diophantine condition concerns how closely a signed sum of two powers of two can
approximate a Fibonacci-scale quantity, and the Luca school treats equations of exactly this
shape by Baker's method, which is effective. If a Baker or Matveev bound applies, even a very
weak one, a finite computation would finish the lemma for all m and the interlacing question
would become unnecessary. Tell me honestly which of these two routes you think is shorter.
