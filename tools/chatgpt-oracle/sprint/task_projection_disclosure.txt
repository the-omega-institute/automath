THE PROBLEM. The abstract currently contains, in substance:

    An asynchronous finite-state kernel identifies each lambda_q (q >= 2) as the Perron root
    of a nonnegative integer matrix and hence proves that lambda_q is an algebraic integer.

That is Carlo Sanna's Theorem 1, by Sanna's method. Verified by reading the paper itself, not
its abstract: Carlo Sanna, "A Note on the Power Sums of the Number of Fibonacci Partitions",
Discrete Analysis 2025:2, doi 10.19086/da.137601, arXiv 2309.12724. He proves lambda_p is the
Perron-Frobenius eigenvalue of the transition matrix of an automaton built from p parallel
copies of Berstel's automaton, hence the greatest real root of an effectively computable monic
integer polynomial, hence an algebraic integer. He is cited elsewhere in this abstract, so the
overlap is not concealed, but a referee who knows that paper will read the sentence above as
claiming his result.

TWO MORE FACTS THE ABSTRACT MUST REFLECT.

1. Sanna's Table 1 already prints lambda_p together with its minimal polynomial over Q for
   p = 1..8. Our polynomials Pi_q for q = 9..17 are the next rows of that table, not an
   independent discovery. I verified this: his table ends at lambda_8 = 9.39867, our q = 9
   gives 11.7784, the normalised lambda^(1/index) descends monotonically through both stretches
   toward sqrt(phi) = 1.27202, and every polynomial in both begins X^d - 2X^(d-1). They must be
   presented as an extension.

2. The Galois determination should not be framed as surprising. I ran the same criterion on
   Sanna's own eight rows and every one is also the full symmetric group: S_3, S_3, S_5, S_5,
   S_7, S_7, S_9. Our nine values continue a pattern rather than break one. It is new because
   he computes no Galois groups, not because the answer is unexpected.
