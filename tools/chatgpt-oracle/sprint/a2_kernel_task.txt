TARGET. The referee listed five routes, any one of which would be a substantial further
improvement, and said what unites them: they "would make the mechanism itself, rather than
one highly successful implementation of it, the central theorem." His first, and the same
thing he originally named as option (b) of the tier threshold, is:

  an abstract kernel theorem encompassing stable, Gaussian, Poisson, and other
  polynomial-tail kernels.

Pursue that.

FRAME IT AS AN ABSTRACTION, NOT A GENERALISATION SWEEP. The productive question is not
"can we redo the proof for more kernels one at a time", it is: WHICH PROPERTIES OF THE
KERNEL DOES THE MECHANISM ACTUALLY USE? Go through the existing argument for the stable
kernel - Theorem 4.27, the standalone two-background KL perturbation lemma, the proxy
positivity and normalization, the o(s^{-r}) background estimates, the Hessian
identification, the cubic remainder - and extract the minimal hypotheses on the smoothing
kernel that each step genuinely requires. Candidates to check, not to assume: bounded
gradient of log p_1, L^1 translation continuity, the moment-mode expansion structure, decay
or regular variation of the tail, and whatever makes the retained common jet well defined.

Then state and prove an abstract theorem under exactly those hypotheses, and verify
membership for the concrete cases:
  - isotropic alpha-stable, 0 < alpha < 2, which must reduce to the existing theorem;
  - Gaussian, which the paper currently excludes (alpha = 2) - say clearly whether the
    abstract hypotheses cover it, and if so what the statement gives there, and if not,
    exactly which hypothesis fails;
  - Poisson;
  - at least one further polynomial-tail kernel.

A clean, minimal hypothesis set with three or four verified members is worth far more than
a broad-sounding theorem with one member.

HONEST OUTCOMES, in order of value:
 1. An abstract theorem with minimal hypotheses and several verified members.
 2. An abstract theorem whose hypotheses are honest but which only one or two known kernels
    satisfy - state that plainly, and say which natural kernels fail and at which step.
 3. A precise identification of the obstruction: the mechanism does NOT abstract, and here
    is the step that is irreducibly stable-specific. State this in the FIRST LINE of your
    report if it is the outcome. This is a real result and is much more valuable than a
    vacuous abstraction.

Do NOT produce a theorem whose hypotheses are simply a restatement of "the stable kernel
works", i.e. a hypothesis list assembled by reading off what the stable proof needed and
that no other kernel is shown to satisfy. That is the failure mode here, and the referee
will see it immediately - he caught the analogous risk on the admissible class last round
and asked whether it was gerrymandered.
