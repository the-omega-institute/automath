1. The finished article holds the ETDS valuation
It does not lose anything essential that my extraction estimate assumed. In fact, the execution is unusually faithful to the proposed architecture.
The paper now begins with an independently meaningful standard object: one-position overlap chains of bounded U-representations of zero. It asks a quantitative transient question and announces the effective linear theorem before introducing cyclic ranks. The introduction also makes the completing-coefficient issue explicit and distinguishes the theorem from normalization, synchronization, Condition F, and zero preservation. 
The central theorem is cleanly stated and genuinely independent of the later application. Theorem 2.2 gives an effective C(U,D), allows the completing coefficient outside the original alphabet but proves the fixed bound ∣c∣≤KU,D​, and gives the correct cycle-or-linear-transient dichotomy. Its proof is short because Lemma 2.1 actually collapses the dynamics, not because an argument has been omitted: after the first quotient, all later quotients and appended coefficients vanish, so an m+1-edge path reaches the zero loop. The remaining finitely many apertures are handled by exact acyclic longest-path computation. 
The application is also now in the right logical position. Lemma 3.1 proves that the coordinatewise-difference construction loses no collision, identifies the exact overlap graph, and relates causal inverse depth to its longest reachable path. Corollary 3.2 then obtains (A1) from Theorem 2.2 rather than treating cyclic ranks as the source of the mechanism. 
Most importantly, the fixed cubic result is not a decorative example. Theorem 4.1 states injectivity, the exact causal length, the precise penultimate obstruction set, next-stage emptiness, and matching sharpness for the bounded-zero graph.  The proof contains the Pisot and Parry checks, an explicit contraction threshold, the suffix-sum recurrence, terminal integer classifications for both parities, and separate verification of m=4,5. The lower-order conclusion therefore rests on a parameter-uniform proof, not on the audit computations.  
Is 20 pages too short?
No. I was not implicitly requiring 24 pages.
The manuscript is short because the adjacent-collapse mechanism is strong. It does not feel abridged in the first three sections. The article has approximately:


four pages of motivation, positioning, and theorem map;


five pages for the standard-object theorem and its proof;


three pages for the exact quotient and inverse-depth corollary;


five pages for the fixed cubic theorem;


two pages of comparative context and references.


That is a healthy allocation for this theorem package. Adding four pages merely to reach the upper end of the earlier estimate would weaken the paper.
There is one locally compressed passage: the odd-aperture part of the terminal classification. The paragraph that lists the five-digit alternatives and then says which preceding digits propagate to e0​=0 is correct in structure, but it asks the reader to reconstruct more of the recurrence propagation than the surrounding proof does.  A worked m=7 instance or two additional displayed propagation steps would improve readability. This is a half-page exposition improvement, not evidence that the article should be 24 pages.
Thus my answer to the first question is:

Yes, it holds the 74% ETDS estimate. I now price the actual main article at 75%, or 77% after the inexpensive repairs below.

3. Two cheap repairs are worth making; no further restructuring is
There is no remaining presentational change remotely comparable in value to the split. The article is now at its natural structural form. I would make only the following repairs.
A. Give the companion paper a formal, stable citation
The introduction and Section 5 name the companion article and assign the full quadratic, simple-Parry, Fibonacci, and Bassino-family material to it.   But the companion does not appear as an item in the reference list.
That is the clearest mismatch between the description in your question and the actual PDF. There is prose attribution, but not a bibliographic citation.
Because Section 5 makes substantive comparative statements—especially the claims that quadratic thresholds are always two or three and have much smaller exact inverse-length formulas—the companion should be publicly accessible and formally cited. The clean solution is:


place the companion on arXiv or another stable preprint repository;


add it to the bibliography with its identifier;


cite that entry at both existing mentions.


This matters more than adding exposition elsewhere. It lets a referee verify that the extraction is a genuine division of results rather than an appeal to an unavailable manuscript.

Reference [6] currently says only:

4. ETDS remains the right journal
No other journal is a better first home.
The closest credible alternative is Journal of Modern Dynamics, whose stated scope emphasizes interactions between dynamical systems and other major areas, including number theory. 美国数学科学研究所 That accurately describes the paper’s arithmetic-symbolic interface. I price it at approximately 69%.
I nevertheless prefer ETDS because the article’s actual center is not a broad interaction program or a new number-theoretic dynamical structure. It is a theorem about finite-state overlap chains and inverse windows for a symbolic recoding. The primary MSC is 37B10, the quotient construction is a pair-graph argument, and the final invariant is future-only inverse depth. ETDS is the more immediately legible editorial home.
Other plausible destinations—Journal of Number Theory, Acta Arithmetica, DCDS-A, or a formal-languages journal—would each capture only one side of the paper. None offers a better combination of readership, fit, and valuation than ETDS.
Final decision
Make the companion citation formal, add the arXiv identifier to reference [6], and submit to ETDS.
Do not spend a cycle at TAMS. Do not expand the paper merely because it is 20 pages. The split succeeded: the paper now presents one visible mechanism, one exact application, and one fixed-system sharpness theorem at the right length and in the right order.
