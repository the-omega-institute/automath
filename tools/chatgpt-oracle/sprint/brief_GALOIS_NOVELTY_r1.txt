BACKGROUND. Let R(n) be the number of representations of n as a sum of distinct Fibonacci
numbers (the Fibonacci partition function, sometimes written R(n) or r(n)). For a fixed
positive integer p, consider the power sums

    S_p(N) = sum over n < F_N of R(n)^p,

and the associated growth constant lambda_p = lim S_p(N)^{1/N}. Carlo Sanna, "A Note on the
Power Sums of the Number of Fibonacci Partitions", Discrete Analysis 2025:2 (arXiv 2309.12724),
proves lambda_p exists for every p, that it is an effectively computable algebraic integer
arising as a Perron eigenvalue of an explicit automaton-derived matrix, and that
lambda_p^{1/p} tends to the square root of the golden ratio. I have independently reproduced
his lambda_p to six significant figures, so I am not disputing any of that.

MY QUESTION IS ONLY ABOUT THE NEXT STEP, WHICH SANNA DOES NOT TAKE. For q = 9 through 17 I
have the exact minimal polynomial Pi_q of lambda_q over the rationals, verified irreducible,
and I have determined the Galois group of each to be the full symmetric group S_d on d = deg
Pi_q, via Dedekind plus Jordan (mod-p factorisation patterns giving 2-transitivity, a prime
cycle of length at most d - 3, and an odd element). The degrees are 7 at q = 9, then 9, 9, 13,
11, 13, 11, 13, 13 for q = 10 up to 17.

The polynomials themselves, so you can recognise them if they are in print:

Pi_9 = t^7 - 2t^6 - 62t^5 - 386t^4 - 2819t^3 - 62t^2 - 900t + 450.
Pi_10 = t^9 - 2t^8 - 96t^7 - 830t^6 - 7945t^5 - 2t^4 - 1852t^3 + 830t^2 - 4t + 4.
Pi_11 = t^9 - 2t^8 - 153t^7 - 1740t^6 - 21249t^5 + 9432t^4 + 86213t^3 + 1484t^2 + 18348t - 9174.
Pi_13 = t^11 - 2t^10 - 388t^9 - 7414t^8 - 148038t^7 + 317916t^6 + 4165856t^5 - 136252t^4
        - 1565891t^3 - 318938t^2 - 289380t + 144690.

Note the recurring shape: leading t^d - 2t^(d-1), and in the even-q cases a trailing pattern
ending in -8t + 8 with several coefficients repeating between the top and bottom halves.
