One narrow question in Diophantine approximation. Everything needed is below; it is short on
purpose.

Let phi be the golden ratio and let ||x|| denote the distance from x to the nearest integer.
Every positive integer n has a unique Zeckendorf expansion as a sum of non-consecutive
Fibonacci numbers; write kmin(n) for the smallest index appearing in it.

I can prove the following comparison, and it is elementary. From phi F_k = F_{k+1} - psi^k with
psi = -1/phi, summing over the Zeckendorf expansion gives phi n = integer - sum_j psi^{k_j}, so
||phi n|| equals that tail exactly; factoring out the last term and bounding the rest by a
geometric series using non-consecutiveness yields

    phi^{-2} <= ||phi n|| / phi^{-kmin(n)} <= phi.

So a small ||phi n|| forces kmin(n) to be large, with explicit constants.

MY QUESTION concerns integers that are sparse in base two. Consider

    u = s 2^i + t 2^j,     s and t each plus or minus one, i > j >= 0,

and also the single-power case u = plus or minus 2^i. Empirically kmin(|u|) does not grow with
i. Taking the maximum of kmin(|u|) over all such u with exponents below m, I get 12 for m up to
14, then 13, then 14 for m in the thirties and forties, 16 at m = 60, and 21 for every m from
80 through 160. The witnesses are 144, then 15360, then 2096896 = 2^21 - 2^8, then larger ones.
So the maximum creeps up at sporadic scales while remaining tiny compared with m.

I want a proof that this maximum grows slower than linearly in m. I do not need a sharp bound.
Anything of the form kmin(|u|) = o(m), or even kmin(|u|) <= m/2 for large m, would suffice for
my application, where the threshold I must stay below is m - 3.

Three things I have already checked, so please do not spend effort there.

That phi is badly approximable does not help. It gives ||phi q|| >= c/q with c near 1/sqrt5,
hence a bound in terms of the SIZE of q, whereas I need one in terms of the number of binary
digits of q. A dense integer and a two-digit integer of the same size receive the identical
bound, and the shortfall against what I need grows like (2/phi)^m, so no improvement of the
constant can rescue it.

The problem is therefore genuinely about binary sparsity, which suggests linear forms in
logarithms. I am aware of the Bravo, Gomez and Luca line on powers of two as sums of Fibonacci
numbers, which treats equations of this shape effectively by Baker's method, and of
Bugeaud, Cipu and Mignotte on the representation of Fibonacci and Lucas numbers in an integer
base, which classifies the Fibonacci numbers with few binary digits.

QUESTIONS.

One. Is the statement I want already in the literature, in either direction? A result bounding
the lowest Zeckendorf index of a binary-sparse integer, or bounding how well phi times such an
integer can approximate an integer, would settle it directly.

Two. If not, can it be proved by a Matveev-style bound on the relevant linear form? A sketch
with the shape of the estimate is more useful to me than a full write-up.

Three. Is it possible that the maximum is in fact unbounded and grows, just very slowly, in a
way that would still keep it below m/2? That would be equally useful and I would rather know.

Please separate what you know from the literature from what you are inferring, and say plainly
if this is out of reach.
