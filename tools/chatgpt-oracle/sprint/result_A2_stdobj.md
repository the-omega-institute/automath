Assessment
1. Verification of the machinery
The account in Part I is materially accurate. I found no incorrect displayed formula or false description of the principal outputs. Four qualifications matter for the field-facing assessment.
First, the abstract two-background perturbation lemma assumes slightly more than the summary records: besides positive backgrounds converging to 1 in L1, it requires the actual perturbed densities to retain a uniform positive lower bound, and it imposes a finite positive-entropy condition on the first background. These are used for remainder removal and finiteness, not merely for cosmetic normalization. 
Second, at general tensor order the paper proves positivity and the multiplicity-free O(d)-block decomposition of the stable quadratic form, but it does not calculate all harmonic-block eigenvalues. Explicit trace/traceless constants are calculated only for the covariance layer of the radial Poisson kernel.   
Third, the abstract kernel result is genuinely independent of a semigroup law, regular variation, or radial symmetry. But that also means that it does not, merely by abstraction, produce a theorem about general Lévy semigroups: it acts on an individual scaled density s−dp(⋅/s), not on an arbitrary time-dependent heat-kernel family. 
Fourth, the manuscript itself places the RKHS and lattice results in appendices and describes the principal shift-invariant-space machinery as imported. The paper-specific content there is the explicit Poisson symbol and its application to the particular centred observation profile. That arc is mathematically legitimate but is not part of the same central research field as the stable-entropy asymptotics. 
The priority boundary is also correctly represented. Chen–Niles-Weed already treat Gaussian smoothing and moment matching for several divergences, including relative entropy, while Ishige–Kawakami–Michihisa and the refined Ishige–Kawakami paper already provide higher-order fractional-diffusion density expansions. arXiv+2arXiv+2 The manuscript accurately confines its proposed novelty to the heavy-tailed quotient-to-entropy transfer, the endpoint moment threshold and sharpness, and the positive law-by-law decomposition. 

2. Correcting the standard-object inventory
Objects that should be struck or narrowed
Three entries in the proposed list are too broad.
Affine images, products, and finite mixtures of densities are operations or closure constructions, not independently studied objects at the level relevant here. The standard objects are the resulting Student, stable, product, or mixture kernels; the closure theorem is a result about those classes.
Csiszár f-divergences and Bregman divergences should not be listed without qualification. The manuscript develops KL divergence, one exact χ2 identity, and the logarithmic two-point Bregman integrand associated with KL. It does not prove a theorem for a class of f-divergences.
Hardy spaces on a strip, general RKHS theory, and general sampling theory should not be presented as central fields of the paper. They occur in a secondary realization of one explicit Poisson kernel space and in an application of a standard principal shift-invariant-space criterion.
Important standard objects that were omitted
The list should add:


anisotropic and non-symmetric strictly stable convolution semigroups;


isotropic unimodal Lévy processes and subordinate Brownian motions;


relative entropy between two simultaneous fractional heat flows;


stable de Bruijn identities and fractional Fisher-information constructions;


entropic convergence in the stable central limit theorem and stable domains of attraction.


These are standard neighbouring objects against which the actual reach of the manuscript can be measured. Entropic convergence to stable laws and fractional Fisher-information approaches already form an external literature independent of this manuscript. arXiv+2arXiv+2 Likewise, anisotropic stable kernels and broader unimodal Lévy heat kernels are established research objects with their own heat-kernel theory. arXiv+2EMS Press+2

3. Strongest reachable theorem about a standard object

It is a full relative-entropy dissipation and integral-representation theorem for two symmetric stable heat flows.
This directly concerns fractional heat semigroups, KL divergence, fractional Laplacians and nonlocal Dirichlet forms. It requires none of the manuscript’s invented terminology.

4.3 Extension or different project?
This is (i) a difficult extension of the present proof, not a different research project requiring a new core idea.
The reasons are concrete:


the relevant stable tail and derivative estimates are already established;


the bounded-score lower bound is already used;


the exact generator algebra is already known;


the Cauchy proof already supplies the annular approximation, chain-rule and Green-pairing architecture;


the endpoint follows from a short convexity argument rather than new asymptotic analysis.


The extension is difficult because the rational identities used for the Cauchy quotient disappear. They must be replaced by stable density comparability and normalized derivative estimates, and every noncompact pairing has to be rechecked in d dimensions. But the proof would remain recognizably the same proof.
By contrast, a theorem for all isotropic unimodal Lévy semigroups or all subordinate Brownian motions would require a uniform weighted heat-kernel local limit theorem and critical quotient estimates for a time-varying kernel family. That would be a new core analytic project. The present machinery does not yet justify that stronger target.

5. Residual objection after the theorem is proved
The strongest remaining objection would be:

The differential identity is formally standard, so the paper must establish that its real contribution is a genuinely new and reasonably sharp domain-and-endpoint theorem, rather than merely a lengthy justification of known Markov-generator calculus under a convenient moment assumption.

That objection has two parts.
First, the pure-jump Dirichlet-form literature already contains broad Hardy–Stein, polarized and Sobolev–Bregman identities. arXiv+3arXiv+3arXiv+3 A new paper could not claim discovery of the two-point logarithmic dissipation algebra. Its claim would have to be the exact moving-denominator theorem for stable heat flows from measure data, the finite-moment domain, the endpoint closure, and the connection to Johnson’s integral-representation problem.
Second, the exponent d+α would be visibly a sufficient hypothesis arising from a uniform quotient bound. It is unlikely to be the ultimate entropy domain. A referee could reasonably ask whether the identity actually holds under finite first moment, finite initial relative entropy, or another natural Dirichlet-form condition. The revision would not have to solve that sharper problem, but it would have to explain why the stated domain is natural, show where the proof genuinely uses it, and avoid calling it optimal without counterexamples.
After that, the principal editorial objection would be architecture: the Cayley coefficient calculus, the stable asymptotics, the new fractional dissipation theorem, and the RKHS lattice appendix would still constitute several mathematical arcs. The proposed theorem would give the entropy-dissipation arc an independently recognizable field object and would substantially reduce that objection, but the RKHS/lattice material would remain secondary and should be presented as such.
Overall conclusion
There is no ceiling forcing the manuscript to remain confined to its own constructions. The machinery can plausibly prove a substantive theorem about a standard object: the KL evolution of two symmetric stable fractional heat flows and its exact nonlocal dissipation representation.
That is the strongest target here because it is:


not a renaming of the positive-density decomposition;


not another coefficient in the same expansion;


not a composition with a classical quadrature equivalence;


not an imported sampling theorem;


directly connected to a recognized stable de Bruijn problem;


supported by the stable kernel estimates and by an existing complete Cauchy domain proof.


The target is difficult but genuinely reachable. Its main risk is not truth; it is establishing a sufficiently sharp novelty boundary against the general pure-jump entropy-calculus literature.
