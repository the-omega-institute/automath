# Terminology audit

## Executive summary (8 lines)

1. I count **68 coined or non-standard term families**: 29 directly collide with established usage, 27 are opaque coinages without a direct collision, and 12 are standard or substantially aligned.
2. The count is by conceptual family, not spelling: for example, `visible value`, `visible branch`, and `visible quotient` are separate, while singular/plural and adjectival variants are not.
3. The most damaging collision is **realization**: it denotes assignments, groupoid-valued lifts, existence constructions, and geometric realization, while a logician expects realization of a formula or type.
4. The second is **state forcing**: the relation is ordinary pointwise satisfaction by every assignment in a set, not set-theoretic, Kripke, Beth, or Kripke-Joyal forcing.
5. The third is the **visible/hidden/branch** family: it renames global sections of a component sheaf, images of homomorphisms, and quotients by those images without a stable mathematical meaning of visibility.
6. `slice` is used correctly for the category-theoretic slice, and `bouquet` correctly for a wedge of spheres; their compounds are opaque, but the base terms should not be renamed.
7. Only a small core is load-bearing: the packaged lift hypothesis, representative-rigidity, the two aggregate quotients, their comparison kernel, and controlled transport among finite presentations.
8. **Verdict:** the vocabulary is predominantly idiosyncratic naming of standard objects and maps; the genuinely new structures do not require most of it.

## Scope and method

This is a terminology audit, not a mathematical correctness review. I inspected all 52 `definition` environments and also searched theorem titles and prose for terms introduced only by use. Locations are source locations in compilation order. "Formal" means a displayed definition environment; "informal" means that prose or a theorem eventually pins down the meaning; "no" means that the paper never gives that expression its own definition. The audit groups variants that denote the same construction, but it does not group mathematically different uses merely because they share a word.

The collision column uses three labels:

- **C**: direct collision or materially misleading reuse of established terminology;
- **O**: opaque/local coinage, but no single established meaning is displaced;
- **A**: standard or substantially aligned usage.

The last column is also the renaming proposal for every **C** entry. For **O** entries it gives the clearest standard-language replacement. Terms marked **A** should normally be retained.

## Detailed collision analysis

### 1. `realization` is internally inconsistent

The manuscript does not merely differ from one external convention. It uses the same word in four incompatible internal roles:

| Occurrence | Actual object | Standard reading |
|---|---|---|
| `R_p` is a set of realizations | Variable assignments in a many-sorted structure | In model theory, a tuple/model realizes a formula or type. |
| `realization prestack`, `realization-stack extension` | A banded category fibred in groupoids with prescribed `pi_0` | A category theorist expects a moduli/realization construction, not an arbitrary lift package. |
| `realization theorem`, `mixed realizations` | Existence of deliberately constructed typed expansions with prescribed maps | A model theorist expects a theorem about realizing types, diagrams, or theories. |
| geometric realization `|K|` | The standard topological realization of a simplicial complex | This is the one standard topological use, but it is buried among the other three. |

This is the single largest impediment to reading. It occurs 198 times and infects theorem statements, hypotheses, and the abstract. The repair must be sense-by-sense, not a global search-and-replace.

### 2. `forcing` announces the wrong semantics

Definition `state-forcing` is exactly universal Tarskian satisfaction over a nonempty team. There are no recursive forcing clauses; quantifiers are evaluated pointwise before the universal quantification over assignments. The manuscript acknowledges this in Remark `flat-forcing-convention`, but only after the title, abstract, introduction, and notation have already activated the standard meanings of forcing. A logician will reasonably test conjunction, disjunction, implication, and quantifier clauses as if this were Kripke-Joyal, Beth, intuitionistic Kripke, or set-theoretic forcing. Calling it **universal team satisfaction** makes the persistence theorem immediate and correctly locates the work relative to team semantics.

### 3. `visible`, `hidden`, and `branch` do not denote one invariant idea

The paper uses "visible" for at least three levels:

1. a terminal section of a sheafification/component sheaf;
2. a quotient by the subgroup generated by coordinates of a chosen cocycle;
3. a cokernel of the universal-coefficient evaluation map.

These are related by maps and sometimes surjections, but they are not the same construction. "Hidden" then means the subgroup killed by the relevant quotient, while "branch" means a global component section. A reader from contextuality may take "visible" to mean empirically observable/support-compatible; a topologist may take a branch to be a local analytic branch; a logician may take it to be a path through a Kripke/tree model. The words therefore hide the actual standard objects. The paper should name `aF(a)`, `Im(ev_omega)`, `coker(ev_omega)`, `intersection K_v`, and `sum K_v` directly.

### 4. `slice`: correct base word, nonstandard compound

Here `C_a=C_{p,s}/a` really is the category-theoretic slice, and the topology is restricted to it. A category theorist's initial reading is therefore correct. The trouble is stylistic:

- "slice-separated reference slices" in the abstract is not standard English or standard category theory;
- `slice-separated` sounds like a new categorical separation axiom;
- `reference slice` is used before `a=[r]_p` and `C/a` are explained.

The exact standard statement is: **the restricted presheaf `F|_{C/a}` is separated**. Keep `slice`; delete the coined adjective.

### 5. `bouquet`: correct topology, opaque site name

In topology, a bouquet is a wedge sum. The manuscript's `K_beta` has geometric realization homotopy equivalent to `wedge_{j=1}^beta S^2`, so this use is correct. A specialist would not misread the base word once that equation appears. The abstract, however, says "bouquet good covers" without saying what is wedged, and "bouquet site" makes the site sound canonical when it is a specifically generated intersection site from chosen barycentric open-star covers. Retain **bouquet** after the phrase **wedge of `beta` 2-spheres**; rename only the generated site.

### 6. Other collisions that change mathematical expectations

- **Conservative extension:** standard consequence conservativity is not the same as preservation of satisfaction on reducts of the higher models. Without an expansion/exhaustivity result for lower models, the former does not follow from the displayed definition.
- **Globally conservative:** the definition is essential surjectivity, whereas a conservative functor reflects isomorphisms.
- **Pure extension:** already means a short exact sequence that is pure in module/abelian-group theory. The paper means membership in the `Ext` summand/kernel in the universal coefficient sequence.
- **Intrinsic:** the quotients are only controlled relative to finite presentations and chosen comparison zigzags, with no path independence. "Intrinsic" overstates the result.
- **Strictification:** the four "codes" are injective set maps into a product with an auxiliary finite set. No weak categorical structure is made strict.
- **Null/total:** a single readout classified as null can have no sheafified value or multiple sheafified values; neither is a null object or a zero value, and "total" only means existence of a global section.
- **Amplitude lift:** its definition supplies a banded groupoid lift of a support presheaf, not numerical amplitudes, a Hilbert-space vector, normalization, or Born probabilities.

### Standard objects given new names

The following are not new objects: a nonempty team of assignments; universal team satisfaction; a thin site; a presheaf; a separated restriction to a slice; plus-construction representatives; a section of a sheafification; a category fibred in groupoids; stackification; essential surjectivity; a full component substack; a banded gerbe and its Giraud class; a Cech cocycle; the subgroup generated by its values; the UCT map `H^2 -> Hom(H_2,A)`; its image and cokernel; annihilators under Pontryagin duality; `A/(intersection K_v)`; `A/(sum K_v)`; and a set-theoretic injection separating quotient fibers. Renaming these objects does not simplify new mathematics; it makes familiar mathematics harder to recognize.

### Terms that should not be renamed

- **slice category/site:** used correctly for `C/a`.
- **bouquet:** used correctly for a wedge sum, provided the spheres are stated at first occurrence.
- **information state:** reasonably aligned with a nonempty set of possible assignments, especially if "team" is supplied as a synonym.

- **component gerbe:** retainable once `visible branch` is replaced by `global component section`; it accurately describes the full gerbe over one connected-component section.

## Draft abstract

A nonzero finite abelian group `G` occurs as the kernel of the canonical map from the label-aware quotient to the quotient detected uniformly across two component sections, for the chosen good-cover models of a wedge of `beta` 2-spheres, exactly when `d(G) <= 2 beta` and `G` is not a cyclic `p`-group. We derive this from the universal coefficient sequence after fixing a finite nerve, a constant finite abelian band, and a banded groupoid lift. For a component-gerbe class `omega`, evaluation on `H_2` gives a homomorphism `ev_omega:H_2(N,Z)->A`; its image and cokernel determine the componentwise and aggregate quotients. When `H_1(N,Z)=0` and `H_2(N,Z)=Z^beta`, the possible image subgroups are exactly those generated by at most `beta` elements. We also identify matching families without global amalgamations with non-neutral component gerbes under explicit separatedness, effectivity, and Cech-to-derived comparison hypotheses.

## Editorial verdict

The editor's complaint is justified at the level of readability even before mathematical evaluation. Core results can be stated almost entirely with standard terms: teams, presheaves, matching families, slice sites, separatedness, stackification, component gerbes, Giraud classes, the universal-coefficient evaluation map, images, cokernels, intersections, sums, and kernels. The few new comparison constructions become easier, not harder, to see once the metaphorical vocabulary is removed.
