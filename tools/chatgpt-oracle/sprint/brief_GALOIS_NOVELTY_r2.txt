I need a publishability judgement on a piece of work whose novelty I have already checked
myself. Please be blunt rather than encouraging.

THE SETTING. Let r(n) count the representations of n as a sum of distinct Fibonacci numbers.
For a fixed positive integer p, the power sums of r(n) over an initial range grow like a power
of the range, and the exponent is governed by a constant lambda_p. Carlo Sanna, in "A Note on
the Power Sums of the Number of Fibonacci Partitions", Discrete Analysis 2025 issue 2, proves
that lambda_p exists for every p, that it is the Perron-Frobenius eigenvalue of the transition
matrix of an automaton built from p parallel copies of Berstel's automaton, and hence that it
is an algebraic integer equal to the greatest real root of an effectively computable monic
integer polynomial. His Table 1 lists lambda_p together with its minimal polynomial over the
rationals for p running from one to eight.

WHAT I HAVE. I computed the minimal polynomials for p from nine to seventeen, verified each
irreducible, and determined the Galois group of each over the rationals to be the full
symmetric group on its degree. The degrees are seven, then nine, nine, thirteen, eleven,
thirteen, eleven, thirteen, thirteen.

WHAT I ALREADY ESTABLISHED, so please do not spend effort re-deriving it. First, my constants
are the continuation of Sanna's table rather than a separate family: his last entry is about
nine point four, mine begins about eleven point eight, the normalised quantities descend
monotonically towards the square root of the golden ratio exactly as his second theorem
requires, and every polynomial in both stretches begins with the same two leading terms.
Second, my polynomials are therefore an extension of a published table produced by a method
that same paper supplies. Third, I ran my Galois criterion on his own eight rows and every one
of them is also the full symmetric group, so my nine values continue a pattern rather than
break one. Fourth, Sanna computes no Galois groups, no discriminants and no splitting
behaviour, so the arithmetic side is genuinely untouched by him.

MY QUESTIONS.

One. Given that the answer is the generic one and that it already holds on the previously
published rows, is "the Galois group is the full symmetric group for these nine explicit
polynomials" publishable as the primary theorem of a paper in a number theory journal? My own
read is that it is not, and that a referee would call it a computation. Tell me if you
disagree and why.

Two. The obvious upgrade is a proof that the Galois group is the full symmetric group for
every p, rather than sixteen verified instances. How hard is that likely to be for a family of
this kind, where the polynomials arise as characteristic polynomials of an explicit growing
family of nonnegative integer matrices and share a rigid leading shape? Point me at the
techniques that have worked for analogous families, and tell me honestly if this is the kind
of thing that is usually out of reach.

Three. If that proof is out of reach, is there a different genuine theorem these objects would
support? I would rather abandon the direction than dress up a computation. Concrete
suggestions only, and say which ones you think are actually attainable versus merely
plausible.

Please separate what you know from the literature from what you are inferring.
