WHY ADDING THEOREMS HAS NOT WORKED. Independent assessment placed this paper at TIER-3 or TIER-4, and the recurring criticisms were: the objects are bespoke (defined by us, so results about them interest only us), the machinery is applied to itself rather than to anything the field already cares about, and the proofs are bookkeeping over imported inputs. Generalising our own constructions further does not fix any of that. What raises tier is one of exactly four things:

 (A) SOLVING A NAMED OPEN PROBLEM. A question that has a proposer, a literature, and people who would notice it being answered. Not a question we invented.
 (B) PROVING SOMETHING ABOUT THE FIELD'S STANDARD OBJECTS, not ours. If our machinery only speaks about objects we defined, it is a private language. If it can be shown to apply canonically to the objects everyone in the field already studies, the same theorems become interesting to everyone.
 (C) REMOVING A HYPOTHESIS. Turning a conditional result unconditional, or removing a genericity/regularity/spectral-gap assumption, is a recognised contribution in its own right.
 (D) SHARPNESS. A matching lower bound, an optimal constant with a construction attaining it, or a converse that closes an iff.
