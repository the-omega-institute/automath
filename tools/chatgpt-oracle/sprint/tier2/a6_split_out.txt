\begin{abstract}
Fix \(s>2\), and weight the canonical regular continued fractions
\([0;a_1,\ldots,a_r]\) with \(a_r\ge2\) and
\(a_1+\cdots+a_r=n\) proportionally to the inverse \(s\)-th power of their
denominators.  We prove a total-variation condensation theorem: with
probability tending to one there is a unique partial quotient larger than
\(n/2\), and the words on its left and right converge jointly to independent
finite words whose masses are proportional to their inverse-continuant
weights.  Thus the largest partial quotient is \(n-O_{\mathbb P}(1)\).  We
give the exact limiting law of its defect, the joint total-variation limit of
its location and the numbers of partial quotients on both sides, and the
asymptotic factorization of the sampled denominator.

The normalization is the fixed-digit-sum continuant sum studied by
Dushistova after the Farey-tree work of Moshchevitin--Zhigljavsky.  We prove
\(Z_n(s)\sim2\{\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)\}^2n^{-s}\) without a tail hypothesis.
Under the exact translation \(n=d+1\), this is the termwise identity
\(b_{2d+1}(s)=\sigma_{s/2}(d+1)\).  We also localize the loss of the
restriction \(u>1\) in Dushistova's Lemma~7: the empty left context is
included in a doubled convolution and counted twice, so the printed leading
coefficient \(R_s+2R_s^2\) has excess \(R_s\) and becomes \(2R_s^2\).

As downstream consequences, at the arithmetic critical point
\(\zeta(\sigma_0-1)/\zeta(\sigma_0)=2\), the condensation estimate supplies
the regularly varying cost law and the constant \(b_C=8\).  A weighted
renewal identity for Weinstein's free generators then gives a stable law and
the noninteger \(m^{3-\sigma_0}\) correction to one-layer Fibonacci
partition sums.  We also prove full one- and two-dimensional large deviation
principles, including affine coexistence faces, and transfer them to an exact
finite-window Zeckendorf model.  The pressure and its negative-temperature
transition are the published \(L^q\)-spectrum of the golden-ratio Bernoulli
convolution in an affine normalization; they are recovered here, not claimed
as new.
\end{abstract}

\section{Condensation for denominator-weighted Brocot fractions}
\label{sec:brocot-condensation}

For a finite word \(\mathbf w=(w_1,\ldots,w_k)\) of positive integers,
let \(\mathcal K(\mathbf w)\) denote its regular continuant, with
\[
 \mathcal K(\varnothing)=1,\qquad
 \mathcal K(w_1)=w_1,
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal K(w_1,\ldots,w_k)
 =w_k\mathcal K(w_1,\ldots,w_{k-1})
  +\mathcal K(w_1,\ldots,w_{k-2}).
\]
Write \(|\mathbf w|_1=w_1+\cdots+w_k\), with
\(|\varnothing|_1=0\), and let \(\ell(\mathbf w)=k\).  Put
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  \mathcal W_{\rm L}&:=\{\varnothing\}\cup
    \bigcup_{k\ge1}\mathbb N^k,\\
  \mathcal W_{\rm R}&:=\{\varnothing\}\cup
    \{(w_1,\ldots,w_k):k\ge1,\ w_i\ge1,\ w_k\ge2\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal W_{\rm R}\) is the set of canonical regular
continued-fraction words, together with the empty word, whereas no terminal
condition is imposed on \(\mathcal W_{\rm L}\).

For \(n\ge2\), let
\[
 \mathcal Q_n:=
 \{[0;a_1,\ldots,a_r]:r\ge1,\ a_i\in\mathbb N,\ a_r\ge2,
                         \ a_1+\cdots+a_r=n\}.
\]
These are precisely the Brocot fractions of order \(n\), written in
canonical regular continued-fraction form.  For \(s>2\), define
\begin{equation}
 Z_n(s):=\sum_{x\in\mathcal Q_n}\operatorname{den}(x)^{-s},
 \qquad
 \mathbb P_{n,s}\{x\}:=\frac{\operatorname{den}(x)^{-s}}{Z_n(s)}.
 \label{eq:brocot-probability}
\end{equation}
The order in this definition is the total digit sum \(n\); it is not the
odd renewal cost used later in the paper.

\begin{theorem}[Total-variation condensation for Brocot fractions]
\label{thm:brocot-condensation}
Fix \(s>2\), and set
\[
 \rho_s:=\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}.
\]
Sample
\(x_n=[0;a_1,\ldots,a_{r_n}]\) according to \(\mathbb P_{n,s}\), and put
\[
 M_n:=\max_{1\le j\le r_n}a_j.
\]
On \(\{M_n>n/2\}\), let \(J_n\) be the unique index attaining the maximum
and define
\[
 U_n=(a_1,\ldots,a_{J_n-1}),
 \qquad
 V_n=(a_{J_n+1},\ldots,a_{r_n}).
\]
On the complementary event set \(U_n=V_n=\varnothing\), and extend \(J_n\)
arbitrarily, say by \(J_n=1\).

Let \(U\) and \(V\) be independent random words with laws
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  \mathbb P\{U=\mathbf u\}
  &=\frac{\mathcal K(\mathbf u)^{-s}}{2\rho_s},
  &\mathbf u&\in\mathcal W_{\rm L},\\
  \mathbb P\{V=\mathbf v\}
  &=\frac{\mathcal K(\mathbf v)^{-s}}{\rho_s},
  &\mathbf v&\in\mathcal W_{\rm R}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:limiting-context-laws}
\end{equation}
Then, as \(n\to\infty\),
\begin{align}
 \mathbb P_{n,s}\{M_n>n/2\}&\longrightarrow1,
 \label{eq:brocot-unique-condensate}\\
 d_{\rm TV}\bigl(\mathcal L_{\mathbb P_{n,s}}(U_n,V_n),
                   \mathcal L(U,V)\bigr)&\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:brocot-context-tv}
\end{align}
Consequently, if \(D:=|U|_1+|V|_1\), then
\begin{equation}
 n-M_n\xrightarrow{\rm TV}D,
 \qquad
 \frac{M_n}{n}\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}1.
 \label{eq:brocot-defect-limit}
\end{equation}
The law of the defect is explicitly
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb P\{D=k\}
 =\frac1{2\rho_s^2}
 \sum_{\substack{\mathbf u\in\mathcal W_{\rm L},\
                   \mathbf v\in\mathcal W_{\rm R}\\
                   |\mathbf u|_1+|\mathbf v|_1=k}}
 \{\mathcal K(\mathbf u)\mathcal K(\mathbf v)\}^{-s},
 \qquad k\ge0.
 \label{eq:brocot-defect-law}
\end{equation}
The location of the condensate and the numbers of digits on its two sides
satisfy the joint limit
\begin{equation}
 (J_n-1,r_n-J_n)\xrightarrow{\rm TV}
 (\ell(U),\ell(V)).
 \label{eq:brocot-location-limit}
\end{equation}
Finally, if \(q_n=\operatorname{den}(x_n)\), then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{q_n}{M_n\mathcal K(U_n)\mathcal K(V_n)}
 \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}1.
 \label{eq:brocot-denominator-ratio}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The theorem concerns canonical continued fractions, their denominators, and
Brocot order only.  The general one-big-jump results for independent
heavy-tailed variables and the total-variation condensation theorems for
Gibbs partitions provide close probabilistic analogues
\cite{ArmendarizLoulakis2011,Stufler2020}.  They do not imply
\Cref{thm:brocot-condensation}: the weight
\(\mathcal K(a_1,\ldots,a_r)^{-s}\) is not a product of weights of the
digits or components.  The two-sided continuant estimate below supplies the
factorization specific to this arithmetic model.

We next connect \(Z_n(s)\) to the denominator layers used later.  For a
reduced fraction \(p/q\in(0,1)\), write its unique negative continued
fraction as
\[
 \frac pq=
 \cfrac1{e_1-\cfrac1{e_2-\ddots-\cfrac1{e_k}}},
 \qquad e_i\ge2,
\]
and set
\[
 d(p/q):=\sum_{i=1}^k(e_i-1),
 \qquad c(p/q):=2d(p/q)+1.
\]
The negative continuant is defined by
\[
 \begin{gathered}
  \mathcal D(\varnothing)=1,qquad \mathcal D(e_1)=e_1,\\
  \mathcal D(e_1,\ldots,e_k)
  =e_k\mathcal D(e_1,\ldots,e_{k-1})
   -\mathcal D(e_1,\ldots,e_{k-2}).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Thus \(q=\mathcal D(e_1,\ldots,e_k)\).  For real \(s\), put
\begin{equation}
 b_\ell(s):=
 \sum_{\substack{0<p<q,\ (p,q)=1\\c(p/q)=\ell}}q^{-s}.
 \label{eq:weighted-letter-definition}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 b_{2d+1}(s)
 =\sum_{k=1}^d
   \sum_{\substack{f_1+\cdots+f_k=d\\f_i\ge1}}
   \mathcal D(f_1+1,\ldots,f_k+1)^{-s},
 \qquad b_{2d}(s)=0.
 \label{eq:weighted-letter-coefficients}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact identification with Dushistova's sum]
\label{prop:dushistova-exact-identification}
For \(n\ge2\) and \(\beta>1\), put
\[
 \mathcal A_n^{\rm D}:=
 \bigcup_{r\ge1}\{(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\in\mathbb N^r:
                         a_r\ge2,\ a_1+\cdots+a_r=n\}
\]
and
\[
 \sigma_\beta(n):=\sum_{\mathbf a\in\mathcal A_n^{\rm D}}
                    \mathcal K(\mathbf a)^{-2\beta}.
\]
Then, term by term,
\begin{equation}
 Z_n(s)=\sigma_{s/2}(n),
 \qquad
 b_{2d+1}(s)=\sigma_{s/2}(d+1)=Z_{d+1}(s)
 \quad(d\ge1,\ s>2).
 \label{eq:dushistova-exact-identification}
\end{equation}
In particular, the field order and renewal indices are related by
\(n=d+1\) and \(c=2d+1=2n-1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The denominator of \([0;a_1,\ldots,a_r]\) is
\(\mathcal K(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\), so the first equality is immediate from
the definitions.  For completeness, the elementary conversion to the
negative expansion is, when \(r\) is even,
\[
 (a_1+1,2^{[a_2-1]},a_3+2,2^{[a_4-1]},\ldots,
   a_{r-1}+2,2^{[a_r-1]}),
\]
where \(2^{[j]}\) denotes a string of \(j\) digits equal to two.  When
\(r>1\) is odd, use the same alternating construction through \(a_{r-1}\),
followed by \(a_r+1\); for \(r=1\), the negative word is \((a_1)\).

represent the same fraction and have the same denominator.  In all cases,
\[
 d(p/q)=a_1+\cdots+a_r-1.
\]
Hence \(c(p/q)=2d+1\) is equivalent to total regular digit sum \(d+1\),
and the second equality follows term by term.

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint correction in the left context]
\label{lem:dushistova-endpoint-correction}
For \(s>2\), put
\[
 r_m(s):=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf v\in\mathcal W_{\rm R}\\
                          |\mathbf v|_1=m}}
          \mathcal K(\mathbf v)^{-s},
 \qquad
 \ell_m(s):=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf u\in\mathcal W_{\rm L}\\
                             |\mathbf u|_1=m}}
          \mathcal K(\mathbf u)^{-s}.
\]
Then
\[
 r_0=\ell_0=1,\qquad r_1=0,\qquad \ell_1=1,
 \qquad \ell_m=2r_m\quad(m>1).
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathcal W_{\rm R}}\mathcal K(\mathbf v)^{-s}
 =R_s:=\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}=\rho_s,
 \qquad
 \sum_{\mathbf u\in\mathcal W_{\rm L}}\mathcal K(\mathbf u)^{-s}
 =2R_s,
 \label{eq:brocot-context-masses}
\end{equation}
and the three left-context cases contribute
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{c|c}
  |\mathbf u|_1=0 & R_s\\
  |\mathbf u|_1=1 & R_s\\
  |\mathbf u|_1>1 & 2(R_s-1)R_s
 \end{array}
 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad 2R_s^2.
 \label{eq:left-context-endpoint-cases}
\end{equation}
In particular, the conflicting printed and corrected coefficients are
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\text{Dushistova: }R_s+2R_s^2}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \boxed{\text{this paper: }2R_s^2}.
 \label{eq:dushistova-conflicting-constants}
\end{equation}
At \(s=\sigma_0\), these are \(10\) and \(8\), respectively.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Canonical regular words of denominator \(q\) parameterize the
\(\varphi(q)\) reduced fractions in \((0,1)\), where \(\varphi\) is Euler's
totient function.  Including the empty
word and using the totient Dirichlet series gives
\[
 1+\sum_{q\ge2}\frac{\varphi(q)}{q^s}
 =\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}=R_s.
\]
For \(m>1\), every canonical word of digit sum \(m\) has exactly two
positive expansions: itself and the word obtained by replacing its final
digit \(a\ge2\) by \(a-1,1\).  This move preserves the digit sum and the
continuant, so \(\ell_m=2r_m\).  At the endpoints, the empty word is the
only word of sum zero, whereas \((1)\) is the only positive word of sum one
and is not canonical.  This proves the identities and
\eqref{eq:left-context-endpoint-cases}.

The precise loss in Dushistova's Lemma~7
\cite[pp.~668--669]{Dushistova2007} is the loss of the restriction \(u>1\).
After separating the \(u=1\) term, the proof replaces the \(u>1\)
left-context sum by twice a canonical convolution denoted \(\Sigma_2\).
Its displayed definition drops \(u>1\), thereby including the empty left
context \(u=0\).  Since \(\Sigma_2\) is doubled, that endpoint is counted
twice rather than once.  With Dushistova's finite-cutoff notation, extending
\(\sigma_\beta(0)=1\) and \(\sigma_\beta(1)=0\), the exact finite identity is
\[
 R_0(w)=\Sigma_1(w)+2\Sigma_2(w)
        -\sum_{v\le w}\sigma_\beta(v).
\]
The subtraction removes exactly one empty-left-context contribution.  Its
limit is \(R_s\), so the printed split has excess exactly \(R_s\).
Retaining the three endpoint classes gives
\(R_s+R_s+2(R_s-1)R_s=2R_s^2\).  This uses the same
empty-continuant, final-digit, and exponent conventions as the published
paper; it is not a normalization change.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Greedy moderate-block bound]
\label{lem:greedy-moderate-block-bound}
For \(s>2\), define
\[
 \eta_s(h):=
 \sum_{\substack{\mathbf u\in\mathcal W_{\rm L}\\|\mathbf u|_1\ge h}}
 \mathcal K(\mathbf u)^{-s}
\]
and let \(G_s(N,h)\) be the total inverse-continuant weight of the words
\(\mathbf u\in\mathcal W_{\rm L}\) with digit sum at least \(N\) and every
digit smaller than \(h\).  For all sufficiently large \(h\),
\begin{equation}
 G_s(N,h)\le
 \frac{2\rho_s}{1-\eta_s(h)}
 \eta_s(h)^{\lfloor N/(2h)\rfloor-1}.
 \label{eq:moderate-block-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Scan a word from the left and close a block at the first digit for which the
block's digit sum reaches \(h\); repeat, leaving the final remainder
unclosed.  This factorization is unique.  Since every digit is smaller than
\(h\), each closed block has digit sum in \([h,2h)\), and a word of digit sum
at least \(N\) has at least \(\lfloor N/(2h)\rfloor-1\) closed blocks.
The concatenation inequality makes the inverse-continuant weight at most the
product of the block weights.  Enlarge each closed-block class to all words
of digit sum at least \(h\), of mass \(\eta_s(h)\), and the remainder class
to all of \(\mathcal W_{\rm L}\), of mass \(2\rho_s\).  Summing the resulting
geometric series proves the bound; \(\eta_s(h)\to0\) by
\eqref{eq:brocot-context-masses}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[One-large-partial-quotient estimates]
\label{prop:brocot-context-estimates}
Let \(P_n(s)\) be the part of \(Z_n(s)\) contributed by words with a digit
greater than \(n/2\).  For every fixed \(s>2\),
\begin{align}
 n^sP_n(s)&\longrightarrow2\rho_s^2,
 \label{eq:principal-large-digit-limit}\\
 Z_n(s)-P_n(s)&=o(n^{-s}),
 \label{eq:noncondensed-negligible}\\
 Z_n(s)&\sim2\rho_s^2n^{-s}.
 \label{eq:brocot-partition-asymptotic}
\end{align}
More precisely, cutting at the unique digit greater than \(n/2\) is a
bijection with pairs
\((\mathbf u,\mathbf v)\in\mathcal W_{\rm L}\times\mathcal W_{\rm R}\)
such that \(|\mathbf u|_1+|\mathbf v|_1<n/2\), the central digit being
\(a=n-|\mathbf u|_1-|\mathbf v|_1\).  For every fixed pair,
\begin{equation}
 n^s\mathcal K(\mathbf u,a,\mathbf v)^{-s}
 \longrightarrow
 \{\mathcal K(\mathbf u)\mathcal K(\mathbf v)\}^{-s},
 \label{eq:fixed-context-limit}
\end{equation}
and throughout this parameterization,
\begin{equation}
 n^s\mathcal K(\mathbf u,a,\mathbf v)^{-s}
 \le2^s\{\mathcal K(\mathbf u)\mathcal K(\mathbf v)\}^{-s}.
 \label{eq:summable-context-domination}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The continuant concatenation formula gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal K(\mathbf u\mathbf v)
 \ge\mathcal K(\mathbf u)\mathcal K(\mathbf v),
 \label{eq:continuant-concatenation-lower-bound}
\end{equation}
and multilinearity gives, with the contexts fixed,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal K(\mathbf u,a,\mathbf v)
 =a\mathcal K(\mathbf u)\mathcal K(\mathbf v)
  +O_{\mathbf u,\mathbf v}(1).
 \label{eq:continuant-large-digit-affine}
\end{equation}
A digit greater than \(n/2\) is unique.  Cutting there gives the asserted
bijection.  If \(\mathbf v\ne\varnothing\), its terminal digit remains at
least two; if \(\mathbf v=\varnothing\), the central digit is greater than
\(n/2\) and is therefore canonical for \(n\ge3\).  Since \(a>n/2\),
\eqref{eq:continuant-concatenation-lower-bound} yields
\eqref{eq:summable-context-domination}, while
\eqref{eq:continuant-large-digit-affine} yields
\eqref{eq:fixed-context-limit}.  The majorant is summable by
\eqref{eq:brocot-context-masses}.  Dominated convergence therefore gives
\eqref{eq:principal-large-digit-limit}.

It remains to bound every word without a digit greater than \(n/2\).  Choose
\[
 \frac{s}{2(s-1)}<\delta<1,
 \qquad h=\lfloor n^\delta\rfloor.
\]
A word having at least two digits not smaller than \(h\) can be cut at its
first two such digits.  Dropping the restrictions on the three intervening
contexts and applying \eqref{eq:continuant-concatenation-lower-bound} bounds
its total weight by
\begin{equation}
 (2\rho_s)^2\rho_s
 \left(\sum_{a\ge h}a^{-s}\right)^2
 =O_s(h^{2-2s})=o(n^{-s}).
 \label{eq:two-large-digits-negligible}
\end{equation}

For words whose digits are all smaller than \(h\),
\Cref{lem:greedy-moderate-block-bound} with \(N=n\) is
superpolynomially small, since \(n/h\to\infty\) and \(\eta_s(h)\to0\).
Every remaining word has exactly one digit \(a\ge h\), with \(a\le n/2\).
Its two surrounding contexts have combined digit sum at least \(n/2\), so
one has digit sum at least \(n/4\).  Cutting at \(a\), bounding the other
context by \(2\rho_s\), and allowing either context to be the long one gives
\[
 4\rho_s\sum_{h\le a\le n/2}a^{-s}G_s(n/4,h).
\]
The first two factors grow at most polynomially, while the last is
superpolynomially small.  Together with
\eqref{eq:two-large-digits-negligible}, this proves
\eqref{eq:noncondensed-negligible}, and hence
\eqref{eq:brocot-partition-asymptotic}.  No regular-variation or renewal
theorem is used in this argument.
\end{proof}

\label{lem:continued-fraction-knauf-normalization}
For \(d\ge1\), write a composition of \(d\) as
\(d=e_1+\cdots+e_r\), and put \(a_i=e_i+1\).  Encode the composition by the
word \(w=w_1\cdots w_{d-1}\in\{L,R\}^{d-1}\) whose \(j\)-th letter is \(R\)
exactly when \(j=e_1+\cdots+e_i\) for some \(i<r\), and is \(L\) otherwise.
With
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 R=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_w=w_1\cdots w_{d-1},
\]
the map \((a_1,\ldots,a_r)\mapsto w\) is a bijection, and
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac pq&=[0;a_1,\ldots,a_r]_-=
 \frac{\text{sum of the first row of }M_w}{\|M_w\|_\Sigma},\\
 q&=\mathcal D(a_1,\ldots,a_r)=\|M_w\|_\Sigma.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:negative-cf-word-bijection}
\end{equation}
Thus \(d(p/q)=d\) is Knauf generation \(d\), the corresponding matrix word
has length \(d-1\), and the generator cost is \(c(p/q)=2d+1\).  In particular,
for \(\beta\in\mathbb R\), the even Knauf sum in the convention of
\cite[Sec.~IV]{FialaKlebanOzluk2003} is exactly
\[
 Z^{K,e}_d(2\beta)
 =\sum_{d(p/q)=d}q^{-2\beta}.
\]
There is no \(d=0\) fraction in \((0,1)\); the first four nonempty layers are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
d&\text{words}&q=\|M_w\|_\Sigma\text{ in word order}&c=2d+1\\ \hline
1&\varnothing&2&3\\
2&L,R&3,\,3&5\\
3&LL,LR,RL,RR&4,\,5,\,5,\,4&7\\
4&\{L,R\}^3&5,\,7,\,8,\,7,\,7,\,8,\,7,\,5&9
\end{array}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choosing the separator positions of a composition is the standard bijection
between compositions of \(d\) and binary words of length \(d-1\).  Starting
from \(M_\varnothing=I\), simultaneous induction on the word length, using
right multiplication by \(L\) or \(R\), gives the displayed subtractive
Euclidean recursion for the numerator and the negative-continuant recursion
for the denominator.  Explicitly, appending \(L\) replaces the last
composition part \(e_r\) by \(e_r+1\), whereas appending \(R\) replaces it by
\((e_r,1)\); substituting these two operations in
\(\mathcal D(a_1,\ldots,a_r)
=a_r\mathcal D(a_1,\ldots,a_{r-1})
-\mathcal D(a_1,\ldots,a_{r-2})\) agrees with
\(M_{wL}=M_wL\) and \(M_{wR}=M_wR\), respectively.  This proves
\eqref{eq:negative-cf-word-bijection}.
The depth and cost identities follow from the definitions.  The Knauf
identity is then term-by-term: its inverse temperature is \(2\beta\), so its
denominator weight is \(q^{-2\beta}\).  The table also fixes the empty-word
and first-generation conventions explicitly.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[New-denominator layer estimate]
\label{lem:knauf-layer-estimate}
For every fixed real \(s<2\),
\begin{equation}
 a_d(s):=
 \sum_{\substack{0<p<q,\ (p,q)=1\\d(p/q)=d}}q^{-s}
 \asymp_s \exp\{d\mathfrak P(s/2)\}.
 \label{eq:knauf-layer-pressure}
\end{equation}
For every fixed \(s\ge2\), one has instead
\[
 \lim_{d\to\infty}\frac1d\log a_d(s)=0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First suppose \(0<s<2\), and put \(\beta=s/2\).  The even Knauf
partition function \(Z^{K,e}_d(2\beta)\) is exactly \(a_d(s)\): its
states are the new Stern--Brocot denominators at depth \(d\).  In the
notation of Fiala, Kleban, and \"Ozl\"uk, the first-coordinate identity
for their Knauf vector, its Perron--Frobenius iteration, and its spectral
asymptotic are given in
\cite[Sec.~IV, especially Eqs.~(23), (26), and (28)]{FialaKlebanOzluk2003}.
They give
\[
 a_d(s)=c_s\lambda(s/2)^d(1+o(1)),\qquad c_s>0.
\]
Their free-energy identification, together with the denominator formula
for Stern--Brocot pressure
\cite[Thm.~1.1 and the denominator formula in Sec.~2]{KessebohmerStratmann2007},
gives \(\log\lambda(s/2)=\mathfrak P(s/2)\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:knauf-layer-pressure} in the range \(0<s<2\).  At \(s=0\),
there are exactly \(2^{d-1}\) new denominators and
\(\mathfrak P(0)=\log2\), so the assertion is immediate.

It remains to supply the estimate for \(s<0\), a range not covered by
the positive-temperature Perron--Frobenius statement just cited.  Put
\(t=-s>0\), and let
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 R=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \lVert M\rVert_\Sigma=\sum_{i,j=1}^2M_{ij}.
\]
 By \Cref{lem:continued-fraction-knauf-normalization}, the new denominators at
 depth \(d\) are in bijection with words \(w\in\{L,R\}^{d-1}\), and
\begin{equation}
 q(w)=\lVert M_w\rVert_\Sigma.
 \label{eq:stern-brocot-matrix-denominator}
\end{equation}
Define
\[
 Z_n(t):=\sum_{w\in\{L,R\}^n}\lVert M_w\rVert_\Sigma^t,
 \qquad n\ge0.
\]
For nonnegative matrices \(A,B\),
\(\lVert AB\rVert_\Sigma\le
\lVert A\rVert_\Sigma\lVert B\rVert_\Sigma\), and hence
\begin{equation}
 Z_{m+n}(t)\le Z_m(t)Z_n(t).
 \label{eq:matrix-partition-submultiplicative}
\end{equation}
For the reverse inequality with one bridging letter, write
\(\alpha=A^T(1,1)^T\) and \(\beta=B(1,1)^T\).  Direct multiplication gives
\[
 \lVert ALB\rVert_\Sigma+\lVert ARB\rVert_\Sigma
 =\lVert A\rVert_\Sigma\lVert B\rVert_\Sigma
   +\alpha_1\beta_1+\alpha_2\beta_2.
\]
Thus at least one \(C\in\{L,R\}\) satisfies
\(\lVert ACB\rVert_\Sigma\ge
\frac12\lVert A\rVert_\Sigma\lVert B\rVert_\Sigma\).  Summing over
all pairs of words yields
\begin{equation}
 Z_{m+n+1}(t)\ge2^{-t}Z_m(t)Z_n(t).
 \label{eq:matrix-partition-bridged}
\end{equation}

Let \(\lambda_t=\lim_n Z_n(t)^{1/n}\), whose existence follows from
\eqref{eq:matrix-partition-submultiplicative}.  That inequality gives
\(Z_n(t)\ge\lambda_t^n\).  Iterating
\eqref{eq:matrix-partition-bridged} \(k-1\) times and then letting
\(k\to\infty\) gives
\[
 Z_{k(n+1)-1}(t)\ge2^{-t(k-1)}Z_n(t)^k,
 \qquad
 Z_n(t)\le2^t\lambda_t^{n+1}.
\]
Consequently \(Z_n(t)\asymp_t\lambda_t^n\).  By
\eqref{eq:stern-brocot-matrix-denominator} and the denominator form of
Stern--Brocot pressure cited above,
\(\log\lambda_t=\mathfrak P(-t/2)\).  Since
\(a_d(-t)=Z_{d-1}(t)\), this proves
\eqref{eq:knauf-layer-pressure} for \(s<0\).

Finally, the logarithmic assertion for \(s\ge2\) is precisely the
denominator formulation of the zero-pressure part of
\cite[Thm.~1.1]{KessebohmerStratmann2007}.
\end{proof}

The following pressure statement is retained because its renewal
normalization is used below.  Its existence, implicit branch, frozen phase,
and nondifferentiable critical point recover the golden-ratio
Bernoulli-convolution results of Feng--Olivier and Feng
\cite{FengOlivier2003,Feng2005}; they are not claimed as new.

\begin{proposition}[Recovery of the Bernoulli-convolution pressure]
\label{thm:exact-negative-pressure}
\label{thm:all-real-pressure}
For every \(s\in\mathbb R\), the radius of convergence of \(B_s\) is
\begin{equation}
 R_B(s)=\exp\{-\mathfrak P(s/2)/2\}.
 \label{eq:Bs-radius}
\end{equation}
Thus \(R_B(s)<1\) for \(s<2\), and \(R_B(s)=1\) for \(s\ge2\).
The nonnegative real roots of \(B_s(r)=1\) are classified as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
s<\sigma_0 & \text{a unique root }r_s\in(0,R_B(s)),\\
s=\sigma_0 & \text{the unique root }r_s=1=R_B(s),\\
s>\sigma_0 & \text{no root in }[0,R_B(s)].
\end{array}
\]
The pressure exists for every real tilt and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 P(-s):=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log S_{-s}(m)
 =\begin{cases}
   -\log r_s,&s<\sigma_0,\\
   0,&s\ge\sigma_0.
  \end{cases}
 \label{eq:all-real-pressure}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for \(t>-\sigma_0\), \(P(t)>0\) is the unique solution of
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{q=2}^{\infty}
 \sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
 q^t e^{-P(t)c(p/q)}=1,
 \label{eq:exact-negative-pressure}
\end{equation}
whereas \(P(t)=0\) for \(t\le-\sigma_0\).  In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \{t\in\mathbb R:P(t)>0\}=(-\sigma_0,\infty).
 \label{eq:exact-zero-pressure-set}
\end{equation}

The restriction of \(P\) to \((-\sigma_0,\infty)\) is real analytic and
strictly convex.  At \(t_c=-\sigma_0\), it has the finite one-sided
derivatives
\begin{equation}
 P'_-(t_c)=0,
 \qquad
 P'_+(t_c)=
 \frac{\displaystyle
  \sum_{q\ge2}\sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
       q^{-\sigma_0}\log q}
 {\displaystyle
  \sum_{q\ge2}\sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
       c(p/q)q^{-\sigma_0}}>0.
 \label{eq:critical-pressure-slopes}
\end{equation}
Consequently, \(P\) is not differentiable at the freezing point.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(a_d(s):=b_{2d+1}(s)\).  The negative-continued-fraction
parameterization gives
\[
 a_d(s)=\sum_{\substack{0<p<q,\ (p,q)=1\\d(p/q)=d}}q^{-s}.
\]
There are \(2^{d-1}\) fractions in this layer, and their denominators satisfy
\(d+1\le q\le F_{d+2}\).  In particular, every \(B_s\) has positive finite
radius.  The layer is precisely the new-denominator layer of the Knauf model.
 The precise estimate in \Cref{lem:knauf-layer-estimate} gives, for fixed
\(s<2\), \eqref{eq:knauf-layer-pressure}, and gives
\(d^{-1}\log a_d(s)\to0\) for \(s\ge2\).  Cauchy--Hadamard now proves
\eqref{eq:Bs-radius}: explicitly,
\(B_s(z)=\sum_{d\ge1}a_d(s)z^{2d+1}\), so the cost \(2d+1\) contributes the
factor \(1/2\) in the radius exponent.  Moreover,
\eqref{eq:knauf-layer-pressure} shows that
the terms of \(B_s\) do not tend to zero on \(|z|=R_B(s)\) when \(s<2\).
At \(s=2\), the positive real boundary also diverges.  For \(s>2\), Tonelli's
theorem gives
\begin{equation}
 B_s(1)=\sum_{q\ge2}\frac{\phi_{\rm E}(q)}{q^s}
 =\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}-1<\infty.
 \label{eq:weighted-renewal-at-one}
\end{equation}

The coefficients are nonnegative and the support contains both \(3\) and
\(5\), so \(B_s\) is strictly increasing on its positive interval of
convergence.  If \(s\le2\), its positive boundary value is infinite; if
\(2<s<\sigma_0\), \eqref{eq:weighted-renewal-at-one} is greater than one.
These facts give the unique interior root for \(s<\sigma_0\).  At
\(s=\sigma_0\), the boundary value is one, while it is less than one for
\(s>\sigma_0\).  This proves the asserted real-root classification.  The
triangle inequality also shows that, for \(s<\sigma_0\), the only zero of
\(1-B_s(z)\) in \(|z|\le r_s\) is the simple zero \(r_s\): equality forces
the degree-three and degree-five terms to have the same positive phase, and
\(\gcd(3,5)=1\).

Concatenation of generating words gives
\(u_{m+n}(s)\ge u_m(s)u_n(s)\).  The letters of costs \(3\) and \(5\) imply
\(u_j(s)>0\) for every \(j\ge8\).  The shifted Fekete argument used in the
proof of the strip result therefore applies without any restriction on
\(s\), and yields
\[
 \gamma_s:=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac1j\log u_j(s).
\]
If \(s<\sigma_0\), the formal identity
\(U_s=(1-B_s)^{-1}\), positivity, and the preceding root classification show
that the radius of \(U_s\) is \(r_s\).  At \(s=\sigma_0\), it is one.  If
\(s>\sigma_0\), then \(U_s(1)=(1-B_s(1))^{-1}<\infty\), while
\(u_j(s)\ge b_j(s)\) prevents the radius of \(U_s\) from exceeding
\(R_B(s)=1\).  Hence
\[
 R_U(s)=\begin{cases}r_s,&s<\sigma_0,\\1,&s\ge\sigma_0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \gamma_s=-\log R_U(s)\ge0.
\]
For \(H_m(s)=\sum_{j=0}^m u_j(s)\), the exact renewal identity gives
\[
 H_m(s)\le S_{-s}(m)\le4H_{m+1}(s).
\]
Since \(m^{-1}\log H_m(s)\to\gamma_s\), this proves
\eqref{eq:all-real-pressure} and the root equation.

For \(t>-\sigma_0\), put
\[
 \mu_t(p/q):=q^t e^{-P(t)c(p/q)}.
\]
The root equation makes this a probability measure.  The root lies strictly
inside the relevant convergence circle, and
\(\log q\ll d(p/q)\), so the defining series and all its parameter
derivatives converge locally uniformly.  The analytic implicit-function
theorem applies.  With \(X=\log q\) and \(C=c(p/q)\), differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 P'(t)=\frac{\mathbb E_{\mu_t}X}{\mathbb E_{\mu_t}C},
 \qquad
 P''(t)=
 \frac{\mathbb E_{\mu_t}[(X-P'(t)C)^2]}
      {\mathbb E_{\mu_t}C}.
 \label{eq:pressure-derivatives}
\end{equation}
The fractions \(1/2\) and \(1/3\) have \((C,q)=(3,2)\) and \((5,3)\),
and \(\log2/3\ne\log3/5\).  Thus the variance in
\eqref{eq:pressure-derivatives} is strictly positive.

It remains to justify passage to the critical endpoint.  For the canonical
regular continued fraction \(p/q=[0;a_1,\ldots,a_r]\), with \(a_r>1\),
the conversion between regular and negative continued fractions gives
\[
 d(p/q)=a_1+\cdots+a_r-1,
 \qquad c(p/q)=2(a_1+\cdots+a_r)-1.
\]
The fixed-denominator mean theorem of Panov and Liehl
\cite{Panov1982,Liehl1983,AistleitnerBordaHauke2023} states that
\[
 \frac1{\phi_{\rm E}(q)}
 \sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}(a_1+\cdots+a_r)
 \sim\frac6{\pi^2}(\log q)^2.
\]
Consequently, both the numerator and denominator in
\eqref{eq:critical-pressure-slopes} converge absolutely, since they are
bounded by constant multiples of
\(\sum q^{1-\sigma_0}\log q\) and
\(\sum q^{1-\sigma_0}(\log q)^2\), respectively.  Dominated convergence in
the first identity of \eqref{eq:pressure-derivatives} gives the stated finite
positive right derivative.  The pressure is identically zero to the left of
\(t_c\), so its left derivative is zero.
\end{proof}

\section{Thermodynamics of the Fibonacci partition function}
\label{sec:standard-thermodynamics}

The renewal identity was derived from Weinstein's standard Fibonacci
partition function before it was transferred to the finite-window fold.  We
now state its one-layer consequences intrinsically.  This removes the bespoke
fold from the probabilistic conclusions.

For \(m\ge1\), recall
\[
 \mathcal I_m=[F_{m+1}-1,F_{m+2}-1)
\]
and define
\[
 Z_m^R(t):=\sum_{n\in\mathcal I_m}R(n)^t
 \qquad(t\in\RR).
\]
If \(\mathbf N_m\) is uniform on \(\mathcal I_m\), put
\[
 Y_m^R:=\frac1m\log R(\mathbf N_m).
\]

\begin{theorem}[One-layer Fibonacci partition thermodynamics]
\label{thm:standard-fibonacci-thermodynamics}
For every \(t\in\RR\),
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log Z_m^R(t)=P(t),
 \label{eq:standard-layer-pressure}
\end{equation}
where \(P\) is the pressure in \Cref{thm:all-real-pressure}.  Thus the
one-layer pressure is zero on \((-\infty,-\sigma_0]\), is positive, real
analytic, and strictly convex on \((-\sigma_0,\infty)\), and has the
one-sided derivatives in \eqref{eq:critical-pressure-slopes}.

For \(s>\sigma_0\), the unnormalized sums satisfy
\begin{equation}
 Z_m^R(-s)\longrightarrow
 \mathcal Z_R(s):=
 \frac2{2-\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)}-1.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-frozen-limit}
\end{equation}
At the critical tilt,
\begin{equation}
 Z_m^R(-\sigma_0)\sim\frac{2m}{\mu_C}.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-critical-size}
\end{equation}

For \(n\in\mathcal I_m\) with \(R(n)>2\), let \(g(n)\) be the generating
number of its Weinstein orbit and put \(\ell_m^R(n)=L(g(n))\); set
\(\ell_m^R(n)=0\) when \(R(n)\le2\).  Let \(\widehat{\mathbf N}_m\) have the
critical Gibbs law
\[
 \Pr\{\widehat{\mathbf N}_m=n\}
 =\frac{R(n)^{-\sigma_0}}{Z_m^R(-\sigma_0)}
 \qquad(n\in\mathcal I_m).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \left(
  \frac1m\log R(\widehat{\mathbf N}_m),
  \frac1m\ell_m^R(\widehat{\mathbf N}_m)
 \right)
 \xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{\mathrm d}(v_cU,U),
 \label{eq:standard-layer-critical-gibbs}
\end{equation}
where \(U\) is uniform on \([0,1]\).

Finally, \((Y_m^R)\) satisfies a full large deviation principle with speed
\(m\) and the good rate function
\begin{equation}
 I(\alpha)=\sup_{t\in\RR}
 \{t\alpha-P(t)+\log\varphi\}.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-ldp-rate}
\end{equation}
Its effective domain is \([0,\alpha_+]\), where
\(\alpha_+=\tfrac12\log\varphi\), and
\[
 I(\alpha)=\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha
 \qquad(0\le\alpha\le v_c).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(H_m(s)=\sum_{j=0}^m u_j(s)\).  The exact one-layer renewal identity
\eqref{eq:weighted-layer-renewal} says
\begin{equation}
 Z_m^R(-s)=2H_{m-1}(s)+u_m(s)-1.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-renewal}
\end{equation}
The proof of \Cref{thm:all-real-pressure} established
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log u_m(s)
 =\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log H_m(s)
 =P(-s).
\]
Since \(u_m(s)\le Z_m^R(-s)\le3H_m(s)\), this proves
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-pressure}.

If \(s>\sigma_0\), then \(U_s(1)=\sum_{j\ge0}u_j(s)<\infty\), so
\(u_m(s)\to0\).  Equations
\eqref{eq:weighted-renewal-generating-function} and
\eqref{eq:weighted-renewal-at-one} give
\[
 U_s(1)=\frac1{1-B_s(1)}
 =\frac1{2-\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)}.
\]
Letting \(m\to\infty\) in \eqref{eq:standard-layer-renewal} proves
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-frozen-limit}.  At \(s=\sigma_0\), the arithmetic
renewal theorem used in \Cref{thm:critical-finite-window-gibbs} gives
\(u_m(\sigma_0)\to1/\mu_C\).  Ces\`aro summation in
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-renewal} proves
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-critical-size}.

We next retain the generating cost.  The four orbit branches displayed above
\eqref{eq:marked-microcanonical-count} show that a generator of cost \(c\)
contributes two points to \(\mathcal I_m\) if \(c<m\), one if \(c=m\), and
none if \(c>m\).  Thus the one-layer boundary weight is
\[
 K_m^R(c)=
 \begin{cases}
  2,&c<m,\\
  1,&c=m,\\
  0,&c>m.
 \end{cases}
\]
With the marked renewal measure \(\mathcal U\) defined before
\eqref{eq:marked-microcanonical-count}, the same free-monoid bijection gives,
for every Borel set
\(B\subset\mathbb R\times(\log2,\infty)\),
\begin{equation}
 \card{\{n\in\mathcal I_m:
   (\ell_m^R(n),\log R(n))\in B\}}
 =\int_B K_m^R(c)e^{\sigma_0h}\,\mathcal U(dc,dh).
 \label{eq:standard-layer-marked-count}
\end{equation}
The points with \(R(n)\le2\) have bounded total critical weight, uniformly in
\(m\), by the uniform layer-count lemma.

For every bounded continuous \(f\) on \([0,1]\), the arithmetic renewal
theorem and a Riemann-sum argument give
\[
 \frac1m\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}u_j(\sigma_0)f(j/m)
 \longrightarrow\frac1{\mu_C}\int_0^1f(y)\,dy.
\]
After multiplication of \eqref{eq:standard-layer-marked-count} by
\(e^{-\sigma_0h}\), the weights are two for \(j<m\) and one at \(j=m\).
Together with \eqref{eq:standard-layer-critical-size}, this proves
\[
 \frac{\ell_m^R(\widehat{\mathbf N}_m)}m
 \xrightarrow{\mathrm d}U.
\]
For the reward coordinate, the critical renewal increments
\((C_i,H_i)\) satisfy
\[
 \frac{H_1+\cdots+H_r}{C_1+\cdots+C_r}
 \longrightarrow\frac{\mu_H}{\mu_C}=v_c
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Every cost increment is at least three.  Consequently the number of renewal
epochs with total cost at most \(m\) and reward-to-cost ratio outside a fixed
\(\delta\)-neighborhood of \(v_c\) is \(o(m)\), pathwise and, by bounded
convergence after division by \(m\), in expectation.  Since \(K_m^R\le2\),
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-marked-count} and
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-critical-size} imply
\[
 \frac1m\log R(\widehat{\mathbf N}_m)
 -v_c\frac{\ell_m^R(\widehat{\mathbf N}_m)}m
 \xrightarrow{\Pr}0.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:standard-layer-critical-gibbs}.

It remains to prove the full LDP, in particular the lower bound at the
nonexposed slopes.  The layer has
\(\card{\mathcal I_m}=F_m\), and hence
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log\card{\mathcal I_m}=\log\varphi.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:standard-layer-pressure} therefore gives the limiting
logarithmic moment generating function
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m
 \log\mathbb E e^{tmY_m^R}=P(t)-\log\varphi.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-log-mgf}
\]
The translated extremal theorem in the Fibonacci-convention lemma gives
\[
 \max_{n\in\mathcal I_m}\frac1m\log R(n)
 \longrightarrow\alpha_+.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-maximum}
\]
Indeed, the two shifted endpoints have partition value one, so they do not
change the maximum on the intervening Fibonacci block.  Thus the variables
are exponentially tight, and the G\"artner--Ellis upper bound gives the
required upper bound for closed sets.

For the lower bound, first let \(t>t_c=-\sigma_0\), write
\(v=P'(t)>v_c\), and
tilt the uniform law on \(\mathcal I_n\) by \(R(k)^t\).  Analyticity of \(P\)
and the exponential Markov inequality applied to
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-pressure} show that this tilted law assigns
exponentially vanishing mass to
\[
 \left\{k\in\mathcal I_n:
   \left|n^{-1}\log R(k)-v\right|\ge\delta\right\}.
\]
Consequently, if
\[
 A_n^R(v,\delta)
 :=\card{\{k\in\mathcal I_n:
 |n^{-1}\log R(k)-v|<\delta\}},
\]
then
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log A_n^R(v,\delta)
 \ge P(t)-tv-|t|\delta.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-exposed-source}
\end{equation}

For large \(n\), every point counted here has multiplicity greater than two.
Each generating orbit contributes at most two points to \(\mathcal I_n\).
Keeping one generator from each represented orbit therefore produces a set
\(\mathcal G_n^R\) with
\[
 \card{\mathcal G_n^R}\ge\tfrac12A_n^R(v,\delta),
 \qquad L(g)\le n\quad(g\in\mathcal G_n^R),
\]
and \(n^{-1}\log R(g)\in(v-\delta,v+\delta)\).

Fix \(0<\alpha<\alpha_+\).  Choose \(t>t_c\) so that
\(v=P'(t)>\alpha\): any \(t>t_c\) works when \(\alpha\le v_c\), while for
\(\alpha>v_c\) take \(t>t_\alpha\), where
\(P'(t_\alpha)=\alpha\).  Only after this choice set
\(\rho=\alpha/v<1\), and put
\(n_m=\lfloor\rho m\rfloor\).  For \(g\in\mathcal G_{n_m}^R\), define
\[
 z_m(g)=
 \begin{cases}
  [a]g,&m-L(g)=2a,\\
  [a]\tau g,&m-L(g)=2a+1.
 \end{cases}
\]
The orbit formulas give \(L(z_m(g))=m\) and
\(R(z_m(g))=R(g)\), so \(z_m(g)\in\mathcal I_m\).  Freeness makes this map
injective.  From \eqref{eq:standard-layer-exposed-source}, first letting
\(\delta\downarrow0\), we obtain the local counting lower bound
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac1m
 \log A_m^R(\alpha,\varepsilon)
 \ge\frac{\alpha}{v}\bigl(P(t)-tv\bigr).
 \label{eq:standard-layer-orbit-filling}
\end{equation}

If \(0<\alpha\le v_c\), let \(t\downarrow t_c\) through the choices above in
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-orbit-filling}; then \(v\downarrow v_c\) and the
right side tends to \(\sigma_0\alpha\).  If
\(v_c<\alpha<\alpha_+\), use the \(t_\alpha\) just defined and let
\(t\downarrow t_\alpha\) through values with
\(P'(t)>\alpha\).  The right side then tends to
\(P(t_\alpha)-t_\alpha\alpha\).  In both cases this is
\(\log\varphi-I(\alpha)\).  Division by
\(\card{\mathcal I_m}=F_m\) proves the LDP lower bound at every interior
point of the effective domain.

The effective domain and the affine formula follow from the same convex-dual
calculation as in \Cref{thm:full-fiber-ldp}, since the pressure is the same.
At \(0\), the left endpoint \(F_{m+1}-1\) has \(R=1\) and supplies the lower
bound directly.  For the other boundary passage, let \(G\) be open and contain
\(a\in[0,\alpha_+]\).  Choose interior points \(a_j\to a\) (at
\(a=\alpha_+\), take \(a_j\uparrow\alpha_+\)).  Relative continuity of the
explicit finite rate gives \(I(a_j)\to I(a)\), while openness gives intervals
about \(a_j\) contained in \(G\) for large \(j\).  The interior local bound,
followed by \(j\to\infty\), gives the lower bound at \(a\).  Taking the
infimum over \(G\cap[0,\alpha_+]\) completes the full LDP.
\end{proof}

 B_m^R:=\frac1m\ell_m^R(\mathbf N_m).

\begin{theorem}[Joint generation-cost and multiplicity large deviations]
\label{thm:joint-cost-reward-ldp}
The vectors \((B_m^R,Y_m^R)\) satisfy a full large deviation principle on
\(\mathbb R^2\) with speed \(m\).  Their limiting logarithmic moment generating
function is
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda(a,t)=\max\{0,a+P(t)\}-\log\varphi
 \qquad(a,t\in\mathbb R).
 \label{eq:joint-cost-reward-log-mgf}
\end{equation}
The good rate function is
\begin{equation}
 J(\beta,\alpha)=
 \begin{cases}
  (1-\beta)\log\varphi+\beta I(\alpha/\beta),
   &0<\beta\le1,\quad 0\le\alpha\le\beta\alpha_+,\\
  \log\varphi,&(\beta,\alpha)=(0,0),\\
  +\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:joint-cost-reward-rate}
\end{equation}
In particular, the rate surface has the affine face
\begin{equation}
 J(\beta,\alpha)=\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha
 \qquad(0\le\beta\le1,\ 0\le\alpha\le\beta v_c).
 \label{eq:joint-cost-reward-flat-face}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal W_j\) be the set of nonempty free generator words of total
cost \(j\), and write \(Q(w)\) for the product of the denominators of the
letters of \(w\).  By \eqref{eq:generator-weight-length},
\begin{equation}
 u_j(-t)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal W_j}Q(w)^t.
 \label{eq:fixed-cost-word-partition}
\end{equation}
The convention \(\ell_m^R=0\) at multiplicities at most two changes only a
bounded number of states.  The one-layer orbit weights in
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-marked-count} therefore give, up to a term bounded
for each fixed \((a,t)\),
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{n\in\mathcal I_m}e^{a\ell_m^R(n)}R(n)^t
 =2\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}e^{aj}u_j(-t)+e^{am}u_m(-t)+O_{a,t}(1).
 \label{eq:joint-cost-reward-renewal}
\end{equation}
More precisely, the bounded correction removes the level-two generator orbit
from its positive cost and records it at cost zero; the level-one endpoint is
already a cost-zero term.

The proof of \Cref{thm:all-real-pressure} established
\(\lim_{j\to\infty}j^{-1}\log u_j(-t)=P(t)\), and \(u_j(-t)>0\) for every
\(j\ge8\).  Applying these facts to the partial sum in
\eqref{eq:joint-cost-reward-renewal}, while retaining the level-one endpoint,
gives
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log
 \sum_{n\in\mathcal I_m}e^{a\ell_m^R(n)}R(n)^t
 =\max\{0,a+P(t)\}.
\]
Since \(\card{\mathcal I_m}=F_m\), this proves
\eqref{eq:joint-cost-reward-log-mgf}.

We next compute its convex dual.  For fixed \(t\),
\[
 \sup_{a\in\mathbb R}\{a\beta-\max(0,a+P(t))\}
 =\begin{cases}
   -\beta P(t),&0\le\beta\le1,\\
   +\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\]
For \(0<\beta\le1\), subsequent optimization over \(t\) gives
\[
 \log\varphi+\beta\sup_t\{t\alpha/\beta-P(t)\}
 =(1-\beta)\log\varphi+\beta I(\alpha/\beta).
\]
At \(\beta=0\), the dual is finite only at \(\alpha=0\), where it equals
\(\log\varphi\).  The effective domain of \(I\) from
\Cref{thm:standard-fibonacci-thermodynamics} now gives exactly
\eqref{eq:joint-cost-reward-rate}.  The variables are supported in a common
compact set up to a vanishing enlargement: \(0\le B_m^R\le1\), and
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-maximum} bounds \(Y_m^R\).  Thus the
G\"artner--Ellis upper bound applies to every closed set.

It remains to prove the lower bound, because the interior points with
\(0<\beta<1\) are not exposed points of \(\Lambda\).  For any \(t>t_c\), put
\(v=P'(t)>v_c\), and on \(\mathcal W_j\) use the probability proportional to
\(Q(w)^t\).  For sufficiently small real \(s\),
\eqref{eq:fixed-cost-word-partition} and the fixed-cost pressure limit give
\[
 \limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac1j\log
 \Pr_t\left\{\left|j^{-1}\log Q(w)-v\right|\ge\delta\right\}<0.
\]
This is the same two-sided exponential Markov argument as in
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-exposed-source}.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac1j\log
 \card{\{w\in\mathcal W_j:\ |j^{-1}\log Q(w)-v|<\delta\}}
 \ge P(t)-tv-|t|\delta.
 \label{eq:fixed-cost-exposed-source}
\end{equation}

Let \(0<\beta\le1\) and suppose first that \(\alpha/\beta>v_c\).  Choose the
unique \(t>t_c\) with \(v=P'(t)=\alpha/\beta\).  Taking integers \(j_m\) with
\(j_m/m\to\beta\),
each word counted in \eqref{eq:fixed-cost-exposed-source} determines at least
one point of \(\mathcal I_m\), because a generator orbit of cost \(j_m\le m\)
meets that layer.  Freeness preserves injectivity.  Hence the local counting
exponent is at least
\(\beta(P(t)-t\alpha/\beta)\), which, after division by \(F_m\), is
\(-J(\beta,\alpha)\).  The same conclusion at \(v_c\) follows by approximation
from \(t\downarrow t_c\).

Now fix \(0<\alpha\le\beta v_c\), choose \(t>t_c\), and put
\(v=P'(t)>v_c\ge\alpha/\beta\); thus \(\alpha/v<\beta\).  Set
\[
 k_m=\left\lfloor\frac{\alpha m}{v}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad j_m=\lfloor\beta m\rfloor,
 \qquad r_m=j_m-k_m.
\]
Because \(\alpha/v<\beta\), the integer \(r_m\) tends linearly to infinity;
the two floor errors are bounded.  Every integer \(r\ge6\) is the exact cost
of a suffix of at most two letters.  For odd \(r\), take the letter \(1/q\)
with \(q=(r+1)/2\), whose cost is \(2q-1=r\).  For even \(r\), take the
ordered pair \((1/2,1/q)\) with \(q=(r-2)/2\); its cost is
\(3+(2q-1)=r\).  This includes \(r=6\), where both letters are \(1/2\), and
\(r=7\), where the one-letter construction has \(q=4\).  Repeated letters
are allowed in Weinstein's free monoid.  The suffix multiplier is at most
\(2q\), so its logarithmic reward is \(O(\log r)=o(m)\).

Append the suffix of cost \(r_m\) to every source word of cost \(k_m\) counted
in \eqref{eq:fixed-cost-exposed-source}.  Right cancellation in the free
monoid makes this map injective, and the total cost is exactly \(j_m\).  The
source reward differs from \(vk_m\) by at most \(\delta k_m\), the floor
error in \(vk_m/m\) is at most \(v/m\), and the suffix contributes \(o(1)\)
after division by \(m\).  Thus the images enter every prescribed
neighborhood of \((\beta,\alpha)\) after first choosing \(\delta\) and then
letting \(m\to\infty\).  Their local counting exponent is at least
\[
 \frac{\alpha}{v}\{P(t)-tv\}.
\]
Letting \(t\downarrow t_c=-\sigma_0\), and using \(P(t)\to0\) and
\(v\downarrow v_c\), gives the exponent \(\sigma_0\alpha\).  For
\(\alpha=0\), choose the suffix of exact cost
\(j_m=\lfloor\beta m\rfloor\) by the same construction; it has reward
\(O(\log m)=o(m)\) and gives exponent zero.  After normalization by \(F_m\),
these bounds equal the negative of
\eqref{eq:joint-cost-reward-flat-face}.  The point \((0,0)\) is supplied by
the level-one endpoint.

To formalize all remaining boundary cases, let
\[
 \mathcal D=\{(\beta,\alpha):0\le\beta\le1,
                 0\le\alpha\le\beta\alpha_+\}
\]
with its relative topology.  The explicit formula
\eqref{eq:joint-cost-reward-rate} is continuous on \(\mathcal D\).  If an
open set \(G\subset\mathbb R^2\) meets \(\mathcal D\) at a boundary point
other than \((0,0)\), choose relative-interior points
\((\beta_j,\alpha_j)\in G\cap\mathcal D\) converging to it; for the upper
edge take \(\alpha_j<\beta_j\alpha_+\), and for \(\beta=1\) take
\(\beta_j<1\).  The local bounds already proved and continuity give the
boundary lower bound after \(j\to\infty\).  Taking the infimum over
\(G\cap\mathcal D\), together with the direct point \((0,0)\), proves the
lower bound for every open set and completes the full LDP.
\end{proof}
