# Code Review Reception

## Overview

Code review requires technical evaluation, not emotional performance.

**Core principle:** Verify before implementing. Ask before assuming. Technical correctness over social comfort.

## The Response Pattern

```
WHEN receiving code review feedback:

1. READ: Complete feedback without reacting
2. UNDERSTAND: Restate requirement in own words (or ask)
3. VERIFY: Check against codebase reality
4. EVALUATE: Technically sound for THIS codebase?
5. RESPOND: Technical acknowledgment or reasoned pushback
6. IMPLEMENT: One item at a time, test each
```

## Forbidden Responses

**NEVER:**
- "You're absolutely right!" (explicit CLAUDE.md violation)
- "Great point!" / "Excellent feedback!" (performative)
- "Let me implement that now" (before verification)

**INSTEAD:**
- Restate the technical requirement
- Ask clarifying questions
- Push back with technical reasoning if wrong
- Just start working (actions > words)

## Handling Unclear Feedback

```
IF any item is unclear:
  STOP - do not implement anything yet
  ASK for clarification on unclear items

WHY: Items may be related. Partial understanding = wrong implementation.
```

**Example:**
```
your human partner: "Fix 1-6"
You understand 1,2,3,6. Unclear on 4,5.

âŒ WRONG: Implement 1,2,3,6 now, ask about 4,5 later
âœ… RIGHT: "I understand items 1,2,3,6. Need clarification on 4 and 5 before proceeding."
```

### From External Reviewers

```
NO COMPLETION CLAIMS WITHOUT FRESH VERIFICATION EVIDENCE

```

\subsection{Squarefree certificates and prime-primary linear rigidity}
\label{sec:linear-collision-rigidity}

The total degree of a critical Mahler certificate can have logarithmic
growth because multiplicities accumulate along power orbits.  The number of
distinct zeros and poles has a sharper bound.  For a reduced normalized
rational function
\[
 R=\frac AB,
 \qquad (A,B)=1,
 \qquad A(0)=B(0)=1,
\]
put
\[
 \rho(R):=\deg\rad(AB).
\]
Thus \(\rho(R)\) is the number of distinct non-zero zeros and poles over
\(\overline{\QQ}\), without multiplicity.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp squarefree Mahler bound]
\label{thm:squarefree-mahler-bound}
Let \(p\ge2\), and suppose that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:squarefree-mahler-coboundary}
 H(z)=\frac{P_0(z)}{P_1(z)}
 =\frac{R(z^p)}{R(z)^p},
\end{equation}
where \(P_0,P_1\in\QQ[z]\) are coprime,
\(P_0(0)=P_1(0)=1\), and \(R\in\QQ(z)^\times\) satisfies \(R(0)=1\).
If
\[
 D=\deg P_0+\deg P_1,
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-squarefree-mahler-bound}
 2(p-1)\rho(R)\le D.
\end{equation}
The constant \(2(p-1)\) is sharp for every \(p\ge2\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(\alpha\in\overline{\QQ}^{\times}\), write
\[
 e(\alpha)=\operatorname{ord}_{z=\alpha}H,
 \qquad
 r(\alpha)=\operatorname{ord}_{z=\alpha}R.
\]
Let \(S=\operatorname{supp}r\) and \(s=\abs S=\rho(R)\).  Since the input
in \eqref{eq:squarefree-mahler-coboundary} is reduced and has constant term
one,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:squarefree-input-l1-degree}
 D=\sum_{\alpha\in\overline{\QQ}^{\times}}\abs{e(\alpha)}.
\end{equation}
Taking divisors gives the identity already used in
\eqref{eq:p-mahler-divisor-recurrence}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:squarefree-divisor-recurrence}
 e(\alpha)=r(\alpha^p)-p r(\alpha).
\end{equation}

For \(\beta\in S\), put
\[
 d_\beta=\#\{\alpha\in S:\alpha^p=\beta\}.
\]
Define a finitely supported function
\(\varphi:\overline{\QQ}^{\times}\to\{-1,0,1\}\) by
\[
 \varphi(\alpha)=
 \begin{cases}
  -\operatorname{sgn}r(\alpha),&\alpha\in S,\\
  \operatorname{sgn}r(\alpha^p),&\alpha\notin S,
       \ \alpha^p\in S,\\
  0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Equations \eqref{eq:squarefree-input-l1-degree} and
\eqref{eq:squarefree-divisor-recurrence} imply
\begin{align}
 D
 &\ge \sum_\alpha e(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha)\notag\\
 &=\sum_{\beta\in S}r(\beta)
   \left(\sum_{\alpha^p=\beta}\varphi(\alpha)-p\varphi(\beta)\right).
 \label{eq:squarefree-dual-pairing}
\end{align}
Fix \(\beta\in S\).  Exactly \(p-d_\beta\) of its \(p\) distinct
\(p\)-th roots lie outside \(S\), and each contributes
\(\abs{r(\beta)}\) to the grouped summand in
\eqref{eq:squarefree-dual-pairing}.  Each of the \(d_\beta\) internal roots
contributes at least \(-\abs{r(\beta)}\), while
\[
 -p r(\beta)\varphi(\beta)=p\abs{r(\beta)}.
\]
The total contribution belonging to \(\beta\) is therefore at least
\(2(p-d_\beta)\abs{r(\beta)}\), and hence at least
\(2(p-d_\beta)\).  Consequently
\[
 D\ge2\sum_{\beta\in S}(p-d_\beta)
 =2\left(ps-\sum_{\beta\in S}d_\beta\right).
\]
Now
\[
 \sum_{\beta\in S}d_\beta
 =\#\{\alpha\in S:\alpha^p\in S\}\le s,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:sharp-squarefree-mahler-bound}.

For sharpness, take \(R(z)=1-z^q\), where \(q\ge1\).  Then
\(\rho(R)=q\), and
\[
 \frac{R(z^p)}{R(z)^p}
 =\frac{1+z^q+\cdots+z^{(p-1)q}}{(1-z^q)^{p-1}}
\]
is reduced.  Its numerator and denominator both have degree
\((p-1)q\), so equality holds in
\eqref{eq:sharp-squarefree-mahler-bound}.
\end{proof}

The squarefree estimate controls collisions and a Taylor jet at the same
time.

\begin{theorem}[Collision--jet inequality]
\label{thm:collision-jet-inequality}
Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:squarefree-mahler-bound}, suppose in
addition that, for some \(x>0\),
\[
 R(t)>0\qquad(0\le t\le x),
\]
and that \(R\ne1\).  Put
\[
 \nu=\operatorname{ord}_{z=0}(R(z)-1),
 \qquad
 c_x(R)=\#\{y\in(0,x):R(y)=1\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collision-jet-bound}
 c_x(R)+\nu\le\rho(R)
 \le\left\lfloor\frac{D}{2(p-1)}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Moreover
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{z=0}(H(z)-1)=\nu.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The real function \(f(t)=\log R(t)\) has a zero of order \(\nu\) at zero.
If
\[
 0<y_1<\cdots<y_k<x
\]
are all its other zeros, then \(k=c_x(R)\).  The derivative
\(f'=R'/R\) has a zero of order at least \(\nu-1\) at zero, and Rolle's
theorem supplies a further zero in each of
\[
 (0,y_1),(y_1,y_2),\ldots,(y_{k-1},y_k).
\]
On the other hand, logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{R'(z)}{R(z)}
 =\sum_{\alpha\in S}\frac{r(\alpha)}{z-\alpha}
 =\frac{N(z)}{\prod_{\alpha\in S}(z-\alpha)},
\]
where \(N\ne0\) and \(\deg N\le\abs S-1=\rho(R)-1\).  Counting the
zeros of \(N\), with multiplicity, yields
\[
 (\nu-1)+k\le\rho(R)-1.
\]
This proves the first inequality in \eqref{eq:collision-jet-bound}; the
second is \Cref{thm:squarefree-mahler-bound}.

Finally, if \(R(z)=1+az^\nu+O(z^{\nu+1})\), with \(a\ne0\), then
\[
 \frac{R(z^p)}{R(z)^p}=1-pa z^\nu+O(z^{\nu+1}),
\]
because \(p\nu>\nu\).  This proves the order identity.
\end{proof}

We now impose only a relative symmetry on a pair of extensions.  Let
\(\ell\) be prime, let \(G\) be a finite abelian \(\ell\)-group, and set
\[
 P_{\chi,\tau}(z)=\det(I-zA_{\chi,\tau}),
 \qquad
 H_\chi(z)=\frac{P_{\chi,\tau}(z)}{P_{\chi,\tau'}(z)}.
\]
The pair \((\tau,\tau')\) is \emph{relatively unit-Adams invariant} if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-unit-adams}
 H_{\chi^u}(z)=H_\chi(z)
 \qquad
 (\chi\in\widehat G,\ (u,\ell)=1).
\end{equation}
This is weaker than requiring the two determinant families to be separately
unit-Adams invariant.

\begin{lemma}[Prime-primary Adams--M\"obius collapse]
\label{lem:prime-primary-adams-collapse}
Assume \eqref{eq:relative-unit-adams}, and put
\[
 \Delta_\chi(z)=F_\chi^\tau(z)-F_\chi^{\tau'}(z)
 =-\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)
  \bigl(\mathcal E_g^\tau(z)-\mathcal E_g^{\tau'}(z)\bigr).
\]
Then \(H_\chi\in\QQ(z)\), and on the common open Perron disk
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-primary-adams-collapse}
 \Delta_\chi(z)
 =-\sum_{j\ge0}\ell^{-j}\Log_0H_\chi(z^{\ell^j})
  +\sum_{j\ge1}\ell^{-j}\Log_0H_{\chi^\ell}(z^{\ell^j}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The abelian form of \eqref{eq:fixed-determinant-open-disc} gives
\[
 \Delta_\chi(z)
 =-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1k\sum_{d\mid k}\mu(d)
       \Log_0H_{\chi^d}(z^k).
\]
Write \(k=\ell^j q\), where \((q,\ell)=1\).  The squarefree divisors with
non-zero M\"obius value are \(d\mid q\), and, when \(j\ge1\), also
\(\ell d\).  Relative unit-Adams invariance gives
\[
 H_{\chi^d}=H_\chi,
 \qquad
 H_{\chi^{\ell d}}=H_{\chi^\ell}.
\]
The factor \(\sum_{d\mid q}\mu(d)\) vanishes unless \(q=1\), leaving
exactly \eqref{eq:prime-primary-adams-collapse}.

If \(K=\QQ(\chi(G))\), every element of \(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\QQ)\)
sends \(\chi\) to \(\chi^u\) for some \((u,\ell)=1\), and it sends
\(H_\chi\) to \(H_{\chi^u}\).  Thus \eqref{eq:relative-unit-adams} fixes
\(H_\chi\) under the full Galois group, proving
\(H_\chi\in\QQ(z)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Prime-primary collision--jet rigidity]
\label{thm:prime-primary-collision-jet-rigidity}
\label{thm:odd-adams-abelian-two-group-finite-sampling}
Let \(\ell\) be prime and let \(G\) be a finite abelian \(\ell\)-group.
Let \(\tau,\tau'\) be one-step \(G\)-cocycles over primitive non-negative
integral base matrices \(A,A'\), of sizes \(v,v'\), with Perron roots
\(\lambda,\lambda'>1\).  Put
\[
 x=\min\{\lambda^{-1},(\lambda')^{-1}\},
\]
and assume relative unit-Adams invariance
\eqref{eq:relative-unit-adams}.  Suppose that, for integers \(L\ge0\) and
\(K\ge1\),
\begin{align}
 p_{n,g}(\tau)&=p_{n,g}(\tau')
 &&(1\le n\le L,\ g\in G),
 \label{eq:prime-primary-initial-jet}\\
 \mathcal E_g^\tau(y_i)&=\mathcal E_g^{\tau'}(y_i)
 &&(g\in G,\ 1\le i\le K),
 \label{eq:prime-primary-profile-samples}
\end{align}
at distinct radii \(0<y_i<x\), at least one of which is algebraic.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-primary-cross-base-threshold}
 K+L\ge\max\{v,v'\},
\end{equation}
then
\[
 p_{n,g}(\tau)=p_{n,g}(\tau')
 \qquad(n\ge1,\ g\in G).
\]
If the base determinants are already equal, it is enough to assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-primary-fixed-determinant-threshold}
 K+L\ge
 \left\lfloor\frac{v+v'}{2(\ell-1)}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Summing \eqref{eq:prime-primary-profile-samples} over \(g\) gives
\[
 \log\det(I-y_iA)=\log\det(I-y_iA')
 \qquad(1\le i\le K).
\]
Both determinants are positive on \((0,x)\), so their values agree at the
\(y_i\).  Likewise, summing \eqref{eq:prime-primary-initial-jet} over \(g\)
and using the primitive-orbit product gives
\[
 \det(I-zA)-\det(I-zA')=O(z^{L+1}).
\]
The polynomial on the left has degree at most \(\max\{v,v'\}\).  Under
\eqref{eq:prime-primary-cross-base-threshold}, its zero of multiplicity at
least \(L+1\) at zero and its \(K\) distinct positive zeros force it to
vanish identically.  Thus in either case under consideration the base
determinants agree and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-primary-trivial-ratio}
 H_{\mathbf1}=1.
\end{equation}

Fourier transformation of \eqref{eq:prime-primary-profile-samples} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-primary-fourier-samples}
 \Delta_\chi(y_i)=0
 \qquad(\chi\in\widehat G,\ 1\le i\le K).
\end{equation}
We prove \(H_\chi=1\) by induction on the order of \(\chi\).  The trivial
case is \eqref{eq:prime-primary-trivial-ratio}.  Let \(\chi\) have order
\(\ell^a\) and suppose that all lower-order character ratios are one.  Then
\(H_{\chi^\ell}=1\), and
\Cref{lem:prime-primary-adams-collapse} reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-primary-one-orbit}
 \Delta_\chi(z)
 =-\sum_{j\ge0}\ell^{-j}\Log_0H_\chi(z^{\ell^j}).
\end{equation}

By \Cref{lem:prime-primary-adams-collapse}, \(H_\chi\in\QQ(z)\).  The
Perron-weighted domination in \Cref{lem:perron-weighted-domination} makes its
numerator and denominator zero-free on \(\abs z<x\).  Since
\(H_\chi(0)=1\) and \(H_\chi\) is real, it is positive on \((0,x)\).
Choose an algebraic sampled radius \(y_0\).  If \(H_\chi\ne1\), then
\eqref{eq:prime-primary-fourier-samples} and
\eqref{eq:prime-primary-one-orbit} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-primary-algebraic-product}
 \prod_{j\ge0}H_\chi(y_0^{\ell^j})^{\ell^{-j}}=1.
\end{equation}
Here all the hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:general-rational-p-mahler-lifting} apply to \(H_\chi\): it is
rational over \(\QQ\), one has \(0<y_0<x<1\), a radius
\(r\in(y_0,x)\) gives the required zero- and pole-free disk, and the
coefficient-height and common-denominator estimates are proved inside that
theorem for every rational input.  That theorem and
\eqref{eq:prime-primary-algebraic-product} therefore supply the unique
normalized \(R_\chi\in\QQ(z)^\times\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-primary-rational-certificate}
 H_\chi(z)=\frac{R_\chi(z^\ell)}{R_\chi(z)^\ell},
 \qquad R_\chi(y_0)=1.
\end{equation}
The positive product defining the lift also shows that
\(R_\chi(t)>0\) for \(0\le t<x\).

Substitution of \eqref{eq:prime-primary-rational-certificate} into
\eqref{eq:prime-primary-one-orbit} telescopes to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-primary-rational-telescope}
 \Delta_\chi(z)=\ell\log R_\chi(z).
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:prime-primary-fourier-samples} gives
\(R_\chi(y_i)=1\) for every \(i\).  Moreover,
\eqref{eq:prime-primary-initial-jet} gives
\(\Delta_\chi(z)=O(z^{L+1})\), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-primary-certificate-jet}
 \nu_\chi:=\operatorname{ord}_{z=0}(R_\chi(z)-1)\ge L+1.
\end{equation}

Write \(H_\chi=P_{0,\chi}/P_{1,\chi}\) in reduced form and put
\(D_\chi=\deg P_{0,\chi}+\deg P_{1,\chi}\).  Cancellation from the two
original determinants can only lower degrees, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-primary-input-degree}
 D_\chi\le v+v'.
\end{equation}
Applying \Cref{thm:collision-jet-inequality} to
\eqref{eq:prime-primary-rational-certificate} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-primary-final-count}
 K+L+1\le K+\nu_\chi
 \le\rho(R_\chi)
 \le\left\lfloor\frac{D_\chi}{2(\ell-1)}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
The last member is at most \(\max\{v,v'\}\), contradicting
\eqref{eq:prime-primary-cross-base-threshold}; when the base determinants were
known in advance, \eqref{eq:prime-primary-input-degree} instead makes
\eqref{eq:prime-primary-final-count} contradict
\eqref{eq:prime-primary-fixed-determinant-threshold}.  Therefore
\(H_\chi=1\), closing the induction.

All character determinants now agree.  The comparison dictionary in
\Cref{prop:comparison-dictionary}, followed by Fourier inversion on \(G\),
recovers every primitive length--element count.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Linear radial determination]
\label{cor:prime-primary-linear-radial-determination}
Under the hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:prime-primary-collision-jet-rigidity}, with \(L=0\),
\(\max\{v,v'\}\) full profile vectors, one algebraically anchored, determine
all primitive length--element data.  In particular, \(V\) radial locations
suffice for bases of size at most \(V\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Exact order of binary radial sampling complexity]
\label{cor:exact-binary-radial-sampling-order}
For \(V\ge6\), the universal binary radial determination number from
\Cref{cor:linear-radial-sampling-lower-bound} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-binary-radial-sampling-order}
 \left\lfloor\frac{V-2}{4}\right\rfloor+1
 \le\mathsf N_{C_2}(V)\le V.
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\mathsf N_{C_2}(V)=\Theta(V)\), which closes the sampling
complexity order exactly.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Odd-prime multi-collisions with delayed discrepancy}
\label{sec:odd-prime-delayed-multicollisions}

The prime-primary theorem has realizable odd-prime examples with linearly
many collisions and a delayed first discrepancy.

\begin{theorem}[Odd-prime multi-collisions with delayed first discrepancy]
\label{thm:odd-prime-delayed-multicollisions}
Let \(\ell\ge3\) be prime and \(m\ge1\).  There are two one-step
\(C_\ell\)-cocycles \(\tau_m,\tau_m'\) over the same positive primitive base
with
\[
 V_m=\ell(2m+1)
\]
vertices such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
 \item every non-trivial character determinant is invariant under all unit
 Adams operations;
 \item both cocycles have strict twisted spectral gap;
 \item their complete element-profile vectors agree at \(m\) distinct
 rational radii in the open Perron interval;
 \item their primitive length--element data agree for every \(n\le m\);
 \item their first disagreement is at length \(m+1\), with the explicit
 vector in \eqref{eq:odd-prime-first-discrepancy} below.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-construction-parameters}
 a=\ell^\ell,
 \qquad K=4\ell^2,
 \qquad s_i=a\bigl(K(m+1)+i\bigr)\quad(1\le i\le m),
\end{equation}
and let \(q=2m+1\).  Index a \(q\)-square matrix \(C_m\) by
\[
 u_0,\ldots,u_m,w_1,\ldots,w_m.
\]
Its only non-zero entries are
\begin{align*}
 (C_m)_{u_{i-1},u_i}&=-a,&
 (C_m)_{u_{i-1},w_i}&=s_i,&
 (C_m)_{w_i,u_i}&=s_i &&(1\le i\le m),\\
 (C_m)_{u_m,u_0}&=-a.
\end{align*}
As in the staged-cycle calculation in
\Cref{thm:realizable-linear-multicollisions}, every non-trivial cycle uses
the closing edge and chooses at each stage either the direct edge or the
two-edge detour.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-staged-determinant}
 Q_m(z):=\det(I-zC_m)
 =1+az\prod_{i=1}^m(-az+s_i^2z^2).
\end{equation}
All entries of \(C_m\) are divisible by \(\ell^\ell\).

Define two \(\ell q\)-square matrices.  The first is cyclic in
\(q\)-square blocks, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-cyclic-blocks}
 (B_m)_{0,1}=\frac{C_m}{\ell^{\ell-1}},
 \qquad
 (B_m)_{j,j+1}=\ell I_q\quad(1\le j\le\ell-2),
 \qquad
 (B_m)_{\ell-1,0}=\ell I_q,
\end{equation}
and all other blocks zero.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-direct-sum-block}
 B_m'=C_m^{\oplus\ell}.
\end{equation}
The blocks in \eqref{eq:odd-prime-cyclic-blocks} are integral, and their
product around the block cycle is \(C_m\).  The block-companion determinant
identity and the direct-sum identity give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-block-determinants}
 \det(I-zB_m)=Q_m(z^\ell),
 \qquad
 \det(I-zB_m')=Q_m(z)^\ell.
\end{equation}
Every entry of both matrices is divisible by \(\ell\).

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-common-base}
 S=\ell s_m,
 \qquad
 A_m=S J_{V_m},
\end{equation}
where \(J_{V_m}\) is the all-ones matrix.  For
\(M\in\{B_m,B_m'\}\), define the edge-count matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-label-counts}
 N_0(M)=\frac{A_m+(\ell-1)M}{\ell},
 \qquad
 N_g(M)=\frac{A_m-M}{\ell}\quad(g\in C_\ell\setminus\{0\}).
\end{equation}
They are integral.  The choice of \(S\) gives
\[
 S>\max_{i,j}\abs{M_{ij}},
 \qquad
 S>(\ell-1)\max_{i,j}(-M_{ij}),
\]
so they are non-negative.  Moreover
\[
 N_0(M)+\sum_{g\ne0}N_g(M)=A_m.
\]
For any non-trivial character \(\chi\) of \(C_\ell\),
\(\sum_{g\ne0}\chi(g)=-1\), and therefore its twisted block is
\[
 N_0(M)+\sum_{g\ne0}\chi(g)N_g(M)=N_0(M)-N_g(M)=M.
\]
Thus every non-trivial character block is \(B_m\) for \(\tau_m\) and
\(B_m'\) for \(\tau_m'\), proving unit-Adams invariance.  Since \(A_m\) is
positive and strictly dominates the absolute values of both twisted blocks,
strict Perron--Frobenius monotonicity gives
\[
 \rad(B_m),\rad(B_m')<\rad(A_m).
\]
The common Perron root is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-perron-root}
 \lambda_m=SV_m=\ell^2(2m+1)s_m.
\end{equation}

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-collision-radii}
 y_{m,i}=\frac{a}{s_i^2}\qquad(1\le i\le m).
\end{equation}
These numbers are positive, rational, and distinct.  They lie in the open
Perron interval.  Indeed,
\[
 s_1^2\ge a^2K^2(m+1)^2,
 \qquad
 a\lambda_m\le2a^2\ell^2(K+1)(m+1)^2,
\]
and
\[
 K^2=16\ell^4>2\ell^2(K+1)=8\ell^4+2\ell^2.
\]
Thus \(s_1^2>a\lambda_m\), and every
\(y_{m,i}<\lambda_m^{-1}\).  The \(i\)-th factor in
\eqref{eq:odd-prime-staged-determinant} vanishes at \(y_{m,i}\), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-q-collisions}
 Q_m(y_{m,i})=1.
\end{equation}

For every non-trivial character, the determinant ratio is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-mahler-ratio}
 H_m(z)=\frac{Q_m(z^\ell)}{Q_m(z)^\ell}.
\end{equation}
The collapse in \Cref{lem:prime-primary-adams-collapse}, or direct
telescoping, gives
\[
 F_\chi^{\tau_m}(z)-F_\chi^{\tau_m'}(z)
 =\ell\log Q_m(z).
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:odd-prime-q-collisions} makes every non-trivial Fourier
profile difference vanish at all \(m\) radii.  The trivial channel vanishes
because the base is common, so Fourier inversion proves equality of the
complete element-profile vectors there.

Finally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-q-leading-term}
 Q_m(z)=1+c_mz^{m+1}+O(z^{m+2}),
 \qquad c_m=(-1)^m a^{m+1}.
\end{equation}
Since \(\ell(m+1)>m+1\), equations
\eqref{eq:odd-prime-mahler-ratio} and
\eqref{eq:odd-prime-q-leading-term} give
\[
 \log H_m(z)=-\ell c_mz^{m+1}+O(z^{m+2}).
\]
Thus every twisted trace agrees through length \(m\), while
\[
 \Tr(B_m^{m+1})-\Tr((B_m')^{m+1})
 =\ell(m+1)c_m.
\]
The trivial trace difference is zero.  Fourier inversion followed by the
triangular primitive-orbit inversion proves equality of every primitive
length--element count for \(n\le m\).  At the first differing length the
lower-length terms have already cancelled, and Fourier inversion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-prime-first-discrepancy}
 \begin{aligned}
 p_{m+1,0}(\tau_m)-p_{m+1,0}(\tau_m')&=(\ell-1)c_m,\\
 p_{m+1,g}(\tau_m)-p_{m+1,g}(\tau_m')&=-c_m
 \qquad(g\ne0).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This also proves that the two periodic-data sets are different.
\end{proof}


 succeeded in 199ms:
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Let \(X_\Gamma^+\) be the one-sided edge shift of a finite primitive directed
multigraph, and let a one-step cocycle \(\tau:E(\Gamma)\to G\) take values in a
finite group.  A primitive orbit \(\gamma\) has a well-defined conjugacy class
\([g_\gamma]\).  Write \(p_{n,C}(\tau)\) for the number of primitive
length-\(n\) orbits with class \(C\), and define its radial class Euler profile
by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-class-euler-profile}
 \mathcal E_C^\tau(y)
 :=\sum_{n\ge1}p_{n,C}(\tau)\log(1-y^n).
\end{equation}
This converges without a twisted-gap hypothesis for
\(0<y<\lambda^{-1}\), where \(\lambda\) is the Perron root of the base.
Under the strict twisted gap, every raw class profile diverges to \(-\infty\)
at \(y=\lambda^{-1}\), and no finite endpoint value of \(\mathcal E_C\) is
used.  The finite endpoint coordinate used below is the
centered profile
\[
 \mathcal E_C^{\circ}(y)
 :=\mathcal E_C(y)-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\log\det(I-yA),
 \qquad 0<y<\lambda^{-1}.
\]
Under the strict gap its radial limit exists by
\Cref{prop:centered-perron-profile}.  The inverse question of this paper is
whether finitely many open-interval values of all the raw profiles determine
every \(p_{n,C}\); endpoint statements concern only the centered profiles or
their equivalent non-trivial Fourier coordinates.

The determinant input is finite.  For each irreducible representation \(\rho\)
of \(G\), the twisted adjacency block \(A_\rho\) has size
\(v\dim\rho\), where \(v=|V(\Gamma)|\), and
\(P_\rho(z)=\det(I-zA_\rho)\).  What makes the radial inverse problem
non-trivial is that \(\mathcal E_C\) is not the logarithm of one of these
determinants.  For a class function \(\phi\), the periodic Artin logarithm is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-artin}
 L_\phi(z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal P}\sum_{r\ge1}
   \frac{\phi(g_\gamma^r)}r z^{r|\gamma|},
\end{equation}
whereas the fixed-label coordinate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-fixed}
 F_\phi(z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal P}\sum_{r\ge1}
   \frac{\phi(g_\gamma)}r z^{r|\gamma|}.
\end{equation}
Thus \(\mathcal E_C^\tau=-F_{\mathbf1_C}^\tau\).  Adams--M\"obius
inversion expresses every \(F_\phi\) through the finite determinant family,
but it does so at all powers \(z^k\).  Finite radial determination is therefore
a special-value problem, not a coefficient-counting consequence of the
determinant degrees.

\subsection{Main results}

We first state the squarefree estimate that drives the linear theorem.  For
\(p\ge2\), reduce
\[
 H(z)=\frac{P_0(z)}{P_1(z)}=\frac{R(z^p)}{R(z)^p},
 \qquad R=\frac AB,
\]
with both fractions normalized and reduced, and put
\(D=\deg P_0+\deg P_1\).  \Cref{thm:squarefree-mahler-bound} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-collision-bound}
 2(p-1)\deg\rad(AB)\le D.
\end{equation}
If \(R\) is positive on a real interval, then
\Cref{thm:collision-jet-inequality} strengthens this to
\[
 \#\{y:R(y)=1\}+\operatorname{ord}_0(R-1)
 \le\deg\rad(AB)\le\left\lfloor\frac{D}{2(p-1)}\right\rfloor.
\]
The estimate counts distinct singularities, so repeated multiplicities along
long power orbits do not introduce the logarithmic factor present in the
total certificate degree.

The proof has two independent parts.  First,
\Cref{thm:determinant-boundary-lifting} shows that one algebraic interior
collision, without a spectral gap, or one endpoint collision under the gap,
forces the exact rational Mahler coboundary
\[
 H(z)=\frac{R(z^2)}{R(z)^2},\qquad R(0)=R(y)=1.
\]
The general lifting theorem does not require determinant parity.  Its
coefficient recursion gives the explicit bound
\((pq)^{2n-1}f_n\in\ZZ\), hence \(\log d_h=O(h)\) for the common
denominators; Kumiko Nishioka's special-value theorem then forces algebraicity,
and the cited 1985 result of Keiji Nishioka gives rationality.  We verify every
hypothesis of Kumiko Nishioka's printed theorem, including the critical
inequality \(p^2<p^3\).  Parity and Dieudonn\'e--Dwork remain available only
as a stronger integrality refinement.  Separately,
\Cref{prop:logarithmic-mahler-divisor-bound} proves the paper's
total-degree Mahler estimate: the reduced input degree \(D\) alone bounds
\(\deg A+\deg B\) for the multiplicative certificate \(R=A/B\) by
\(b_p(D)=O_p(D\log D)\).  This is not a bound for an additive
logarithmic-derivative certificate or for an arbitrary rational solution of a
general linear Mahler equation.  The \(\Omega_p(D\log D)\) family makes the
order sharp, and \Cref{cor:realizable-logarithmic-certificate-sharpness}
transports that sharpness to standard realizable \(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.
The supporting \Cref{thm:effective-rational-mahler-coboundary} also records
an explicit height estimate and fixed-base bit complexity, while its finite
Pad\'e step implements rational reconstruction once a degree cap is known.
The collision radii are exactly the positive zeros of \(R-1\).

The principal inverse theorem is not confined to a common base, to one binary
coordinate, or to the prime two.  Let \(\ell\) be prime, let \(G\) be a finite
abelian \(\ell\)-group, and suppose that the pair of cocycles satisfies
\[
 \frac{P_{\chi^u,\tau}(z)}{P_{\chi^u,\tau'}(z)}
 =\frac{P_{\chi,\tau}(z)}{P_{\chi,\tau'}(z)}
 \qquad(\chi\in\widehat G,\ (u,\ell)=1).
\]
By \Cref{thm:prime-primary-collision-jet-rigidity}, \(K\) common profile
vectors, with one algebraic anchor, together with agreement of the first
\(L\) primitive vectors determine all primitive length--element counts when
\(K+L\ge\max\{v,v'\}\).  In particular, \(V\) radial locations suffice for
bases of at most \(V\) vertices.  Summing the element profiles first recovers
the base determinant.  The prime-primary Adams--M\"obius collapse is
\[
 \Delta_\chi(z)
 =-\sum_{j\ge0}\ell^{-j}\log H_\chi(z^{\ell^j})
  +\sum_{j\ge1}\ell^{-j}\log H_{\chi^\ell}(z^{\ell^j}).
\]
Induction on the order of \(\chi\) applies the scalar lifting and the
collision--jet inequality.  One observation at a radius means the entire
\(G\)-valued vector \((\mathcal E_g(y))_{g\in G}\), so the radial depth is
independent of the rank and exponent of \(G\).  For \(C_2\) the relative
symmetry is automatic.

One centered radius cannot suffice.  \Cref{thm:c2-boundary-collision} gives
two strict-gap labellings of the same four-vertex graph with different
length-one class counts but identical centered class Euler profiles at the
Perron radius.  Their raw class profiles both diverge there.  The centered
identity is exact and comes from
\(Q(z^2)/Q(z)^2\), where \(Q(z)=1-z+4z^2\) and \(Q(1/4)=1\).

The realizable lower bound grows linearly.
\Cref{thm:realizable-linear-multicollisions} constructs, for every \(m\ge1\),
two strict-gap cocycles on the same positive \(4m+2\)-vertex base with
different periodic data and equality of both element profiles at \(m\)
distinct rational radii.  Hence
\[
 \mathsf N_{C_2}(V)\ge
 \left\lfloor\frac{V-2}{4}\right\rfloor+1.
\]
For odd primes, \Cref{thm:odd-prime-delayed-multicollisions} constructs two
unit-Adams-invariant \(C_\ell\)-cocycles on a common
\(\ell(2m+1)\)-vertex positive base.  Their full profiles agree at \(m\)
rational radii, their primitive data agree through length \(m\), and their
first discrepancy at length \(m+1\) is explicit.  Separately,
\Cref{cor:realizable-logarithmic-certificate-sharpness} realizes the abstract
\(\Omega(D\log D)\) certificate-degree family on standard covers.

For an arbitrary finite group, \Cref{thm:radial-profile-recovery} proves,
again without a twisted-gap hypothesis, recovery from a set with an interior
accumulation point.  We do not claim a
finite polynomial sample bound in that generality.  The precise obstruction
to extending the present method is recorded after
\Cref{thm:finite-radial-sampling}: already for \(C_3\), the
Adams--M\"obius coefficient is non-zero at infinitely many prime powers and
does not reduce to a one-variable Mahler orbit.  The missing input is a
special-value and effective zero theorem for that coupled cyclotomic Euler
system.  The same obstruction remains for generic \(C_3\)- and
\(C_4\)-cocycles outside the relative unit-Adams condition.  Thus the
prime-primary theorem identifies the symmetric subclass handled by the
one-variable method; it does not assert an unrestricted finite-group theorem.

\subsection{Prior work and scope}

The Frobenius-class objects used here have a direct dynamical lineage.
Parry--Pollicott prove a Chebotarev asymptotic for primitive closed orbits in a
prescribed Frobenius conjugacy class in finite Galois covers of Axiom~A flows,
and Noorani--Parry prove the finite homogeneous-extension analogue for shifts
\cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev,NooraniParry1992ChebotarevShifts}.
Mohamed--Noorani then prove a class-restricted Mertens product for finite-group
extensions of SFTs: their Theorem~1 has the product
\[
 \prod_{\substack{|\gamma|\le N\\g_\gamma\in C}}
 (1-e^{-h|\gamma|})
\]
with exponent \(|C|/|G|\) and a constant expressed through Artin
\(L\)-values \cite[Theorem~1, pp.~124--125]
{MeslizaNoorani1999FrobeniusMertens}.  Noorani's later toral paper instead
classifies the lift-degree partitions of closed orbits in a finite torsion
cover and gives asymptotic counts for each realizable partition
\cite{Noorani2003ClosedOrbitsToral}.  These are orbit-distribution and product
results, not finite-special-value inverse theorems.

At the formula level, the standard Artin function in that literature has
\[
 \log L(z,\phi)
 =\sum_{\gamma}\sum_{m\ge1}
   \frac{\phi(g_\gamma^m)}m z^{m|\gamma|}
 =L_\phi(z),
\]
whereas taking the logarithm of a product whose membership condition remains
\([g_\gamma]=C\) gives
\[
 \sum_{[g_\gamma]=C}\log(1-z^{|\gamma|})
 =-F_{\mathbf1_C}(z),\qquad
 F_\phi(z)=\sum_{\gamma}\sum_{m\ge1}
   \frac{\phi(g_\gamma)}m z^{m|\gamma|}.
\]
Thus class-restricted Euler products, character orthogonality, and the
\(\Pi_\phi,L_\phi\) coordinates are prior art.  The fixed-label transform
\(F_\phi\) is already implicit in the logarithm of the 1999 product.  What is
specific here is that \(F_\phi\), rather than \(L_\phi\), is passed through the
Adams--M\"obius formula \eqref{eq:fixed-adams-mobius} and then used in a
finite-special-value inverse problem.

The algebraic-solution rationality step is also prior.  Ostrowski treats the
linear multiplicative equation
\(\Phi(\varphi(z))=g(z)\Phi(z)\) for rational data
\cite{Ostrowski1968AlgebraicSolutions}; that equation does not directly cover
the square in \(F(z^2)=H(z)^{-1}F(z)^2\).  Kumiko Nishioka's
Theorem~5.1.7 is the standard reference for the rational--transcendental
dichotomy for \(k\)-Mahler functions defined by a linear functional equation
\cite[Theorem~5.1.7]{Nishioka1996MahlerFunctions}; see also the modern
restatement and proof in \cite[Corollary~8]{BellCoonsRowland2013MahlerDichotomy}.
Our equation is quadratic in \(F\) and therefore outside that linear class;
The cited nonlinear class of Keiji Nishioka,
\(f(z^p)=\mathscr R(z,f(z))\), is the applicable result.  We use that 1985
result in the following one-variable form: if
\(f(z^p)=\mathscr R(z,f(z))\), with \(\mathscr R\) rational and \(f\)
algebraic over \(\CC(z)\), then \(f\in\CC(z)\)
\cite{Nishioka1985AlgebraicSolutions}.  Taking
\(p=2\) and \(\mathscr R(z,Y)=Y^2/H(z)\) proves the rationality used in
\Cref{prop:algebraic-mahler-coboundary}; that rationality is not claimed as a
contribution of this paper.

The decision problem does reduce to published additive and linear Mahler
algorithms.  Write \(H=P_0/P_1\), let \(\sigma f(z)=f(z^p)\), and, for a
prospective rational solution, put \(u=zR'/R\).  Logarithmic differentiation
gives the exact reduction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-logarithmic-derivative-reduction}
 (\sigma-1)u=\frac{z}{p}\frac{H'}H.
\end{equation}
Arreche--Zhang give a complete effective obstruction and certificate
algorithm for precisely \((\sigma-1)u=f\)
\cite{ArrecheZhang2022MahlerResidues}; the rational-solution algorithm of
Chyzak--Dreyfus--Dumas--Mezzarobba also applies after homogenization
\cite{ChyzakDreyfusDumasMezzarobba2018MahlerSolutions}.  Explicitly, if
\(f\ne0\), then
\((\sigma-(\sigma f)/f)(\sigma-1)u=0\), and one filters the computed rational
solution space by \((\sigma-1)u=f\).  Solutions of the additive equation are
determined only up to a constant, since the rational \(\sigma\)-invariants are
constants.  After replacing an additive certificate by its unique
representative satisfying \(u(0)=0\), it comes from a normalized rational
\(R\) exactly when \(u/z\) has no polynomial part, is regular at zero, and
has only simple finite poles with integer residues.  In that case the
product of the corresponding rational irreducible factors, normalized at
zero, has logarithmic derivative \(u/z\); the quotient
\(R(z^p)/(H(z)R(z)^p)\) has zero logarithmic derivative and equals one at
zero.  This proves the converse and the reduction, rather than merely a
formal resemblance.

The Riccati equations for first-order factors instead contain products of
successive shifts
\cite{ChyzakDreyfusDumasMezzarobba2025FirstOrderFactors}, and Pegis treats a
different constant-coefficient nonlinear equation
\cite{Pegis1996NonlinearMahler}.  Thus bare existence and decidability are not
claimed here.

The Mahler package has three levels.  \emph{The original quantitative core}
is the input-only divisor estimate for the multiplicative certificate itself,
not for \(u\) and not for a general linear Mahler solution, together with its
\(\Omega_p(D\log D)\) lower-bound family and the realizable transfer in
\Cref{cor:realizable-logarithmic-certificate-sharpness}.  \emph{The useful
incremental layer} is the explicit height control and resulting fixed-\(p\)
integer bit-complexity estimate.  Chyzak--Dreyfus--Dumas--Mezzarobba compute
all rational solutions of a general linear Mahler equation, with their
complexity measured primarily in arithmetic operations over the coefficient
field \cite{ChyzakDreyfusDumasMezzarobba2018MahlerSolutions}; that is distinct
from, but not displaced by, the integer coefficient-height analysis here.
\emph{The prior or standard layer} consists of bare existence and decidability
through the cited additive and linear algorithms, and the affine Pad\'e step,
which is largely standard rational reconstruction once a degree bound is
available.  Exact substitution into the cleared identity remains necessary
for correctness, but is not asserted as a separate conceptual contribution.

Accordingly, the parity-free special-value interface is not presented as a
standalone novelty claim: it is the combination of Kumiko Nishioka's
special-value theorem, the cited algebraic-solution rationality result of
Keiji Nishioka, and the elementary denominator estimate above.  The surviving
contribution is parity-compatible algebraic collision lifting in the
dynamical setting, the sharp input-only multiplicative-certificate divisor
bound with its height and bit-complexity consequences, and cross-base finite
radial determination for odd-Adams-invariant finite abelian \(2\)-groups,
together with realizable certificate-degree sharpness and the linear sampling
lower bound on standard \(C_2\)-covers.  The
class Euler products, their character expansion, the general periodic-data
dictionary, both Nishioka inputs, and the analytic identity-theorem recovery
statement are background rather than novelty claims.  Boyle--Schmieding's computable periodic invariants
\cite{BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions} and represented
cospectrality and switching in gain-graph theory
\cite{Zaslavsky1989BiasedGraphs,CavaleriDAngeliDonno2021BalanceGainGraphs,
CavaleriDonno2022Cospectrality} delimit the dynamical side further.

Three printed open-problem interfaces delimit the claim.  Boyle and
Schmieding ask to ``characterize the polynomials \(\det(I-tA)\) arising from
\(G\)-primitive matrices'' and to ``characterize the trace series \(T_A\) and
conjugate trace series \(\kappa T_A\)''
\cite[Realization Problems~6.1(2)--(3)]{BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions}.
Our theorem starts with two realized extensions, so its determinant and trace
formulas do not solve either positivity-realization problem.  Chyzak,
Dreyfus, Dumas, and Mezzarobba leave open polynomial-time solution of general
linear Mahler equations for all order--degree regimes
\cite[Sec.~4.3]{ChyzakDreyfusDumasMezzarobba2018MahlerSolutions}; our fixed-
\(p\) Pad\'e criterion treats one normalized multiplicative coboundary equation
and does not settle that all-parameter complexity problem.  O'Hare asks for finite-data rigidity for
unimodal and Markov maps \cite[Introduction]{OHare2025FiniteDataRigidity}.
The present theorem is an exact symbolic result for orbit-class Euler data,
not a derivative-data rigidity theorem for those maps.

The separately submitted Supplementary Material contains the Frobenius-class Mertens correction from which the profiles arose, its
quotient-cover refinement, and the exact \(S_3\) witness are now separated
from the inverse argument.  This paper uses only
the fixed-label coordinate and does not claim that the underlying Mertens
product or Frobenius equidistribution is new.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For relatively unit-Adams-invariant extensions by any finite abelian
\(\ell\)-group \(G\), with \(\ell\) prime, over two primitive bases of at
most \(V\) vertices, \(V\) radial locations in the common open Perron
interval determine the complete primitive length--element profile, provided
that one location is algebraic.  More generally, \(K\) radial profiles and
agreement of the first \(L\) primitive vectors suffice when
\(K+L\ge\max\{v,v'\}\).  This radial depth is independent of the rank and
exponent of \(G\), while each location supplies a full
\(\abs G\)-component vector.  The bases need not coincide.  A
strict twisted gap permits centered, not raw, class profiles at a Perron
endpoint in the binary scalar theorem.  The determinant ratio governing the
test is canonically the ratio of the standard Artin--Mazur zeta functions of
the two binary covers.  The proof is effective: one algebraic collision
produces a unique rational Mahler certificate.  Its squarefree degree satisfies
\(2(p-1)\deg\rad(AB)\le D\), and the collision--jet inequality converts this
directly into the linear radial theorem.  For every fixed \(p\ge2\), the
separate total-degree estimate bounds the numerator-plus-denominator degree
of this multiplicative certificate, from the reduced input degree alone, by
\(b_p(D)=O_p(D\log D)\).  Its order is sharp abstractly and on realizable
standard \(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.  The explicit height and fixed-\(p\) bit
complexity estimates are useful further consequences; the finite Pad\'e
system is rational reconstruction under the resulting cap.  The exact four-vertex
pair shows why a single centered Perron-boundary vector cannot be an inverse
invariant; every corresponding raw class profile diverges to \(-\infty\).
For every \(m\), an explicit binary pair on a common \(4m+2\)-vertex positive
base has \(m\) distinct rational collisions but different periodic data.
For each odd prime \(\ell\), a pair on \(\ell(2m+1)\) vertices has \(m\)
rational collisions, agrees through length \(m\), and first differs at length
\(m+1\) by an explicit element vector.
The logarithmic certificate-degree order is also sharp on standard realizable
\(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.

The determinant lifting theorem is a specialization of a parity-free rational
critical \(p\)-Mahler statement.  Its coefficient recursion gives the exact
bound
\[
 (pq)^{2n-1}f_n\in\ZZ,\qquad
 \log d_h\le(2h-1)\log(pq),
\]
which is sufficient for Kumiko Nishioka's 1982 special-value theorem.
The cited algebraic-solution rationality result of Keiji Nishioka from 1985
then makes the algebraic solution rational, and normalization over \(\QQ\)
supplies the coboundary.  Determinant parity is therefore a convenient strong
sufficient condition yielding integral coefficients through
Dieudonn\'e--Dwork, not an essential lifting hypothesis.  The endpoint form
still uses the strict gap only to continue through the Perron circle.

The full prime-primary Adams--M\"obius collapse is written explicitly, and
induction on character order reduces each relatively unit-Adams-invariant
channel to one \(\ell\)-Mahler orbit.  The same mechanism does not presently
yield a polynomial sample
bound for every finite group.  For \(C_3\), Adams--M\"obius inversion already couples
two character determinants at infinitely many arithmetically distributed
powers, rather than one lacunary Mahler orbit.  A general finite-group theorem
therefore requires a new special-value and effective zero theory for these
coupled Euler systems.  Generic \(C_4\) outside the relative unit-Adams-invariant
subclass has the analogous coupled obstruction.  In its absence, the proved
general statement remains unconditional analytic recovery from a profile set
with an interior accumulation point.

The novelty claim is correspondingly narrow.  The parity-free lifting theorem
is explicitly a combination of the two Nishioka inputs and the elementary
denominator estimate, rather than a new special-value theory.
Frobenius-class Euler products, their character expansion,
algebraic-solution rationality for rational functional equations, and the
general periodic-data dictionary are cited background.  The original Mahler
increment is the input-only \(b_p(D)\) divisor estimate for the multiplicative
certificate, its \(\Omega_p(D\log D)\) family, and the transfer of that
sharpness to realizable \(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.  The height bound and fixed-
\(p\) integer bit complexity are useful but incremental.  Bare existence and
decidability reduce, by logarithmic differentiation, to the published
additive and linear Mahler algorithms; Pad\'e reconstruction is largely
standard after a degree cap is known.  The central result is instead the
cross-base prime-primary linear inverse theorem, together with the realizable
binary and odd-prime multi-collision families.

The principal dynamical theorem remains an inverse theorem for the
paper-specific sampled fixed-label Euler values.  Its standard dynamical
conclusion is represented periodic-data equivalence, via the cited
determinant/periodic-data dictionary.  It does not advance conjugacy, strong
shift equivalence, cocycle cohomology, switching, Bowen--Franks or flow
equivalence, or full positivity realization.  The structural reasons are
that class counts discard marked-orbit holonomy, determinant data discard
positive matrix factorization, the relative realization lemma chooses a
large common base only after the candidate polynomials are fixed, and the
arithmetic reduction controls a scalar \(p\)-power orbit rather than the
coupled character systems required beyond the stated class.

## Nishioka source verification for the linear collision audit (16 August 2026)

The two similarly named sources used by the scalar lifting theorem were
checked separately.  They are by different authors and supply different
steps.

- **Kumiko Nishioka (1982):** *On a problem of Mahler for transcendency of
  function values*, J. Austral. Math. Soc. Ser. A **33** (1982), 386--393,
  DOI `10.1017/S1446788700018814`.  Crossref returns Kumiko Nishioka, volume
  33, issue 3, and pages 386--393; Semantic Scholar resolves the same DOI and
  links the Cambridge publisher PDF.  The Cambridge PDF was checked directly.
  Its theorem on p. 387 treats a transcendental convergent power series
  satisfying the algebraic equation (1.2), assumes coefficient-size and
  common-denominator bounds `log [a_h], log d_h <= c h^L`, the orbit conditions
  `T^i alpha != 0` and `g(T^i alpha) != 0`, and
  `M(p+N)n^2 < p^(2+1/L)`, and concludes that `f(alpha)` is transcendental.
  For the manuscript's equation, the checked substitution is
  `Tz=z^p`, `Q_0=P_0`, `Q_1=-P_1 u^p`, `g=P_0`, and
  `N=0, n=1, m=M=p, U=L=1`; the strict inequality is `p^2<p^3`.
  The manuscript's linear coefficient-height and denominator bounds give the
  printed growth hypothesis, while zero-freedom of `H=P_0/P_1` on the orbit
  gives `P_0(alpha^(p^i)) != 0` and analyticity.  Thus this source supports the
  algebraic-special-value implication used in the lifting theorem.

- **Keiji Nishioka (1985):** *Algebraic function solutions of a certain class
  of functional equations*, Arch. Math. (Basel) **44** (1985), 330--335,
  DOI `10.1007/BF01235775`.  Crossref and the official Springer record return
  Keiji Nishioka, volume 44, issue 4, and pages 330--335; Semantic Scholar
  resolves the same DOI.  The publisher full text is subscription-only, so the
  theorem statement was cross-checked against John H. Loxton's zbMATH review
  Zbl `0568.12014` (`zbMATH` document `3906608`).  The review records the
  following special case of the paper's result: if
  `f(z_1^(p_1),...,z_n^(p_n)) = F(z_1,...,z_n,f(z_1,...,z_n))`, with `F`
  rational, and the Laurent series `f` is algebraic over
  `C(z_1,...,z_n)`, then `f` belongs to `C(z_1,...,z_n)`.  The manuscript uses
  the one-variable specialization `F(z,Y)=Y^p/H(z)`, exactly within that
  class.  This source supplies algebraic-to-rational descent over `C`; the
  further descent to `Q(z)` comes from the manuscript's rational Taylor
  coefficients and coefficientwise Galois invariance.

No priority conclusion is drawn from these checks.  They verify the imported
interfaces and attribution, not novelty of the new divisor and collision
arguments.

 257: \begin{lemma}[Integral critical Mahler product]
 258: \label{lem:critical-mahler-integrality}
 259: Let \(P_0,P_1\in\ZZ[z]\) have constant term one and satisfy
 260: \(P_0\equiv P_1\pmod2\).  Put
 261: \[
 262:  H(z)=\frac{P_0(z)}{P_1(z)},
 263: \]
 264: and suppose that both polynomials are zero-free on \(\abs z<r\),
 265: where \(0<r\le1\).  On this disk define
 266: \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-mahler-product}
 267:  F(z)=\prod_{\nu\ge0}H(z^{2^\nu})^{2^{-\nu-1}},
 268:  \qquad F(0)=1,
 269: \end{equation}
 270: using the logarithm germ at zero.  Then \(F\) is analytic and non-vanishing,
 271: \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-mahler-equation}
 272:  F(z)^2=H(z)F(z^2),
 273: \end{equation}
 274: and \(F\in\ZZ[[z]]\).
 275: \end{lemma}
 276: 
 277: \begin{proof}
 278: Normal convergence of the logarithmic series on compact subdisks proves the
 279: analytic assertions and shifting the product proves
 280: \eqref{eq:critical-mahler-equation}.  The assumed congruence gives
 281: \(P_0\equiv P_1\pmod2\), hence
 282: \[
 283:  H(z)^{-1}\in1+2z\ZZ_2[[z]].
 284: \]
 285: Since
 286: \(F(z^2)/F(z)^2=H(z)^{-1}\), the Dieudonn\'e--Dwork criterion
 287: \cite[Theorem~1.1]{PomeratStraub2024RootsPowerSeries} gives
 288: \(F\in\ZZ_2[[z]]\).  Also \(F\in\QQ[[z]]\), directly from
 289: \eqref{eq:critical-mahler-product}.  For an odd prime \(p\), compare the
 290: coefficient of \(z^n\) in \eqref{eq:critical-mahler-equation}.  Its left-hand
 291: side is \(2f_n\) plus an expression in \(f_0,\ldots,f_{n-1}\), while its
 292: right-hand side belongs to \(\ZZ_p\) whenever the preceding coefficients do,
 293: because \(P_1(0)=1\).  Induction and \(2\in\ZZ_p^\times\) give
 294: \(F\in\ZZ_p[[z]]\) for every odd \(p\).  A rational number integral at every
 295: prime is an integer, proving the claim coefficientwise.
 296: \end{proof}

 298: \begin{lemma}[Linear-exponent denominators for rational critical products]
 299: \label{lem:rational-critical-mahler-denominators}
 300: Let \(p\ge2\), let \(P_0,P_1\in\QQ[z]\) have constant term one, and let
 301: \(q\ge1\) be an integer for which \(qP_0,qP_1\in\ZZ[z]\).  The unique formal
 302: series \(F(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}f_nz^n\in1+z\QQ[[z]]\) satisfying
 303: \begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-critical-mahler-equation}
 304:  P_1(z)F(z)^p=P_0(z)F(z^p)
 305: \end{equation}
 306: obeys
 307: \begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-critical-mahler-denominator-bound}
 308:  (pq)^{2n-1}f_n\in\ZZ\qquad(n\ge1).
 309: \end{equation}
 310: Consequently the first \(h+1\) coefficients have a common positive
 311: denominator \(d_h\) satisfying
 312: \begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-critical-mahler-common-denominator}
 313:  d_h\mid(pq)^{2h-1},
 314:  \qquad \log d_h\le(2h-1)\log(pq)\qquad(h\ge1).
 315: \end{equation}
 316: \end{lemma}
 317: 
 318: \begin{proof}
 319: Put \(\widetilde P_i=qP_i\), so that
 320: \(\widetilde P_i\in\ZZ[z]\) and \(\widetilde P_i(0)=q\).  Comparison of the
 321: coefficient of \(z^n\) in
 322: \(\widetilde P_1F^p=\widetilde P_0F(z^p)\) gives
 323: \begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-critical-mahler-recursion}
 324:  pqf_n=\Phi_n(f_1,\ldots,f_{n-1}),
 325: \end{equation}
 326: where \(\Phi_n\) has integral coefficients.  Indeed, the only occurrence of
 327: \(f_n\) is obtained by choosing it in one of the \(p\) factors of \(F^p\)
 328: and constant terms everywhere else.  Every remaining linear term is \(f_j\)
 329: with \(j<n\); every remaining nonlinear monomial can be written
 330: \(f_{j_1}\cdots f_{j_s}\), where \(s\ge2\), all \(j_i\ge1\), and
 331: \(j_1+\cdots+j_s\le n\).
 332: 
 333: Set \(c=pq\).  We prove \eqref{eq:rational-critical-mahler-denominator-bound}
 334: by induction.  If it holds below \(n\), a linear term \(f_j\), \(j<n\), has
 335: denominator dividing \(c^{2j-1}\), hence \(c^{2n-2}\).  A nonlinear monomial
 336: as above has denominator dividing
 337: \[
 338:  c^{\sum_i(2j_i-1)}
 339:  =c^{2\sum_i j_i-s}\mid c^{2n-2}.
 340: \]
 341: Thus \(c^{2n-2}\Phi_n\in\ZZ\), and division by \(c\) in
 342: \eqref{eq:rational-critical-mahler-recursion} proves the induction step.
 343: The exponents are increasing with \(n\), so the common-denominator assertion
 344: and its logarithmic bound follow immediately.
 345: \end{proof}

 354: \begin{lemma}[Critical quadratic zero estimate]
 355: \label{lem:critical-mahler-zero-estimate}
 356: Let \(P_0,P_1\in\CC[z]\) have constant term one, set
 357: \(\eta=\max\{\deg P_0,\deg P_1\}\), and let an analytic germ
 358: \(F(0)=1\) satisfy
 359: \[
 360:  P_1(z)F(z)^2=P_0(z)F(z^2).
 361: \]
 362: If \(F\) is transcendental over \(\CC(z)\), then every non-zero
 363: \(Q\in\CC[z,Y]\) with \(\deg_zQ\le D\) and \(\deg_YQ\le N\) satisfies
 364: \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-mahler-zero-bound}
 365:  \operatorname{ord}_{z=0}Q(z,F(z))
 366:  \le D+4DN+\eta N^2.
 367: \end{equation}
 368: \end{lemma}
 369: 
 370: \begin{proof}
 371: First take an irreducible factor \(q\in\CC[z,Y]\) of positive
 372: \(Y\)-degree and write \(d=\deg_zq\), \(n=\deg_Yq\).  Clear the denominator
 373: in its pullback by setting
 374: \[
 375:  \widetilde q(z,Y)=P_0(z)^n
 376:  q\!\left(z^2,\frac{P_1(z)}{P_0(z)}Y^2\right).
 377: \]
 378: This is a polynomial, with
 379: \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-pullback-orders}
 380:  \deg_Y\widetilde q=2n,\qquad
 381:  \deg_z\widetilde q\le2d+\eta n,\qquad
 382:  \operatorname{ord}_0\widetilde q(z,F(z))
 383:  =2\operatorname{ord}_0q(z,F(z)).
 384: \end{equation}
 385: If \(q(0,1)\ne0\), then \(\operatorname{ord}_0q(z,F(z))=0\), so there is
 386: nothing to prove.  Assume henceforth that \(q(0,1)=0\).
 387: We claim that \(q\nmid\widetilde q\).  Otherwise the irreducible curve
 388: \(C:q=0\) is invariant under the rational map
 389: \[
 390:  \Phi(z,Y)=\left(z^2,\frac{P_1(z)}{P_0(z)}Y^2\right).
 391: \]
 392: Pass to the normalization of \(C\).  The induced map permutes forward the
 393: finite set of places above \((0,1)\), so this finite dynamical system contains
 394: a periodic place.  Its Puiseux germ \(Y=G(z)\), based at \((0,1)\), therefore
 395: satisfies for some \(\ell\ge1\) the \(\ell\)-fold iterate of the same equation
 396: as \(F\).  Consequently \(U=G/F\) obeys
 397: \[
 398:  U(z^{2^\ell})=U(z)^{2^\ell},\qquad U(0)=1.
 399: \]
 400: If \(U=1+cz^\alpha+\cdots\), with \(c\ne0\) and the least positive Puiseux
 401: exponent \(\alpha\), the least non-constant exponents on the two sides are
 402: respectively \(2^\ell\alpha\) and \(\alpha\), a contradiction.  Thus
 403: \(U=1\), which would make \(F=G\) algebraic.  This proves the claim.
 404: 
 405: Hence \(R_q(z)=\operatorname{Res}_Y(q,\widetilde q)\) is non-zero.  A
 406: B\'ezout identity in \(Y\), evaluated at \(Y=F(z)\), and
 407: \eqref{eq:critical-pullback-orders} imply
 408: \[
 409:  \operatorname{ord}_0q(z,F(z))
 410:  \le\operatorname{ord}_0R_q
 411:  \le\deg R_q
 412:  \le4dn+\eta n^2.
 413: \]
 414: Finally write \(Q=a(z)\prod_iq_i^{e_i}\), separating its polynomial content
 415: in \(z\).  The content contributes at most \(D\).  With multiplicities,
 416: \(\sum_i e_id_i\le D\) and \(\sum_i e_in_i\le N\); therefore
 417: \(\sum_i e_id_in_i\le DN\) and
 418: \(\sum_i e_in_i^2\le N^2\).  Summing the factor bounds proves
 419: \eqref{eq:critical-mahler-zero-bound}.
 420: \end{proof}

 431: \begin{proposition}[Algebraic critical products are rational coboundaries]
 432: \label{prop:algebraic-mahler-coboundary}
 433: Let \(p\ge2\), let \(H\in\QQ(z)^\times\), \(H(0)=1\), and suppose that
 434: \(F\in1+z\QQ[[z]]\) is algebraic over \(\CC(z)\) and satisfies
 435: \(F(z)^p=H(z)F(z^p)\).  Then there is a unique
 436: \(R\in\QQ(z)^\times\), normalized by \(R(0)=1\), such that
 437: \begin{equation}\label{eq:general-rational-mahler-coboundary}
 438:  H(z)=\frac{R(z^p)}{R(z)^p}.
 439: \end{equation}
 440: \end{proposition}
 441: 
 442: \begin{proof}
 443: The cited 1985 algebraic-solution rationality result of Keiji Nishioka gives,
 444: in the one-variable form used here, that an algebraic Laurent-series solution of
 445: \(f(z^p)=\mathscr R(z,f(z))\), with \(\mathscr R\) rational, belongs to
 446: \(\CC(z)\) \cite{Nishioka1985AlgebraicSolutions}.  Apply it with
 447: \(\mathscr R(z,Y)=Y^p/H(z)\).  Thus \(F\in\CC(z)\).
 448: Because the normalized Taylor germ of \(F\) has coefficients in \(\QQ\),
 449: coefficientwise Galois invariance gives \(F\in\QQ(z)\).  Taking
 450: \(R=F^{-1}\) in the displayed functional equation gives
 451: \[
 452:  H(z)=\frac{R(z^p)}{R(z)^p},\qquad R(0)=1.
 453: \]
 454: For uniqueness, if \(U(0)=1\) and \(U(z^p)=U(z)^p\), a least non-constant
 455: term \(az^n\) would occur in degree \(pn\) on the left and degree \(n\) on
 456: the right.  Hence \(U=1\).  The rationality implication used here is the cited
 457: result of Keiji Nishioka; the only descent specific to the present
 458: normalization is from the rational Taylor germ to \(\QQ(z)\).
 459: \end{proof}

 554: \begin{theorem}[Two-base interior determinant lifting and endpoint extension]
 555: \label{thm:determinant-boundary-lifting}
 556: Let \(A,A'\) be primitive non-negative integer matrices, not necessarily of
 557: the same size, with Perron roots \(\lambda,\lambda'>1\).
 558: Put \(x=\min\{\lambda^{-1},(\lambda')^{-1}\}\).  Let \(B,B'\) be integer
 559: matrices of the respective sizes of \(A,A'\) such that, entrywise,
 560: \[
 561:  \abs{B_{ij}}\le A_{ij},
 562:  \qquad \abs{B'_{ij}}\le A'_{ij},
 563: \]
 564: and set
 565: \[
 566:  H(z)=\frac{\det(I-zB)}{\det(I-zB')}.
 567: \]
 568: For every algebraic number \(y\) with \(0<y<x\), the following are
 569: equivalent:
 570: \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
 571:  \item \(\Pi_y(H)=1\);
 572:  \item there is a unique \(R\in\QQ(z)^\times\) such that
 573:  \[
 574:   R(0)=R(y)=1,
 575:   \qquad H(z)=\frac{R(z^2)}{R(z)^2}.
 576:  \]
 577: \end{enumerate}
 578: If \(\rad(B)<\lambda\) and \(\rad(B')<\lambda'\), the equivalence also
 579: holds at \(y=x\).
 580: \end{theorem}
 581: 
 582: \begin{proof}
 583: The Perron-weighted norm estimate in the proof of
 584: \Cref{lem:perron-weighted-domination}, applied separately to the two bases,
 585: gives \(\rad(B)\le\lambda\) and \(\rad(B')\le\lambda'\).  If \(y<x\), choose
 586: \(r\) with \(y<r<x\); if \(y=x\) and both strict gaps hold, choose \(r>y\)
 587: below both reciprocal spectral radii.  In either case both determinant
 588: polynomials are non-zero on \(\abs z<r\).  They are real, non-zero on
 589: \([0,y]\), and equal one at zero, so they are positive there and \(H>0\) on
 590: that interval.  Define \(F=F_{H,2}\) by
 591: \eqref{eq:general-critical-p-mahler-product}.  Its normalization gives
 592: \begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-product-square-normalization}
 593:  F(y)^2=\Pi_y(H),
 594: \end{equation}
 595: not \(F(y)=\Pi_y(H)\).
 596: 
 597: If \(\textup{(i)}\) holds, positivity and
 598: \eqref{eq:critical-product-square-normalization} give \(F(y)=1\).
 599: \Cref{thm:general-rational-p-mahler-lifting}, with \(p=2\), then gives the
 600: unique normalized rational certificate and \(R(y)=1\).  This is
 601: \(\textup{(ii)}\).  Conversely, \(\textup{(ii)}\) and
 602: \eqref{eq:rational-mahler-telescope} give \(\textup{(i)}\).

\subsection{Odd-Adams-invariant abelian two-group extensions}
\label{sec:odd-adams-sampling}

The dyadic reduction extends beyond elementary two-groups precisely when the
odd Adams directions are constant.  We first record the two algebraic facts
needed for that extension.

\begin{lemma}[Odd-Adams integrality and M\"obius collapse]
\label{lem:odd-adams-mobius-collapse}
Let \(G\) be a finite abelian \(2\)-group and let
\(\tau:E(\Gamma)\to G\) and \(\tau':E(\Gamma')\to G\) be one-step
cocycles.  For \(\chi\in\widehat G\), write
\[
 P_{\chi,\tau}(z)=\det(I-zA_{\chi,\tau}),\qquad
 P_{\chi,\tau'}(z)=\det(I-zA_{\chi,\tau'}),
\]
and assume, separately for the two cocycles, the condition denoted (OA):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-adams-determinant-invariance}
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{\chi^u,\tau}(z)&=P_{\chi,\tau}(z),\\
 P_{\chi^u,\tau'}(z)&=P_{\chi,\tau'}(z)
 \end{aligned}
 \qquad(\chi\in\widehat G,\ u\ \text{odd}).
\end{equation}
Then \(P_{\chi,\tau},P_{\chi,\tau'}\in\ZZ[z]\) for every \(\chi\).
If
\[
 H_\chi(z):=\frac{P_{\chi,\tau}(z)}{P_{\chi,\tau'}(z)},\qquad
 \Delta_\chi(z):=F_\chi^\tau(z)-F_\chi^{\tau'}(z),
\]
then, on the common open Perron disk,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-adams-mobius-collapse}
 \Delta_\chi(z)
 =-\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}\Log_0H_\chi(z^{2^j})
  +\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}\Log_0H_{\chi^2}(z^{2^j}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that \(\chi\) has order \(2^a\), and put
\(K=\QQ(\chi(G))\).  The entries of \(A_{\chi,\tau}\) lie in the ring of
integers of \(K\), so the coefficients of \(P_{\chi,\tau}\) are algebraic
integers.  Every element of \(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\QQ)\) acts on the values
of \(\chi\) by \(\chi(g)\mapsto\chi(g)^u\) for some odd \(u\), and therefore
sends \(P_{\chi,\tau}\) to \(P_{\chi^u,\tau}\).  Condition
\eqref{eq:odd-adams-determinant-invariance} fixes this polynomial under the
whole Galois group.  Its coefficients are rational algebraic integers, hence
integers.  The same argument applies to \(\tau'\).

For the collapse, the abelian specialization of
\eqref{eq:fixed-determinant-open-disc} gives the absolutely convergent identity
\begin{align}
 \Delta_\chi(z)
 &=-\sum_{r,m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
      \Log_0H_{\chi^m}(z^{rm})\notag\\
 &=-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1k\sum_{m\mid k}\mu(m)
      \Log_0H_{\chi^m}(z^k).
 \label{eq:odd-adams-collapse-before-divisors}
\end{align}
Write \(k=2^jq\), where \(q\) is odd.  The odd divisors contributing to the
inner sum have the form \(m=d\), \(d\mid q\), and
\[
 \sum_{\substack{m\mid k\\m\ {\rm odd}}}\mu(m)
 =\sum_{d\mid q}\mu(d)
 =\begin{cases}1,&q=1,\\0,&q>1.\end{cases}
\]
If \(j\ge1\), the square-free even divisors are exactly \(m=2d\), with
\(d\mid q\) odd, and
\[
 \sum_{\substack{m\mid k\\m\ {\rm even}}}\mu(m)
 =\sum_{d\mid q}\mu(2d)
 =-\sum_{d\mid q}\mu(d).
\]
For odd \(d\), condition (OA) gives
\[
 H_{\chi^d}=H_\chi,\qquad
 H_{\chi^{2d}}=H_{(\chi^2)^d}=H_{\chi^2}.
\]
Thus the inner divisor sum in
\eqref{eq:odd-adams-collapse-before-divisors} vanishes unless \(q=1\);
for \(k=1\) it is \(\Log_0H_\chi(z)\), and for \(k=2^j\), \(j\ge1\), it is
\[
 \Log_0H_\chi(z^{2^j})-\Log_0H_{\chi^2}(z^{2^j}).
\]
Substitution proves \eqref{eq:odd-adams-mobius-collapse}.  This is an
identity of analytic germs, and hence throughout the common Perron disk.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Odd-Adams-invariant finite radial determination]
\label{thm:odd-adams-abelian-two-group-finite-sampling}
Let \(G\) be a finite abelian \(2\)-group.  Let
\(\tau:E(\Gamma)\to G\) and \(\tau':E(\Gamma')\to G\) be one-step
cocycles over primitive non-negative integral base matrices \(A,A'\), whose
sizes are at most \(V\) and whose Perron roots are
\(\lambda,\lambda'>1\).  Assume that both cocycles satisfy (OA) in
\eqref{eq:odd-adams-determinant-invariance}.  Put
\[
 x=\min\{\lambda^{-1},(\lambda')^{-1}\},\qquad
 M(V)=2V\lceil\log_2(4V)\rceil.
\]
For \(g\in G\), define
\[
 \mathcal E_g^\tau(y)
 =\sum_{n\ge1}p_{n,g}(\tau)\log(1-y^n),\qquad 0<y<x,
\]
and define \(\mathcal E_g^{\tau'}\) analogously.  Suppose that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-adams-sampled-profile-equality}
 \mathcal E_g^\tau(y_i)=\mathcal E_g^{\tau'}(y_i)
 \qquad(g\in G,\ 1\le i\le M(V))
\end{equation}
at \(M(V)\) distinct radii in \((0,x)\), at least one of which is algebraic.
Then
\[
 p_{n,g}(\tau)=p_{n,g}(\tau')
 \qquad(n\ge1,\ g\in G).
\]
The number \(M(V)\) of radial locations is independent of the rank and
exponent of \(G\), while the total number of scalar equalities in
\eqref{eq:odd-adams-sampled-profile-equality} is \(\abs G M(V)\).
Neither a common base nor a strict twisted spectral gap is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All profile series converge absolutely on the stated interval by the
periodic-point estimate in \Cref{thm:finite-radial-sampling}.  Summing
\eqref{eq:odd-adams-sampled-profile-equality} over \(g\) gives
\[
 \log\det(I-y_iA)=\log\det(I-y_iA')
 \qquad(1\le i\le M(V)).
\]
The determinants are positive on \((0,x)\).  Their difference has degree at
most \(V\), vanishes at the sampled radii and at zero, and hence is zero:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-adams-base-determinant-equality}
 P_{\mathbf1,\tau}(z)=\det(I-zA)=\det(I-zA')
 =P_{\mathbf1,\tau'}(z).
\end{equation}
Thus \(H_{\mathbf1}=1\).

Fourier transformation of the sampled element-profile equalities gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-adams-sampled-fourier-equality}
 \Delta_\chi(y_i)
 =-\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)
   \bigl(\mathcal E_g^\tau(y_i)-\mathcal E_g^{\tau'}(y_i)\bigr)
 =0
\end{equation}
for every \(\chi\in\widehat G\) and every \(i\).  We prove
\(H_\chi=1\) by induction on the order of \(\chi\).  The order-one case is
\eqref{eq:odd-adams-base-determinant-equality}.  Assume the assertion for
all characters of order less than \(2^a\), and let \(\chi\) have order
\(2^a\).  Then \(H_{\chi^2}=1\), so
\Cref{lem:odd-adams-mobius-collapse} reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-adams-inductive-dyadic-orbit}
 \Delta_\chi(y)
 =-\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}\log H_\chi(y^{2^j})
 \qquad(0<y<x).
\end{equation}

By \Cref{lem:odd-adams-mobius-collapse}, \(H_\chi\in\QQ(z)^\times\).
Entrywise,
\[
 \abs{(A_{\chi,\tau})_{uv}}\le A_{uv},\qquad
 \abs{(A_{\chi,\tau'})_{uv}}\le A'_{uv}.
\]
Perron-weighted domination therefore makes both character determinants
zero-free on their open Perron disks.  Since they are real polynomials with
value one at zero, they and \(H_\chi\) are positive on \((0,x)\).

Choose an algebraic sampled radius \(y_0\).  Equations
\eqref{eq:odd-adams-sampled-fourier-equality} and
\eqref{eq:odd-adams-inductive-dyadic-orbit} give
\[
 \prod_{j\ge0}H_\chi(y_0^{2^j})^{2^{-j}}=1.
\]
If \(H_\chi\ne1\), \Cref{thm:general-rational-p-mahler-lifting} with \(p=2\)
gives the unique normalized \(R_\chi\in\QQ(z)^\times\) such that
\[
 H_\chi(z)=\frac{R_\chi(z^2)}{R_\chi(z)^2},\qquad R_\chi(y_0)=1.
\]
Write \(H_\chi=P_{0,\chi}/P_{1,\chi}\) in reduced form and put
\(D_\chi=\deg P_{0,\chi}+\deg P_{1,\chi}\).  Both determinants have degree
at most \(V\), so \(D_\chi\le2V\).  The rational telescope and
\Cref{prop:logarithmic-mahler-divisor-bound} show that every real zero of
\(\Delta_\chi\) is a non-zero root of \(R_\chi-1\), and that there are at
most
\[
 b_2(D_\chi)-1
 =D_\chi\lceil\log_2(2D_\chi)\rceil-1
 \le M(V)-1
\]
such roots.  This contradicts the \(M(V)\) distinct zeros in
\eqref{eq:odd-adams-sampled-fourier-equality}.  Hence \(H_\chi=1\), closing
the induction.

All character determinants now agree between the two systems.  The
cross-base periodic-data dictionary in \Cref{prop:comparison-dictionary},
followed by Fourier inversion on \(G\), recovers every
\(p_{n,g}\), as asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Concrete scope of (OA)]
\label{rem:odd-adams-concrete-scope}
Condition (OA) is automatic for \(G=(C_2)^r\).  A stronger entrywise
sufficient condition for a general abelian \(2\)-group is that, for every
ordered vertex pair, every \(g\in G\), and every odd \(u\), the number of
\(g\)-labelled edges equals the number of \(g^u\)-labelled edges.  It then
gives \(A_{\chi^u}=A_\chi\), not merely equality of their determinants.

For \(C_4=\langle a\rangle\), this condition says exactly that the
\(a\)- and \(a^{-1}\)-labelled edge counts agree entrywise; the \(e\)- and
\(a^2\)-counts are unrestricted.  It does not force factorization through
\(C_2\): a one-vertex graph with equally many \(a\)- and \(a^{-1}\)-loops
has primitive base and holonomy subgroup \(C_4\).  Likewise, assigning equal
counts on each odd-power orbit in \(C_{2^s}\) gives examples with genuine
holonomy of order \(2^s\).
\end{remark}

\begin{abstract}
Let \(G\) be a finite abelian \(2\)-group, and assume that the twisted
determinants of two one-step \(G\)-cocycles are invariant under every odd
Adams operation \(\chi\mapsto\chi^u\).  The cocycles lie over primitive edge
shifts whose vertex counts are at most \(V\); the bases need not be the same.
For the primitive length--element counts \(p_{n,g}\), put
\(\mathcal E_g(y)=\sum_{n\ge1}p_{n,g}\log(1-y^n)\).  Without a twisted-gap
hypothesis, equality of every element profile at
\[
 2V\lceil\log_2(4V)\rceil
\]

\(p_{n,g}\), provided that one sampled radius is algebraic.  Thus the number
of radial locations is independent of the rank and exponent of \(G\), although
each location supplies the full \(\abs G\)-component profile vector.  The
hypothesis is automatic for \(G=(C_2)^r\) and permits genuine holonomy of
orders \(4,8,\ldots\).  For a reduced binary
character-determinant ratio of
total degree \(D>0\), one algebraic collision identifies the complete real
collision set, which has at most
\(D\lceil\log_2(2D)\rceil-1\) points.

The sign-determinant ratio is canonically the ratio of the ordinary
Artin--Mazur zeta functions of the two \(C_2\)-covers.  The inverse mechanism
is arithmetic: a single algebraic interior collision forces this ratio to be
a normalized rational Mahler coboundary
\(R(z^2)/R(z)^2\).  More generally, a rational critical \(p\)-Mahler product
with one algebraic value lifts by an elementary linear-exponent denominator
estimate, Kumiko Nishioka's critical special-value theorem, and Keiji
Nishioka's cited algebraic-solution rationality result.  Determinant parity and the
Dieudonn\'e--Dwork criterion give a stronger integral refinement but are not
needed for lifting.  For the multiplicative certificate \(R=A/B\) itself,
a divisor \(\ell_1\) estimate gives the input-only bound
\(\deg A+\deg B\le b_p(D)=O_p(D\log D)\) in terms of the reduced input degree
\(D\).  An \(\Omega_p(D\log D)\) family makes this order sharp, and the same
order occurs for standard realizable \(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.  Explicit
height control and fixed-\(p\) polynomial bit complexity are further
quantitative consequences; once the degree cap is known, the displayed
Pad\'e step is rational reconstruction.  Equality of the
sampled scalar profiles first recovers the two base determinants; the scalar
lifting theorem then applies to every character of \(G\).

Perron-boundary profile vector does not suffice; the uncentered class profiles
diverge there.  More generally, two strict-gap cocycles on a common

recovery from a radial set with an interior accumulation point and identify
the obstruction to a finite bound: already for \(C_3\), Adams--M\"obius
inversion couples determinant logarithms at infinitely many arithmetically
distributed powers, outside the one-variable Mahler theorem used here.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For odd-Adams-invariant extensions by any finite abelian \(2\)-group \(G\)
over two primitive bases of at most \(V\) vertices,
\(2V\lceil\log_2(4V)\rceil\) radial locations in the common open Perron
interval determine the complete primitive length--element profile, provided
that one location is algebraic.  This radial depth is independent of the rank
and exponent of \(G\), but each location supplies a full
\(\abs G\)-component vector, for \(\abs G M(V)\) scalar comparisons in total.
The bases need not coincide.  The theorem contains the elementary
\((C_2)^r\) result and covers standard extensions with genuine holonomy of
orders \(4,8,\ldots\).  A
strict twisted gap permits centered, not raw, class profiles at a Perron
endpoint in the binary scalar theorem.  The determinant ratio governing the
test is canonically the ratio of the standard Artin--Mazur zeta functions of
the two binary covers.  The proof is effective: one collision produces a
unique rational Mahler certificate.  For every fixed \(p\ge2\), the paper's
main quantitative Mahler estimate bounds the numerator-plus-denominator degree
of this multiplicative certificate, from the reduced input degree alone, by
\(b_p(D)=O_p(D\log D)\).  Its order is sharp abstractly and on realizable
standard \(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.  The explicit height and fixed-\(p\) bit
complexity estimates are useful further consequences; the finite Pad\'e
system is rational reconstruction under the resulting cap.  The exact four-vertex
pair shows why a single centered Perron-boundary vector cannot be an inverse
invariant; every corresponding raw class profile diverges to \(-\infty\).
For every \(m\), an explicit pair on a common \(4m+2\)-vertex positive base
has \(m\) distinct rational collisions but different periodic data, giving the
realizable lower bound
\(\mathsf N_{C_2}(V)\ge\lfloor(V-2)/4\rfloor+1\).
The logarithmic certificate-degree order is also sharp on standard realizable
\(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.  These are different statements: the positive-real
sampling lower bound is \(\Omega(V)\), and no
\(\Omega(V\log V)\) collision bound is claimed.

The determinant lifting theorem is a specialization of a parity-free rational
critical \(p\)-Mahler statement.  Its coefficient recursion gives the exact
bound
\[
 (pq)^{2n-1}f_n\in\ZZ,\qquad
 \log d_h\le(2h-1)\log(pq),
\]
which is sufficient for Kumiko Nishioka's 1982 special-value theorem.
The cited algebraic-solution rationality result of Keiji Nishioka from 1985
then makes the algebraic solution rational, and normalization over \(\QQ\)
supplies the coboundary.  Determinant parity is therefore a convenient strong
sufficient condition yielding integral coefficients through
Dieudonn\'e--Dwork, not an essential lifting hypothesis.  The endpoint form
still uses the strict gap only to continue through the Perron circle.

The full Adams--M\"obius collapse is written explicitly, and induction on
character order reduces each odd-Adams-invariant channel to one dyadic
Mahler orbit.  The same mechanism does not presently yield a polynomial sample
bound for every finite group.  For \(C_3\), Adams--M\"obius inversion already couples
two character determinants at infinitely many arithmetically distributed
powers, rather than one lacunary Mahler orbit.  A general finite-group theorem
therefore requires a new special-value and effective zero theory for these
coupled Euler systems.  Generic \(C_4\) outside the odd-Adams-invariant
subclass has the analogous coupled obstruction.  In its absence, the proved
general statement remains unconditional analytic recovery from a profile set
with an interior accumulation point.

The novelty claim is correspondingly narrow.  The parity-free lifting theorem
is explicitly a combination of the two Nishioka inputs and the elementary
denominator estimate, rather than a new special-value theory.
Frobenius-class Euler products, their character expansion,
algebraic-solution rationality for rational functional equations, and the
general periodic-data dictionary are cited background.  The original Mahler
increment is the input-only \(b_p(D)\) divisor estimate for the multiplicative
certificate, its \(\Omega_p(D\log D)\) family, and the transfer of that
sharpness to realizable \(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.  The height bound and fixed-
\(p\) integer bit complexity are useful but incremental.  Bare existence and
decidability reduce, by logarithmic differentiation, to the published
additive and linear Mahler algorithms; Pad\'e reconstruction is largely
standard after a degree cap is known.  The tier-carrying result is instead the
cross-base odd-Adams-invariant abelian \(2\)-group inverse theorem with rank-
and exponent-independent radial depth, together with the realizable linear
sampling lower bound for standard \(C_2\)-covers.

The principal dynamical theorem remains an inverse theorem for the
paper-specific sampled fixed-label Euler values.  Its standard dynamical
conclusion is represented periodic-data equivalence, via the cited
determinant/periodic-data dictionary.  It does not advance conjugacy, strong
shift equivalence, cocycle cohomology, switching, Bowen--Franks or flow
equivalence, or full positivity realization.  The structural reasons are
that class counts discard marked-orbit holonomy, determinant data discard
positive matrix factorization, the relative realization lemma chooses a
large common base only after the candidate polynomials are fixed, and the
arithmetic reduction controls a scalar \(p\)-power orbit rather than the
coupled character systems required beyond the stated class.

finite group.  A primitive orbit \(\gamma\) has a well-defined conjugacy class
\([g_\gamma]\).  Write \(p_{n,C}(\tau)\) for the number of primitive
length-\(n\) orbits with class \(C\), and define its radial class Euler profile
by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-class-euler-profile}
 \mathcal E_C^\tau(y)
 :=\sum_{n\ge1}p_{n,C}(\tau)\log(1-y^n).
\end{equation}
This converges without a twisted-gap hypothesis for
\(0<y<\lambda^{-1}\), where \(\lambda\) is the Perron root of the base.
Under the strict twisted gap, every raw class profile diverges to \(-\infty\)
at \(y=\lambda^{-1}\), and no finite endpoint value of \(\mathcal E_C\) is
used.  The finite endpoint coordinate used below is the
centered profile
\[
 \mathcal E_C^{\circ}(y)
 :=\mathcal E_C(y)-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\log\det(I-yA),
 \qquad 0<y<\lambda^{-1}.
\]
Under the strict gap its radial limit exists by
\Cref{prop:centered-perron-profile}.  The inverse question of this paper is
whether finitely many open-interval values of all the raw profiles determine
every \(p_{n,C}\); endpoint statements concern only the centered profiles or
their equivalent non-trivial Fourier coordinates.

The determinant input is finite.  For each irreducible representation \(\rho\)
of \(G\), the twisted adjacency block \(A_\rho\) has size
\(v\dim\rho\), where \(v=|V(\Gamma)|\), and
\(P_\rho(z)=\det(I-zA_\rho)\).  What makes the radial inverse problem
non-trivial is that \(\mathcal E_C\) is not the logarithm of one of these
determinants.  For a class function \(\phi\), the periodic Artin logarithm is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-artin}
 L_\phi(z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal P}\sum_{r\ge1}
   \frac{\phi(g_\gamma^r)}r z^{r|\gamma|},
\end{equation}
whereas the fixed-label coordinate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-fixed}
 F_\phi(z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal P}\sum_{r\ge1}
   \frac{\phi(g_\gamma)}r z^{r|\gamma|}.
\end{equation}
Thus \(\mathcal E_C^\tau=-F_{\mathbf1_C}^\tau\).  Adams--M\"obius
inversion expresses every \(F_\phi\) through the finite determinant family,
but it does so at all powers \(z^k\).  Finite radial determination is therefore
a special-value problem, not a coefficient-counting consequence of the
determinant degrees.

\subsection{Main results}

We first state the sharper scalar estimate for binary extensions on a fixed base.  Let
\(G=C_2\), let \(B,B'\) be the two sign blocks, and reduce
\[
 H(z)=\frac{\det(I-zB)}{\det(I-zB')}=\frac{P_0(z)}{P_1(z)}
\]
with \(D=\deg P_0+\deg P_1\).  By
\Cref{prop:c2-standard-zeta-ratio}, \(H\) is the ratio of the ordinary
Artin--Mazur zeta functions of the two standard binary covers.  Without any
twisted-gap assumption, if \(H\ne1\), then
\Cref{thm:finite-radial-sampling} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-collision-bound}
 \#\{y\in\overline{\mathbb Q}\cap(0,\lambda^{-1}):
   \mathcal E_C^\tau(y)=\mathcal E_C^{\tau'}(y)
   \text{ for every }C\}
 \le D\lceil\log_2(2D)\rceil-1.
\end{equation}
Consequently equality of every class profile at any
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-universal-sample-budget}
 B(v,C_2)=2v\lceil\log_2(4v)\rceil
\end{equation}
distinct radii in the open Perron interval, at least one of which is algebraic,
forces
\(p_{n,C}(\tau)=p_{n,C}(\tau')\) for all \(n,C\).  The radii need not be
chosen adaptively.  If both sign blocks have spectral radius below
\(\lambda\), the collision and recovery statements extend to the Perron
endpoint for centered class-profile differences, not for the divergent raw
profiles.

The proof has two independent parts.  First,
\Cref{thm:determinant-boundary-lifting} shows that one algebraic interior
collision, without a spectral gap, or one endpoint collision under the gap,
forces the exact rational Mahler coboundary
\[
 H(z)=\frac{R(z^2)}{R(z)^2},\qquad R(0)=R(y)=1.
\]
The general lifting theorem does not require determinant parity.  Its
coefficient recursion gives the explicit bound
\((pq)^{2n-1}f_n\in\ZZ\), hence \(\log d_h=O(h)\) for the common
denominators; Kumiko Nishioka's special-value theorem then forces algebraicity,
and the cited 1985 result of Keiji Nishioka gives rationality.  We verify every
hypothesis of Kumiko Nishioka's printed theorem, including the critical
inequality \(p^2<p^3\).  Parity and Dieudonn\'e--Dwork remain available only
as a stronger integrality refinement.  Second,
\Cref{prop:logarithmic-mahler-divisor-bound} proves the paper's main
quantitative Mahler estimate: the reduced input degree \(D\) alone bounds
\(\deg A+\deg B\) for the multiplicative certificate \(R=A/B\) by
\(b_p(D)=O_p(D\log D)\).  This is not a bound for an additive
logarithmic-derivative certificate or for an arbitrary rational solution of a
general linear Mahler equation.  The \(\Omega_p(D\log D)\) family makes the
order sharp, and \Cref{cor:realizable-logarithmic-certificate-sharpness}
transports that sharpness to standard realizable \(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.
The supporting \Cref{thm:effective-rational-mahler-coboundary} also records
an explicit height estimate and fixed-base bit complexity, while its finite
Pad\'e step implements rational reconstruction once a degree cap is known.
The collision radii are exactly the zeros of
\(R-1\), giving
\eqref{eq:intro-collision-bound}.

The principal inverse theorem is not confined to a common base, to one binary
coordinate, or to groups of exponent two.  Let \(G\) be any finite abelian
\(2\)-group and suppose that, for each cocycle,
\[
 P_{\chi^u}(z)=P_\chi(z)
 \qquad(\chi\in\widehat G,\ u\ {\rm odd})\qquad\text{\rm(OA)}.
\]
By \Cref{thm:odd-adams-abelian-two-group-finite-sampling}, two primitive
bases of at most \(V\) vertices are determined at the level of all primitive
length--element counts by
\[
 M(V)=2V\lceil\log_2(4V)\rceil
\]
common profile samples, with one algebraic anchor.  Summing the element
profiles first recovers the base determinant.  The new arithmetic identity is
the complete Adams--M\"obius collapse
\[
 \Delta_\chi(z)
 =-\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}\log H_\chi(z^{2^j})
  +\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}\log H_{\chi^2}(z^{2^j}).
\]
Induction on the order of \(\chi\) then applies the scalar lifting and zero
bound.  One observation at a radius means the entire \(G\)-valued vector
\((\mathcal E_g(y))_{g\in G}\).  Hence the theorem uses \(M(V)\)
radial locations independently of the rank and exponent of \(G\), but it
compares \(\abs G M(V)\) scalar profile values.  Condition (OA) is automatic
for \(G=(C_2)^r\); for \(C_4=\langle a\rangle\), equal \(a\)- and
\(a^{-1}\)-edge counts give a concrete sufficient condition while retaining
genuine order-four holonomy.

One centered radius cannot suffice.  \Cref{thm:c2-boundary-collision} gives
two strict-gap labellings of the same four-vertex graph with different
length-one class counts but identical centered class Euler profiles at the
Perron radius.  Their raw class profiles both diverge there.  The centered
identity is exact and comes from
\(Q(z^2)/Q(z)^2\), where \(Q(z)=1-z+4z^2\) and \(Q(1/4)=1\).

The realizable lower bound grows linearly.
\Cref{thm:realizable-linear-multicollisions} constructs, for every \(m\ge1\),
two strict-gap cocycles on the same positive \(4m+2\)-vertex base with
different periodic data and equality of both element profiles at \(m\)
distinct rational radii.  Hence
\[
 \mathsf N_{C_2}(V)\ge
 \left\lfloor\frac{V-2}{4}\right\rfloor+1.
\]
This is a genuine \(\Omega(V)\) sampling lower bound on standard
Artin--Mazur zeta ratios.  Separately,
\Cref{cor:realizable-logarithmic-certificate-sharpness} realizes the abstract
\(\Omega(D\log D)\) certificate-degree family on standard covers.  The latter
does not supply \(\Omega(V\log V)\) positive real collisions, and no such
sampling lower bound is claimed.

For an arbitrary finite group, \Cref{thm:radial-profile-recovery} proves,
again without a twisted-gap hypothesis, recovery from a set with an interior
accumulation point.  We do not claim a
finite polynomial sample bound in that generality.  The precise obstruction
to extending the present method is recorded after
\Cref{thm:finite-radial-sampling}: already for \(C_3\), the
Adams--M\"obius coefficient is non-zero at infinitely many prime powers and
does not reduce to a one-variable Mahler orbit.  The missing input is a
special-value and effective zero theorem for that coupled cyclotomic Euler
system.  The same obstruction remains for a generic \(C_4\)-cocycle outside
(OA).  Thus the abelian \(2\)-group theorem identifies the odd-Adams-invariant
subclass handled by the one-variable method; it does not assert an unrestricted
finite-group theorem.

\subsection{Prior work and scope}

The Frobenius-class objects used here have a direct dynamical lineage.
Parry--Pollicott prove a Chebotarev asymptotic for primitive closed orbits in a
prescribed Frobenius conjugacy class in finite Galois covers of Axiom~A flows,
and Noorani--Parry prove the finite homogeneous-extension analogue for shifts
\cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev,NooraniParry1992ChebotarevShifts}.
Mohamed--Noorani then prove a class-restricted Mertens product for finite-group
extensions of SFTs: their Theorem~1 has the product
\[
 \prod_{\substack{|\gamma|\le N\\g_\gamma\in C}}
 (1-e^{-h|\gamma|})
\]
with exponent \(|C|/|G|\) and a constant expressed through Artin
\(L\)-values \cite[Theorem~1, pp.~124--125]
{MeslizaNoorani1999FrobeniusMertens}.  Noorani's later toral paper instead
classifies the lift-degree partitions of closed orbits in a finite torsion
cover and gives asymptotic counts for each realizable partition
\cite{Noorani2003ClosedOrbitsToral}.  These are orbit-distribution and product
results, not finite-special-value inverse theorems.

At the formula level, the standard Artin function in that literature has
\[
 \log L(z,\phi)
 =\sum_{\gamma}\sum_{m\ge1}
   \frac{\phi(g_\gamma^m)}m z^{m|\gamma|}
 =L_\phi(z),
\]
whereas taking the logarithm of a product whose membership condition remains
\([g_\gamma]=C\) gives
\[
 \sum_{[g_\gamma]=C}\log(1-z^{|\gamma|})
 =-F_{\mathbf1_C}(z),\qquad
 F_\phi(z)=\sum_{\gamma}\sum_{m\ge1}
   \frac{\phi(g_\gamma)}m z^{m|\gamma|}.
\]
Thus class-restricted Euler products, character orthogonality, and the
\(\Pi_\phi,L_\phi\) coordinates are prior art.  The fixed-label transform
\(F_\phi\) is already implicit in the logarithm of the 1999 product.  What is
specific here is that \(F_\phi\), rather than \(L_\phi\), is passed through the
Adams--M\"obius formula \eqref{eq:fixed-adams-mobius} and then used in a
finite-special-value inverse problem.

The algebraic-solution rationality step is also prior.  Ostrowski treats the
linear multiplicative equation
\(\Phi(\varphi(z))=g(z)\Phi(z)\) for rational data
\cite{Ostrowski1968AlgebraicSolutions}; that equation does not directly cover
the square in \(F(z^2)=H(z)^{-1}F(z)^2\).  Kumiko Nishioka's
Theorem~5.1.7 is the standard reference for the rational--transcendental
dichotomy for \(k\)-Mahler functions defined by a linear functional equation
\cite[Theorem~5.1.7]{Nishioka1996MahlerFunctions}; see also the modern
restatement and proof in \cite[Corollary~8]{BellCoonsRowland2013MahlerDichotomy}.
Our equation is quadratic in \(F\) and therefore outside that linear class;
The cited nonlinear class of Keiji Nishioka,
\(f(z^p)=\mathscr R(z,f(z))\), is the applicable result.  We use that 1985
result in the following one-variable form: if
\(f(z^p)=\mathscr R(z,f(z))\), with \(\mathscr R\) rational and \(f\)
algebraic over \(\CC(z)\), then \(f\in\CC(z)\)
\cite{Nishioka1985AlgebraicSolutions}.  Taking
\(p=2\) and \(\mathscr R(z,Y)=Y^2/H(z)\) proves the rationality used in
\Cref{prop:algebraic-mahler-coboundary}; that rationality is not claimed as a
contribution of this paper.

The decision problem does reduce to published additive and linear Mahler
algorithms.  Write \(H=P_0/P_1\), let \(\sigma f(z)=f(z^p)\), and, for a
prospective rational solution, put \(u=zR'/R\).  Logarithmic differentiation
gives the exact reduction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-logarithmic-derivative-reduction}
 (\sigma-1)u=\frac{z}{p}\frac{H'}H.
\end{equation}
Arreche--Zhang give a complete effective obstruction and certificate
algorithm for precisely \((\sigma-1)u=f\)
\cite{ArrecheZhang2022MahlerResidues}; the rational-solution algorithm of
Chyzak--Dreyfus--Dumas--Mezzarobba also applies after homogenization
\cite{ChyzakDreyfusDumasMezzarobba2018MahlerSolutions}.  Explicitly, if
\(f\ne0\), then
\((\sigma-(\sigma f)/f)(\sigma-1)u=0\), and one filters the computed rational
solution space by \((\sigma-1)u=f\).  Solutions of the additive equation are
determined only up to a constant, since the rational \(\sigma\)-invariants are
constants.  After replacing an additive certificate by its unique
representative satisfying \(u(0)=0\), it comes from a normalized rational
\(R\) exactly when \(u/z\) has no polynomial part, is regular at zero, and
has only simple finite poles with integer residues.  In that case the
product of the corresponding rational irreducible factors, normalized at
zero, has logarithmic derivative \(u/z\); the quotient
\(R(z^p)/(H(z)R(z)^p)\) has zero logarithmic derivative and equals one at
zero.  This proves the converse and the reduction, rather than merely a
formal resemblance.

The Riccati equations for first-order factors instead contain products of
successive shifts
\cite{ChyzakDreyfusDumasMezzarobba2025FirstOrderFactors}, and Pegis treats a
different constant-coefficient nonlinear equation
\cite{Pegis1996NonlinearMahler}.  Thus bare existence and decidability are not
claimed here.

The Mahler package has three levels.  \emph{The original quantitative core}
is the input-only divisor estimate for the multiplicative certificate itself,
not for \(u\) and not for a general linear Mahler solution, together with its
\(\Omega_p(D\log D)\) lower-bound family and the realizable transfer in
\Cref{cor:realizable-logarithmic-certificate-sharpness}.  \emph{The useful
incremental layer} is the explicit height control and resulting fixed-\(p\)
integer bit-complexity estimate.  Chyzak--Dreyfus--Dumas--Mezzarobba compute
all rational solutions of a general linear Mahler equation, with their
complexity measured primarily in arithmetic operations over the coefficient
field \cite{ChyzakDreyfusDumasMezzarobba2018MahlerSolutions}; that is distinct
from, but not displaced by, the integer coefficient-height analysis here.
\emph{The prior or standard layer} consists of bare existence and decidability
through the cited additive and linear algorithms, and the affine Pad\'e step,
which is largely standard rational reconstruction once a degree bound is
available.  Exact substitution into the cleared identity remains necessary
for correctness, but is not asserted as a separate conceptual contribution.

Accordingly, the parity-free special-value interface is not presented as a
standalone novelty claim: it is the combination of Kumiko Nishioka's
special-value theorem, the cited algebraic-solution rationality result of
Keiji Nishioka, and the elementary denominator estimate above.  The surviving
contribution is parity-compatible algebraic collision lifting in the
dynamical setting, the sharp input-only multiplicative-certificate divisor
bound with its height and bit-complexity consequences, and cross-base finite
radial determination for odd-Adams-invariant finite abelian \(2\)-groups,
together with realizable certificate-degree sharpness and the linear sampling
lower bound on standard \(C_2\)-covers.  The
class Euler products, their character expansion, the general periodic-data
dictionary, both Nishioka inputs, and the analytic identity-theorem recovery
statement are background rather than novelty claims.  Boyle--Schmieding's computable periodic invariants
\cite{BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions} and represented
cospectrality and switching in gain-graph theory
\cite{Zaslavsky1989BiasedGraphs,CavaleriDAngeliDonno2021BalanceGainGraphs,
CavaleriDonno2022Cospectrality} delimit the dynamical side further.

Three printed open-problem interfaces delimit the claim.  Boyle and
Schmieding ask to ``characterize the polynomials \(\det(I-tA)\) arising from
\(G\)-primitive matrices'' and to ``characterize the trace series \(T_A\) and
conjugate trace series \(\kappa T_A\)''
\cite[Realization Problems~6.1(2)--(3)]{BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions}.
Our theorem starts with two realized extensions, so its determinant and trace
formulas do not solve either positivity-realization problem.  Chyzak,
Dreyfus, Dumas, and Mezzarobba leave open polynomial-time solution of general
linear Mahler equations for all order--degree regimes
\cite[Sec.~4.3]{ChyzakDreyfusDumasMezzarobba2018MahlerSolutions}; our fixed-
\(p\) Pad\'e criterion treats one normalized multiplicative coboundary equation
and does not settle that all-parameter complexity problem.  O'Hare asks for finite-data rigidity for
unimodal and Markov maps \cite[Introduction]{OHare2025FiniteDataRigidity}.
The present theorem is an exact symbolic result for orbit-class Euler data,
not a derivative-data rigidity theorem for those maps.

The separately submitted Supplementary Material contains the Frobenius-class Mertens correction from which the profiles arose, its
quotient-cover refinement, and the exact \(S_3\) witness are now separated
from the inverse argument.  This paper uses only
the fixed-label coordinate and does not claim that the underlying Mertens
product or Frobenius equidistribution is new.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For odd-Adams-invariant extensions by any finite abelian \(2\)-group \(G\)
over two primitive bases of at most \(V\) vertices,
\(2V\lceil\log_2(4V)\rceil\) radial locations in the common open Perron
interval determine the complete primitive length--element profile, provided
that one location is algebraic.  This radial depth is independent of the rank
and exponent of \(G\), but each location supplies a full
\(\abs G\)-component vector, for \(\abs G M(V)\) scalar comparisons in total.
The bases need not coincide.  The theorem contains the elementary
\((C_2)^r\) result and covers standard extensions with genuine holonomy of
orders \(4,8,\ldots\).  A
strict twisted gap permits centered, not raw, class profiles at a Perron
endpoint in the binary scalar theorem.  The determinant ratio governing the
test is canonically the ratio of the standard Artin--Mazur zeta functions of
the two binary covers.  The proof is effective: one collision produces a
unique rational Mahler certificate.  For every fixed \(p\ge2\), the paper's
main quantitative Mahler estimate bounds the numerator-plus-denominator degree
of this multiplicative certificate, from the reduced input degree alone, by
\(b_p(D)=O_p(D\log D)\).  Its order is sharp abstractly and on realizable
standard \(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.  The explicit height and fixed-\(p\) bit
complexity estimates are useful further consequences; the finite Pad\'e
system is rational reconstruction under the resulting cap.  The exact four-vertex
pair shows why a single centered Perron-boundary vector cannot be an inverse
invariant; every corresponding raw class profile diverges to \(-\infty\).
For every \(m\), an explicit pair on a common \(4m+2\)-vertex positive base
has \(m\) distinct rational collisions but different periodic data, giving the
realizable lower bound
\(\mathsf N_{C_2}(V)\ge\lfloor(V-2)/4\rfloor+1\).
The logarithmic certificate-degree order is also sharp on standard realizable
\(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.  These are different statements: the positive-real
sampling lower bound is \(\Omega(V)\), and no
\(\Omega(V\log V)\) collision bound is claimed.

The determinant lifting theorem is a specialization of a parity-free rational
critical \(p\)-Mahler statement.  Its coefficient recursion gives the exact
bound
\[
 (pq)^{2n-1}f_n\in\ZZ,\qquad
 \log d_h\le(2h-1)\log(pq),
\]
which is sufficient for Kumiko Nishioka's 1982 special-value theorem.
The cited algebraic-solution rationality result of Keiji Nishioka from 1985
then makes the algebraic solution rational, and normalization over \(\QQ\)
supplies the coboundary.  Determinant parity is therefore a convenient strong
sufficient condition yielding integral coefficients through
Dieudonn\'e--Dwork, not an essential lifting hypothesis.  The endpoint form
still uses the strict gap only to continue through the Perron circle.

The full Adams--M\"obius collapse is written explicitly, and induction on
character order reduces each odd-Adams-invariant channel to one dyadic
Mahler orbit.  The same mechanism does not presently yield a polynomial sample
bound for every finite group.  For \(C_3\), Adams--M\"obius inversion already couples
two character determinants at infinitely many arithmetically distributed
powers, rather than one lacunary Mahler orbit.  A general finite-group theorem
therefore requires a new special-value and effective zero theory for these
coupled Euler systems.  Generic \(C_4\) outside the odd-Adams-invariant
subclass has the analogous coupled obstruction.  In its absence, the proved
general statement remains unconditional analytic recovery from a profile set
with an interior accumulation point.

The novelty claim is correspondingly narrow.  The parity-free lifting theorem
is explicitly a combination of the two Nishioka inputs and the elementary
denominator estimate, rather than a new special-value theory.
Frobenius-class Euler products, their character expansion,
algebraic-solution rationality for rational functional equations, and the
general periodic-data dictionary are cited background.  The original Mahler
increment is the input-only \(b_p(D)\) divisor estimate for the multiplicative
certificate, its \(\Omega_p(D\log D)\) family, and the transfer of that
sharpness to realizable \(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.  The height bound and fixed-
\(p\) integer bit complexity are useful but incremental.  Bare existence and
decidability reduce, by logarithmic differentiation, to the published
additive and linear Mahler algorithms; Pad\'e reconstruction is largely
standard after a degree cap is known.  The tier-carrying result is instead the
cross-base odd-Adams-invariant abelian \(2\)-group inverse theorem with rank-
and exponent-independent radial depth, together with the realizable linear
sampling lower bound for standard \(C_2\)-covers.

The principal dynamical theorem remains an inverse theorem for the
paper-specific sampled fixed-label Euler values.  Its standard dynamical
conclusion is represented periodic-data equivalence, via the cited
determinant/periodic-data dictionary.  It does not advance conjugacy, strong
shift equivalence, cocycle cohomology, switching, Bowen--Franks or flow
equivalence, or full positivity realization.  The structural reasons are
that class counts discard marked-orbit holonomy, determinant data discard
positive matrix factorization, the relative realization lemma chooses a
large common base only after the candidate polynomials are fixed, and the
arithmetic reduction controls a scalar \(p\)-power orbit rather than the
coupled character systems required beyond the stated class.
