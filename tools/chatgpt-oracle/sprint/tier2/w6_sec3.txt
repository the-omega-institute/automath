=== PART 1: TWO REAL DEFECTS IN SECTION 3 ===

1. LEMMA 3.1 IS FALSE AS PRINTED. It says a matrix P satisfies the residual bound if and only if the
   corresponding intervals have a common point. For a fixed P the existence of SOME common point is
   not sufficient: the common point must be the particular entry P(x,y). The proof starts with the
   fixed entry and then silently switches to an existential interval problem. Replace with whichever
   of the two correct formulations the downstream use needs:
     (a) there EXISTS P with the bound iff every interval family has nonempty intersection; or
     (b) for a FIXED P, the bound holds iff P(x,y) lies in the intersection over omega in the fibre
         of x of the intervals centred at c_omega(y)/m with radius eps, for every x and y.
   Check which one the infimum formula that follows actually needs, and make the proof match.

2. PROPOSITION 3.2 IS MISSING THE STOCHASTIC UPPER BOUND. Lemma 3.1 gives only that restricting to
   stochastic rows cannot lower the infimum; the proposition then asserts both infima equal 1/6 while
   proving the unrestricted upper bound and the stochastic lower bound.

   The repair, which I verified numerically from the printed fibre table, is: at eps = 1/6 set
     L_xy = max(0, (max_omega c_omega(y) - 1)/6),   U_xy = min(1, (min_omega c_omega(y) + 1)/6),
   and check row by row that the sum over y of L_xy is at most 1 and the sum over y of U_xy is at
   least 1; a stochastic row can then be chosen inside the coordinate box. My computation confirms
   this for all 21 rows, with the uniform bounds
     max over x of sum_y L_xy = 1/2,     min over x of sum_y U_xy = 7/2.
   Both match the referee independently. As a control, at eps = 0 the same test fails for 17 of the
   21 rows, so the criterion discriminates and is not vacuous. Print the uniform bounds; they are
   stronger and shorter than a row-by-row table.
