\begin{abstract}
We prove local asymptotic equivalence, in Le Cam distance, between a
deterministic window of a stationary lattice renewal indicator and an
undershot fixed-size independent sample from its Palm interarrival law.  The
comparison is two-sided and is implemented by explicit parameter-free Markov
kernels.  It transfers bounded-loss risks, yields Palm information divided by
mean cycle length as information per unit calendar time, and gives the
corresponding semiparametric efficiency bound, including after restriction to
fixed local cones.

We apply this result to one isolated real double pole in a sampled serial
generalized-Erlang renewal model.  The serial order is fixed, the sampling
interval is known, all other rates are simple and uniformly separated, and
the observation uses the serial positive killed-reset realization.  The
regular collision coordinate is the squared half-separation
\(\delta=\{(\theta_1-\theta_2)/2\}^2\).  A pole-order argument proves that its
score is not absorbed by the nuisance scores: the collision, centre, and
separated-rate derivatives have respectively fourth-, third-, and second-order
poles.  The stationary experiment is uniformly locally asymptotically normal
and converges to a Gaussian shift on
\(\mathbb R^{n-1}\times[0,\infty)\).  The residualized score test attains the
one-sided local power envelope.  A measurable recurrence estimator attains
\(N^{-1/4}\) recovery of the unordered colliding rates, and a fixed-base
two-point threshold lower bound proves optimality in rate.  No exact continuum
minimax constant and no extension to multiple collisions, unknown order,
unknown sampling interval, or nonserial phase-type representations is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A stationary renewal record observed through a deterministic calendar window
does not consist of a deterministic number of complete cycles.  The first and
last cycles are censored, the number of intervening cycles is random, and a
window may contain no renewal.  These features are harmless for many
first-order calculations, but they matter when an i.i.d. Palm argument is used
as a substitute for the actual experiment.  Conditioning a prescribed number
of cycles to fit in the window is not legitimate: it changes their joint law.

Our first result removes that boundary-cycle problem at the level of
statistical experiments.  Given a stationary renewal window of length \(N\),
one kernel extracts the first
\(m_N=\lfloor N/\mu_0-N^\gamma\rfloor\) complete interarrivals, where
\(1/2<\gamma<1\).  In the reverse direction, a kernel uses the supplied Palm
sample and appends base-law cycles after it is exhausted.  The two couplings
fail only if the record contains too few complete cycles, or if generation in
the reverse direction needs more than the supplied cycles plus a short
base-law continuation.  Hellinger locality controls that continuation.  Both
deficiency directions therefore vanish uniformly, without conditioning Palm
gaps on fitting inside the window.

The equivalence theorem is useful beyond a parametric model.  Bounded-loss
procedures transfer in both directions, information per calendar unit is Palm
information divided by mean cycle length, and an i.i.d. canonical-gradient
variance is multiplied by mean cycle length.  The same statements remain
valid for a fixed local cone or constrained local subset, with efficiency and
minimax bounds interpreted in that restricted experiment.  This qualification
is essential at a singular boundary.

Our application is the collision of two rates in a sampled serial
generalized-Erlang renewal law.  If independent exponential holding times have
rates \(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n\), their sum is sampled at a known interval
\(\Delta\tau\).  At \(\theta_1=\theta_2=\lambda\), the sign of the rate split
is invisible.  The smooth quotient coordinate is
\[
 c=\frac{\theta_1+\theta_2}{2},\qquad
 \delta=\left(\frac{\theta_1-\theta_2}{2}\right)^2\ge0.
\]
Root-\(N\) inference for \(\delta\) therefore becomes fourth-root recovery of
the unordered pair \(\{\!\{c-\sqrt\delta,c+\sqrt\delta\}\!\}\).

Three ingredients make the singular conclusion statistically complete.
First, the sampled survival tail is a confluent exponential-polynomial
sequence whose minimal recurrence and nonsingular leading Hankel matrix give
regular finite-dimensional quotient coordinates even at collision.  Second,
the collision derivative has an exact fourth-order pole, whereas the centre
derivative has order three and each separated-rate derivative has its own
order-two pole.  After sampling, their distinguishing components are
\(k^3e^{-\lambda\Delta\tau k}\),
\(k^2e^{-\lambda\Delta\tau k}\), and
\(ke^{-\theta_j\Delta\tau k}\).  This proves positive information rather

recurrence estimator estimates the regular cluster coordinates at the
root-\(N\) rate; projection of \(\delta\) onto \([0,\infty)\) and a square
root yield the attainable \(N^{-1/4}\) multiset rate.  A two-point threshold
argument supplies the matching lower rate.

The order of the results reflects this logic.  \Cref{thm:stationary-renewal-palm-equivalence-main}

\section{Local equivalence of stationary renewal windows and Palm samples}
\label{sec:renewal-equivalence-main}

The observation in this section is a stationary lattice renewal indicator,
not interval counts from a point process that may have several arrivals in
one bin.  This is the standard equilibrium-renewal experiment: the renewal
epochs themselves are observed on the lattice.  Gill--Keiding compare the
distinct censoring patterns arising under stationary renewal sampling
\cite{GillKeiding2010RenewalSampling}.  In continuous time,
Zhao--Nagaraja give the corresponding window-censored endpoint/complete-gap
likelihood and show that Fisher information per unit window converges to Palm
interarrival information divided by the mean interarrival time
\cite{ZhaoNagaraja2011WindowCensoredRenewal}.  The result below strengthens
that information-level comparison to two-sided local experiment equivalence:
it removes the
deterministic-window censoring and random-cycle-count complications at local
statistical resolution by comparing the entire experiment with a fixed-size
Palm sample.

For probability measures on a countable space, use
\[
 H^2(P,Q)=\sum_x\{\sqrt{P(x)}-\sqrt{Q(x)}\}^2,
 \qquad \|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}=\frac12\sum_x|P(x)-Q(x)|.
\tag{LE1}
\]
Thus \(\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}\le H(P,Q)\).  Write
\(\delta(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)\) for deficiency from experiment
\(\mathcal E\) to experiment \(\mathcal F\), and
\(\Delta(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)\) for the maximum of the two deficiencies.

\begin{theorem}[Local equivalence of a stationary renewal window and Palm interarrivals]
\label{thm:stationary-renewal-palm-equivalence-main}
Let \(p_0\) be a probability mass function on
\(\mathbb N_+=\{1,2,\ldots\}\), with finite mean
\[
 \mu_0=\sum_{d\ge1}d p_0(d).
\]
For any mass function \(p\) on \(\mathbb N_+\), put
\[
 \mu(p)=\sum_{d\ge1}d p(d),\qquad
 e_p(a)=\frac{\Pr_p(D>a)}{\mu(p)},\quad a\in\mathbb N_0,
\tag{LE2}
\]
so \(e_p\) is the equilibrium forward-recurrence, or residual-life,
distribution.  For every \(N\), let \(\mathcal P_N\) be a class of
interarrival masses containing \(p_0\).  Suppose constants \(c,C>0\), not
depending on \(N\), satisfy
\[
 \sup_N\sup_{p\in\mathcal P_N}\EE_p e^{cD}\le C,
 \qquad
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal P_N}|\mu(p)-\mu_0|\le C N^{-1/2},
\tag{LE3}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal P_N}H^2(p,p_0)\le \frac C N,
 \qquad
 \sup_{p\in\mathcal P_N}H^2(e_p,e_{p_0})\le \frac C N.
\tag{LE4}
\]
Let \(\mathsf P_{N,p}\) be the law of the equilibrium renewal indicator
\[
 (X_0,\ldots,X_{N-1}),\qquad
 X_t=\mathbf1_{\{t\text{ is a renewal epoch}\}},
\tag{LE5}
\]
on the deterministic lattice window \(\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\).  Fix
\(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), and, for all sufficiently large \(N\), define
\[
 m_N=\left\lfloor\frac N{\mu_0}-N^\gamma\right\rfloor,
 \qquad \mathsf Q_{N,p}=p^{\otimes m_N}.
\tag{LE6}
\]
For the local experiments
\[
 \mathcal E_N=(\{0,1\}^N,\{\mathsf P_{N,p}:p\in\mathcal P_N\}),
 \qquad
 \mathcal F_N=(\mathbb N_+^{m_N},
               \{\mathsf Q_{N,p}:p\in\mathcal P_N\}),
\]
one has
\[
 \Delta(\mathcal E_N,\mathcal F_N)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{LE7}
\]
The convergence is uniform over \(p\in\mathcal P_N\).  More precisely, if
\(K_N\) is the number of complete interarrivals between observed renewal
epochs and
\[
 r_N=\lceil2N^\gamma\rceil,
\tag{LE8}
\]
then the kernels constructed in the proof satisfy
\[
 \delta(\mathcal E_N,\mathcal F_N)
 \le \sup_{p\in\mathcal P_N}
       \mathsf P_{N,p}\{K_N<m_N\}=o(1),
\tag{LE9}
\]
and
\[
 \delta(\mathcal F_N,\mathcal E_N)
 \le \sup_{p\in\mathcal P_N}
       \Pr_p\{L_N>m_N+r_N\}
       +\sqrt{\frac{C(1+r_N)}N}=o(1).
\tag{LE10}
\]
Here \(L_N\) is the number of interarrivals after the first renewal needed
to cross the right boundary in the equilibrium construction in the proof.
The randomizations in \textup{(LE9)}--\textup{(LE10)} may use
\(p_0,N,\gamma\), but not the unknown \(p\).

Consequently, bounded-loss procedures transfer in both directions with
uniformly vanishing risk discrepancy, and the experiments have the same
local asymptotic minimax risks for bounded losses.  For every finite-
dimensional uniformly differentiable-in-quadratic-mean submodel through
\(p_0=p_{\theta_0}\) whose bounded root-\(N\) local arrays lie in
\(\mathcal P_N\), the information per unit calendar time is
\[
 I_{\rm cal}(\theta_0)
 =\frac1{\mu_0}I_{\rm Palm}(\theta_0).
\tag{LE11}
\]
If a pathwise differentiable scalar functional of \(p\) has i.i.d.
canonical gradient \(\varphi\in L_0^2(p_0)\), its efficiency bound under
\(\sqrt N\)-normalization of the stationary record is
\[
 \mu_0\EE_{p_0}\{\varphi(D)^2\},
\tag{LE12}
\]
with \(\mu_0\EE_{p_0}\{\varphi(D)\varphi(D)^\top\}\) for a vector-valued
functional.  The equivalence and bounded-loss risk-transfer conclusions remain
valid after restriction to a fixed cone or other fixed local subset of
\(\mathcal P_N\).  The information matrix remains that of the ambient DQM
family, while information and efficiency consequences are interpreted in the
corresponding restricted local experiment; at a boundary or in a tangent cone,
the relevant efficiency or minimax bound can therefore be a constrained-
experiment bound rather than the ordinary unconstrained canonical-gradient
variance.  No global adaptive equivalence over an unknown compact set of
centres is asserted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
An equilibrium renewal indicator can be generated by drawing
\(A\sim e_p\), independently drawing \(D_1,D_2,\ldots\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}p\),
and placing renewals at
\[
 A,\quad A+D_1,\quad A+D_1+D_2,\ldots .
\tag{LE13}
\]
Only the nonnegative part of this construction is needed for the observed
window.  It gives the forward recurrence from the left boundary, the
complete Palm interarrivals, and the backward recurrence, or age, at the
right boundary.  On a record with at least one renewal it is the generative
form of the stationary factorization \textup{(SL4)}.  The all-zero record is
the event \(A>N-1\) and has its separate probability; it is not represented
by that product formula.

The common exponential moment gives uniform second moments and a common
exponential tail for \(A\).  The ordinary renewal-count comparison used in
\Cref*{lem:uniform-stopped-renewal-score-main} of the supplementary material therefore applies without a
parametric chart: Kolmogorov's maximal inequality, with constants controlled
by the common second moment, and the equilibrium-delay tail give
\[
 K_N=\frac N{\mu(p)}+O_{\Pr_p}(\sqrt N),
 \qquad
 L_N=\frac N{\mu(p)}+O_{\Pr_p}(\sqrt N)
\tag{LE14}
\]
uniformly over \(p\in\mathcal P_N\).  In \textup{(LE14)}, uniform
\(O_P\) means that the tail bound is uniform in both \(N\) and \(p\).
The local mean condition gives
\[
 \frac N{\mu(p)}=\frac N{\mu_0}+O(\sqrt N)
\tag{LE15}
\]
uniformly.  Since \(N^\gamma/\sqrt N\to\infty\),
\textup{(LE14)}--\textup{(LE15)} imply
\[
 \sup_p\Pr_p(K_N<m_N)\to0,
 \qquad
 \sup_p\Pr_p(L_N>m_N+r_N)\to0.
\tag{LE16}
\]

We first construct a kernel \(R_N\) from a record to an i.i.d. sample.
Order the observed renewal sites as
\(0\le C_1<\cdots<C_J<N\).  If \(J-1\ge m_N\), output
\[
 (C_2-C_1,\ldots,C_{m_N+1}-C_{m_N}).
\tag{LE17}
\]
Otherwise output an independent draw from \(p_0^{\otimes m_N}\).  This is a
Markov kernel on the entire finite record space.  Couple the record to the
unconditioned construction \textup{(LE13)} and couple the desired Palm
sample to \((D_1,\ldots,D_{m_N})\).  On \(\{K_N\ge m_N\}\), the output in
\textup{(LE17)} equals that Palm sample.  Hence the coupling inequality gives
\textup{(LE9)}.

This argument does not condition the first \(m_N\) cycles on fitting in the
window.  Such conditioning would generally change their joint law.  Instead,
the kernel and the unconditioned Palm sample agree on a common probability
space except on \(\{K_N<m_N\}\).  The undershoot \(N^\gamma\), with
\(\gamma>1/2\), makes that exceptional event uniformly negligible and is the
step that removes the boundary-cycle conditioning problem.

For the reverse direction, define a kernel \(S_N\) on every input
\(d=(d_1,\ldots,d_{m_N})\) as follows.  Independently draw
\(A_0\sim e_{p_0}\).  Starting with a renewal at \(A_0\), use the supplied
interarrivals \(d_1,\ldots,d_{m_N}\), then append independent
\(p_0\)-distributed interarrivals until the first renewal strictly to the
right of \(N-1\), and return the resulting binary indicator on the window.
If \(A_0>N-1\), return the all-zero record.  Positivity of the interarrivals
makes the procedure terminate after finitely many steps, and its definition
by countable sums and threshold comparisons makes it a Borel Markov kernel.

To compare its output with \(\mathsf P_{N,p}\), use the same first \(m_N\)
\(p\)-distributed interarrivals in both constructions.  Couple the desired
initial delay \(e_p\) and the next \(r_N\) desired \(p\)-interarrivals to
the kernel's \(e_{p_0}\) delay and \(p_0\)-interarrivals.  Hellinger
tensorization gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 H^2(e_p\otimes p^{\otimes r_N},
     e_{p_0}\otimes p_0^{\otimes r_N})
 &\le H^2(e_p,e_{p_0})+r_NH^2(p,p_0)\\
 &\le \frac{C(1+r_N)}N.
\end{aligned}
\tag{LE18}
\]
On the event that this coupling agrees and \(L_N\le m_N+r_N\), the two
binary records are identical.  Total variation is bounded by Hellinger
distance under convention \textup{(LE1)}, so the coupling inequality and
\textup{(LE18)} prove \textup{(LE10)}.  Since
\(r_N=O(N^\gamma)\) and \(\gamma<1\), its Hellinger term tends to zero.
Both kernels are thus fully uniform in the unknown \(p\), proving
\textup{(LE7)}.

The bounded-loss and local-minimax conclusions are the standard risk-transfer
consequences of deficiency \cite{LeCamYang2000}.  In a DQM submodel,
\(m_N/N\to1/\mu_0\), so the i.i.d. Palm information, when normalized by
calendar length \(N\), gives \textup{(LE11)}.  The same sample-size
conversion multiplies an i.i.d. canonical-gradient variance by \(\mu_0\),
which proves \textup{(LE12)} and its covariance version.  Restricting the
parameter set can only decrease both suprema in the deficiency bounds.
Thus equivalence and bounded-loss risk transfer survive restriction.  The
ambient DQM information matrix is unchanged, but efficiency and minimax bounds
must then be read in the corresponding restricted local experiment.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite serial coordinates at collision}

The next proposition retains only the serial tail facts needed for the

\begin{proposition}[Confluent sampled-tail coordinates]
\label{prop:serial-tail-coordinates-main}
Fix \(n\ge2\), known \(\Delta\tau>0\), and positive rates
\(\theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n)\), with repetitions allowed.  If the
distinct rates are \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\), with multiplicities
\(m_1,\ldots,m_r\), and \(z_j=e^{-\lambda_j\Delta\tau}\), then the sampled
survival tail in \textup{(1.1)} has the confluent exponential-polynomial form
\[
 S_\theta(k)=\sum_{j=1}^r\sum_{\ell=0}^{m_j-1}
 c_{j\ell}k^\ell z_j^k,\qquad c_{j,m_j-1}\ne0.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Its minimal recurrence polynomial is
\[
 \chi_\theta(x)=\prod_{j=1}^r(x-z_j)^{m_j}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
The leading \(n\times n\) Hankel matrix
\((S_\theta(i+j))_{0\le i,j<n}\) is invertible.  Consequently
\(S_\theta(0),\ldots,S_\theta(2n-1)\) determine \(\chi_\theta\), the
collision multiplicities, and the unordered sampled-rate multiset
\(\{\!\{z_1,\ldots,z_1,\ldots,z_r,\ldots,z_r\}\!\}\); known
\(\Delta\tau\) then recovers the unordered rate multiset.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Laplace transform of \(W=X_1+\cdots+X_n\) and that of its survival
function are
\[
 \EE e^{-sW}=\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{\theta_i}{s+\theta_i},\qquad
 \int_0^\infty e^{-st}\PP(W>t)\,dt
 =\frac{1-\prod_{i=1}^n\theta_i/(s+\theta_i)}s.
\tag{2.3}
\]
The second transform has at \(-\lambda_j\) a pole of exact order \(m_j\).
Partial-fraction inversion and sampling at \(t=k\Delta\tau\) give
\textup{(2.1)} with nonzero top confluent coefficient.  Standard confluent
Prony algebra gives the exact minimal annihilator \textup{(2.2)}
\cite{KunisRoemerVonDerOhe2020Prony}.

It remains to justify the finite-coordinate claim at repeated roots.  Let
\(\mathcal O(Q)\) have successive rows \(e_n^\top Q^j\), and let
\(\mathcal C(Q)\) have successive columns \(Q^j\mathbf1\), for
\(0\le j<n\).  Both are nonsingular because the nonzero bidiagonal products
are exposed triangularly.  The exponential is injective on the real spectrum
of \(Q\), so Hermite interpolation gives a polynomial \(p\) satisfying
\(Q=p(e^{\Delta\tau Q})\).  The corresponding observation and reachability
spaces for \(K=e^{\Delta\tau Q}\) are therefore full.  Hence
\[
 (S_\theta(i+j))_{0\le i,j<n}=\mathcal O(K)\mathcal C(K)
\tag{2.4}
\]
is invertible.  The first \(2n\) tails uniquely solve the order-\(n\)
recurrence.  Its roots and algebraic multiplicities recover
\(\lambda_j=-\Delta\tau^{-1}\log z_j\) and \(m_j\), proving the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{The isolated-double-pole experiment}

Fix \(n\ge2\), \(0<\varepsilon<M<\infty\), \(c_0>0\), and known
\(\Delta\tau>0\).  The compact collision stratum is
\[
\mathcal C_n(\varepsilon,M,c_0)=
\left\{(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda_3,\ldots,\lambda_n):
\begin{array}{l}
\lambda,\lambda_j\in[\varepsilon,M],\\
\lambda_3<\cdots<\lambda_n,\\
|\lambda_j-\lambda|\ge c_0,\\
|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|\ge c_0\quad(3\le i<j\le n)
\end{array}\right\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

labelling of physical serial states.  Only one real double pole is allowed,
and all remaining rates are simple and uniformly separated.

\[
 c=\frac{\theta_1+\theta_2}{2},\qquad
 \delta=\left(\frac{\theta_1-\theta_2}{2}\right)^2\ge0,
 \qquad \eta=(c,\theta_3,\ldots,\theta_n)\in\RR^{n-1}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

\(\mu_{\eta,\delta}\) be the Palm gap mass, tail, and mean cycle length in

 \dot\ell_{\eta,\delta}(k)

 \right|_{(\eta_0,0)},
\qquad
 I_n(\eta_0)=\frac1{\mu_{\eta_0,0}}
 \sum_{k\ge0}g_{\eta_0,0}(k)
 \dot\ell_{\eta,\delta}(k)\dot\ell_{\eta,\delta}(k)^\top.
\tag{3.3}

\begin{proposition}[Isolated-double-pole score nondegeneracy]
\label{prop:sampled-double-pole-nondegeneracy-main}
On \(\mathcal C_n(\varepsilon,M,c_0)\), the Palm gap family is analytic in
\((\eta,\delta)\) on a fixed physical one-sided neighbourhood of
\(\delta=0\), and its one-cycle score coordinates are linearly independent.
More precisely, the continuous-survival derivatives in the collision,
centre, and separated-rate coordinates have respectively an exact
fourth-order pole at \(-\lambda\), an order-three pole at \(-\lambda\), and
an order-two pole at the corresponding \(-\theta_j\).  Sampling produces the
distinguishing components
\[
 k^3e^{-\lambda\Delta\tau k},\qquad
 k^2e^{-\lambda\Delta\tau k},\qquad
 ke^{-\theta_j\Delta\tau k}\quad(3\le j\le n).
\tag{3.4}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \inf_{\eta_0\in\mathcal C_n(\varepsilon,M,c_0)}
 \lambda_{\min}\{I_n(\eta_0)\}>0.
\tag{3.5}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(W=X_1+\cdots+X_n\), where the independent exponential holding times
have rates \(c+\sqrt\delta,c-\sqrt\delta,\theta_3,\ldots,\theta_n\), and put
\[
 B_\eta(s)=\prod_{j=3}^n\frac{\theta_j}{s+\theta_j};
\]
the empty product is one.  The Laplace transform of the continuous survival
function \(\overline F_{\eta,\delta}(t)=\PP(W>t)\) is
\[
 H_{\eta,\delta}(s)=\frac1s\left\{1-
 \frac{c^2-\delta}{(s+c)^2-\delta}B_\eta(s)\right\}.
\tag{3.6}
\]
At a base point with \(c=\lambda\), direct differentiation gives
\[
 \partial_\delta H_{\eta,0}(s)
 =B_\eta(s)\frac{s+2\lambda}{(s+\lambda)^4},\qquad
 \partial_cH_{\eta,0}(s)
 =-\frac{2\lambda B_\eta(s)}{(s+\lambda)^3},
\tag{3.7}
\]
and, for \(j\ge3\),
\[
 \partial_{\theta_j}H_{\eta,0}(s)
 =-\frac{\lambda^2}{(s+\lambda)^2}
 \frac1{(s+\theta_j)^2}
 \prod_{\substack{\ell=3\\\ell\ne j}}^n
 \frac{\theta_\ell}{s+\theta_\ell}.
\tag{3.8}
\]
In \textup{(3.8)}, differentiating \(\theta_j/(s+\theta_j)\) cancels its
factor \(s\) against the outer \(1/s\); all remaining numerator factors are
nonzero at the separated poles.

Uniform separation gives \(B_\eta(-\lambda)\ne0\).  Thus the collision
derivative has an exact order-four pole at \(-\lambda\), with nonzero leading
coefficient \(\lambda B_\eta(-\lambda)\), whereas the centre derivative has
order three.  At \(-\theta_j\), the derivative in \(\theta_j\) has order two
and every other coordinate derivative has at most order one.  Inverse Laplace
transformation and sampling give exactly the components in \textup{(3.4)},
with nonzero coefficients.

Suppose \(a^\top\dot\ell_{\eta,\delta}=0\) in
\(L^2(g_{\eta,0})\).  Full support implies
\(a^\top\partial g_{\eta,0}(k)=0\) for every \(k\), and absolute summability
gives the same identity for the tail derivatives.  Exponential-polynomial
sequences at distinct bases are linearly independent: successive difference
operators \(E-z_j\), equivalently their rational generating functions,
separate each base and its highest polynomial degree.  The cubic component at
\(e^{-\lambda\Delta\tau}\) forces \(a_\delta=0\); the unique linear component
at each separated base forces \(a_{\theta_j}=0\); and the remaining quadratic
component at the collision base forces \(a_c=0\).  The scores are linearly
independent.

Finally, \textup{(3.6)} is rational and analytic in \((\eta,\delta)\) on a
common one-sided neighbourhood whose pole clusters remain separated.  Partial
fractions give, for every fixed derivative order,
\[
 \sup_{(\eta,\delta)}|\partial_{\eta,\delta}^a
 S_{\eta,\delta}(k)|
 \le C_a(1+k)^{m_a}e^{-\varepsilon\Delta\tau k/2}.
\tag{3.9}
\]
First differences give the same absolute bound for \(g\).  The required
relative bounds are proved separately in
\Cref*{lem:uniform-sampled-bin-tail-score-main} of the supplementary material by conditioning complete-data
scores on a bin or tail event.  Dominated summation makes the information
continuous.  Pointwise positive definiteness, continuity, and compactness
prove \textup{(3.5)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stationary isolated-double-pole limit experiment]
\label{thm:stationary-serial-double-pole-main}
Fix \(n,\varepsilon,M,c_0\), known \(\Delta\tau>0\), and the serial positive
killed-reset realization \textup{(1.2)}--\textup{(1.3)}, and suppose
\(\mathcal C_n(\varepsilon,M,c_0)\ne\varnothing\).  Let
\(P_{\eta,\delta}^{(N)}\) be the stationary length-\(N\) binary renewal
record.  The conclusions below concern one isolated real double pole in this
fixed serial order, with all other rates simple and separated as in
\textup{(3.1)}.  They do not cover simultaneous collisions, unknown order,
unknown \(\Delta\tau\), or nonserial phase-type representations.

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(A) Uniform LAN and the limit experiment.}
For bounded \(u\in\RR^{n-1}\) and \(v\in[0,\infty)\), uniformly over the
collision stratum,
\[
 \log\frac{dP_{\eta_0+u/\sqrt N,v/\sqrt N}^{(N)}}
 {dP_{\eta_0,0}^{(N)}}
 =\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix}^{\!\top}\Delta_{N,\eta_0}
 -\frac12\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix}^{\!\top}
 I_n(\eta_0)\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\end{pmatrix}
 +o_{P_{\eta_0,0}}(1),
\tag{3.10}
\]
where
\(\Delta_{N,\eta_0}\Rightarrow\mathcal N_n\{0,I_n(\eta_0)\}\)
uniformly in bounded-Lipschitz distance.  The local experiments converge to
the Gaussian shift on \(\RR^{n-1}\times[0,\infty)\), and the information is
uniformly nondegenerate by \textup{(3.5)}.

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(B) Efficient collision test.}
Partition \(I_n\) according to \((\eta,\delta)\) and set
\[
 J_n(\eta)=I_{\delta\delta}
 -I_{\delta\eta}I_{\eta\eta}^{-1}I_{\eta\delta}>0.
\tag{3.11}
\]
There is a total measurable residualized-score test \(\phi_N\) of
\(H_0:\delta=0\) against \(H_1:\delta>0\) with uniform asymptotic level
\(\alpha\).  It uses the null recurrence fit from
\Cref{est:serial-collision-recurrence-main}, empirical calendar information,
and gives no rejection whenever that fit leaves a fixed common score chart,
the empirical nuisance block is singular, or its empirical Schur complement
is nonpositive.  This gate is uniformly asymptotically inactive.  At
\((\eta_N,\delta_N)=(\eta_0+u/\sqrt N,v/\sqrt N)\), its limiting power is
\[
 1-\Phi_{\rm N}\{z_{1-\alpha}-v\sqrt{J_n(\eta_0)}\}.
\tag{3.12}
\]
This is the Gaussian half-space power envelope among tests with uniform local
asymptotic level \(\alpha\).  The residualized score coincides with the
likelihood-ratio rule only in the limiting Gaussian experiment; no
finite-record likelihood-ratio argmax transfer is asserted.

\smallskip\noindent\textup{(C) Attainable and lower recovery rates.}
The measurable procedure in \Cref{est:serial-collision-recurrence-main}
gives root-\(N\) estimates of \(\eta\) and \(\delta\) and an induced rate
multiset \(\widehat{\mathcal R}_{n,N}\) satisfying
\[
 d_{\rm m}\!\left(\widehat{\mathcal R}_{n,N},
 \{\!\{c_N+\sqrt{\delta_N},c_N-\sqrt{\delta_N},
 \theta_{3,N},\ldots,\theta_{n,N}\}\!\}\right)=O_P(N^{-1/4})
\tag{3.13}
\]
uniformly over the compact stratum and bounded \((u,v)\).  The centre and
canonically ordered separated rates remain root-\(N\).  At every fixed base,
\Cref{lem:two-point-threshold-rate-main} gives a strictly positive lower
limit for threshold risk on two alternatives separated by
\(\sqrt{v_0}N^{-1/4}\).  Hence \(N^{-1/4}\) is uniformly attainable on the
stated bounded local families and pointwise locally minimax-optimal in rate.
This is not an exact asymptotic minimax-risk or sharp-constant theorem over a
continuum of alternatives.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[The two-state collision]
\label{cor:two-state-collision-main}
For \(n=2\), the stratum restrictions on separated rates are vacuous and
\Cref{thm:stationary-serial-double-pole-main} applies with \(\eta=c\).  In
particular,
\[
 \mathcal L\{\overline F_{c,\delta}\}(s)
 =\frac1s\left\{1-\frac{c^2-\delta}{(s+c)^2-\delta}\right\},
\quad
 \left.\partial_\delta\mathcal L\{\overline F_{c,\delta}\}(s)
 \right|_0=\frac{s+2c}{(s+c)^4},
\tag{3.14}
\]
whereas the centre derivative is \(-2c/(s+c)^3\).  Thus the stationary
two-state record has the Gaussian half-plane experiment on
\(\RR\times[0,\infty)\), the residualized test has power
\(1-\Phi_{\rm N}\{z_{1-\alpha}-v\sqrt{J_2(c)}\}\), and the unordered pair
is recoverable at the matching upper and fixed-base two-point lower rate
\(N^{-1/4}\).  The sign of the rate split is not identifiable.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is the specialization of \Cref{thm:stationary-serial-double-pole-main}
with the empty product \(B_\eta=1\).  Direct differentiation of
\textup{(3.14)} gives the displayed fourth- and third-order poles.  Detailed
two-state tail, recurrence, score, and estimator calculations are recorded in
\Cref*{sec:two-state-calculations-supp} of the supplementary material.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Two-point threshold-risk lower bound]
\label{lem:two-point-threshold-rate-main}
Fix a collision base \((\eta_0,0)\), hold \(\eta_0\) fixed, and compare
\(P_{0,N}=P_{\eta_0,0}^{(N)}\) with
\(P_{1,N}=P_{\eta_0,v_0/\sqrt N}^{(N)}\), where \(v_0>0\).  Let their
unordered rate multisets be \(\mathcal R_{0,N}\) and \(\mathcal R_{1,N}\),
and put \(h_N=\sqrt{v_0}N^{-1/4}\).  For \(n=2\) their matching distance is
exactly \(h_N\) whenever the alternative is admissible.  For \(n>2\), the
same equality holds whenever \(h_N<c_0/2\), in particular for all sufficiently
large \(N\).  For every \(0<a<\sqrt{v_0}/2\),
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\inf_{\widehat{\mathcal R}_N}
 \max_{i=0,1}P_{i,N}\!\left\{
 N^{1/4}d_{\rm m}(\widehat{\mathcal R}_N,\mathcal R_{i,N})>a
 \right\}
 \ge \Phi_{\rm N}\!\left(
 -\frac{v_0\sqrt{I_{\delta\delta}(\eta_0)}}2\right)>0.
\tag{3.15}
\]
The conclusion is pointwise at a fixed base, uses two alternatives and
threshold loss, and asserts optimality in rate rather than an exact minimax
risk over a continuum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Matching every unchanged separated rate to itself and the colliding entries to
\(c-h_N\) and \(c+h_N\) gives distance at most \(h_N\).  If \(n>2\) and
\(h_N<c_0/2\), every separated rate is at distance at least
\(c_0-h_N>h_N\) from either perturbed collision entry, so no matching through
a separated rate improves it.  The equality is immediate for \(n=2\).

The balls of radius \(aN^{-1/4}\) around the two candidates are disjoint.
Assigning an estimate to its nearer candidate turns simultaneous estimation
success into correct testing of \(P_{0,N}\) against \(P_{1,N}\).  Under
\(P_{0,N}\), the LAN likelihood ratio converges to
\[
 \exp\{v_0Z-\tfrac12v_0^2I_{\delta\delta}(\eta_0)\},
 \qquad Z\sim\mathcal N(0,I_{\delta\delta}(\eta_0)).
\tag{3.16}
\]
Its limit has mean one, and mutual contiguity makes the likelihood ratios
uniformly integrable.  Their total-variation distances therefore converge to
that of the two Gaussian shifts separated by
\(v_0\sqrt{I_{\delta\delta}(\eta_0)}\).  The maximum error of any test is at
least its equal-prior average error, whose limiting infimum is the right side
of \textup{(3.15)}.  Taking a measurable estimator within \(o(1)\) of the
infimum at each \(N\) proves the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{estimator}[Measurable sampled-tail recurrence estimator]
\label{est:serial-collision-recurrence-main}
Delete the two incomplete endpoint cycles and let
\(G_1,\ldots,G_{K_N}\) be the complete zero-run gaps.  Form
\[
 \widehat S_k=K_N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}\mathbf1_{\{G_j\ge k\}},
 \qquad0\le k\le2n-1,
\tag{3.17}
\]
with fixed values when \(K_N=0\).  Solve the empirical Hankel recurrence on
its nonsingular gate, use a fixed fallback otherwise, and select measurably
from a finite analytic contour atlas separating the unique two-root cluster
from the \(n-2\) simple roots.  In the cluster, use its elementary symmetric
coordinates and transform them analytically to \((c,\widetilde\delta)\);
set \(\widehat\delta=(\widetilde\delta)^+\).  The resulting estimators are
total and Borel measurable and satisfy, uniformly over the compact stratum and
bounded local alternatives,
\[
 \widehat\eta_N-\eta_N=O_P(N^{-1/2}),\qquad
 \widehat\delta_N-\delta_N=O_P(N^{-1/2}).
\tag{3.18}
\]
The pair \(\widehat c_N\pm\sqrt{\widehat\delta_N}\), together with the
canonically ordered simple-rate estimates, gives \textup{(3.13)}.
\end{estimator}

The mechanism in \Cref{est:serial-collision-recurrence-main} is short even
though its total construction is technical.  The finite vector of empirical
tail coordinates is root-\(N\); invertibility from
\Cref{prop:serial-tail-coordinates-main} makes the recurrence coefficients
root-\(N\).  Simple roots and the two-root cluster have compatible analytic
charts, while \((c,\delta)\) are regular analytic cluster coordinates.
Projection onto \([0,\infty)\) is nonexpansive, and
\(|\sqrt{x^+}-\sqrt y|\le\sqrt{|x-y|}\) for \(y\ge0\), giving the
fourth-root rate after taking square roots.  The finite-atlas contour proof,
chart compatibility, exceptional-event fallbacks, and measurability details
are in \Cref*{sec:estimator-atlas-supp} of the supplementary material.

\section{Discussion and relation to prior work}

\subsection*{Stationary-window censoring and Fisher information}

Theorem~1 is a statement about the whole local experiment, not only the
leading information calculation.  Classical stationary renewal sampling
distinguishes complete gaps, length-biased containing intervals, backward
times, and forward times; the available likelihood depends on the sampling
scheme \cite{GillKeiding2010RenewalSampling}.  For a continuous-time window,
Zhao and Nagaraja derived the endpoint and complete-gap likelihood and showed
that Fisher information per unit window converges to interarrival information
divided by mean interarrival time
\cite{ZhaoNagaraja2011WindowCensoredRenewal}.  Theorem~1 explains why that
calendar scaling is decision-theoretically stable in the present lattice
experiment.

The essential step is the record-to-sample coupling.  Extracted complete gaps
are not treated as an i.i.d. sample conditional on fitting.  Instead, the
record and an unconditioned Palm sequence are generated on the same space and
agree unless fewer than \(m_N\) cycles are visible.  The undershoot
\(N^\gamma\), larger than renewal-count fluctuations but smaller than \(N\),
makes that event negligible.  The reverse kernel is equally important: it
must create a full stationary window from a shorter supplied sample without
knowing the local parameter.  A base equilibrium delay and base-law
continuation are close enough over only \(O(N^\gamma)\) extra coordinates by
Hellinger tensorization.  This is where both deficiency directions, and not
merely an asymptotic statistic, enter.

The theorem stops at observable independent renewal cycles.  For a generic
Markov-renewal, semi-Markov, or Markovian arrival observation, a reverse kernel
cannot append independent base-law interarrivals.  The equivalence theorem
does not cover those models.

\subsection*{Le Cam transfer and constrained experiments}

Two-sided deficiency convergence transfers every bounded-loss procedure with
vanishing uniform risk discrepancy.  It also identifies local asymptotic
minimax risks and lets the Palm experiment supply calendar-time information.
For a pathwise differentiable functional, the multiplication of the i.i.d.
canonical-gradient covariance by \(\mu_0\) follows from the same effective
sample-size conversion.

Restriction to a fixed cone needs careful wording.  The ambient DQM family
still determines the information matrix, but the admissible local parameter
set changes the decision problem.  At \(\delta=0\), the limit is a Gaussian
shift on \(\RR^{n-1}\times[0,\infty)\), not an unconstrained Gaussian shift.
Thus an ordinary unconstrained canonical-gradient variance need not itself be
the relevant constrained efficiency or minimax bound.  Theorem~1 asserts no
global adaptive equivalence over an unknown compact collection of centres.

\subsection*{Locally conic structure and the statistical pole calculation}

General locally conic and loss-of-identifiability theory describes why a
quotient coordinate and a nonstandard boundary can appear
\cite{DacunhaCastelleGassiat1997LocallyConic,LiuShao2003LossIdentifiability}.
It does not by itself show that the collision direction in this sampled
renewal model has positive efficient information.  Proposition~3 supplies
that missing model calculation.  The collision derivative's exact
fourth-order pole cannot be synthesized from the order-three centre derivative
or the separated order-two derivatives.  After sampling, exponential-
polynomial linear independence converts pole order into score linear
independence.  The resulting Schur complement is therefore strictly positive
uniformly on the compact isolated-collision stratum.

The half-space likelihood-ratio rule and residualized score agree in the
limiting Gaussian experiment, as expected from classical boundary likelihood
theory \cite{Chernoff1954LikelihoodRatio,SelfLiang1987NonstandardLRT}.  We do
not transfer a finite-record likelihood maximizer.  The implemented statistic
is instead a total measurable plug-in efficient score, with explicit
nonrejection gates whose probabilities vanish uniformly under the stated
local families.

\subsection*{Finite mixtures, confluent Prony systems, and collision rates}

Generalized-Erlang and Coxian theory provides the serial distributional
antecedent \cite{HeZhang2008MinimalCoxian,HeZhang2009GeneralizedErlang}.
Proposition~2 uses only the portion needed here: a sampled serial survival
tail has a minimal confluent recurrence, and its leading Hankel matrix remains
invertible at repeated roots.  The proposition is not a general phase-type
representation result.

Confluent and near-colliding Prony analyses describe the algebraic instability
of individual roots
\cite{BatenkovYomdin2013ConfluentProny,KunisRoemerVonDerOhe2020Prony}.
Our estimator avoids labelling the colliding roots: it estimates the first two
power sums, hence the regular elementary symmetric cluster coordinates, in a
finite compatible analytic atlas.  That construction provides a measurable
procedure in the stationary record, rather than an existence assertion based
on formal root continuity.

Finite-mixture results likewise show that coalescence can slow parameter
recovery, but the rate depends on the model's singularity structure
\cite{Chen1995FiniteMixtureRate,HeinrichKahn2018FiniteMixtureMinimax,
HoNguyen2019SingularityStructures}.  Here \(N^{-1/4}\) follows specifically
from root-\(N\) regular estimation of the squared half-separation followed by
a square root.  The lower statement is deliberately no broader than its
proof: a fixed-base two-point threshold loss witnesses that no uniformly
faster local multiset rate is possible.  It is not an exact minimax constant
over a continuum.

\subsection*{Why this is not a super-resolution observation model}

Near-colliding point-source recovery and optical resolution testing can also
display fourth-root scales.  Batenkov, Goldman, and Yomdin study bandlimited
spectral data with bounded deterministic error and cluster-size-dependent
location and amplitude errors
\cite{BatenkovGoldmanYomdin2021SuperResolution}.  Kulaitis, Munk, and Werner
study one blurred source against two sources under Poisson and Gaussian
microscopy observations \cite{KulaitisMunkWerner2021Resolution}.  Their
fourth-root behavior and ours share a quadratic loss of first-order separation
information, but the observation operators, noise laws, parameter spaces, and
losses differ.  Those minimax bounds do not transfer to a stationary renewal
window, and our two-point lower bound does not transfer back to their models.

The ceiling of Theorem~4 is therefore substantive.  Theorem~1 resolves
stationary-window censoring and random cycle count for renewal experiments.
The collision conclusion remains restricted to one isolated real double pole,
fixed serial order, simple separated remaining rates, known sampling interval,
and the serial positive realization.  Multiple collisions and general
phase-type representation geometry require different local charts and additional
score calculations.

We first make the route from the stationary record to the local experiment

\[
 S_{\eta,\delta}(k)=\PP_{\eta,\delta}\{W>k\Delta\tau\},\qquad
 g_{\eta,\delta}(k)=S_{\eta,\delta}(k)-S_{\eta,\delta}(k+1).
\tag{4.1}
\]
The required uniform score bounds are supplied in the supplement.

\[
 H^2(g_{\eta_N,\delta_N},g_{\eta_0,0})=O(N^{-1}),
\tag{4.2}
\]
uniformly.  The equilibrium residual-life mass is

 H^2(e_{\eta_N,\delta_N},e_{\eta_0,0})=O(N^{-1}),\qquad

\tag{4.8}

 \Delta_{\delta\cdot\eta,N}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform sampled-bin and sampled-tail derivatives]
\label{lem:uniform-sampled-bin-tail-score-main}
For fixed \(n,\varepsilon,M,c_0\), and \(\Delta\tau>0\), there is a common
physical one-sided neighbourhood \(\mathcal Q^+\) of
\(\mathcal C_n(\varepsilon,M,c_0)\), in the coordinates
\(q=(\eta,\delta)\), on which \(c\pm\sqrt\delta>0\) and, for every
multi-index \(a\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{q\in\mathcal Q^+}|\partial_q^a\log g_q(k)|
 &\le C_a(1+k)^{2|a|},&& 1\le|a|\le3,\\
 \sup_{q\in\mathcal Q^+}|\partial_q^a\log S_q(k)|
 &\le C_a(1+k)^{2|a|},&& 1\le|a|\le2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{U1}
\]
All parameter derivatives extend continuously to \(\delta=0\).  In addition,
\(g_q(k)\le C(1+k)^{n-1}r^k\) for a common \(r<1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Compress the colliding holding times into \(Y=X_1+X_2\).  Relative to
Lebesgue measure on \((0,\infty)^{n-1}\), the complete holding-time density is
\[
 f_q(y,x_3,\ldots,x_n)
 =(c^2-\delta)e^{-cy}\frac{\sinh(\sqrt\delta\,y)}{\sqrt\delta}
 \prod_{j=3}^n\theta_j e^{-\theta_jx_j},
\tag{U2}
\]
where the quotient at \(\delta=0\) is \(y\).  Write
\(\sinh(\sqrt\delta\,y)/\sqrt\delta=y h(\delta y^2)\), where
\(h(x)=\sinh(\sqrt x)/\sqrt x\) is positive and analytic on
\([0,\infty)\).  On a sufficiently small common neighbourhood,
\(c^2-\delta\) and every rate are bounded away from zero.  The derivatives
through order three of \(\log h(x)\) are bounded on \([0,\infty)\): they are
continuous at zero by the power series, and their elementary hyperbolic
expressions are bounded as \(x\to\infty\).  Differentiating \(\log f_q\)
therefore gives, uniformly on \(\mathcal Q^+\),
\[
 |\partial_q^a\log f_q(y,x_3,\ldots,x_n)|
 \le C_a\{1+(y+x_3+\cdots+x_n)^{2|a|}\},
 \qquad 1\le |a|\le3.
\tag{U3}
\]

Put \(W=Y+X_3+\cdots+X_n\).  Differentiation under the integral in a
sampling bin expresses every derivative of \(\log g_q(k)\) through order
three as a finite universal polynomial in conditional expectations of the
complete-data derivatives in \textup{(U3)}, conditional on
\(k\Delta\tau<W\le(k+1)\Delta\tau\).  On this event every nonnegative
holding time is at most \((k+1)\Delta\tau\), which proves the first bound in
\textup{(U1)}.  This argument controls the ratio by the bin probability
itself and hence does not infer a relative bound from an absolute derivative
bound.

For the tail ratios, each exponential density is log-concave, convolution
preserves log-concavity, and integration over a half-line shows that the
survival function of \(W\) is log-concave.  Thus \(W\) is IFR and its residual
life satisfies
\[
 \PP_q(W-t>x\mid W>t)\le \PP_q(W>x),\qquad t,x\ge0.
\tag{U4}
\]
All rates on \(\mathcal Q^+\) exceed a common \(\underline\theta>0\), so
\(W\) is stochastically dominated by an
Erlang\((n,\underline\theta)\) variable.  Consequently, for every fixed
integer \(m\),
\[
 \sup_{q\in\mathcal Q^+}\EE_q(W^m\mid W>t)
 \le C_m(1+t)^m.
\tag{U5}
\]
The same conditional-score polynomial argument, now conditional on
\(W>k\Delta\tau\), proves the second bound in \textup{(U1)}.  Finally,
stochastic domination by the Erlang variable gives the common
polynomial-exponential mass envelope.  The envelopes justify every
differentiation under the integral and continuity at \(\delta=0\).

\begin{lemma}[Uniform stopped renewal-score CLT and LLN]
\label{lem:uniform-stopped-renewal-score-main}
Let \(\mathcal Q\) be compact and let the integer Palm gaps \(G\) have full
support, a common exponential moment, and parameter-continuous masses.  Let a
stationary binary renewal record of length \(N\) contain \(K_N\) complete
gaps, with initial forward recurrence (residual life) \(A_N\) and terminal
backward recurrence (age) \(R_N\), and put
\(D=G+1\), \(\mu_q=\EE_qD\).  Uniformly in \(q\in\mathcal Q\), and also for
triangular arrays \(q_N\in\mathcal Q\),
\[
 K_N=N/\mu_{q_N}+O_{P_{q_N}}(\sqrt N),
\qquad
 \max\{A_N,R_N,G_1,\ldots,G_{K_N}\}=O_{P_{q_N}}(\log N).
\tag{U6}
\]
If \(b_q(G)\in\RR^d\) is parameter-continuous,
\(\EE_qb_q(G)=0\), and is bounded by a common polynomial in \(G\), then
\[
 \sup_{q\in\mathcal Q}d_{\rm BL}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_q\!\left[N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}b_q(G_j)\right],
 \mathcal N_d\!\left(0,\mu_q^{-1}\EE_q[b_qb_q^\top]\right)
 \right)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{U7}
\]
For every similarly dominated matrix reward \(H_q\),
\[
 N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}H_q(G_j)
 -\mu_q^{-1}\EE_qH_q(G)\longrightarrow0
\tag{U8}
\]
uniformly in probability.  If \(B_q\) has a common polynomial envelope, then
\[
 N^{-3/2}\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}B_q(G_j)=o_P(1)
\]
uniformly.  These conclusions
hold jointly and hence apply to the score, stopped Hessian, and third-order
likelihood remainder under bounded root-\(N\) local triangular arrays.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The common exponential moment gives uniform bounds for every moment of
\(D\), with \(0<\inf_q\mu_q\le\sup_q\mu_q<\infty\).  For the ordinary
renewal count, compare the partial sums of \(D_j-\mu_q\) at
\(\lfloor N/\mu_q\pm x\sqrt N\rfloor\).  Chebyshev's inequality and
Kolmogorov's maximal inequality give a bound \(C/x^2\), uniformly in \(q\),
for deviation of the count by more than \(x\sqrt N\).  The stationary delay
changes this count only through the initial incomplete cycle, which is
\(O_P(\log N)\) by the bound below.  This proves the first part of
\textup{(U6)} without requiring independence between a reward and its cycle
length.

The equilibrium forward-recurrence formula
\(\PP_q(A_N\ge t)=\mu_q^{-1}\sum_{j\ge t}S_q(j)\), and its terminal analogue,
have a common exponential-polynomial bound.  There are at most \(N\) complete
gaps because \(D\ge1\).  A union bound for those gaps and the two endpoint
variables therefore makes the probability that the maximum exceeds
\(C_0\log N\) tend to zero uniformly for sufficiently large \(C_0\).  This
proves the second part of \textup{(U6)}.

For \(m_N=\lfloor N/\mu_q\rfloor\), the triangular-array Lindeberg theorem
applied to the deterministic sum of \(b_q(G_j)\) gives the normal law in
\textup{(U7)}.  The common polynomial envelope and exponential moment verify
Lindeberg uniformly.  Replacing \(m_N\) by \(K_N\) changes the sum by at most
the maximum of centred partial sums over \(O_P(\sqrt N)\) indices.
Kolmogorov's inequality makes that difference \(o_P(\sqrt N)\) uniformly;
this is the stopped-score, or Anscombe, step.  The same replacement in a
law of large numbers is \(o_P(N)\), proving \textup{(U8)}.  The last assertion
follows from \(K_N=O_P(N)\) and uniform integrability of the envelope.

To verify the displayed uniformity rather than only pointwise convergence,
take an arbitrary parameter sequence.  Compactness supplies a convergent
subsequence; parameter continuity plus the common exponential envelope gives
convergence of the means, covariance matrices, Lindeberg terms, and all LLN
integrals along it.  The preceding inequalities have constants independent of
the subsequence.  Every subsequence therefore has a further subsequence with
the asserted joint limits, which is the uniform subsequence criterion and
also proves the triangular-array statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Efficient-score plug-in equicontinuity]
\label{lem:efficient-score-plugin-main}
On the canonically ordered collision stratum, write the one-cycle null scores
as \(s_\eta\) and \(s_\delta\), and define
\[
 \psi_\eta=s_\delta-I_{\delta\eta}I_{\eta\eta}^{-1}s_\eta,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{{\rm eff},N}(\eta)=N^{-1/2}
 \sum_{j=1}^{K_N}\psi_\eta(G_j).
\tag{U9}
\]
For every fixed \(L<\infty\),
\[
 \sup_{\eta}\sup_{\|\eta'-\eta\|\le L/\sqrt N}
 |\Delta_{{\rm eff},N}(\eta')-\Delta_{{\rm eff},N}(\eta)|
 =o_{P_{\eta,0}}(1).
\tag{U10}
\]
The conclusion also holds under bounded contiguous local alternatives.  The
empirical calendar information and its Schur complement, evaluated anywhere
in the same root-\(N\) ball, converge uniformly to \(I_n(\eta)\) and
\(J_n(\eta)\).  Hence any measurable root-\(N\) null fit may be inserted in
the standardized efficient central sequence with an \(o_P(1)\) change.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Uniform nondegeneracy makes the projection coefficient in \textup{(U9)} twice
continuously differentiable with polynomial score envelopes.  By construction,
\[
 \EE_{\eta,0}\psi_\eta=0,
 \qquad \EE_{\eta,0}(\psi_\eta s_\eta^\top)=0.
\tag{U11}
\]
Differentiating the first identity, justified by
\Cref{lem:uniform-sampled-bin-tail-score-main}, gives
\[
 \EE_{\eta,0}(\partial_\eta\psi_\eta)
 =-\EE_{\eta,0}(\psi_\eta s_\eta^\top)=0.
\tag{U12}
\]
Thus the derivative of \(\Delta_{{\rm eff},N}\) is a normalized stopped sum
of centred rewards and is \(O_P(1)\) uniformly by
\Cref{lem:uniform-stopped-renewal-score-main}.  A second-order Taylor expansion
over a ball of radius \(L/\sqrt N\) has first term \(O_P(N^{-1/2})\); its
second term is bounded by
\(N^{-1/2}O_P(N)O(N^{-1})=O_P(N^{-1/2})\).  This proves \textup{(U10)}.
The stopped LLN applied to score outer products, followed by the same Taylor
bound and continuity of matrix inversion on the uniformly positive-definite
set, proves information consistency.  The normalization and the two
stationary endpoint log-score increments obey the same conclusion: their parameter
increments are bounded by \(N^{-1/2}\) times a polynomial in
\(\max(A_N,R_N)\), which is \(O_P(\log N)\) by \textup{(U6)}.  Thus they
contribute \(o_P(1)\) uniformly to the plug-in difference.  Contiguity
transfers every null \(o_P(1)\) assertion to bounded local alternatives.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Confluent sampled-tail coordinates]

\[
 S_\theta(k)=\sum_{j=1}^r\sum_{\ell=0}^{m_j-1}
 c_{j\ell}k^\ell z_j^k,\qquad c_{j,m_j-1}\ne0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

\[
 \chi_\theta(x)=\prod_{j=1}^r(x-z_j)^{m_j}.
\tag{2.2}

collision multiplicities, and the unordered sampled-rate multiset

\[

\section{Discussion and relation to prior work}

\subsection*{Stationary-window censoring and Fisher information}

Theorem~1 is a statement about the whole local experiment, not only the
leading information calculation.  Classical stationary renewal sampling
distinguishes complete gaps, length-biased containing intervals, backward
times, and forward times; the available likelihood depends on the sampling
scheme \cite{GillKeiding2010RenewalSampling}.  For a continuous-time window,
Zhao and Nagaraja derived the endpoint and complete-gap likelihood and showed
that Fisher information per unit window converges to interarrival information
divided by mean interarrival time
\cite{ZhaoNagaraja2011WindowCensoredRenewal}.  Theorem~1 explains why that
calendar scaling is decision-theoretically stable in the present lattice
experiment.

The essential step is the record-to-sample coupling.  Extracted complete gaps
are not treated as an i.i.d. sample conditional on fitting.  Instead, the
record and an unconditioned Palm sequence are generated on the same space and
agree unless fewer than \(m_N\) cycles are visible.  The undershoot
\(N^\gamma\), larger than renewal-count fluctuations but smaller than \(N\),
makes that event negligible.  The reverse kernel is equally important: it
must create a full stationary window from a shorter supplied sample without
knowing the local parameter.  A base equilibrium delay and base-law
continuation are close enough over only \(O(N^\gamma)\) extra coordinates by
Hellinger tensorization.  This is where both deficiency directions, and not
merely an asymptotic statistic, enter.

The theorem stops at observable independent renewal cycles.  For a generic
Markov-renewal, semi-Markov, or Markovian arrival observation, a reverse kernel
cannot append independent base-law interarrivals.  The equivalence theorem
does not cover those models.

\subsection*{Le Cam transfer and constrained experiments}

Two-sided deficiency convergence transfers every bounded-loss procedure with
vanishing uniform risk discrepancy.  It also identifies local asymptotic
minimax risks and lets the Palm experiment supply calendar-time information.
For a pathwise differentiable functional, the multiplication of the i.i.d.
canonical-gradient covariance by \(\mu_0\) follows from the same effective
sample-size conversion.

Restriction to a fixed cone needs careful wording.  The ambient DQM family
still determines the information matrix, but the admissible local parameter
set changes the decision problem.  At \(\delta=0\), the limit is a Gaussian
shift on \(\RR^{n-1}\times[0,\infty)\), not an unconstrained Gaussian shift.
Thus an ordinary unconstrained canonical-gradient variance need not itself be
the relevant constrained efficiency or minimax bound.  Theorem~1 asserts no
global adaptive equivalence over an unknown compact collection of centres.

\subsection*{Locally conic structure and the statistical pole calculation}

General locally conic and loss-of-identifiability theory describes why a
quotient coordinate and a nonstandard boundary can appear
\cite{DacunhaCastelleGassiat1997LocallyConic,LiuShao2003LossIdentifiability}.
It does not by itself show that the collision direction in this sampled
renewal model has positive efficient information.  Proposition~3 supplies
that missing model calculation.  The collision derivative's exact
fourth-order pole cannot be synthesized from the order-three centre derivative
or the separated order-two derivatives.  After sampling, exponential-
polynomial linear independence converts pole order into score linear
independence.  The resulting Schur complement is therefore strictly positive
uniformly on the compact isolated-collision stratum.

The half-space likelihood-ratio rule and residualized score agree in the
limiting Gaussian experiment, as expected from classical boundary likelihood
theory \cite{Chernoff1954LikelihoodRatio,SelfLiang1987NonstandardLRT}.  We do
not transfer a finite-record likelihood maximizer.  The implemented statistic
is instead a total measurable plug-in efficient score, with explicit
nonrejection gates whose probabilities vanish uniformly under the stated
local families.

\subsection*{Finite mixtures, confluent Prony systems, and collision rates}

Generalized-Erlang and Coxian theory provides the serial distributional
antecedent \cite{HeZhang2008MinimalCoxian,HeZhang2009GeneralizedErlang}.
Proposition~2 uses only the portion needed here: a sampled serial survival
tail has a minimal confluent recurrence, and its leading Hankel matrix remains
invertible at repeated roots.  The proposition is not a general phase-type
representation result.

Confluent and near-colliding Prony analyses describe the algebraic instability
of individual roots
\cite{BatenkovYomdin2013ConfluentProny,KunisRoemerVonDerOhe2020Prony}.
Our estimator avoids labelling the colliding roots: it estimates the first two
power sums, hence the regular elementary symmetric cluster coordinates, in a
finite compatible analytic atlas.  That construction provides a measurable
procedure in the stationary record, rather than an existence assertion based
on formal root continuity.

Finite-mixture results likewise show that coalescence can slow parameter
recovery, but the rate depends on the model's singularity structure
\cite{Chen1995FiniteMixtureRate,HeinrichKahn2018FiniteMixtureMinimax,
HoNguyen2019SingularityStructures}.  Here \(N^{-1/4}\) follows specifically
from root-\(N\) regular estimation of the squared half-separation followed by
a square root.  The lower statement is deliberately no broader than its
proof: a fixed-base two-point threshold loss witnesses that no uniformly
faster local multiset rate is possible.  It is not an exact minimax constant
over a continuum.

\subsection*{Why this is not a super-resolution observation model}

Near-colliding point-source recovery and optical resolution testing can also
display fourth-root scales.  Batenkov, Goldman, and Yomdin study bandlimited
spectral data with bounded deterministic error and cluster-size-dependent
location and amplitude errors
\cite{BatenkovGoldmanYomdin2021SuperResolution}.  Kulaitis, Munk, and Werner
study one blurred source against two sources under Poisson and Gaussian
microscopy observations \cite{KulaitisMunkWerner2021Resolution}.  Their
fourth-root behavior and ours share a quadratic loss of first-order separation
information, but the observation operators, noise laws, parameter spaces, and
losses differ.  Those minimax bounds do not transfer to a stationary renewal
window, and our two-point lower bound does not transfer back to their models.

The ceiling of Theorem~4 is therefore substantive.  Theorem~1 resolves
stationary-window censoring and random cycle count for renewal experiments.
The collision conclusion remains restricted to one isolated real double pole,
fixed serial order, simple separated remaining rates, known sampling interval,
and the serial positive realization.  Multiple collisions and general
phase-type representation geometry require different local charts and additional
score calculations.
