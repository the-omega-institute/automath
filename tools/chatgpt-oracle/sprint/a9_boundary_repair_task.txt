3. THE OPTIONAL ADDITION - include it if it goes in cleanly, and say so if it does not.
   The assessor supplies one small correct theorem that belongs in a boundary section, stated
   entirely in contextuality and topology terms:

     Equivariant no-selection. Suppose an assignment associates to every empirical model e a class
     Theta(e) in H^2(N(M_e); A_e), natural under isomorphisms of empirical models. Then Theta(e)
     lies in the Aut(e)-invariant subgroup of H^2(N(M_e); A_e). Consequently, if that invariant
     subgroup is zero, every such natural degree-two obstruction vanishes on e.

   The proof is immediate: apply naturality to each automorphism of e. He rates its correctness at
   98% and its impact as modest, and it answers the obvious reader question - whether symmetry
   could be used to select a class - with the answer that symmetry can instead FORBID one. Add it
   as a clearly subordinate proposition inside the boundary section, with its one-line proof and
   that consequence spelled out. It is not a headline result and must not be presented as one.
   DO NOT recast the paper around an impossibility theorem. The assessor states plainly that the
   universal form - no functor from empirical models to banded prestacks can be natural and produce
   nonzero degree-two classes - is false or indefensible without severe qualifications, because
   enriching the category with operator multiplication or central-extension data does produce
   natural nonzero degree-two classes.
