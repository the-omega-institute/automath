Overall verdict
There is one plausible tier-raising route, but it requires changing the paper’s mathematical centre.
The target is not another two-state quotient, image equation, fibre parametrization, covariance formula, or model-specific test. Those directions are capped by the existing order-two canonical-representation and weak-equivalence theory. Mészáros–Telek already give a canonical representation of the whole order-two DRAP/DMAP class and describe it through four process-level parameters; Ramírez-Cobo–Lillo give a complete characterization of weakly equivalent MAP2 processes. The viable upgrade is:

A uniform stationary-record singular limit experiment, with efficient testing and a matching N−1/4 minimax rate, for collisions of rates in sampled generalized-Erlang/serial phase-type renewal processes.

This would move the paper from “a highly explicit analysis of one constrained D-MAP(2)” to a theorem about a standard phase-type family and a genuine nonregular statistical phenomenon. Merely proving the corresponding statement for the current two-state counter would repair an important gap, but would probably not raise the paper by a full tier.
The repairs in the present PDF do matter. The A–E registry and dependency claims are now coherent; the stopped batch-means argument exposes the correct balance

(b) Proving something about the field’s standard objects: applies, and is essential
The target should be stated for:

sampled generalized-Erlang distributions, equivalently serial DPH(n) killed-reset renewal records with one repeated rate.

Generalized-Erlang/Coxian and phase-type distributions are standard objects. The present paper already invokes their representation theory, but only to obtain a finite-coordinate population-identifiability corollary. 
The new result must be advertised as a theorem about singular statistical inference for sampled generalized-Erlang renewal processes. The two-state killed-reset counter should become the n=2 worked example.
This lever is the main source of the proposed theorem’s tier impact. Without it, even a perfect stationary n=2 LAN theorem remains too model-local.

(c) Removing a hypothesis: applies in two precise ways, but not to the reset condition
Hypothesis removal 1: deterministic product sample → stationary record
The current singular calculation assumes

independent complete gaps. The paper expressly says this is not a stationary-record endpoint equivalence.  Removing that sampling idealization is mathematically meaningful because the observed object throughout the paper is the stationary binary record.
Hypothesis removal 2: two phases → arbitrary fixed serial order
The present symmetric-split calculation is two-rate. Extending the theorem to one isolated collision in an arbitrary fixed generalized-Erlang order makes the phenomenon representation-theoretic rather than device-specific.
Hypothesis that should not be advertised as removable: deterministic post-click reset
The manuscript correctly proves that allowing a positive marked transition into R destroys the fixed Palm restart and invalidates the renewal product, covariance transfer, and quotient route in general.  Removing that hypothesis would require hidden-Markov or Markov-renewal likelihood theory and a different identifiability analysis. It is not a strengthening obtainable from the current proof.

5. Strongest remaining higher-tier objection
Even after the proposed theorem is proved, the hardest credible referee objection would be:

“After reparametrizing by squared root separation, the new result is an application of ordinary one-sided LAN to a smooth renewal family. The rest of the manuscript is still an overgrown union of explicit DMAP2 algebra, weak-equivalence geometry, specification tests, semiparametric Markov alternatives, growing-dimensional Gaussian coupling, and a projection example. The paper has not demonstrated that its flagship theorem is either general enough or central enough to justify this architecture.”

That objection would be serious. General phase-type Fisher-information calculations already exist, regular dead-time event-detection LAN and efficient estimators now exist, and generic boundary and loss-of-identifiability theory is standard. What would resolve it
1. Make the n-phase theorem, not the two-state corollary, the headline
The paper must prove the pole-order nondegeneracy uniformly for an isolated double pole in arbitrary fixed serial order. That is the part not supplied automatically by generic boundary theory.
2. Include the complete stationary experiment
The theorem must cover the actual stationary binary record, including random renewal stopping and endpoint censoring. A proof only for independent complete gaps will not suffice.
3. Include both optimal testing and matching estimation rates
A LAN display alone is not enough. The paper needs:


efficient nuisance residualization;


the exact one-sided local power envelope;


a contiguity-based N−1/4 lower bound;


an explicit rate-attaining estimator.

6. Finish the canonical-coordinate priority reduction
The three-inclusion quotient must either be explicitly derived from the Mészáros–Telek coordinates and labelled as a useful specialization, or be shown to contain a genuinely new minimal-observation statement not present there. Until then it should not remain a coequal flagship.
7. Remove any physical-system implication not supported by validation
The current PDF correctly says that the sampled counter is an interpretation of the constrained kernel, not an independently analysed physical system.  A mathematically recentered article should maintain that restraint. A higher-tier physical or engineering claim would require a real detector model, simulation study, or experimental comparison beyond the present theorem package.

Final go/no-go assessment
Go on the n-phase stationary double-pole theorem, beginning with the n=2 stationary proof as an internal milestone.
No-go on trying to raise the tier through further two-state quotient formulas, additional image tests, longer fibre classifications inside the same constrained cone, more delta methods, or a polished proof of the existing product-gap split expansion. Those may improve correctness but do not alter the paper’s priority class.
The structural ceiling of the current architecture is that deterministic reset converts the problem to a renewal distribution, while the population representation questions then reduce largely to standard order-two canonical coordinates, minimal-realization equivalence, or generalized-Erlang representation. The proposed stationary singular theorem escapes that ceiling because it asks a different and genuinely sharper question: what is the complete local statistical experiment, and the exact recoverability boundary, when a standard phase-type representation loses root identifiability through pole collision?
