One question about the rate of convergence of a continued-fraction power sum. Short on purpose.

SETUP. For a finite word a = (a_1, ..., a_r) of positive integers let K(a) be the regular
continuant, so K is the denominator of the continued fraction [0; a_1, ..., a_r]. Call the word
canonical when a_r >= 2, which picks exactly one representative per rational in (0,1). For
n >= 2 and s > 2 put

    Z_n(s) = sum over canonical words with a_1 + ... + a_r = n of K(a)^(-s).

Fix the critical point sigma_0 = 2.4787507857... defined by R(s) = zeta(s-1)/zeta(s) = 2.

THE CLAIM I AM TESTING. A manuscript asserts Z_n(s) ~ C n^(-s) with C = 2 R_s^2, which is 8 at
sigma_0, and states that this corrects a published value of R_s + 2 R_s^2 = 10.

WHAT THE NUMERICS SAY, and why they settle nothing. The quantity n^s Z_n rises to about 15.28,
turns over near n = 27, and by n = 29 has only begun to descend. Direct enumeration is over
2^(n-1) compositions and dies around n = 22 to 24. I wrote a Stern-Brocot walk with the two
moves (u,v) -> (v,u+v) and (u,v) -> (u,u+v), each costing one unit of digit sum, truncated on v
with a rigorous error bound; it reaches n = 24 comfortably and then exhausts memory. The
obvious tightening fails because growth is not geometric along every branch: the move
(u,v) -> (u,u+v) leaves u fixed, so from (1,v) repeated application gives (1,v+1), (1,v+2), ...,
which is linear.

So every value I have lies before the turning point, and I cannot distinguish a sequence
converging to 8 from one converging to something else. I am not claiming the manuscript is
wrong. I want the rate.

WHAT I THINK THE RIGHT INSTRUMENT IS. The generating function sum_n Z_n(s) z^n should be
governed by a transfer operator of Gauss-map type carrying the digit-sum weight,

    (L f)(x) = sum over a >= 1 of z^a (a + x)^(-s) f(1/(a+x)),

with the n^(-s) law coming from the singularity at z = 1 produced by the large-digit tail
sum_a z^a a^(-s), which is of type (1-z)^(s-1). If that is right, the leading constant and the
correction exponent should both be readable from the expansion at that singularity, and the
subleading behaviour would give the convergence rate directly rather than through finite
differences.

QUESTIONS.

One. Is the constant C = 2 R_s^2 correct, and is there a clean derivation of it from the
singularity structure rather than from a counting argument?

Two. What is the rate? Specifically, is n^s Z_n(s) - C of order n^(-1), or n^(1-s), or
logarithmic, or something else? The turnover near n = 27 suggests a competition between two
terms of comparable size at small n, and identifying both would explain it.

Three. Is the turnover itself predicted by that structure? If the correction has a sign change
or two competing exponents, saying so would let me stop treating the numerics as anomalous.

Please separate what you can derive from what you would be guessing, and say plainly if the
constant cannot be pinned down this way.
