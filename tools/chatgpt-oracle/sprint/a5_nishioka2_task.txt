There is a standard, open-access, verbatim-checkable statement of the LINEAR case:

  Bell, Coons and Rowland, "The rational-transcendental dichotomy of Mahler functions",
  arXiv:1210.2070v2, Corollary 8:
    "Let k >= 2 be an integer and F(z) in C[[z]] be a k-Mahler function. If F(z) is
     algebraic, then F(z) is a rational function."
  attributed there to
    Ku. Nishioka, "Mahler Functions and Transcendence", Lecture Notes in Mathematics
    vol. 1631, Springer-Verlag, 1996, Theorem 5.1.7.

CRUCIAL LIMITATION, and the point of this task. A k-Mahler function is by definition a
solution of a LINEAR equation sum_j a_j(z) F(z^{k^j}) = 0. Our equation

    F(z^2) = H(z)^{-1} F(z)^2

is QUADRATIC in F, hence not linear, so Ku. Nishioka's Theorem 5.1.7 does NOT cover our
rationality step. Keiji Nishioka's 1985 class f(z^p) = R(z, f(z)) with R rational is the
nonlinear class that does cover it. This is consistent with the referee's remark that
Ostrowski (1968) treats only the linear multiplicative equation.
