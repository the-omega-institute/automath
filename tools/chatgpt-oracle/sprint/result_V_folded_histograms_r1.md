1. Significance: send it for external review
I would not desk-reject this paper.
On significance alone, it clears The Fibonacci Quarterly’s threshold, though not by a large margin. The result is small, but it has the right shape for this journal: one exact if-and-only-if classification, sharp boundary values, an immediate obstruction in the complementary regime, and a recognizable Sturmian corollary. The Fibonacci normalization is essential rather than decorative. The journal describes its remit as Fibonacci numbers and related sequences, with articles intended for both research mathematicians and readers earlier in their careers; Zeckendorf-type questions remain part of its published subject matter. Fibonacci Association+1
The principal significance objection is obvious: the negative half of the classification is settled at length two because both 00 and 11 occur, while the positive halves appear to reduce to “no 11” and, after complementation, “no 00.” A hostile referee may therefore describe the theorem as an elegant observation rather than a substantial classification. That would make it too slight for a general combinatorics or symbolic-dynamics journal. It does not, in my judgment, make it too slight for The Fibonacci Quarterly. Six pages is the correct scale for it.
2. Acceptance probability as submitted
58%.
That incorporates the genuine venue fit, the modest but publishable theorem, and the risk that one referee regards the entire result as essentially a two-letter factor calculation.
3. The single highest-value change
Give a length-by-length classification in the middle regime δ<β<1−δ.
At present, that regime defeats the property “injective at every length” merely because m=2 fails. The paper apparently says nothing about whether Foldm​ remains noninjective for every m≥3, becomes injective again at some lengths, or follows a more interesting arithmetic pattern. Determining exactly which lengths are injective would remove the strongest significance objection: that the advertised all-length theorem is logically decided by one two-letter collision.
A clean finite-m theorem would raise my estimate by approximately 15 percentage points, from 58% to about 73%.
4. Weakest load-bearing step
The first point I would attack is the complement-invariance or fold-congruence lemma used in the high-density case β≥1−δ.
The complement of the rotation coding has interval length 1−β≤δ, so its blocks have no adjacent ones and are already Zeckendorf words. But injectivity transfers back to the original blocks only if the manuscript proves something equivalent to
Foldm​(u)=Foldm​(v)⟺Foldm​(1−u)=Foldm​(1−v).
The raw Fibonacci-value differences are negated by complementation, but that alone is insufficient unless equality after truncating a Zeckendorf expansion has first been identified rigorously with congruence modulo Fm+2​, or with a difference in {0,±Fm+2​} on the relevant range. Fibonacci digits are not ordinary positional digits, so “discard the top digit” cannot simply be called reduction modulo Fm+2​ without proof.
The required repair should be elementary: use
0≤N(w)≤j=2∑m+1​Fj​=Fm+3​−2
to show that only the Fm+2​-digit can be discarded and that the adjacency restriction prevents a hidden carry from changing the retained digits. If that range argument is already present, the step is sound. If the paper merely asserts modularity from truncation, it has a real gap, but a repairable, not fatal, one. I cannot determine from the synopsis whether that lemma is presently proved at the required level.
5. Journal target
The Fibonacci Quarterly is the right first target.
I would submit it there rather than diverting it to another journal. The result’s narrowness is a liability elsewhere, while its direct Fibonacci content and short exact form are positive features here.
