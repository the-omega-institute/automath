THE DIAGNOSIS. "The manuscript is excessively defensive. Its abstract, theorem statements, model
paragraphs, and discussion repeatedly state what is not being claimed. The same restrictions - one
isolated collision, fixed serial order, known sampling interval, and no general phase-type
conclusion - are given several times." He calls the qualifications mathematically appropriate but
rhetorically overrepresented, and names this the single objection most likely to harm the paper
editorially: a reader finishes "with an unusually clear memory of what the authors decline to prove
but a less immediate sense of the one general theorem and one singular application that they do
prove."

So the governing rule for this revision: each restriction is stated ONCE, where it belongs - in the
hypotheses of the theorem it qualifies - and is not repeated in the abstract, the model paragraphs,
the discussion, and the conclusion as well. The ceiling must remain visible and honest. It must
stop being the loudest thing in the paper.
