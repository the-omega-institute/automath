You assessed this manuscript as a referee for this journal, wrote a report, and then, as the editor,
gave an enumerated list of required changes. That list has been implemented. The attached PDF is the
revised manuscript.

This is the round that decides whether the revision worked, so please read the paper as it now
stands rather than checking it against your own list item by item. A manuscript can satisfy every
enumerated item and still not be acceptable, and it can miss an item and be fine. Your first
question should be the one a referee actually asks: would you accept this?

Answer in three parts.

PART ONE - THE DECISION.

Give your recommendation first, in one line: accept, accept with minor revisions, major revisions,
reject and resubmit, or reject. Then the reasoning.

Compare it explicitly to the recommendation you gave before, and say what moved it. If it did not
move, say that plainly - a revision that changes nothing material is a useful finding and we would
rather hear it than be told the paper improved. If it moved in the wrong direction, say that too:
it is possible to cut a paper into something thinner rather than sharper, and if that has happened
here we need to know now rather than after a rejection.

PART TWO - WHAT THE REVISION ACTUALLY DID.

We are not asking you to grade compliance. We are asking what changed in the reading experience.

 - Does the paper now have a visible spine - can you say, after two pages, what it proves and why
   those results belong together?
 - Is the prose still doing the thing you objected to? You described manuscripts in this project as
   reading like a record of their own previous audits, written to pre-empt hypothetical objections.
   Is that gone, reduced, or merely relocated? Point at any passage where it survives.
 - Did anything valuable go with the cuts? Name any result, argument or piece of context whose
   removal you think was a mistake, and say whether you would ask for it back.
 - Is the length now right for this journal, and is the main-text-to-appendix proportion right?
   Several papers in this project ended up below the range you estimated, because we instructed
   against padding. Say whether the result reads as appropriately concise or as thin.

PART THREE - WHAT REMAINS.

If your recommendation is anything other than accept, give the remaining items, ordered by how much
each matters, and separate them the way you did before: which need mathematics we do not yet have,
and which are writing and organization. Be specific about location.

If the honest answer is that the paper is now at the standard you would accept and the remaining
variance is referee taste and journal selectivity, say that plainly and stop. That is the outcome we
are working toward and we will act on it by submitting.

Two things we would rather you were blunt about than diplomatic.

First, if the revision has introduced a new problem - a claim that now reads as unconditional when
it is not, a result whose support left with a deleted section, a hierarchy that now buries something
important - that matters more than anything on the original list, and it should lead your report.

Second, if you think this paper is not going to clear this journal's bar regardless of further
editorial work, say so and say why. We would rather retarget than spend another cycle. A clear
statement that the ceiling is where it is, with the reason, is worth more to us than an encouraging
list of improvements.
