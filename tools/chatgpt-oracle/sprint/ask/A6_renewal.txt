Please reassess the attached manuscript as an external referee, focusing first on the new arithmetic critical stable-renewal theorem and its claim to novelty.

Background, stated without softening it: you previously recommended rejection of this paper from TAMS. You wrote that, even if the proofs were fully repaired and the priority discussion fully rewritten, you expected your final verdict to remain reject. The priority repairs have now been made. Sidorov--Vershik, Hu, Lau--Ngai, Feng--Olivier, and Feng are explicitly credited; the all-real pressure theorem and frozen-half-line statements have been demoted to attributed recovery propositions; and the title and abstract have been rebuilt so that these concessions come first. When subsequently asked what single theorem could raise the paper's tier, you identified the arithmetic critical stable-renewal theorem below, assigning roughly a 35% chance that it could be proved and a 70% chance that, if proved, it would survive a literature audit as genuinely new. The manuscript now claims a proof.

Here is the theorem and the arithmetic input on which it rests. Let \(\sigma_0>2\) be the unique solution of
\[
  \frac{\zeta(\sigma_0-1)}{\zeta(\sigma_0)}=2,
\]
and put \(\alpha=\sigma_0-1\in(1,2)\). Under the critical letter law,
\[
 \Pr\{(C,H)=(c(p/q),\log q)\}=q^{-\sigma_0}
\]
for every reduced \(p/q\in(0,1)\), where \(c(p/q)\) is the manuscript's odd continued-fraction cost. If
\[
 b_{2d+1}(s):=
 \sum_{\substack{q\ge2,\ 1\le p<q,\ (p,q)=1\\c(p/q)=2d+1}}q^{-s},
\]
then
\[
 \Pr\{C=2d+1\}=b_{2d+1}(\sigma_0).
\]
The claimed local arithmetic estimate is
\[
 \tag{T1} b_{2d+1}(\sigma_0)\sim b_Cd^{-\sigma_0}\qquad(d\to\infty),
\]
and its direct summation gives
\[
 \tag{T2} \Pr\{C>x\}\sim K_Cx^{1-\sigma_0}\qquad(x\to\infty),
\]
with
\[
 K_C=\frac{2^{\sigma_0-1}}{\sigma_0-1}b_C
     =\frac{2^{\sigma_0+2}}{\sigma_0-1}.
\]

Crucially, \(b_C\) is not merely named as the limit implicit in (T1). It is presented and evaluated independently as the absolutely convergent continuant context sum
\[
 b_C:=
 \sum_{\mathbf u\in\mathcal C_{\rm L}}
 \sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathcal C_{\rm R}}
 \{\mathcal K(\mathbf u)\mathcal K(\mathbf v)\}^{-\sigma_0}
 =2\left(\frac{\zeta(\sigma_0-1)}{\zeta(\sigma_0)}\right)^2
 =8.
\]
Here \(\mathcal K\) is the regular continuant, \(\mathcal C_{\rm L}\) consists of the empty word and all finite positive-integer words, and \(\mathcal C_{\rm R}\) consists of the empty word and all such nonempty words whose final digit is at least two. More generally, the manuscript proves for every fixed \(s>2\) that
\[
 b_{2d+1}(s)\sim b(s)d^{-s},\qquad
 b(s)=\sum_{\mathbf u\in\mathcal C_{\rm L}}
       \sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathcal C_{\rm R}}
       \{\mathcal K(\mathbf u)\mathcal K(\mathbf v)\}^{-s}
 =2\left(\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}\right)^2.
\]

The renewal consequences are also part of the theorem. Writing \(\mu_C=\mathbb E C\) and
\[
 u_j:=\sum_{r\ge0}\Pr\{C_1+\cdots+C_r=j\}
\]
for the renewal sequence, the manuscript claims
\[
 u_j=\frac1{\mu_C}
 +\frac{K_C}{\mu_C^2(\sigma_0-2)}j^{2-\sigma_0}
 +o(j^{2-\sigma_0}).
\]
For the one-layer Fibonacci partition sum
\(Z_m^R(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal I_m}R(n)^t\) and the finite-window fiber sum
\(S_t(m)=\sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^t\), the exact renewal identities then give
\[
 Z_m^R(-\sigma_0)
 =\frac{2m}{\mu_C}
 +\frac{2K_C}{\mu_C^2(\sigma_0-2)(3-\sigma_0)}m^{3-\sigma_0}
 +o(m^{3-\sigma_0}),
\]
and
\[
 S_{-\sigma_0}(m)
 =\frac{4m}{\mu_C}
 +\frac{4K_C}{\mu_C^2(\sigma_0-2)(3-\sigma_0)}m^{3-\sigma_0}
 +o(m^{3-\sigma_0}).
\]
In addition, \(C\) is claimed to lie in the domain of attraction of a spectrally positive \((\sigma_0-1)\)-stable law: if \(a_n>0\) is chosen so that \(n\Pr\{C>a_n\}\to1\), then
\[
 \frac{C_1+\cdots+C_n-n\mu_C}{a_n}
 \xrightarrow{\mathrm d}\mathcal S_{\sigma_0-1},
\]
where the Levy measure of \(\mathcal S_{\sigma_0-1}\) has density
\((\sigma_0-1)x^{-\sigma_0}\) on \((0,\infty)\).

The proof strategy for the hard arithmetic step is as follows. A canonical regular continued fraction \([0;a_1,\ldots,a_r]\), with \(a_r\ge2\), has denominator \(\mathcal K(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\) and layer parameter \(d=a_1+\cdots+a_r-1\). With \(n=d+1\), the proof first isolates a digit exceeding \(n/2\), which is necessarily unique. Cutting at that digit gives left and right contexts \((\mathbf u,\mathbf v)\), with middle digit \(a=n-|\mathbf u|_1-|\mathbf v|_1\). The affine large-digit formula
\[
 \mathcal K(\mathbf u,a,\mathbf v)
 =a\mathcal K(\mathbf u)\mathcal K(\mathbf v)+O_{\mathbf u,\mathbf v}(1)
\]
gives pointwise convergence, while continuant concatenation gives the summable majorant
\[
 n^s\mathcal K(\mathbf u,a,\mathbf v)^{-s}
 \le 2^s\{\mathcal K(\mathbf u)\mathcal K(\mathbf v)\}^{-s}.
\]
Dominated convergence therefore extracts the full left--right context sum.

The proof then treats the complement rather than assuming it negligible. Choose
\(s/[2(s-1)]<\delta<1\) and \(h=\lfloor n^\delta\rfloor\). Words with two digits at least \(h\) have total weight
\[
 O_s(h^{2-2s})=o(n^{-s}).
\]
For words with all digits below \(h\), a greedy decomposition closes a block once its digit sum reaches \(h\). If
\[
 \eta_s(h)=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf u\in\mathcal C_{\rm L}\\|\mathbf u|_1\ge h}}
 \mathcal K(\mathbf u)^{-s},
\]
then \(\eta_s(h)\to0\), and the total weight of moderate words with digit sum at least \(N\) is bounded by
\[
 \frac{L_s}{1-\eta_s(h)}
 \eta_s(h)^{\lfloor N/(2h)\rfloor-1},
\]
which is \(o(n^{-A})\) for every fixed \(A\) when \(N\ge n/4\). The remaining case, with exactly one \(h\)-large digit but no digit exceeding \(n/2\), leaves a moderate context of digit sum at least \(n/4\) and is controlled by the same bound. Thus both the two-\(n^\delta\)-large-digit regime and the all-moderate greedy-block regime are \(o(n^{-s})\). The manuscript treats this local estimate as excluding residual local lattice modulation and semistable/log-periodic oscillation, rather than averaging such effects away in a tail theorem.

Your anti-cheating condition was that this result should count only if the hard arithmetic tail estimate were proved in its own right, and not if regular variation were assumed and standard renewal theory merely applied on top. The manuscript explicitly says in its introduction: "The local denominator-layer theorem \(\Cref{thm:arithmetic-denominator-layer}\) is a separate arithmetic result; its proof does not assume the regular variation later used by the renewal transfer." Consistently with that statement, (T1) is proved first by the continuant argument above, (T2) is then obtained by direct summation, and only after that are classical arithmetic renewal and stable-domain-of-attraction results invoked. Please verify the dependency rather than accepting the prose assertion.

The theorem and proof are analytic. No numerical experiment is offered as support for (T1), (T2), \(b_C=8\), or the renewal expansion. The existing scripts are sanity checks only and are not part of the evidence for the theorem.
