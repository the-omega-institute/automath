Please act as a referee for one new theorem in main.pdf, entitled "Arbitrary-order law-by-law tail-defect decomposition." I am asking for a correctness, threshold, priority, and venue assessment, not for a sympathetic reading.

Background from your previous round: you set an explicit threshold. You wrote that correcting the literature and supplying technical detail would make the paper's theorems credible and publishable, but would not significantly change your JFA-tier judgment. You said that what would change that judgment was either (i) an arbitrary-order, law-by-law, nonnegative tail-defect decomposition, or (ii) an abstract mechanism theorem covering Gaussian, stable, and more general kernels and divergences. You also recorded that the manuscript already had, at covariance order under a finite second moment, a law-by-law decomposition separating the universal quadratic term from a nonnegative \(\Phi\)-energy of the unexpanded far tail, with vanishing of that energy as an exact necessary and sufficient condition. We have now attempted exactly the first of your two options.

Here is the complete statement and the proof mechanism that I would like you to audit. Let \(d\geq 1\), \(0<\alpha<2\), and let \(r\geq 1\) be an integer. Let \(p_1^{(\alpha,d)}\) be the density of a centred isotropic strictly \(\alpha\)-stable law on \(\mathbb R^d\), and use the spatial scaling
\[
p_s^{(\alpha,d)}(x)=s^{-d}p_1^{(\alpha,d)}(x/s),\qquad s>0.
\]
Set
\[
\Omega_{\alpha,d}(dy)=p_1^{(\alpha,d)}(y)\,dy,
\qquad
F_y^{(\alpha,d)}(z)=\frac{p_1^{(\alpha,d)}(y-z)}{p_1^{(\alpha,d)}(y)},
\]
and let \(\Phi(u)=(1+u)\log(1+u)-u\), whose natural domain is \((-1,\infty)\). For a multi-index \(\gamma\), write
\[
m_\gamma(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}x^\gamma\,\lambda(dx).
\]
Let \(\nu\) and \(\eta\) be probability laws on \(\mathbb R^d\) satisfying
\[
m_\gamma(\nu)=m_\gamma(\eta)\quad\text{for every }|\gamma|<r,
\qquad
\int_{\mathbb R^d}|x|^r\,(\nu+\eta)(dx)<\infty.
\]
No nonzero first-unmatched moment is assumed. For \(\lambda\in\{\nu,\eta\}\), define the order-\(j\) moment mode
\[
B_j^\lambda(y)
=\frac{(-1)^j}{p_1^{(\alpha,d)}(y)}
  \sum_{|\gamma|=j}\frac{m_\gamma(\lambda)}{\gamma!}
  \partial^\gamma p_1^{(\alpha,d)}(y),
\]
the exterior mass and the unexpanded exterior translate
\[
\tau_s^\lambda=\lambda(\{|x|>s\}),
\qquad
V_s^\lambda(y)=\int_{\{|x|>s\}}
F_y^{(\alpha,d)}(x/s)\,\lambda(dx),
\]
and the retained common lower-order jet
\[
C_{r-1,s}(y)
=\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}s^{-j}B_j^\nu(y)
=\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}s^{-j}B_j^\eta(y).
\]
The equality of the two expressions for \(C_{r-1,s}\) follows from moment matching below order \(r\); the sum is empty when \(r=1\). Define the two tail-background proxies
\[
A_s^\lambda(y)
=1+C_{r-1,s}(y)+V_s^\lambda(y)-\tau_s^\lambda.
\]
The theorem asserts that, for all sufficiently large \(s\), both \(A_s^\nu\) and \(A_s^\eta\) are strictly positive and satisfy
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^d}A_s^\lambda\,d\Omega_{\alpha,d}=1.
\]
Consequently they produce genuine Lebesgue probability densities
\[
k_{r,s}^\lambda(x)
=s^{-d}p_1^{(\alpha,d)}(x/s)A_s^\lambda(x/s).
\]
The proposed nonnegative tail-defect energy is their Kullback--Leibler divergence:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_{r,s}(\nu,\eta)
&=D_{\rm KL}(k_{r,s}^\nu\Vert k_{r,s}^\eta)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb R^d}A_s^\eta
\Phi\!\left(\frac{A_s^\nu-A_s^\eta}{A_s^\eta}\right)
\,d\Omega_{\alpha,d}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The theorem says that this energy is finite. With
\[
\Delta_r=\int_{\mathbb R^d}x^{\otimes r}\,(\nu-\eta)(dx),
\]
define
\[
B_{\Delta_r}(y)
=\frac{(-1)^r}{r!p_1^{(\alpha,d)}(y)}
  \langle\Delta_r,D^rp_1^{(\alpha,d)}(y)\rangle
=B_r^\nu(y)-B_r^\eta(y)
\]
and
\[
\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)
=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R^d}B_{\Delta_r}(y)^2\,d\Omega_{\alpha,d}(y).
\]
Then, law by law as \(s\to\infty\),
\[
D_{\rm KL}\!\left(p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\nu\,\Vert\,
p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\eta\right)
=\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)s^{-2r}
+\mathcal E_{r,s}(\nu,\eta)+o(s^{-2r}).
\]
In particular, the theorem gives the exact necessary-and-sufficient tail criterion
\[
s^{2r}D_{\rm KL}\!\left(p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\nu\,\Vert\,
p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\eta\right)
\longrightarrow\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
s^{2r}\mathcal E_{r,s}(\nu,\eta)\longrightarrow0.
\]
There is no regular-variation assumption, no absolute-continuity assumption on either input law, and no moment assumption above order \(r\).

The obstruction this construction is designed to overcome is the following. For \(r\geq3\), the natural tail difference \(V_s^\nu-V_s^\eta\) is signed. Applying \(\Phi\) directly to that difference can leave the domain \((-1,\infty)\), and in any event supplies no structural nonnegativity. Here nonnegativity is obtained by construction, because \(\mathcal E_{r,s}\) is a KL divergence between two genuine probability densities; it is not obtained by an inequality applied to a signed tail expression.

For clarity, the key analytic expansion in the proof is as follows. If
\[
q_s^\lambda(y)=\int F_y^{(\alpha,d)}(x/s)\,\lambda(dx),
\]
then the exact split is
\[
q_s^\lambda=A_s^\lambda+s^{-r}B_r^\lambda+R_s^\lambda,
\qquad
\|R_s^\lambda\|_\infty+
\|R_s^\lambda\|_{L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d})}=o(s^{-r}).
\]
Also \(\tau_s^\lambda=o(s^{-r})\), \(V_s^\lambda\geq0\),
\(\int V_s^\lambda\,d\Omega_{\alpha,d}=\tau_s^\lambda\), and
\(\int B_j^\lambda\,d\Omega_{\alpha,d}=0\). These facts are used both to prove eventual positivity and unit mass of \(A_s^\lambda\) and to control the backgrounds.

Set \(\epsilon=s^{-r}\), \(U_\lambda=\epsilon B_r^\lambda\), and
\(f(a,b)=a\log(a/b)-a+b\). After removing the \(R_s^\lambda\) terms at cost \(o(\epsilon^2)\), the proof expands
\[
\int f(A_s^\nu+tU_\nu,A_s^\eta+tU_\eta)\,d\Omega_{\alpha,d}
\]
at \(t=0\). The first variation is claimed to be \(o(\epsilon^2)=o(s^{-2r})\). More explicitly, it is
\[
\int\left\{U_\nu\log\frac{A_s^\nu}{A_s^\eta}
+U_\eta\left(1-\frac{A_s^\nu}{A_s^\eta}\right)\right\}
\,d\Omega_{\alpha,d}=o(s^{-2r}).
\]
The second-order Taylor contribution (one half of the Hessian) is claimed to be
\[
\frac12s^{-2r}\int
(B_r^\nu-B_r^\eta)^2\,d\Omega_{\alpha,d}+o(s^{-2r}),
\]
using the exact Hessian expression
\[
\int\left\{
\frac{U_\nu^2}{A_s^\nu}
-\frac{2U_\nu U_\eta}{A_s^\eta}
+\frac{A_s^\nu U_\eta^2}{(A_s^\eta)^2}
\right\}d\Omega_{\alpha,d}.
\]
The third-order Taylor remainder is claimed to be \(O(\epsilon^3)=O(s^{-3r})\), from a third-derivative bound by \(C\epsilon^3(1+A_s^\nu)\). This two-background expansion is the only machinery we claim is new. Every other step--the raw-tail split, normalization, finite-moment dominated convergence, and remainder handling--is inherited from the order-two proof.

We also performed the following consistency check. When \(r=2\), \(\eta=\delta_0\), and \(\nu\) is centred, the new energy reduces to
\[
\int\Phi(V_s^\nu-\tau_s^\nu)\,d\Omega_{\alpha,d},
\]
and differs by \(o(s^{-4})\) from the existing one-sided order-two energy
\(\int\Phi(V_s^\nu)\,d\Omega_{\alpha,d}\).

This theorem is proved analytically. No numerical battery supports it. Our existing numerical scripts are sanity checks only and were not used as evidence for the theorem.

Please answer the following as a referee:

1. Is the proof correct? Name every step that you cannot follow or believe is wrong. In particular, please audit specifically (a) positivity of \(A_s^\lambda\) for all sufficiently large \(s\), (b) the assertion that each \(A_s^\lambda\) integrates to one, (c) the \(o(s^{-2r})\) first variation, (d) the Hessian identification with \(\frac12s^{-2r}\int(B_r^\nu-B_r^\eta)^2\,d\Omega_{\alpha,d}\), and (e) the \(O(s^{-3r})\) third-order remainder. Please distinguish a missing written justification from a false claim.

2. Is this genuinely the result you named as the tier threshold, or does it fall short of what you meant by an "arbitrary-order law-by-law nonnegative tail-defect decomposition"? In particular, does retaining the common lower-order jet in both proxy densities and defining the defect as their relative entropy satisfy that phrase in the substantive sense you intended? If it falls short, please say precisely how and formulate the additional statement that would meet your threshold.

3. Is the result new? Please name the nearest prior work and compare hypotheses and conclusions. A referee on a sibling paper of ours found an omitted prior result that destroyed that paper's priority claim, so this is critical rather than ceremonial: does an arbitrary-order tail-defect decomposition of this kind already exist in the literature, for Gaussian kernels, stable kernels, or both? Please separate prior first-unmatched-moment KL asymptotics from prior nonnegative law-by-law tail-defect decompositions with an exact vanishing criterion.

4. With this theorem included, what is the honest venue judgment now, especially relative to JFA, and what else would be required for that judgment to improve? Please make the judgment conditional on any correctness or priority concern you identify rather than averaging those concerns away.

A hard rejection with reasons is more useful to us than a soft acceptance. We would rather learn now that the proof is wrong than after submission. Please attach to every objection a concrete account of what would resolve it: a missing estimate or lemma, a corrected hypothesis, a narrower theorem, a counterexample that forces withdrawal, or a specific literature citation that defeats priority.
