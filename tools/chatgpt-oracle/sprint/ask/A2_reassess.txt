WHAT WAS DONE

The pilot lemma was run first, as you instructed, and it passed under exactly the finite
(d+alpha)-moment hypothesis. No moment margin and no auxiliary density assumption were needed. The
(d+alpha) moment is used at exactly one point, the upper quotient bound

    p_1(y-w)/p_1(y) <~ (1 + |w|)^{d+alpha};

the bounded stable score supplies the strictly positive lower bound and the gradient estimate
|grad log Q_t^lambda| <= 2 ||grad log p_1||_infinity. For u = Q^mu/Q^nu, the compact range of u and
bounded grad log u give Lambda(u(y),u(z)) <~ |y-z|^2 near the diagonal, so the kernel is bounded by
|y-z|^{2-d-alpha} locally and |y-z|^{-d-alpha} away from it, both integrable for 0 < alpha < 2.

Your principal uncertainty was addressed directly rather than avoided. The noncompact Green
pairing is closed through an annular operator A_{eps,R} converging to (-Delta)^{alpha/2} in L^1 for
every test function in W^{2,1}, with small jumps controlled by the symmetric second-difference
estimate and large jumps by the tail integral. Stable smoothing puts f_t, g_t in W^{2,1}; the
pilot bounds make u_t and log u_t bounded; the pairings with (f_t, log u_t) and (g_t, u_t)
therefore converge separately in L^1, and only after both limits exist is the finite-annulus
algebra combined into the nonnegative Lambda-form. No cancellation at infinity is assumed
formally.

All six parts you specified are proved, with the stable-reference version and the dimension-one
change of variables q = s_0 t/(1-t) giving the interpolation representation.

On novelty, your warning was taken literally. The manuscript now states in the text that the
two-point logarithmic algebra is not new, and cites the Hardy-Stein, polarized Hardy-Stein,
Sobolev-Bregman and pure-jump Dirichlet-form literature, including Klimsiak-Rozkosz, whose
conditional Hardy-Stein identities already cover ratios of harmonic functions and general convex
functions. The novelty claim is confined to the moving-denominator parabolic theorem for two
symmetric stable heat flows from measure data, its explicit sufficient finite-moment domain, the
separate noncompact and t = infinity closures, and the connection to Johnson's integral-
representation problem. The manuscript explicitly disclaims any theorem for all isotropic unimodal
Levy semigroups or all subordinate Brownian motions.

On the domain, the exponent d+alpha is presented only as a natural sufficient hypothesis. Where it
is genuinely used is stated; finite first moment, finite initial relative entropy, and intrinsic
Dirichlet-form domains are left explicitly open rather than claimed as settled or optimal.

The paper grew from 79 to 86 pages and from 25 to 26 numbered theorems. The abstract and
introduction now lead with the stable-flow theorem and treat the RKHS and lattice material as
secondary, which is the architecture change you asked for.
