STANDARD INPUTS IMPORTED AND CITED, NOT CLAIMED AS OURS

1. Universal satisfaction on nonempty teams of assignments, singleton-team agreement with ordinary Tarskian semantics, and persistence under team refinement are standard team-semantic facts.

2. Presheaves, the plus-plus construction of sheafification, separatedness, stackification of categories fibred in groupoids, and the identification of the component sheaf of a stackification with the sheafification of the presheaf of components are standard sheaf and stack theory. The relevant sources are Mac Lane--Moerdijk and the Stacks Project.

3. Classification of abelian-band gerbes by Giraud H^2, neutrality as existence of a global object, the local-object/overlap-isomorphism/triple-overlap-defect construction of a Cech 2-cocycle, change by coboundary, and transport under banded equivalence are standard Giraud--Breen--Brylinski--Murray gerbe theory.

4. The Cech-to-derived passage is never claimed without an explicit comparison isomorphism or a Leray good-cover hypothesis. The nerve lemma, open-star good covers of barycentric subdivisions, and the identification of ordered Cech cochains with simplicial cochains on the selected finite nerve are standard.

5. The universal coefficient exact sequence

   0 -> Ext^1(H_1(N;Z),A) -> H^2(N;A) -> Hom(H_2(N;Z),A) -> 0,

   its naturality, injectivity of the divisible group T, Pontryagin character separation for finite abelian groups, elementary subgroup identities, and the structure theorem for finite abelian groups are imported algebraic inputs.

6. The Abramsky--Brandenburger support-presheaf interface is imported. The Abramsky--Mansfield--Barbosa obstruction is the relative Cech H^1 class gamma_e(s) of a presheaf, equivalently a torsor class indexed by a chosen local section; Caru's torsor account and the later Barbosa--Kharoof--Okay comparison results are prior work. We do not identify that H^1 class with our H^2 gerbe class.

WHAT THE REVISED PAPER CLAIMS AS ITS OWN CONTRIBUTION

The originality claim is deliberately limited to: organizing those inputs into the stated typed semantic framework; isolating exact hypotheses under which matching without amalgamation is equivalent to non-neutral component gerbes; a particular presentation-comparison groupoid and its presentation-relative homological image/cokernel; the two finite-family aggregate quotients and their comparison; explicit finite-site prestacks realizing prescribed component classes and homological images; the resulting subgroup and finite-group existence classifications; a restricted lower-language separation theorem; a terminal-fibre rigidity theorem for strictly pullback-stable representatives; and the exact negative comparison with bare empirical models. The quotient and exact-sequence algebra is presented as formal once its subgroup data are fixed. We do not claim a new general classification of gerbes, a presentation-free invariant, a classification of empirical models, or an unconditional construction of non-split lifts from lower-layer objects.

Here are the principal results, with their full operative hypotheses.

A. Semantic and sheafification results, mostly supporting material.

An information state is p=(Gamma,R), with R a nonempty team of assignments in the finite context Gamma, and M,p |= phi means that every assignment in R satisfies phi in ordinary Tarskian semantics. The map from point assignments to singleton teams preserves satisfaction. The higher layers L_1 <= L_2 <= L_3 <= L_4 are truth-preserving reduct expansions: each is the class of supplied expansions by data of the stipulated types, and lower-language atomic clauses are interpreted through the reduct. This is explicitly a definition of broad generated model classes, not an expansion-existence or closure theorem for an independently given restricted class.

For an admissible parameter r at p, let a=[r]_p, let C_a be the pullback-based slice site, and let F:C_a^op -> Set be the supplied presheaf of local sections. If F is separated at every object of C_a, then a strict matching family on a cover of a exists if and only if (aF)(a) is nonempty. The forward implication needs no separatedness; the reverse uses the plus-plus representation and separatedness on every cover member, pullback overlap, and refinement, not merely at a. Thus sheafification removes matching-without-amalgamation for this separated restriction.

The supplement also proves a deliberately narrow undefinability result: in one explicit model there are four admissible reference elements that are indistinguishable by every parameter-free, constant-free, automorphism-invariant one-variable formula of the Sigma_1 reduct, while respectively realizing (matching and global), (matching but no global), (local but no matching), and (no local section). Hence the six corresponding local/global failure predicates are not definable on that admissible subdomain in that restricted fragment. No claim is made for formulas with parameters, tuples, the Sigma_2 presheaf structure, or arbitrary lower languages.

B. Component gerbes and the matching-without-amalgamation criterion.

Let E be an A-banded stack on C_a equipped with an isomorphism pi_0(E) ~= aF. For every global component section v in (aF)(a), the full substack E[v] on objects with component v is an A-banded gerbe. Its Giraud class defines

   delta_E:(aF)(a) -> H^2(C_a,A),    v |-> [E[v]],

and [E[v]]=0 iff E[v] is neutral iff E[v](a) is nonempty. The map is transported by a banded equivalence E -> E' compatible with the component-sheaf identifications and the induced band isomorphism. This is a componentwise packaging of standard gerbe classification, not a new classification theorem.

For the comparison with the original presheaf predicate, the paper separately assumes: (H1) a chosen banded groupoid lift P_r of F and its stackification L_r; (H2) a fixed abelian band sheaf A on C_a; (H3) essential surjectivity P_r(a) -> L_r(a) on the terminal fibre; and separatedness of F on all of C_a. Under exactly these assumptions,

   matching without an amalgamation in F(a)

holds iff (aF)(a) is nonempty and every component gerbe G_v is non-neutral. For the equivalent formulation by nonzero derived classes omega_v, the additional hypothesis (H4) is required: for every v there is a filtered cofinal family of covers whose needed intersections trivialize the band and for which colim_U Cech H^2(U,A) -> H^2(C_a,A) is an isomorphism (a Leray condition is a sufficient verification). Without H4, only the cover-relative Cech class is asserted.

C. Terminal-fibre rigidity from the supplement.

A representative-rigid lift means that one representative of each presheaf section is chosen and preserved strictly by every pullback. Assume such an A-banded groupoid lift P_r, assume F is separated at every object of C_a, and write u:F -> aF. Essential surjectivity on the terminal fibre implies that u_a is surjective. Conversely, if H^1(C_a,A)=0, surjectivity of u_a implies terminal essential surjectivity. Under that H^1-vanishing, the following are equivalent: terminal essential surjectivity; u_a is bijective; and F satisfies the sheaf gluing condition at a. Consequently a separated presheaf with matching without amalgamation cannot have a representative-rigid lift that is terminally essentially surjective. The paper treats this as a rigidity result showing that a useful non-split lift must contain genuinely noncanonical lift data.

D. Finite-nerve homological image and cokernel.

Fix one global component v, one finite cover U with finite nerve N, a trivialization of its band as a constant finite abelian group A, and a selected finite-nerve class omega in H^2(N;A). If omega is also called a derived component class, H4 or the explicit finite good-cover Leray theorem is assumed. Let

   ev_omega:H_2(N;Z) -> A

be the UCT evaluation, K_omega=Im(ev_omega), and Q_omega=A/K_omega. These are only presentation-relative. They transport along a specified groupoid freely generated by: refinements whose nerve map induces an H_2-isomorphism and pulls back omega; cohomologous cocycle replacement; band retrivialization; and formal inverses. Different zigzags may induce different band automorphisms; no path independence or invariant outside this comparison groupoid is claimed.

For X_omega={chi in Hom(A,T): chi_*(omega)=0}, UCT naturality and Ext^1(H_1(N),T)=0 give

   X_omega=Ann(K_omega),
   K_omega=intersection_{chi in X_omega} ker(chi),
   Q_omega ~= A/K_omega.

For every surjection q:A -> B, q factors uniquely through A -> Q_omega iff q kills K_omega iff q_*(omega) has zero H_2-evaluation iff q_*(omega) lies in Ext^1(H_1(N;Z),B). If H_1(N;Z)=0, these are also equivalent to q_*(omega)=0. A class has K_omega=0 exactly when it lies in the UCT Ext^1 kernel. These are UCT and finite-duality consequences attached to the selected presentation; the paper's contribution is their organization and use, not the underlying algebra.

For a chosen 2-cocycle alpha, the supplement also defines the coordinate subgroup H_alpha generated by all displayed values alpha_ijk and the quotient A/H_alpha. It proves K_omega is contained in H_alpha and that A/K_omega surjects onto A/H_alpha. This coordinate quotient is explicitly cocycle-representative-dependent.

E. Finite-family aggregation.

Assume a finite nonempty set V of global component sections, a common finite abelian band A, and a finite-nerve representative for every omega_v. Put K_v=Im(ev_omega_v). The label-aware and common-character quotients are

   Q_lab=A/(intersection_v K_v),
   Q_com=A/(sum_v K_v).

The canonical map Q_lab -> Q_com sits in the short exact sequence

   0 -> (sum_v K_v)/(intersection_v K_v)
     -> Q_lab -> Q_com -> 0.

The kernel is called the component-forgetting kernel. This is elementary finite-group algebra once the K_v are supplied. The associated minimum auxiliary-register cardinalities are just exact fibre cardinalities: |K_omega| for one quotient, |(sum K_v)/(intersection K_v)| for the comparison, and max_v |K_v| for one common register separating every componentwise quotient.

F. Explicit finite-site construction and subgroup classification, from the supplement.

Let U be a finite good-cover presentation with connected finite nerve N, and let C_U be the finite intersection site, including a strict initial object for empty intersections. Let A_U be the genuine constant sheaf associated with a finite abelian group A, assume H_1(N;Z)=0, and use the specified Leray comparison. For a finite nonempty label set Lambda and arbitrary homomorphisms

   phi_lambda:H_2(N;Z) -> A,

the supplement explicitly constructs a presheaf F and an A_U-banded prestack P by normalized simplicial 2-cocycles. In the broad typed-expansion model class, its stackification has global components indexed by Lambda, component class omega_lambda with ev_omega_lambda=phi_lambda and K_lambda=Im(phi_lambda), and neutral components exactly where phi_lambda=0. The prestack is terminally essentially surjective, with the empty-to-empty case counted as vacuous when no component is neutral. Matching always exists; matching without amalgamation holds iff every phi_lambda is nonzero. The aggregate quotients are exactly A/(intersection Im phi_lambda) and A/(sum Im phi_lambda).

If H_2(N;Z) ~= Z^beta, a family of subgroups K_lambda <= A is attainable by this construction iff each K_lambda is generated by at most beta elements. This is an existence theorem internal to the fixed finite nerve, constant band, Leray comparison, explicitly constructed non-split prestack, and broad typed-expansion convention. It is not an existence theorem for a lift canonically obtained from an empirical model or other pre-existing standard object.

G. Main finite-group classification.

Fix beta>=1 and a finite connected simplicial complex K_beta homotopy equivalent to the wedge of beta copies of S^2. For every m>=0 take the open-star cover of Sd^m K_beta. Let C_a be the pullback site generated by X_beta=|K_beta|, a strict initial empty object, and all nonempty finite intersections of all those covers, with the generated topology. These open-star covers are a filtered cofinal good-cover family satisfying H4 for every constant finite abelian band.

For a finite abelian group G, the following are equivalent:

   (i) G is nonzero, its minimum generator number d(G) is at most 2 beta, and G is not cyclic of order p^n for a prime p and n>=1;

   (ii) within the broad typed-expansion class, there are a presheaf and a chosen non-split G-banded groupoid lift on that site with exactly two global components v_1,v_2, both component gerbes non-neutral, and

       K_{v_1} intersection K_{v_2}=0,
       K_{v_1}+K_{v_2}=G.

When these conditions hold, Q_lab and the component-forgetting kernel are both isomorphic to G, Q_com=0, and matching without amalgamation holds. The proof combines the explicit construction above with the elementary finite-abelian criterion that G decomposes as G_1 direct-sum G_2, with both G_i nonzero and d(G_i)<=beta, iff d(G)<=2 beta and G is not a cyclic p-group. The statement is not claimed for bare empirical models.

H. Exact limit of the empirical-model comparison.

For a finite Abramsky--Brandenburger empirical model e with support presheaf S_e, compatible supported local families are naturally the same as global supported assignments and the terminally augmented sheafified sections. Thus strong contextuality is absence of a compatible family, not matching without amalgamation in the present sense. There is a canonical discrete zero-band stack Disc(aS_e), and after a band A is separately supplied there is a canonical split lift Disc(aS_e) x BA. Every component gerbe of that split lift is neutral, so every Giraud class and K_v is zero. These assignments are pseudonatural only for the context refinements explicitly specified in the paper. No component-section obstruction map with component sheaf aS_e can recover AMB classes for all finite models: a strongly contextual model has (aS_e)(X)=empty, so the H^2 component map has empty domain, while AMB's H^1 classes remain indexed by local sections and can be nonzero. Therefore the wedge classification transfers only after an extra band and non-split banded lift are supplied; it is not a classification of groups canonically attached to empirical models.

THE ONE EXTERNAL ROUTE PREVIOUSLY IDENTIFIED

You previously identified the explicit comparison between a crossed-module characteristic 3-class and the Cech 2-class of a lifting gerbe, through the Neeb--Wagemann--Wockel Cechization map tau, as the one named open route worth betting on, at roughly 45% probability of success. The precise reference is Neeb--Wagemann--Wockel, "Making Lifting Obstructions Explicit," Proc. London Math. Soc. 106 (2013), 589--620, Problem 8.1(b), DOI 10.1112/plms/pds047, arXiv:1108.5853. NWW ask whether

   tau([c]) = +/- delta_1([G])

in Cech H^2(Q,Z).

One correction is essential. A lifted conjugation action on a central extension does not automatically make K-hat -> G a crossed module. Any serious restricted version must assume the strict crossed-module structure, including both Peiffer identities, or add an equivalent Peiffer compatibility condition. The present article's concluding remark now assumes that K-hat -> G is a crossed module and expressly says that the comparison is not proved.

What this paper already supplies on the lifting-gerbe side is limited but concrete: the local objects, overlap isomorphisms, triple-overlap defect 2-cocycle, change by Cech coboundary, cover refinement, Cech-to-derived comparison when H4 holds, and transport of Giraud classes under compatible banded equivalence. What it does not supply is a chain model for locally continuous or locally smooth H^3, a representative formula for the crossed-module characteristic 3-cocycle, NWW's tau calculation in the same cover system as the lifting gerbe, the chain cochain/homotopy identifying the two representatives, determination of the sign, or the required naturality. The UCT image/cokernel results are only downstream consequences after such an identity has been established; they cannot establish it.
