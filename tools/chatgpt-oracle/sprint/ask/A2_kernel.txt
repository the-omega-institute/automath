You have already reviewed this paper three times. In the previous round, you accepted Theorem 4.27, the arbitrary-order, law-by-law nonnegative tail-defect decomposition, as meeting the tier threshold you had named. You also accepted the proof closure, the non-vacuousness corollary, the convention-robustness result, and the asymptotic coefficient-rigidity statement, finding no false step in any of them. You judged that the mechanism "reads as a central theorem rather than an isolated successful construction" and that a JFA submission was credible. You then listed five developments, each of which would be a substantial further improvement and would make the mechanism itself the central theorem. The first was an abstract kernel theorem encompassing stable, Gaussian, Poisson, and other polynomial-tail kernels, the same development you originally identified as option (b) of the tier threshold. This revision attempts exactly that first development.

The new result is the unnumbered "Abstract positive tail-jet kernel theorem." Fix an integer $r\geq 1$. A strictly positive probability density $p\in C^r(\mathbb R^d)$ is called tail-jet admissible of order $r$ if:

(K1) For every multi-index $\gamma$ with $1\leq |\gamma|\leq r$,
$$
\left\|\frac{\partial^\gamma p}{p}\right\|_\infty<\infty.
$$

(K2) The top normalized derivative has the quotient translation modulus
$$
\omega_{p,r}(t):=\max_{|\gamma|=r}\ \sup_{|h|\leq t}
\left\|\frac{\partial^\gamma p(\cdot-h)-\partial^\gamma p}{p}\right\|_\infty,
\qquad 0<t\leq1,
$$
finite for $0<t\leq1$ and satisfying $\omega_{p,r}(t)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$.

(K3) The translate entropy is finite and, for some $C_{p,r}<\infty$,
$$
\mathsf H_p(z):=D_{\rm KL}\bigl(p(\cdot-z)\|p\bigr)
\leq C_{p,r}(1+|z|^r),\qquad z\in\mathbb R^d.
$$

Put $p_s(x)=s^{-d}p(x/s)$, $\Omega_p(dy)=p(y)\,dy$, and $F_y^p(z)=p(y-z)/p(y)$. For a law $\lambda$ with finite $r$th absolute moment define
$$
B_{p,j}^\lambda(y)=\frac{(-1)^j}{p(y)}
\sum_{|\gamma|=j}\frac{m_\gamma(\lambda)}{\gamma!}\partial^\gamma p(y),
\qquad 1\leq j\leq r.
$$
If probability laws $\nu,\eta$ satisfy
$$
m_\gamma(\nu)=m_\gamma(\eta)\quad(|\gamma|<r),
\qquad \int |x|^r(\nu+\eta)(dx)<\infty,
$$
set, for $\lambda\in\{\nu,\eta\}$,
$$
\tau_s^\lambda=\lambda(|x|>s),\qquad
V_{p,s}^\lambda(y)=\int_{|x|>s}F_y^p(x/s)\lambda(dx),
$$
and define the common retained jet and the two proxies by
$$
C_{p,r-1,s}=\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}s^{-j}B_{p,j}^\nu
=\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}s^{-j}B_{p,j}^\eta,
\qquad
A_{p,s}^\lambda=1+C_{p,r-1,s}+V_{p,s}^\lambda-\tau_s^\lambda,
$$
with the sum empty for $r=1$. The theorem says that, for all sufficiently large $s$, both $A_{p,s}^\lambda$ are positive probability densities relative to $\Omega_p$. Consequently
$$
k_{p,r,s}^\lambda(x)=s^{-d}p(x/s)A_{p,s}^\lambda(x/s)
$$
are probability densities and
$$
\mathcal E_{p,r,s}(\nu,\eta)
:=D_{\rm KL}(k_{p,r,s}^\nu\|k_{p,r,s}^\eta)
$$
is finite and nonnegative. If
$$
\Delta_r=\int x^{\otimes r}(\nu-\eta)(dx),\qquad
B_{p,\Delta_r}(y)=\frac{(-1)^r}{r!p(y)}
\langle\Delta_r,D^rp(y)\rangle,
$$
and
$$
\mathcal Q_{p,r}(\Delta_r)=\frac12\int B_{p,\Delta_r}^2\,d\Omega_p,
$$
then
$$
D_{\rm KL}(p_s*\nu\|p_s*\eta)
=\mathcal Q_{p,r}(\Delta_r)s^{-2r}
+\mathcal E_{p,r,s}(\nu,\eta)+o(s^{-2r}).
$$
Moreover, $\mathcal Q_{p,r}$ is finite and positive definite on $\operatorname{Sym}^r(\mathbb R^d)$, and hence, law by law,
$$
s^{2r}D_{\rm KL}(p_s*\nu\|p_s*\eta)\to\mathcal Q_{p,r}(\Delta_r)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
s^{2r}\mathcal E_{p,r,s}(\nu,\eta)\to0.
$$

The manuscript explicitly assigns a separate proof function to each hypothesis: (K1) defines and bounds the retained modes, makes their integrals vanish, and supplies the bounded-logarithmic-gradient lower bound; (K2) supplies the uniform little-$o$ Taylor remainder, with its $L^1$ counterpart following from (K1); and (K3) is used only to make the retained-tail and proxy entropies finite. The theorem assumes no decay law, regular variation, semigroup structure, radial symmetry, or isotropy.

The accompanying unnumbered proposition, "Verified kernels and the Gaussian boundary," proves that the theorem applies, for every $r\geq1$, to every isotropic strictly $\alpha$-stable density with $0<\alpha<2$, to the Poisson kernel in every dimension, and to every multivariate Student kernel
$$
p_{\beta,d}(y)=c_{\beta,d}(1+|y|^2)^{-\beta/2},\qquad \beta>d.
$$
Within the Student family, only $\beta=d+1$ is the Poisson/stable case. Thus the verified class contains polynomial-tail members that are not the kernel on which the original proof was built. For a stable density, the specialization uses exactly the paper's existing $F_y$, moment modes, proxies, energy, and quadratic form, and therefore reproduces Theorem 4.27 unchanged rather than merely implying a weakened version of it.

The proposition also gives a sharp obstruction for the standard Gaussian $g$. One has
$$
\partial^\gamma g/g=(-1)^{|\gamma|}H_\gamma,
\qquad \nabla\log g(y)=-y,
$$
so the normalized derivatives are unbounded and (K1) already fails at the first derivative. Its translate quotient is
$$
F_y^g(z)=\exp\{y\cdot z-|z|^2/2\},
$$
whose infimum over $y$ is zero for every $z\neq0$; this removes the uniform lower bound required by the two-background expansion. The additive proxy itself fails concretely in dimension one at $r=2$: with $\nu=\delta_m$ and $\eta=(\delta_{m-a}+\delta_{m+a})/2$, where $m\neq0$ and $a>0$, the two laws match all moments below order two. For $s>|m|+a$ their retained tails vanish, and both prescribed proxies are
$$
1+\frac{my}{s},
$$
which is negative on a half-line. Thus proxy positivity, the background lower bound, and the bounded cubic remainder fail at the same additive-jet step. Gaussian translate entropy is not the obstruction for $r\geq2$, since $\mathsf H_g(z)=|z|^2/2$. The claim is not that Gaussian first-unmatched-moment asymptotics are false; weighted Hermite methods can prove them under stronger input assumptions. The claim is that they are not instances of this positive additive tail-proxy mechanism.

All of these arguments are analytic. There is no numerical support for the theorem, the kernel verifications, or the Gaussian exclusion; the existing scripts are sanity checks only.

Please assess the following as a referee:

1. Is the abstract positive tail-jet kernel theorem correct? Please identify any step in its proof that you cannot follow or believe is wrong, rather than giving only a global verdict.

2. Most importantly, is this hypothesis set genuinely minimal and genuinely abstract, or is it merely a restatement of what the stable proof happened to need, dressed as axioms? This is the same trap you checked for in the admissible class in the previous round. Does any hypothesis do no actual work? Can any hypothesis be weakened without losing the conclusion? Are the stable, Poisson, and full Student families enough to demonstrate that the class is not built around one kernel, particularly given that only $\beta=d+1$ in the Student family is Poisson/stable?

3. You asked for stable, Gaussian, Poisson, and other polynomial-tail kernels. The theorem covers stable, Poisson, and Student kernels, while Gaussian smoothing is excluded by a counterexample rather than included. Does this sharp Gaussian exclusion satisfy the substance of what you asked for, or does it fall short? If this mechanism genuinely cannot reach Gaussian smoothing, is identifying that boundary itself a result worth stating prominently, or is it a limitation that caps the paper?

4. Is the Gaussian counterexample correct, and does it prove exactly what the manuscript claims it proves? In particular, does it establish failure of the positive additive tail-proxy mechanism, without overclaiming failure of Gaussian moment asymptotics by other methods?

5. Taking Theorem 4.27 together with its non-vacuousness corollary, the convention-robustness and coefficient-rigidity statements, and now the abstract theorem and its verified kernel class, is the mechanism now the central theorem in the sense you meant? What is your honest venue judgment at this point, and what would still be required?

A hard rejection with reasons is more useful to us than a soft acceptance. We would rather learn now that the abstraction is hollow than learn it after submission. Please attach to every objection a concrete account of what would resolve it: a missing lemma or estimate, a weakened or replaced hypothesis, an additional genuinely distinct kernel, a narrower claim, a structural rewrite, or some other specific remedy.
