The manuscript is "Minimal preimages of the Fibonacci rank map: squarefree fibers and weighted covers" (33 pages). Its current venue standing is a resubmission to The Fibonacci Quarterly following a reject-and-resubmit decision; it is not an accepted paper. 1. The machinery actually present

Please assess what the following apparatus can be pointed at. This is a description of inputs and outputs, not a claim that every output is a new theorem.

1. Rank divisibility and lcm reduction. The paper consumes the classical identities

  q | F_m iff alpha(q) | m,
  gcd(F_r,F_s) = F_gcd(r,s),
  alpha(lcm(u,v)) = lcm(alpha(u),alpha(v)).

Given a factorization m = product p_i^{e_i} with m | F_n, it replaces m by the labels lambda_i = alpha(p_i^{e_i}) and the lowered labels lambda_i^- = alpha(p_i^{e_i-1}). It produces an lcm test: alpha(m)=n, and divisibility-minimality inside alpha^{-1}(n), are checked by requiring the full lcm to be n and the lcm to drop below n whenever one prime-power exponent is lowered. The same rank criterion produces

  B_n = Div(F_n) \ union_{p|n} Div(F_{n/p})

and shows that B_n is an upper set in the divisor lattice of F_n.

2. Alpha-contraction and Mobius inversion. The paper imports Stroinski's identity

  sum_{d|F_n} f(d) = sum_{d|n} f_alpha(d),
  f_alpha(d) = sum_{alpha(m)=d} f(m),

and applies divisor-lattice Mobius inversion. It consumes tau(F_c) or omega(F_c) over c|n and produces first-appearance counts, in particular

  #Pi_alpha(d) = a(d) = sum_{c|d} mu(d/c) omega(F_c).

The witness-cover classification itself does not depend on this counting identity, but its exact weights do.

3. Prime-power rank lifting. For z_p=alpha(p) and, when p is not 2 or 5, h_p=nu_p(F_{z_p}), the paper imports/proves from standard valuation formulas

  alpha(2)=3, alpha(4)=6, alpha(2^e)=3*2^{e-2} for e>=3,
  alpha(5^e)=5^e,
  alpha(p^e)=p^{max(e-h_p,0)} z_p for p not in {2,5}.

It consumes z_p, h_p, an exponent e, and a target index n. It produces the prime-power levels at which rank actually changes, the fact that lowering a nonprime atomic power changes only its underlying p-coordinate, and a unique possible ladder candidate for any prescribed essential/full support slot. The manuscript's explicit candidates are 4 when p=2 and nu_2(n)=1; 2^{nu_2(n)+2} when p=2 and nu_2(n)>=2; 5^{nu_5(n)} when p=5 and nu_5(n)>=2; and p^{h_p+nu_p(n)} for p not in {2,5}. A labelled ladder slot contains at most one candidate. Exact-rank prime existence is imported from Carmichael and Bilu-Hanrot-Voutier, with the exact-rank exceptions 6 and 12 and the discriminant-related rank-5 prime treated separately.

4. Coordinate separation and lowered-label covers. For a prime power theta=p^e with d(theta)=alpha(theta)|n, the paper records the coordinates of n supplied at full exponent by d(theta), and the subset lost when theta is lowered to p^{e-1}. The lowering test consumes these two coordinate sets and produces an exact criterion for whether the global lcm drops. A pairwise coprime collection passes when its full-coordinate sets cover P(n) and every member has a lost coordinate supplied by no other member. Private coordinates inject the selected prime powers into P(n). Multiplication then transfers such a cover to a divisibility-minimal element of alpha^{-1}(n), while unique factorization recovers the cover. For prime atoms, lowering replaces the label by 1, so this is exactly Wagner's deletion condition; only ladder atoms create the nontrivial lowered-label case.

5. Transfer from ordinary minimal covers to Fibonacci moduli. For each nonempty S subset P(n), the paper forms

  n_S = product_{ell in S} ell^{nu_ell(n)}

and consumes an irredundant cover C of P(n) together with choices p_S satisfying alpha(p_S)=n_S. Except for supports producing ranks 2, 6, or 12, classical exact-rank existence supplies the choices. The transfer produces product_{S in C} p_S in the minimal exact fiber and preserves the support hypergraph. It also produces the exact weighted count

  sum_C product_{S in C} a(n_S).

On squarefree n, reversing the prime factorization shows that this transferred class is exactly the divisibility-minimal squarefree part of alpha^{-1}(n). A second transfer replaces each available pair (S,p) by a vertex in a coordinate hypergraph and identifies selected products with inclusion-minimal transversals; the standard split-incidence construction then identifies the same count with minimal connected dominating sets in an associated split graph. The particular coordinate hypergraph and split graph are constructed by this manuscript even though minimal transversals and split graphs are standard objects.

6. Cover enumeration and private-coordinate entropy. The paper consumes Hearne-Wagner's exact formula for labelled irredundant covers and compares it directly with the labelled split-graph central sum

  b_k = sum_{s=0}^k binom(k,s) 2^{s(k-s)}.

The canonical least-private-element encoding and the central-range lower estimate produce C_k/b_k -> 1 and hence log C_k=(log 2/4)k^2+O(k). Independently, for a target n with k=omega(n), the lower construction consumes a map H:Q -> 2^P\{empty} and produces (2^{floor(k/2)}-1)^{ceil(k/2)} distinct prime-only covers. The upper encoding chooses a private coordinate for each atom; after its private-coordinate set is fixed, it leaves at most two essential-support types, at most R(n) atoms per type, and 2^{k-s} choices of remaining full support. It produces

  #M_n <= sum_{s=1}^k binom(k,s)(2R(n))^s 2^{s(k-s)}.

This is combinatorial separation plus an unresolved arithmetic multiplicity, not a distribution theorem for ranks of apparition.

7. Rank-window deaggregation. The paper groups exact ranks d|n by the set of prime coordinates at which d has the full exponent of n. If n=product ell^{e_ell}, a fixed nonempty support window J contains product_{ell notin J} e_ell possible ranks. Consuming the individual multiplicities a(d), the prime-ladder dichotomy, and the one-candidate ladder slots gives P(n)<=R(n)<=P(n)+1, where P(n) is the largest sum of a(d) in one window. The pointwise comparison

  0 <= log R(n)-log A*(n) <= log 2 + sum_{ell^{e_ell}||n} log e_ell

and a convergent prime-power mean estimate produce a total deaggregation cost O(x log log x) in sum_{n<=x} omega(n) log R(n). Thus the mean obstruction is reduced to the largest single visible a(d), but that single-rank maximum is not bounded at the required scale.

8. Fibotomic size and residue-class separation. The paper consumes the standard fibotomic factorization

  mathfrak F_d = product_{r|d} F_r^{mu(d/r)} = Psi_d(1),

the divisibility of mathfrak F_d by the radical product_{alpha(p)=d} p, the congruence p == +/-1 (mod d) for exact-rank primes p not in {2,5}, and Binet's formula. Ordering k=a(d) exact-rank primes produces

  product_{i=1}^k p_i >= (2d/3)^k (floor(k/2)!)^2
                             (floor(k/2)+1)^{k-2 floor(k/2)},

while Binet gives log mathfrak F_d = phi_E(d) log phi + E_d with |E_d| bounded by an absolute convergent-series constant. The separation of available residue classes from the fibotomic size budget produces the entropy inequality in Theorem 6.4 and, uniformly,

  a(d) <= (log phi/2+o(1)) phi_E(d)/log d.

This is a pointwise capacity bound; the manuscript explicitly does not derive exact-rank dispersion, a small least exact-rank prime, a Wall-Sun-Sun result, or a Pisano-period result from it.

9. A sharp information-separation argument. For weights w_S>=1 on nonempty subsets of a k-set, expansion of product_{S in C}w_S over all irredundant covers consumes only the total mass A=sum_S w_S and the fact that every nonempty S occurs in a minimal cover. It produces

  Z_V(w) >= C_k + A-(2^k-1),

with equality obtained by concentrating all excess weight on the full set (uniquely for k>=3). This shows exactly that the cumulative datum omega(F_n)=sum_{d|n}a(d) does not determine the weighted-cover dispersion. The extremizing weight profile is abstract; the paper does not claim it is realized by Fibonacci numbers.

10. Independent inverse-dynamics transfer. This part does not use the cover machinery. It consumes classical existence of an exact-rank prime at every index >=3 except 6 and 12, the known classification of fixed points of alpha, and the known absence of nontrivial cycles. Iterating the choice alpha(p_r)=p_{r-1}, with p_r|F_{p_{r-1}}<phi^{p_{r-1}}, produces infinite prime backward rays and primes in every nontrivial fixed-point basin and every positive fixed-point-order class. Its arithmetic input is still only classical exact-rank prime existence.

2. Separate the manuscript's objects from the field's objects

Please begin from the following separation, then correct it before using it.

(a) Objects defined, selected, or named by this manuscript for its own purposes

* The notation B_n and the name "birth layer" for the standard exact fiber alpha^{-1}(n), and M_n for its divisibility-minimal elements. The underlying exact fiber is standard; the selected minimal subposet and its notation are the manuscript's object.
* "Atomic" prime powers Atom(d), their lowered ranks d^-(theta), the full and essential coordinate supports T_n(theta), E_n(theta), and the atomic families A_{I,J}(n). These package standard rank lifting in manuscript-specific language.
* An "n-witness cover." Its prime-only skeleton is not new: it is the unordered Wagner minimal multiplicative cover. The manuscript-specific addition is the lowered-label rule for ladder atoms plus its Fibonacci realization.
* The particular support hypergraph H_n(q), its connected subfamily M_n^conn, and the manuscript's coordinate hypergraph H_n^rp. Hypergraphs and minimal transversals are standard; these target-dependent hypergraphs are not pre-existing field objects.
* The "rank-pure" sector R_n^rp, the exceptional support family E_n, and the normalized weighted-cover excess W(n).
* The prime windows P_J(n), maximal atomic rank-window multiplicity R(n), visible maximum A*(n), and the sufficient maximum-window hypotheses H1 and H2.
* The particular weighted cover polynomial whose weights are a(n_S), and its abstract fixed-total-mass extremizer. Weighted set-cover polynomials in general are not claimed to be invented here, but this Fibonacci specialization and its role are manuscript-defined.
* The term "prime inverse ray" for the backward-orbit construction. Backward orbits are standard dynamics; this prime-restricted construction is the manuscript's application.
* The notation mathfrak F_d for Psi_d(1). The underlying fibotomic polynomial and its evaluation are standard, not new objects.

(b) Provisional list of standard objects, invariants, and problem families already studied in other papers in this area

* The Fibonacci rank of apparition alpha(n), also called the order of appearance or entry point z(n). These are names for the same invariant, not three different objects.
* The exact fibers alpha^{-1}(d), exact-rank primes {p:alpha(p)=d}, cumulative bounded-rank prime counts, and primitive parts of F_d. The notation Pi_alpha(d) and a(d) is local, but the underlying exact-rank/first-appearance primes are standard.
* The Pisano period pi(n), equivalently the order of the Fibonacci state matrix modulo n, and the standard relation between period, rank, and order.

* Primitive prime divisors and primitive parts in Fibonacci and general Lucas sequences, Zsygmondy-Carmichael theory, the Bilu-Hanrot-Voutier defective-index theory, and questions about the size or multiplicity of primitive prime divisors.
* Distributional statistics of alpha(p): rank divisibility d|alpha(p), the distribution of alpha(p)/p, growing or fixed exact-rank classes, and the index of appearance iota_F(p)=(p-(5/p))/alpha(p) for p not dividing 10.
* The rank/order-of-appearance map as a dynamical system: its fixed points, periodic and preperiodic points, basins, fixed-point order, forward orbits, and backward orbits. The fixed-point and basin terminology used here comes from the existing dynamics literature.
* Self-Fibonacci divisors n|F_n and their counting function, which are standard objects in the Luca-Tron/Pomerance line rather than objects defined by this manuscript.
* Fibotomic polynomials Psi_d, Fibonacci primitive factors/radicals, and the factorization of Fibonacci polynomials into fibotomic factors.

These are field objects in extremal/enumerative combinatorics, but the manuscript's Fibonacci-labelled instances are not independently standard objects.
