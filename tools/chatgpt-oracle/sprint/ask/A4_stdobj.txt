I. Technical machinery actually available

Please evaluate possible targets from the following machinery map, not from a list of the manuscript's theorem statements. Each item says what the device consumes and what it produces.

1. Affine recurrence action. From a strictly increasing integer sequence satisfying, after an index J,

   U_{n+d}=a_0U_n+a_1U_{n+1}+...+a_{d-1}U_{n+d-1},  a_0 != 0,

   and an LSD-first digit epsilon, the manuscript acts on

   (N_r,U_r,...,U_{r+d-1})^T

   by

   D_epsilon = [[1, epsilon e_1^T],[0,C]],

   where C is the companion matrix. It produces exact concatenation identities M_x=D_{x_l}...D_{x_1} and

   det D_epsilon=(-1)^{d-1}a_0.

   Hence every block action is invertible modulo q when gcd(q,a_0)=1. In the Zeckendorf specialization the state is (N_r,F_{r+1},F_{r+2})^T, each digit matrix has determinant -1, and every block acts invertibly modulo every q.

2. Language-to-orbit extraction. From an infinite k-MCFL inside a unique canonical representation language, closure under a fixed left quotient first moves past the recurrence transient. The weak pumping lemma of Seki--Matsumura--Fujii--Kasami then produces one fixed family

   W(t)=a u_1 v_1^t w_1 s_1^t u_2 ... u_k v_k^t w_k s_k^t u_{k+1},  t>=0,

   with positive total pumped length. Canonical injectivity turns the distinct word lengths into pairwise distinct values N(t); it does not, in the general setting, produce monotonicity of N(t), control every long word, or control the whole language. Conversely, an explicit linear MCFG of fan-out 2k turns any such fixed family back into an MCFL. In the separate Zeckendorf/CFL argument, Ogden's lemma applies to every sufficiently long word and supplies two synchronized pumps; choosing a return modulo the square of the current value produces the iterative coprime-quotient mechanism.

3. Finite-group return. Given the pumped block matrices and an admissible modulus q, a common multiple of their orders in GL_{d+1}(Z/qZ) produces

   N(t+H(q)) == N(t) (mod q) for every t>=0,

   for the same factorization independently of q. The proof permits

   H(q)=|GL_{d+1}(Z/qZ)| <= q^{(d+1)^2}.

   When |a_0|=1, all moduli are admissible. Choosing moduli such as N_i^2 or M_i N_i^{r_i+1}, and then using pairwise distinctness to choose a larger return value, produces divisibility and high-congruence quotients. For nonunits, this device sees only moduli coprime to a_0.

4. Kronecker/Cayley--Hamilton lifting. From the 2k powered affine blocks B_1,...,B_{2k}, the tensor product K=B_1 tensor ... tensor B_{2k} expresses N(t) as a fixed linear functional of K^t. It produces an integer linear recurrence for N(t) of order at most

   R=(d_r+1)^{2k},

   where d_r is the minimal tail-recurrence order. This also turns the exact identity N(t+1)=bN(t) into a finite check of the first R terms.

5. Fixed-support separation and arithmetic rigidity. Starting from bounded prime support outside the primes dividing the minimal tail constant c_0, the finite-group return first selects an arithmetic subsequence on which the outside support is fixed. The Kronecker lift makes that subsequence a pairwise distinct positive linear recurrence. Splitting characteristic roots into root-of-unity equivalence classes makes each residue subsequence nondegenerate. Evertse's Theorem 3 supplies the uniform limit that the largest prime-ideal norm occurring in Z(r)/Z(s) tends to infinity for a nondegenerate recurrence with at least two distinct nonzero characteristic roots; fixed rational-prime support gives the contradictory bound max p^{[K:Q]}. A Hankel-rank/Galois argument then makes the sole root rational, Schur's theorem eliminates a nonconstant polynomial factor, and integrality and positivity produce an arithmetic subsequence exactly c b^t with integers c>=1 and b>=2. The conclusion concerns an extracted subsequence, not the original recurrence globally.

6. Adic and Cantor--Bendixson separation. With S={p:p|a_0}, local returns are interpreted in the topology generated by congruences modulo q coprime to a_0. They produce a countable orbit with no isolated points; Broughan's classifications then identify it with Q and identify its closure in product_{p notin S} Z_p with the Cantor set. For

   X_{K,E}={n>=1: omega_{S^c}(n)<=K and v_p(n)<=E_p for p in S},

   Dirichlet's theorem supplies nearby points by multiplying by a new prime r==1 (mod q), while the isolating modulus product l_i^{e_i+1} times an auxiliary prime separates a point using the full outside-prime budget. This produces the exact intrinsic derivative identity D_S X_{K,E}=X_{K-1,E} and finite scattered height K+1.

7. Weak-Perron growth and greedy separation. From the hypothesis that the minimal tail polynomial is the minimal polynomial of a weak Perron number beta, the manuscript chooses h with rho=beta^h Perron, expands

   U_n=sum_gamma A_gamma gamma^n,

   uses Galois covariance to show all A_gamma are nonzero, and uses a Fourier--Vandermonde separation of the peripheral phases plus strict increase to propagate positivity through every residue class. It produces, for each r mod h,

   U_{hn+r}=C_r rho^n+O(theta^n),  0<theta<rho,  C_r>0,

   and hence U_n^{1/n}->beta. This is the mechanism overlapping Charlier--Kreczman. The standard greedy length interval [U_{m-1},U_m) then consumes a geometric value c b^t represented by a word of length L+Dt and produces, by a two-sided root squeeze, b=beta^D. The minimal tail annihilator ideal transfers this to the equivalent algebraic statements beta^m=B in Z_{>=2}, mu_U(X)|X^m-B, and eventual scalar periodicity U_{n+m}=BU_n.

8. Effective candidate verification. Given a DFA for the canonical language and certified minimal tail data, the finite transition monoid decides whether a proposed synchronized family is accepted for all t, while item 4 decides its geometric identity. Enumeration therefore produces only positive semidecidability. It does not produce a computable bound on fan-out or block lengths, because shortening a DFA loop need not preserve its affine recurrence matrix.

II. Keep the manuscript's objects separate from the field's objects

(a) Objects defined, packaged, or named by this manuscript for its own purposes:

- A "canonical eventually recurrent numeration system" satisfying (U1), (U2), and (U4). This is a manuscript-specific umbrella, deliberately narrower than some uses of Pisot numeration and different from an abstract numeration system or a general beta-shift.
- A recurrence witness, its witness-relative "unit system," and its exceptional prime set {p:p|a_0}. The dependence on a possibly nonminimal witness is part of the manuscript's setup, not a standard invariant of a numeration system.
- The minimal tail recurrence polynomial mu_U and its prime set S_r. This is mostly a repackaging of the standard minimal shift-annihilating/characteristic polynomial of the eventual linear recurrence; only its role as the invariant replacement for an inflated witness is manuscript-specific.
- A fixed synchronized local-congruence orbit/ray W(t), and a "geometric synchronized scheme" for which val_U(W(t))=c b^t. The synchronized power pattern comes from the standard weak MCFL pumping lemma, but the named scheme and its numeration-value condition are the manuscript's objects.
- The deleted-prime congruence topology tau_S as used here, the strata X_{K,E}, the intrinsic derivative D_S on those strata, and their "local prime-factor rank." The underlying adic topology and completion are Broughan's standard objects; X_{K,E} and the deleted-prime intrinsic calculation are the manuscript's packaging.
- The paper's representation languages Rep_U(B), including its prime, bounded-omega, bounded-Omega, finite-support, and bounded-outside-support slices. Taking representations of an arithmetic set is standard, but these named slices are the paper's test objects.
