Objects and notation

Let F_0=0, F_1=F_2=1, and F_{j+2}=F_{j+1}+F_j, and let
phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2. Let R(n) be the number of partitions of n into distinct
Fibonacci numbers F_2,F_3,..., so the two occurrences of the unit Fibonacci number are
identified; set R(n)=0 for n<0. Put

  I_m=[F_{m+1}-1,F_{m+2}-1) cap Z,       m>=1.

For the finite-window model, let Omega_m={0,1}^m and let X_m be the binary words
x=x_1...x_m with no adjacent 1s. Define

  N_m(omega)=sum_{j=1}^m omega_j F_{j+1},
  V_m(x)=sum_{j=1}^m x_j F_{j+1}.

The map V_m is a bijection from X_m to {0,...,F_{m+2}-1}. The fold is

  Fold_m(omega)=V_m^{-1}(N_m(omega) mod F_{m+2}),

and d_m(x)=|Fold_m^{-1}(x)|. Write d_m^(#)(r)=d_m(V_m^{-1}(r)) and
S_t(m)=sum_{x in X_m}d_m(x)^t for real t.

For a reduced p/q in (0,1), write its unique negative continued fraction as

  p/q=1/(a_1-1/(a_2-...-1/a_r)),        a_i>=2,

and put d(p/q)=sum_i(a_i-1) and c(p/q)=2d(p/q)+1. Let sigma_0>2 be the
unique solution

  zeta(sigma_0-1)/zeta(sigma_0)=2,

so sigma_0=2.478750785733960..., and define

  mu_C=sum_{q>=2} sum_{1<=p<q,(p,q)=1} c(p/q)q^{-sigma_0},

  v_c={sum_{q>=2} sum_{1<=p<q,(p,q)=1}q^{-sigma_0}log q}/mu_C,
  t_c=-sigma_0,       alpha_+=(1/2)log phi.

The pressure P is zero for t<=-sigma_0. For t>-sigma_0, P(t)>0 is the
unique solution

  sum_{q>=2} sum_{1<=p<q,(p,q)=1}
      q^t exp(-P(t)c(p/q))=1.

Main results in the present manuscript

1. Results for the field's standard Fibonacci partition function R. For every real t,

  Z_m^R(t):=sum_{n in I_m}R(n)^t,
  lim_{m->infinity} m^{-1}log Z_m^R(t)=P(t).

The function P is zero on (-infinity,-sigma_0], positive, real analytic, and strictly
convex on (-sigma_0,infinity), with P'_-(t_c)=0 and P'_+(t_c)=v_c>0. For every
s>sigma_0,

  Z_m^R(-s) -> 2/{2-zeta(s-1)/zeta(s)}-1,

whereas at criticality

  Z_m^R(-sigma_0) ~ 2m/mu_C.

For n in I_m with R(n)>2, let g(n) be the generator of its Weinstein orbit and let
ell_m^R(n)=L(g(n)); put ell_m^R(n)=0 for R(n)<=2. If Nhat_m has the critical Gibbs law
proportional to R(n)^{-sigma_0} on I_m, then

  (m^{-1}log R(Nhat_m),m^{-1}ell_m^R(Nhat_m))
       -> (v_c U,U) in distribution,

where U is uniform on [0,1]. If N_m is uniform on I_m, then
Y_m^R=m^{-1}log R(N_m) satisfies a full LDP with speed m and good rate

  I(alpha)=sup_{t in R}{t alpha-P(t)+log phi}.

Its effective domain is [0,alpha_+], and

  I(alpha)=log phi-sigma_0 alpha             for 0<=alpha<=v_c.

Thus the proof includes local lower bounds for the nonexposed slopes created by the
pressure corner, not only the Gartner-Ellis upper bound and exposed-slope lower bounds.

The paper also keeps the generating cost. With B_m^R=m^{-1}ell_m^R(N_m), the vectors
(B_m^R,Y_m^R) satisfy a full LDP on R^2 with speed m. Their limiting log-mgf is

  Lambda(a,t)=max{0,a+P(t)}-log phi,

and their good rate function is

  J(beta,alpha)=(1-beta)log phi+beta I(alpha/beta)

when 0<beta<=1 and 0<=alpha<=beta alpha_+; J(0,0)=log phi; and J=+infinity
otherwise. In particular,

  J(beta,alpha)=log phi-sigma_0 alpha

on 0<=beta<=1, 0<=alpha<=beta v_c, so the coexistence interval lifts to an
explicit affine face of the two-dimensional rate surface.

2. Exact finite-window correspondences. Define

  dtilde_m(n)=[z^n] product_{j=1}^m(1+z^{F_j}),
  Rdagger(n)=R(n)+R(n-1),

and

  pi_m(n)=F_{m+1}n+F_{m+1}-1 mod F_{m+2}.

For every m>=1, pi_m permutes Z/F_{m+2}Z and, for 0<=n<F_{m+2},

  d_m^(#)(pi_m(n))
    =dtilde_m(n)
    =Rdagger(n)-Rdagger(n-F_{m+1}).

Pointwise, for every m>=0 and 0<=r<F_{m+2},

  d_m^(#)(r)=R(F_{m+2}+r)   if 0<=r<=F_{m+1}-2,
  d_m^(#)(r)=R(r)           if F_{m+1}-1<=r<F_{m+2},

with an empty interval allowed in the initial cases. Hence the complete fiber list is
exactly the list of R on the two consecutive standard layers I_m and I_{m+1}.

3. Exact largest fibers and all maximizers. Let D_m=max_x d_m(x). Then

  D_0=D_1=1,
  D_{2k}=F_{k+2}                  for k>=0,
  D_{2k+1}=2F_{k+1}              for k>=1,

and consequently D_m=D_{m-2}+D_{m-4} for every m>=6, without an auxiliary
recurrence hypothesis. The manuscript also gives every maximizing residue. To state the
generic part, put G_j=F_{j+1}. For k>=5 define

  I_{2k+1}=G_{k+1}G_{k-3}+1      if k is even,
  I_{2k+1}=G_kG_{k-2}+1          if k is odd.

For k>=7 define

  I_{2k}=G_{k+2}G_{k-5}+G_3+1    if k is even,
  I_{2k}=G_{k+1}G_{k-4}+G_3+1    if k is odd,

  J_{2k}=G_{k+1}G_{k-3}+1        if k is even,
  J_{2k}=G_kG_{k-2}+1            if k is odd.

For m=2k>=10, the maximizing residues are

  {I_{2k+1}-1, G_{2k}-1-I_{2k+1}}.

For m=2k-1>=13, they are

  {I_{2k}-1,J_{2k}-1,G_{2k-1}-1-I_{2k},G_{2k-1}-1-J_{2k}}.

The remaining sets are

  M_0={0}; M_1={0,1}; M_2={0}; M_3={0,1,3}; M_4={0,3};
  M_5={3}; M_6={3,8}; M_7={3,8,11,16}; M_8={8,16,24};

the generic even formula covers m=10 and m=12. The maximum therefore has exactly two
maximizers in generic even windows and four in generic odd windows.

4. Local fiber spectrum and frozen unnormalized limits. Let Psi(k) be Weinstein's number
of generating orbits at multiplicity k and let

  C_m(k)=|{x in X_m:d_m(x)=k}|.

For every m>=1, C_m(1)=2, at residues F_{m+1}-1 and F_{m+2}-1. For every k>=2,

  C_m(k)<=4Psi(k),
  C_m(k)=4Psi(k) when m>=2k.

Thus the discrete measures sum_k C_m(k)delta_k converge vaguely to

  2delta_1+4 sum_{k>=2}Psi(k)delta_k.

For s>sigma_0,

  S_{-s}(m) -> Zcal(s):=4/{2-zeta(s-1)/zeta(s)}-2.

More exactly, S_t(m) has this finite limit for t< -sigma_0 and diverges to
infinity for t>=-sigma_0. As s decreases to sigma_0,

  Zcal(s)=4/{kappa(s-sigma_0)}+O(1),

where

  kappa=-[d/ds zeta(s-1)/zeta(s)]_{s=sigma_0}.

For fixed s>sigma_0, the multiplicity Gibbs laws
C_m(k)k^{-s}/S_{-s}(m) converge in total variation to

  Q_s(1)=2/Zcal(s),
  Q_s(k)=4Psi(k)k^{-s}/Zcal(s), k>=2.

If K has law Q_s, then, as s decreases to sigma_0,

  E_s log K=1/(s-sigma_0)+O(1),
  Var_s(log K)=1/(s-sigma_0)^2+O(1).

At the endpoint itself,

  S_{-sigma_0}(m) ~ 4m/mu_C.

If Xhat_m has the finite-window critical Gibbs law proportional to
d_m(x)^{-sigma_0}, and ell_m(x) is the cost of the generating orbit for d_m(x)>2
(zero otherwise), then

  (m^{-1}log d_m(Xhat_m),m^{-1}ell_m(Xhat_m))
       -> (v_c U,U) in distribution.

5. Orbit growth, pressure recovery, and finite-window large deviations. The generator
counts obey the Tauberian laws

  sum_{k<=x}Psi(k) ~ x^{sigma_0}/(sigma_0 kappa),
  sum_{k<=x}Psi(k)k^{-sigma_0} ~ (1/kappa)log x.

The paper derives the exact weighted renewal identities

  Z_j^R(-s)=2 sum_{ell=0}^{j-1}u_ell(s)+u_j(s)-1,
  S_{-s}(m)=4 sum_{j=0}^{m-1}u_j(s)+3u_m(s)+u_{m+1}(s)-2,

where u_0(s)=1, u_j(s)=sum_{ell=1}^j b_ell(s)u_{j-ell}(s), and

  b_ell(s)=sum_{p/q reduced in (0,1), c(p/q)=ell}q^{-s}.

These formulas give the critical constants and the marked coexistence laws. The pressure
statement above is also recovered in these renewal coordinates. If X_m is uniform on X_m
and Y_m=m^{-1}log d_m(X_m), then Y_m satisfies the same full LDP and rate I as the
one-layer R model, with effective domain [0,alpha_+] and the exact local exponential rate

  lim_{epsilon->0} lim_{m->infinity}m^{-1}log
      Pr{|Y_m-alpha|<epsilon}
    =-[log phi-sigma_0 alpha]

for every 0<alpha<v_c. The new step in that lower bound is a parity-complete orbit-filling

6. Exact finite-prime-support interface, with no claimed directional asymptotic. Fix
distinct primes P={p_1,...,p_d}. For a nonzero vector a in N^d put
q_a=product_i p_i^{a_i} and

  A_a(u)=sum_{1<=h<q_a,(h,q_a)=1}u^{c(h/q_a)},
  A_P(z,u)=sum_{a!=0}A_a(u)z^a.

If U_j(q_n) counts Weinstein generators with R(g)=q_n and L(g)=j, with
U_0(1)=1, then exactly

  sum_{n in N^d}sum_{j>=0}U_j(q_n)z^n u^j=1/{1-A_P(z,u)},

and, for q_n>2,

  C_m(q_n)=sum_{j>=0}K_m(j)U_j(q_n),

where K_m(j)=4 for j<=m-1, 3 for j=m, 1 for j=m+1, and 0 for j>=m+2.
At u=1 the series is the rational function

  1/{2-product_{i=1}^d(1-z_i)/(1-p_i z_i)}.

The paper deliberately does not claim a directional coefficient asymptotic or a quenched

Imported results versus claims that are ours

The following are imported and are not novelty claims.

* Sidorov-Vershik's level-m Fibonacci representation frequency is exactly the same finite
  subset-sum coefficient used here after their convention F_j^(SV)=F_{j+1}; its normalized
  mass 2^{-m}f_m(k) approximates the Erdos measure.
* Hu studied local dimensions of the golden-ratio Bernoulli convolution. Lau-Ngai gave its
  exact L^q-spectrum for all q>0. Feng-Olivier proved that the Erdos measure is weak Gibbs,

  transition. Feng gave the all-real spectrum and the same positive-root/frozen-branch
  structure. Under the manuscript's convention the exact dictionary is

      P(t)=t log 2-tau_mu(t)log phi.

  Therefore existence of all-real pressure, the positive implicit branch, the frozen
  negative phase, nondifferentiability at the critical tilt, and the generic Legendre
  multifractal formalism are prior results.
* Kocabova-Masakova-Pelantova's classification of the extrema of R and of all maximizing

* Weinstein's free generator monoid, multiplier multiplicativity, orbit decomposition,
  eventual layer stabilization, largest-index formulas, and generator Dirichlet series are
  imported. The Tauberian theorem used on that series is classical.
* The Stern-Brocot/Knauf denominator pressure, the arithmetic renewal theorem,
  Wiener-Ikehara, Gartner-Ellis exposed-slope theory, and smooth-point multivariate
  coefficient methods are imported tools. Sanna's positive-integer power-sum asymptotics

  related results of Chow-Slattery, Chow-Jones, Shallit, Kempton, and others are treated as

What we claim as the surviving contribution is narrower: the pointwise affine fold/subset-

The audit also found three live named nearby problems, none solved by the present paper:

* Weinstein (2016), Conjecture 9.1: a convex-hull description of the points (c,F(c)) in the
