Thank you for identifying the two principal problems in the previous version: the peripheral-spectrum residue analysis was compressed at precisely its highest-risk point, requiring you to reconstruct omitted details in order to check it, and the priority discussion omitted Charlier and Kreczman, "Numeration systems without a dominant root and regularity," arXiv:2512.13180v1, despite its substantial overlap with the asymptotic mechanism we had presented as our new technical step.

We have revised both points. The peripheral-spectrum residue analysis is now written out in full, with the coefficients and tail thresholds handled explicitly. The comparison with Charlier--Kreczman now appears in the main-results introduction, in a dedicated prior-work discussion, and at the statement of Theorem 2.25 itself.

After reading their full preprint, however, we concluded that the overlap is larger than your report indicated. Their Remark 12 gives the converse quotient-limit mechanism for an arbitrary linear recurrence when the dominating eigenvalues share a common p-th power and the term moduli eventually increase. Under the hypotheses of our Theorem 2.25, all three relevant conditions hold: separability gives equal peripheral multiplicities, weak-Perron periodicity gives the common power, and strict increase gives eventual increase. Thus our asymptotic mechanism is derivable from theirs, rather than merely adjacent to it. The manuscript now states this explicitly.

What we believe remains ours is different in scope. Charlier--Kreczman do not treat MCFL sublanguages, bounded prime support, synchronized geometric schemes, Evertse quotient rigidity, or the five-way radical and scalar-periodicity classification. Conversely, we do not characterize regularity of the whole greedy language. On our present reading, the surviving contribution is the passage from bounded outside-prime support on an infinite finite-fan-out MCFL to an exact synchronized scheme.

The ceiling of the result is unchanged and remains explicit in the manuscript: the mechanism extracts one synchronized orbit; it does not control the whole language, every sufficiently long word, or simultaneous representations in two systems.
