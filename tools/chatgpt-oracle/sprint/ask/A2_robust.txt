The first result is the proposition "Robustness of the positive tail-jet convention." It assumes the hypotheses and notation of Theorem 4.27. An admissible tail-jet convention is a pair \((\chi,H)\) for which there are fixed constants \(0<a<b<\infty\), independent of \(s\), such that each \(\chi_s:\mathbb R^d\to[0,1]\) is measurable and
\[
0\leq \chi_s\leq 1,\qquad
\chi_s(z)=0\ \text{for }|z|\leq a,\qquad
\chi_s(z)=1\ \text{for }|z|\geq b,
\]
and for which \(H_s\) is common to the two laws and satisfies
\[
\int H_s\,d\Omega_{\alpha,d}=0,
\qquad
\sup_s\left(\|H_s\|_\infty+
\|H_s\|_{L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d})}\right)<\infty.
\]
For a law \(\lambda\), define
\[
\tau_{s,\chi}^{\lambda}
:=\int \chi_s(x/s)\,\lambda(dx),
\qquad
V_{s,\chi}^{\lambda}(y)
:=\int \chi_s(x/s)F_y^{(\alpha,d)}(x/s)\,\lambda(dx),
\]
and
\[
A_s^{\lambda;\chi,H}
:=1+C_{r-1,s}+V_{s,\chi}^{\lambda}
-\tau_{s,\chi}^{\lambda}+s^{-r}H_s.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(s\), these are positive probability densities relative to \(\Omega_{\alpha,d}\), and the proxy entropy
\[
\mathcal E_{r,s}^{\chi,H}(\nu,\eta)
:=\int A_s^{\eta;\chi,H}
\Phi\!\left(
\frac{A_s^{\nu;\chi,H}-A_s^{\eta;\chi,H}}
{A_s^{\eta;\chi,H}}
\right)d\Omega_{\alpha,d}
\]
is finite. The proposition proves
\[
D_{\rm KL}(p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\nu\|p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\eta)
=\mathcal E_{r,s}^{\chi,H}(\nu,\eta)
+\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)s^{-2r}+o(s^{-2r}).
\]
Consequently, for any two admissible conventions \((\chi,H)\) and \((\widetilde\chi,\widetilde H)\),
\[
\left|\mathcal E_{r,s}^{\chi,H}(\nu,\eta)
-\mathcal E_{r,s}^{\widetilde\chi,\widetilde H}(\nu,\eta)\right|
=o(s^{-2r}).
\]
Thus the exact vanishing criterion and the Hessian constant \(\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)\) are unchanged by the convention within this class.

The class is claimed explicitly to contain the original hard cutoff; every hard cutoff \(\mathbf 1_{\{|x|>c_s s\}}\) with
\[
0<\inf_s c_s\leq\sup_s c_s<\infty;
\]
every smooth radial cutoff \(\theta(|x|/(c_s s))\), where \(\theta=0\) on \([0,1]\) and \(\theta=1\) on \([2,\infty)\); arbitrary measurable, nonradial, \(s\)-dependent transitions in a fixed scaled annulus; and an infinite-dimensional family of common bounded zero-mass gauges \(H_s\).

The second result is the proposition "Partial rigidity within the retained-tail ansatz." Fix an admissible retention profile \(\chi\), and consider positive normalized proxies
\[
\widehat A_s^\lambda
=1+J_s+V_{s,\chi}^\lambda-\tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda,
\qquad \lambda\in\{\nu,\eta\},
\]
where \(J_s\) is common to the two laws and has zero \(\Omega_{\alpha,d}\)-mean. Assume that the proxies are order-\(r\) jet-faithful in the precise sense that there are zero-mean modes \(G_s^\lambda\), uniformly bounded in \(L^\infty\), and remainders \(S_s^\lambda\) such that
\[
q_s^\lambda
=\widehat A_s^\lambda+s^{-r}G_s^\lambda+S_s^\lambda,
\qquad
\|S_s^\lambda\|_\infty+
\|S_s^\lambda\|_{L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d})}=o(s^{-r}).
\]
Then
\[
H_s:=s^r(J_s-C_{r-1,s})
\]
is a common zero-mean family bounded in both \(L^\infty\) and \(L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d})\), and
\[
\|G_s^\lambda-(B_r^\lambda-H_s)\|_\infty
+\|G_s^\lambda-(B_r^\lambda-H_s)\|_{L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d})}
=o(1).
\]
In particular,
\[
\|G_s^\nu-G_s^\eta-B_{\Delta_r}\|_\infty
+\|G_s^\nu-G_s^\eta-B_{\Delta_r}\|_{L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d})}
=o(1).
\]
Thus, within this ansatz, the lower common jet is forced modulo a bounded common order-\(r\) gauge, while the law-difference order-\(r\) mode and hence its Hessian are forced asymptotically. Moreover, if the normalized proxy is initially written as
\[
1+J_s+V_{s,\chi}^\lambda+c_s^\lambda
\]
with a scalar counterterm, normalization forces
\(c_s^\lambda=-\tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda\) exactly.

The limitations are deliberate and should be part of your assessment. The rigidity proposition does not select a cutoff or retention profile, does not eliminate the common order-\(r\) gauge, does not cover proxies outside the retained raw-tail/common-core ansatz, does not establish global uniqueness or canonicity, and does not prove entropy minimality. The manuscript retains its blanket disclaimer that the proxy is neither claimed to be unique nor canonical among all positive proxy constructions, and qualifies that disclaimer only by the robustness and partial-rigidity results above.

Both results are proved analytically. No numerical battery supports either result; the existing scripts are sanity checks only and are not substitutes for proof.
