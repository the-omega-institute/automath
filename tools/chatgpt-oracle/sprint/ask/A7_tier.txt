Definitions and main results proved in the manuscript

Let F_0=0, F_1=1, and let alpha(q) be the least r>=1 for which q divides F_r, with alpha(1)=1. For n>=2 let B_n=alpha^{-1}(n) and let M_n be the divisibility-minimal elements of B_n; B_2=M_2 is empty. Write P(n) for the prime divisors of n, omega(n)=#P(n), Pi_alpha(d)={p prime: alpha(p)=d}, and a(d)=#Pi_alpha(d).

1. Exact classification of the minimal exact fiber. For a prime power theta=p^e set d(theta)=alpha(p^e) and d^-(theta)=alpha(p^{e-1}); call theta atomic when d^-(theta)<d(theta). If d(theta) divides n, define its full support

   T_n(theta)={ell in P(n): nu_ell(d(theta))=nu_ell(n)}

and its essential support

   E_n(theta)={ell in T_n(theta): nu_ell(d^-(theta))<nu_ell(n)}.

An n-witness cover is a finite pairwise-coprime set Theta of atomic prime powers such that every d(theta) divides n, the sets T_n(theta) cover P(n), and every theta has an essential coordinate absent from all other full supports:

   E_n(theta) is not contained in the union of T_n(eta) over eta!=theta.

For every n>=2, multiplication Theta -> product(theta in Theta) is a bijection from n-witness covers to M_n, with inverse given by the prime-power factors of m. Hence every m in M_n has a unique atomic factorization and omega(m)<=omega(n). Equivalently, if m=product p_i^{e_i} divides F_n, then m lies in M_n exactly when lcm_i alpha(p_i^{e_i})=n and replacing any alpha(p_i^{e_i}) by alpha(p_i^{e_i-1}) makes the lcm strictly smaller than n.

2. Complete Fibonacci prime-power realization used in that classification. If z_p=alpha(p) and, for p not in {2,5}, h_p=nu_p(F_{z_p}), then

   alpha(2)=3, alpha(4)=6, alpha(2^e)=3*2^{e-2} for e>=3;
   alpha(5^e)=5^e for e>=1;
   alpha(p^e)=p^{max(e-h_p,0)}z_p for p not in {2,5}.

Thus the atoms are 2, 4, 2^e for e>=4 (8 is not atomic), every 5^e, every prime p not in {2,5}, and p^e for p not in {2,5} when e>h_p. A prime atom has E_n=T_n; a nonprime ladder atom has E_n either empty or the singleton containing its underlying prime. For each nonempty support pair I subseteq J subseteq P(n), the atomic family A_{I,J}(n) is either the prime window consisting of the disjoint union of exact-rank prime classes with full support J (only when I=J), or a ladder family with I={p}; each labelled ladder slot has at most one explicit candidate. These statements make every atomic family finite and make the upper bounds independently checkable.

3. Canonical rank-pure and squarefree sectors. For n>=3, V=P(n), nonempty S subseteq V, put

   n_S=product(ell in S) ell^{nu_ell(n)},  a_n(S)=a(n_S),

and exclude the at most two supports for which n_S is 2, 6, or 12. For every irredundant cover C of V by distinct nonempty, nonexceptional subsets, choose p_S in Pi_alpha(n_S). The products product_{S in C}p_S form a sector R_n^rp contained in M_n, uniquely represented, with exact count

   #R_n^rp = sum_C product_{S in C} a(n_S),

where

   a(n_S)=sum_{d|n_S} mu(n_S/d) omega(F_d).

This sector is canonically in bijection with the inclusion-minimal transversals of the coordinate hypergraph whose vertices are pairs (S,p), p in Pi_alpha(n_S), and whose coordinate edge for ell consists of the pairs with ell in S. If n>=3 is squarefree, then

   R_n^rp = {m in M_n: m is squarefree}
          = the divisibility-minimal squarefree elements of alpha^{-1}(n).

For odd n no support is exceptional, so every irredundant cover is realized. If C_k is the number of irredundant covers of a labelled k-set, then #M_n>=C_k and

   C_k ~ vartheta_{k mod 2} binom(k,floor(k/2)) 2^{k^2/4},
   log C_k=(log 2)k^2/4+O(k).

With

   W(n)=C_k^{-1} sum_C product_{S in C}a(n_S),  k=omega(n),

one has, on odd n,

   log #R_n^rp=(log 2)k^2/4+log W(n)+O(k),  W(n)>=1.

Consequently the desired almost-all formula

   log #M_n ~ (log 2)/4 (log log n)^2

would necessarily require log W(n)=o((log log n)^2) outside o(x) odd integers n<=x. This condition is not known to be sufficient, because ladder and other non-rank-pure covers must also be bounded; already 169 lies in M_91 but outside R_91^rp.

4. Unconditional counting bounds, with the unresolved arithmetic term displayed rather than hidden. Let R(n) be the maximum of 1 and the cardinalities of the atomic families A_{I,J}(n). For n>=3, M_n is nonempty. If k=omega(n)>=3 and r=floor(k/2), then

   (2^r-1)^{k-r} <= #M_n
   <= sum_{s=1}^k binom(k,s)(2R(n))^s 2^{s(k-s)}.

Therefore, uniformly when omega(n)->infinity,

   (log 2)omega(n)^2/4-O(1)
   <= log #M_n
   <= (log 2)omega(n)^2/4+omega(n)log R(n)+O(omega(n)).

The paper does not claim an unconditional asymptotic or an exact quadratic coefficient for the full M_n. It also proves the coarser bounds #M_n<=n^{omega(n)}, limsup log(#M_n)log log n/(log n)^2<=1, and

   ((log 2)/4+o(1))x(log log x)^2
   <= sum_{3<=n<=x}log #M_n
   <= (1+o(1))x log x log log x.

The hypotheses log R(n)=o(omega(n)) almost everywhere and

   sum_{n<=x}omega(n)log R(n)=o(x(log log x)^2)

would respectively imply the desired normal order and matching mean order, but they are stated only as sufficient hypotheses H1 and H2. No positive evidence for them is claimed. Indeed, the published Bugeaud-Luca-Mignotte-Siksek conjecture omega(F_n) >> log n for composite n would make both H1 and H2 false on a positive-density set.

5. Exact-rank arithmetic estimates. If A*(n) is the maximum of 1 and a(d) over divisors d|n having at least one full prime-power coordinate relative to n, then

   sum_{3<=n<=x}omega(n)log R(n)
   =sum_{3<=n<=x}omega(n)log A*(n)+O(x log log x).

Thus aggregation of several exact ranks into one support window costs only O(x log log x); the unresolved mean term is genuinely the visible single-rank maximum. If phi_E is Euler's totient and varphi=(1+sqrt(5))/2, the fibotomic integer

   Ffrak_d=product_{r|d}F_r^{mu(d/r)}=Psi_d(1)

is a positive integer divisible by product_{p in Pi_alpha(d)}p. For k>=0, m=floor(k/2), and

   H_d(k)=k log(2d/3)+2 log(m!)+(k-2m)log(m+1),

there is an absolute C_F such that H_d(a(d))<=phi_E(d)log varphi+C_F for every d>=3, with the corresponding finitely weighted inequality. Uniformly,

   a(d) <= ((log varphi)/2+o(1)) phi_E(d)/log d,
   A*(n) <= ((log varphi)/2+o(1)) phi_E(n)/log n.

The paper expressly says these pointwise estimates are far too large to control W(n) at the required scale and do not supply moving exact-fiber dispersion.

6. A sharp but abstract limitation of cumulative data. For a labelled k-set V and weights w_S>=1 on all nonempty S, let

   Z_V(w)=sum_{C in MinCov(V)} product_{S in C}w_S,
   A=sum_{empty!=S subseteq V}w_S.

Then Z_V(w)>=C_k+A-(2^k-1), sharply for every A>=2^k-1; for k>=3 equality uniquely concentrates all excess weight on the full support V. For odd squarefree n this gives

   #{squarefree m in M_n}>=C_k+omega(F_n)-(2^k-1).

The equality profile is an abstract information-theoretic extremizer, not a profile proved realizable by Fibonacci exact-rank primes. Thus omega(F_n)=sum_{d|n}a(d) alone cannot provide the dispersion needed for W(n).

7. Prime inverse dynamics. For every integer y>=7, y!=12, there are pairwise distinct primes p_1,p_2,... with p_0=y and alpha(p_r)=p_{r-1}; they may be chosen with p_r<E_r(y), where E_0(t)=t and E_{r+1}(t)=varphi^{E_r(t)}. Consequently every fixed-point basin Omega_x with fixed x>5 contains infinitely many primes, quantitatively at least log* X-O_x(1) below X, and for every k>=1 there are infinitely many primes of fixed-point order k, with the explicit lower bound stated in the manuscript. These conclusions answer two questions of FitzGibbons-Javaheri-Miller-Verga in the stronger prime form.

What is imported, and what is actually ours

The following are imported and must not be credited to us: Fibonacci rank divisibility and strong divisibility (Wall and Vinson); alpha(lcm(u,v))=lcm(alpha(u),alpha(v)) in the Fibonacci case (Luca-Tron, with Renault for the more general sequence); classical prime-power rank lifting and valuation formulas (Wall/Lengyel and related sources); existence of an exact-rank prime for every d>=3 except d=6,12 (Carmichael and Bilu-Hanrot-Voutier, with the small ranks handled separately); Stroinski's alpha-contraction and the resulting Mobius first-appearance formulas; Hearne-Wagner's minimal finite-set covers and their exact enumeration; Wagner's 1978 minimal multiplicative covers of an integer; labelled split-graph asymptotics of Bender-Richmond-Wormald/Troyka; fibotomic definitions and factorization history from Webb-Parberry, Levy, and later Byer et al.; Marques's classification of the fixed points; and Luca-Tron's theorem that every alpha-orbit reaches a fixed point.

Our claim is narrower: the Fibonacci exact-fiber realization of Wagner's cover skeleton; replacing deletion of a prime label by lowering a prime-power rank label; the unique correspondence with all divisibility-minimal exact preimages; the canonical squarefree exact-fiber slice and its exact a(n_S)-weighted count; the explicit separation of the support entropy from the unresolved exact-rank multiplicities; the deaggregation and fibotomic upper estimates in the form stated above; and the sharp abstract warning that cumulative primitive-prime mass cannot replace exact-rank dispersion. The inverse-ray corollaries appear to be new statements, but their entire prime-existence mechanism is imported and they do not use the weighted-cover machinery.

Priority position, stated against our interest

The closest structural predecessor is not merely generic set-cover theory but Carl G. Wagner, "Minimal multiplicative covers of an integer," Discrete Mathematics 24 (1978), 87-94. Wagner directly studies divisor tuples with lcm n whose lcm falls when any component is deleted. In the prime-atom case our witness condition is exactly Wagner's condition after forgetting order. The genuinely additional case is the nontrivial lowered label of a Fibonacci prime-power ladder. Hearne-Wagner already enumerated minimal set covers; the minimal-transversal formulation is standard hypergraph duality, and its associated split-graph formulation is also standard. Therefore neither the cover skeleton nor the words "minimal transversal" are an independent combinatorial breakthrough here.

The nearest arithmetic literature identified in the audits includes Kiss on primitive divisors and averages involving ranks of primes, Stroinski on alpha-contractions and cumulative counts, Sanna and Cera Da Conceicao on fixed rank-divisibility densities (and Sanna, under GRH, on fixed indices of appearance), and work on primitive divisor size. None found in the bounded searches controls the simultaneous growing exact fibers a(n_S) over the divisor lattice of a typical n. Database non-detection is not proof of priority.

The Bugeaud-Luca-Mignotte-Siksek conjecture omega(F_n)>>log n is not solved here and, rather than supporting our sufficient maximum-window hypotheses, predicts their failure. The paper does not solve the Cubre-Rouse problem of infinitely many primes p with alpha(p)=p+1, Wall's/Wall-Sun-Sun problem, Granville's odd-valuation primitive-divisor conjecture in the Fibonacci case, or the general output-polynomial hypergraph transversal problem. Its canonical hypergraphs are a special coordinate-star class. The current almost-all weighted-dispersion interface is a problem isolated by this paper, not a previously named open problem that can be advertised as settled.

The two FitzGibbons-Javaheri-Miller-Verga questions were checked against the final 2025 paper, and no prior resolution was found in searches through 15 August 2026; however, that is a bounded literature audit, not proof that no unindexed or unpublished resolution exists. More importantly for tier, the proof is a direct iteration of classical exact-rank prime existence. A previous assessment correctly said that it is a new and recordable corollary/application, not a new primitive-divisor theorem, prime construction, distribution result, or sieve mechanism, and it cannot by itself carry the paper at a higher tier.
