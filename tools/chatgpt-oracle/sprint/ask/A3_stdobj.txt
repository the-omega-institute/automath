1. The machinery actually used

Please judge possible targets from the following apparatus, not from a list of the manuscript's conclusions.

(i) Rank-to-congruence transfer. For a strictly increasing Pisot linear numeration system U=(u_j) with u_0=1 and canonical digit set D_U={0,...,d_U}, the canonical greedy U-normal words X_{U,m} are exactly those x for which sum_{j=0}^k x_j u_j<u_{k+1} for 0<=k<m. Positional valuation is a bijection X_{U,m}->{0,...,u_m-1}. The manuscript extends the same integer valuation to the full cube D_U^m, reduces it modulo u_m, and returns the unique normal word with that residue. Consequently, equality of two output windows is equivalent, with no loss, to the rank-difference congruence

    sum_{j=0}^{m-1} u_j e_{t+j} = k_t u_m,

where every e_j lies in Delta_U=[-d_U,d_U] cap Z. Because the raw domain is the full digit shift, every bounded difference digit is realized by taking positive and negative parts. This consumes a consecutive positional-rank bijection and a full raw alphabet; it produces an exact collision description by banded Toeplitz congruences, not an estimate and not an equality of numerical beta-values.

(ii) Exact difference quotient of the classical pair graph. The preceding congruences close on the graph Gamma_{U,m} with vertices Delta_U^{m-1}; shifting a vertex and appending one digit gives an edge exactly when the corresponding rank difference is 0 modulo u_m. Starting states have nonzero first coordinate. Finite collision words are exactly directed paths from those states, the optimal memory-zero inverse anticipation is exactly the longest such path length, and a reachable cycle is exactly the pumpable obstruction to injectivity. In the simple-Parry specialization, Q_m>d makes the zero vertex its own unique predecessor, which upgrades injectivity to existence of a future-only inverse. This part consumes the classical pair graph plus the special coordinatewise-difference closure of this rank map; it produces an exact arithmetic quotient and a longest-path problem. The pair-graph principle and reachable-cycle test themselves are classical.

(iii) Explicit carry interval and Pisot-conjugate contraction. Write the Binet expansion

    u_n=sum_i c_i beta_i^n,

with beta=beta_0>1, |beta_i|<1 for i>=1, c_0>0, and effective b_U,A_U>0 satisfying b_U beta^n<=u_n<=A_U beta^n. Every collision-row carry lies in the explicit interval [-K_U^*,K_U^*], where

    K_U^*=ceil(d_U A_U/(b_U(beta-1))).

For nonintegral beta the manuscript then takes the finite algebraic separation

    delta_U^*=min{|a+k-beta l|:
                   a in Delta_U, |k|,|l|<=K_U^*,
                   a+k-beta l != 0} > 0.

For each embedding it forms

    A_{t,i}=sum_{j=0}^{m-1} e_{t+j} beta_i^j-k_t beta_i^m.

The contracting embeddings bound |A_{t,0}| by

    M_U=(1/c_0) sum_{i>=1}|c_i|
        (d_U/(1-|beta_i|)+K_U^*),

and hence bound the right side of the exact adjacent-row identity

    beta^m(e_{t+m}+k_t-beta k_{t+1})
       = beta A_{t+1,0}-A_{t,0}+e_t

by B_U=d_U+(1+beta)M_U. Once beta^m delta_U^*>B_U, separation forces

    k_{t+1}=0 and e_{t+m}=-k_t.

Thus the large-aperture mechanism consumes two adjacent bounded rank-congruence rows and produces collapse of the next carry and the new boundary digit. It does not consume a normalization transducer, Condition F, zero preservation, or a bounded-zero automaton. In an injective graph, iterating the collapse makes a path of m+1 collision rows reach the zero state, which is impossible because that state has a loop; hence the large-aperture path length is at most m. The finitely many smaller apertures are patched by exact acyclic longest-path searches, giving an effective fixed-system constant C_U. This finite patch is part of the proof and should not be mistaken for a uniform state count.

(iv) Bounded-zero bridge. Each row can be written as the bounded zero representation

    [e_t,e_{t+1},...,e_{t+m-1},-k_t]_U=0.

Successive rows overlap by one digit. This consumes rank congruences and their bounded carries and produces a family of overlapping bounded zero words. It is a genuine bridge to the standard theory of zero representations, but it is not numerical normalization: the congruence is among integer language ranks. The manuscript's phrase "bounded-zero strip" is its own packaging of these consecutive zero words.

(v) Fixed cubic terminal mechanism. For the root theta of x^3-2x^2+x-1 and counts Q_0=1,Q_1=2,Q_2=4 with Q_{j+3}=2Q_{j+2}-Q_{j+1}+Q_j, a direct growth estimate restricts carries to {-1,0,1}. The general adjacent-row contraction is checked explicitly from m>=6, while m=4,5 are exhausted separately. After carry collapse, full suffix sums R_t=sum_{j>=0}Q_j e_{t+j} obey the exact recurrence identity

    R_t=e_t+e_{t+2}+2R_{t+1}-R_{t+2}+R_{t+3}.

Four consecutive zero suffix sums therefore force e_t=-e_{t+2}. Small terminal equations are exhaustively enumerated and then propagated backward, producing the two signed terminal words and excluding one more row. This consumes the cubic recurrence, the bounded carry set, and a finite terminal table; it produces exact sharp survival paths. It is a highly system-specific sharpness mechanism, not evidence by itself for a general beta-normalization theorem.

(vi) Quadratic annihilation and nearest-multiple separation. For the two quadratic Pisot chambers the legal-word counts satisfy Q_r=aQ_{r-1}+s bQ_{r-2}, with s=1 in the negative-conjugate chamber and s=-1 in the positive-conjugate chamber. A ratio analysis, Euclidean remainder audit in the positive-recurrence case, and an integral-norm argument in the negative-recurrence case prove, for r>=4 and 1<=|e|<=d,

    dist(b e Q_{r-1}, Q_r Z) >= Q_{r-2}.

Applying the recurrence annihilator to three successive sliding congruences cancels their interior coefficients and leaves

    e_t+delta e_{t+1}-s b Q_{m-1}e_{t+m+1}=0 mod Q_m,

where delta=Q_1-a is 1 or 0. Separation makes the far digit vanish; terminal inequalities and back-substitution then kill the remaining kernel. The endpoint m=3 is handled by a complete bounded-triple enumeration. This consumes a second-order recurrence and bounded digits; it produces a trivial m-row sliding kernel and explicit local decoding. The aperture-two cases are instead decided from the exact two-digit fold table.

(vii) Overlap presentation, lifting, and cover identification. The natural graph has raw (m-1)-blocks as vertices, de Bruijn overlap as its underlying graph, and the folded m-window as each edge label. It consumes the local rank fold and produces a finite labeled presentation of the image. Nonwrapping rank differences prove left- and right-resolvingness. Once an output word of length m labels a unique raw path, it is intrinsically synchronizing; this gives follower separation and identifies the graph, by the standard Fischer-Krieger criterion, as the right Fischer cover in the fixed output alphabet. Gluing the uniquely decoded overlapping raw blocks gives a finite-type presentation. The manuscript is explicit that the resulting exponential state count is relative to that output alphabet and is not a conjugacy invariant, since the image above threshold is conjugate to a full shift.

(viii) Simple-Parry and Bassino-family refinements. For a nonintegral simple-Parry word d_beta(1)=t_1...t_p0^infinity, the standard Parry-Bertrand recurrence and consecutive colexicographic rank feed directly into the arithmetic difference graph; no Pisot hypothesis is needed for that quotient or its aperture-two analysis. In Bassino's cubic Pisot family, a parameter-uniform two-window carry exclusion combines growth bounds with a quadratic energy for u_j=Q_{j+1}-beta Q_j: if the two nonreal conjugates have sum s and product p, then

    I_j=u_{j+1}^2-s u_j u_{j+1}+p u_j^2,
    I_{j+1}=p I_j.

Completed-square bounds control the shifted weighted sum and force the second carry to vanish. An induction then removes the endpoints of a collision word, reduces aperture r to r-1, and classifies the three terminal possibilities. This consumes a very special cubic recurrence and its conjugate geometry; it produces exact terminal paths in that published family.

(ix) Auxiliary prototype transfers. In the Fibonacci prototype, d'Ocagne's identity gives a modular cross-multiplication formula, and reversal plus complementation of alternating digits conjugates a shifted-weight residue model to the canonical one by the affine permutation r -> F_{m+1}(r+1)-1 modulo F_{m+2}. This transfers whole fiber-size multisets. It is useful bookkeeping and a source of exact prototype formulas, but it does not feed the fixed-system Pisot contraction in (iii).

2. Separate the manuscript's objects from the field's objects

(a) Objects defined or specially named by this manuscript

1. The cyclic language-rank fold Fold_{U,m} or Fold_{beta,m}: extend a fixed-length canonical positional rank to the full digit cube, reduce it cyclically modulo the number of legal words, and return the canonical word with that residue.
2. The resulting cyclic language-rank sliding recoding Phi_{U,m}, its image Y_{beta,m}, and its finite-block versions. These are particular sliding block codes, but the particular code family is introduced here.
3. The exact "future-only inverse length" ell_cau. This is a mild repackaging of a standard inverse sliding block code constrained to memory zero: ell_cau-1 is its least anticipation. It is not synonymous with the general notion of right-closing delay.
4. The obstruction sets N_{U,m,L} and N_{beta,m,L}, equivalently the manuscript's banded Toeplitz sliding rank-congruence kernels with a nonzero first digit.
5. Gamma_{U,m} and Gamma_{beta,m} as the coordinatewise-difference arithmetic quotients for this fold. Their generic ancestry is the classical pair/fiber graph; the specific quotient and weights are map-specific.
6. "Bounded-zero strips," meaning one-digit-overlapping rows [e_t,...,e_{t+m-1},-k_t]_U=0. Each row is a standard bounded zero representation; the strip formulation and its aperture parameter are this manuscript's construction.
7. The nontrivial overlap threshold m_*(beta), finite-block onset delta(beta,m), and the named two-window branch locus for this fold. Thresholds and branch loci are ordinary concepts, but these particular quantities belong to the constructed map.
8. The boundary-modified constant-type rank data and the Fibonacci/metallic "stabilization" terminology used for the fold prototypes. The underlying Zeckendorf, Ostrowski, and Parry rank structures are standard; the cyclic stabilization operation is not.
9. The explicit signed terminal words E_m and E_{n,r}, terminal collision sets, and the special obstruction recursions used to prove sharp depth.

(b) Standard objects that genuinely occur in the surrounding literature and are used or directly touched here

1. Pisot numbers; Pisot linear numeration systems; canonical greedy U-expansions, U-normal representations, representation languages, and positional valuation/rank.
2. Greedy beta-expansions, the beta-transformation, d_beta(1) and d_beta^*(1), beta-shifts, Parry and simple-Parry numbers, Parry polynomials, canonical Parry-Bertrand numeration, and the standard Fibonacci/Zeckendorf, Ostrowski/metallic, quadratic Pisot, and Bassino cubic simple-Parry examples.
3. Numerical beta-normalization and addition relations, their finite transducers in Pisot bases, bounded zero representations and their automata, Condition F, and zero preservation. These are comparison objects, not what the cyclic rank congruence itself computes.
4. Full shifts, shifts of finite type, sofic and synchronized shifts, factor maps and sliding block codes, topological conjugacies, code degree and fibers, inverse block codes, memory and anticipation, right- and left-resolving presentations, follower sets, intrinsically synchronizing words, Markov order, and right Fischer/Krieger covers.
5. Classical pair graphs, fiber-product graphs, bundle graphs, collision graphs, de Bruijn overlap graphs, reachable-cycle injectivity tests, and periodic collision certificates for local maps.
6. Rational relations, subsequential transducers, bounded-delay and synchronization theory, including conversion to synchronized or letter-to-letter form under an already known bounded-delay hypothesis.
7. Finite-state encoders for constrained systems, encoder presentations, and decoder/look-ahead bounds expressed in the size of a presenting graph together with memory and anticipation parameters.

Please do not assume list (b) is right merely because it uses standard terminology. In particular, numerical beta-normalization is a standard object but the paper repeatedly says its fold is not normalization; the question is whether the machinery can be redirected to say something about normalization, not whether a change of vocabulary can make the present result look like one.
