The positioning repair is done and it turned up something you did not have when you wrote that
assessment. On reading the full Albayrak-Bell paper rather than its abstract, the overlap is wider
than you indicated. Their Theorem 1.1 holds for every d over the synchronously padded tuple
alphabet, and their Proposition 2.1 admits general polynomial-growth bounded regular languages
rather than only slender ones. So besides containing the regular integer-base specialization of
our Theorem 3.2, it covers multidimensional sparse automatic tuple sets and the regular
poly-slender integer-base shadow, and it is quantitatively stronger on the overlap. We narrowed our
closing disclaimer accordingly: the claim now does not extend to NONREGULAR poly-slender
representation languages, rather than to poly-slenderness generally.

That leaves one asymmetry which we have recorded as our limitation and which is the subject of
this question. Albayrak-Bell conclude finiteness WITH AN EXPLICIT BOUND in terms of the bases, the
dimension and the minimal automata. Our Theorem 3.2 concludes only finiteness.

THIS IS A QUESTION ABOUT ACQUIRING THAT MISSING STRENGTH, NOT ABOUT FINDING A NEW THEOREM.

 1. Can our theorem be made quantitative? Specifically, can the finiteness conclusion be replaced
    by an effective bound on the size or on the largest element of X, in terms of data available in
    our setting - the two recurrences, the weak Perron numbers alpha and beta, the paired-loop
    parameters of the two slender context-free grammars, and the transfer constants of Lemma 3.1?
    Answer at the level of which step blocks it. The architecture is: finite paired-loop covers,
    finitely many linear recurrence sequences, then Mignotte common-value finiteness.

 2. Where exactly does effectivity fail, if it does? Our common-value input is Mignotte 1978,
    whose proof we are told is effective. If the obstruction is instead in the paired-loop
    decomposition, or in the residue-class restriction, or in the dominant-root transfer of Lemma
    3.1, say which - and say whether that step is inherently ineffective or merely not yet made
    effective.

 3. If a bound is reachable, what would it cost and what would it buy? Give a success probability
    as a number, an honest estimate of whether this is a paragraph, a section, or a separate
    project, and say whether a quantitative version would change the venue judgement at all. If it
    would not - if Monatshefte remains right even with a bound - say that plainly, because then we
    should not spend the effort.

 4. If a bound is NOT reachable, is that itself worth stating in the paper? A recorded, argued
    reason why the context-free case resists the quantitative treatment that the regular case
    admits would be more useful to a reader than silence, and it would explain the asymmetry with
    Albayrak-Bell rather than leaving it as an unexplained weakness. If you agree, say what the
    obstruction is in a form we could write down.
