WHAT WAS DONE

The theorem is proved in the form you specified: if U and V are strictly increasing standard
greedy linear numeration sequences whose least eventual recurrence polynomials are the minimal
polynomials of multiplicatively independent weak Perron numbers alpha, beta > 1, and if for one set
X the full representation languages Rep_U(X) and Rep_V(X) are both slender context-free, then X is
finite. The architecture is the one you prescribed - finite paired-loop covers, finitely many
linear recurrences, Mignotte common-value finiteness - and it uses none of the finite-group return
times, deleted-prime adic topology, Evertse quotient theorem, geometric-subsequence rigidity or
divisibility-tree machinery.

The dominant-root transfer lemma was audited at the level of the actual affine block matrices
rather than inferred from the growth squeeze, which is what you asked for. Separability of the
minimal polynomial makes C diagonalizable, so every C^{|b|} remains diagonalizable even when
distinct conjugates coalesce under exponentiation; the peripheral eigenvalues have modulus
alpha^{|b|} > 1 and therefore cannot collide with the affine eigenvalue 1, so the affine row
contributes neither a peripheral eigenvalue nor a peripheral Jordan block; vectorization gives the
powering matrix K = M_v^T tensor M_x, and choosing the weak-Perron period h makes every peripheral
eigenvalue of K^h equal to the single positive number Lambda = alpha^{h(|v|+|x|)}, with the
peripheral generalized eigenspace semisimple by the tensor-product Jordan formula. Positivity of
the leading coefficient, obtained from the greedy length interval and the weak-Perron residue
asymptotics, rules out peripheral cancellation. All three risks you named - peripheral collisions,
Jordan factors and cancellation - are addressed explicitly rather than assumed away.

On priority, the search is recorded as a negative search result and not as a novelty claim. The
paired-loop classification is taken from Ilie, Rozenberg and Salomaa, and the common-value
finiteness from Mignotte 1978, both cited in their exact published form. One sourcing gap is
recorded honestly: the Elsevier full text of Mignotte's article was unreachable from this network,
so its statement was cross-checked through Crossref metadata and Kiss's published formulation
rather than read directly.

The two scope corrections you asked for are applied. The adic framing now states that Broughan's
adic topologies, completions and ambient prime-factor strata are standard, that only the
deleted-prime sets, their intrinsic derivative and the local prime-factor rank are ours, and that
Proposition 2.10 is a local refinement rather than a new ambient classification. The manuscript
also explicitly disclaims conclusions about Renyi beta-expansions, beta-shifts, Parry languages,
beta-integers, Cantor real bases and abstract numeration systems, with the structural reasons
stated.

The Charlier-Kreczman position is unchanged: their Proposition 10 and Remark 12 own the
quotient-limit mechanism, the new theorem may use that asymptotic input only with the same
attribution, and neither our residue asymptotics nor the greedy length squeeze is presented as the
priority-bearing step.
