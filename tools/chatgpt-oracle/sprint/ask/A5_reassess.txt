That risk materialised, and this is the main thing we want you to look at.

WHAT WAS DONE

The general-p theorem was built and is now Theorem 3.13, with all seven parts: at most one
normalized rational solution; a deterministic decision-and-construction algorithm; the degree bound
deg A + deg B <= N_p(D); the explicit height bound; the single-affine-Pade reconstruction from the
first 2 N_p(D) + 1 coefficients with an exact acceptance identity; polynomial bit complexity for
fixed p; and Omega_p(D log D) sharpness. Lemma 3.14 is the consolidated general-p reconstruction
lemma, proving the computable coefficient bounds, quotient-independence of the Pade solution,
recovery of every rational solution of degree at most N_p(D), sound rejection, and the bit bound.

But the prior-art search came back negative for us, and the manuscript now says so plainly rather
than asserting a distinction. Logarithmic differentiation with u(z) = z R'(z)/R(z) turns
H = P_0/P_1 and H = R(z^p)/R(z)^p into the additive Mahler equation (sigma - 1) u = (z/p) H'/H.
Arreche and Zhang's Mahler discrete residues decide exactly that rational summability problem, and
Chyzak-Dreyfus-Dumas-Mezzarobba's linear Mahler solver decides the resulting rational problem after
filtering. The converse is effective: a rational u comes from a normalized R exactly when u/z is
regular at zero, has no polynomial part, and has only simple poles with integer residues, after
which Galois invariance groups the poles into rational irreducible factors. So bare decidability is
already subsumed by prior additive or linear Mahler algorithms. The introduction states this
reduction explicitly.

What we now claim is only the direct one-system reconstruction package: the explicit sharp-order
O_p(D log D) input-only degree bound, the height and coefficient bounds, exact acceptance and
rejection, and fixed-p polynomial bit complexity. Both Nishioka theorems, the fixed-class Euler
product and the determinant/periodic-data dictionary remain outside the ledger.

One correction to your own audit: you attributed to Proposition 3.11 the bound
N_p(D) = ceil((pD/2) m_p(D)). The manuscript actually proves the stronger
b_p(D) = ceil((2D/p) m_p(D)). Since b_p(D) <= N_p(D) for p >= 2, Proposition 3.11 still yields
Theorem 3.13(iii), but the formulas are not the same. Please confirm or correct us.

You also found a real defect, which is fixed: the proof of Theorem 3.17 called the mod-2 character

and (OA) is quantified over the character group, not over group elements.

The limitation you insisted on is recorded: the principal dynamical theorem remains an inverse
