Write a question putting THIS paper to an external expert, and write it ONLY to the output
path named at the end of this task. No preamble, no commentary, no other files. The question
must be self-contained: the expert sees only your text plus main.pdf.

THIS QUESTION IS DELIBERATELY DIFFERENT FROM BOTH PREVIOUS ROUNDS. DO NOT REUSE THEIR SHAPE.

Round one asked "is the increment enough for this journal, and if not, which journal fits?"
That has a built-in downward bias and produced seven downgrade recommendations.

Round two asked "what named, reachable new result would raise the tier?" That was much better
and produced real targets. But its answers kept landing inside our own object universe, and
the same diagnosis came back on paper after paper:

    our theorems are about objects THIS MANUSCRIPT DEFINES, whereas a tier raise requires a
    structural theorem about the objects THE FIELD ITSELF already studies.

So this round attacks exactly that. Its job is not to ask what more we can prove. It is to ask
WHAT THE FIELD ALREADY CARES ABOUT, and whether our machinery touches any of it.

The question you write must do the following, in this order.

 1. STATE THE PAPER'S MACHINERY, NOT ITS THEOREMS. Read the manuscript and describe, precisely,
    the technical apparatus it actually runs on - the estimates, identities, contraction or
    separation arguments, graph or automaton constructions, transfer or lifting steps. Say what
    each one consumes and what it produces. The expert must be able to judge what the machinery
    could be pointed at, and that is a different description from a list of results.

 2. SEPARATE OUR OBJECTS FROM THE FIELD'S OBJECTS, HONESTLY AND EXPLICITLY. Give two lists.
      (a) Objects this manuscript defines or names for its own purposes.
      (b) Objects that appear in other people's papers and in survey articles of this field -
          the standard ones, with their standard names.
    Do not pad list (b) with things we merely cite in passing. Do not disguise a list (a) object
    as a list (b) object by giving it a classical-sounding name. If a notion of ours is a mild
    repackaging of a standard notion, say so and give the standard name.

 3. THEN ASK HIM FOR AN INVENTORY, and ask him to correct list (b) first - to add the standard
    objects of this field that we omitted, and to strike anything we wrongly promoted. For each
    object on his corrected list, ask for a one-line verdict: does the machinery of section 1
    say anything about it - yes, partially, or no - and if partially, what exactly is missing.

 4. THEN THE CENTRAL DEMAND. Ask him to name the strongest theorem about an object on HIS
    corrected list that our machinery could plausibly reach, and to state it as a theorem, with
    hypotheses.

    IMPOSE THIS TEST ON IT, IN THESE WORDS OR CLOSE TO THEM: the target theorem must be
    statable WITHOUT USING ANY TERM THIS MANUSCRIPT INVENTED. If stating it requires our
    vocabulary, it is not a theorem about the field's objects and it does not qualify. Ask him
    to write the statement out in that constrained form so the test is visibly met.

 5. Ask, for that target: which specific lemmas or estimates of ours feed it; what ingredient is
    missing; whether obtaining it is a difficult extension of the present proof or a different
    research project; and a SUCCESS PROBABILITY AS A NUMBER.

 6. Ask what the strongest objection to the paper would still be afterwards.

CONSTRAINTS TO WRITE INTO THE QUESTION:

 - Do NOT answer the tier question by recommending a lower-tier journal. We are asking what to
   prove, not where to submit.
 - Do NOT propose adding further theorems of the kind already present. Mechanical accumulation
   moved nothing across eight papers and left most of them with priority omissions, because
   exact statements composed from standard equivalences carry little independent priority.
 - Do NOT accept a restatement of one of our results in classical-sounding language as though it
   were a theorem about the field's objects. That is the failure mode we most want checked, and
   the test in item 4 exists to catch it.
 - An honest exit is required and is valuable: if the machinery genuinely says nothing about any
   standard object of the field, he should say so plainly and name the structural feature that
   confines it to our own constructions. A well-argued ceiling lets us stop; vague encouragement
   does not.

Give the paper's position honestly, including its current venue standing and any priority
objection already raised against it. Do not oversell. Do not undersell either - stating the
results as weaker than they are produces a correspondingly deflated answer.

Read the manuscript before writing. Every result, hypothesis and constant you attribute to the
paper must be checked against the source, not recalled. Attribution errors in the question have
already cost us one round.
