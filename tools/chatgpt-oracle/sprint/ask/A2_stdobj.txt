I. What the proof machinery actually does

1. Kernel-quotient jets and the local/tail split. For a scaled density p_s(x)=s^{-d}p(x/s), the paper divides p_s*lambda by p_s and writes the quotient as

   q_s^lambda(y)=integral F_y(x/s) lambda(dx),    F_y(z)=p(y-z)/p(y).

   At order r it assumes bounded normalized derivatives partial^gamma p/p for 1 <= |gamma| <= r and uniform quotient-continuity of the translated order-r derivatives. It Taylor-expands F_y(x/s) only where |x| <= s, retains the exterior translate mixture exactly, and subtracts the exterior Taylor modes. A finite r-th absolute moment then gives the exact form

   q_s^lambda=A_s^lambda+s^{-r}B_r^lambda+R_s^lambda,
   ||R_s^lambda||_infinity+||R_s^lambda||_{L^1(p(y)dy)}=o(s^{-r}).

   Here the common lower-order moment jet is put into A_s^lambda, while the positive unexpanded exterior mixture and its mass correction make A_s^lambda a normalized positive density for all large s. Thus the split consumes bounded normalized kernel jets, a quotient translation modulus, matching moments below r, and finite r-th moments; it produces two positive backgrounds, a bounded order-r mode, and a strong little-oh remainder. No semigroup law, radial symmetry, stable scaling, or regular variation is used in this abstract step.

2. Entropy finiteness and kernel closure. Bounded first normalized derivative gives the bounded-score estimate

   D_KL(p(. - z) || p) <= ||grad log p||_infinity |z|.

   Convexity then makes the retained exterior mixtures and the proxy relative entropy finite under the same finite r-th moment. The kernel assumptions are verified for every isotropic strictly alpha-stable density, 0<alpha<2, and every multivariate Student density c_(beta,d)(1+|y|^2)^(-beta/2), beta>d. They are preserved under invertible affine images, finite tensor products, and finite positive mixtures. This closure step consumes verified normalized-derivative bounds for base kernels and produces generally nonradial, nonstable kernels to which the same quotient split applies. The Gaussian is intentionally outside this bounded-score/additive-background mechanism: its normalized derivatives are Hermite polynomials, its score is unbounded, and even the common first-order additive proxy can become negative. The paper does not claim that Gaussian asymptotics fail or that no multiplicative Gaussian proxy can exist.

3. Two-background KL/Bregman transfer. On a probability space, the paper expands relative entropy between two moving positive backgrounds, not merely against the constant density 1. In the abstract version, A_i,s >= c>0, A_i,s -> 1 in L^1, ||A_1,s-A_2,s||_1=o(epsilon_s), bounded zero-mean perturbations enter at size epsilon_s, and the remainders are o(epsilon_s) in L^infinity and L^1. Removal of the remainders, a first-variation estimate, identification of the Hessian, and an integrated third-derivative bound give

   D_KL(q_1,s || q_2,s)
   =D_KL(A_1,s || A_2,s)
    +(epsilon_s^2/2) integral (B_1-B_2)^2 dOmega+o(epsilon_s^2).

   This consumes positive backgrounds whose difference is especially small in L^1 and produces an additive nonnegative background-divergence term plus the universal quadratic Hessian term. A second critical transfer works with a denominator converging only in L^1 and a remainder in L^q: if pq=2r, the L^q error o(s^{-p}) contributes o(s^{-2r}) to KL. This is the principal nonlinear transfer mechanism.

4. Stable critical translate estimates and the optimal moment exponent. For an isotropic strictly alpha-stable density, beta=d+alpha, the proof uses

   p_1(y) asymp (1+|y|)^(-beta),
   |partial^gamma p_1(y)| <= C_gamma(1+|y|)^(-beta-|gamma|),

   and the induced normalized derivative and L^1-translation bounds. It proves the translate-quotient growth

   ||F_.(z)||_{L^q(p_1dy)}^q asymp (1+|z|)^{beta(q-1)}

   and, at first unmatched tensor order r,

   q_(r,alpha,d)=min{2,1+2r/(d+alpha)},
   p_(r,alpha,d)=2r/q_(r,alpha,d)
                  =max{r,2r(d+alpha)/(d+alpha+2r)}.

   Taylor through floor(p_(r,alpha,d)), Minkowski, and dominated convergence turn a finite p_(r,alpha,d)-moment into an L^q quotient remainder o(s^{-p_(r,alpha,d)}); the identity p_(r,alpha,d) q_(r,alpha,d)=2r then feeds the critical Bregman transfer. This consumes the polynomial-tail and derivative profile of the stable kernel and produces the endpoint, all-order s^{-2r} KL asymptotic with a positive quadratic form on the first unmatched symmetric moment tensor.

5. Sharpness by cancellation blocks and spatial separation. To show that the preceding moment exponent is uniform and sharp when d+alpha>2r, the paper uses the signed r-th forward-difference block

   2^{1-r} sum_{k=0}^r (-1)^{r-k} binom(r,k) delta_{(k+1)Re}.

   Its positive and negative parts are probability measures, all moments below r cancel, and the r-th directional moment is 2^{1-r}r!R^r. These blocks are placed at lacunary radii R_(j+1)>4(r+1)R_j with weights w_j=a_jR_j^{-rho}. At scale s_j=kappa R_j w_j^{1/(d+alpha)}, a ball around an atom present on only one side receives probability at least a_r w_j from one smoothed law and at most C kappa^{d+alpha}w_j from the other. Data processing to that ball and its complement, followed by a uniform Bernoulli-KL lower bound, yields D_KL >= c w_j. This consumes exact finite-difference cancellation and lacunary geometric separation; it produces counterexamples below p_(r,alpha,d), including pairs with a finite nonzero r-th tensor difference for rho>=r.

6. Cauchy/Cayley coefficient calculus and bounded-mode converses. In one dimension the exact translate quotient

   R_epsilon(y)=(1+y^2)/(1+(y-epsilon)^2)

   is put in the Cayley coordinate y=tan(theta), z=e^{2i theta}. The Chebyshev generating function gives bounded Taylor modes u_n and finite Laurent representatives Q_n; the global two-region remainder is O(|epsilon|^{L+1}) for |epsilon|<=1 and O(|epsilon|^L) for |epsilon|>1. Expanding Phi(v)=(1+v)log(1+v)-v and extracting Laurent constant terms turns products of the Q_n into a finite algorithm for all retained entropy coefficients; parity kills all odd coefficients. This consumes a finite (2N-2)-th centered absolute moment and produces the KL expansion through t^{-2N}, with A_4=(E X_c^2)^2/8.

   Separately, the exact Fourier coefficients

   a_t(X)=iX/(2t-iX),    c_k(t)=E[a_t(X)^k]

   satisfy c_(k+1)=-c_k-(t/k)c_k', Parseval gives chi-square as 2 sum_{k>=1}|c_k|^2 under finite variance, and (1+c_1(t))/(2t) is the Laplace transform of the characteristic function. The identity -Re c_1(t)=E[X^2/(4t^2+X^2)] supplies monotone truncated even-moment remainders. Bounded tests plus Pinsker turn this into converses, including the exact variance threshold and the infinite-moment half of the Gaussian-quadrature comparison. This apparatus consumes exact Cauchy rational identities and produces transform reconstruction and moment-threshold lower bounds, not merely more formal coefficients.

7. Tensor contractions. For multidimensional Poisson or stable kernels, multivariate Taylor modes are contracted against symmetric moment tensors. Radial beta-prime integrals and spherical fourth-moment identities separate the trace and traceless covariance components; Fourier nonvanishing near the origin proves positive definiteness of the general order-r quadratic form, and the O(d) harmonic decomposition records its invariant blocks. This consumes the kernel derivatives and rotation invariance and produces coordinate-free positive tensor energies.

8. Nonlocal entropy dissipation and endpoint closure. For the one-dimensional Cauchy semigroup, finite variance gives, on every [a,b] compactly contained in (0,infinity), the explicit quotient bounds

   1/[2(1+mu_2/a^2)] <= u_t <= 2+2mu_2/a^2,
   Lip(u_t) <= 3a^{-1}(1+mu_2/a^2).

   These bounds dominate the logarithmic Bregman kernel against the Cauchy jump singularity. An L^1 Nemytskii chain rule, annular approximations to the Cauchy generator, and two noncompact Green pairings for (h_t,log u_t) and (g_t,u_t) are combined by the exact two-point Bregman identity. The separate endpoint H(T)->0 then produces H(t)=integral_t^infinity I_nu(s)ds. This consumes finite variance and compact-window quotient control; it produces a rigorous domain statement and an integrated relative-entropy dissipation identity. Through q=st/(1-t), it also yields the finite-variance alpha=1 case of Johnson's Open Problem 4, but the manuscript expressly does not claim the general symmetric-stable problem.

9. Hilbert-space and lattice lifting. A residue calculation gives the secant Gram kernel K(a,b)=2/[4+(a-b)^2]. Poisson-transform/Fourier injectivity makes the secant family complete in L^2_0(dy/[pi(1+y^2)]), and Gram matching extends it unitarily to the RKHS H_K. Fourier multiplication identifies H_K with sqrt(pi)e^{-|D|}L^2(R) and gives holomorphic extension to |Im z|<1. On the integer orbit, Poisson summation computes the Toeplitz symbol

   sigma(theta)=pi cosh(2(pi-|theta|))/sinh(2pi),  |theta|<=pi,

   with lower and upper bounds pi/sinh(2pi) and pi coth(2pi). An imported principal shift-invariant-space criterion then produces Riesz bounds, an inverse restriction map, a cardinal kernel, and reciprocal-symbol norm formulas. The unitary map lifts the centered Poisson quotient to an RKHS observation profile; integer samples reconstruct its orthogonal projection onto the integer-translate space, and Fourier uniqueness identifies full recovery with support of the centered input law on tZ. There is no graph or automaton construction in the paper: the discrete mechanism is Toeplitz-symbol diagonalization, Riesz-basis interpolation, and an orthogonal projection/lifting argument.
