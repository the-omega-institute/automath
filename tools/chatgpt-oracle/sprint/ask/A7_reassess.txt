The missing ingredient is now Lemma 6.5, citing Granville, "Primitive prime factors in
second-order linear recurrence sequences", Acta Arithmetica 155 (2012), Corollaries 3 and 4. Under
our convention it gives, for n >= 13, Psi_n(1) = c_n U_n^prim with c_n = 1 or c_n a prime divisor
of n, hence log U_n^prim = phi_E(n) log(golden ratio) + O(log n). The discriminant factor 5 is
reconciled explicitly: z(5) = 5, so 5 is never primitive at n >= 13, and a residual 5 in the
cyclotomic factor is absorbed into c_n and divides n. An exact-integer check over all 198 indices

is a sanity check, not the argument.

ONE DEPARTURE FROM YOUR OUTLINE, WHICH WE BELIEVE IS A CORRECTION AND WOULD LIKE YOU TO CONFIRM.
Your Step 2 quoted the bound a(n) <= (2/log phi + o(1)) phi(n)/log n. The manuscript's Theorem 6.4
has the reciprocal-type coefficient, a(n) <= (log phi / 2 + o(1)) phi_E(n)/log n. Using your

coefficient gives exactly 2 log n. The proof as written therefore uses the manuscript's
coefficient. It also supplies a step your outline omitted: O(log n) = o(phi_E(n)), via the
standard lower bound phi_E(n) >> n / log log 3n, which is what lets the error term be absorbed. If
we have misread your intended normalization, please say so.

The theorem states: for every eps > 0 there is N_eps >= 13 such that for every n >= N_eps, either
F_n has a primitive prime divisor p >= n^{2-eps}, or F_n has a primitive prime divisor q with
q^2 | F_n, in which case z(q) = n and q is a Wall-Sun-Sun prime. Consequently, if no Wall-Sun-Sun
primes exist, liminf log P_prim(F_n)/log n >= 2. The repeated-factor alternative is identified via
the standard equivalence q^2 | F_{z(q)} iff q^2 | F_{q-(5/q)}, cited to Klaska. The paper states
plainly that it does not resolve Wall's question.

The comparison with Hong is stated: he gives a primitive divisor outside n+-1, ..., kappa n +- 1
for each fixed kappa, hence at least (kappa+1)n - 1; ours is far stronger in exponent but
conditional on excluding the lifting anomaly.
