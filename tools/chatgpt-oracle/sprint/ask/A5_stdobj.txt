1. THE MACHINERY ACTUALLY PRESENT IN THE PAPER

The following is a machinery map, not a list of claimed theorems. Check it against main.pdf and correct any error before using it.

(i) Finite-state representation blocks and Perron domination.

Input: a primitive nonnegative integral adjacency matrix A with Perron root lambda > 1, a one-step finite-group edge cocycle tau, and a finite-dimensional unitary representation rho of G.

Operation: form the block matrix

    (A_rho)_{ij} = sum_{e:i->j} rho(tau(e))

and the ordinary commutative determinant polynomial

    P_rho(z) = det(I-z A_rho).

Using a positive Perron eigenvector of A and unitarity, the paper puts a weighted sup norm on block vectors and proves

    ||A_rho||_v <= lambda,
    rad(A_rho) <= lambda,
    |Tr(A_rho^n)| <= |V| dim(rho) lambda^n.

Output: control of traces and convergence, and zero-freeness of P_rho in the open Perron disk. A strict inequality rad(A_rho) < lambda for nontrivial rho supplies radial continuation at the Perron endpoint. It does not make the raw class Euler profiles finite there.

(ii) Trace--determinant and character transforms.

Input: the family {P_rho} and the periodic holonomies of the cocycle.

Operation: expand

    -Log_0 P_rho(z) = sum_{n>=1} Tr(A_rho^n) z^n/n

and use the irreducible character table to pass between represented traces and period-n point counts by holonomy conjugacy class. Then use the triangular necklace/Witt relation between period-n point counts and primitive-orbit counts.

Output: the standard equivalence among equality of all twisted determinant polynomials, equality of all represented trace sequences, equality of all conjugacy-class periodic-point counts, and equality of all primitive length--conjugacy-class counts. The paper explicitly says that this is periodic-data equivalence, not topological conjugacy, strong shift equivalence, cocycle cohomology, or switching equivalence.

(iii) Adams--Mobius conversion between periodic and fixed-label logarithms.

Input: the periodic Artin logarithms

    L_phi(z) = sum_gamma sum_{r>=1} phi(g_gamma^r) z^{r|gamma|}/r,

which are finite linear combinations of -Log_0 P_rho, and the Adams operations psi^m phi(g) = phi(g^m).

Operation: apply necklace/Witt Mobius inversion with the Adams action:

    Pi_phi(z) = sum_{m>=1} mu(m) L_{psi^m phi}(z^m)/m,

    F_phi(z) = sum_{r>=1} Pi_phi(z^r)/r
             = sum_{r,m>=1} mu(m) L_{psi^m phi}(z^{rm})/(rm).

Output: the fixed primitive-label logarithm F_phi, and hence the logarithms of fixed Frobenius-class Euler products, from determinant values at all power substitutions z^k. This step also isolates the exact obstruction to replacing F_phi by the usual periodic Artin logarithm L_phi: repetitions act on g_gamma by g_gamma -> g_gamma^r.

(iv) Separation and dyadic collapse for abelian 2-groups.

Input: two cocycles over possibly different primitive bases, the full vector of element-indexed values at a radius, and, for a finite abelian 2-group, the condition

    P_{chi^u,tau}(z) = P_{chi,tau}(z)

for every character chi and every odd u, separately for each cocycle.

Operation: sum the element coordinates to recover equality of the two base determinant polynomials (their degrees are at most V), then Fourier transform the remaining coordinates. Odd-Adams invariance makes the odd-divisor Mobius sums cancel, leaving only powers of 2:

    Delta_chi(z)
      = -sum_{j>=0} 2^{-j} Log_0 H_chi(z^{2^j})
        +sum_{j>=1} 2^{-j} Log_0 H_{chi^2}(z^{2^j}),

where H_chi is the ratio of the two character determinant polynomials. Induction on the order of chi removes the second term after the chi^2 channel has been recovered.

Output: each remaining character comparison becomes a one-variable dyadic multiplicative functional equation. The cancellation is automatic for G=(C_2)^r. For higher-exponent abelian 2-groups it is an actual restriction, although the manuscript gives C_4 and C_{2^s} examples with genuine higher-order holonomy. For C_3, and for generic C_4 outside this restriction, the same Adams--Mobius coefficients survive at infinitely many arithmetically distributed powers and couple several determinant ratios; the one-variable dyadic reduction fails.

(v) Algebraic special-value lifting.

Input: a rational function H in Q(z)^x with H(0)=1, zero-free on a disk, and the critical product

    F_{H,p}(z) = product_{nu>=0} H(z^{p^nu})^{p^{-nu-1}},

which satisfies F_{H,p}(z)^p = H(z)F_{H,p}(z^p). In the dynamical application p=2, positivity on a real radial segment turns equality of the relevant logarithmic coordinate at an algebraic radius y into F_{H,2}(y)=1.

Operation: a coefficient recursion gives, after q clears the coefficients of the reduced numerator and denominator of H,

    (pq)^{2n-1} f_n in Z,
    log d_h <= (2h-1) log(pq).

Cauchy bounds give linear coefficient heights. Kumiko Nishioka's special-value theorem, with the manuscript's checked parameters N=0, n=1, m=M=p, U=L=1 and p^2 < p^3, turns algebraicity of F_{H,p}(y) into algebraicity of the function. Keiji Nishioka's cited nonlinear algebraic-solution theorem then makes it rational.

Output: a unique normalized R in Q(z)^x with

    H(z) = R(z^p)/R(z)^p,
    R(0)=1,

and, in the positive real application, the collision condition is equivalent to R(y)=1. Parity is not consumed by this lifting chain. When P_0 == P_1 mod 2, the Dieudonne--Dwork criterion separately produces integral Taylor coefficients.

(vi) Divisor control, zero counting, and finite reconstruction.

Input: a reduced rational identity P_0/P_1 = R(z^p)/R(z)^p of total input degree D.

Operation: take zero/pole orders to obtain

    e(alpha) = r(alpha^p)-p r(alpha),

invert this recurrence along forward p-power orbits, and bound the l_1 norm of the divisor of R. For p=2 this gives

    deg numerator(R) + deg denominator(R)
      <= D ceil(log_2(2D)).

The normalized formal solution is computed recursively through degree 2N, a single affine Pade system of size N is solved, common factors are cancelled, and one polynomial identity accepts or rejects the result.

Output: an effective rational-coboundary decision/reconstruction procedure, an explicit height bound, and the fact that all real comparison zeros are roots of numerator(R)-denominator(R). For nonconstant H there are at most

    D ceil(log_2(2D))-1

nonzero such roots. This is the source of the manuscript's 2V ceil(log_2(4V)) sampling budget, not a direct consequence of the degree of an SFT zeta function.

(vii) Positive realization and explicit separation examples.

Input: two integer polynomials P_0,P_1 with constant term 1 and P_0 == P_1 mod 2, or a chosen polynomial Q with a controlled factorization.

Operation: realize P_0,P_1 as det(I-zC_i) using integral companion matrices; choose a positive integral matrix A, in the common parity class, that strictly dominates |C_i| entrywise; then (A+C_i)/2 and (A-C_i)/2 are the edge-count matrices of a C_2-cocycle and strict Perron--Frobenius monotonicity gives rad(C_i)<rad(A). For the multicollision family the paper builds Q_m and block matrices with

    det(I-zB_m)=Q_m(z^2),
    det(I-zB'_m)=Q_m(z)^2,

and chooses rational y_{m,i} with Q_m(y_{m,i})=1.

Output: standard two-sheeted SFT covers whose ordinary zeta-function ratio is the prescribed determinant ratio; an exact four-vertex centered endpoint collision; m distinct rational open-interval comparison zeros on a common positive (4m+2)-vertex base; the lower bound

    N_{C_2}(V) >= floor((V-2)/4)+1;

and a separate realizable family showing Omega(D log D) certificate degree. The manuscript correctly does not claim Omega(V log V) positive real comparison zeros.

2. KEEP THE PAPER'S OBJECTS AND THE FIELD'S OBJECTS SEPARATE

Here is my provisional separation. Correct it before drawing any conclusion.

(a) Objects defined, packaged, or specially named by this manuscript for its inverse problem

1. The radial class/element Euler profiles

       E_C(y) = sum_{n>=1} p_{n,C} log(1-y^n)

   and their finite vectors at selected radii.

2. The centered Perron profile

       E_C^o(y) = E_C(y)-(|C|/|G|)log det(I-yA),

   used to remove the common divergent trivial channel at an endpoint.

3. "Finite radial determination," the associated comparison/collision sets, the sampling number N_{C_2}(V), and the phrase "one algebraic anchor." These package a new inverse-data question; they are not standard conjugacy invariants merely because they are built from standard orbit counts.

4. The condition labelled (OA), namely odd-Adams invariance of each character determinant family, as a selected class on which the Adams--Mobius system collapses.

5. The paper's "dyadic boundary functional," "positive rational Mahler certificate," and normalized certificate R attached to a radial comparison. Rational Mahler coboundaries and multiplicative functional equations are not invented here, but this certificate vocabulary and its use as the classifier of profile collisions are paper-specific packaging.

6. The fixed-label coordinate F_phi as the paper's principal coordinate. This must not be presented as a wholly new field object: the manuscript itself says that -F_{1_C} is the logarithm of the fixed-class Euler product already implicit in Mohamed--Noorani's 1999 Mertens product. What is paper-specific is its explicit Adams--Mobius determinant readout and its use in this finite-special-value inverse problem.

7. The particular "complete real collision set" identification through R(y)=1 and the O(V log V) radial-location budget.

(b) Candidate standard objects, with their standard names, found in the surrounding literature

1. Mixing shifts of finite type, their nonnegative integral adjacency matrices, and their topological conjugacy invariants; periodic-point sequences and Artin--Mazur zeta functions are standard but incomplete conjugacy invariants.

2. The Artin--Mazur/Ruelle dynamical zeta function of an SFT (the usual periodic-orbit zeta, rational and equal to det(I-zA)^{-1} in this setting), and representation-twisted Artin or Ruelle L-functions/determinants for finite-group extensions.

3. Locally constant finite-group cocycles and skew-product extensions over SFTs; cocycle cohomology/Livsic equivalence, switching or gauge equivalence, G-SFTs/G-primitive matrices, represented trace or conjugacy-class trace series, and periodic holonomy/Frobenius-class data.

4. Prime-orbit/Chebotarev counting in prescribed Frobenius classes and class-restricted Mertens products for finite covers of symbolic or hyperbolic systems.

5. Strong shift equivalence over Z_+, shift equivalence, dimension groups, and the Williams classification/problem. These concern conjugacy or its algebraic surrogates, not mere equality of determinant polynomials. The historical "Williams problem" needs to be named precisely and not treated as still open in a form known to be false.

6. Bowen--Franks groups and flow equivalence of SFTs (including the corresponding G-SFT/finite-group-extension questions). A Bowen--Franks group is not a complete topological-conjugacy invariant, and the present manuscript does not compute or control it.

7. The Boyle--Schmieding realization problems for determinant polynomials, trace series, and conjugacy-class trace series arising from G-primitive matrices. The manuscript starts from already realized extensions and proves injectivity/compression statements between them; that is not a characterization of the image of a positivity-realization map.

8. Mahler functions, Mahler's method, algebraic special values, rational solutions of Mahler functional equations, and effective algorithms for linear Mahler equations or Mahler--Riccati-type equations. The manuscript's equation is the nonlinear critical multiplicative equation F(z^p)=F(z)^p/H(z), not a general linear Mahler equation.

9. Adams operations on representation rings and lambda-rings, together with necklace/Witt-vector transforms. The manuscript uses psi^m chi(g)=chi(g^m) and classical Mobius inversion, but does not prove a structural theorem about lambda-rings.

10. The Dieudonne--Dwork lemma/criterion for integrality of power series. This is the standard object actually used. "Dieudonne determinants" should be struck from this inventory unless you find a genuine noncommutative determinant issue in main.pdf: every determinant formed in the manuscript is an ordinary determinant of a complex or integral matrix, and the Dieudonne--Dwork integrality criterion is not a Dieudonne determinant.

11. Gain/voltage graphs, switching classes, and fundamental-cycle gains. The appendix recalls the standard switching criterion but expressly does not deduce switching from determinant equality.
