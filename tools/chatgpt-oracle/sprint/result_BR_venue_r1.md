First choice: Annales de l’Institut Henri Poincaré, Probabilités et Statistiques
This is the journal I would put in the cover letter.
The paper’s strongest identity is now probabilistic, not merely number-theoretic. Result 1 is a sharp condensation theorem for a weighted random combinatorial object: uniqueness of the giant digit, joint total-variation convergence of the two environments, an exact n−1 rate and limiting constant, and defect/location laws. Result 4 is a finite-size critical-window theorem with an explicit crossover law and stable fluctuations. Those are the two results an editor should use to classify the paper.
AIHP explicitly publishes high-quality work throughout modern probability. More importantly, it has recently published work in almost precisely the relevant conceptual territory. 2. Returning to Transactions
Do not return to Transactions. It is a waste of a cycle.
Adding result 4 makes the paper materially better. It repairs the earlier description of the manuscript as consisting primarily of a refined condensation asymptotic and an arithmetic coefficient correction. It does not, in my judgment, turn the paper into a Transactions paper.
The reason is not that result 4 is incremental. It is that the advance remains concentrated in one highly structured arithmetic model. Transactions publishes substantial longer papers across all areas of mathematics, and the editorial comparison is consequently broader than the comparison at a probability or number-theory specialist journal. 美国数学学会 The present paper gives an impressive exact analysis of a particular weighted Stern–Brocot/Fibonacci renewal system; it does not yet extract a probabilistic mechanism whose relevance is visibly independent of that system.
A return would be defensible only if result 4 were promoted into a general theorem for a nontrivial class of contextual heavy-tailed renewal or composition models, with the Fibonacci model as one application. Merely adding the present model-specific crossover theorem to the manuscript that Transactions already rejected is unlikely to reverse the significance judgment. I would put the probability of a favorable Transactions outcome below 10%. The reputational issue is minor; the lost editorial cycle is the real cost.
The correct interpretation of the new theorem is:

It raises the manuscript from “right-sized for JNT” to “worth trying at a strong probability specialist journal.”

It does not raise it back to Transactions.

The single change that would most raise the probability
Rebuild the introduction around one comparison theorem: explain, theorem by theorem, exactly why existing Gibbs-partition and one-big-jump results do not imply results 1 and 4.
This should not be a generic literature paragraph. It should be a compact mathematical comparison placed immediately after the principal theorem statements. In particular, it should establish four points:


Standard condensation theory may predict a unique giant component, but does not provide the joint two-sided total-variation limit with the exact n−1 constant.


The continuant weight is not an ordinary product weight: the left and right environments interact through the denominator, and the asymptotic factorization is itself part of the theorem rather than an initial independence assumption.


Existing phase-diagram results may yield stable fluctuations or a mixture at criticality, but do not yield this m−1 finite-size window, the exponentially tilted uniform cost law, and the corresponding scale mixture of spectrally negative stable fluctuations.

4. Split or not
Do not split the 30-page paper.
There is one mathematically clean cut, namely after result 2:


Condensation paper: results 1 and 2—sharp total-variation condensation for denominator-weighted continued fractions, exact context laws and rate, denominator factorization, and the corrected scalar coefficient.


Critical-renewal paper: results 3 and 4—the exact Fibonacci renewal transfer, critical-layer asymptotics, finite-size crossover, stable scale mixture, and the fixed-exponent regimes.


That split is nevertheless inferior.
The first paper would be a solid JNT paper. Result 1 is substantial enough to anchor it, and result 2 supplies a memorable arithmetic consequence. But it would recover almost exactly the pre-crossover valuation: a strong, specialized refinement paper rather than the present broader phase-transition paper.
The second paper would have the more ambitious theorem but a weakened foundation. It would either have to quote the exact denominator-layer asymptotic and context constant from the first paper, making its principal normalization look imported, or repeat enough of the condensation analysis to become artificially self-contained. The exact value 8, the critical correction, and the scale-mixture law are persuasive because the manuscript derives them in one chain from the two-sided continuant condensation. Separating that chain makes result 4 look more formal, not less.
The present architecture has a genuine progression:
sharp two-sided condensation⟶exact denominator tail⟶arithmetic renewal transfer⟶critical-window crossover.
That is one paper. Results 2 and 3 are not competing centers; they are the calibration and transfer mechanism connecting the two substantial theorems. At 30 pages, there is no length-based reason to sacrifice that coherence.
Final instruction for the cover letter: replace Transactions with Annales de l’Institut Henri Poincaré, Probabilités et Statistiques, lead with the finite-size crossover and sharp total-variation condensation, and describe the Dushistova correction as an exact arithmetic consequence rather than the headline.
