Abstract quote from the arXiv page:

```text
Let \Sigma_{g'}\to \Sigma_g be a cover of an orientable surface of genus g by an orientable surface of genus g', branched at n points, with Galois group H. Such a cover induces a virtual action of the mapping class group \text{Mod}_{g,n+1} of a genus g surface with n+1 marked points on H^1(\Sigma_{g'}, \mathbb{C}). When g is large in terms of the group H, we calculate precisely the connected monodromy group of this action. The methods are Hodge-theoretic and rely on a "generic Torelli theorem with coefficients."
```

Main theorem quote from the v2 TeX source, Section 1.1:

```text
Let H be a finite group and let \Sigma_{g',n'}\to \Sigma_{g,n} be an
H-cover associated to a surjection \varphi: \pi_1(\Sigma_{g,n},
x)\twoheadrightarrow H, where x is a base point of \Sigma_{g,n}. Let \overline{r} be the maximal dimension of an irreducible representation of H. Suppose that either
 1. n=0 and g\geq 2 \overline{r}+2, or
 2. n is arbitrary and g> \operatorname{max}(2\overline{r}+1, \overline{r}^2).
Let \operatorname{Mod}_\varphi be the stabilizer of \varphi inside \operatorname{Mod}_{g,n+1}.
Then, using notation as in (1.1), the identity component of the Zariski closure of the image of
R_\varphi: \operatorname{Mod}_{\varphi}\to \operatorname{Sp}(H^1(\Sigma_{g'}, \mathbb{C}))^H
is the commutator subgroup of \operatorname{Sp}(H^1(\Sigma_{g'}, \mathbb{C}))^H.
```

The same introductory remark gives the explicit finite-group specialization anchored here:

```text
Note that when H is abelian, \overline{r} = 1, and so it suffices to take g \geq 4 in the statement of Theorem 1.3.
If H is dihedral, then \overline{r}=2.
```

## Stage-1 Scope

The paper's main claims are abstract Zariski-closure and Hodge-theoretic monodromy statements. This Stage-1 checker does not verify those global theorems. It anchors a small explicit input from the statement of Theorem 1.3 and its following remark:

1. For finite abelian groups, every complex irreducible representation has dimension `1`, so `rbar = 1`.
2. Substituting `rbar = 1` into the theorem's two genus hypotheses gives `g >= 4` in both the unbranched and arbitrary-`n` cases.
3. As an auxiliary source-consistency check, small dihedral groups have irreducible representation dimensions bounded by `2`, matching the remark's `rbar = 2`.

This is a real but narrow anchor: it verifies the explicit finite-group character-degree arithmetic exposed in the abstract/introduction/main-theorem material, not the monodromy conclusion.

## Verifier Design

The verifier is pure Python plus SymPy 1.14.0. It independently constructs finite-group regular representation matrices for:

- cyclic products such as `C2`, `C3`, `C2 x C2`, `C2 x C3`, and `C4 x C2`,
- dihedral groups `D_3`, `D_4`, `D_5`, and `D_6`.

For each group it forms the conjugacy classes, builds the class-sum operators on the regular representation, and simultaneously diagonalizes the commutative algebra generated by those class sums. In the regular representation, an irreducible character of degree `d` contributes a joint eigenspace of dimension `d^2`, so each recovered eigenspace dimension must be a perfect square and its square root is the irreducible representation degree.

The PASS condition is the abelian specialization:

```text
rbar(abelian H) = 1
unbranched theorem bound: g >= 2*rbar + 2 = 4
arbitrary-n theorem bound: g > max(2*rbar + 1, rbar^2), hence integer g >= 4
```
