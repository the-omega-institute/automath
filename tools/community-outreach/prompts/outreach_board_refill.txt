# Selection criteria — every candidate MUST satisfy

1. **Real open problem** — appears in a public or inspectable venue (GitHub issue/PR/discussion, arXiv preprint, X thread, blog/forum, problem registry, workshop page, or another credible source). Not your own invention. Cite the source URL.
2. **Untouched by AI / no recent SOTA paper** — preference for problems with no current AI-generated partial result and no 2024-2026 paper that closes the natural attack surface. (`erdosproblems.com/<N>` "AI contributions" tab → empty is a strong signal.)
3. **High impact before source convenience** — prioritize influential named conjectures, public certificate/verifier gaps, high-visibility specialist discussions, classification/rigidity/extremal problems, or problems that could become a serious paper/public artifact. Do not chase a recent arXiv small question merely because it is recent.
4. **Solvable with our machinery eventually** — prefer problems where Omega/automath gives immediate leverage, but do not require an existing main-paper bridge up front. If a problem is genuinely interesting and plausibly solvable, propose it and name the bridge we would try to build later. Low current fit is acceptable only when the topic has exceptional academic impact.
5. **Doable size** — a single oracle / codex / claude run in 1-3 weeks could produce a publishable contribution (full proof, partial proof, or finite certificate). Not a 10-year program. Not trivial restatement.
6. **High publication value** — main structure / classification / rigidity / extremality / obstruction / impossibility theorem. Not "compute one more case of an existing pattern".
7. **Publishable terminal goal by default** — except for explicitly tracked collaboration/email lanes, the terminal artifact must be a paper, short note, public certificate/verifier package, serious forum/registry contribution, or other externally reviewable mathematical result. Do not propose a target whose natural end state is only a private email.
8. **Distinguishable from our 21 publication papers** — read PROGRAM_BOARD + MAIN_PAPER_INDEX; if a candidate is already someone-else's-side-of-our-own-paper, drop it.
9. **Clear final display form** — before proposing the candidate, decide what the first public artifact would be: registry/forum comment, author email, short note, arXiv follow-up, paper appendix, Lean-formalization handoff, or drop. If you cannot name a concrete artifact and audience, do not propose it.

# Anti-patterns — explicitly REJECT candidates that

- Are pure formalization of a published proof in Lean (we have a separate pipeline for that).
- Are already active collaborations / sent emails / tracked issue threads in `targeted_context_refresh.json`; those should be progressed under their existing task, not re-added to the board.
- Are "compute slightly larger case of <existing finite verification>".
- Are "recent arXiv paper asks a tiny follow-up question; maybe add a small table".
- Are low-impact author-email follow-ups where the likely artifact is only a private note to a non-influential author.
- Are non-collaboration targets whose only plausible endpoint is email rather than a publishable or public mathematical artifact.
- Are derivative tweaks inside another person's small paper rather than a result with independent mathematical weight.
- Are merely broad research directions, surveys, or "maybe Automath can help" ideas without a crisp theorem/counterexample/certificate target.
- Have low academic impact unless they are a clean certificate/theorem target that could become a short note.
- Live in research areas where Lean / our toolset has no leverage (e.g. analytic number theory hard analysis, smooth dynamical systems prerequisites we don't have).
- Have known closed-form / SOTA proofs in 2024-2026 papers that close the question.
- Read like a generic research direction rather than a specific implication.

# Output format

Your job is source discovery and mathematical judgment, not schema formatting.
Natural-language bullets or a compact table are fine. A local Codex adapter will
convert your response to the board JSON schema after you finish.

For each candidate, make these labels explicit enough for the adapter to read:

- `title`: short title, ≤80 chars
- `source_url`: canonical public URL
- `type`: DECIDABLE, EXISTENCE, CLASSIFICATION, EXTREMALITY, OBSTRUCTION, or RIGIDITY
- `statement`: one-sentence precise statement of what would be proved
- `untouched_evidence`: which AI-contribution / SOTA gap leaves this open
- `omega_fit_detail`: which Omega lemmas / structures plug in, with file paths if known
- `fit_score`: 0-10
- `topic_score`: 0-10
- `effort_estimate_days`: integer
- `risk_level`: low, med, or high
- `first_attack_step`: what the first prompt / script we'd write looks like
- `final_display_form`: concrete public/private artifact + audience, e.g. OPG comment + author email with reproducible certificate
- `success_gate`: what must be true before any external send, e.g. theorem statement + script output + operator-approved final markdown
- `rationale`: 2-3 sentences on why this is real, doable, and not already taken
