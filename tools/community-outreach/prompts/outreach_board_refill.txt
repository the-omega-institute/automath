# Selection criteria — every candidate MUST satisfy

1. **Real open problem** — appears in a public venue (erdosproblems.com, OPG, AimPL, recent arXiv preprint, Tao's blog, an active forum thread). Not your own invention. Cite the source URL.
2. **Untouched by AI / no recent SOTA paper** — preference for problems with no current AI-generated partial result and no 2024-2026 paper that closes the natural attack surface. (`erdosproblems.com/<N>` "AI contributions" tab → empty is a strong signal.)
3. **Fits our Omega toolset** — i.e. one of: ZMod / FiberRing arithmetic; finite-field equational theories; Folding / Window6; Sym²/Λ² spectral structure; signed companion matrices; Walsh-Stokes cubical cochain framing. Look at LEAN4_README to see what's actually formalized vs paper-only.
4. **Doable size** — a single oracle / codex / claude run in 1-3 weeks could produce a publishable contribution (full proof, partial proof, or finite certificate). Not a 10-year program. Not trivial restatement.
5. **High publication value** — main structure / classification / rigidity / extremality / obstruction / impossibility theorem. Not "compute one more case of an existing pattern".
6. **Distinguishable from our 21 publication papers** — read PROGRAM_BOARD + MAIN_PAPER_INDEX; if a candidate is already someone-else's-side-of-our-own-paper, drop it.

# Anti-patterns — explicitly REJECT candidates that

- Are pure formalization of a published proof in Lean (we have a separate pipeline for that).
- Are "compute slightly larger case of <existing finite verification>".
- Live in research areas where Lean / our toolset has no leverage (e.g. analytic number theory hard analysis, smooth dynamical systems prerequisites we don't have).
- Have known closed-form / SOTA proofs in 2024-2026 papers that close the question.
- Read like a generic research direction rather than a specific implication.
