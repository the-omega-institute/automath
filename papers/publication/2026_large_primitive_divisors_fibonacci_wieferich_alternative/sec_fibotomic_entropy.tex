\section{Fibotomic rank entropy}
\label{sec:fibotomic-entropy}

Let $F_0(x)=0$, $F_1(x)=1$, and
$F_{n+1}(x)=xF_n(x)+F_{n-1}(x)$ be the Fibonacci polynomials.  Their
fibotomic factors $\Psi_d(x)$ are defined by
\[
 F_n(x)=\prod_{d\mid n}\Psi_d(x).
\]
This factorization and the irreducibility of the fibotomic polynomials are
standard \cite{WebbParberry1969FibonacciPolynomials,
Levy2001FibonacciPolynomials}; a systematic later treatment is given in
\cite{CameronCountsLundMillerPiechnikWong2022Fibotomic}.

\begin{lemma}[Fibotomic integer and exact-rank radical]
\label{lem:fibotomic-radical}
For $d\geq3$, put
\[
 \mathfrak F_d:=\prod_{r\mid d}F_r^{\mu(d/r)}.
\]
Then
\[
 \mathfrak F_d=\Psi_d(1)\in\mathbb N,
 \qquad
 \prod_{p\in\Pi_\alpha(d)}p\mid\mathfrak F_d.
\]
For $d=3,5$, the exact-rank radicals are respectively $2$ and $5$; for
$d=6,12$, the exact-rank layer is empty.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Mobius inversion in the multiplicative group of nonzero rational functions
gives
\[
 \Psi_d(x)=\prod_{r\mid d}F_r(x)^{\mu(d/r)}.
\]
Evaluating at $x=1$ proves
$\mathfrak F_d=\Psi_d(1)$.  The fibotomic polynomial has positive integral
value at $1$, so this identity also proves integrality despite the negative
exponents in the Mobius product.

If $p\in\Pi_\alpha(d)$, then $p\mid F_d$ but $p\nmid F_r$ for every proper
divisor $r$ of $d$.  In the integral factorization
$F_d=\prod_{r\mid d}\Psi_r(1)$, divisibility of $\Psi_r(1)$ by $p$ for
$r<d$ would imply $p\mid F_r$.  Hence $p\mid\Psi_d(1)$.  Multiplying over
the distinct exact-rank primes proves the radical divisibility.  The four
small-rank assertions follow from $\alpha(2)=3$, $\alpha(5)=5$, and the
classical exceptional ranks $6$ and $12$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fibotomic rank entropy]
\label{thm:fibotomic-rank-entropy}
Let $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ and let $\phi$ denote Euler's totient function.
For $d\geq3$ and $k\geq0$, set $m=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ and
\[
 \mathcal H_d(k):=
 k\log\frac{2d}{3}+2\log(m!)+(k-2m)\log(m+1).
\]
There is an absolute constant $C_F$ such that, for every $d\geq3$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fibotomic-rank-entropy}
 \mathcal H_d(a(d))\leq\phi(d)\log\varphi+C_F.
\end{equation}
More generally, every finitely supported family of nonnegative real numbers
$(\lambda_d)_{d\geq3}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weighted-fibotomic-rank-entropy}
 \sum_{d\geq3}\lambda_d\mathcal H_d(a(d))
 \leq(\log\varphi)\sum_{d\geq3}\lambda_d\phi(d)
 +C_F\sum_{d\geq3}\lambda_d.
\end{equation}
Uniformly as $d\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pointwise-exact-rank-bound}
 a(d)\leq
 \left(\frac{\log\varphi}{2}+o(1)\right)
 \frac{\phi(d)}{\log d}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Binet's formula gives
\[
 F_r=\frac{\varphi^r}{\sqrt5}
 \bigl(1-(-\varphi^{-2})^r\bigr).
\]
The identities
$\sum_{r\mid d}\mu(d/r)=0$ and
$\sum_{r\mid d}r\mu(d/r)=\phi(d)$ therefore give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fibotomic-binet-error}
 \log\mathfrak F_d=\phi(d)\log\varphi+E_d,
 \qquad
 |E_d|\leq
 C_F:=\sum_{j\geq1}
 \left|\log\bigl(1-(-\varphi^{-2})^j\bigr)\right|.
\end{equation}
The series defining $C_F$ converges geometrically.

For $p\notin\{2,5\}$, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-lifting} gives
\[
 d=\alpha(p)\mid p-(5/p),
\]
so $p\equiv\pm1\pmod d$.  The ranks $3$ and $5$ have the single
exact-rank primes $2$ and $5$, and the ranks $6$ and $12$ have none; these
four cases satisfy the claimed bound directly.  For every remaining $d$,
write the exact-rank primes in increasing order as
$p_1<\cdots<p_k$.  There are at most two candidates in each pair of
residue classes $jd\pm1$, and hence
\[
 p_i\geq d\left\lceil\frac i2\right\rceil-1
 \geq\frac{2d}{3}\left\lceil\frac i2\right\rceil.
\]
With $m=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$, it follows that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rank-congruence-product-bound}
 \prod_{i=1}^kp_i\geq
 \left(\frac{2d}{3}\right)^k(m!)^2(m+1)^{k-2m}.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:fibotomic-radical},
\eqref{eq:fibotomic-binet-error}, and
\eqref{eq:rank-congruence-product-bound} prove
\eqref{eq:fibotomic-rank-entropy}.  Multiplication by
$\lambda_d\geq0$ and finite summation prove
\eqref{eq:weighted-fibotomic-rank-entropy}.

Stirling's lower bound applied to
\eqref{eq:fibotomic-rank-entropy} yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rank-entropy-coarse}
 a(d)\bigl(\log d+\log\max\{1,a(d)\}-O(1)\bigr)
 \leq\phi(d)\log\varphi+O(1).
\end{equation}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$.  If
$a(d)\leq\phi(d)/(\log d)^2$, then
\eqref{eq:pointwise-exact-rank-bound} follows immediately for all
sufficiently large $d$.  Otherwise the standard uniform lower bound
$\phi(d)\gg d/\log\log(3d)$
\cite{HardyWright2008Introduction}, together with
\eqref{eq:rank-entropy-coarse}, gives
\[
 \log a(d)=\log d+O(\log\log d).
\]
Substitution in \eqref{eq:rank-entropy-coarse} gives
\[
 (2+o(1))a(d)\log d\leq\phi(d)\log\varphi.
\]
All estimates hold beyond thresholds depending only on $\varepsilon$, so
the resulting $o(1)$ is uniform in $d$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:pointwise-exact-rank-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The upper-bound-sieve barrier]
\label{rem:sieve-barrier}
The factor $1/2$ in \eqref{eq:pointwise-exact-rank-bound} is not improved
by inserting the Brun--Titchmarsh theorem into the same argument.  We give
the calculation because it identifies the precise limitation.  Put
\[
 L_d:=\mathop{\rm lcm}(d,2).
\]
Then $\phi(L_d)=\phi(d)$.  If $p\in\Pi_\alpha(d)$ and $d\geq13$, then $p$
is odd and $d\mid p-(5/p)$.  Parity therefore gives
$p\equiv\pm1\pmod {L_d}$.  Write the exact-rank primes as
$p_1<\cdots<p_k$.  For $x>L_d$, Brun--Titchmarsh
\cite{MontgomeryVaughan1973LargeSieve} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:brun-titchmarsh-rank-primes}
 \#\{j:p_j\leq x\}
 \leq \pi(x;L_d,1)+\pi(x;L_d,-1)
 \leq \frac{4x}{\phi(d)\log(x/L_d)}
\end{equation}
On the other hand, the two
progressions contain at most $2x/L_d+2$ positive integers up to $x$.
Consequently $p_j/L_d\geq j/4$ for $j\geq4$.  Taking $x=p_j$ in
\eqref{eq:brun-titchmarsh-rank-primes} thus yields, for $j\geq5$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:brun-titchmarsh-ordered-rank-prime}
 p_j\geq \frac{j\phi(d)}4\log\frac{p_j}{L_d}
 \geq \frac{j\phi(d)}4\log\frac j4.
\end{equation}

Suppose first that $k\leq\phi(d)/(\log d)^2$.  This is already
$o(\phi(d)/\log d)$.  Otherwise set
$J=\lceil k/\log d\rceil$.  The standard uniform estimate
$\phi(d)\gg d/\log\log(3d)$ gives
\[
 J\gg \frac{d}{(\log d)^3\log\log(3d)},
\]
while \eqref{eq:rank-entropy-coarse} gives $k\ll\phi(d)/\log d$.
For $J\leq j\leq k$, it follows that
\[
 \log j=\log d+O(\log\log d),
 \qquad
 \log\phi(d)=\log d+O(\log\log\log(3d)).
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:brun-titchmarsh-ordered-rank-prime} therefore gives,
uniformly for all but $o(k)$ indices,
\[
 \log p_j\geq(2-o(1))\log d.
\]
Summing and using Lemma~\ref{lem:fibotomic-radical} recovers only
\[
 (2-o(1))k\log d
 \leq\sum_{j=1}^k\log p_j
 \leq\log\mathfrak F_d
 =(\log\varphi+o(1))\phi(d),
\]
which is exactly the leading constant in
\eqref{eq:pointwise-exact-rank-bound}.

At the critical scale $j\asymp\phi(d)/\log d$, the right side of
\eqref{eq:brun-titchmarsh-ordered-rank-prime} has size
$\phi(d)^2d^{o(1)}=d^{2+o(1)}$.  Thus the sieve removes a logarithmic
density of composite candidates, but after taking logarithms this changes
each contribution by only $o(\log d)$.  Improving the numerical constant
in Brun--Titchmarsh, or replacing it by an upper-bound sieve of the
conjecturally correct order, cannot change the coefficient $2$.

There is likewise no additional pointwise large-sieve input in the
condition $p\mid\mathfrak F_d$ for one fixed $d$: with only one integer of
controlled size, that condition supplies precisely the mass inequality
$\sum_{p\in\Pi_\alpha(d)}\log p\leq\log\mathfrak F_d$ already used above.
To prove
\[
 a(d)\leq(c+o(1))\frac{\phi(d)}{\log d},
 \qquad c<\frac{\log\varphi}{2},
\]
by a counting argument, one would need an additional estimate specific to
the divisibility condition $p\mid F_d$ (or to exact rank), strong enough to
force
\[
 \sum_{p\in\Pi_\alpha(d)}\log p
 \geq(2+\delta+o(1))a(d)\log d
\]
for some fixed $\delta>0$ in the critical range.  Equivalently, one must
force the geometric mean of the exact-rank primes onto the scale
$d^{2+\delta+o(1)}$.  Progression support, primality, distinctness, and the
size of the fibotomic factor, when used only through standard upper-bound
sieve estimates, do not provide such power-scale sparsity.
\end{remark}
