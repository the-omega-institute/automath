\begin{abstract}
Let $F_n$ be the $n$th Fibonacci number.  We prove a pointwise alternative
between two phenomena that are usually studied separately.  For every
$\varepsilon>0$ and every sufficiently large $n$, either $F_n$ has a
primitive prime divisor at least $n^{2-\varepsilon}$, or some primitive
prime divisor of $F_n$ occurs there with exponent at least two and is
therefore a Fibonacci--Wieferich prime.  This alternative yields the
unconditional bound
\[
 \max_{\substack{p\mid F_n\\p\nmid5F_1\cdots F_{n-1}}}
 p^{\nu_p(F_n)}\geq n^{2-o(1)}.
\]
Thus a single primitive primary component is near-quadratic at every large
index.  If there are only finitely many Fibonacci--Wieferich primes, then
the same lower bound holds for the largest primitive prime divisor itself.

The proof combines the integral fibotomic factor with the congruence
$p\equiv\pm1\pmod n$ for primes of exact apparition rank $n$.  Ordering
those primes gives a factorial lower bound for their radical and hence the
uniform estimate
\[
 \#\{p:\alpha(p)=n\}
 \leq\left(\frac{\log\varphi}{2}+o(1)\right)
 \frac{\phi(n)}{\log n}.
\]
The classical factorization of the primitive part then forces either a
near-quadratic prime base or exceptional rank lifting.
\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Fibonacci numbers, primitive divisors, rank of
apparition, fibotomic polynomials, Wall--Sun--Sun primes,
Fibonacci--Wieferich primes.
