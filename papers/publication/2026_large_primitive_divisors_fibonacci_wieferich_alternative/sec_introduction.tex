\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Write $F_0=0$, $F_1=1$, and $F_{n+1}=F_n+F_{n-1}$.  A prime $p$ is a
primitive prime divisor of $F_n$ if
\[
 p\mid F_n
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 p\nmid 5F_1F_2\cdots F_{n-1}.
\]
The factor $5$ enforces the standard discriminant-excluding convention for
the Fibonacci sequence.  Classical primitive-divisor theorems guarantee
such a prime for every $n\geq13$
\cite{Carmichael1913PrimitiveDivisors,Yabuta2001PrimitiveDivisorFibonacci,
BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas}.  They do not by themselves
force the prime to be large.

At the other extreme, a prime $p>5$ is called a Wall--Sun--Sun prime, or a
Fibonacci--Wieferich prime, when
\[
 p^2\mid F_{p-(5/p)},
\]
where $(5/p)$ is the Legendre symbol.  No such prime is known.  The lifting
condition is equivalent to the first occurrence of $p$ in the Fibonacci
sequence already having multiplicity at least two; see
\cite{Klaska2018WallConjecture} and Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-lifting}.

The main result shows that these two difficulties are complementary at each
sufficiently large index.  Given $\varepsilon>0$, every sufficiently large
$F_n$ has either a primitive prime divisor $p\geq n^{2-\varepsilon}$ or a
primitive Fibonacci--Wieferich divisor.  Thus the absence of exceptional
lifting would force a near-quadratic lower bound at every large index, not
merely on a positive-density subsequence.
The threshold is effective; Theorem~
\ref{thm:large-primitive-wieferich-alternative} gives a deliberately
conservative closed choice in terms of $\varepsilon$.

The alternative also has an unconditional consequence at the level of
primary components.  If the exceptional branch supplies a primitive prime
$q$ with $q^2\mid F_n$, then $n=\alpha(q)$ divides $q-(5/q)$, and hence
$q\geq n-1$.  It follows that, at every sufficiently large index, some
primitive primary component $p^{\nu_p(F_n)}$ is at least $n^{2-o(1)}$.
Moreover, finiteness, rather than nonexistence, of the
Fibonacci--Wieferich primes is enough to recover the corresponding
near-quadratic bound for the prime base.

The mechanism is the fibotomic integer
\[
 \mathfrak F_n=\prod_{d\mid n}F_d^{\mu(n/d)}.
\]
Its logarithm is $\phi(n)\log\varphi+O(1)$, while, for $n\geq13$, every
exact-rank prime lies in one of the two residue classes $\pm1$ modulo $n$.  If there are
$a(n)$ such primes, their ordered product is therefore bounded below by a
factorial expression.  Comparing the two estimates yields
\[
 a(n)\leq
 \left(\frac{\log\varphi}{2}+o(1)\right)
 \frac{\phi(n)}{\log n}.
\]
Granville's Lucas cyclotomic-factor theorem then identifies the primitive
part of $F_n$ inside $\mathfrak F_n$, with at most one nonprimitive prime
factor coming from $n$ \cite{Granville2012PrimitivePrimeFactors}.  If every
primitive divisor occurs to exponent one, their total logarithmic mass and
their number force the largest one to have logarithm at least
$(2-o(1))\log n$.

The constant in this argument is sharp relative to three deliberately
weakened axioms.  Theorem~\ref{thm:sieve-mass-barrier} formalizes the proof
class consisting of (i) progression support $x\equiv\pm1\pmod{L_n}$, (ii) the
pointwise upper-counting bound, for $x\geq e^2L_n$,
$N_n(x)\leq\kappa_n x/(\phi(n)\log(x/L_n))$ with $\kappa_n\geq1$ and
$\log\kappa_n=o(\log n)$, and (iii) the fibotomic mass bound
$\sum_{x\in\mathcal P_n}\log x\leq(\log\varphi)\phi(n)+C_F$.  It constructs
admissible configurations with every cardinality below the coefficient
$\log\varphi/2$, so no smaller coefficient follows from axioms (i)--(iii)
alone.  This is a relative sharpness result for the specified three-axiom
proof class; crossing the coefficient requires information not encoded in
those axioms, such as divisibility-specific input.

The rank-of-apparition and prime-power formulas used here also underlie a
companion treatment of minimal exact fibers of the Fibonacci rank map
\cite{MaZhang2026MinimalPreimages}.  All inputs needed for the present
argument are stated and justified below; no result is imported silently
from that manuscript.

Section~\ref{sec:rank-lifting} records the rank and lifting facts.
Section~\ref{sec:fibotomic-entropy} proves the fibotomic rank-entropy
estimate and identifies its upper-bound-sieve barrier.
Section~\ref{sec:primitive-alternative} proves the main
alternative and its unconditional primary-component consequence, and
Section~\ref{sec:comparison} compares these conclusions with known
large-divisor results and states their limitations.
