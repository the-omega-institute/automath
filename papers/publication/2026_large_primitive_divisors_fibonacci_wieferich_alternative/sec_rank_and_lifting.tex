\section{Ranks of apparition and prime-power lifting}
\label{sec:rank-lifting}

For a positive integer $m$, let
\[
 \alpha(m):=\min\{r\geq1:m\mid F_r\}
\]
be its rank of apparition.  For $d\geq1$, put
\[
 \Pi_\alpha(d):=\{p\text{ prime}:\alpha(p)=d\},
 \qquad a(d):=\#\Pi_\alpha(d).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Rank congruence and lifting]
\label{prop:rank-lifting}
Let $p\notin\{2,5\}$ be prime, put $z_p=\alpha(p)$, and set
$h_p=\nu_p(F_{z_p})$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $p\mid F_m$ if and only if $z_p\mid m$;
\item $z_p\mid p-(5/p)$, and consequently $p\nmid z_p$;
\item for every $u\geq1$,
\[
 \nu_p(F_{z_pu})=h_p+\nu_p(u);
\]
\item $p^2\mid F_{z_p}$ if and only if
$p^2\mid F_{p-(5/p)}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the classical rank-divisibility theorem, and the
second is the classical rank congruence
\cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm,Vinson1963RankApparition}.  Since
$p\nmid p-(5/p)$, the divisibility in (ii) also gives $p\nmid z_p$.
The valuation formula in (iii) is the standard Fibonacci lifting law
\cite[Theorems~1 and~2]{Lengyel1995OrderFibonacciLucas}; see also
\cite{MedinaRowland2015PRegularity}.

For (iv), write $p-(5/p)=z_pu$.  Part (ii) implies $p\nmid u$.
Part (iii) therefore gives
\[
 \nu_p\bigl(F_{p-(5/p)}\bigr)
 =h_p+\nu_p(u)=h_p=\nu_p(F_{z_p}),
\]
which proves the equivalence.
\end{proof}

For $n\geq13$, an exact-rank prime of rank $n$ is the same thing as a
primitive prime divisor of $F_n$ in the discriminant-excluding convention.
Indeed, exact rank excludes every earlier Fibonacci number, and the only
prime excluded solely by the discriminant is $5$, whose rank is $5$.

\begin{theorem}[Classical exact-rank existence]
\label{thm:exact-rank-existence}
For every $n\geq3$ with $n\notin\{6,12\}$, there is a prime $p$ such that
$\alpha(p)=n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $n=3,4,5$, take $p=2,3,5$, respectively.  For $n\geq7$ with
$n\neq12$, the primitive-divisor theorem for the Fibonacci sequence gives
a prime dividing $F_n$ and no earlier Fibonacci number
\cite{Carmichael1913PrimitiveDivisors,Yabuta2001PrimitiveDivisorFibonacci,
BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas}.  Its rank of apparition is
therefore $n$.
\end{proof}
