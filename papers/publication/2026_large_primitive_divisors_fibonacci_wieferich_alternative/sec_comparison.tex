\section{Comparison and limitations}
\label{sec:comparison}

Stewart proved an unconditional lower bound
\[
 n\exp\!\left(\frac{\log n}{104\log\log n}\right)
\]
for the greatest prime factor of the relevant Lucas cyclotomic factor,
beyond an effective sequence-dependent threshold
\cite[Theorem~1]{Stewart2013DivisorsLucasLehmer}.  Hong gives an explicit
Fibonacci formulation: for every fixed positive integer $\kappa$, each
sufficiently large $F_n$ has a primitive divisor outside
$n\pm1,\ldots,\kappa n\pm1$, and hence one at least
$(\kappa+1)n-1$ \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Hong2025BigPrimitiveDivisors}.  The
exponent $2-o(1)$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:large-primitive-wieferich-alternative}
is stronger, but it is conditional on excluding exceptional lifting at the
index under consideration.

In Kiss's 1988 chapter, Theorem~2 fixes a nondegenerate Lucas sequence and
$0<\lambda<1$, and proves that a positive proportion of indices $n\leq x$
have a primitive prime-power factor exceeding $n^{2-\lambda}$, for all
sufficiently large $x$ \cite[Theorem~2]{Kiss1988PrimitiveDivisors}.  This is
a positive-density statement for the full prime power, not a pointwise lower
bound for its prime base; Kiss explicitly notes that the argument does not
decide whether the bases or the exponents are large.  Theorem~
\ref{thm:large-primitive-wieferich-alternative} instead treats every
sufficiently large rank and separates base-prime size from multiplicity at
that same rank.  Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-primary-component} then gives,
at every sufficiently large rank, a single primitive primary component at
least $n^{2-o(1)}$; unlike the positive-density result, this conclusion is
unconditional.

An earlier result of Kiss gives a different bridge to Wieferich-type primes:
if, for a fixed $0<\delta<1$, the greatest primitive divisor of a Lucas term
is below $n^{1+\delta}$ for almost all indices, then a positive-density set
of indices has a term divisible by a Wieferich-type prime
\cite{Kiss1987WieferichLucas}.  That result is neither pointwise nor an
exact-rank alternative.  The bibliographic metadata for this 1987 paper has
not been independently confirmed in the available indexes; the exact search
and access limitation are recorded in the literature audit accompanying this
article.

The results do not decide whether Fibonacci--Wieferich primes exist.  They
also do not give an unconditional near-quadratic primitive prime base: in
the exceptional branch, the near-quadratic object is the primary component
$q^{\nu_q(F_n)}$, which may have the smaller base $q\geq n-1$.  Finiteness
of the exceptional primes is sufficient for the prime-base conclusion, but
is itself unknown.  Theorem~\ref{thm:sieve-mass-barrier} also shows why a
standard upper-bound sieve, when used only through axiom~\textup{(ii)}, does
not improve the coefficient in the rank-cardinality estimate: it controls the
ambient primes in the progressions, whereas an improvement from this
three-axiom scheme requires information not encoded in axioms
\textup{(i)--(iii)}, such as divisibility-specific input.  Finally, the
literature searches behind the comparison
are evidence about the checked corpora, not a proof of global priority.

A computational exclusion of Wall--Sun--Sun primes below a prime bound
$B$ does not by itself exclude the exceptional branch for indices $n<B$.
Indeed, $\alpha(q)=n$ gives $n\leq q+1$, which is a lower bound on the
possible exceptional prime rather than an upper bound.  Such a computation
does exclude the branch when $F_n<B$, because then every prime divisor of
$F_n$ is below $B$; this is only a finite small-index range and does not
overlap the asymptotic range supplied by the conservative explicit
threshold \eqref{eq:explicit-alternative-threshold}.
