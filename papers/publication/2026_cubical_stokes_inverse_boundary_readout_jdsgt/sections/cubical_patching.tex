\subsection{Exact patching profile on a sink-connected finite cubical complex}

The one-cell profile does not in general patch by assigning an extremizer to
each cell. In the conservative dual-network setting used below, a shared face
carries one cochain value with opposite incidence signs in its two adjacent
cells. The global problem is then a capacitated flow problem on the dual
graph. The norm minimization itself is the standard anisotropic
max-flow/min-cut, or graph-calibrability, problem; see
\cite{Nozawa1990,MazonSoleraToledo2020}. The paper-specific issue is the exact
near-saturation profile on a prescribed Cheeger cut. The following theorem
computes that profile and isolates the compatibility condition that is absent
on one cell.

Let $\mathcal K$ be a finite pure cubical complex of dimension $k$.  Its
oriented $k$-cells are denoted by $\mathcal C$, and its oriented
codimension-one faces by $\mathcal E$. Write $a_e>0$ for the $(k-1)$-volume
of $e$ and $v_C>0$ for the volume of $C$. Let $B$ be the cell-face incidence
matrix, so that
\[
(Bf)_C=\sum_{e\subset\partial C}\operatorname{inc}(C,e)f_e.
\]
Assume that, after adjoining one exterior sink vertex $\ast$, $B$ is the
reduced signed incidence matrix of the augmented dual network $\widehat G$:
every internal face column has one $+1$ and one $-1$, every boundary-face
column has one nonzero entry, and every cell vertex is connected to $\ast$.
Thus $\widehat G$ has vertex set $\mathcal C\cup\{\ast\}$ and one edge for
each face: an internal face joins its two incident cells, while a boundary
face joins its incident cell to $\ast$. In this subsection, ``connected
component'' means a connected component of $\widehat G$ (and a component of
the cell-only dual adjacency graph is named explicitly when needed).

For $S\subset\mathcal C$, let $\delta S$ be the set of faces incident with
exactly one cell of $S$, and put
\[
 v(S):=\sum_{C\in S}v_C,
 \qquad
 a(\delta S):=\sum_{e\in\delta S}a_e,
 \qquad
 h_{\mathcal K}:=
 \max_{\varnothing\ne S\subset\mathcal C}
 \frac{v(S)}{a(\delta S)}.
\]
The maximum is taken over proper or non-proper subsets; the exterior vertex
$\ast$ is the sink in the augmented dual network. For a face $e\in\delta S$, let
 $s_S(e)\in\{\pm1\}$ be the sign for which $s_S(e)f_e$ is the flux directed
 out of $S$.  Finally, set
\[
 \|f\|_{\mathcal K,a,\infty}:=
 \max_{e\in\mathcal E}\frac{|f_e|}{a_e},
 \qquad
 \mathcal F_M:=\{f\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal E}:Bf=v,\ |f_e|\le Ma_e\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact compatible patching profile on a sink-connected dual network]
\label{thm:exact-cubical-patching}
Let $\mathcal K$ be a finite pure $k$-dimensional cubical complex with the
positive weights $a_e$ and $v_C$ above. Assume that, after adjoining one
exterior sink vertex, its cell-face matrix $B$ is the reduced incidence matrix
of the augmented dual graph: every internal face column has one $+1$ and one
$-1$, every boundary-face column has one nonzero incidence, and every cell
vertex is connected to the sink. Let $Q\subset\mathcal C$ attain the maximum
defining $h_{\mathcal K}$. Then:

\begin{enumerate}
\item
\[
 \min\{\|f\|_{\mathcal K,a,\infty}:Bf=v\}=h_{\mathcal K},
 \qquad
 \mathcal F_{h_{\mathcal K}}\ne\varnothing.
\]
Every $f\in\mathcal F_{h_{\mathcal K}}$ saturates the $Q$-cut outward:
\[
 s_Q(e)f_e=h_{\mathcal K}a_e
 \qquad(e\in\delta Q).
\]

\item If $M=h_{\mathcal K}+\delta$ with $\delta\ge0$, then every
 $f\in\mathcal F_M$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patched-deficit-identity}
 \sum_{e\in\delta Q}a_e
 \left(M-s_Q(e)\frac{f_e}{a_e}\right)
 =a(\delta Q)(M-h_{\mathcal K})
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patched-atomic-upper-bound}
 \sum_{e\in\delta Q}a_e
 \left|\frac{f_e}{a_e}-s_Q(e)h_{\mathcal K}\right|
 \le
 \Phi_Q(\delta),
\end{equation}
where
\[
 \Phi_Q(\delta):=2\max_{T\subset\delta Q}
 \min\left\{
 \delta\bigl(a(\delta Q)-a(T)\bigr),
 \bigl(2h_{\mathcal K}+\delta\bigr)a(T)
 \right\}.
\]

\item The exact compatible profile is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patched-exact-profile}
 \Psi_Q(\delta):=
 \max_{f\in\mathcal F_{h_{\mathcal K}+\delta}}
 \sum_{e\in\delta Q}a_e
 \left|\frac{f_e}{a_e}-s_Q(e)h_{\mathcal K}\right|
 =2\max_{T\subset\delta Q}\Gamma_T(h_{\mathcal K}+\delta),
\end{equation}
where, with $c_T(e):=s_Q(e)$ for $e\in T$ and $c_T(e):=0$ otherwise,
\[
 \Gamma_T(M):=
 \min_{p\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal C}}
 \left\{
 h_{\mathcal K}a(T)-\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}v_Cp_C
 +M\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}a_e
 \left|c_T(e)-(B^Tp)_e\right|
 \right\}.
\]
In particular, $\Psi_Q(\delta)\le\Phi_Q(\delta)$, and equality holds if and
only if at least one maximizing set $T$ in the definition of $\Phi_Q(\delta)$
has an atomic equality vector whose boundary flux extends to an element of
$\mathcal F_{h_{\mathcal K}+\delta}$.

More explicitly, put $A=a(\delta Q)$ and, for every nonempty proper $T$, set
\[
 \tau_T:=\min\{\delta(A-a(T)),(2h_{\mathcal K}+\delta)a(T)\},
\]
and let $\mathcal A_T(\delta)$ be the set of vectors
$q=(q_e)_{e\in\delta Q}$ satisfying
\[
 -2h_{\mathcal K}-\delta\le q_e\le0\quad(e\in T),
 \qquad
 0\le q_e\le\delta\quad(e\notin T),
\]
\[
 -\sum_{e\in T}a_e q_e
 =\sum_{e\notin T}a_e q_e
 =\tau_T.
\]
For $q\in\mathcal A_T(\delta)$, define the outward data
$\widehat g_e:=a_e(h_{\mathcal K}+q_e)$.  The extension condition is
equivalent to the finite family of cut inequalities
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patched-extension-cuts}
 \left|v(U)-\sum_{e\in\delta Q\cap\delta U}
 s_U(e)s_Q(e)\widehat g_e\right|
 \le
 (h_{\mathcal K}+\delta)
 \sum_{e\in\delta U\setminus\delta Q}a_e
 \qquad(U\subset\mathcal C).
\end{equation}
Thus the one-cell profile patches exactly when its extremizing
atomic boundary data satisfy all internal cut capacities.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the reduced-incidence hypothesis, each face is an edge of the augmented
dual network $\widehat G$, with the exterior vertex $\ast$ as a sink. The
sink-connectivity hypothesis makes every nonempty cell cut have a positive
boundary capacity and makes the demand vector feasible for sufficiently large
capacities. The finite anisotropic max-flow/min-cut theorem therefore gives
\[
 \min\{M:\mathcal F_M\ne\varnothing\}
 =\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subset\mathcal C}
 \frac{v(S)}{a(\delta S)}=h_{\mathcal K}.
\]
This is the standard capacitated-flow formulation of the anisotropic network
theorem. For completeness, the equality case needed here follows directly
from complementary slackness. If $Q$ attains the displayed maximum and
$f\in\mathcal F_{h_{\mathcal K}}$, summing $Bf=v$ over $Q$ and using the
opposite-incidence entries of every internal face gives
\[
 v(Q)=\sum_{e\in\delta Q}s_Q(e)f_e
 \le h_{\mathcal K}a(\delta Q)=v(Q).
\]
Every summand in the difference is nonnegative, so all of them vanish.  This
proves (1) and the saturation assertion.



For $M=h_{\mathcal K}+\delta$, define
\[
 q_e:=s_Q(e)\frac{f_e}{a_e}-h_{\mathcal K}
 \qquad(e\in\delta Q).
\]
Summing the cell equations over $Q$ again uses the reduced-incidence
condition: all faces internal to $Q$ have one $+1$ and one $-1$ entry and
cancel, leaving only $\delta Q$. Thus, using
$v(Q)=h_{\mathcal K}a(\delta Q)$ gives
$\sum_{e\in\delta Q}a_e q_e=0$.
The capacity bound gives
\[
 -2h_{\mathcal K}-\delta\le q_e\le\delta.
\]
The left side of \eqref{eq:patched-atomic-upper-bound} is
$\sum a_e|q_e|$.  If $T=\{e:q_e<0\}$ and
$\tau=\sum_{q_e>0}a_e q_e$, then
\[
 \tau\le\delta(A-a(T)),
 \qquad
 \tau\le(2h_{\mathcal K}+\delta)a(T),
 \qquad
 \sum a_e|q_e|=2\tau.
\]
Taking the maximum over $T$ proves (2), including the deficit identity.

For (3), the zero-mean identity also gives, for every fixed $f$,
\[
 \sum_{e\in\delta Q}a_e|q_e|
 =2\max_{T\subset\delta Q}
 \left(h_{\mathcal K}a(T)-\sum_{e\in T}s_Q(e)f_e\right).
\]
The maximum over $f$ and the finite maximum over $T$ commute.  For fixed $T$,
strong linear-programming duality applied to
\[
 \min\left\{\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}c_T(e)f_e:
 Bf=v,\ |f_e|\le Ma_e\right\}
\]
gives
\[
 \max_{p\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal C}}
 \left\{\sum_Cv_Cp_C
 -M\sum_ea_e|c_T(e)-(B^Tp)_e|\right\}.
\]
Substitution is exactly \eqref{eq:patched-exact-profile}.  The vectors in
$\mathcal A_T(\delta)$ are precisely the equality vectors in the two-cap
estimate for the fixed sign set $T$, so the final assertion is equivalent to
the extendibility of at least one such vector.

Finally, after fixing the oriented values
$s_Q(e)\widehat g_e$ on $\delta Q$, the remaining unknown
face fluxes have capacities $(h_{\mathcal K}+\delta)a_e$ and residual cell
demands given by the left side of \eqref{eq:patched-extension-cuts}.  The
Hoffman circulation criterion (equivalently, max-flow/min-cut with the
augmented sink $\ast$)
states that these demands are feasible exactly when all the cut inequalities
\eqref{eq:patched-extension-cuts} hold.  This proves the compatibility
criterion and completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Geometric scope and necessity of the network hypotheses]
The algebraic hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-cubical-patching} hold if
$\mathcal K$ is coherently oriented and pure, every codimension-one face is
incident with one or two top cells, and every component of the cell-only dual
adjacency graph has nonempty boundary. After adjoining the exterior vertex,
each such component is connected to the sink. For a cubical pseudomanifold,
this amounts to orientability with a coherent orientation and no closed
component. The theorem's ``connected component'' is, by definition, a
component of the augmented network $\widehat G$ introduced above.

Both counterexamples below show that the two network hypotheses are necessary
for a theorem uniform over positive $a_e$ and $v_C$. They are two forms of the
same obstruction: without reduced incidence, internal fluxes do not conserve;
without a sink-connected component, positive total source cannot leave the
component.

For $k=1$ let $\mathcal K$ consist of two unit intervals $C_1,C_2$ meeting at a
vertex $e_1$, with outer vertices $e_0,e_2$, oriented so that
$\partial C_1=e_1-e_0$ and $\partial C_2=e_1-e_2$; take $a_e=1$ and
$v_{C_1}=v_{C_2}=1$. Then $h_{\mathcal K}=1$, attained by
$Q=\{C_1,C_2\}$, yet $f=(-\tfrac12,\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)$ satisfies $Bf=v$
with $\|f\|_{\mathcal K,a,\infty}=\tfrac12<h_{\mathcal K}$. The column of
$e_1$ is $(+1,+1)$ rather than $(+1,-1)$, so summing the cell equations
produces the doubled internal contribution $2f_{e_1}$ instead of cancellation.
This violates the reduced-incidence hypothesis.

For the boundary of a $3$-cube, viewed as a pure $2$-complex, every edge lies
in two cells and the local incidences can be opposite, but there is no exterior
face. Hence $\sum_C(Bf)_C=0$ for all $f$ while $\sum_Cv_C>0$, so $Bf=v$ has
no solution at all; for the full cell set $\delta S=\varnothing$ and the
ratio defining $h_{\mathcal K}$ has zero denominator. This violates sink
connectivity and records the corresponding infeasibility for a closed
component carrying positive total source.
\end{remark}

The distinction between $\Phi_Q$ and $\Psi_Q$ is genuine. The two-by-two
rectangular subdivision below is coherently oriented, has one or two cells per
codimension-one face, and every cell-only dual component meets the exterior;
it therefore lies inside the repaired theorem's domain. Take horizontal cell
widths
$1,2$, vertical cell heights $2,7$, and let every cell source equal its area.
The total area and outer anisotropic perimeter are $27$ and $24$, so
$h_{\mathcal K}=9/8$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:box-self-cheeger}, applied to every
cell subpatch.  At $\delta=9/4$ one has $M=27/8$ and
$\Phi_Q(\delta)=72$: the maximizing boundary subsets have total weight $8$.
Label the left and bottom boundary faces of $C_{00}$ by $e_0,e_1$, the left
and top faces of $C_{01}$ by $e_2,e_3$, the right and bottom faces of
$C_{10}$ by $e_4,e_5$, and the right and top faces of $C_{11}$ by $e_6,e_7$.
Their weights are
\[
(a_{e_0},\ldots,a_{e_7})=(2,1,7,1,2,2,7,2).
\]
For a two-level extremizer at this deficit, the negative subset must have
weight $8$ and the corresponding outward boundary fluxes are $-M$ on that
subset and $+M$ on its complement.  The nine possible negative subsets and a
violating cell cut in \eqref{eq:patched-extension-cuts} are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
T & \text{violating }U\\ \hline
\{1,2\},\{2,3\}&\{C_{01}\}\\
\{0,1,3,4,5\},\{0,1,3,4,7\},\{0,1,3,5,7\}&\{C_{00}\}\\
\{1,6\},\{3,6\}&\{C_{00},C_{10}\}\\
\{0,4,5,7\},\{1,3,4,5,7\}&\{C_{10}\}.
\end{array}
\]
For $T=\{1,2\}$ and $T=\{2,3\}$ the residual-demand/capacity pairs are
$(109/4,27)$ and $(34,27)$.  The three sets in the second row give
$(97/8,81/8)$; the two sets in the third row give $(87/8,81/8)$ and
$(141/8,81/8)$; and the two sets in the last row give $(35/2,27/2)$.
Thus every entry violates \eqref{eq:patched-extension-cuts}.  Subsets of
weight at most $7$ or at least $9$ cannot attain
$72$ by the two-cap bound.  Since $\mathcal F_{27/8}$ is compact and the trace
error is continuous, the failure of every equality vector implies
\[
 \Psi_Q(9/4)<72=\Phi_Q(9/4).
\]
Interface compatibility therefore imposes a strict loss relative to the
one-cell profile, even though the exact compatible value is given by
\eqref{eq:patched-exact-profile}.

The Hoffman interior cut criterion is precisely the finite-dimensional form of
the bounded divergence-free trace-extension question treated in the companion
manuscript \emph{Sharp coefficient bounds for the centered homotopy operator
and quantitative boundary readout on boxes}.  There, microstructured
divergence-free trace extensions show that, for $k\ge2$, the continuous
trace-stability constant is $\mathsf C_{\rm tr}(R)=2P_1(R)$: by refining the
inward-flux set below every fixed cell scale, the tangential primitive tends to
zero and arbitrarily fine boundary layers evade the finite aggregation
obstruction.  Thus the strict inequality
$\Psi_Q(9/4)<72=\Phi_Q(9/4)$ is sharp for the fixed cell decomposition
exhibited here, but does not imply any loss in the continuous constant.
