\subsection{Exact patching profile on a finite cubical complex}

The one-cell profile does not in general patch by assigning an extremizer to
each cell.  A shared face carries one cochain value, whereas its incidence
signs in two adjacent cells are opposite.  The correct global problem is a
capacitated flow problem on the dual graph.  The norm minimization itself is
the standard anisotropic max-flow/min-cut, or graph-calibrability, problem;
see \cite{Nozawa1990,MazonSoleraToledo2020}.  The new issue here is the exact
near-saturation profile on a prescribed Cheeger cut.  The following theorem
computes that profile and isolates the compatibility condition that is absent
on one cell.

Let $\mathcal K$ be a finite pure cubical complex of dimension $k$.  Its
oriented $k$-cells are denoted by $\mathcal C$, and its oriented
codimension-one faces by $\mathcal E$.  A face is incident with one or two
cells.  Write $a_e>0$ for the $(k-1)$-volume of $e$ and $v_C>0$ for the volume
of $C$.  Let $B$ be the cell-face incidence matrix, so that
\[
(Bf)_C=\sum_{e\subset\partial C}\operatorname{inc}(C,e)f_e.
\]
For $S\subset\mathcal C$, let $\delta S$ be the set of faces incident with
exactly one cell of $S$, and put
\[
 v(S):=\sum_{C\in S}v_C,
 \qquad
 a(\delta S):=\sum_{e\in\delta S}a_e,
 \qquad
 h_{\mathcal K}:=
 \max_{\varnothing\ne S\subset\mathcal C}
 \frac{v(S)}{a(\delta S)}.
\]
The maximum is taken over proper or non-proper subsets; the exterior of the
complex is the sink in the dual graph.  For a face $e\in\delta S$, let
 $s_S(e)\in\{\pm1\}$ be the sign for which $s_S(e)f_e$ is the flux directed
 out of $S$.  Finally, set
\[
 \|f\|_{\mathcal K,a,\infty}:=
 \max_{e\in\mathcal E}\frac{|f_e|}{a_e},
 \qquad
 \mathcal F_M:=\{f\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal E}:Bf=v,\ |f_e|\le Ma_e\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact compatible patching profile]
\label{thm:exact-cubical-patching}
Let $\mathcal K$, $B$, $a_e$, $v_C$, and $h_{\mathcal K}$ be as above, and
let $Q\subset\mathcal C$ attain the maximum defining $h_{\mathcal K}$.  Then:

\begin{enumerate}
\item
\[
 \min\{\|f\|_{\mathcal K,a,\infty}:Bf=v\}=h_{\mathcal K},
 \qquad
 \mathcal F_{h_{\mathcal K}}\ne\varnothing.
\]
Every $f\in\mathcal F_{h_{\mathcal K}}$ saturates the $Q$-cut outward:
\[
 s_Q(e)f_e=h_{\mathcal K}a_e
 \qquad(e\in\delta Q).
\]

\item If $M=h_{\mathcal K}+\delta$ with $\delta\ge0$, then every
 $f\in\mathcal F_M$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patched-deficit-identity}
 \sum_{e\in\delta Q}a_e
 \left(M-s_Q(e)\frac{f_e}{a_e}\right)
 =a(\delta Q)(M-h_{\mathcal K})
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patched-atomic-upper-bound}
 \sum_{e\in\delta Q}a_e
 \left|\frac{f_e}{a_e}-s_Q(e)h_{\mathcal K}\right|
 \le
 \Phi_Q(\delta),
\end{equation}
where
\[
 \Phi_Q(\delta):=2\max_{T\subset\delta Q}
 \min\left\{
 \delta\bigl(a(\delta Q)-a(T)\bigr),
 \bigl(2h_{\mathcal K}+\delta\bigr)a(T)
 \right\}.
\]

\item The exact compatible profile is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patched-exact-profile}
 \Psi_Q(\delta):=
 \max_{f\in\mathcal F_{h_{\mathcal K}+\delta}}
 \sum_{e\in\delta Q}a_e
 \left|\frac{f_e}{a_e}-s_Q(e)h_{\mathcal K}\right|
 =2\max_{T\subset\delta Q}\Gamma_T(h_{\mathcal K}+\delta),
\end{equation}
where, with $c_T(e):=s_Q(e)$ for $e\in T$ and $c_T(e):=0$ otherwise,
\[
 \Gamma_T(M):=
 \min_{p\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal C}}
 \left\{
 h_{\mathcal K}a(T)-\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}v_Cp_C
 +M\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}a_e
 \left|c_T(e)-(B^Tp)_e\right|
 \right\}.
\]
In particular, $\Psi_Q(\delta)\le\Phi_Q(\delta)$, and equality holds if and
only if at least one maximizing set $T$ in the definition of $\Phi_Q(\delta)$
has an atomic equality vector whose boundary flux extends to an element of
$\mathcal F_{h_{\mathcal K}+\delta}$.

More explicitly, put $A=a(\delta Q)$ and, for every nonempty proper $T$, set
\[
 \tau_T:=\min\{\delta(A-a(T)),(2h_{\mathcal K}+\delta)a(T)\},
\]
and let $\mathcal A_T(\delta)$ be the set of vectors
$q=(q_e)_{e\in\delta Q}$ satisfying
\[
 -2h_{\mathcal K}-\delta\le q_e\le0\quad(e\in T),
 \qquad
 0\le q_e\le\delta\quad(e\notin T),
\]
\[
 -\sum_{e\in T}a_e q_e
 =\sum_{e\notin T}a_e q_e
 =\tau_T.
\]
For $q\in\mathcal A_T(\delta)$, define the outward data
$\widehat g_e:=a_e(h_{\mathcal K}+q_e)$.  The extension condition is
equivalent to the finite family of cut inequalities
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patched-extension-cuts}
 \left|v(U)-\sum_{e\in\delta Q\cap\delta U}
 s_U(e)s_Q(e)\widehat g_e\right|
 \le
 (h_{\mathcal K}+\delta)
 \sum_{e\in\delta U\setminus\delta Q}a_e
 \qquad(U\subset\mathcal C).
\end{equation}
Thus the one-cell profile patches exactly when its extremizing
atomic boundary data satisfy all internal cut capacities.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Regard each face as an edge of the dual graph, with the exterior as a sink.
The finite anisotropic max-flow/min-cut theorem gives
\[
 \min\{M:\mathcal F_M\ne\varnothing\}
 =\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subset\mathcal C}
 \frac{v(S)}{a(\delta S)}=h_{\mathcal K}.
\]
This is the standard capacitated-flow formulation of the anisotropic network
theorem; the cell-face incidence matrix is its signed incidence matrix.
For completeness, the equality case needed here follows directly from
complementary slackness.  If $Q$ attains the displayed maximum and
$f\in\mathcal F_{h_{\mathcal K}}$, summing $Bf=v$ over $Q$ gives
\[
 v(Q)=\sum_{e\in\delta Q}s_Q(e)f_e
 \le h_{\mathcal K}a(\delta Q)=v(Q).
\]
Every summand in the difference is nonnegative, so all of them vanish.  This
proves (1) and the saturation assertion.

For $M=h_{\mathcal K}+\delta$, define
\[
 q_e:=s_Q(e)\frac{f_e}{a_e}-h_{\mathcal K}
 \qquad(e\in\delta Q).
\]
Summing the cell equations over $Q$ and using
$v(Q)=h_{\mathcal K}a(\delta Q)$ gives
$\sum_{e\in\delta Q}a_e q_e=0$.
The capacity bound gives
\[
 -2h_{\mathcal K}-\delta\le q_e\le\delta.
\]
The left side of \eqref{eq:patched-atomic-upper-bound} is
$\sum a_e|q_e|$.  If $T=\{e:q_e<0\}$ and
$\tau=\sum_{q_e>0}a_e q_e$, then
\[
 \tau\le\delta(A-a(T)),
 \qquad
 \tau\le(2h_{\mathcal K}+\delta)a(T),
 \qquad
 \sum a_e|q_e|=2\tau.
\]
Taking the maximum over $T$ proves (2), including the deficit identity.

For (3), the zero-mean identity also gives, for every fixed $f$,
\[
 \sum_{e\in\delta Q}a_e|q_e|
 =2\max_{T\subset\delta Q}
 \left(h_{\mathcal K}a(T)-\sum_{e\in T}s_Q(e)f_e\right).
\]
The maximum over $f$ and the finite maximum over $T$ commute.  For fixed $T$,
strong linear-programming duality applied to
\[
 \min\left\{\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}c_T(e)f_e:
 Bf=v,\ |f_e|\le Ma_e\right\}
\]
gives
\[
 \max_{p\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal C}}
 \left\{\sum_Cv_Cp_C
 -M\sum_ea_e|c_T(e)-(B^Tp)_e|\right\}.
\]
Substitution is exactly \eqref{eq:patched-exact-profile}.  The vectors in
$\mathcal A_T(\delta)$ are precisely the equality vectors in the two-cap
estimate for the fixed sign set $T$, so the final assertion is equivalent to
the extendibility of at least one such vector.

Finally, after fixing the oriented values
$s_Q(e)\widehat g_e$ on $\delta Q$, the remaining unknown
face fluxes have capacities $(h_{\mathcal K}+\delta)a_e$ and residual cell
demands given by the left side of \eqref{eq:patched-extension-cuts}.  The
Hoffman circulation criterion (equivalently, max-flow/min-cut with a sink)
states that these demands are feasible exactly when all the cut inequalities
\eqref{eq:patched-extension-cuts} hold.  This proves the compatibility
criterion and completes the proof.
\end{proof}

The distinction between $\Phi_Q$ and $\Psi_Q$ is genuine.  It already appears
on a two-by-two rectangular subdivision.  Take horizontal cell widths
$1,2$, vertical cell heights $2,7$, and let every cell source equal its area.
The total area and outer anisotropic perimeter are $27$ and $24$, so
$h_{\mathcal K}=9/8$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:box-self-cheeger}, applied to every
cell subpatch.  At $\delta=9/4$ one has $M=27/8$ and
$\Phi_Q(\delta)=72$: the maximizing boundary subsets have total weight $8$.
Label the left and bottom boundary faces of $C_{00}$ by $e_0,e_1$, the left
and top faces of $C_{01}$ by $e_2,e_3$, the right and bottom faces of
$C_{10}$ by $e_4,e_5$, and the right and top faces of $C_{11}$ by $e_6,e_7$.
Their weights are
\[
(a_{e_0},\ldots,a_{e_7})=(2,1,7,1,2,2,7,2).
\]
For a two-level extremizer at this deficit, the negative subset must have
weight $8$ and the corresponding outward boundary fluxes are $-M$ on that
subset and $+M$ on its complement.  The nine possible negative subsets and a
violating cell cut in \eqref{eq:patched-extension-cuts} are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
T & \text{violating }U\\ \hline
\{1,2\},\{2,3\}&\{C_{01}\}\\
\{0,1,3,4,5\},\{0,1,3,4,7\},\{0,1,3,5,7\}&\{C_{00}\}\\
\{1,6\},\{3,6\}&\{C_{00},C_{10}\}\\
\{0,4,5,7\},\{1,3,4,5,7\}&\{C_{10}\}.
\end{array}
\]
For $T=\{1,2\}$ and $T=\{2,3\}$ the residual-demand/capacity pairs are
$(109/4,27)$ and $(34,27)$.  The three sets in the second row give
$(97/8,81/8)$; the two sets in the third row give $(87/8,81/8)$ and
$(141/8,81/8)$; and the two sets in the last row give $(35/2,27/2)$.
Thus every entry violates \eqref{eq:patched-extension-cuts}.  Subsets of
weight at most $7$ or at least $9$ cannot attain
$72$ by the two-cap bound.  Since $\mathcal F_{27/8}$ is compact and the trace
error is continuous, the failure of every equality vector implies
\[
 \Psi_Q(9/4)<72=\Phi_Q(9/4).
\]
Interface compatibility therefore imposes a strict loss relative to the
one-cell profile, even though the exact compatible value is given by
\eqref{eq:patched-exact-profile}.
