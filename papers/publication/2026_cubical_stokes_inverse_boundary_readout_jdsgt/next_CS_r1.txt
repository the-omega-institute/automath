You are refereeing the attached 27-page paper. In an earlier round you found that its Theorem 6.6
was false as stated. I independently verified your counterexample and the theorem has been
withdrawn: the exact cubical patching statement is now marked "hypotheses incomplete" and carries a
remark giving both the two-interval counterexample and the 3-cube-boundary counterexample.

Two questions.

(1) WHAT IS LEFT. With the patching theorem withdrawn, does a coherent paper remain, or was that
theorem load-bearing for the rest? Go through the results that depend on it, directly or through a
chain, and tell me which ones survive intact, which now hold only under extra hypotheses (state
them), and which fall. If the honest answer is that the remaining content is a collection of
computations without a theorem, say that -- I would rather withdraw the manuscript than submit it.

(2) CAN THE THEOREM BE SAVED. The remark identifies the missing hypotheses only negatively, by
counterexample. Is there a natural sufficient condition under which exact cubical patching DOES
hold? Both counterexamples appear to turn on the same mechanism; if you can name that mechanism,
name the hypothesis that excludes it, and say whether the resulting theorem is still interesting or
has been assumed into triviality. A repaired theorem that holds only when the obstruction is
hypothesized away is worth less than an honest withdrawal, and I want you to tell me which of the
two this would be.

If the paper survives, name the journal and a rough numeric probability of acceptance there.
Do not summarize the paper.
