\begin{abstract}
We develop an exact coefficient calculus for the relative entropy of a
one-dimensional law after large-scale Cauchy smoothing.  The Cayley transform
turns normalized Poisson translates into finite Laurent modes and identifies
the pullback reference measure with Haar measure.  This yields an all-order
even expansion, a finite Fourier algorithm for its coefficients, and sharp
moment thresholds for their canonical evaluation.  In particular, finite
variance is equivalent to bounded fourth-order scaled relative entropy, and
the sixth- and eighth-order coefficient defects admit explicit positive
decompositions and rigidity statements.

Moment matching links the coefficient hierarchy to Gaussian quadrature.  For
an \(n\)-node quadrature law, the first unmatched moment determines the
leading relative-entropy coefficient; the missing-moment boundary and
regularly varying alternatives are treated separately.  At every integer
order, two critically heavy-tailed laws satisfy a square law governed by the
signed difference of their truncated boundary moments.  We also prove an
optimal all-order moment theorem for multivariate Poisson smoothing and
diagonalize its leading tensor coefficient over the harmonic Fischer
decomposition, as well as an exact nonlocal Bregman tail identity for the
moving Cauchy comparison and
Poisson-specific raw-tail decompositions that retain the nonlinear part not
captured by a finite moment jet.

The same Cayley modes generate a reproducing kernel Hilbert space.  We
identify its Poisson image, compute the integer-orbit symbol, and prove the
associated cardinal projection and observation-channel sampling theorem.
Integer samples recover exactly the lattice projection; full recovery holds
precisely on the corresponding lattice subspace.  Thus the coefficient,
quadrature, and sampling results are organized by a single observation
mechanism: Cauchy smoothing compactifies the law into Fourier data whose
finite jets control entropy and whose full mode profile supports Hilbert-space
reconstruction.
\end{abstract}
