\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Let
\[
 P_t(x)=\frac1\pi\frac{t}{t^2+x^2},\qquad t>0,
\]
be the one-dimensional Poisson, or Cauchy, kernel.  For a probability law
\(\nu\) with centre \(\bar\gamma\), we study the large-scale relative
entropy
\[
 H_\nu(t)=D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\mathord\cdot-\bar\gamma)).
\]
The normalized translate quotient is rational, and the Cayley coordinate
\[
 z=\frac{x-\ii t}{x+\ii t}
\]
maps the real line to the unit circle while sending the Cauchy reference to
Haar measure.  Consequently the Taylor modes of the quotient become finite
Laurent polynomials.  This elementary compactification is the common source
of the three parts of the paper.

First, it gives a finite coefficient calculus for \(H_\nu(t)\).  The density
quotient has an all-order expansion whose modes are polynomial functions of
the centred moments of \(\nu\); Haar integration reduces every entropy
coefficient to a constant Laurent term.  Parity removes the odd powers.  The
result is an explicit even expansion
\[
 H_\nu(t)=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N})
\]
under the stated finite-moment hypotheses, together with a finite Fourier
algorithm for each \(A_{2m}\).  The coefficient domain is sharp: canonical
finiteness of \(A_{2m}\) is equivalent to the precise centred absolute
moment required by its top Cayley mode.  At the leading order, finite
variance is equivalent to boundedness of \(t^4H_\nu(t)\), including the
converse for arbitrary laws rather than only those admitting a Taylor
expansion.

Second, moment matching converts the same expansion into a quadrature
statement.  If two laws agree below the first unmatched order, the leading
relative entropy is the squared norm of that unmatched mode.  Applied to an
\(n\)-node Gaussian quadrature law, exactness below degree \(2n\) identifies
the first missing moment with the squared \(L^2(\nu)\)-norm of the monic
orthogonal polynomial.  At the critical regularly varying boundary, a
two-law square theorem replaces the nonexistent unmatched moment by the
signed difference of its truncated versions, including odd orders.  This
boundary cannot be inferred from a formal coefficient substitution, and in
higher dimensions vague tail data alone does not determine a nonlinear
entropy limit.

For the radial Poisson kernel in arbitrary dimension, its polynomial tail
index determines the optimal complete-moment hypothesis at every first
unmatched tensor order.  The resulting orthogonally invariant quadratic
form is evaluated completely: the Fischer trace decomposition diagonalizes
it, and every harmonic weight is an explicit terminating hypergeometric
beta integral.  This extends the covariance trace--traceless calculation to
all tensor orders and gives, in particular, a closed skewness-tensor
coefficient.

The coefficient identities also produce higher defect formulas.  The sixth-
and eighth-order coefficients admit decompositions in orthogonal polynomial
coordinates, yielding a two-level defect ladder, amplification, and
two-point rigidity.  These statements retain their original moment
hypotheses: the seventh-moment bridge used for an eighth-order remainder is
not replaced by a purely algebraic calculation.

Third, the complete Cayley mode family carries more information than any
finite coefficient jet.  Its Gram kernel defines a Hilbert completion, and
the Poisson image realizes that completion as a strip RKHS.  Integer
translates generate a lattice subspace with an explicitly computable symbol.
The associated cardinal operator reconstructs the orthogonal lattice
projection from integer observation samples.  Full recovery is therefore a
subspace statement, not a claim that arbitrary laws are determined by a
finite or lattice-restricted entropy jet.

The moving Cauchy comparison supplies a dynamic counterpart to the static
coefficient calculus.  We prove the nonlocal Bregman identity with complete
domain and endpoint closure and derive its coefficient consequences.  A
Poisson raw-tail decomposition then separates the universal covariance mode
from the nonlinear energy carried beyond the smoothing scale.  This tail
description and the quadrature boundary results explain where a finite mode
jet ceases to control the full entropy.

\subsection*{Main results and organization}

Sections~2 and~3 establish the Cayley--Haar normalization and the Poisson
quotient calculus.  Section~4 proves the moving-quotient Bregman identity and
its endpoint representation.  Section~5 develops moment matching, Gaussian
quadrature, and the sharp dimensional entropy thresholds.  Section~6 gives
the sharp multivariate tensor theorem and its harmonic resolution.
The higher coefficient defects and stability statements complete
Section~5.  Section~7 records the Poisson raw-tail consequences.
Sections~8 and~9 construct the mode RKHS,
its strip realization, and the integer-lattice observation theorem.

The paper uses Gaussian quadrature only as an exact algebraic moment-matching
device; numerical integration is not part of any proof.  Likewise, the
sampling theorem recovers the lattice projection, with full reconstruction
only under membership in the lattice subspace.  These scope boundaries are
part of the theorem statements rather than additional claims of the article.
