\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Bounded representations of zero are a standard finite-state object in
Pisot numeration.  Their regularity supports normalization and arithmetic
algorithms, but regularity by itself does not control how long an
aperture-dependent chain of overlapping zero representations can remain
transient.  We consider the following quantitative question.  Fix a Pisot
recurrence $U=(u_j)$ and a coefficient alphabet $[-D,D]\cap\mathbb Z$.  At
aperture $m$, use a length-$m$ coefficient word as a state transition exactly
when it can be completed by a coefficient of the distinguished weight $u_m$
to represent zero, and overlap consecutive edges by one position.  How long
can a path beginning with an exposed nonzero coefficient survive if it never
reaches a cycle?

\begin{theoremA}
Let $U=(u_j)$ be strictly increasing with $u_0=1$, and suppose that its
integral recurrence has as characteristic polynomial the minimal polynomial
of a nonintegral Pisot number.  For every $D\ge1$ there is an effectively
computable $m_*(U,D)$ such that, for every $m\ge m_*(U,D)$, the zero loop is
the only directed cycle of the bounded-zero overlap graph and every path
from a first-coordinate-nonzero vertex has at most $m$ edges.  At every
aperture $m\ge2$, the absence of a reachable cycle implies the effective
bound $C(U,D)m$ for a suitable computable $C(U,D)<\infty$.
\end{theoremA}

\begin{theoremB}
Let $U$ be any strictly increasing Pisot numeration system with $u_0=1$,
canonical greedy digit set, and canonical normal words.  There is an
effectively computable $m_U$ such that the cyclic language-rank recoding
$\Phi_{U,m}$ is injective and
\[
\ell_{\rm cau}(U,m)\le m+1
\qquad(m\ge m_U).
\]
In particular,
$\limsup_{m\to\infty}\ell_{\rm cau}(U,m)/m\le1$.
\end{theoremB}

\begin{theoremC}
Let $\theta$ be the leading root of $x^3-2x^2+x-1$ and let $U_\theta$ be the
binary recurrence beginning $1,2,4$.  Its injective apertures are exactly
\[
\{m\ge2:\Phi_{\theta,m}\text{ is injective}\}
=\{2\}\cup\{m\ge4\}.
\]
At aperture two the fold is the identity.  At aperture three its sole
nonsingleton path-space fiber consists of the two constant binary points.
For every $m\ge4$,
\[
\ell_{\rm cau}(\theta,m)=2\left\lfloor\frac m2\right\rfloor-1.
\]
The maximum path length from a first-coordinate-nonzero vertex of
$G_m(U_\theta,1)$ is $2\lfloor m/2\rfloor-2$, so the linear order in
Theorem~A is sharp and the asymptotic coefficient $1$ in Theorem~B is
optimal.
\end{theoremC}

Theorem~A is proved in the precise form of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded-zero-linear-transient}.  The coefficient completing
an edge is not assumed to belong to $[-D,D]$; the proof gives the uniform
bound $|c|\le K_{U,D}$.  This mixed-alphabet edge condition is what permits
the overlap graph to record all bounded zero representations at aperture
$m$.

The mechanism is local.  Lemma
\ref{lem:bounded-zero-arbitrary-alphabet-collapse} compares two consecutive
zero representations under every algebraic embedding.  The contracting
conjugates bound the nondominant sums, while an effective finite separation
in the dominant embedding forces every later quotient to vanish once the
aperture crosses a computable threshold.  The path then reaches the zero
vertex within $m+1$ overlaps.  At sufficiently large aperture that vertex
has no nonzero predecessor, which excludes every nonzero cycle and gives
the coefficient-one path bound without an acyclicity assumption.  The
finitely many smaller apertures are handled by exact acyclic longest-path
searches when the conditional all-aperture bound is invoked.  The argument
uses only Pisot contraction and the recurrence weights and does not require
Condition~F.

For Theorem~B, order the legal length-$m$ words by canonical rank, extend the
same positional rank to the full digit cube, reduce it modulo the number
$u_m$ of legal words, and return the legal word with that residue.  Overlap
these recoded windows along the shift.  Lemma
\ref{lem:pisot-exact-collision-quotient} shows that the coordinatewise-
difference quotient loses no collision: every bounded digit difference is
realized by its positive and negative parts.  The quotient is exactly
$G_m(U,d_U)$, the longest path from a first-coordinate-nonzero state is one
less than the least future-only inverse window, and injectivity is equivalent
to the absence of a reachable cycle.  The eventual graph theorem therefore
makes the recoding injective for all sufficiently large apertures and gives
the universal asymptotic bound in Theorem~B.  At smaller injective apertures,
the effective estimate $\ell_{\rm cau}(U,m)\le C_Um$ remains valid.

Finite-state recognition of bounded zero representations in Pisot systems is
classical \cite{Frougny1992}, but it gives no aperture-linear survival bound
for these moving overlap graphs.  Classical pair and fiber graphs supply
reachable-cycle tests and finite inverse windows for injective local maps
\cite{AmorosoPatt1972,Richardson1972,Nasu1977,Head1989,LindMarcus1995,
Nasu1995}.  Their generic state-count bound is exponential in $m$ here.
Likewise, Ashley's decoder-window estimates are linear in the number of
states of a presenting graph, not in this aperture
\cite{Ashley1988,Ashley1996}.  The bounded-delay theorem of Frougny and
Sakarovitch starts from a rational relation already known to have bounded
delay; it does not derive an $O(m)$ delay from injectivity
\cite{FrougnySakarovitch1991}.  The arithmetic adjacent-collapse lemma is
what changes the scale from exponential to linear.

Theorem~C shows that the threshold in Theorem~B is intrinsic rather than an
artifact of the contraction estimate: the same fixed cubic fold is the
identity at aperture two, loses injectivity at aperture three, and is
injective at every aperture from four onward.  It also supplies the optimal
coefficient within a single fixed system: the recurrence and alphabet remain
fixed while the aperture grows.  Bassino's cubic construction instead varies
the base with the desired inverse length
\cite{Bassino2002}; varying-system unboundedness alone gives no lower growth
order for a fixed recurrence.

Section~\ref{sec:bounded-zero-transients} proves Theorem~A, and
Section~\ref{sec:cyclic-rank-application} identifies the exact collision
quotient and proves Theorem~B.  Section~\ref{sec:fixed-cubic-sharpness}
proves Theorem~C together with the terminal classification.  Related exact
classifications for quadratic and simple-Parry systems are obtained in
\cite{MaZhang2026ExactOverlap}; no result from that paper is used here.
