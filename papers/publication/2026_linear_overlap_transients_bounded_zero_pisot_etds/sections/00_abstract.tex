\begin{abstract}
We study one-position overlap chains of bounded representations of zero in a
fixed Pisot linear numeration system.  If $U=(u_j)$ is strictly increasing,
$u_0=1$, and its recurrence has a nonintegral Pisot characteristic root,
then for every fixed coefficient bound $D$ there is an effective constant
$C(U,D)$.  Unless a nonzero overlap chain reaches a directed cycle, its
transient has fewer than $C(U,D)m$ edges at aperture $m$.  The proof uses an
arbitrary-alphabet adjacent-collapse lemma: contracting conjugates force the
second quotient in two consecutive zero representations to vanish and fix
the newly exposed coefficient.  No Condition~F or zero-preservation
hypothesis is required.

As an exact application, consider the cyclic rank recoding obtained by
extending the canonical rank of legal length-$m$ words to the full digit
cube and reducing it modulo $u_m$.  Its coordinatewise-difference graph is
exactly the bounded-zero overlap graph for the canonical difference
alphabet.  Hence injectivity implies an aperture-linear future-only inverse
depth.  A fixed binary cubic recurrence with leading root of
$x^3-2x^2+x-1$ attains the linear order: for every $m\ge4$ its cyclic rank
recoding is injective and has exact causal length
$2\lfloor m/2\rfloor-1$.  We classify the penultimate obstruction set as
$\{E_m,-E_m\}$ and prove that the next set is empty.  The same words show
that the bounded-zero transient bound itself has sharp linear order.
\end{abstract}
