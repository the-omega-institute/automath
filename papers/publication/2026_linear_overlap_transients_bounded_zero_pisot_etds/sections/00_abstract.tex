\begin{abstract}
We prove eventual acyclicity for one-position overlap graphs of bounded
representations of zero in a fixed Pisot linear numeration system.  If
$U=(u_j)$ is strictly increasing, $u_0=1$, and its recurrence has a
nonintegral Pisot characteristic root, then for every coefficient bound $D$
there is an effective $m_*(U,D)$ such that, at every aperture $m\ge m_*(U,D)$,
the zero loop is the only directed cycle and every nonzero anchored path has
at most $m$ edges.  At all apertures, the absence of a reachable cycle gives
an effective $C(U,D)m$ transient bound.  The proof compares adjacent zero
representations under the algebraic embeddings: contraction bounds the
nondominant contributions, and finite separation in the dominant embedding
forces the successive quotients to collapse.

For cyclic recodings obtained by reducing canonical language ranks modulo
$u_m$, the coordinate-difference graph is exactly this bounded-zero overlap
graph.  The recoding of every fixed Pisot system is therefore injective at
all sufficiently large apertures, and its future-only inverse length
satisfies $\limsup_{m\to\infty}\ell_{\rm cau}(U,m)/m\le1$.  For the fixed
binary cubic recurrence
with leading root of $x^3-2x^2+x-1$, the recoding is injective for every
$m\ge4$ and has exact causal length $2\lfloor m/2\rfloor-1$; hence the
universal asymptotic coefficient and the linear transient order are sharp.
\end{abstract}
