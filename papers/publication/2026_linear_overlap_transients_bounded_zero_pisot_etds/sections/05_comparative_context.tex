\section{Comparative context}
\label{sec:comparative-context}

The fixed cubic theorem and the broader family results answer different
questions.  Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-cubic-sharpness} keeps the recurrence,
alphabet, and base fixed while the aperture grows.  Its limit
$\ell_{\rm cau}(\theta,m)/m\to1$ proves that a theorem uniform over fixed
Pisot systems cannot improve the order $O_U(m)$ to $o(m)$.  By contrast,
Bassino's cubic construction varies the base with the desired inverse
length \cite{Bassino2002}.  Varying-system unboundedness does not establish a
lower order inside any one recurrence, while fixed-system sharpness does not
classify how exact thresholds change across a parameter family.

The base $\theta$ is also a canonical simple-Parry example:
$d_\theta(1)=1101\,0^\infty$.  That identification supplies the legal
language and its rank recurrence, but the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-cubic-sharpness} uses none of the general
simple-Parry quotient theory.  After the Parry word is checked, the argument
needs only the recurrence for $Q_j(\theta)$, a direct quotient bound, the
contracting-conjugate estimate, the suffix-sum identity, and the terminal
integer equations.  Keeping that proof here makes the sharpness statement
logically complete rather than treating it as computational evidence.

Quadratic Pisot systems behave more rigidly.  Their least nontrivial cyclic
rank threshold is always two or three, and their exact future-only inverse
lengths are bounded by much smaller formulas than the fixed cubic sequence
above.  Those facts do not enter the contraction theorem: the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded-zero-linear-transient} neither specializes the
degree nor uses a classification of legal languages.  Conversely, the
quadratic classification depends on exact chamber arithmetic, critical
fibers, Fischer covers, and Markov order, which would obscure the
bounded-zero mechanism if reproduced here.

The companion article by the present authors, \emph{Exact overlap thresholds
and future-only inverse depth for cyclic ranks of quadratic and simple-Parry
beta-languages} \cite{MaZhang2026ExactOverlap}, develops those comparisons in
full.  It contains the
explicit Fibonacci model, the complete quadratic-Pisot threshold and local
structure, the coordinate-difference quotient for arbitrary nonintegral
simple-Parry bases, and Bassino-family unboundedness.  No result from that
article is used in Sections~\ref{sec:bounded-zero-transients}--
\ref{sec:fixed-cubic-sharpness}; the only shared material is the definition
of cyclic language-rank recoding and the distinction from numerical
$\beta$-normalization.

This separation also fixes the meaning of the sharpness claim.  The
effective constant $C(U,D)$ includes exact longest-path searches at the
finitely many apertures below the contraction threshold.  We do not claim
that this particular constant is optimal.  Sharpness refers to growth order:
for the single recurrence $U_\theta$ and the fixed coefficient alphabet
$\{-1,0,1\}$, the exact longest finite path is
$2\lfloor m/2\rfloor-2$, hence asymptotic to $m$.  The same terminal words
are realized as raw digit differences, so the corresponding inverse length
is one larger.  The graph theorem and its cyclic-rank corollary therefore
have matching linear lower order for the same fixed arithmetic object.
