\section{Periodic-data and gain-graph background}
\label{sec:inverse-background}

This appendix records two standard translations needed to delimit the scope
of the determinant formulas.  Conjugacy-class periodic data for finite-group
extensions are treated by Boyle and Schmieding
\cite{BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions}; represented
\(G\)-cospectrality and switching are part of gain-graph theory
\cite{Zaslavsky1989BiasedGraphs,CavaleriDAngeliDonno2021BalanceGainGraphs,
CavaleriDonno2022Cospectrality}.  The statements below are included only to
fix conventions for directed marked multigraphs.

\subsection{Determinants and conjugacy-class periodic data}

Let \(\CC[G]\) have basis \([g]\), and put
\[
 \mathbb A_\tau:=
 \left(\sum_{e:i\to j}[\tau(e)]\right)_{i,j\in V}\in M_V(\CC[G]).
\]
If \(\kappa(\sum_ga_g[g])=(\sum_{g\in C}a_g)_C\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-kappa-periodic-data}
 \kappa\!\left(\operatorname{tr}(\mathbb A_\tau^n)\right)
   =(N_{n,C}(\tau))_{C\in\operatorname{Conj}(G)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Standard periodic-data dictionary]
\label{prop:comparison-dictionary}
Let \(\tau:E(\Gamma)\to G\) and
\(\tau':E(\Gamma')\to G\) be two labellings, with no identification of the
marked directed multigraphs required.  The following data are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
 \item \(P_{\rho,\tau}=P_{\rho,\tau'}\) for every
   \(\rho\in\Irr(G)\);
 \item \(\Tr(A_{\rho,\tau}^n)=\Tr(A_{\rho,\tau'}^n)\) for every \(\rho,n\);
 \item \(N_{n,C}(\tau)=N_{n,C}(\tau')\) for every \(n,C\);
 \item \(p_{n,C}(\tau)=p_{n,C}(\tau')\) for every \(n,C\).
\end{enumerate}
This is an equivalence of periodic data, not a conjugacy or switching
classification.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The normalized identity
\[
 -\Log_0P_{\rho,\tau}(z)
 =\sum_{n\ge1}\Tr(A_{\rho,\tau}^n)z^n/n
\]
gives (i)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(ii).  Expanding closed paths gives
\[
 \Tr(A_{\rho,\tau}^n)=\sum_C N_{n,C}(\tau)\chi_\rho(C),
\]
and the irreducible character table is invertible, proving
(ii)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(iii).  Finally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-periodic-primitive-triangular}
 N_{n,C}=\sum_{d\mid n}d
   \sum_{\substack{D\in\operatorname{Conj}(G)\\D^{,n/d}=C}}p_{d,D}.
\end{equation}
The \(d=n\) term is \(np_{n,C}\); induction on \(n\) proves
(iii)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(iv).  These are the standard character and
necklace/Witt transforms of the periodic data.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Directed fundamental-cycle gains}

For each marked directed edge \(e:u\to v\), adjoin a formal inverse
orientation \(\bar e:v\to u\), impose \(\bar{\bar e}=e\), and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formal-reverse-gain}
 \tau(\bar e):=\tau(e)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The resulting doubled graph is used only for traversing walks; the formal
inverse is not an additional edge of the shift.  The gain of a walk in the
doubled graph is the chronological product of \(\tau(e)\) and
\(\tau(e)^{-1}\) according to the orientations traversed.

A switching function \(u:V\to G\) acts by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-switching-action}
 \tau^u(e)=u(o(e))^{-1}\tau(e)u(t(e)).
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:formal-reverse-gain} is compatible with this action, and a
closed-walk gain is conjugated by the switching value at its base vertex.

\begin{proposition}[Standard fundamental-cycle switching criterion]
\label{prop:fundamental-cycle-certificate}
Assume the underlying undirected graph is connected.  Choose a spanning tree
\(T\), a root \(v_0\), and one marked directed edge over each tree edge.
Use the formal inverse when a tree path traverses a chosen edge backward, and
switch two cocycles \(\tau,\tau'\) so that all chosen tree-edge gains are the
identity.  Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_r\), where \(r=|E|-|V|+1\), be the remaining
marked edges.  For each \(e_i\), let \(\gamma_i\) be the closed walk in the
doubled graph that follows the tree path from \(v_0\) to \(o(e_i)\), traverses
\(e_i\), and returns from \(t(e_i)\) to \(v_0\) along the tree.  Define
\[
 g_i:=\tau(\gamma_i),\qquad g_i':=\tau'(\gamma_i).
\]
Then \(\tau\) and \(\tau'\) are switching equivalent if and only if there is
one \(h\in G\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-simultaneous-conjugacy}
 g_i'=h^{-1}g_i h\qquad(1\le i\le r).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Recursive switching from the root normalizes the selected tree edges; formal
inverse gains ensure that the recursion is independent of the direction in
which a tree edge is traversed.  After normalization, the gains of the
fundamental cycles determine the cocycle.  A switching that preserves all
tree identities is constant on vertices, with common value \(h\), and hence
acts on every fundamental-cycle gain by the simultaneous conjugation in
\eqref{eq:appendix-simultaneous-conjugacy}.  Conversely, such an \(h\),
combined with the two tree-normalizing switchings, gives a switching between
the original cocycles.  This is the usual voltage/gain-graph criterion
\cite{GrossTucker1987TopologicalGraphTheory,
CavaleriDAngeliDonno2021BalanceGainGraphs}.
\end{proof}

The appendix is relative to the fixed marked-edge presentation.  It makes no
claim that determinant equality implies switching, no claim of invariance
under state splitting or base automorphisms, and no inverse-rigidity claim
beyond the cited periodic-data and gain-graph theory.
