\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For arbitrary \(E=(C_2)^r\)-extensions over two primitive bases of at most
\(V\) vertices, \(2V\lceil\log_2(4V)\rceil\) radial locations in the common
open Perron interval determine the complete primitive length--element profile,
provided that one location is algebraic.  This radial depth is independent of
\(r\), but each location supplies a full \(2^r\)-component vector, for
\(2^rM(V)\) scalar comparisons in total.  The bases need not coincide.  A
strict twisted gap permits centered, not raw, class profiles at a Perron
endpoint in the binary scalar theorem.  The determinant ratio governing the
test is canonically the ratio of the standard Artin--Mazur zeta functions of
the two binary covers.  The proof is genuinely effective:
one collision produces a unique rational Mahler certificate, its numerator
and denominator satisfy an \(O(D\log D)\) degree bound and an explicit
height bound,
and a finite Pad\'e system reconstructs or rejects it.  The exact four-vertex
pair shows why a single centered Perron-boundary vector cannot be an inverse
invariant; every corresponding raw class profile diverges to \(-\infty\).
The logarithmic degree order is sharp only for general rational divisor
equations; no optimality claim is made for realizable SFT sampling.

The strongest implication in this chain is the determinant lifting theorem.
Its interior form is unconditional, while its endpoint form uses the strict
gap only to continue through the Perron circle.  The arithmetic proof uses
more than positivity of a rational function:
determinant parity supplies integral coefficients for the critical Mahler
product.  Across different bases, equality of the scalar sampled profiles
first recovers equality of the base determinants and hence restores this
parity coordinate by coordinate.  Nishioka's 1982 theorem converts an
algebraic special value into algebraicity of the function, and his 1985
theorem makes that algebraic solution rational; the latter rationality is
prior work.  The remaining normalization over \(\QQ\) then supplies the
coboundary.  Removing the parity hypothesis would require a
different arithmetic input.

The same mechanism does not presently yield a polynomial sample bound for
every finite group.  For \(C_3\), Adams--M\"obius inversion already couples
two character determinants at infinitely many arithmetically distributed
powers, rather than one lacunary Mahler orbit.  A general finite-group theorem
therefore requires a new special-value and effective zero theory for these
coupled Euler systems.  In its absence, the proved general statement remains
unconditional analytic recovery from a profile set with an interior
accumulation point.

The novelty claim is correspondingly narrow.  Frobenius-class Euler products,
their character expansion, algebraic-solution rationality for rational
functional equations, and the general periodic-data dictionary are cited
background.  The claimed increment is the combination of parity-compatible
algebraic collision lifting, effective rational-coboundary bounds, and the
cross-base elementary-two-group theorem with rank-independent radial depth.
