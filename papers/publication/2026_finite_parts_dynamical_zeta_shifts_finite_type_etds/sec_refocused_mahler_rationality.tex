The nonlinear rationality step needed below follows from the linear Mahler
dichotomy after logarithmic differentiation.  The residual passage from a
rational logarithmic derivative to a rational function is forced by the
power-map divisor relation.

\begin{proposition}[Algebraic critical Mahler germs are rational]
\label{prop:algebraic-mahler-coboundary}
Let \(p\ge2\), let \(H\in\CC(z)^\times\) satisfy \(H(0)=1\), and suppose
that a convergent germ \(F\in1+z\CC[[z]]\), algebraic over \(\CC(z)\),
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algebraic-critical-mahler-equation}
 F(z)^p=H(z)F(z^p).
\end{equation}
Then \(F\in\CC(z)\).  If, in addition, \(H\in\QQ(z)\) and
\(F\in\QQ[[z]]\), there is a unique \(R\in\QQ(z)^\times\), normalized by
\(R(0)=1\), such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-rational-mahler-coboundary}
 H(z)=\frac{R(z^p)}{R(z)^p}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 u(z)=z\frac{F'(z)}{F(z)}\in z\CC[[z]],
 \qquad
 a(z)=-\frac{z}{p}\frac{H'(z)}{H(z)}\in\CC(z).
\]
Logarithmic differentiation of
\eqref{eq:algebraic-critical-mahler-equation} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algebraic-critical-log-derivative}
 u(z^p)-u(z)=a(z).
\end{equation}
If \(a=0\), comparison of coefficients in \(u(z^p)=u(z)\) gives \(u=0\),
and the normalization gives \(F=1\).  Suppose that \(a\ne0\).  Applying
\(z\mapsto z^p\) to
\eqref{eq:algebraic-critical-log-derivative} and eliminating the
inhomogeneous term yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algebraic-critical-linear-mahler-equation}
 a(z)\bigl(u(z^{p^2})-u(z^p)\bigr)
 -a(z^p)\bigl(u(z^p)-u(z)\bigr)=0.
\end{equation}
After denominators are cleared, this is a non-trivial linear \(p\)-Mahler
equation.  The function \(u\) is algebraic because \(F\) is algebraic.
The rational--transcendental dichotomy for linear Mahler functions therefore
gives \(u\in\CC(z)\)
\cite[Theorem~5.1.7]{Nishioka1996MahlerFunctions}; equivalently one may use
\cite[Corollary~8]{BellCoonsRowland2013MahlerDichotomy}.

We next show that this rational logarithmic derivative forces \(F\) itself
to be rational in the present critical equation.  Remove from
\(\mathbb P^1(\CC)\) the finite union of the singular sets of \(F\) and
\(u(z)/z\).  Analytic continuation on the complement preserves the
differential equation
\[
 F'(z)=\frac{u(z)}zF(z).
\]
Consequently monodromy acts on the chosen branch by constant multipliers.
Because \(F\) has only finitely many branches, the image of this monodromy
action is a finite subgroup of \(\CC^\times\).  If \(m\) annihilates that
subgroup, then \(G:=F^m\) is a single-valued algebraic function.  Its Puiseux
expansions have no residual monodromy, so it is meromorphic on
\(\mathbb P^1(\CC)\), and hence \(G\in\CC(z)^\times\).

Raising \eqref{eq:algebraic-critical-mahler-equation} to the \(m\)-th power
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algebraic-critical-rational-power}
 G(z)^p=H(z)^mG(z^p).
\end{equation}
For \(\alpha\in\CC^\times\), put
\(d(\alpha)=\operatorname{ord}_{z=\alpha}G\).  Taking the order at any
\(\beta\in\CC^\times\) in
\eqref{eq:algebraic-critical-rational-power} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algebraic-critical-divisor-congruence}
 p d(\beta)\equiv d(\beta^p)\pmod m.
\end{equation}
If \(d(\alpha)\not\equiv0\pmod m\), then repeated use of
\eqref{eq:algebraic-critical-divisor-congruence} shows that
\(d(\beta)\not\equiv0\pmod m\) for every \(\beta\) satisfying
\(\beta^{p^n}=\alpha\).  There are \(p^n\) such points for every \(n\),
contradicting the finite support of the divisor of \(G\).  Thus every finite
non-zero order of \(G\) is divisible by \(m\).  The order at zero is zero
because \(G(0)=1\), and the degree-zero condition for a principal divisor
then gives the same divisibility at infinity.

It follows that \(m^{-1}\operatorname{div}(G)\) is an integral divisor of
degree zero on \(\mathbb P^1\), hence is the divisor of a rational function
\(Q\).  Therefore \(G=cQ^m\) for some \(c\in\CC^\times\).  After choosing
\(c_0^m=c\), the algebraic function \(F/(c_0Q)\) has \(m\)-th power one and
is constant.  Hence \(F\in\CC(z)\).

Under the final coefficient hypotheses, coefficientwise Galois invariance
gives \(F\in\QQ(z)\).  Taking \(R=F^{-1}\) gives
\eqref{eq:general-rational-mahler-coboundary}.  If two normalized rational
functions give the same coboundary, their quotient \(U\) satisfies
\(U(z^p)=U(z)^p\) and \(U(0)=1\).  A least non-constant Taylor term would
have different degrees on the two sides, so \(U=1\), proving uniqueness.
\end{proof}
