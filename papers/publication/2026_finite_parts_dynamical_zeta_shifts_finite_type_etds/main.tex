\author[Haobo Ma]{Haobo Ma}
\address{CHRONOAI}

\date{}
\renewcommand{\shortauthors}{Haobo Ma}

\begin{abstract}
For a mixing subshift of finite type carrying a finite-group edge cocycle, we
determine the kernel of the map from Liv\v{s}ic cohomology classes to the full
family of irreducible twisted determinants.  Two cocycles have every twisted
determinant in common if and only if they have the same unmarked primitive
orbit counts at every length and in every conjugacy class.  This need not be
Liv\v{s}ic cohomology: the smallest counterexample is the full two-shift with
\(G=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\), where the labelings \((0,1)\) and \((1,0)\)
have identical trivial and sign determinants although a marked fixed orbit
separates them.  Both skew products are mixing and all twisted blocks are
semisimple.  We give precise positive hypotheses: on an edge-resolving graph,
semisimplicity together with a common vertex-compatible spectral-projector
certificate forces a single vertex gauge and hence Liv\v{s}ic cohomology.
More sharply, continuous cohomology between one-step cocycles is exactly
vertex-gauge cohomology.  We define the intrinsic spectral cohomology
multiplicity \(\mu_{\Gamma,G}\); the determinant family determines a cocycle
if and only if \(\mu_{\Gamma,G}=1\), and this integer is computable by a
finite exact enumeration of \(\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(|\Gamma|),G)/G\).
For an \(m\)-loop bouquet and finite abelian \(G\), Fourier inversion gives
the closed formula \(\mu_{\Gamma,G}=m!/\prod_g n_g!\).  Consequently every
primitive non-trivial abelian bouquet extension is non-rigid.  We also prove
that graph, group, and Perron-peripheral spectrum alone cannot support a
sharp criterion.
The forward mechanism behind the kernel theorem is an Adams--M\"obius
inversion, since repetitions change a primitive label by \(g\mapsto g^m\).
At the Perron boundary we retain every twisted eigenvalue
\(\lambda\zeta\), obtain the class exponent \(\delta_C\), and compute the
renormalized Frobenius-class product constants from branch-compatible reduced
determinant coordinates \(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\).  Thus the inverse
rigidity boundary and its minimal obstruction sit inside the same finite
symbolic-dynamical determinant calculus as the Perron-boundary and
fixed-label product theorems.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Guide to the references}

The bibliography is cited in three distinct roles.  The first group consists
of proof antecedents used inside the arguments: standard Perron--Frobenius and
symbolic-dynamical references for the base SFT and determinant formalism,
finite-group character theory and Adams operations, and the precise
twisted-determinant identities from which the paper starts.  The symbolic and
Perron--Frobenius base is
\cite{ArtinMazur1965PeriodicPoints,BowenLanford1970Zeta,Manning1971Axiom,
Ruelle1976ZetaExpanding,Ruelle1978Thermodynamic,Parry1983PrimeOrbit,
LindMarcus1995,ParryPollicott1990Zeta,Seneta2006NonnegativeMatrices,
Wielandt1950Unzerlegbare}.
The finite-character and Adams-operation input is
\cite{Serre1977LinearRepresentations,
AtiyahTall1969GroupRepresentationsLambdaRings,Knutson1973LambdaRings,
DressSiebeneicher1988BurnsideRing,MetropolisRota1983Necklaces,
Wehrhahn1990AperiodicRings}.  The twisted determinant and graph-Artin
antecedents are
\cite{Fried1983PeriodicPointsTwistedCoefficients,
Fried1983HomologicalIdentitiesClosedOrbits,AdachiSunada1987TwistedPF,
Hashimoto1989ZetaFiniteGraphs,Hashimoto1990ZetaLFunctionsFiniteGraphs,
Hashimoto1992ArtinDensityPrimeCycles,Sunada1986LFunctionsGeometry} and
\cite{StarkTerras1996FiniteGraphsCoverings,
StarkTerras2000FiniteGraphsCoveringsII,StarkTerras2007FiniteGraphsCoveringsIII}.
Together these papers supply the finite-dimensional trace, determinant,
character, and M\"obius/Adams mechanisms used in the proofs of the open-disc
determinant inversion and its Peter--Weyl reformulations.

The second group is the classical comparison layer.  These papers fix the
Chebotarev, Mertens, product, Liv\v{s}ic, and homology-class conventions
against which the renormalized constants are compared.  The Chebotarev and
product-normalisation references are
\cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev,NooraniParry1992ChebotarevShifts,
Noorani1997HomogeneousExtensionsMarkov,Sharp1991Mertens,
MeslizaNoorani1999FrobeniusMertens}.  The homology-class, Liv\v{s}ic, and
skew-product comparison references are
\cite{KatsudaSunada1990ClosedOrbitsHomology,
Lalley1987DistributionPeriodicOrbits,Sharp1993ClosedOrbitsHomologyAnosov,
Parry1999LivsicNonAbelian,ParryPollicott1997LivsicCompactLie,
ParryPollicott2008BauerSkewProducts,PollicottSharp2007Chebotarev,
PollicottSharp2008ArtinReciprocitySkewProducts}.  In the present paper they
are not substitutes for the Adams--M\"obius determinant readout; rather they
provide the established orbit-distribution scale, scalar normalisation, and
strict-gap product conventions used for comparison.

The third group is recent adjacent context.  These works concern finite group
actions on SFTs, \(G\)-SFT classification, group-language density,
noncommutative skew products, orbit-growth and periodic-length questions,
finite-data rigidity, and resonance or periodic-orbit invariants.  The finite
group action and \(G\)-SFT classification context is
\cite{AdlerKitchensMarcus1985FiniteGroupActions,
Fiebig1993PeriodicFiniteGroupActions,
SilverWilliams2005InvariantFiniteGroupActions,
BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions,
BoyleCarlsenEilers2020FlowEquivalenceGSFT,
Matsumoto2016ExtensionsSubshiftsFiniteGroups,
Epperlein2026FreeInertGSFTs}.  The group-language and skew-product context is
\cite{BertheGouletOuelletPerrin2025RationalLanguagesDensity,
BertheGouletOuelletNybergBroddaPerrinPetersen2026GroupLanguages,
CrismaleDelVecchioGrisetaRossi2025NoncommutativeSkewProduct}.  The
growth-rate, finite-data, resonance, and periodic-length context is
\cite{DougallSharp2021AnosovGrowthGroupExtensions,
Coles2025VassilievWritheAxiomA,Humbert2025FirstRuelleResonance,
OHare2025FiniteDataRigidity,DeJong2026PeriodicOrbitLengths} and
\cite{NordinNoorani2021BouquetDyckOrbitGrowth,
NordinNooraniMohd2024OrbitGrowthSoficPFT,
NordinNooraniMohd2025SoficDyckOrbitGrowth}.  These papers are cited to locate
the finite-symbolic and finite-data landscape around the paper.  They are
comparison-only citations and are not hypotheses or proof antecedents for the
Perron-boundary determinant-to-product-coordinate theorem.
