\begin{abstract}
Let two one-step \(C_2\)-cocycles label the same primitive \(v\)-vertex edge
shift, and let \(p_{n,C}\) be the primitive length--class counts.  At a radius
\(y\), the class Euler profile is
\(\mathcal E_C(y)=\sum_{n\ge1}p_{n,C}\log(1-y^n)\).  Under the strict twisted
spectral gap this profile extends to the Perron endpoint.  Without any
twisted-gap hypothesis we prove an effective inverse theorem: equality of
every class profile at any
\[
 (2v)^2\bigl(2^{\lfloor\log_2(2v)\rfloor+1}-1\bigr)<16v^3
\]
distinct algebraic radii in the open Perron interval forces equality of
\(p_{n,C}\) for all \(n,C\); under the strict gap the endpoint may also be
used.  More sharply, if the reduced sign-determinant ratio has total degree
\(D>0\), it has at most
\(D^2(2^{\lfloor\log_2D\rfloor+1}-1)-1\) algebraic collision radii.

The sign-determinant ratio is canonically the ratio of the ordinary
Artin--Mazur zeta functions of the two \(C_2\)-covers.  The inverse mechanism
is arithmetic: a single algebraic interior collision forces this ratio to be
a normalized rational Mahler coboundary
\(R(z^2)/R(z)^2\).  We prove this by determinant parity, the
Dieudonn\'e--Dwork criterion, Nishioka's critical special-value theorem, and
an explicit algebraic-to-rational descent.  A divisor bound then gives degree
and height estimates for \(R\), and one finite Pad\'e system reconstructs or
rejects the certificate.  An exact four-vertex pair shows that one
Perron-boundary value does not suffice.  For general finite groups we prove
recovery from a radial set with an interior accumulation point and identify
the obstruction to a finite bound: already for \(C_3\), Adams--M\"obius
inversion couples determinant logarithms at infinitely many arithmetically
distributed powers, outside the one-variable Mahler theorem used here.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Guide to the references}
The trace and dynamical-zeta formalism is taken from
\cite{BowenLanford1970Zeta,ParryPollicott1990Zeta}; necklace/Witt inversion
is taken from \cite{MetropolisRota1983Necklaces}.  The arithmetic inputs are
Nishioka's special-value theorem, the Dieudonn\'e--Dwork criterion, and the
D-finite--Mahler rationality theorem
\cite{Nishioka1982MahlerFunctionValues,PomeratStraub2024RootsPowerSeries,
BellCoonsRowland2013MahlerDichotomy}.  The nearest algorithmic comparison is
\cite{ChyzakDreyfusDumasMezzarobba2018MahlerSolutions}.  The inverse-data
appendix uses Boyle--Schmieding's conjugacy-class periodic data and the
gain-graph literature
\cite{BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions,
CavaleriDAngeliDonno2021BalanceGainGraphs,CavaleriDonno2022Cospectrality,
AbiadBelardoKhramova2024Switching,Zaslavsky1989BiasedGraphs,
GrossTucker1987TopologicalGraphTheory}.
