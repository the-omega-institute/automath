\title[Finite-group SFT extensions and product coordinates]{Adams--M\"obius primitive inversion and constant-coordinate refinements for finite-group extensions of shifts of finite type}

\author[Haobo Ma]{Haobo Ma}
\address{CHRONOAI}

\date{}
\renewcommand{\shortauthors}{Haobo Ma}

\begin{abstract}
For a mixing subshift of finite type carrying a finite-group cocycle, we
study how finite-dimensional twisted determinants determine primitive orbit
data by conjugacy class.  Periodic labels record powers of primitive labels,
so passing from periodic determinant logarithms to primitive label series
requires Adams operations \(g\mapsto g^m\) together with M\"obius
inversion.  This yields an explicit Peter--Weyl determinant formula for the
primitive channels.  Under the strict twisted spectral gap
\(\eta<\lambda\), the channels have Perron-point values and give
class-indexed Mertens constants.  The Euler-product asymptotic itself is then
obtained internally from the additive class estimate and the explicit
product-tail reduction; its leading exponent \(|C|/|G|\) is the classical
comparison scale.  The new product-level assertion is the
constant-coordinate refinement after that scale is separated: a
branch-compatible, Adams-corrected fixed-label determinant formula for the
constant, with the scalar Perron channel separated, the boundary triple
\((1,1,\mathbf1)\) deleted, and non-trivial coordinates
\(F_\varrho\), not periodic Artin logarithms \(\mathcal L_\varrho\).
We prove determinant coordinate readout for these constants and give a local
finite \(S_3\) witness, with explicit values \(F_\varepsilon(1/2)\) and
\(F_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)\), showing that naive Artin substitution fails at the
product-constant level.  Scalar finite parts and the canonical cyclic-lift root
layer serve only as finite-matrix normalisation and comparison devices; the
main result is the internally derived Adams-corrected symbolic-dynamical
determinant formula for the fixed-label constant coordinates, with the
classical Frobenius-class product theorems used only for comparison of
conventions.
\end{abstract}
