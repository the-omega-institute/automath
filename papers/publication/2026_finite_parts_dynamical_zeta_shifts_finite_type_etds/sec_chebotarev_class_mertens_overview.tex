\subsection{Class Mertens constants and Adams-corrected Euler products}
\label{subsec:class-mertens-adams-products}

The class-Mertens arguments have one Perron-boundary spine:
\Cref{thm:perron-boundary-determinant-product}.  Peripheral primitive
channels give the renormalized class weights, reduced fixed-label determinant
finite parts give the Frobenius-class product constants, and
\Cref{cor:renormalized-coordinate-readout} gives the product-coordinate
readout.  The strict-gap formulas retained below are the clean computational
specialization of \Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization}.  The finite-enclosure
and Artin-substitution results record consequences of this renormalized
determinant vector.

This subsection first proves the renormalized Perron-boundary theorem package,
then records the strict-gap finite-group Mertens constants as computational
forms recovered from it.  The finite-dimensional passage is
\[
\begin{array}{c}
  \text{additive class constants}\\
  \downarrow\;\text{product-tail reduction}\\
  \text{fixed-label Euler transform}\\
  \downarrow\;\text{finite determinant evaluation}\\
  \text{determinant readout}\\
  \downarrow\;\text{consequences}\\
  \text{Artin obstruction}.
\end{array}
\]
Here \Cref{thm:perron-boundary-determinant-product} supplies the primary
additive renormalized constants and the reduced determinant and product
formulae.  The older
\Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit,thm:fixed-label-euler-transform,thm:class-euler-product-mertens}
are the strict-gap computational forms recovered by
\Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization}.  The determinant layer then evaluates the fixed-label vector,
compares the resulting coordinates, and isolates the Artin-substitution
obstruction without introducing a second family of product constants.

The two coordinates used throughout the subsection are
\(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), for the additive constants, and
\(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), for the product constants.  The periodic Artin
logarithm \(\mathcal L_\varrho\) appears only as the comparison object in the
final obstruction theorem.  The scalar normalisation separates in the two
finite Fourier correspondences
\[
  \{\mathcal M_C\}_C
  \;\text{with }\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \{\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\}_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1},
  \qquad
  \{K_C\}_C\;\text{with }\gamma+\log C(A)
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \{F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\}_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}.
\]
In the primary boundary theorem these correspondences are replaced by the
renormalized coordinate profile of
\Cref{thm:perron-boundary-determinant-product,cor:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.
In the strict-gap specialization, the first correspondence is the class-Mertens
formula together with the Hermitian class transform and the additive scalar
identity \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\).  The second is the strict-gap product
formula after the scalar product tail has been combined with the finite part,
giving the product normalisation \(\gamma+\log C(A)\).  The following lemma records this
two-stage scalar bookkeeping before the determinant-dependence statement.

\begin{lemma}[Scalar normalisation for additive and product constants]
  \label{lem:scalar-normalisation-two-stage}
  For the primitive scalar channel of the base shift,
  \[
    \boxed{\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)}.
  \]
  Moreover the finite-part identity of \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form}
  is equivalently
  \[
    \boxed{
    \mathcal M(A)
    +\sum_{r\ge2}\frac{\Pi_{\mathbf1}(\lambda^{-r})}{r}
    =\gamma+\log C(A)}.
  \]
  Thus additive class-Mertens constants use the trivial determinant data needed
  to compute \(\FP(A)\), while product constants use \(C(A)\) only after the
  scalar Euler-product tail \(\sum_{r\ge2}\Pi_{\mathbf1}(\lambda^{-r})/r\)
  has been added.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The first identity is the scalar orbit-Mertens normalisation: by definition
  \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\).  For the product identity we record the
  scalar calculation here.  From the Euler product
  \(\zeta_A(z)=\prod_{n\ge1}(1-z^n)^{-p_n(A)}\), substitute
  \(z=t/\lambda\), \(0<t<1\), and separate the primitive logarithm:
  \[
    \log\zeta_A(t/\lambda)
    =P_A(t)+\sum_{s\ge2}\frac{\Pi_{\mathbf1}((t/\lambda)^s)}{s}.
  \]
  Since \(C(A)=\lim_{t\uparrow1}(1-t)\zeta_A(t/\lambda)\), taking the logarithm
  of \((1-t)\zeta_A(t/\lambda)\) and then the Abel limit gives
  \[
    \FP(A)=\log C(A)-
      \sum_{r\ge2}\frac{\Pi_{\mathbf1}(\lambda^{-r})}{r}.
  \]
  The tail is absolutely convergent because \(r\ge2\) places the scalar
  primitive series strictly inside the Perron disc.  This is the same identity
  proved again in \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form}, but it is used
  here before the strict-gap product specialization so that the scalar
  normalisation is local to the product proof.
  Adding \(\gamma\) and substituting
  \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\) gives the displayed product normalisation.
\end{proof}

The next proposition is the body-level dependency record for the strict-gap
computational forms in this subsection.  It is deliberately subordinate to the
Perron-boundary determinant spine: the general constants are determined by
\Cref{prop:finite-data-main-chain-closure}, while the linear chain below
records what the boundary theorem becomes after the strict exclusion
\(\eta<\lambda\).

\begin{proposition}[Strict-gap finite determinant specialization for the class constants]
  \label{prop:class-mertens-product-dependency-chain}
  Assume \(\lambda>1\) and the strict twisted spectral gap
  \(\eta=\max_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda\), with the empty
  maximum interpreted as \(0\).  Fix the determinant polynomials
  \(P_\pi(z)=\det(I-zA_\pi)\), the power maps on conjugacy classes, the scalar
  normalisations \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\) and
  \(\alpha_A:=\gamma+
  \log C(A)\), and the strict-gap branch convention of
  \Cref{def:perron-boundary-logarithm-convention}.  Then this specialization of
  \Cref{prop:finite-data-main-chain-closure,cor:strict-gap-specialization}
  determines the older constants as follows:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*]
    \item the open-disc determinant logarithms \(P_\pi\), together with the
    Adams power maps, determine the primitive Peter--Weyl channels and class
    counts;
    \item the strict gap gives the Perron values
    \(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) for \(\varrho\ne\mathbf1\) and hence
    \[
      \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)=
      \frac{|C|}{|G|}\left(\mathcal M(A)+
        \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
          \Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\right);
    \]
    \item the product-tail reduction and the scalar identity of
    \Cref{lem:scalar-normalisation-two-stage} give
    \[
      K_C=
      \exp\!\left(-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\left(\alpha_A+
        \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
          F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\right)\right);
    \]
    \item for each \(\varrho\ne\mathbf1\), the determinant boundary readout is
    \[
      F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})=
      \sum_{r,m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
      \sum_{\substack{\pi\in\Irr(G)\\(r,m,\pi)\ne(1,1,\mathbf1)}}
        a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}\mathcal L_{rm,\pi},
    \]
    where the scalar Perron triple is deleted before endpoint evaluation,
    non-trivial \(rm=1\) terms are Abel limits, and all \(rm\ge2\) terms are
    ordinary interior determinant values.
  \end{enumerate}
  Conversely, after the scalar product normalisation \(\alpha_A\) and the same
  branch chart are fixed, the positive product vector \((K_C)_C\) determines
  the non-trivial fixed-label vector by
  \[
    F_\pi(\lambda^{-1})=
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \left(-\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C-\alpha_A\right)\chi_\pi(C),
    \qquad \pi\ne\mathbf1 .
  \]
  Hence later finite-enclosure, strict-gap coordinate-readout, stability,
  abelian/cyclic support-boundary, and Artin-obstruction statements test or
  reuse this scalar-normalised specialization of the renormalized determinant
  coordinate vector.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The primary finite-data assertion is
  \Cref{prop:finite-data-main-chain-closure}; imposing
  \(\eta<\lambda\) gives \Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization}.  In that branch,
  item~\textup{(a)} is \Cref{prop:main-theorem-i-assembly-map}.  Item~\textup{(b)} is
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit} and the class-indicator Fourier expansion.
  Item~\textup{(c)} is the product constant formula in
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}, whose proof uses the product-tail
  bound together with the scalar normalisation from
  \Cref{lem:scalar-normalisation-two-stage}.
  Item~\textup{(d)} is the Perron-boundary evaluation in
  \Cref{lem:main-iii-boundary-evaluation,prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}.
  The inverse formula is finite character orthogonality for the Hermitian
  class-function inner product.  These cited statements are precisely the
  product-tail reduction, scalar normalisation, and determinant boundary
  readout used in the strict-gap product formula; all later comparison
  statements operate on the resulting vector and therefore are downstream
  consequences of the renormalized spine.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Renormalized product theorem controls the downstream product readouts]
  \label{cor:class-product-main-theorem-primacy}
  The primary Frobenius-class product asymptotic used in this article is
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-frobenius-product}; under the strict-gap hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}, it specializes to
  \[
    P_C(N)=K_C N^{-|C|/|G|}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr),
  \]
  with \(K_C\) and \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) as defined in
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.  Every later product-coordinate
  statement in this subsection factors through the finite vector
  \[
    \bigl(\gamma+\log C(A),\,
      (F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}))_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\bigr)
  \]
  and the Hermitian character table of \(G\).  In particular,
  \Cref{thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity,thm:class-product-minimal-obstruction-skeleton,thm:class-product-novelty-boundary}
  use the same product constant, leading exponent, and Adams-corrected
  fixed-label determinant coordinate \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The product asymptotic, the exponent \(|C|/|G|\), and the two displayed
  formulas for \(K_C\) are exactly the conclusion of
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.  The determinant evaluation of the
  non-trivial product coordinates is the equality
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})=\mathfrak F_\varrho(A,\tau)\) in the same theorem,
  equivalently the Perron-boundary readout of
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}.  The preceding proposition
  shows that the remaining product data are obtained by finite character
  orthogonality from the scalar normalisation and the non-trivial fixed-label
  vector.  The cited downstream statements use only these objects: the
  determinant-coordinate readout in
  \Cref{thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity} is the finite Fourier chart for
  the already constructed constants; the fixed-coordinate support statement in
  \Cref{thm:class-product-minimal-obstruction-skeleton} is a rank statement
  inside that chart; and the boundary statement in
  \Cref{thm:class-product-novelty-boundary} separates the classical exponent
  layer from the same fixed-label vector.  None changes the product
  asymptotic or defines a new value of \(K_C\), proving the factorisation.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Product-constant refinements]
  Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}, fix the
  product scalar normalisation
  \(\alpha_A=\mathcal M(A)+\sum_{r\ge2}\Pi_{\mathbf1}(\lambda^{-r})/r
  =\gamma+\log C(A)\) and the non-trivial
  strict-gap fixed-label vector
  \[
    \mathsf F(A,\tau;G):=
    \bigl(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\bigr)_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}.
  \]
  The strict-gap specialization \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens} gives
  \[
    K_C=
    \exp\!\left(-\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \left(\alpha_A+
        \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
          F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\right)
      \right),
  \]
  so the pair \((\alpha_A,\mathsf F(A,\tau;G))\) is the coordinate source for
  the later product refinements.  The hyperplane readout, assembled finite
  determinant formula and truncation, fixed-coordinate fibre statement, and
  Parseval obstruction budget in
  \Cref{cor:class-product-hyperplane-stability,cor:assembled-adams-frobenius-product-formula,thm:class-product-minimal-obstruction-skeleton,cor:adams-obstruction-bound}
  are obtained by rewriting this displayed formula, substituting
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}, or applying Fourier inversion and
  Parseval in the zero-mean class-function chart.  They add no hypothesis to
  the product asymptotic and no product constant beyond the renormalized
  product coordinates and their strict-gap specialization.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the product-coordinate consequences]
  \label{prop:class-product-consequence-scope}
  Assume the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.  Once the
  scalar product normalisation
  \(\alpha_A=\gamma+\log C(A)\), the positive logarithm branch of
  \((K_C)_C\), and the strict-gap scalar-triple deletion convention of
  \Cref{def:perron-boundary-logarithm-convention} are fixed, every product
  coordinate-readout, stability, finite-truncation, comparison, and Artin-obstruction
  statement in the rest of this subsection is a consequence of the single
  finite Fourier equivalence
  \[
    (K_C)_C
    \longleftrightarrow
    H_K(C):=-\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C-\alpha_A
    \longleftrightarrow
    \bigl(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\bigr)_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}.
  \]
  In particular those statements neither enlarge the product theorem nor
  claim a new Frobenius-class Mertens exponent.  Their role is to make explicit
  the determinant-coordinate readout, the finite-dimensional Parseval error
  budget, and the obstruction to replacing the fixed-label values by periodic
  Artin logarithms.
\end{remark}

Indeed, the first arrow is the definition of the scalar-normalised logarithmic class
  profile after the positive product branch and \(\alpha_A\) have been fixed.
  The second arrow is Hermitian Fourier inversion on class functions, applied
  to the formula for \(K_C\) in
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.  The determinant-coordinate
  interpretation of the resulting vector is
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}, which identifies each
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) with its Adams--M\"obius determinant expression
  in the same Perron-boundary chart.  Fourier inversion and Parseval give the
  later readout, stability, lower-bound, and replacement-error statements, and
  the comparison with the periodic Artin logarithm is obtained by substituting
  \(B_\varrho=\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) into the same finite chart.
  None of these operations changes the asymptotic
  \(P_C(N)=K_CN^{-|C|/|G|}(1+O(N^{-1}))\) or its classical exponent; they only
  analyse the already constructed constants.
