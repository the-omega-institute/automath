\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The problem addressed in this paper is primitive orbit determinant readout
for finite-group extensions of mixing subshifts of finite type. Let
\((X_A^+,\sigma)\) be a mixing subshift with primitive adjacency matrix
\(A\), let \(\lambda=\rho(A)\), and let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a cocycle
into a finite group. For every irreducible representation \(\varrho\)
of \(G\), the twisted transition matrix \(A_\varrho\) records periodic
labels through its traces. These traces are periodic data. The
primitive labels are subtler: when a primitive orbit with label \(g\)
is repeated \(m\) times, its label becomes \(g^m\). Thus primitive
extraction is not ordinary M\"obius inversion in the non-abelian
setting; it must be coupled to the Adams operations
\(\psi^m\phi(g)=\phi(g^m)\) on class functions.

The centre of the paper is the Adams--M\"obius passage from twisted
dynamical determinants to primitive Peter--Weyl channels and then to the
constant coordinates in Frobenius-class Euler products.  The internal
product-tail argument gives the Frobenius-class asymptotic
\[
  P_C(N)=K_C N^{-|C|/|G|}(1+o(1)).
\]
The leading scale in this formula is the classical comparison scale.  The
finite-state problem addressed here is the determinant computation and
rigidity of the constant \(K_C\) after that scale has been separated;
classical Frobenius-class product theorems are cited only to compare the same
exponent and conventions.  Under the strict twisted gap its non-trivial Fourier
coordinates are given by the fixed-label Euler transform
\[
  F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
  =\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-r})}{r},
\]
not by the periodic Artin logarithm \(\mathcal L_\varrho\).  Replacing
\(F_\varrho\) by \(\mathcal L_\varrho\) is already false in the finite
\(S_3\) model of \Cref{app:s3-model-computations}.  The contribution is
therefore the Adams-corrected fixed-label determinant formula for
\(K_C\), its finite recovery, and the obstruction to Artin substitution,
after the product-tail exponent has been separated.

The finite-group argument is organized around three main theorems and one
fixed boundary-data package.  Main Theorem~I recovers primitive channels from
periodic twisted determinants.  Main Theorem~II evaluates the non-trivial
channels at the Perron point under the strict finite spectral exclusion
\(\eta<\lambda\).  Main Theorem~III converts those values, through the
fixed-label Euler transform \(F_\varrho\), into the Adams-corrected constant
coordinates of the Frobenius-class product.  In every Perron-boundary statement
the data are not just determinant polynomials: they are
\[
  \mathfrak D_\partial(A,\tau;G)=
  \bigl(G,\mathrm{Pow}_m,\{P_\pi(z)\}_{\pi\in\Irr(G)},
    \lambda,C(A),\mathfrak b,\eta<\lambda\bigr),
\]
where \(P_\pi(z)=\det(I-zA_\pi)\), \(\mathrm{Pow}_m:g\mapsto g^m\) is the
finite Adams-operation table on conjugacy classes, and \(\mathfrak b\) records
the branch choices for the Abel, interior, and deleted-singular-triple
logarithms used at \(z=\lambda^{-1}\).  The other sections only check
hypotheses, normalise the scalar channel, or compute examples for this tuple.
The class transform used throughout is
the Hermitian character-orthogonality convention: its forward coefficient
is \(\chi_\varrho^\ast(C):=\overline{\chi_\varrho(C)}=\chi_\varrho(C^{-1})\)
for unitary characters, while inverse readout of a
non-trivial Fourier coordinate uses \(\chi_\varrho(C)\) without the star.

For the finite-group theorems, \(A\) is always the primitive integer
adjacency matrix of a finite directed multigraph.  Only in the scalar
normalisation section and scalar appendices may \(A\) be an arbitrary primitive
non-negative real matrix, where \(p_n(A)\) denotes the algebraic M\"obius
coefficient rather than an orbit count.  The scalar finite part of \(\zeta_A\) and the cyclic root-of-unity layer are
used in two specific ways.  The finite part supplies the trivial-channel
normalisation \(\mathcal M(A)\), while root-of-unity sampling is attached to
the canonical cyclic lift in which every edge is labelled by the generator of
\(\ZZ/q\ZZ\), rather than to arbitrary cyclic quotients of the fixed cocycle.
The proof roadmap is
\[
  \begin{array}{c}
    \text{twisted determinants}
    \xrightarrow{\text{trace}}
    \text{periodic channels} \\[2pt]
    \xrightarrow{\text{Adams--M\"obius}}
    \text{primitive channels} \\[2pt]
    \xrightarrow[\eta<\lambda]{z=\lambda^{-1}}
    \text{class Mertens constants}
    \xrightarrow{\text{orthogonality}}
    \text{class profile}.
  \end{array}
\]
Here the Adams--M\"obius arrow is the primitive extraction step; the last
arrow is ordinary character orthogonality after the Perron-point values
have been constructed. The scalar normalisation is recorded in the next
subsection.  The scalar dynamical \(\zeta\)-function
\[
  \zeta_A(z)
  =\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{\Tr(A^n)}{n}z^n\right)
  =\det(I-zA)^{-1}
\]
enters only through the trivial representation channel. Its rationality
and orbit-counting role are classical, going back to
Bowen--Lanford \cite{BowenLanford1970Zeta} and Manning
\cite{Manning1971Axiom}, with further development through Ruelle's
thermodynamic formalism
\cite{Ruelle1976ZetaExpanding,Ruelle1978Thermodynamic} and the
Parry--Pollicott transfer-operator programme
\cite{ParryPollicott1990Zeta}. These citations give context only; the proofs
below use the fixed finite matrices and the stated Perron-point gap.

\subsection{Finite-group main results}

Let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a cocycle into a finite group, and let
\(A_\varrho\) be the twisted transition matrices. For a class function
\(\phi:G\to\CC\) write
\[
  T_{\phi,n}:=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n)}
    \phi(\tau_n(x)),
  \qquad
  \mathcal L_\phi(z):=\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{T_{\phi,n}}{n}z^n,
\]
and
\[
  \Pi_\phi(z):=\sum_{\gamma\in\cP}\phi(\tau(\gamma))z^{|\gamma|}.
\]
For a conjugacy class \(C\subset G\), let \(p_{n,C}\) denote the number
of primitive orbits of length \(n\) whose cocycle label lies in \(C\).
The scalar orbit-Mertens constant \(\mathcal M(A)\) is the
trivial-channel constant defined in \Cref{def:abel-finite-part}; the
  finite-matrix formula behind this normalisation is the calculation in
  \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form}.

\subsection{Contributions and scope}\label{sec:intro-scope-of-results}

The paper solves the following finite-state problem.  For a fixed matrix
extension \((A,\tau,G)\), determine from the twisted determinant family
\(\{\det(I-zA_\varrho):\varrho\in\Irr(G)\}\) the primitive
Peter--Weyl coordinates at the Perron point and hence the constants in
conjugacy-class Mertens sums and Frobenius-class Euler products.

\begin{remark}[Operational form of the gap hypothesis]
  \label{rem:intro-operational-gap}
  If \(G\) is trivial we set \(\eta=0\).  Otherwise the strict twisted
  spectral gap is the finite check
  \(
    \eta=\max_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda
  \).
  Equivalently, by \Cref{lem:twisted-gap-skew-product-peripheral}, the
  skew-product adjacency matrix \(\widetilde A\) has peripheral spectral
  multiset exactly the single simple eigenvalue \(\lambda\); when
  \(\widetilde A\) is irreducible this is the same as primitivity of
  \(\widetilde A\).  This condition is not automatic from primitivity of
  \(A\).  It is a separate finite spectral exclusion on the cocycle.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Class-product calibration for Main Theorem III]
  \label{rem:intro-class-product-calibration}
  Under the hypotheses of Main Theorem~III, the Frobenius-class product
  asymptotic splits into two logically separate layers.  The existence of the
  product asymptotic and the exponent \(|C|/|G|\) are proved here from the
  additive class-Mertens estimate and the explicit product-tail reduction;
  classical Chebotarev--Mertens product theorems are cited for comparison of
  conventions.  More precisely, after the scalar finite-part
  normalisation \(C(A)\) is fixed, the non-trivial contribution to every
  \(K_C\) is exactly the Hermitian class Fourier transform of the fixed-label
  Euler vector
  \(\{F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\}_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\), with
  \[
    F_\varrho(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\varrho(z^r)}{r},
  \]
  and these values are recovered from the finite twisted determinant data by
  the Adams--M\"obius determinant formula.  Replacing this fixed-label vector by
  the periodic Artin logarithm vector
  \(\{\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\}_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\) gives the same
  class-product constants for all conjugacy classes if and only if the
  Peter--Weyl Adams-obstruction vector vanishes.
  The internal product-tail argument in
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens} supplies the product asymptotic,
  the exponent \(|C|/|G|\), and the positive constant
  \[
    K_C=\exp\!\left(-\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \left(\gamma+\log C(A)+
      \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\right)\right).
  \]
  Thus the scalar term \(\gamma+\log C(A)\) is common to all classes and the
  remaining class-dependent term is precisely the Hermitian class Fourier
  transform of the non-trivial fixed-label Euler vector.  The determinant
  recovery of each coordinate \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) is
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}, using the Adams--M\"obius
  determinant expansion with the scalar Perron triple deleted before endpoint
  evaluation.  Finally, \Cref{prop:three-logarithm-product-interface} separates
  the fixed-label Euler transform from the periodic Artin logarithm, and
  \Cref{prop:class-product-comparison,thm:adams-obstruction-class-product}
  prove that the class-product constants obtained by Artin substitution agree
  with the fixed-label constants for every conjugacy class exactly when the
  non-trivial Peter--Weyl obstruction vector
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) has zero class
  Fourier transform, equivalently when all its non-trivial Peter--Weyl
  coordinates vanish.  This is the stated calibration.
\end{remark}

The following summary records the three formulas in one place before the proofs.
\begin{theoremsummary}[Main theorem spine]
  \summaryrefstep\label{stmt:main-theorem-spine}
  Let \(A\) be the primitive non-negative integer adjacency matrix of a finite
  directed multigraph and let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a finite-group edge cocycle.
  For every class function \(\phi\), Main Theorem~I recovers the primitive
  series on \(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\) by
  \[
    \Pi_\phi(z)=
    \sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\,
      \mathcal L_{\psi^m\phi}(z^m),
    \qquad
    \mathcal L_\phi(z)=
    \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}\widehat\phi(\varrho)
      \log\det(I-zA_\varrho)^{-1}.
  \]
  Equivalently the primitive class counts are
  \[
    p_{n,C}=
    \frac{|C|}{|G|\,n}
    \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
    \sum_{m\mid n}\mu(m)
      \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        \Tr(A_\pi^{n/m}).
  \]
  Main Theorem~II adds \(\lambda>1\) and the certified strict gap
  \(\eta<\lambda\).  Then
  \[
    \sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
    =
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}\log N+\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)+O(N^{-1}),
  \]
  with
  \[
    \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)=
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}\biggl(\mathcal M(A)+
      \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
        \Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\biggr).
  \]
  Main Theorem~III uses the same boundary tuple and the fixed-label Euler
  transform \(F_\varrho(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\Pi_\varrho(z^r)/r\), not the periodic
  Artin logarithm, to prove
  \[
    P_C(N)=K_C\,N^{-|C|/|G|}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr),
  \]
  where
  \[
    K_C=\exp\biggl(-\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \biggl(\gamma+\log C(A)+
        \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
          F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\biggr)\biggr).
  \]
  The Perron-boundary determinant value of each non-trivial product coordinate is
  \[
    F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})=
    \sum_{r,m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
    \sum_{\substack{\pi\in\Irr(G)\\(r,m,\pi)\ne(1,1,\mathbf1)}}
      a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}\mathcal L_{rm,\pi},
    \qquad \varrho\ne\mathbf1,
  \]
  with the branch convention of
  \Cref{def:perron-boundary-logarithm-convention}: the scalar Perron triple is
  deleted before endpoint evaluation, non-trivial \(rm=1\) terms use the Abel
  branch, and \(rm\ge2\) terms are ordinary interior values.
\end{theoremsummary}
This summary is proved by
\Cref{prop:main-theorem-i-assembly-map,thm:class-mertens-explicit,thm:fixed-label-euler-transform,thm:class-euler-product-mertens,prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity}.
The comparison with the periodic Artin logarithm is
\Cref{prop:class-product-comparison,thm:adams-obstruction-class-product}; the
finite \(S_3\) rational bounds later give a concrete example, not a hypothesis
of the theorem.

After the main product formula is proved, two short consequences record its
stability and coordinate meaning: lower-order finite tails with zero Abel
contribution do not change \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\), \(K_C\), or
\(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), and the fixed branch-compatible determinant chart
has the expected non-trivial Peter--Weyl coordinate rank.  These are used as
consequences of the product formula, not as additional hypotheses.

As a structural consequence of Main Theorem~II, quotient maps
\(q:G\twoheadrightarrow Q\) intertwine both the periodic logarithms and
primitive series,
\[
  \mathcal L_{q^\ast f}^{G}(z)=\mathcal L_f^{Q}(z),
  \qquad
  \Pi_{q^\ast f}^{G}(z)=\Pi_f^{Q}(z)
  \qquad(|z|<\lambda^{-1}).
\]
For
\[
  \mathcal B_{A,\tau;G}(C):=\frac{|G|}{|C|}\Delta_C(A,\tau;G),
\]
the Peter--Weyl decomposition splits the Perron-point class profile into
its one-dimensional sector and its higher-dimensional sector. The span of
all cyclic quotient observables is exactly the one-dimensional sector;
therefore its projection is the abelian shadow, orthogonal to the genuine
non-abelian defect. Under \(\eta<\lambda\), the full primitive class
profile is generated by cyclic quotient shadows exactly when every
higher-dimensional primitive channel
\(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) vanishes. These assertions are proved in
\Cref{thm:quotient-functoriality,cor:abelian-cyclic-shadow,thm:abelian-shadow-defect,thm:cyclic-detection-boundary}.

Similarly, scalar records formed only from the fixed matrix \(A\) cannot by
themselves recover the non-trivial finite-group profile for a family of
cocycles over the same base.  The projection boundary in
\Cref{thm:scalar-normalisation-projection-boundary} says that such scalar data
determine a single normalised class profile exactly when the non-trivial
Perron-point channels \(\Pi_\varrho^\tau(\lambda^{-1})\),
\(\varrho\ne\mathbf1\), are already independent of \(\tau\).  Thus the scalar
finite-part and cyclic-lift statements below serve only as fixed-matrix
normalisations and comparison tools; \Cref{cor:fixed-matrix-record-no-descent}
gives the corresponding two-cocycle separation.

An explicit \(S_3\)-extension separates the two obstruction types at the
level relevant to Main Theorem~III.  Its fixed-label Euler-transform
bounds
\(F_\varepsilon(1/2)\in(-381/1000,-380/1000)\) and
\(F_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)\in(-923/1000,-922/1000)\) force the product-constant
obstruction to naive Artin substitution.  The accompanying primitive
bounds show that the sign-character trace vanishes but its primitive
value is non-zero by the quadratic Adams mechanism, while the standard
two-dimensional channel gives the rationally verified higher-dimensional shadow
defect. This gives a finite-state obstruction model at the level of the
determinant constants.

The quotient and shadow consequences are proved in
\Cref{thm:quotient-functoriality,thm:scalar-normalisation-projection-boundary,cor:abelian-cyclic-shadow,thm:abelian-shadow-defect,thm:cyclic-detection-boundary,thm:s3-certified-nonabelian-defect}; the canonical cyclic-lift root layer supplies only the scalar comparison boundary and is not an invariant of the original finite-group cocycle.
