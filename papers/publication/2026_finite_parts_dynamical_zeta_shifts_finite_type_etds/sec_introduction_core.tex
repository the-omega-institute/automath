\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The problem addressed in this paper is primitive orbit determinant readout
for finite-group extensions of mixing subshifts of finite type. Let
\((X_A^+,\sigma)\) be a mixing subshift with primitive adjacency matrix
\(A\), let \(\lambda=\rho(A)\), and let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a cocycle
into a finite group. For every irreducible representation \(\varrho\)
of \(G\), the twisted transition matrix \(A_\varrho\) records periodic
labels through its traces. These traces are periodic data. The
primitive labels are subtler: when a primitive orbit with label \(g\)
is repeated \(m\) times, its label becomes \(g^m\). Thus primitive
extraction is not ordinary M\"obius inversion in the non-abelian
setting; it must be coupled to the Adams operations
\(\psi^m\phi(g)=\phi(g^m)\) on class functions.

The centre of the paper is the Adams--M\"obius passage from twisted
dynamical determinants to primitive Peter--Weyl channels and then to the
renormalised constant coordinates in Frobenius-class Euler products.  The
central theorem is the Perron-boundary version: all twisted eigenvalues
\(\lambda\zeta\) on the Perron circle are retained, the multiplicities
\(s_\varrho=m_{\varrho,1}\) determine the logarithmic class exponents
\(\delta_C\), and the constants are computed from reduced determinant finite
parts in the boundary blocks.  The internal product-tail argument gives the
Frobenius-class asymptotic
\[
  P_C(N)=K_C^{\mathrm{ren}} N^{-\delta_C}(1+O(N^{-1})).
\]
When the only boundary collision is the scalar trivial channel this reduces
to the strict-gap specialization with exponent \(|C|/|G|\).  Classical
Frobenius-class product theorems are cited only to compare the same exponent
and conventions in that specialization.  In the general boundary case the
non-trivial Fourier coordinates are given by the renormalised fixed-label
Euler transform
\[
  F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}
  =\lim_{t\uparrow1}
    \left(F_\varrho(t\lambda^{-1})+s_\varrho\log(1-t)\right),
\]
not by the periodic Artin logarithm \(\mathcal L_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\).
Replacing \(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\) by
\(\mathcal L_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\) is already false in the finite
\(S_3\) model of \Cref{app:s3-model-computations}.  The contribution is
therefore the Perron-boundary Adams-corrected fixed-label determinant formula
for \(K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\), its strict-gap recovery, and the obstruction to
Artin substitution.  The scalar, abelian-shadow, and cyclic-lift results later
in the paper supply the projection and normalization data used by this formula.

The finite-group argument is organized around one boundary package and its
strict-gap specialization.  The theorem summary below gives the formulas;
\Cref{subsec:scope-normalizations-support} records the boundary data, branch
normalization, scalar convention, and supporting sections in one place.  The
class transform used throughout is the Hermitian character-orthogonality
convention:
\[
  \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
  :=\overline{\chi_\varrho(C)}
  =\chi_\varrho(C^{-1})
\]
for unitary characters, while inverse readout of a non-trivial Fourier
coordinate uses \(\chi_\varrho(C)\) without the star.

\subsection{Finite-group main results}

Let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a cocycle into a finite group, and let
\(A_\varrho\) be the twisted transition matrices. For a class function
\(\phi:G\to\CC\) write
\[
  T_{\phi,n}:=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n)}
    \phi(\tau_n(x)),
  \qquad
  \mathcal L_\phi(z):=\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{T_{\phi,n}}{n}z^n,
\]
and
\[
  \Pi_\phi(z):=\sum_{\gamma\in\cP}\phi(\tau(\gamma))z^{|\gamma|}.
\]
For a conjugacy class \(C\subset G\), let \(p_{n,C}\) denote the number
of primitive orbits of length \(n\) whose cocycle label lies in \(C\).
The scalar orbit-Mertens constant \(\mathcal M(A)\) is the
trivial-channel constant defined in \Cref{def:abel-finite-part}; the
  finite-matrix formula behind this normalisation is the calculation in
  \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form}.

\subsection{Contributions}\label{sec:intro-scope-of-results}

The paper solves the following finite-state problem.  For a fixed matrix
extension \((A,\tau,G)\), determine from the open-disc twisted determinant
family
\(\{\det(I-zA_\varrho):\varrho\in\Irr(G)\}\), and from the
branch-normalized Perron-boundary coordinate package
\(\mathfrak D_\partial^{\mathrm{ren}}(A,\tau;G)\) when boundary values are
needed, the primitive Peter--Weyl coordinates at the Perron point and hence
the constants in conjugacy-class Mertens sums and Frobenius-class Euler
products.

\begin{remark}[Operational form of the gap hypothesis]
  \label{rem:intro-operational-gap}
  If \(G\) is trivial we set \(\eta=0\).  Otherwise the strict twisted
  spectral gap is the finite check
  \(
    \eta=\max_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda
  \).
  Equivalently, by \Cref{lem:twisted-gap-skew-product-peripheral}, the
  skew-product adjacency matrix \(\widetilde A\) has peripheral spectral
  multiset exactly the single simple eigenvalue \(\lambda\); when
  \(\widetilde A\) is irreducible this is the same as primitivity of
  \(\widetilde A\).  This condition is not automatic from primitivity of
  \(A\).  It is a separate finite spectral exclusion on the cocycle.
\end{remark}

The following summary records the primary formulas in one place before the
proofs.
\begin{theoremsummary}[Main Theorems A--B and Corollaries C--D]
  \summaryrefstep\label{stmt:main-theorem-spine}
  Let \(A\) be the primitive non-negative integer adjacency matrix of a finite
  directed multigraph and let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a finite-group edge cocycle.

  \emph{Main Theorem A: open-disc Adams--M\"obius determinant inversion.}
  For every class function \(\phi\), the primitive series is recovered on
  \(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\) by
  \[
    \Pi_\phi(z)=
    \sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\,
      \mathcal L_{\psi^m\phi}(z^m),
    \qquad
    \mathcal L_\phi(z)=
    \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}\widehat\phi(\varrho)
      \log\det(I-zA_\varrho)^{-1}.
  \]
  Equivalently the primitive class counts are
  \[
    p_{n,C}=
    \frac{|C|}{|G|\,n}
    \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
    \sum_{m\mid n}\mu(m)
      \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        \Tr(A_\pi^{n/m}).
  \]
  \emph{Main Theorem B: Perron-boundary renormalized class and product
  theorem.}
  The Perron-boundary theorem replaces the strict exclusion of boundary
  eigenvalues by the finite data
  \[
    \Gamma_\pi,\quad m_{\pi,\zeta},\quad s_\pi=m_{\pi,1},\quad
    \Lambda_\perp,\quad \Lambda_\partial .
  \]
  With these algebraic multiplicities,
  \[
    \pi_{n,\varrho}(A,\tau)
    =
    \frac{\lambda^n}{n}
      \sum_{\zeta\in\Gamma_\varrho}
        m_{\varrho,\zeta}\zeta^n
    +O(\Lambda_\partial^n/n).
  \]
  Hence
  \[
    \sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
    =
    \delta_C\log N+M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}+O(N^{-1}),
  \]
  with
  \[
    \delta_C=
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)s_\varrho,
    \qquad
    M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}=
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)(s_\varrho\gamma+R_\varrho).
  \]
  The fixed-label Euler transform is renormalized by
  \[
    F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \lim_{t\uparrow1}
      \left(F_\varrho(t\lambda^{-1})+s_\varrho\log(1-t)\right),
  \]
  and is evaluated by the reduced determinant formula
  \[
    F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \sum_{r,m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
      \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}
        a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        B_{rm,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}},
  \]
  where \(k=1\) terms are reduced Perron-boundary finite parts and
  \(k\ge2\) terms are ordinary interior determinant logarithms.  The
  Frobenius-class product theorem is
  \[
    P_C(N)=K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\,N^{-\delta_C}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr),
  \]
  where
  \[
    K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \exp\!\left(
      -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
        \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}
          \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
          (s_\varrho\gamma+F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}})
    \right).
  \]
  \emph{Corollary C: strict-gap specialization.}
  The strict-gap formula with exponent \(|C|/|G|\) is recovered when
  \(s_{\mathbf1}=1\), \(s_\pi=0\) for \(\pi\ne\mathbf1\), and no non-trivial
  peripheral phase occurs.  In that case
  \(\delta_C=|C|/|G|\) and
  \(F_\rho^{\mathrm{ren}}=F_\rho(\lambda^{-1})\) for every non-trivial
  \(\rho\).

  \emph{Corollary D: product-coordinate inversion and Artin obstruction.}
  The product logarithms give the shifted coordinates
  \(s_\pi\gamma+F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}\):
  \[
    H_C^{\mathrm{ren}}:=
    -\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \sum_\pi \chi_\pi^\ast(C)(s_\pi\gamma+F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}),
  \]
  while the exponent profile gives the boundary multiplicities by the
  finite Fourier inversion
  \[
    s_\pi=\sum_C\delta_C\chi_\pi(C).
  \]
  Consequently \((K_C^{\mathrm{ren}})_C\) together with
  \((\delta_C)_C\), equivalently \((s_\pi)_\pi\), recovers the reduced
  fixed-label coordinates:
  \[
    F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \sum_C\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      H_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\chi_\pi(C)-s_\pi\gamma .
  \]
  Thus the pair
  \(\bigl((K_C^{\mathrm{ren}})_C,(\delta_C)_C\bigr)\), equivalently
  \(\bigl((K_C^{\mathrm{ren}})_C,(s_\pi)_\pi\bigr)\), is the lossless
  product-coordinate readout for the reduced fixed-label vector after the
  positive logarithm branch is fixed.  Positive product constants without
  \((\delta_C)_C\) recover only the shifted vector
  \(s_\pi\gamma+F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}\).  The comparison with periodic Artin
  logarithms is the obstruction statement of
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-artin-obstruction}.
\end{theoremsummary}
This summary is proved by the Perron-boundary theorem
\Cref{thm:perron-boundary-determinant-product}, the strict-gap specialization
\Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization}, the product-coordinate readout
\Cref{cor:renormalized-coordinate-readout}, and the renormalized Artin
obstruction \Cref{thm:renormalized-artin-obstruction}.  The finite \(S_3\)
rational bounds later give a concrete example of the obstruction.

\subsection{Scope, normalizations, and support material}
\label{subsec:scope-normalizations-support}

All later scope qualifications refer to the following conventions.  The main
object is a fixed finite SFT cocycle \((A,\tau,G)\).  The paper proves
determinant readout of primitive Peter--Weyl channels, the Perron-boundary
class-Mertens constants, and the Adams-corrected fixed-label
Frobenius-product constants from the open-disc determinant family and the
branch-normalized boundary tuple.  It does not use the determinant polynomials
alone at the Perron boundary: the Abel branches, reduced finite parts,
peripheral multiplicities \(s_\pi\), and scalar/product logarithm
normalization are part of the stated input.

The named interfaces are deliberately few.  Main Theorem~A is the open-disc
Adams--M\"obius primitive inversion assembled in
\Cref{prop:main-theorem-i-assembly-map}; Main Theorem~B is the
Perron-boundary determinant and product theorem
\Cref{thm:perron-boundary-determinant-product}; Corollary~C is the strict-gap
specialization \Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization}; and Corollary~D is the
product-coordinate inversion together with the Artin-substitution obstruction,
namely
\Cref{cor:renormalized-coordinate-readout,thm:renormalized-artin-obstruction}.
Later Fourier inversion, stability, rank, comparison, appendix
reproducibility-enclosure, and Artin-substitution statements are consequences or computational
specializations of this coordinate package.  In particular they do not
introduce a second Frobenius-product constant, a new class exponent, or a
branch-free determinant invariant.

The Frobenius-class product asymptotic is derived here from the renormalized
class-Mertens estimate and the explicit product-tail reduction.  Its exponent
is the Perron-boundary density \(\delta_C\), and its positive constant is the
Hermitian class Fourier transform of the reduced fixed-label vector
\((s_\varrho\gamma+F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}})_\varrho\).  Classical
Chebotarev--Mertens product theorems are cited for comparison of conventions
and for the strict-gap scale, where \(\delta_C=|C|/|G|\).  Replacing
\(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\) by the periodic Artin logarithm
\(\mathcal L_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\) gives the same class-product constants
for all conjugacy classes if and only if the renormalized Peter--Weyl
Adams-obstruction vector vanishes, as proved in
\Cref{thm:renormalized-artin-obstruction}.

The scalar, eta-gap, abelian-shadow, cyclic-quotient, cyclic-lift, and \(S_3\)
sections have the following roles.  The scalar finite part fixes the
trivial-channel normalization.  The eta-gap criterion identifies the
strict-gap specialization and its equality obstruction.  Abelian and cyclic
shadow statements are orthogonal projection facts about the completed
primitive profile.  The canonical cyclic-lift root layer is a fixed-matrix
comparison model, not a quotient invariant of the chosen cocycle.  The
rational \(S_3\) certificates are local exact-arithmetic witnesses for the
Adams correction and the failure of naive Artin substitution.

For the finite-group theorems, \(A\) is the primitive integer adjacency matrix
of a finite directed multigraph.  Only the scalar normalisation material allows
an arbitrary primitive non-negative real matrix, where \(p_n(A)\) denotes the
algebraic M\"obius coefficient.  The scalar finite part enters only through the
trivial representation channel.  The cyclic root-of-unity layer is attached to
the canonical cyclic lift, not to arbitrary cyclic quotients of the fixed
cocycle.
