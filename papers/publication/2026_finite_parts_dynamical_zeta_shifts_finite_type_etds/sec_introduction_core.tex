\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The problem addressed in this paper is primitive orbit determinant readout
for finite-group extensions of mixing subshifts of finite type. Let
\((X_A^+,\sigma)\) be a mixing subshift with primitive adjacency matrix
\(A\), let \(\lambda=\rho(A)\), and let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a cocycle
into a finite group. For every irreducible representation \(\varrho\)
of \(G\), the twisted transition matrix \(A_\varrho\) records periodic
labels through its traces. These traces are periodic data. The
primitive labels are subtler: when a primitive orbit with label \(g\)
is repeated \(m\) times, its label becomes \(g^m\). Thus primitive
extraction is not ordinary M\"obius inversion in the non-abelian
setting; it must be coupled to the Adams operations
\(\psi^m\phi(g)=\phi(g^m)\) on class functions.

The centre of the paper is the Adams--M\"obius passage from twisted
dynamical determinants to primitive Peter--Weyl channels and then to the
renormalised constant coordinates in Frobenius-class Euler products.  The
main theorem is the Perron-boundary version: all twisted eigenvalues
\(\lambda\zeta\) on the Perron circle are retained, the multiplicities
\(s_\varrho=m_{\varrho,1}\) determine the logarithmic class exponents
\(\delta_C\), and the constants are computed from reduced determinant finite
parts in the boundary blocks.  The internal product-tail argument gives the
Frobenius-class asymptotic
\[
  P_C(N)=K_C^{\mathrm{ren}} N^{-\delta_C}(1+O(N^{-1})).
\]
When the only boundary collision is the scalar trivial channel this reduces
to the strict-gap specialization with exponent \(|C|/|G|\).  Classical
Frobenius-class product theorems are cited only to compare the same exponent
and conventions in that specialization.  In the general boundary case the
non-trivial Fourier coordinates are given by the renormalised fixed-label
Euler transform
\[
  F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}
  =\lim_{t\uparrow1}
    \left(F_\varrho(t\lambda^{-1})+s_\varrho\log(1-t)\right),
\]
not by the periodic Artin logarithm \(\mathcal L_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\).
Replacing \(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\) by
\(\mathcal L_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\) is already false in the finite
\(S_3\) model of \Cref{app:s3-model-computations}.  The contribution is
therefore the Perron-boundary Adams-corrected fixed-label determinant formula
for \(K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\), its strict-gap recovery, the cyclic-lift boundary
example, and the obstruction to Artin substitution.

The finite-group argument is organized around one Perron-boundary package and
its strict-gap specialization.  The open-disc Adams--M\"obius theorem recovers
primitive channels from periodic twisted determinants.  The boundary package
then records the peripheral sets \(\Gamma_\pi\), multiplicities
\(m_{\pi,\zeta}\), non-oscillatory weights \(s_\pi\), and a finite
semisimplicity certificate; it proves the primitive-channel asymptotic, the
renormalized class Mertens theorem, the reduced fixed-label determinant
formula, and the Frobenius-class product
\[
  P_C(N)=K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}N^{-\delta_C}(1+O(N^{-1})).
\]
The strict finite spectral exclusion \(\eta<\lambda\) appears only after this
as the specialization in which the only Perron collision is the scalar trivial
channel.  The boundary data are not just determinant polynomials:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  \mathfrak D_\partial^{\mathrm{ren}}(A,\tau;G)=
  \Bigl(&G,\mathrm{Pow}_m,\{P_\pi(z)\}_{\pi\in\Irr(G)},\lambda,
    \{\Gamma_\pi\}_\pi,\{m_{\pi,\zeta}\}_{\pi,\zeta},\{s_\pi\}_\pi,\\
    &\Lambda_\perp,\Lambda_\partial,\mathfrak s_\partial,
    \mathfrak b_\partial^{\mathrm{ren}},\mathfrak n_\partial\Bigr),
  \end{aligned}
\]
where \(P_\pi(z)=\det(I-zA_\pi)\), \(\mathrm{Pow}_m:g\mapsto g^m\) is the
finite Adams-operation table on conjugacy classes,
\(\mathfrak s_\partial\) is the semisimplicity certificate,
\(\mathfrak b_\partial^{\mathrm{ren}}\) records reduced finite-part and
interior determinant branches, and \(\mathfrak n_\partial\) records the scalar
normalization.  The other sections check hypotheses, normalise the scalar
channel, or compute examples for this tuple.
The class transform used throughout is
the Hermitian character-orthogonality convention: its forward coefficient
is \(\chi_\varrho^\ast(C):=\overline{\chi_\varrho(C)}=\chi_\varrho(C^{-1})\)
for unitary characters, while inverse readout of a
non-trivial Fourier coordinate uses \(\chi_\varrho(C)\) without the star.

For the finite-group theorems, \(A\) is always the primitive integer
adjacency matrix of a finite directed multigraph.  Only in the scalar
normalisation section and scalar appendices may \(A\) be an arbitrary primitive
non-negative real matrix, where \(p_n(A)\) denotes the algebraic M\"obius
coefficient rather than an orbit count.  The scalar finite part of \(\zeta_A\) and the cyclic root-of-unity layer are
used in two specific ways.  The finite part supplies the trivial-channel
normalisation \(\mathcal M(A)\), while root-of-unity sampling is attached to
the canonical cyclic lift in which every edge is labelled by the generator of
\(\ZZ/q\ZZ\), rather than to arbitrary cyclic quotients of the fixed cocycle.
The proof roadmap is
\[
  \begin{array}{c}
    \text{twisted determinants}
    \xrightarrow{\text{trace}}
    \text{periodic channels} \\[2pt]
    \xrightarrow{\text{Adams--M\"obius}}
    \text{primitive channels} \\[2pt]
    \xrightarrow{\text{Perron boundary}}
    \text{renormalized class/product constants}
    \xrightarrow{\text{orthogonality}}
    \text{class profile}.
  \end{array}
\]
Here the Adams--M\"obius arrow is the primitive extraction step; the last
arrow is ordinary character orthogonality after the Perron-point values
have been constructed. The scalar normalisation is recorded in the next
subsection.  The scalar dynamical \(\zeta\)-function
\[
  \zeta_A(z)
  =\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{\Tr(A^n)}{n}z^n\right)
  =\det(I-zA)^{-1}
\]
enters only through the trivial representation channel. Its rationality
and orbit-counting role are classical, going back to
Bowen--Lanford \cite{BowenLanford1970Zeta} and Manning
\cite{Manning1971Axiom}, with further development through Ruelle's
thermodynamic formalism
\cite{Ruelle1976ZetaExpanding,Ruelle1978Thermodynamic} and the
Parry--Pollicott transfer-operator programme
\cite{ParryPollicott1990Zeta}. These citations give context only; the proofs
below use the fixed finite matrices and the stated Perron-point gap.

\subsection{Finite-group main results}

Let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a cocycle into a finite group, and let
\(A_\varrho\) be the twisted transition matrices. For a class function
\(\phi:G\to\CC\) write
\[
  T_{\phi,n}:=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n)}
    \phi(\tau_n(x)),
  \qquad
  \mathcal L_\phi(z):=\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{T_{\phi,n}}{n}z^n,
\]
and
\[
  \Pi_\phi(z):=\sum_{\gamma\in\cP}\phi(\tau(\gamma))z^{|\gamma|}.
\]
For a conjugacy class \(C\subset G\), let \(p_{n,C}\) denote the number
of primitive orbits of length \(n\) whose cocycle label lies in \(C\).
The scalar orbit-Mertens constant \(\mathcal M(A)\) is the
trivial-channel constant defined in \Cref{def:abel-finite-part}; the
  finite-matrix formula behind this normalisation is the calculation in
  \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form}.

\subsection{Contributions and scope}\label{sec:intro-scope-of-results}

The paper solves the following finite-state problem.  For a fixed matrix
extension \((A,\tau,G)\), determine from the twisted determinant family
\(\{\det(I-zA_\varrho):\varrho\in\Irr(G)\}\) the primitive
Peter--Weyl coordinates at the Perron point and hence the constants in
conjugacy-class Mertens sums and Frobenius-class Euler products.

\begin{remark}[Operational form of the gap hypothesis]
  \label{rem:intro-operational-gap}
  If \(G\) is trivial we set \(\eta=0\).  Otherwise the strict twisted
  spectral gap is the finite check
  \(
    \eta=\max_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda
  \).
  Equivalently, by \Cref{lem:twisted-gap-skew-product-peripheral}, the
  skew-product adjacency matrix \(\widetilde A\) has peripheral spectral
  multiset exactly the single simple eigenvalue \(\lambda\); when
  \(\widetilde A\) is irreducible this is the same as primitivity of
  \(\widetilde A\).  This condition is not automatic from primitivity of
  \(A\).  It is a separate finite spectral exclusion on the cocycle.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Class-product calibration for the Perron-boundary product theorem]
  \label{rem:intro-class-product-calibration}
  The Frobenius-class product asymptotic is derived here from the
  renormalized class-Mertens estimate and the explicit product-tail reduction.
  Its exponent is the Perron-boundary density \(\delta_C\), and its positive
  constant is the Hermitian class Fourier transform of the reduced
  fixed-label vector
  \((s_\varrho\gamma+F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}})_\varrho\).  Classical
  Chebotarev--Mertens product theorems are cited for comparison of
  conventions and for the strict-gap scale, where
  \(\delta_C=|C|/|G|\).  Replacing
  \(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\) by the periodic Artin logarithm
  \(\mathcal L_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\) gives the same class-product
  constants for all conjugacy classes if and only if the renormalized
  Peter--Weyl Adams-obstruction vector vanishes, as proved in
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-artin-obstruction}.
\end{remark}

The following summary records the primary formulas in one place before the
proofs.
\begin{theoremsummary}[Perron-boundary determinant and product theorem]
  \summaryrefstep\label{stmt:main-theorem-spine}
  Let \(A\) be the primitive non-negative integer adjacency matrix of a finite
  directed multigraph and let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a finite-group edge cocycle.
  For every class function \(\phi\), the open-disc Adams--M\"obius theorem
  recovers the primitive series on \(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\) by
  \[
    \Pi_\phi(z)=
    \sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\,
      \mathcal L_{\psi^m\phi}(z^m),
    \qquad
    \mathcal L_\phi(z)=
    \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}\widehat\phi(\varrho)
      \log\det(I-zA_\varrho)^{-1}.
  \]
  Equivalently the primitive class counts are
  \[
    p_{n,C}=
    \frac{|C|}{|G|\,n}
    \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
    \sum_{m\mid n}\mu(m)
      \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        \Tr(A_\pi^{n/m}).
  \]
  The Perron-boundary theorem replaces the strict exclusion of boundary
  eigenvalues by the finite data
  \[
    \Gamma_\pi,\quad m_{\pi,\zeta},\quad s_\pi=m_{\pi,1},\quad
    \Lambda_\perp,\quad \Lambda_\partial .
  \]
  Under the semisimplicity certificate,
  \[
    \pi_{n,\varrho}(A,\tau)
    =
    \frac{\lambda^n}{n}
      \sum_{\zeta\in\Gamma_\varrho}
        m_{\varrho,\zeta}\zeta^n
    +O(\Lambda_\partial^n/n).
  \]
  Hence
  \[
    \sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
    =
    \delta_C\log N+M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}+O(N^{-1}),
  \]
  with
  \[
    \delta_C=
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)s_\varrho,
    \qquad
    M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}=
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)(s_\varrho\gamma+R_\varrho).
  \]
  The fixed-label Euler transform is renormalized by
  \[
    F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \lim_{t\uparrow1}
      \left(F_\varrho(t\lambda^{-1})+s_\varrho\log(1-t)\right),
  \]
  and is evaluated by the reduced determinant formula
  \[
    F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \sum_{r,m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
      \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}
        a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        \mathcal B_{rm,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}},
  \]
  where \(k=1\) terms are reduced Perron-boundary finite parts and
  \(k\ge2\) terms are ordinary interior determinant logarithms.  The
  Frobenius-class product theorem is
  \[
    P_C(N)=K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\,N^{-\delta_C}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr),
  \]
  where
  \[
    K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \exp\!\left(
      -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
        \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}
          \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
          (s_\varrho\gamma+F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}})
    \right).
  \]
  The coordinate readout is lossless:
  \[
    H_C^{\mathrm{ren}}:=
    -\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \sum_\pi \chi_\pi^\ast(C)(s_\pi\gamma+F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}),
  \]
  and
  \[
    F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \sum_C\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      H_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\chi_\pi(C)-s_\pi\gamma .
  \]
  The strict-gap formula with exponent \(|C|/|G|\) is recovered when
  \(s_{\mathbf1}=1\), \(s_\pi=0\) for \(\pi\ne\mathbf1\), and no non-trivial
  peripheral phase occurs.
\end{theoremsummary}
This summary is proved by
\Cref{thm:peripheral-primitive-channel-asymptotic,thm:renormalized-class-mertens,thm:renormalized-fixed-label-determinant,thm:renormalized-frobenius-product,thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.
The comparison with the periodic Artin logarithm is the renormalized
obstruction of \Cref{thm:renormalized-artin-obstruction}; the finite \(S_3\)
rational bounds later give a concrete example, not a hypothesis of the
theorem.

After the Perron-boundary product and readout theorems
\Cref{thm:renormalized-frobenius-product,thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout}
are proved, the later strict-gap formulas are used only as computational
specializations.  In that branch, lower-order finite tails with zero Abel
contribution do not change the recovered constants, and the fixed
branch-compatible determinant chart has the expected non-trivial Peter--Weyl
coordinate rank.  These statements provide stability and finite-coordinate
bookkeeping for the specialization, not additional hypotheses or competing
product theorems.

As a structural consequence of the Adams--M\"obius primitive inversion,
quotient maps
\(q:G\twoheadrightarrow Q\) intertwine both the periodic logarithms and
primitive series,
\[
  \mathcal L_{q^\ast f}^{G}(z)=\mathcal L_f^{Q}(z),
  \qquad
  \Pi_{q^\ast f}^{G}(z)=\Pi_f^{Q}(z)
  \qquad(|z|<\lambda^{-1}).
\]
For
\[
  \mathcal B_{A,\tau;G}(C):=\frac{|G|}{|C|}\Delta_C(A,\tau;G),
\]
the Peter--Weyl decomposition splits the Perron-point class profile into
its one-dimensional sector and its higher-dimensional sector. The span of
all cyclic quotient observables is exactly the one-dimensional sector;
therefore its projection is the abelian shadow, orthogonal to the genuine
non-abelian defect. In the strict-gap specialization supplied by
\Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization}, the full primitive class profile is
generated by cyclic quotient shadows exactly when every higher-dimensional
primitive channel
\(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) vanishes. These assertions are proved in
\Cref{thm:quotient-functoriality,cor:abelian-cyclic-shadow,thm:abelian-shadow-defect,thm:cyclic-detection-boundary}.

Similarly, scalar records formed only from the fixed matrix \(A\) cannot by
themselves recover the non-trivial finite-group profile for a family of
cocycles over the same base.  The scalar support boundary in
\Cref{thm:finite-part-scalar-interface-no-descent} says that such scalar data
determine a single normalised class profile exactly when the non-trivial
Perron-point channels \(\Pi_\varrho^\tau(\lambda^{-1})\),
\(\varrho\ne\mathbf1\), are already independent of \(\tau\).  Thus the scalar
finite-part and cyclic-lift statements below serve only as fixed-matrix
normalisations and comparison tools.

An explicit \(S_3\)-extension separates the two obstruction types at the
level relevant to the strict-gap specialization of the renormalized product
theorem.  Its fixed-label Euler-transform
bounds
\(F_\varepsilon(1/2)\in(-381/1000,-380/1000)\) and
\(F_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)\in(-923/1000,-922/1000)\) force the product-constant
obstruction to naive Artin substitution.  The accompanying primitive
bounds show that the sign-character trace vanishes but its primitive
value is non-zero by the quadratic Adams mechanism, while the standard
two-dimensional channel gives the rationally verified higher-dimensional shadow
defect. This gives a finite-state obstruction model at the level of the
determinant constants.

The quotient and shadow consequences used by this obstruction are proved in
\Cref{thm:quotient-functoriality,cor:abelian-cyclic-shadow,thm:abelian-shadow-defect,thm:cyclic-detection-boundary,thm:s3-certified-nonabelian-defect}; the canonical cyclic-lift root layer supplies only the scalar comparison boundary and is not an invariant of the original finite-group cocycle.
