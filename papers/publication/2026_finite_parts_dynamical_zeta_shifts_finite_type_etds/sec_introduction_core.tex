\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The problem addressed in this paper is primitive orbit reconstruction
for finite-group extensions of mixing subshifts of finite type. Let
\((X_A^+,\sigma)\) be a mixing subshift with primitive adjacency matrix
\(A\), let \(\lambda=\rho(A)\), and let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a cocycle
into a finite group. For every irreducible representation \(\varrho\)
of \(G\), the twisted transition matrix \(A_\varrho\) records periodic
labels through its traces. These traces are periodic data. The
primitive labels are subtler: when a primitive orbit with label \(g\)
is repeated \(m\) times, its label becomes \(g^m\). Thus primitive
extraction is not ordinary M\"obius inversion in the non-abelian
setting; it must be coupled to the Adams operations
\(\psi^m\phi(g)=\phi(g^m)\) on class functions.

The centre of the paper is the Adams--M\"obius passage from twisted
dynamical determinants to primitive Peter--Weyl channels and then to the
constants in Frobenius-class Euler products.  The classical
Frobenius-class Mertens asymptotic supplies the exponent
\(|C|/|G|\):
\[
  P_C(N)=K_C N^{-|C|/|G|}(1+o(1)).
\]
The finite-state problem addressed here is the determinant computation and
rigidity of the constant \(K_C\) after that classical asymptotic layer has
been separated.  Under the strict twisted gap its non-trivial Fourier
coordinates are given by the fixed-label Euler transform
\[
  F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
  =\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-r})}{r},
\]
not by the periodic Artin logarithm \(\mathcal L_\varrho\).  Replacing
\(F_\varrho\) by \(\mathcal L_\varrho\) is already false in the finite
\(S_3\) model of \Cref{app:s3-model-computations}.  The contribution is
therefore the Adams-corrected fixed-label determinant formula for
\(K_C\), its finite recovery, and the obstruction to Artin substitution,
after the classical class-Mertens exponent has been separated.

The finite-group argument is organized around three main theorem packages.
Main Theorem~I is the Adams--M\"obius inversion theorem: twisted determinant
logarithms determine the primitive Peter--Weyl channels.  Main
Theorem~II is the Perron-point theorem: after the finite-state gap
\(\eta<\lambda\) is imposed, those primitive channels evaluate at
\(z=\lambda^{-1}\) and give the additive conjugacy-class Mertens
constants.  Main Theorem~III is the product theorem: the same data, now
through the fixed-label Euler transform \(F_\varrho\), give the positive
Frobenius-class Euler-product constants.  The remaining results support this
chain: eta-gap diagnostics verify the finite-dimensional hypothesis; quotient
and shadow results describe functorial loss of information; scalar finite
parts give the trivial-channel normalisation; and the finite rational
enclosures make the determinant values in the \(S_3\) witness reproducible.
The class transform used throughout is
the Hermitian character-orthogonality convention: its forward coefficient
is \(\chi_\varrho^\ast(C):=\overline{\chi_\varrho(C)}\), equivalently
\(\chi_\varrho(C^{-1})\) for unitary characters, while inverse readout of a
non-trivial Fourier coordinate uses \(\chi_\varrho(C)\) without the star.

The scalar finite part of \(\zeta_A\) and the cyclic root-of-unity layer are
used in two specific ways.  The finite part supplies the trivial-channel
normalisation \(\mathcal M(A)\), while root-of-unity sampling is attached to
the canonical cyclic lift in which every edge is labelled by the generator of
\(\ZZ/q\ZZ\), rather than to arbitrary cyclic quotients of the fixed cocycle.
The logical direction of the proofs is
\[
  \begin{array}{c}
    \text{twisted determinants}
    \xrightarrow{\text{trace}}
    \text{periodic channels} \\[2pt]
    \xrightarrow{\text{Adams--M\"obius}}
    \text{primitive channels} \\[2pt]
    \xrightarrow[\eta<\lambda]{z=\lambda^{-1}}
    \text{class Mertens constants}
    \xrightarrow{\text{orthogonality}}
    \text{class profile}.
  \end{array}
\]
Here the Adams--M\"obius arrow is the primitive extraction step; the last
arrow is ordinary character orthogonality after the Perron-point values
have been constructed. The scalar normalisation is recorded in the next subsection.

The proof chain is short: determinant traces give periodic Peter--Weyl
channels; Adams--M\"obius inversion extracts fixed primitive labels; the strict
twisted gap permits evaluation at \(z=\lambda^{-1}\); and character
orthogonality converts those values into class Mertens and class-product
constants.  The later quotient, eta-gap, scalar, and finite-enclosure sections
serve this chain by checking hypotheses or computing its ingredients in finite
models.  The scalar dynamical \(\zeta\)-function
\[
  \zeta_A(z)
  =\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{\Tr(A^n)}{n}z^n\right)
  =\det(I-zA)^{-1}
\]
enters only through the trivial representation channel. Its rationality
and orbit-counting role are classical, going back to
Bowen--Lanford \cite{BowenLanford1970Zeta} and Manning
\cite{Manning1971Axiom}, with further development through Ruelle's
thermodynamic formalism
\cite{Ruelle1976ZetaExpanding,Ruelle1978Thermodynamic} and the
Parry--Pollicott transfer-operator programme
\cite{ParryPollicott1990Zeta}. These citations give context only; the proofs
below use the fixed finite matrices and the stated Perron-point gap.

\subsection{Finite-group main results}

Let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a cocycle into a finite group, and let
\(A_\varrho\) be the twisted transition matrices. For a class function
\(\phi:G\to\CC\) write
\[
  T_{\phi,n}:=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n)}
    \phi(\tau_n(x)),
  \qquad
  \mathcal L_\phi(z):=\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{T_{\phi,n}}{n}z^n,
\]
and
\[
  \Pi_\phi(z):=\sum_{\gamma\in\cP}\phi(\tau(\gamma))z^{|\gamma|}.
\]
For a conjugacy class \(C\subset G\), let \(p_{n,C}\) denote the number
of primitive orbits of length \(n\) whose cocycle label lies in \(C\).
The scalar orbit-Mertens constant \(\mathcal M(A)\) is the
trivial-channel constant defined in \Cref{def:abel-finite-part}; the
  finite-matrix formula behind this normalisation is the calculation in
  \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form}.

\subsection{Contributions and scope}\label{sec:intro-scope-of-results}

The paper solves the following finite-state problem.  For a fixed matrix
extension \((A,\tau,G)\), determine from the twisted determinant family
\(\{\det(I-zA_\varrho):\varrho\in\Irr(G)\}\) the primitive
Peter--Weyl coordinates at the Perron point and hence the constants in
conjugacy-class Mertens sums and Frobenius-class Euler products.

The main results are organized as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
  \item Main Theorem~I gives the finite Adams--M\"obius inversion formula
  that recovers the primitive series \(\Pi_\phi(z)\) from the determinant
  logarithms on \(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\).  The Adams operation
  \(\psi^m\phi(g)=\phi(g^m)\) is forced by the repetition rule for primitive
  labels.
  \item Main Theorem~II assumes the strict twisted gap \(\eta<\lambda\) and
  evaluates the non-trivial primitive channels at \(z=\lambda^{-1}\), giving
  the Perron-point class-Mertens constants by character orthogonality.
  \item Main Theorem~III proves the Adams-corrected determinant formula for the
  Frobenius-class product constant.  The standard Frobenius-class product
  theorem supplies the existence of the product asymptotic and the exponent
  \(|C|/|G|\); the finite-state argument here identifies the remaining
  constant.  Its non-trivial coordinates are the fixed-label Euler
  transform values
  \[
    F_\phi(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\phi(z^r)}{r},
    \qquad
    \Pi_\phi(z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal P}
         \phi(\tau(\gamma))z^{|\gamma|},
  \]
  rather than the periodic Artin logarithm
  \(\mathcal L_\phi(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}T_{\phi,n}z^n/n\).
\end{enumerate}

Thus the product result has a classical leading layer and a finite-state
constant layer.  Product existence and the exponent \(|C|/|G|\) are imported
from the Chebotarev--Mertens orbit-counting picture, while the paper proves the
determinant recovery of the fixed-label Euler vector, the resulting formula for
\(K_C\), and the finite obstruction to Artin substitution.  The local
comparison in \Cref{prop:three-logarithm-product-interface}, the product
comparison in \Cref{prop:class-product-comparison}, and the calibration theorem
\Cref{thm:class-product-novelty-boundary} make this division explicit.

The remaining sections supply the normalisations and examples needed for this
chain.  Scalar finite parts fix the trivial-channel normalization; quotient and
shadow arguments identify the coordinates visible under abelian observation;
eta-gap tests give finite checks for the Perron-point hypothesis; and the
\(S_3\) computation gives a concrete Artin-substitution obstruction.  For a
fixed extension \((A,\tau,G)\), the central formulas use only the matrices
\(A_\varrho\), the character table, the Adams coefficients for the power maps
\(g\mapsto g^m\), the Perron data \((\lambda,\eta)\), and the scalar constant
\(\mathcal M(A)\).  The trace identity and Adams--M\"obius extraction give Main
Theorem~I; the strict gap permits the Perron-point evaluations of Main
Theorem~II; and the product-tail together with the fixed-label determinant
calculation gives Main Theorem~III.

\begin{proposition}[Class-product calibration]
  \label{prop:intro-class-product-calibration}
  Under the hypotheses of Main Theorem~III, the scalar-normalised
  Frobenius-class product constants are exactly the Hermitian class Fourier
  transform of the non-trivial fixed-label Euler vector.  More precisely, for
  each conjugacy class \(C\subset G\),
  \[
    -\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C-(\gamma+\log C(A))
    =
    \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
      \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}),
  \]
  and, after the scalar normalisation \(C(A)\) is fixed, the class-product
  constant vector \((K_C)_C\) is equivalent by finite Peter--Weyl inversion to
  the non-trivial fixed-label Euler vector
  \((F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}))_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\).  The comparison with the
  periodic Artin logarithm is consequently a comparison of this fixed-label
  vector after the classical exponent \(|C|/|G|\) has already been separated.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  The displayed formula is the product constant formula of
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}, with the determinant evaluation of
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) supplied by
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity}.
  Since the irreducible characters form an orthonormal basis for class
  functions on the finite group \(G\), the Hermitian class Fourier transform on
  the non-trivial class-function subspace is invertible; this gives the stated
  equivalence after the scalar coordinate \(C(A)\) has been fixed.  The sign and
  branch conventions used for this inversion are recorded in
  \Cref{lem:class-product-sign-normalisation-conventions,lem:class-product-hermitian-normalisation}.
  Finally \Cref{prop:class-product-comparison,thm:adams-obstruction-class-product}
  identify the difference between the fixed-label Euler vector and the periodic
  Artin logarithm, while
  \Cref{thm:class-product-novelty-boundary} separates this determinant-level
  contribution from the classical exponent layer.
\end{proof}

Under the hypotheses of Main Theorem~III the product constants satisfy
\[
  -\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C-(\gamma+\log C(A))
  =
  \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
    \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
\]
for each conjugacy class \(C\), with
\(\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)=\overline{\chi_\varrho(C)}\).  Thus, after the scalar
normalisation \(C(A)\) is fixed, the non-trivial fixed-label Euler vector is
equivalent to the class-product constant vector by Peter--Weyl inversion.
The sign normalisation, inverse Fourier statement, determinant rigidity, and
Artin-substitution comparison are proved in
\Cref{lem:class-product-sign-normalisation-conventions,lem:class-product-hermitian-normalisation,prop:class-product-comparison,thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity,thm:adams-obstruction-class-product}.

Main Theorem~II adds \(\lambda>1\) and the strict finite-dimensional
spectral gap
\[
  \eta:=\max_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda,
\]
with the empty maximum interpreted as \(0\) when \(G\) is trivial.  Under
this hypothesis the non-trivial primitive channels have Abel-path values at
\(z=\lambda^{-1}\), and the conjugacy-class Mertens constants
\(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) are obtained by character orthogonality.
Main Theorem~III then inserts those constants into the Frobenius-class Euler
product and gives the Adams-corrected constant \(K_C\).  The classical
Chebotarev and class-Mertens results supply the exponent \(|C|/|G|\) and the
existence of the leading product asymptotic; the new contribution here is
the finite determinant recovery of the constants after that leading term has
been separated.

The inputs for Main Theorems~I--III are the finite adjacency matrix \(A\), the
finite group \(G\), the twisted matrices \(A_\varrho\), the character table and
Adams coefficients of \(G\), the Perron root \(\lambda\), and, for the
Perron-point statements, the strict gap \(\eta<\lambda\).  Scalar normalisation
enters the product formula through \(C(A)\) and
\(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\), proved later from the scalar determinant in
\Cref{sec:finite-part}.  Main Theorem~I is assembled in
\Cref{prop:main-theorem-i-assembly-map}; Main Theorem~II is
\Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit,thm:class-mertens-fourier}; and Main
Theorem~III is
\Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens,prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity}.
In Main Theorem~III every determinant logarithm reaches \(K_C\) through the
fixed-label coordinates \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\),
  \(\varrho\ne\mathbf1\).  The scalar Perron triple
  \((r,m,\pi)=(1,1,\mathbf1)\) has coefficient
  \(a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}=0\) in every non-trivial coordinate, by
  \Cref{lem:fixed-label-singular-triple-deletion}; hence it is removed before the
  boundary determinant evaluation in
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,cor:main-iii-boundary-determinant-convention}.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-state reduction]
  \label{prop:intro-finite-state-transfer-split}
  The proof of Main Theorems~I--III is closed inside the finite-state data
  \((A,\tau,G)\).  Its required inputs are the finite matrices
  \(A_\varrho\), the character table and Adams coefficients of the finite group
  \(G\), the Perron root \(\lambda\), the strict finite-dimensional gap
  \(\eta<\lambda\) for Perron-point statements, and the scalar finite-part
  normalisation \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\).  H\"older/Ruelle transfer
  operators, infinite-dimensional Fredholm determinant conventions, resonance
  continuation, and inverse reconstruction problems are natural extensions of
  the finite-state theory but are not hypotheses in the proof of these three
  theorems.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Main Theorem~I is exactly the finite twisted trace identity, determinant
  logarithm, Adams--M\"obius inversion, and Peter--Weyl extraction assembled in
  \Cref{prop:main-theorem-i-assembly-map}.  Main Theorem~II adds only the
  finite spectral condition \(\eta<\lambda\) and uses the finite-matrix
  Perron-point estimates and class Fourier inversion of
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit,prop:class-mertens-deviations,thm:class-mertens-fourier}.
  Main Theorem~III uses the product-tail reduction, fixed-label Euler transform,
  and finite determinant boundary evaluation in
  \Cref{prop:class-euler-product-main-chain-reduction,thm:fixed-label-euler-transform,thm:class-euler-product-mertens,prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,cor:main-iii-boundary-determinant-convention}.
  The scalar term is supplied by \Cref{sec:finite-part}, and
  \Cref{prop:perron-boundary-stability} shows that lower-order finite tails do
  not alter the constants used by Main Theorems~II--III.  The cited inputs are
  finite matrices, finite-group character operations, scalar finite parts, and
  Perron--Frobenius theory for finite non-negative matrices, so the proof is
  independent of analytic transfer-operator machinery.
\end{proof}

Under the hypotheses of Main Theorem~III the class product has the form
\[
  P_C(N)=K_C N^{-|C|/|G|}(1+O(N^{-1}))
\]
for every conjugacy class \(C\subset G\).  The exponent \(|C|/|G|\) is the
Frobenius-density term inherited from the classical orbit-counting theory.  The
finite-state contribution of this paper is the non-scalar constant coordinate
\[
  -\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C-(\gamma+
  \log C(A))
  =
  \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
    \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}),
\]
where the fixed-label Euler vector is recovered from the finite twisted
determinant family by the Adams--M\"obius determinant formula.  The body proves
this as \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens,prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity} and records the Artin-logarithm
comparison in \Cref{prop:class-product-comparison,thm:adams-obstruction-class-product}.

The scalar finite part of \(\zeta_A\), the canonical cyclic-lift comparison
calculation, and the rational interval checks in the appendices play
auxiliary roles.  The scalar calculation supplies the trivial-channel value
\(\mathcal M(A)\), while the canonical cyclic lift is only a fixed-matrix
comparison model.  Full fixed-matrix reconstruction, scalar-boundary
certificates, secondary spectral-edge asymptotics, general observation-budget
refinements, and sibling-paper determinant packages are not used as inputs to
the theorem chain.  The lower-order-tail stability lemma in
\Cref{prop:perron-boundary-stability} shows that compensated
lower-order tails vanish in the constants used by Main Theorems~II--III. Thus
the proofs below use only finite-matrix determinant identities, finite-group
character orthogonality, Adams--M\"obius inversion, and Perron--Frobenius
theory for finite non-negative matrices.

The following three statements are orientation summaries for the proof chain;
the formal theorem and proof labels are the body results cited after each
summary.  Main Theorem~I converts periodic twisted determinants into primitive Peter--Weyl
channels by Adams--M\"obius inversion.  Main Theorem~II evaluates the
resulting non-trivial primitive channels at the Perron point under
\(\eta<\lambda\).  Main Theorem~III identifies the Euler-product constants by
the fixed-label transform \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), proves its determinant
formula, and compares it with the periodic Artin logarithm
\(\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\).

\begin{theoremsummary}[Main Theorem I: finite determinant recovery of primitive channels]
  \phantomsection\label{stmt:main-theorem-i-adams-mobius-linearisation}
  \label{stmt:intro-adams-mobius-linearisation}
  \label{stmt:intro-finite-group}
  \label{stmt:intro-adams-mobius}
  Let \(A\) be primitive and let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a finite-group edge
  cocycle.  For every class function \(\phi:G\to\CC\), the primitive
  label series \(\Pi_\phi(z)\) on \(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\) is determined by
  the finite family of twisted determinant polynomials
  \[
    \{\det(I-zA_\varrho):\varrho\in\Irr(G)\}
  \]
  together with the Adams operations \(\psi^m\phi(g)=\phi(g^m)\).  More
  precisely,
  \[
    \Pi_\phi(z)=
    \sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\,
      \mathcal L_{\psi^m\phi}(z^m),
    \qquad
    \mathcal L_\phi(z)=
    \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}\widehat\phi(\varrho)
      \log\det(I-zA_\varrho)^{-1}.
  \]
  Consequently the primitive Frobenius-class counts \(p_{n,C}\) are
  obtained by finite character-table operations and coefficient
  extraction from the same determinant family.
\end{theoremsummary}
\begin{proof}
  The formal proof is \Cref{prop:main-theorem-i-assembly-map}: the twisted trace
  identity and determinant logarithm give \(\mathcal L_\phi\), Adams--M\"obius
  inversion gives \(\Pi_\phi\), and Peter--Weyl inversion gives the class counts.
\end{proof}

\begin{theoremsummary}[Main Theorem II: Perron-point class Mertens constants]
  \phantomsection\label{stmt:main-theorem-ii-class-mertens-constants}
  \label{stmt:intro-class-mertens-constants}
  \label{stmt:intro-class-mertens}
  Assume \(\lambda>1\) and the twisted spectral gap
  \(\eta=\max_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda\).  Then for
  every conjugacy class \(C\subset G\),
  \[
    \sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
    =
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}\log N+\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)+O(N^{-1}),
  \]
  where
  \[
    \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)=
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}\biggl(\mathcal M(A)+
      \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
        \Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\biggr).
  \]
  The non-trivial Perron-point values
  \(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) are the absolutely convergent boundary
  values, equivalently the Abel regular values of the primitive
  Peter--Weyl series under the strict finite-matrix gap
  \(\eta<\lambda\).  Their Hermitian class Fourier transform recovers the full
  normalised deviation profile and gives the Parseval identity proved in
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-fourier}.
\end{theoremsummary}
\begin{proof}
  This is the combination of \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit}, which gives
  the asymptotic and constant, with
  \Cref{prop:class-mertens-deviations,thm:class-mertens-fourier}, which gives
  the Fourier--Parseval form.
  These results assume \(\lambda>1\) and the strict twisted
  gap \(\eta<\lambda\), and the boundary values used are the regular
  non-trivial Perron-point values furnished by Main Theorem~I.
\end{proof}

\begin{theoremsummary}[Main Theorem III: Adams-corrected Frobenius-class Euler-product constants]
  \phantomsection\label{stmt:main-theorem-iii-adams-frobenius-products}
  \label{stmt:intro-adams-frobenius-products}
  Under the hypotheses of Main Theorem~II, namely \(\lambda>1\) and the strict
  twisted spectral gap \(\eta<\lambda\), define the fixed-label Euler transform
  \[
    F_\varrho(z):=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\varrho(z^r)}{r}.
  \]
  This is distinct from the periodic Artin logarithm
  \(\mathcal L_\varrho(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\Pi_{\psi^r\varrho}(z^r)/r\) in
  general.  For every conjugacy class \(C\),
  \[
    P_C(N):=
    \prod_{\substack{\gamma\ \mathrm{primitive}\; |\gamma|\le N\\
      \tau(\gamma)\in C}}
      (1-\lambda^{-|\gamma|})
    =K_C\,N^{-|C|/|G|}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr),
  \]
  with
  \[
    K_C=\exp\biggl(-\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \biggl(\gamma+\log C(A)+
        \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
          F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\biggr)\biggr).
  \]
  The factor \(|C|/|G|\) multiplies the entire Hermitian class-indicator
  bracket, including the non-trivial fixed-label sum.  Each
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) is computable from the same finite
  twisted determinant data as Main Theorem~I by substituting the
  Adams--M\"obius determinant formula into the displayed series.  Thus
  the product asymptotic is calibrated by the classical Frobenius-class
  Mertens theorem, while the finite-state assertion here is the primitive-label
  fixed-label Euler determinant recovery of \(K_C\) and the resulting
  obstruction to replacing \(F_\varrho\) by the periodic Artin logarithm.  Moreover
  \Cref{cor:class-product-classical-layer-uniqueness} shows that, after the
  classical exponent and scalar normalisation are fixed, no other
  branch-compatible non-trivial Peter--Weyl vector yields the same class-product
  constants.  As a structural refinement, the non-trivial
  Peter--Weyl boundary is a rank-sharp coordinate record for the
  scalar-normalised product constants in fixed branch-compatible determinant
  coordinates:
  \Cref{cor:class-product-minimal-obstruction-skeleton} proves that every
  real linear record, after the scalar baseline is fixed, determining all
  branch-compatible product constants has rank
  at least the number of non-trivial conjugacy-class degrees of freedom,
  and that the Adams-corrected determinant coordinates attain this bound.
  This coordinate statement is made in the fixed determinant chart and concerns
  the constant layer after the classical leading asymptotic is fixed.

  The body proof proceeds through the additive Perron-point theorem
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit,prop:class-mertens-deviations,thm:class-mertens-fourier},
  the product-tail reduction and fixed-label product formula
  \Cref{prop:class-euler-product-main-chain-reduction,prop:class-product-internal-k-certificate,thm:fixed-label-euler-transform,thm:class-euler-product-mertens},
  the branch-compatible determinant evaluation
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,prop:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm,cor:class-euler-product-determinant-evaluation},
  and the scalar-normalised coordinate comparison
  \Cref{thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity,prop:class-product-comparison,thm:adams-obstruction-class-product}.
\end{theoremsummary}
\begin{proof}
  This summary is a synopsis of the body theorem chain; the detailed
  arguments are the following body results.  The fixed-label Euler
  transform is established in
  \Cref{thm:fixed-label-euler-transform}; applying it class by class gives the
  product asymptotic and the displayed expression for \(K_C\) in
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.  The claim that every value
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), and hence every \(K_C\), is recovered from the
  finite twisted determinant data is proved in
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,prop:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm,thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity,cor:assembled-adams-frobenius-product-formula}.
  The scalar-normalised coordinate and rank statements are precisely
  \Cref{cor:class-product-minimal-obstruction-skeleton}, while the comparison
  with the periodic Artin logarithm is
  \Cref{prop:class-product-comparison,thm:adams-obstruction-class-product}.
  Thus the proof uses only the chain cited in the statement; the finite
  \(S_3\) rational bounds enter later only as explicit witnesses to the
  obstruction and not as hypotheses for the product theorem.
\end{proof}

The product constants are stable under lower-order finite tails:
\Cref{prop:perron-boundary-stability} shows that any such
refinement whose compensated tails vanish leaves \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\),
\(K_C\), and the recovered values \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) unchanged.
It is an asymptotic comparison lemma for the main constants.

The corollary-level refinements
\Cref{cor:class-product-minimal-obstruction-skeleton,cor:adams-obstruction-bound}
then assemble the already proved constants into the fixed finite
determinant-coordinate Peter--Weyl vector and a Parseval obstruction bound.
They are structural consequences and finite witnesses for the scalar-normalised
product constants in that branch-compatible chart.

\paragraph{Proof guide.}
  The trace identity underlying Main Theorem~I is
  \Cref{lem:twisted-trace-identity}; the determinant linearisation and
  Adams--M\"obius primitive inversion are
  \Cref{thm:class-function-linearisation,cor:class-function-adams-mobius}.
  The determinant-germ formula and coefficient forms are
  \Cref{cor:primitive-peter-weyl-determinant,thm:primitive-peter-weyl-trace,thm:class-count-trace-recovery}.  Main Theorem~II is proved by
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit,prop:class-mertens-deviations,thm:class-mertens-fourier}.  Main Theorem~III is
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}; the proof is organized in
  \Cref{prop:main-iii-product-constant-factorisation}, which separates the additive
  Mertens input, the product-tail reduction, the fixed-label Fourier
  coordinates, and the determinant readout. The determinant evaluation in
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,cor:class-euler-product-determinant-evaluation}
  identifies the constant without adding hypotheses to the main theorems, and
  \Cref{cor:class-product-classical-layer-uniqueness} proves that no different
  branch-compatible non-trivial coordinate vector gives the same constants once
  the classical exponent and scalar normalisation are held fixed.
  The eta-gap criterion \Cref{thm:eta-gap-rigidity} explains when the
  strict-gap hypothesis fails for the fixed finite cocycle; its one-dimensional loop-character and
  perfect-loop-group consequences are
  \Cref{cor:eta-gap-character-classification,cor:eta-gap-perfect-group}.
  These results give finite-state tests for the Perron-point hypothesis.

  The lower-order-tail comparison lemma is
  \Cref{prop:perron-boundary-stability}: once the determinant
  coordinates and product constants have been fixed by the main chain,
  compensated finite tails with zero Abel contribution add no Perron-boundary
  coordinate, Mertens constant, or Euler-product constant.

As a structural consequence of Main Theorem~II, quotient maps
\(q:G\twoheadrightarrow Q\) intertwine both the periodic logarithms and
primitive series,
\[
  \mathcal L_{q^\ast f}^{G}(z)=\mathcal L_f^{Q}(z),
  \qquad
  \Pi_{q^\ast f}^{G}(z)=\Pi_f^{Q}(z)
  \qquad(|z|<\lambda^{-1}).
\]
For
\[
  \mathcal B_{A,\tau;G}(C):=\frac{|G|}{|C|}\Delta_C(A,\tau;G),
\]
the Peter--Weyl decomposition splits the Perron-point class profile into
its one-dimensional sector and its higher-dimensional sector. The span of
all cyclic quotient observables is exactly the one-dimensional sector;
therefore its projection is the abelian shadow, orthogonal to the genuine
non-abelian defect. Under \(\eta<\lambda\), the full primitive class
profile is generated by cyclic quotient shadows exactly when every
higher-dimensional primitive channel
\(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) vanishes. These assertions are proved in
\Cref{thm:quotient-functoriality,cor:abelian-cyclic-shadow,thm:abelian-shadow-defect,thm:cyclic-detection-boundary}.

Similarly, a scalar record formed only from the fixed matrix \(A\), such
as \(\FP(A)\), \(\mathcal M(A)\), the reduced polynomial \(F_A\), the
branch-lifted cyclic-lift record \(\{\Psi_q(A)\}_{q\ge1}\), or a torsion
layer \(\{F_A(\omega_q^j)\}_{0\le j<q}\), cannot by itself recover the
non-trivial finite-group profile for a family of cocycles over the same
base matrix. The precise projection boundary is
\Cref{thm:scalar-normalisation-projection-boundary}: scalar data determine
a single normalised class profile across such a family exactly when all
non-trivial Perron-point channels
\(\Pi_\varrho^\tau(\lambda^{-1})\), \(\varrho\ne\mathbf1\), are already
independent of \(\tau\). Thus the scalar finite-part and cyclic-lift
statements below serve as fixed-matrix normalisations and comparison tools; the
two-cocycle separation is \Cref{cor:fixed-matrix-record-no-descent}.

An explicit \(S_3\)-extension separates the two obstruction types at the
level relevant to Main Theorem~III.  Its fixed-label Euler-transform
bounds
\(F_\varepsilon(1/2)\in(-381/1000,-380/1000)\) and
\(F_\rho(1/2)\in(-923/1000,-922/1000)\) force the product-constant
obstruction to naive Artin substitution.  The accompanying primitive
bounds show that the sign-character trace vanishes but its primitive
value is non-zero by the quadratic Adams mechanism, while the standard
two-dimensional channel gives the rationally verified higher-dimensional shadow
defect. This gives a finite-state obstruction model at the level of the
determinant constants.

The quotient/shadow consequence is proved in
\Cref{thm:quotient-functoriality,thm:scalar-normalisation-projection-boundary,cor:fixed-matrix-record-no-descent,cor:abelian-cyclic-shadow,thm:abelian-shadow-defect,thm:cyclic-detection-boundary,thm:s3-certified-nonabelian-defect}; the canonical cyclic-lift root layer supplies the scalar comparison boundary.
