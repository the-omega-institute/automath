\section{Scalar boundary checks}
\label{app:scalar-boundaries}

This appendix records the scalar finite-part facts used to police the boundary
between fixed-matrix normalisation and finite-group class data.  The scalar
quantity \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\) supplies the trivial-channel
normalisation in Main Theorems~II--III.  The non-trivial class data are instead
the fixed-label Perron values determined by the twisted determinants of
\((A,\tau,G)\).  The results below make this separation explicit: scalar
variation and scalar truncation are useful finite-matrix tools, but they do not
descend to the non-trivial fixed-label product coordinates unless those
coordinates are already constant or vanish.
Accordingly this appendix supports the scalar boundary used by
\Cref{thm:scalar-normalisation-projection-boundary}; it does not supply an
eta-gap criterion, a class-product determinant formula, or a replacement for
the Adams--M\"obius finite-group inversion.

\begin{theorem}[Scalar finite part under local parameter variation]
  \label{thm:finite-part-holomorphic-variation}
  Let \(U\subset\CC\) be a domain and let
  \(\theta\mapsto A(\theta)\in M_d(\CC)\) be holomorphic.  Suppose that
  \(\lambda(\theta)\) is a holomorphic simple eigenvalue of \(A(\theta)\) on
  \(U\), that \(|\lambda(\theta)|>1\), and that every other eigenvalue \(\nu\)
  of \(A(\theta)\) satisfies \(|\nu|<|\lambda(\theta)|\).  Fix
  \(\theta_0\in U\).  After shrinking to a simply connected neighbourhood
  \(V\Subset U\) of \(\theta_0\), the scalar finite part admits a holomorphic
  branch
  \[
    \mathfrak{FP}_{V}(\theta)
    =
    \log C(\theta)
    +\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}
      \log\det(I-\lambda(\theta)^{-m}A(\theta))^{-1},
  \]
  where
  \[
    C(\theta):=
    \lim_{z\to\lambda(\theta)^{-1}}
      (1-\lambda(\theta)z)\det(I-zA(\theta))^{-1}.
  \]
  The series converges normally on \(V\).  If \(A(\theta)\) is primitive
  non-negative for real \(\theta\in V\cap\RR\), with Perron root
  \(\lambda(\theta)>1\), then
  \(\mathfrak{FP}_{V}(\theta)=\FP(A(\theta))\) for the compatible real
  logarithm branch.  Moreover, on \(V\),
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    \frac{d}{d\theta}\mathfrak{FP}_{V}(\theta)
    &=
    \frac{d}{d\theta}\log C(\theta)  \\
    &\quad+
    \sum_{m\ge2}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}
      \Tr\!\left(
        (I-\lambda(\theta)^{-m}A(\theta))^{-1}
        \frac{d}{d\theta}
          \bigl(\lambda(\theta)^{-m}A(\theta)\bigr)
      \right),
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  and the differentiated series also converges normally on compact subsets of
  \(V\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Since \(\lambda(\theta_0)\) is simple and spectrally separated from the
  remaining eigenvalues, analytic perturbation theory gives, after shrinking
  \(U\) if necessary, a holomorphic spectral projection for this eigenvalue and
  a number \(\kappa\in(0,1)\) such that the non-Perron spectrum of
  \(A(\theta)\) lies in \(|\nu|\le\kappa|\lambda(\theta)|\) on a neighbourhood
  \(V\Subset U\) of \(\theta_0\).  We also shrink \(V\) so that it is simply
  connected and \(|\lambda(\theta)|\ge\lambda_0>1\).

  Factor
  \[
    \det(I-zA(\theta))=(1-\lambda(\theta)z)D(\theta,z),
    \qquad
    D(\theta,z):=\frac{\det(I-zA(\theta))}{1-\lambda(\theta)z}.
  \]
  The removable singularity at \(z=\lambda(\theta)^{-1}\) is removed by the
  simple spectral projection, so \(D\) is holomorphic near
  \(\{(\theta,\lambda(\theta)^{-1}):\theta\in V\}\).  The spectral separation
  gives \(D(\theta,\lambda(\theta)^{-1})\ne0\), hence
  \(C(\theta)=D(\theta,\lambda(\theta)^{-1})^{-1}\) is holomorphic and
  non-vanishing.  Since \(V\) is simply connected, \(
  \log C(\theta)\) has a holomorphic branch.

  For \(m\ge2\), the matrix \(I-\lambda(\theta)^{-m}A(\theta)\) is invertible.
  The Perron eigenvalue contributes \(1-\lambda(\theta)^{1-m}\ne0\), and every
  other eigenvalue \(\nu\) contributes a factor with
  \[
    |\nu\lambda(\theta)^{-m}|
    \le \kappa|\lambda(\theta)|^{1-m}
    \le \kappa\lambda_0^{1-m}<1.
  \]
  Thus the determinant logarithms in the series are holomorphic on \(V\), after
  choosing their branches from the base point \(\theta_0\).

  Let \(K\Subset V\).  Put
  \[
    L:=\inf_{\theta\in K}|\lambda(\theta)|>1,
    \qquad
    B:=\sup_{\theta\in K}\norm{A(\theta)}.
  \]
  For all large \(m\), \(\norm{\lambda(\theta)^{-m}A(\theta)}\le BL^{-m}\le1/2\)
  uniformly on \(K\).  The identity
  \[
    \log\det(I-X)^{-1}=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\Tr(X^j)}{j}
  \]
  for \(\norm X<1\) gives
  \[
    \left|
      \log\det(I-\lambda(\theta)^{-m}A(\theta))^{-1}
    \right|
    \le d\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{(BL^{-m})^j}{j}
    \le 2dB L^{-m},
  \]
  uniformly on \(K\).  The Weierstrass test proves normal convergence.  Applying
  the same bound on a slightly larger compact subset and using Cauchy's formula
  gives normal convergence of the differentiated series on \(K\).  Termwise
  differentiation is therefore allowed, and
  \[
    \frac{d}{d\theta}\log\det B(\theta)^{-1}
    =-\Tr(B(\theta)^{-1}B'(\theta))
  \]
  with \(B(\theta)=I-\lambda(\theta)^{-m}A(\theta)\) gives the displayed
  derivative.

  Finally, for real primitive non-negative matrices the base branch can be
  chosen to agree with the positive real reduced determinant branch.  The
  fixed-matrix closed form \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form} then
  identifies the holomorphic expression with \(\FP(A(\theta))\) along the real
  parameter branch.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Scalar perturbations do not create class corrections]
  \label{prop:scalar-boundary-no-class-correction}
  Assume the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit}.  Let \(V\) be a
  holomorphic perturbation neighbourhood for the scalar finite part of \(A\) as
  in \Cref{thm:finite-part-holomorphic-variation}.  Suppose one tries to replace
  all class Mertens constants by scalar perturbations, in the sense that there
  are complex numbers \(b_C\), depending only on the conjugacy class \(C\), for
  which
  \[
    \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)=b_C+\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathfrak{FP}_{V}(\theta_0)
  \]
  at the base point \(A=A(\theta_0)\).  Then necessarily
  \[
    b_C=\frac{|C|}{|G|}\gamma
      +\frac{|C|}{|G|}
        \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
          \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}).
  \]
  In particular, the scalar branch accounts only for the trivial Perron-channel
  term.  The Peter--Weyl correction vanishes for all classes if and only if
  \(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})=0\) for every non-trivial
  \(\varrho\in\Irr(G)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Along the real primitive branch through the base point,
  \Cref{thm:finite-part-holomorphic-variation} gives
  \(\mathfrak{FP}_{V}(\theta_0)=\FP(A)\).  The scalar normalisation in
  \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form} gives
  \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\).  Subtracting
  \((|C|/|G|)\FP(A)\) from the class constant formula
  \eqref{eq:class-mertens-constant} in \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit} yields
  the displayed expression for \(b_C\).

  Hence the scalar branch supplies exactly the universal factor
  \((|C|/|G|)\FP(A)\); all non-scalar class information is the finite
  Peter--Weyl sum over non-trivial irreducible representations.  If every
  non-trivial Perron-point value vanishes, this correction is zero for every
  class after subtracting the universal Euler constant contribution.  Conversely,
  if the correction is zero for every conjugacy class, then the class function
  \[
    C\longmapsto
    \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
      \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
  \]
  is identically zero.  Irreducible characters form an orthonormal basis for
  class functions on \(G\), so every non-trivial Fourier coefficient
  \(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Scalar records do not determine class products]
  \label{thm:scalar-class-product-no-descent}
  Fix a primitive base matrix \(A\) with Perron root \(\lambda>1\), fix a finite
  group \(G\), and let
  \[
    \mathcal T_{\mathrm{gap}}(A,G)
    :=\{\tau:E_A\to G:\eta(\tau)<\lambda\}.
  \]
  Let \(\mathscr T\subset\mathcal T_{\mathrm{gap}}(A,G)\) be non-empty.  For
  \(\tau\in\mathscr T\), write \(K_C(A,\tau;G)\) for the positive product
  constants of \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.  If a scalar
  fixed-matrix record \(\mathcal R(A)\) determines all normalized class-product
  constants on \(\mathscr T\), then every non-trivial fixed-label value
  \(F_\varrho^\tau(\lambda^{-1})\), \(\varrho\ne\mathbf1\), is independent of
  \(\tau\) on \(\mathscr T\).  More precisely, for any
  \(\tau,\tau'\in\mathscr T\),
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-product-no-descent-distance}
    \sum_C\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \left|
        -\frac{|G|}{|C|}
        \log\frac{K_C(A,\tau;G)}{K_C(A,\tau';G)}
      \right|^2
    =
    \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
      \left|
        F_\varrho^\tau(\lambda^{-1})
        -F_\varrho^{\tau'}(\lambda^{-1})
      \right|^2,
  \end{equation}
  with the logarithms taken on the real positive branch.  Consequently scalar
  data descend to the class-product package only on fibres where the
  non-trivial fixed-label Perron vector is already constant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}, the positive product constants
  satisfy the logarithmic formula \eqref{eq:class-euler-product-k-f}.  The
  scalar part \(\gamma+\log C(A)\) is the same for \(\tau\) and \(\tau'\),
  because it depends only on \(A\).  Subtracting the two normalized logarithmic
  formulas therefore gives, for each conjugacy class \(C\),
  \[
    -\frac{|G|}{|C|}
    \log\frac{K_C(A,\tau;G)}{K_C(A,\tau';G)}
    =
      \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
        \bigl(
          F_\varrho^\tau(\lambda^{-1})
          -F_\varrho^{\tau'}(\lambda^{-1})
        \bigr).
  \]
  The irreducible characters form an orthonormal basis for class functions with
  inner product
  \[
    \langle f,h\rangle=\sum_C\frac{|C|}{|G|}f(C)\overline{h(C)}.
  \]
  Applying Parseval to the displayed class function gives
  \eqref{eq:scalar-product-no-descent-distance}.

  If \(\mathcal R(A)\) determines all normalized class-product constants on
  \(\mathscr T\), then those normalized constants are the same for any two
  cocycles in the same scalar fibre; here the whole family has the same scalar
  fibre because \(A\) is fixed.  The left side of
  \eqref{eq:scalar-product-no-descent-distance} is then zero, so every
  non-trivial fixed-label difference on the right is zero.  Conversely, if the
  non-trivial fixed-label vector is constant, the same formula shows that the
  normalized product vector is constant.  Thus scalar descent is possible
  exactly on such fibres.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Effective scalar finite-part truncation]
  \label{thm:logM-truncation-bound}
  Let \(A\) be a primitive non-negative \(d\times d\) matrix with Perron root
  \(\lambda>1\), and put
  \[
    K_{\mathrm{tr}}(A):=
    \sup_{j\ge1}\frac{|\Tr(A^j)|}{\lambda^j}.
  \]
  For \(M\ge2\), define the \(M\)-term determinant truncation
  \[
    \FP_M(A):=
    \log C(A)+\sum_{2\le m\le M}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}
      \log\det(I-\lambda^{-m}A)^{-1}.
  \]
  Then
  \[
    |\FP(A)-\FP_M(A)|
    \le
    \frac{K_{\mathrm{tr}}(A)\lambda^{-M}}
      {(M+1)(1-\lambda^{-M})(1-\lambda^{-1})}.
  \]
  Consequently the same bound controls the scalar orbit-Mertens
  normalisation:
  \[
    |\mathcal M(A)-(\gamma+\FP_M(A))|
    \le
    \frac{K_{\mathrm{tr}}(A)\lambda^{-M}}
      {(M+1)(1-\lambda^{-M})(1-\lambda^{-1})}.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form},
  \[
    \FP(A)-\FP_M(A)
    =
    \sum_{m>M}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}
      \log\det(I-\lambda^{-m}A)^{-1}.
  \]
  For \(m\ge2\), the spectral radius of \(\lambda^{-m}A\) is
  \(\lambda^{1-m}<1\), and hence
  \[
    \log\det(I-\lambda^{-m}A)^{-1}
    =
    \sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\Tr(A^j)}{j}\lambda^{-mj}.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    \left|
      \log\det(I-\lambda^{-m}A)^{-1}
    \right|
    &\le
    K_{\mathrm{tr}}(A)
      \sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\lambda^j\lambda^{-mj}}{j}  \\
    &=
    K_{\mathrm{tr}}(A)
      \sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\lambda^{-(m-1)j}}{j}
    =
    K_{\mathrm{tr}}(A)\bigl[-\log(1-\lambda^{1-m})\bigr].
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  Since \(m>M\), one has \(\lambda^{1-m}\le\lambda^{-M}\).  The elementary
  inequality \(-\log(1-x)\le x/(1-x)\), \(0\le x<1\), gives
  \[
    -\log(1-\lambda^{1-m})
    \le
    \frac{\lambda^{1-m}}{1-\lambda^{-M}}.
  \]
  Hence
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    |\FP(A)-\FP_M(A)|
    &\le
    \frac{K_{\mathrm{tr}}(A)}{1-\lambda^{-M}}
      \sum_{m>M}\frac{\lambda^{1-m}}{m} \\
    &\le
    \frac{K_{\mathrm{tr}}(A)}{(M+1)(1-\lambda^{-M})}
      \sum_{m=M+1}^{\infty}\lambda^{1-m}  \\
    &=
    \frac{K_{\mathrm{tr}}(A)\lambda^{-M}}
      {(M+1)(1-\lambda^{-M})(1-\lambda^{-1})}.
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  The assertion for \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\) follows by adding the same
  constant \(\gamma\) to both finite-part quantities.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite scalar normalisation certificate]
  \label{cor:scalar-finite-part-finite-io-certificate}
  Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:logM-truncation-bound}, fix a tolerance
  \(\epsilon>0\).  The finite certificate inputs are the matrix \(A\), its
  Perron root \(\lambda>1\), the scalar normalising constant \(\log C(A)\), the
  determinant polynomial \(\det(I-zA)\), and a trace majorant
  \(K_{\mathrm{tr}}(A)\).  Choose a truncation level \(M\ge2\) for which
  \[
    B_M(A):=
    \frac{K_{\mathrm{tr}}(A)\lambda^{-M}}
      {(M+1)(1-\lambda^{-M})(1-\lambda^{-1})}
    \le \epsilon/2 .
  \]
  If \(I_M\subset\RR\) is any rational interval enclosing \(\FP_M(A)\), with
  numerical radius \(R_{\mathrm{num}}\le\epsilon/2-B_M(A)\), then
  \[
    \FP(A)
    \in
    I_M+[-B_M(A)-R_{\mathrm{num}},\,B_M(A)+R_{\mathrm{num}}].
  \]
  After adding \(\gamma\), the same interval encloses \(\mathcal M(A)\).  This
  scalar certificate is a finite normalisation record only; by
  \Cref{thm:scalar-class-product-no-descent}, it certifies class-product
  constants only after the non-trivial fixed-label Perron vector has been
  supplied separately or is already constant on the cocycle fibre.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The truncation theorem gives \(|\FP(A)-\FP_M(A)|\le B_M(A)\).  The interval
  \(I_M\) contains the computed value \(\FP_M(A)\) with radius
  \(R_{\mathrm{num}}\).  The triangle inequality therefore places \(\FP(A)\) in
  the displayed Minkowski sum.  The choice
  \(B_M(A)+R_{\mathrm{num}}\le\epsilon/2+(\epsilon/2-B_M(A))=\epsilon\)
  gives the requested tolerance.  Adding the constant \(\gamma\) translates the
  interval and hence encloses \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\).  The final
  sentence is exactly the no-descent conclusion of
  \Cref{thm:scalar-class-product-no-descent}: the scalar certificate depends
  only on \(A\), while the non-trivial product coordinates are the
  fixed-label Perron values.
\end{proof}
