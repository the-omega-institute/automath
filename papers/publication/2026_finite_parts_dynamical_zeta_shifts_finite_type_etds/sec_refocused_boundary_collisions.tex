\section{Boundary-point collisions and radial recovery}
\label{sec:boundary-collisions}

The full represented determinant family determines the primitive
length--class profile by \Cref{prop:comparison-dictionary}.  A finite vector
of radial class Euler values is substantially coarser.  We first make this
loss explicit at one Perron-boundary radius and then prove finite recovery for
binary extensions.

\begin{theorem}[Exact radial-profile collision on a fixed \(C_2\)-extension]
\label{thm:c2-boundary-collision}
Let \(G=C_2=\{e,s\}\), with non-trivial character
\(\varepsilon(s)=-1\), and let the base adjacency matrix be
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&4&0&0\\
 1&1&0&2\\
 0&0&0&4\\
 0&2&1&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
There are two labellings \(\tau,\tau':E(\Gamma)\to C_2\) on the same marked
directed multigraph whose sign blocks are respectively
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&-2&0&0\\
 -1&-1&0&-2\\
 0&0&0&-2\\
 0&2&-1&1
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B'=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&-4&0&0\\
 1&1&0&0\\
 0&0&0&-4\\
 0&0&1&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both labellings satisfy the strict twisted gap.  At the Perron radius
\(x=1/4\), all their radial class Euler profiles agree:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2-collision-constants}
 \mathcal E_{\{e\}}^\tau(x)=\mathcal E_{\{e\}}^{\tau'}(x),
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_{\{s\}}^\tau(x)=\mathcal E_{\{s\}}^{\tau'}(x),
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal E_C\) is defined in
\eqref{eq:intro-class-euler-profile}.
Nevertheless, their length-one primitive data are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2-collision-length-one}
 \bigl(p_{1,\{e\}}(\tau),p_{1,\{s\}}(\tau)\bigr)=(1,1),
 \qquad
 \bigl(p_{1,\{e\}}(\tau'),p_{1,\{s\}}(\tau')\bigr)=(2,0).
\end{equation}
In particular, one common radial sample of every class profile does not
determine the primitive length--class profile, even when the graph and group
are fixed and both extensions satisfy the strict gap.  The two labellings are
not related by vertex switching and global conjugacy.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every row of \(A\) has sum four.  The underlying directed graph is strongly
connected and has loops, so \(A\) is primitive and \(\lambda=4\).  For each
entry, the numbers of \(e\)- and \(s\)-labelled edges are
\[
 \frac{A_{ij}+B_{ij}}2,\qquad \frac{A_{ij}-B_{ij}}2
\]
for \(\tau\), and the same formulas with \(B'\) for \(\tau'\).  Inspection
shows that these are non-negative integers.  Thus the displayed matrices are
realized on the same marked collection of parallel edges.

Put \(Q(z)=1-z+4z^2\).  Exact determinant calculation gives
\begin{align}
 \det(I-zB)&=1-z^2+4z^4=Q(z^2),
 \label{eq:c2-collision-first-determinant}\\
 \det(I-zB')&=1-2z+9z^2-8z^3+16z^4=Q(z)^2.
 \label{eq:c2-collision-second-determinant}
\end{align}
The characteristic polynomials are
\[
 \det(xI-B)=x^4-x^2+4,
 \qquad
 \det(xI-B')=(x^2-x+4)^2.
\]
For the first polynomial, the substitution \(y=x^2\) shows that
\(y\overline y=4\), hence every root has modulus \(\sqrt2\).  The roots of
\(x^2-x+4\) have modulus two.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2-collision-gaps}
 \rad(B)=\sqrt2<4,
 \qquad
 \rad(B')=2<4.
\end{equation}

Write \(L_B(z)=-\log\det(I-zB)\) and define \(L_{B'}\) similarly, with the
germs normalized at zero.  In \(C_2\),
\(\psi^m\varepsilon=\varepsilon\) for odd \(m\) and
\(\psi^m\varepsilon=\mathbf1\) for even \(m\).  Since the trivial block is
the same for the two labellings, \eqref{eq:fixed-adams-mobius} gives, first on
\(|z|<1/4\),
\begin{align}
 F_{\varepsilon}^{\tau}(z)-F_{\varepsilon}^{\tau'}(z)
 &=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1k
   \left(\sum_{\substack{m\mid k\\m\ \mathrm{odd}}}\mu(m)\right)
   \bigl(L_B(z^k)-L_{B'}(z^k)\bigr)\notag\\
 &=\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}
   \bigl(L_B(z^{2^j})-L_{B'}(z^{2^j})\bigr).
 \label{eq:c2-collision-dyadic-reduction}
\end{align}
Here the odd-divisor M\"obius sum is one when \(k\) is a power of two and
zero otherwise.  If \(q_j=\log Q(z^{2^j})\), then
\eqref{eq:c2-collision-first-determinant}--
\eqref{eq:c2-collision-second-determinant} turn the last series into
\[
 \sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}(2q_j-q_{j+1})=2q_0.
\]
The series converges absolutely up to \(0\le z\le1/4\), since
\(q_j=O(z^{2^j})\).  Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c2-collision-exact-difference}
 F_{\varepsilon}^{\tau}(z)-F_{\varepsilon}^{\tau'}(z)
 =2\log Q(z)\qquad(0\le z\le1/4).
\end{equation}
Because \(Q(1/4)=1\), the two sign coordinates agree at the Perron point.
The trivial coordinates agree because the base matrix is fixed.  Since
\(\mathcal E_C=-F_{\mathbf1_C}\) and
\(\mathbf1_{\{e\}}=(\mathbf1+\varepsilon)/2\),
\(\mathbf1_{\{s\}}=(\mathbf1-\varepsilon)/2\), this proves
\eqref{eq:c2-collision-constants}.  For reference, direct reduction gives
\[
 \det(I-zA)=(1-4z)(1+2z-3z^2-4z^3),
 \qquad C(A)=\frac45.
\]

The only length-one primitive orbits are the loops at vertices two and four.
Their signs are respectively \((-1,1)\) for \(B\) and \((1,1)\) for \(B'\),
which proves \eqref{eq:c2-collision-length-one}.  Finally, vertex switching
acts on a sign block by diagonal sign conjugacy and leaves every loop label
unchanged.  Global conjugacy is trivial in \(C_2\).  Thus the two labellings
cannot be switching--conjugate.  Equivalently, their distinct characteristic
polynomials already preclude diagonal similarity.
\end{proof}

The telescoping in the proof is an exact kernel identity, rather than a
coincidence detected by truncation.

\begin{proposition}[Dyadic boundary functional]
\label{prop:dyadic-boundary-functional}
Let \(0<x<1\), and let \(Q\) be a polynomial with \(Q(0)=1\) and no zero on
the radial segment \([0,x]\).  Use the logarithm germ continued from zero
along that segment and define
\[
 \mathscr B_x(P):=-\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}\log P(x^{2^j})
\]
whenever the series converges.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic-boundary-coboundary}
 \mathscr B_x\bigl(Q(z^2)\bigr)
 -\mathscr B_x\bigl(Q(z)^2\bigr)=2\log Q(x).
\end{equation}
Hence \(Q(x)=1\) places the non-constant determinant ratio
\(Q(z^2)/Q(z)^2\) in the kernel of this single boundary functional.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(q_j=\log Q(x^{2^j})\).  Since \(q_j=O(x^{2^j})\), both series converge
absolutely, and shifting the first one gives
\[
 -\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}q_{j+1}
 +2\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}q_j=2q_0.
\]
This is \eqref{eq:dyadic-boundary-coboundary}.
\end{proof}

The polynomial identity above belongs to a slightly larger class of finite
rational certificates.  The positivity condition in the next statement is
essential: without it, the positive real powers defining the boundary product
need not exist.

\begin{proposition}[Positive rational Mahler certificates]
\label{prop:rational-mahler-certificate}
Let \(0<x<1\), let \(H\in\QQ(z)^\times\) satisfy \(H(0)=1\), and assume
that \(H(t)>0\) for \(0\le t\le x\).  Suppose that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-mahler-coboundary}
 H(z)=\delta R(z):=\frac{R(z^2)}{R(z)^2}
\end{equation}
for some \(R\in\overline{\QQ}(z)^\times\) with \(R(0)=1\).  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
 \item the function \(R\) is unique and in fact belongs to
   \(\QQ(z)^\times\);
 \item it has neither a zero nor a pole on \([0,x]\), and \(R(t)>0\) on
   that interval;
 \item the positive real product
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-mahler-product}
    \Pi_x(H):=\prod_{j\ge0}H(x^{2^j})^{2^{-j}}
   \end{equation}
   converges and satisfies
   \begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-mahler-telescope}
    \Pi_x(H)=R(x)^{-2}.
   \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, within the positive rational coboundary class
\[
 \mathscr R_x^+:=\{R\in\QQ(z)^\times:R(0)=1,
                         \ \delta R(t)>0\text{ on }[0,x]\},
\]
one has the exact kernel identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-rational-certificate-kernel}
 \ker(\Pi_x)\cap\delta\mathscr R_x^+
 =\{\delta R:R\in\mathscr R_x^+,\ R(x)=1\}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
First, \(\delta\) is injective on rational functions normalized to have value
one at zero.  Indeed, if \(U(0)=1\) and \(U(z^2)=U(z)^2\), write
\(U(z)=1+az^n+O(z^{n+1})\) with \(a\ne0\) and \(n\) least.  The first
non-constant term has degree \(2n\) on the left and degree \(n\) on the
right, a contradiction.  Hence \(U=1\).

For every automorphism
\(\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\QQ}/\QQ)\),
\eqref{eq:rational-mahler-coboundary} gives
\(\delta(\sigma R/R)=1\) and \((\sigma R/R)(0)=1\).  Injectivity gives
\(\sigma R=R\), so \(R\in\QQ(z)^\times\); it also proves uniqueness.

Let \(v_R(t_0)\) denote the zero or pole order of \(R\) at a real
\(t_0\in(0,x]\).  Since \(H(t_0)\) is finite and non-zero,
\eqref{eq:rational-mahler-coboundary} implies
\[
 v_R(t_0^2)=2v_R(t_0).
\]
If \(v_R(t_0)\ne0\), iteration produces infinitely many distinct zeros or
poles accumulating at zero, contrary to rationality and \(R(0)=1\).
Thus \(R\) has none on \([0,x]\); continuity and \(R(0)=1\) then give
\(R>0\) there.

Finally, \(\log H(t)=O(t)\) at zero, so
\eqref{eq:rational-mahler-product} converges absolutely after taking real
logarithms.  Its finite truncations telescope exactly:
\[
 \prod_{j=0}^{N}
 \left(\frac{R(x^{2^{j+1}})}{R(x^{2^j})^2}\right)^{2^{-j}}
 =\frac{R(x^{2^{N+1}})^{2^{-N}}}{R(x)^2}.
\]
The numerator tends to one because \(R(0)=1\), proving
\eqref{eq:rational-mahler-telescope} and then
\eqref{eq:positive-rational-certificate-kernel}.
\end{proof}

We now prove that the converse in
\eqref{eq:positive-rational-certificate-kernel} holds at every interior
algebraic point for parity-compatible determinant ratios over one or two
bases, and also at a common Perron endpoint under the strict gap.  The argument has
two arithmetic inputs.  The first is the Dieudonn\'e--Dwork integrality
criterion; the second is Nishioka's published special-value theorem for
implicit Mahler equations.  We isolate the parts specific to the present
determinant setting.

\begin{lemma}[Integral critical Mahler product]
\label{lem:critical-mahler-integrality}
Let \(P_0,P_1\in\ZZ[z]\) have constant term one and satisfy
\(P_0\equiv P_1\pmod2\).  Put
\[
 H(z)=\frac{P_0(z)}{P_1(z)},
\]
and suppose that both polynomials are zero-free on \(\abs z<r\),
where \(0<r\le1\).  On this disk define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-mahler-product}
 F(z)=\prod_{\nu\ge0}H(z^{2^\nu})^{2^{-\nu-1}},
 \qquad F(0)=1,
\end{equation}
using the logarithm germ at zero.  Then \(F\) is analytic and non-vanishing,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-mahler-equation}
 F(z)^2=H(z)F(z^2),
\end{equation}
and \(F\in\ZZ[[z]]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Normal convergence of the logarithmic series on compact subdisks proves the
analytic assertions and shifting the product proves
\eqref{eq:critical-mahler-equation}.  The assumed congruence gives
\(P_0\equiv P_1\pmod2\), hence
\[
 H(z)^{-1}\in1+2z\ZZ_2[[z]].
\]
Since
\(F(z^2)/F(z)^2=H(z)^{-1}\), the Dieudonn\'e--Dwork criterion
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{PomeratStraub2024RootsPowerSeries} gives
\(F\in\ZZ_2[[z]]\).  Also \(F\in\QQ[[z]]\), directly from
\eqref{eq:critical-mahler-product}.  For an odd prime \(p\), compare the
coefficient of \(z^n\) in \eqref{eq:critical-mahler-equation}.  Its left-hand
side is \(2f_n\) plus an expression in \(f_0,\ldots,f_{n-1}\), while its
right-hand side belongs to \(\ZZ_p\) whenever the preceding coefficients do,
because \(P_1(0)=1\).  Induction and \(2\in\ZZ_p^\times\) give
\(F\in\ZZ_p[[z]]\) for every odd \(p\).  A rational number integral at every
prime is an integer, proving the claim coefficientwise.
\end{proof}

The next estimate is not needed to reprove the special-value theorem below;
it records an effective feature of the critical quadratic equation itself.
The general zero-order theorem of T\"opfer requires the strict degree condition
\(tm<d\) \cite[Theorem~1]{Topfer1998ZeroOrderMahler}; here there is one
function and the equation has \(t=d=2\), so that theorem does not cover the
critical equality treated below.

\begin{lemma}[Critical quadratic zero estimate]
\label{lem:critical-mahler-zero-estimate}
Let \(P_0,P_1\in\CC[z]\) have constant term one, set
\(\eta=\max\{\deg P_0,\deg P_1\}\), and let an analytic germ
\(F(0)=1\) satisfy
\[
 P_1(z)F(z)^2=P_0(z)F(z^2).
\]
If \(F\) is transcendental over \(\CC(z)\), then every non-zero
\(Q\in\CC[z,Y]\) with \(\deg_zQ\le D\) and \(\deg_YQ\le N\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-mahler-zero-bound}
 \operatorname{ord}_{z=0}Q(z,F(z))
 \le D+4DN+\eta N^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First take an irreducible factor \(q\in\CC[z,Y]\) of positive
\(Y\)-degree and write \(d=\deg_zq\), \(n=\deg_Yq\).  Clear the denominator
in its pullback by setting
\[
 \widetilde q(z,Y)=P_0(z)^n
 q\!\left(z^2,\frac{P_1(z)}{P_0(z)}Y^2\right).
\]
This is a polynomial, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-pullback-orders}
 \deg_Y\widetilde q=2n,\qquad
 \deg_z\widetilde q\le2d+\eta n,\qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_0\widetilde q(z,F(z))
 =2\operatorname{ord}_0q(z,F(z)).
\end{equation}
If \(q(0,1)\ne0\), then \(\operatorname{ord}_0q(z,F(z))=0\), so there is
nothing to prove.  Assume henceforth that \(q(0,1)=0\).
We claim that \(q\nmid\widetilde q\).  Otherwise the irreducible curve
\(C:q=0\) is invariant under the rational map
\[
 \Phi(z,Y)=\left(z^2,\frac{P_1(z)}{P_0(z)}Y^2\right).
\]
Pass to the normalization of \(C\).  The induced map permutes forward the
finite set of places above \((0,1)\), so this finite dynamical system contains
a periodic place.  Its Puiseux germ \(Y=G(z)\), based at \((0,1)\), therefore
satisfies for some \(\ell\ge1\) the \(\ell\)-fold iterate of the same equation
as \(F\).  Consequently \(U=G/F\) obeys
\[
 U(z^{2^\ell})=U(z)^{2^\ell},\qquad U(0)=1.
\]
If \(U=1+cz^\alpha+\cdots\), with \(c\ne0\) and the least positive Puiseux
exponent \(\alpha\), the least non-constant exponents on the two sides are
respectively \(2^\ell\alpha\) and \(\alpha\), a contradiction.  Thus
\(U=1\), which would make \(F=G\) algebraic.  This proves the claim.

Hence \(R_q(z)=\operatorname{Res}_Y(q,\widetilde q)\) is non-zero.  A
B\'ezout identity in \(Y\), evaluated at \(Y=F(z)\), and
\eqref{eq:critical-pullback-orders} imply
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_0q(z,F(z))
 \le\operatorname{ord}_0R_q
 \le\deg R_q
 \le4dn+\eta n^2.
\]
Finally write \(Q=a(z)\prod_iq_i^{e_i}\), separating its polynomial content
in \(z\).  The content contributes at most \(D\).  With multiplicities,
\(\sum_i e_id_i\le D\) and \(\sum_i e_in_i\le N\); therefore
\(\sum_i e_id_in_i\le DN\) and
\(\sum_i e_in_i^2\le N^2\).  Summing the factor bounds proves
\eqref{eq:critical-mahler-zero-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Normalized saturation of rational coboundaries]
\label{lem:rational-mahler-saturation}
Let \(H,Q\in\QQ(z)^\times\) satisfy \(H(0)=Q(0)=1\).  If, for some
integer \(m\ge1\),
\[
 H^m=\delta Q=\frac{Q(z^2)}{Q(z)^2},
\]
then there is an \(R\in\QQ(z)^\times\), with \(R(0)=1\), such that
\(H=\delta R\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(a\in\overline{\QQ}^\times\), let \(h(a)\) and \(q(a)\) denote the
coefficients of \([a]\) in the divisors of \(H\) and \(Q\), respectively.
Taking divisors gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:saturation-divisor-recurrence}
 mh(a)=q(a^2)-2q(a),
 \qquad q(a^2)\equiv2q(a)\pmod m.
\end{equation}
Fix \(a\ne0,\infty\).  For sufficiently large \(N\), among the
\(2^N\)-th roots of \(a\) choose \(b_N\) outside the finite support of
\(\operatorname{div}Q\).  Iterating the congruence along the chain from
\(b_N\) to \(a\) gives \(q(a)\equiv2^Nq(b_N)\equiv0\pmod m\).
The coefficient at zero is zero because \(Q(0)=1\), and the degree-zero
condition on a principal divisor gives the same divisibility at infinity.
Thus \(\operatorname{div}Q=mD\).

The divisor \(D\) is Galois invariant and has degree zero.  Since
\(\operatorname{Pic}^0(\mathbb P^1_{\QQ})=0\), choose
\(S\in\QQ(z)^\times\) with \(\operatorname{div}S=D\), normalized by
\(S(0)=1\).  Then \(Q/S^m\) is constant, and evaluation at zero gives
\(Q=S^m\).  It follows that \((H/\delta S)^m=1\).  This rational function is
constant and has value one at zero, so \(H=\delta S\); take \(R=S\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Algebraic critical products are rational coboundaries]
\label{prop:algebraic-mahler-coboundary}
Let \(H\in\QQ(z)^\times\), \(H(0)=1\), and suppose that
\(F\in1+z\QQ[[z]]\) is algebraic over \(\CC(z)\) and satisfies
\(F(z)^2=H(z)F(z^2)\).  Then \(H=\delta R\) for a unique
\(R\in\QQ(z)^\times\) with \(R(0)=1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(G=zF'/F\).  Logarithmic differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-derivative-mahler}
 G(z)=G(z^2)+\frac z2\frac{H'(z)}{H(z)}.
\end{equation}
Put \(r(z)=zH'(z)/(2H(z))\).  If \(r\ne0\), the equations
\(G(z)-G(z^2)=r(z)\) and
\(G(z^2)-G(z^4)=r(z^2)\) give the non-zero homogeneous equation
\[
 r(z^2)G(z)-\bigl(r(z)+r(z^2)\bigr)G(z^2)+r(z)G(z^4)=0.
\]
After clearing denominators this is a scalar \(2\)-Mahler equation with
polynomial coefficients.  If \(r=0\),
\eqref{eq:log-derivative-mahler} and \(G(0)=0\) give \(G=0\).  Since algebraic
functions are D-finite, B\'ezivin's theorem that a D-finite Mahler function is
rational
\cite[Theorem~1]{BellCoonsRowland2013MahlerDichotomy} yields
\(G\in\CC(z)\), and its rational Taylor coefficients give
\(G\in\QQ(z)\).

On the complement of the finite singular set, analytic continuation preserves
the rational logarithmic derivative \(F'/F=G/z\).  Hence the quotient of any
two branches of \(F\) is constant.  The finite monodromy group acts through
roots of unity on these branches, so for some \(m\ge1\), \(T=F^m\) is a
single-valued algebraic function.  A single-valued algebraic function on the
Riemann sphere is rational, and therefore \(T\in\CC(z)^\times\).  Since its
germ belongs to \(1+z\QQ[[z]]\), Galois invariance of that germ gives
\(T\in\QQ(z)^\times\) and \(T(0)=1\).  Raising the functional equation to
the \(m\)-th power gives
\[
 H^m=\frac{T(z)^2}{T(z^2)}=\delta(T^{-1}).
\]
Apply \Cref{lem:rational-mahler-saturation}, and then use the normalized
injectivity proved in \Cref{prop:rational-mahler-certificate} for uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-base interior determinant lifting and endpoint extension]
\label{thm:determinant-boundary-lifting}
Let \(A,A'\) be primitive non-negative integer matrices, not necessarily of
the same size, with Perron roots \(\lambda,\lambda'>1\), and suppose that
\[
 \det(I-zA)=\det(I-zA').
\]
Put \(x=\min\{\lambda^{-1},(\lambda')^{-1}\}\).  Let \(B,B'\) be integer
matrices of the respective sizes of \(A,A'\) such that, entrywise,
\[
 \abs{B_{ij}}\le A_{ij},\quad B_{ij}\equiv A_{ij}\pmod2,
 \qquad
 \abs{B'_{ij}}\le A'_{ij},\quad B'_{ij}\equiv A'_{ij}\pmod2,
\]
and set
\[
 H(z)=\frac{\det(I-zB)}{\det(I-zB')}.
\]
For every algebraic number \(y\) with \(0<y<x\), the following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
 \item \(\Pi_y(H)=1\);
 \item there is a unique \(R\in\QQ(z)^\times\) such that
 \[
  R(0)=R(y)=1,
  \qquad H(z)=\frac{R(z^2)}{R(z)^2}.
 \]
\end{enumerate}
If \(\rad(B)<\lambda\) and \(\rad(B')<\lambda'\), the equivalence also
holds at \(y=x\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Perron-weighted norm estimate in the proof of
\Cref{lem:perron-weighted-domination}, applied separately to the two bases,
gives \(\rad(B)\le\lambda\) and \(\rad(B')\le\lambda'\).  If \(y<x\), choose
\(r\) with \(y<r<x\); if \(y=x\) and both strict gaps hold, choose \(r>y\)
below both reciprocal spectral radii.  In either case both determinant
polynomials are non-zero on \(\abs z<r\).  They are real, non-zero on
\([0,y]\), and equal one at zero, so they are positive there and \(H>0\) on
that interval.  Moreover,
\[
 \det(I-zB)\equiv\det(I-zA)=\det(I-zA')
 \equiv\det(I-zB')\pmod2.
\]
Define \(F\) by \eqref{eq:critical-mahler-product}.  By
\Cref{lem:critical-mahler-integrality}, it is analytic on \(\abs z<r\), has
integer coefficients, and satisfies \eqref{eq:critical-mahler-equation}.
Crucially, the product normalization is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-product-square-normalization}
 F(y)^2=\Pi_y(H),
\end{equation}
not \(F(y)=\Pi_y(H)\).

We now verify every hypothesis of Nishioka's special-value theorem
\cite[Theorem, p.~387]{Nishioka1982MahlerFunctionValues}.  Write the rational
function \(H\) in reduced form
\[
 H=\frac{\widehat P_0}{\widehat P_1},
 \qquad
 \widehat P_0,\widehat P_1\in\ZZ[z],
 \qquad
 \gcd(\widehat P_0,\widehat P_1)=1,
 \qquad
 \widehat P_0(0)=\widehat P_1(0)=1.
\]
The common factors removed here do not affect zero-freeness on \(\abs z<r\).
In the notation of the cited theorem, take
\[
 Tz=z^2,
 \qquad
 Q_0(z,u)=\widehat P_0(z),
 \qquad
 Q_1(z,u)=-\widehat P_1(z)u^2.
\]
Then the functional equation is exactly
\[
 Q_0(z,F(z))F(Tz)+Q_1(z,F(z))=0.
\]
The two polynomials \(Q_0,Q_1\) are relatively prime in
\(\overline{\QQ}[z,u]\): a common factor cannot contain \(u\), since
\(\widehat P_0(0)=1\), and the remaining assertion is the displayed
coprimality of \(\widehat P_0,\widehat P_1\).  Nishioka's auxiliary
polynomial \(g(z)\), obtained as a polynomial combination of the \(Q_i\)
and independent of \(u\), may therefore be chosen simply as
\(g(z)=Q_0(z,u)=\widehat P_0(z)\).

The numerical parameters are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  p&=2, & N&=0, & n&=1, & m&=2,\\
  M&=\max\{p+N,m\}=2, & U&=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(m\) is the maximum \(u\)-degree of the \(Q_i\), exactly as in the
printed definition; that theorem permits both \(N=0\) and \(L=1\).
Thus, with coefficient-growth exponent \(L=1\), her strict inequality is
\[
 M(p+N)n^2=2\cdot2\cdot1=4
 <2^{2+1/L}=8.
\]
It remains to check the arithmetic and orbit conditions, rather than merely
record the parameters.  Write \(F(z)=\sum_{h\ge0}a_hz^h\).  By
\Cref{lem:critical-mahler-integrality}, all \(a_h\) lie in the fixed number
field \(\QQ\) and are algebraic integers.  Choose \(s\) with \(y<s<r\).
Cauchy's estimate on \(\abs z=s\) gives
\[
  \abs{a_h}\le \max_{\abs z=s}\abs{F(z)}\,s^{-h}.
\]
Hence Nishioka's coefficient-size and common-denominator bounds hold with
\(L=1\): the denominators are one and the logarithmic coefficient height is
at most linear in \(h\).  The evaluation point \(y\) is a non-zero algebraic
number and \(0<Uy<1\).  Moreover,
\[
 T^i y=y^{2^i}\ne0,
 \qquad
 g(T^i y)=\widehat P_0(y^{2^i})\ne0
 \quad(i\ge0),
\]
because \(0<y^{2^i}\le y<r\) and the reduced numerator is zero-free
on \(\abs z<r\).  Finally \(F(y)\) converges because \(F\) is analytic
there.
All hypotheses of the cited theorem are therefore satisfied.  It follows
that if \(F\) were transcendental over \(\CC(z)\), then \(F(y)\) would be
transcendental.

Assume \(\textup{(i)}\).  Positivity and \eqref{eq:critical-product-square-normalization}
give \(F(y)=1\), so the cited theorem forces \(F\) to be algebraic.  By
\Cref{prop:algebraic-mahler-coboundary}, \(H=\delta R\) for the unique
normalized rational \(R\).  Now
\Cref{prop:rational-mahler-certificate} gives
\(1=\Pi_y(H)=R(y)^{-2}\) and \(R(y)>0\), hence \(R(y)=1\).  This proves
\(\textup{(ii)}\).  Conversely, \(\textup{(ii)}\) and
\eqref{eq:rational-mahler-telescope} give \(\textup{(i)}\), and the
same proposition gives uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the lifting theorem]
The determinant parity in \Cref{thm:determinant-boundary-lifting} is essential
to the integrality step, so the theorem does not assert the converse for an
arbitrary positive rational \(H\).  Its realizable class is nevertheless broad.
Fix \(r\ge2\), an odd \(a>0\), and a positive even \(b\), and let \(A\) have
diagonal entries \(a\) and off-diagonal entries \(b\).  Then
\(\lambda=a+(r-1)b\).  Any diagonal \(B=\operatorname{diag}(c_i)\) and
\(B'=\operatorname{diag}(d_i)\), with odd
\(\abs{c_i},\abs{d_i}\le a\), satisfies the same-base compatibility and
strict gaps, and realizes
\[
 H(z)=\frac{\prod_i(1-c_i z)}{\prod_i(1-d_i z)}.
\]
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Logarithmic divisor bound for multiplicative Mahler coboundaries]
\label{prop:logarithmic-mahler-divisor-bound}
For an integer \(p\ge2\) and \(D\ge1\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logarithmic-mahler-bound-definition}
 m_p(D):=\min\{m\ge1:p^m(p-1)\ge2D\},
 \qquad
 b_p(D):=\left\lceil\frac{2D}{p}m_p(D)\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
Let \(P_0,P_1\in\QQ[z]\) be coprime, with constant term one and
\(D=\deg P_0+\deg P_1>0\).  If a normalized rational function
\[
 R=\frac AB\in\QQ(z)^\times,
 \qquad (A,B)=1,\qquad A(0)=B(0)=1,
\]
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p-mahler-coboundary}
 \frac{P_0(z)}{P_1(z)}=\frac{R(z^p)}{R(z)^p},
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logarithmic-mahler-degree-bound}
 \deg A+\deg B\le b_p(D).
\end{equation}
If \(P_0/P_1\ne1\), the equation \(R(z)=1\) has at most
\(b_p(D)-1\) distinct non-zero complex solutions.  In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binary-logarithmic-mahler-degree-bound}
 b_2(D)=D\lceil\log_2(2D)\rceil.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(\alpha\in\overline{\QQ}^{\times}\), let
\[
 e(\alpha)=\operatorname{ord}_{z=\alpha}(P_0/P_1),
 \qquad r(\alpha)=\operatorname{ord}_{z=\alpha}R.
\]
The reduced input has
\(\sum_\alpha\abs{e(\alpha)}=D\), while
\(\sum_\alpha\abs{r(\alpha)}=\deg A+\deg B\).  Taking divisors in
\eqref{eq:p-mahler-coboundary} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p-mahler-divisor-recurrence}
 e(\alpha)=r(\alpha^p)-pr(\alpha).
\end{equation}
Iteration along the forward \(p\)-power orbit yields
\[
 r(\alpha)=p^{-n}r(\alpha^{p^n})
 -\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}p^{-j-1}e(\alpha^{p^j}).
\]
The first term tends to zero because \(r\) has finite support.  This remains
true on a periodic root-of-unity orbit, since the numerator is bounded and
the factor \(p^{-n}\) tends to zero.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p-mahler-forward-inversion}
 r(\alpha)=-\sum_{j\ge0}p^{-j-1}e(\alpha^{p^j}).
\end{equation}

Put \(S=\operatorname{supp}r\) and
\(T=\sum_\alpha\abs{r(\alpha)}\).  For every \(j\),
the map \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{p^j}\) on
\(\overline{\QQ}^{\times}\) is exactly \(p^j\)-to-one.  Splitting
\eqref{eq:p-mahler-forward-inversion} at \(m=m_p(D)\), using
\(\#S\le T\) and \(\max_\alpha\abs{e(\alpha)}\le D\), gives
\begin{align*}
 T
 &\le \sum_{j=0}^{m-1}p^{-j-1}
       \sum_{\alpha\in S}\abs{e(\alpha^{p^j})}
   +\sum_{\alpha\in S}\sum_{j\ge m}
       p^{-j-1}\abs{e(\alpha^{p^j})}\\
 &\le \frac{mD}{p}+\frac{TD}{p^m(p-1)}
 \le \frac{mD}{p}+\frac T2.
\end{align*}
Thus \(T\le2mD/p\); integrality of \(T\) proves
\eqref{eq:logarithmic-mahler-degree-bound}.

Normalized injectivity of the coboundary map, proved in
\Cref{prop:rational-mahler-certificate}, shows that \(P_0/P_1\ne1\) implies
\(R\ne1\).  The non-zero polynomial \(A-B\) has degree at most
\(\deg A+\deg B\) and vanishes at zero.  It therefore has at most
\(b_p(D)-1\) distinct non-zero roots.  Formula
\eqref{eq:binary-logarithmic-mahler-degree-bound} follows directly from
\eqref{eq:logarithmic-mahler-bound-definition}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The logarithmic order cannot be improved from divisor data alone]
\label{rem:logarithmic-mahler-degree-sharpness}
Let \(Q\in\QQ[z]\) have degree \(q>0\) and \(Q(0)=1\), and suppose that
\(Q(z^{p^{J+1}})\) and \(Q(z)\) are coprime.  Then
\[
 R_J(z):=\prod_{j=0}^{J}Q(z^{p^j})^{p^{J-j}}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \frac{R_J(z^p)}{R_J(z)^p}
 =\frac{Q(z^{p^{J+1}})}{Q(z)^{p^{J+1}}},
 \qquad
 \deg R_J=(J+1)p^Jq,\qquad D=2p^{J+1}q.
\]
The coprimality hypothesis holds, for example, for \(Q(z)=1-2z\).
Consequently these normalized rational coboundaries have certificate degree
of order \(D\log D\).  Thus no \(o(D\log D)\) bound can follow from the
rational divisor equation without additional realizability restrictions.
\end{remark}

The preceding lifting theorem supplies a rational certificate abstractly.  We
now show that its existence and the certificate itself are decidable by a
single finite Pad\'e calculation.  For an integral polynomial \(P\), write
\[
 h(P):=\log\max_j\abs{[z^j]P}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Effective normalized rational coboundary criterion]
\label{thm:effective-rational-mahler-coboundary}
Let \(P_0,P_1\in\ZZ[z]\) be coprime, with
\(P_0(0)=P_1(0)=1\).  Put \(d_i=\deg P_i\) and \(D=d_0+d_1\).
If \(D=0\), then \(P_0=P_1=1\), and the unique normalized solution is
\(R=1\).  If \(D>0\), put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-bounds}
 L=\lfloor\log_2D\rfloor,
 \qquad
 N=b_2(D)=D\lceil\log_2(2D)\rceil,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-height-bound}
 \Lambda=h(P_0)+h(P_1)
   +\frac12\log\bigl((d_0+1)(d_1+1)\bigr),
 \qquad
 K=N\log2+D(L+1)\Lambda.
\end{equation}
There is a finite algorithm over \(\QQ\), using only \(P_0,P_1\), which
decides whether
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-equation}
 \frac{P_0(z)}{P_1(z)}
 =\frac{R(z^2)}{R(z)^2},
 \qquad R\in\QQ(z)^\times,\quad R(0)=1,
\end{equation}
and constructs \(R\) when it exists.  The solution is unique.  In its unique
reduced form
\[
 R=\frac{A}{B},
 \qquad A,B\in\ZZ[z],\quad (A,B)=1,\quad A(0)=B(0)=1,
\]
it satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-output-bounds}
 \deg A+\deg B\le N,
 \qquad
 \max\{h(A),h(B)\}\le K.
\end{equation}
 In particular, \(\max\{\deg A,\deg B\}\le
 D\lceil\log_2(2D)\rceil\).  Two necessary conditions
are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-obstructions}
 d_0=d_1,
 \qquad P_0\equiv P_1\pmod2.
\end{equation}

More explicitly, let \(S(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}s_nz^n\in1+z\QQ[[z]]\) be the
unique formal solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-formal-equation}
 P_0(z)S(z)^2=P_1(z)S(z^2).
\end{equation}
Writing \(p_{i,j}=[z^j]P_i\), its coefficients are obtained successively from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-coefficient-recurrence}
\begin{split}
 2s_n={}&
 \sum_{\substack{0\le j\le d_1\\j\le n,\ n-j\ {\rm even}}}
   p_{1,j}s_{(n-j)/2} \\
 &-\sum_{\substack{0\le j\le d_0,\ u,v\ge0,\ u+v=n-j\\
          (j,u,v)\ne(0,0,n),(0,n,0)}}p_{0,j}s_us_v
 \qquad(n\ge1).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
After computing \(s_0,\ldots,s_{2N}\), solve the affine linear system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-pade-system}
 \sum_{j=0}^N b_js_{k-j}=0
 \quad(N+1\le k\le2N),
 \qquad b_0=1,\qquad s_m=0\ (m<0).
\end{equation}
If it is inconsistent, reject.  Otherwise choose any solution, put
\[
 B_*(z)=\sum_{j=0}^Nb_jz^j,
 \qquad
 A_*(z)=\sum_{k=0}^N
       \left(\sum_{j=0}^{\min\{k,N\}}b_js_{k-j}\right)z^k,
\]
cancel their common factor, and normalize both constant terms to one.  Accept
the resulting \(A/B\) precisely when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-final-identity}
 P_0(z)A(z)^2B(z^2)=P_1(z)A(z^2)B(z)^2.
\end{equation}
Thus the procedure requires neither factorization over
\(\overline{\QQ}\) nor enumeration of divisors.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that \(R\) exists.  The degree estimate is
\Cref{prop:logarithmic-mahler-divisor-bound} with \(p=2\).  For the height
estimate, retain the following support information.  For
\(\alpha\in\overline{\QQ}^{\times}\), set
\[
 r(\alpha)=\operatorname{ord}_{z=\alpha}R,
 \qquad
 e(\alpha)=\operatorname{ord}_{z=\alpha}(P_0/P_1).
\]
The map \(z\mapsto z^2\) is unramified at \(\alpha\ne0\), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-divisor-recurrence}
 e(\alpha)=r(\alpha^2)-2r(\alpha).
\end{equation}
Along the forward square orbit, write \(r_j=r(\alpha^{2^j})\) and
\(e_j=e(\alpha^{2^j})\).  Iteration gives
\[
 r_0=2^{-m}r_m-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}2^{-j-1}e_j.
\]
The divisor of \(R\) has finite support, so \(r_m\) is bounded.  Letting
\(m\to\infty\), and using \(\abs{e_j}\le D\), gives
    \(\abs{r(\alpha)}\le D\).  If \(r(\alpha)\ne0\), the forward orbit must
meet the support of \(e\).  If \(k\) is its first such index, then
\(r_{j+1}=2r_j\) for \(j<k\); hence
\[
 2^k\le2^k\abs{r_0}=\abs{r_k}\le D,
 \qquad k\le L.
\]
Consequently every finite non-zero zero or pole of \(R\) is a zero of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-window-polynomial}
 W(z):=\prod_{k=0}^{L}P_0(z^{2^k})P_1(z^{2^k}).
\end{equation}
Because each input polynomial is primitive and has constant term one, every
rational irreducible factor of \(W\), normalized to have constant term one,
lies in \(\ZZ[z]\).  The multiplicity bound above therefore gives
\[
 A(z)B(z)\mid W(z)^D\quad\hbox{in }\ZZ[z].
\]
 This divisibility will be used only for the height estimate; the sharper degree
 bound has already been obtained from
 \Cref{prop:logarithmic-mahler-divisor-bound}.

Let \(M(\,\cdot\,)\) denote Mahler measure.  The standard estimates
\[
 \log M(Q)\le h(Q)+\frac12\log(\deg Q+1),
 \qquad
 h(Q)\le\deg Q\log2+\log M(Q)
\]
and \(M(Q(z^m))=M(Q)\) give
\[
 \log M(W)\le(L+1)\Lambda.
\]
Mahler measure is multiplicative, and a non-zero integral polynomial has
measure at least one.  The preceding divisibility thus implies
\[
 \log M(A),\ \log M(B)\le D(L+1)\Lambda.
\]
Together with the degree bound, this proves
\eqref{eq:effective-mahler-height-bound}--
\eqref{eq:effective-mahler-output-bounds}.

At infinity,
\(\operatorname{ord}_\infty R(z^2)=2\operatorname{ord}_\infty R\), so the right-hand side
of \eqref{eq:effective-mahler-equation} has order zero there.  This proves
\(d_0=d_1\).  Moreover, reduction modulo two gives
\(A(z^2)=A(z)^2\) and \(B(z^2)=B(z)^2\).  Hence the right-hand side is one
in \(\mathbb F_2(z)\), proving \(P_0\equiv P_1\pmod2\).

The two occurrences of \(s_n\) on the left of
\eqref{eq:effective-mahler-formal-equation} have coefficient two; every
other coefficient in degree \(n\) involves only
\(s_0,\ldots,s_{n-1}\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:effective-mahler-coefficient-recurrence} and the uniqueness of
\(S\).  If a rational solution exists, its Taylor series is \(S\).  The
degree bound therefore implies that its denominator occurs among the
solutions of \eqref{eq:effective-mahler-pade-system}.

It remains to justify that an arbitrary solution of that system is harmless.
For two resulting pairs \((A_i,B_i)\), one has
\[
 B_iS-A_i=O(z^{2N+1}),
\]
and consequently
\[
 A_1B_2-A_2B_1=O(z^{2N+1}).
\]
The polynomial on the left has degree at most \(2N\), and hence vanishes
identically.  Thus every solution of the affine system produces the same
rational approximant.  If a genuine \(R\) exists, this approximant is \(R\);
conversely, \eqref{eq:effective-mahler-final-identity} is exactly the cleared
form of \eqref{eq:effective-mahler-equation}.  The final identity therefore
gives both sound rejection and complete reconstruction.  Uniqueness also
follows from the normalized injectivity of \(\delta\) proved in
\Cref{prop:rational-mahler-certificate}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Termination and a conservative bit bound]
\label{rem:effective-rational-mahler-complexity}
Let
\[
 d=\max\{1,d_0,d_1\},
 \qquad
 \tau=1+\max_i\left\lceil\log_2\norm{P_i}_\infty\right\rceil,
 \qquad b=\tau+\log(D+1).
\]
All loops stop at \(2N\), and the only linear system has \(N\) equations and
\(N\) unknowns.  If the parity condition in
\eqref{eq:effective-mahler-obstructions} holds, the
Dieudonn\'e--Dwork argument of
\Cref{lem:critical-mahler-integrality} applies verbatim to
\eqref{eq:effective-mahler-formal-equation}, so \(S\in\ZZ[[z]]\).  On the
circle
\[
 \abs z=\rho:=(4d2^\tau)^{-1}
\]
one has \(\abs{P_i(z)-1}\le1/4\).  The convergent local product for \(S\)
is
\[
 S(z)=\prod_{\nu\ge0}
 \left(\frac{P_1(z^{2^\nu})}{P_0(z^{2^\nu})}\right)^{2^{-\nu-1}}.
\]
Indeed,
\[
 \abs{P_i(z)-1}
 \le\frac{d2^{\tau-1}\rho}{1-\rho}<\frac14,
\]
and the logarithm series gives
\(\abs{\log S(z)}\le2\log(4/3)<\log2\).  Thus
\(\abs{S(z)}<2\) on the circle, and Cauchy's estimate gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-coefficient-size}
 \abs{s_n}\le2(4d2^\tau)^n.
\end{equation}
Thus the required series coefficients have \(O(Nb)\) bits.  In Bareiss
fraction-free elimination, every intermediate entry is a minor and has
\(O(N^2b)\) bits.  With schoolbook integer arithmetic, the complete procedure
therefore uses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-mahler-bit-complexity}
 O(N^7b^2)\quad\hbox{bit operations},
 \qquad O(N^4b)\quad\hbox{bits of storage}.
\end{equation}
These deliberately elementary bounds prove polynomial-time termination; no
claim of optimality is made.  Fast integer and modular linear algebra improve
them, but an exact sharper exponent would require a separate complexity
analysis.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Same-base effective specialization]
\label{cor:same-base-effective-rational-mahler}
Let \(A\) be an \(n\times n\) non-negative integral matrix and let \(B,B'\)
be integral matrices satisfying
\[
 \abs{B_{ij}},\abs{B'_{ij}}\le A_{ij},
 \qquad B_{ij}\equiv B'_{ij}\equiv A_{ij}\pmod2.
\]
Assume that
\(P_0=\det(I-zB)\) and \(P_1=\det(I-zB')\) are coprime, and put
\(a_*=\max\{1,A_{ij}:1\le i,j\le n\}\).  Then the algorithm of
\Cref{thm:effective-rational-mahler-coboundary} decides and constructs every
normalized rational certificate for \(P_0/P_1\), using integer coefficient
recursion before the Pad\'e step.  Uniformly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:same-base-effective-input-bounds}
 D\le2n,\qquad N\le2n\lceil\log_2(4n)\rceil,\qquad
 h(P_0),h(P_1)\le n\log(na_*),
\end{equation}
and one may use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:same-base-effective-height-bound}
 K\le N\log2+2n(\lfloor\log_2(2n)\rfloor+1)
       \bigl(2n\log(na_*)+\log(n+1)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The determinant congruence modulo two gives \(P_0\equiv P_1\pmod2\).
For \(0\le k\le n\), expansion by principal minors and domination by the
permanent give
\[
 \abs{[z^k]\det(I-zB)}
 \le\sum_{\substack{I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\\|I|=k}}
       \operatorname{per}(A[I,I])
 \le n^ka_*^k\le(na_*)^n,
\]
and the same estimate holds for \(B'\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:same-base-effective-input-bounds}; substituting those inequalities
in \eqref{eq:effective-mahler-height-bound} proves
\eqref{eq:same-base-effective-height-bound}.  Coprimality is stated because
the numerical bounds of \Cref{thm:effective-rational-mahler-coboundary} apply
to the reduced input pair.  A non-coprime determinant ratio may first be
cancelled, but no unchanged-height assertion for the uncancelled pair is
being made here.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Unconditional interior finite sampling and collision dichotomy]
\label{thm:finite-radial-sampling}
Let \(G=C_2\), and let
\(\tau,\tau':E(\Gamma)\to C_2\) be two labellings on the same marked
directed multigraph with primitive base matrix \(A\), Perron root
\(\lambda>1\), and sign blocks \(B,B'\).  Put \(x=\lambda^{-1}\), and set
\[
 \Delta_\varepsilon(y)
 :=F_\varepsilon^\tau(y)-F_\varepsilon^{\tau'}(y)
 \qquad(0<y<x).
\]
For
\(C\in\operatorname{Conj}(C_2)\), the corresponding pairwise radial
class-profile difference is
\[
 \mathcal D_C(y):=\sum_{n\ge1}
 \bigl(p_{n,C}(\tau)-p_{n,C}(\tau')\bigr)\log(1-y^n);
\]
both series converge absolutely for \(0<y<x\).  Write
\[
 H(z):=\frac{\det(I-zB)}{\det(I-zB')}
      =\frac{P_0(z)}{P_1(z)}
\]
in reduced form with coprime \(P_0,P_1\in\ZZ[z]\) and
\(P_0(0)=P_1(0)=1\), and put \(D=\deg P_0+\deg P_1\).
Equivalently, \(H=\zeta_{\widetilde X_{\tau'}}/
\zeta_{\widetilde X_\tau}\) by \Cref{prop:c2-standard-zeta-ratio}.

If \(D=0\), then the two labellings have identical primitive
length--class profiles.  If \(D>0\), define
\[
 N=D\lceil\log_2(2D)\rceil.
\]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-radial-algebraic-collision-bound}
 \#\{y\in\overline{\QQ}\cap(0,x):
          \Delta_\varepsilon(y)=0\}\le N-1.
\end{equation}
If the set in
\eqref{eq:finite-radial-algebraic-collision-bound} is non-empty, then the
finite Pad\'e procedure of
\Cref{thm:effective-rational-mahler-coboundary} constructs the unique reduced
\[
 R=\frac{A_0}{B_0}\in\QQ(z)^\times,
 \qquad A_0(0)=B_0(0)=1,
 \qquad \deg A_0+\deg B_0\le N,
\]
for which \(H(z)=R(z^2)/R(z)^2\).  The complete real collision set is then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-radial-complete-collision-set}
 \{y\in(0,x):\Delta_\varepsilon(y)=0\}
 =\{y\in(0,x):A_0(y)-B_0(y)=0\}.
\end{equation}
In this case the complete real collision set has at most \(N-1\) elements.
Consequently, if \(v\) is the size of the base matrix and
\[
 N_*=2v\lceil\log_2(4v)\rceil,
\]
then equality at any \(N_*\) distinct radii
\(y_1,\ldots,y_{N_*}\in(0,x)\), meaning
\(\Delta_\varepsilon(y_i)=0\) for every \(i\), at least one of which is
algebraic, forces
\[
 p_{n,C}(\tau)=p_{n,C}(\tau')
 \qquad(n\ge1,\ C\in\operatorname{Conj}(C_2)).
\]
Equivalently, simultaneous vanishing of
\(\mathcal D_{\{e\}}\) and \(\mathcal D_{\{s\}}\) at \(N_*\) such radii
recovers the entire primitive length--class profile.
If \(\rad(B),\rad(B')<\lambda\), all statements remain valid after replacing
every occurrence of \((0,x)\) by \((0,x]\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every primitive length-\(n\) orbit contributes \(n\) period-\(n\) points, so
\(np_{n,C}\le\Tr(A^n)=O(\lambda^n)\).  This proves the asserted absolute
convergence at every \(y<x\).

The base is fixed, so its trivial determinant coordinate is the same for
\(\tau\) and \(\tau'\).  The \(C_2\) Adams--M\"obius reduction used in
\eqref{eq:c2-collision-dyadic-reduction}, now with arbitrary sign blocks,
therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-radial-boundary-identity}
 \Delta_\varepsilon(y)
 =-\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}\log H(y^{2^j})
 =-\log\Pi_y(H)
 \qquad(0<y<x).
\end{equation}
The Perron-weighted domination estimate gives
\(\rad(B),\rad(B')\le\lambda\).  Hence both determinant polynomials are
zero-free on \(\abs z<x\), are positive on \([0,y]\) for each \(y<x\), and
the series in \eqref{eq:finite-radial-boundary-identity} converges there.
Thus
\[
 \Delta_\varepsilon(y)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Pi_y(H)=1.
\]

If \(D=0\), then \(H=1\).  The trivial and sign determinant coordinates are
both equal, so \Cref{prop:comparison-dictionary} gives equality of all
primitive length--class data.  The same conclusion holds whenever \(H=1\).

Suppose now that \(H\ne1\).  If there is no algebraic collision radius,
\eqref{eq:finite-radial-algebraic-collision-bound} is immediate.  Otherwise
choose one such radius \(y_0\).  The algebraic-radius form of
\Cref{thm:determinant-boundary-lifting} gives a unique normalized
\(R\in\QQ(z)^\times\) with
\[
 H(z)=\frac{R(z^2)}{R(z)^2},
 \qquad R(y_0)=1.
\]
The effective criterion constructs its reduced numerator and denominator
\(A_0,B_0\) and gives \(\deg A_0+\deg B_0\le N\).

For every real \(0<y<x\),
\Cref{prop:rational-mahler-certificate} applies on \([0,y]\) and gives
\(\Pi_y(H)=R(y)^{-2}\) with \(R(y)>0\).  Hence
\[
 \Delta_\varepsilon(y)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 R(y)=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A_0(y)-B_0(y)=0,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:finite-radial-complete-collision-set}.  Since \(H\ne1\),
the polynomial \(A_0-B_0\) is non-zero.  Its degree is at most \(N\), while
\(A_0(0)-B_0(0)=0\).  It therefore has at most \(N-1\) distinct non-zero
roots, proving \eqref{eq:finite-radial-algebraic-collision-bound}.

Each determinant has degree at most \(v\), so reduction gives \(D\le2v\).
The displayed function defining \(N\) is increasing in the positive integer
\(D\); hence \(N\le N_*\).  If \(\Delta_\varepsilon\) vanished at \(N_*\)
distinct radii, one of which is algebraic, the complete real collision-set
bound \(N-1<N_*\) would be contradicted when \(H\ne1\).  Thus \(H=1\), and
the periodic data dictionary gives the
asserted recovery.  Finally, for \(C_2=\{e,s\}\)
the fixed base gives equality of the scalar coordinate, while
\(\mathbf1_{\{e\}}=(\mathbf1+\varepsilon)/2\) and
\(\mathbf1_{\{s\}}=(\mathbf1-\varepsilon)/2\).  Therefore equality of
\(\Delta_\varepsilon(y)\) is equivalent to
\(\mathcal D_{\{e\}}(y)=\mathcal D_{\{s\}}(y)=0\), proving the last
assertion.

If both sign blocks have strict spectral radius below \(\lambda\), their
determinants are zero-free on a disk strictly larger than \(\abs z\le x\).
The identity \eqref{eq:finite-radial-boundary-identity}, the lifting theorem,
and the rational telescoping formula then hold also at \(x\).  The same proof
therefore gives every asserted endpoint extension.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dimension-free sampling for elementary two-groups]
\label{thm:elementary-two-group-finite-sampling}
Let \(E=(C_2)^r\), with \(r\ge0\).  Let
\(\tau:E(\Gamma)\to E\) and \(\tau':E(\Gamma')\to E\) be one-step
cocycles over primitive non-negative integral base matrices \(A,A'\), whose
sizes are at most \(V\) and whose Perron roots \(\lambda,\lambda'>1\) need
not agree.  Put
\[
 x=\min\{\lambda^{-1},(\lambda')^{-1}\},
 \qquad M(V)=2V\lceil\log_2(4V)\rceil.
\]
For \(g\in E\), define
\[
 \mathcal E_g^\tau(y)=\sum_{n\ge1}p_{n,g}(\tau)\log(1-y^n),
 \qquad 0<y<x,
\]
and define \(\mathcal E_g^{\tau'}\) analogously.  Suppose that
\[
 \mathcal E_g^\tau(y_i)=\mathcal E_g^{\tau'}(y_i)
 \qquad(g\in E,\ 1\le i\le M(V))
\]
at \(M(V)\) distinct radii in \((0,x)\), at least one of which is
algebraic.  Then
\[
 p_{n,g}(\tau)=p_{n,g}(\tau')
 \qquad(n\ge1,\ g\in E).
\]
In particular, the sample count is independent of \(r\), and neither a common
base presentation nor a strict spectral gap for the non-trivial character
blocks is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Absolute convergence follows for each system from the periodic-point estimate
used in \Cref{thm:finite-radial-sampling}.  Summing the assumed identities over
\(g\in E\) gives, at every sampled radius,
\[
 \log\det(I-y_iA)=\sum_{g\in E}\mathcal E_g^\tau(y_i)
 =\sum_{g\in E}\mathcal E_g^{\tau'}(y_i)
 =\log\det(I-y_iA').
\]
Both determinants are positive on \((0,x)\).  Their difference has degree at
most \(V\), vanishes at the \(M(V)\ge V\) distinct non-zero sample points,
and also vanishes at zero.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elementary-two-group-base-determinant-equality}
 \det(I-zA)=\det(I-zA').
\end{equation}

Every \(\chi\in\widehat E\) takes values in \(\{\pm1\}\).  Let
\(B_{\chi,\tau}\) and \(B_{\chi,\tau'}\) be the corresponding character
blocks and put
\[
 H_\chi(z)=\frac{\det(I-zB_{\chi,\tau})}
                  {\det(I-zB_{\chi,\tau'})}.
\]
Since \(\chi(g)\equiv1\pmod2\), entrywise reduction gives
\[
 B_{\chi,\tau}\equiv A\pmod2,
 \qquad B_{\chi,\tau'}\equiv A'\pmod2.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:elementary-two-group-base-determinant-equality}, this
is precisely the parity input in
\Cref{thm:determinant-boundary-lifting}; the two blocks need not have the same
size.

Fourier transformation of the sampled class-profile identities gives
\[
 \Delta_\chi(y_i):=
 \sum_{g\in E}\chi(g)
 \bigl(\mathcal E_g^\tau(y_i)-\mathcal E_g^{\tau'}(y_i)\bigr)=0.
\]
For the trivial character this is already
\eqref{eq:elementary-two-group-base-determinant-equality}.  For non-trivial
\(\chi\), one has \(\chi^m=\chi\) for odd \(m\) and
\(\chi^m=\mathbf1\) for even \(m\).  The Adams--M\"obius calculation in
\eqref{eq:finite-radial-boundary-identity}, with the now common trivial
determinant coordinate, therefore gives
\[
 \Delta_\chi(y)=\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}\log H_\chi(y^{2^j}).
\]

Choose an algebraic sampled radius \(y_0\).  If \(H_\chi\ne1\),
\Cref{thm:determinant-boundary-lifting} supplies the unique normalized
\(R_\chi\in\QQ(z)^\times\) such that
\[
 H_\chi(z)=\frac{R_\chi(z^2)}{R_\chi(z)^2},
 \qquad R_\chi(y_0)=1.
\]
Write the reduced ratio \(H_\chi=P_{0,\chi}/P_{1,\chi}\) and put
\(D_\chi=\deg P_{0,\chi}+\deg P_{1,\chi}\).  Both character determinants
have degree at most \(V\), so \(D_\chi\le2V\).  By
\Cref{prop:logarithmic-mahler-divisor-bound} and the positive telescoping
identity in \Cref{prop:rational-mahler-certificate}, all real zeros of
\(\Delta_\chi\) lie among the non-zero roots of \(R_\chi-1\), of which there
are at most
\[
 D_\chi\lceil\log_2(2D_\chi)\rceil-1
 \le M(V)-1.
\]
The \(M(V)\) sampled zeros are therefore impossible.  Thus \(H_\chi=1\)
for every character \(\chi\).  The cross-base periodic-data dictionary in
\Cref{prop:comparison-dictionary}, followed by character Fourier inversion,
now gives equality of every primitive length--element count.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Located obstruction for general finite groups]
\label{rem:general-group-finite-sampling-interface}
For a same-base pair and \(\pi\in\Irr(G)\), put
\(H_\pi=P_{\pi,\tau}/P_{\pi,\tau'}\); then \(H_{\mathbf1}=1\).
Rearranging \eqref{eq:fixed-determinant-open-disc} by \(k=rm\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-group-adams-mobius-obstruction}
 \begin{aligned}
 F_{\chi_\rho}^\tau(z)-F_{\chi_\rho}^{\tau'}(z)
   &=-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1k\sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}
       b_{\rho,\pi}(k)\Log_0H_\pi(z^k),\\
 b_{\rho,\pi}(k)
   &=\sum_{m\mid k}\mu(m)a_{\rho,\pi}^{(m)}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For \(C_2\), projection away from the common trivial channel makes these
coefficients vanish unless \(k\) is a power of two.  That lacunarity is exactly
what produces the scalar Mahler product in
\eqref{eq:finite-radial-boundary-identity}.

It already fails for \(C_3=\langle g\rangle\).  If \(\chi(g)=\omega\), record
the Adams states in the ordered basis
\((\mathbf1,\chi,\chi^2)\).  Their M\"obius transform is
\[
 \mathbf b(k)=\sum_{m\mid k}\mu(m)e_{m\bmod3}.
\]
For a prime \(p\), this is zero when \(p\equiv1\pmod3\), equals
\(e_1-e_2\) when \(p\equiv2\pmod3\), and equals \(e_1-e_0\) when
\(p=3\).  There are infinitely many primes congruent to two modulo three:
if \(p_1,\ldots,p_r\) were all of them, any prime divisor congruent to two
modulo three of \(3p_1\cdots p_r-1\) would be new.  Thus
\eqref{eq:general-group-adams-mobius-obstruction} couples the two non-trivial
determinant ratios at infinitely many arithmetically distributed powers and
does not satisfy the scalar finite-degree Mahler equation used in
\Cref{thm:determinant-boundary-lifting}.

Accordingly the proof of a polynomial radial sampling bound for every finite
group stops here.  The missing input is a special-value theorem forcing
rationality from one algebraic common collision for this coupled cyclotomic
Euler system, together with an effective degree or zero bound for the resulting
certificate.  Neither the scalar lifting theorem nor the Pad\'e bound proved
above supplies that input.  The finite computation in
\Cref{sec:exactness} only checks the displayed \(C_3\) coefficients in a
finite range.
\end{remark}

This mechanism is strictly weaker than full \(G\)-cospectrality, equivalently
represented cospectrality in every irreducible representation:
\eqref{eq:c2-collision-first-determinant} and
\eqref{eq:c2-collision-second-determinant} are different.  Represented
cospectrality in every irreducible representation instead requires equality
of the full twisted spectra and therefore preserves the complete periodic profile
\cite{BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions,
CavaleriDonno2022Cospectrality}.

We next delimit the finite minimality assertion.  A \emph{regular polynomial
\(C_2\)-certificate} is a tuple \((A,B,B',Q)\) in which \(A\) is a primitive
\(m\)-out-regular integral adjacency matrix, \(B,B'\) are compatible sign
blocks, \(Q\in\ZZ[z]\) is non-constant with \(Q(0)=Q(1/m)=1\), both sign
blocks have spectral radius less than \(m\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-polynomial-certificate}
 \det(I-zB)=Q(z^2),
 \qquad
 \det(I-zB')=Q(z)^2.
\end{equation}
Its complexity is ordered lexicographically as
\[
 \bigl(2,\deg Q,|V|,m,|E|\bigr).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Minimality in the regular polynomial certificate class]
\label{prop:c2-certificate-minimality}
The certificate in \Cref{thm:c2-boundary-collision} has complexity
\((2,2,4,4,16)\), and this is the least complexity of a regular polynomial
\(C_2\)-certificate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first coordinate is fixed by the non-trivial binary group.  Since
\(Q(0)=Q(1/m)=1\), a non-constant \(Q\) cannot have degree one, so
\(\deg Q\ge2\).  If \(\deg Q=2\), then \(Q(z^2)\) has degree four, whereas
\(\deg\det(I-zB)\le |V|\); hence \(|V|\ge4\).

It remains to exclude \(m=2,3\) at \(\deg Q=2\) and \(|V|=4\).  Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimality-q-form}
 Q(z)=1-kz+kmz^2,
 \qquad k\in\ZZ\setminus\{0\},
\end{equation}
which follows from \(Q(1/m)=1\).  When \(m=2\), the characteristic
polynomial forced by the second identity in
\eqref{eq:regular-polynomial-certificate} is
\((x^2-kx+2k)^2\).  If its roots have modulus strictly less than two, their
product gives \(|k|<2\).  The choice \(k=-1\) has a root \(-2\), and
\(k=0\) makes \(Q\) constant; therefore strict gap forces \(k=1\).

There are exactly ten non-negative integral four-entry rows of sum two,
namely \(2e_i\) and \(e_i+e_j\) for \(i<j\).  We exhaust the resulting
\(10^4\) matrices.  Primitivity is decided exactly by testing positivity of
a power through exponent ten, the four-dimensional Wielandt bound.  This
leaves \(2208\) primitive base matrices.  For each such matrix, every
compatible signed entry is enumerated from
\[
 B_{ij}\in\{-A_{ij},-A_{ij}+2,\ldots,A_{ij}\}.
\]
For an integral \(4\times4\) matrix, put \(t_r=\Tr(B^r)\).  Newton's
identities give
\[
 \det(I-zB)=1-t_1z+\frac{t_1^2-t_2}{2}z^2
 -\frac{t_1^3-3t_1t_2+2t_3}{6}z^3+\det(B)z^4.
\]
Thus the two target polynomials
\(1-z^2+2z^4\) and \((1-z+2z^2)^2\) are tested by the integer quadruples
\[
 (t_1,t_2,t_3,\det B)=(0,2,0,2),
 \qquad (2,-6,-10,4),
\]
respectively.  The exhaustive integer enumeration finds \(48\) primitive
bases supporting the first target and no primitive base supporting the
second.  Hence no certificate exists for \(m=2\).  The enumeration and all
counts are reproduced by the exact-arithmetic verifier cited in
\Cref{sec:exactness}.

When \(m=3\), compatibility gives
\(B\mathbf1\equiv A\mathbf1\equiv\mathbf1\pmod2\).  Therefore
\(I-B\) is singular over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\det(I-B)\) is even.  On the
other hand, the first identity in
\eqref{eq:regular-polynomial-certificate} and
\eqref{eq:minimality-q-form} give
\[
 \det(I-B)=Q(1)=1-k+3k=1+2k,
\]
which is odd.  Thus \(m=3\) is impossible.  Since \(m>1\), it follows that
\(m\ge4\), and regularity gives \(|E|=m|V|\ge16\).  The matrices in
\Cref{thm:c2-boundary-collision} attain every bound.
\end{proof}

The minimality assertion is deliberately confined to the finite polynomial
certificate class in \eqref{eq:regular-polynomial-certificate}; it is not a
minimality statement for the rational certificates decided by
\Cref{thm:effective-rational-mahler-coboundary}, and it does not address
positive rational functions outside the determinant-parity hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:determinant-boundary-lifting}.

Although one Perron-point value loses information, an analytic radial profile
restores it.  The following is a sufficient recovery criterion; no optimality
claim about the amount of profile data is intended.

\begin{theorem}[Radial-profile recovery]
\label{thm:radial-profile-recovery}
Let \(\tau,\tau':E(\Gamma)\to G\) be two labellings on the same primitive
base matrix with Perron root \(\lambda>1\).  For every conjugacy class \(C\),
define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-profile-difference}
 D_C(z):=\sum_{n\ge1}
 \bigl(p_{n,C}(\tau)-p_{n,C}(\tau')\bigr)\log(1-z^n),
\end{equation}
where the logarithm is normalized at zero.  Every \(D_C\) is analytic on
\(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\).  If, for every \(C\), \(D_C\) vanishes on a set
\(S\subset\{|z|<\lambda^{-1}\}\) having an accumulation point in that disc,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-profile-recovery}
 p_{n,C}(\tau)=p_{n,C}(\tau')
 \qquad(n\ge1,\ C\in\operatorname{Conj}(G)).
\end{equation}
It is enough, for example, to take
\(S=\{\lambda^{-r}:r\ge2\}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every primitive length-\(n\) orbit contributes \(n\) period-\(n\) points, so
\(np_{n,C}\le\Tr(A^n)=O(\lambda^n)\).  Since
\(-\log(1-z^n)=O(|z|^n)\) uniformly on compact subdiscs of
\(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\), \eqref{eq:radial-profile-difference} converges normally
there and defines an analytic function.

If \(D_C\) vanishes on \(S\), the analytic identity theorem gives
\(D_C\equiv0\).  Suppose that \(n_0\) is the least length at which the two
primitive counts for this class differ.  In the expansion
\[
 D_C(z)=-\sum_{n\ge1}\sum_{r\ge1}
  \frac{p_{n,C}(\tau)-p_{n,C}(\tau')}{r}z^{nr},
\]
every contribution to the coefficient of \(z^{n_0}\) from a proper divisor
of \(n_0\) vanishes by minimality.  That coefficient is therefore
\(-\bigl(p_{n_0,C}(\tau)-p_{n_0,C}(\tau')\bigr)\ne0\), a contradiction.
This proves \eqref{eq:radial-profile-recovery}.  Finally,
\(\lambda^{-r}<\lambda^{-1}\) for \(r\ge2\), and these points accumulate at
zero inside the disc.
\end{proof}

For the collision in \Cref{thm:c2-boundary-collision},
\eqref{eq:c2-collision-exact-difference} equals \(2\log Q(z)\), and
\(Q(z)=1\) on \(0<z\le1/4\) only at \(z=1/4\).  Thus its equality is an
isolated Perron-boundary evaluation and cannot persist on any set allowed by
\Cref{thm:radial-profile-recovery}.
