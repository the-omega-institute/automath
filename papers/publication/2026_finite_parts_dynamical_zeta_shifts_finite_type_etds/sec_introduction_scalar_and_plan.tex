\subsection{Scalar finite-matrix inputs}

We record only the scalar inputs needed for notation and comparison.
They are proved later in \Cref{sec:finite-part}. After removing the
Perron factor one obtains the reduced determinant
\[
  C(A):=\det\nolimits'(I-A/\lambda)^{-1}
\]
and the reduced spectral polynomial
\[
  F_A(t):=\frac{\det(I-tA/\lambda)}{1-t}
  =\prod_{j=2}^d\left(1-\frac{\mu_j}{\lambda}t\right),
\]
where \(\mu_2,\dots,\mu_d\) are the non-Perron eigenvalues. The
Abel-regularised finite-part constant
\[
  \FP(A):=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\bigl(P_A(r)+\log(1-r)\bigr),
  \qquad
  P_A(r):=\sum_{n\ge 1}p_n(A)\lambda^{-n}r^n,
\]
measures the deviation of the primitive orbit sum from the harmonic
series.

The scalar normalisation used in the class-product theorem chain is
\begin{align*}
  \FP(A)
  &=\log C(A)
    +\sum_{k\ge 2}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}
      \log\det(I-\lambda^{-k}A)^{-1}\\
  &=\log C(A)
    +\sum_{n\ge 1}p_n(A)
      \bigl(\lambda^{-n}+\log(1-\lambda^{-n})\bigr).
\end{align*}
The determinant and primitive-orbit closed formulas for \(\FP(A)\) are proved
in \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form}; the identity
\(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\) and the scalar orbit-Mertens asymptotic
\[
  \sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_n(A)}{\lambda^n}
  =\log N+\mathcal M(A)+o(1)
\]
are \Cref{cor:finite-part-mertens-asymptotic}.  These scalar facts normalise
the trivial Peter--Weyl channel; the non-trivial class coordinates and the
eta-gap certificate come from the finite twisted matrices in
\Cref{prop:eta-gap-finite-state-certificate,prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}.
The fact that fixed-matrix scalar records do not determine non-trivial
fixed-label Perron values is proved later in
\Cref{thm:finite-part-scalar-interface-no-descent,rem:fixed-matrix-scalar-interface-only};
it is a boundary statement for the scalar normalisation, not a second source
of class constants.

Under the twisted spectral gap, the body records only the shadow facts needed
to interpret the \(S_3\) obstruction in
\Cref{thm:abelian-shadow-defect,lem:cyclic-quotients-one-dimensional-characters,thm:cyclic-detection-boundary}.
This support package consists of the orthogonal decomposition into abelian
shadow and genuine non-abelian defect, together with the identification of the
one-dimensional sector seen by cyclic quotients of the fixed cocycle.  General
quotient functoriality and cyclic-observability reconstruction are not part of
the theorem chain here.  The scalar root-of-unity determinant layers
\(\log\det(I-\omega_q^jzA)^{-1}\) occur only in the fixed-matrix cyclic-lift
comparison model of \Cref{sec:finite-part}, separate from quotient pullbacks
of the fixed cocycle.

The second scalar component is the fixed-matrix lift model, adjacent to inverse
spectral questions for SFTs \cite{LindMarcus1995}.  In this article it is used
only as finite-dimensional normalisation and as a comparison boundary separating
scalar cyclic-lift records from non-abelian class-profile invariants.  When the
terminology of cyclic, torsion, or exponential scalar records appears below, it
serves only this boundary role; reconstruction or identifiability questions for
those records are not inputs to the theorem chain below.

\subsection{Relation to existing work}

The natural comparison class is the symbolic-dynamics and dynamical
\(\zeta\)-function literature on prime cycles, finite extensions, and
twisted determinant factorizations.  In that literature the leading
Chebotarev distribution and Frobenius-class product asymptotics provide the
classical comparison conventions.  Parry--Pollicott's Chebotarev theorem for Axiom~A
Galois coverings \cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev}, Noorani--Parry's
finite homogeneous-extension theorem for shifts
\cite{NooraniParry1992ChebotarevShifts}, Sharp's Mertens theorem for
Axiom~A flows \cite{Sharp1991Mertens}, and Mesliza--Noorani's
Frobenius-class Mertens product theorem
\cite{MeslizaNoorani1999FrobeniusMertens} are cited as the classical comparison
layer for the renormalized class-Mertens and Frobenius-product constants.  In
the finite SFT setting of this paper, the primary theorem package keeps the
Perron-boundary peripheral data instead of imposing the strict twisted gap:
the exponent is the class boundary weight \(\delta_C\), the product constant is
\(K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and the non-scalar coordinates are the reduced
determinant finite parts \(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\).  The classical
references calibrate the exponent scale, orientation, and naming conventions
rather than supply an external hypothesis.

At the determinant level, the paper is closer to Fried's twisted periodic
point formulae \cite{Fried1983PeriodicPointsTwistedCoefficients,Fried1983HomologicalIdentitiesClosedOrbits}, the twisted
Perron--Frobenius framework of Adachi--Sunada
\cite{AdachiSunada1987TwistedPF}, and graph-Artin determinant theories of
Hashimoto, Sunada, Stark--Terras, and related finite-graph covering work
\cite{Hashimoto1989ZetaFiniteGraphs,Hashimoto1990ZetaLFunctionsFiniteGraphs,Hashimoto1992ArtinDensityPrimeCycles,Sunada1986LFunctionsGeometry,StarkTerras1996FiniteGraphsCoverings,StarkTerras2000FiniteGraphsCoveringsII,StarkTerras2007FiniteGraphsCoveringsIII}.  Those works encode periodic
or prime-cycle data by determinants or representation-theoretic
\(L\)-functions.  The present finite-state calculation fixes one
finite-group SFT cocycle and asks the inverse question needed for the
constants: from the full family of twisted determinants, recover the
primitive Peter--Weyl channels.  The extra operation is the finite-group
Adams correction \(\psi^m\phi(g)=\phi(g^m)\), forced by repetitions of a
primitive orbit.

Finite group actions and extensions of SFTs form the adjacent symbolic
setting.  Adler--Kitchens--Marcus \cite{AdlerKitchensMarcus1985FiniteGroupActions},
Fiebig \cite{Fiebig1993PeriodicFiniteGroupActions}, Silver--Williams
\cite{SilverWilliams2005InvariantFiniteGroupActions}, Boyle--Schmieding
\cite{BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions}, Boyle--Carlsen--Eilers
\cite{BoyleCarlsenEilers2020FlowEquivalenceGSFT}, and Matsumoto
\cite{Matsumoto2016ExtensionsSubshiftsFiniteGroups} treat finite actions,
quotients, periodic data, and classification or obstruction questions.
Here the extension is fixed; the issue is not existence or
flow-equivalence classification but determinant-level recovery of the
renormalized primitive-channel constants, the class weights \(\delta_C\), and
the Adams-corrected fixed-label product constants.

Recent finite-data and obstruction papers give a useful comparison for what is
finite and what is invariant in related dynamical settings.  Coles's finite
data criteria for shifts, Humbert's first-Ruelle-resonance theorem for Anosov
flows with smooth potentials, O'Hare's finite-data obstructions, and
the finite-state Artin/representation-theoretic examples of
Crismale--Del Vecchio--Griseta--Rossi
\cite{Coles2025VassilievWritheAxiomA,Humbert2025FirstRuelleResonance,OHare2025FiniteDataRigidity,CrismaleDelVecchioGrisetaRossi2025NoncommutativeSkewProduct}
provide comparison points for related finite-data and obstruction problems.
The present paper works with finite twisted matrices, character-table
orthogonality, and the finite Perron-boundary semisimplicity certificate,
rather than with Banach-space resonance or reconstruction machinery outside
the fixed finite-matrix setting.  The condition \(\eta<\lambda\) appears below
only as the strict-gap branch where the renormalized boundary data reduce to
ordinary Perron-point values.

The comparison with the preceding literature is as follows.  Periodic twisted
determinants and the Chebotarev/Mertens comparison layer are classical.  The
determinant-level contribution here is the finite
Adams--M\"obius inverse formula that recovers primitive Peter--Weyl channels
rather than periodic Artin labels, and then evaluates the resulting
Perron-boundary finite parts.  The narrow priority claim is the internally
derived constant-coordinate layer:
\Cref{thm:renormalized-class-mertens,thm:renormalized-fixed-label-determinant,thm:renormalized-frobenius-product,thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout}
recover \(\delta_C\), \(M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\),
\(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and \(K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\) from the finite
boundary tuple.  In the strict branch the familiar fixed-label Euler transform
\[
  F_\varrho(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\varrho(z^r)}{r}
\]
is the specialization of the reduced value \(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\).  Its
branch-compatible determinant finite part, its Fourier readout into
\(K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and the obstruction to replacing it by the periodic
Artin logarithm \(\mathcal L_\varrho\) are the new constant-level layer, with
the classical product theorems cited only as comparison for the same asymptotic
scale.

\subsection{Plan of the paper}

\Cref{sec:preliminaries} fixes the symbolic, determinant, and finite-group
notation.  \Cref{sec:chebotarev} proves the Adams--M\"obius primitive inversion:
twisted determinants give primitive Peter--Weyl channels, the finite
Perron-boundary certificate gives renormalized Frobenius-class Mertens
constants, and the fixed-label determinant finite parts give the
Adams-corrected product constants.  \Cref{sec:eta-gap} studies the strict
twisted gap \(\eta<\lambda\) as a finite-dimensional specialization criterion
for \Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization} and the older clean formulas.
\Cref{sec:shadows} records the abelian-shadow projection and cyclic-observable
support boundary needed for the non-abelian obstruction example; it does not
develop a quotient reconstruction theory.

\Cref{sec:finite-part} isolates the scalar finite-part normalisation used for
the trivial channel and proves the projection boundary for scalar comparison
data.  The appendices give the \(S_3\) model computations
\Cref{app:s3-model-computations}, the rational interval estimates
\Cref{app:s3-log-certificates}, and the fixed-label rational enclosure data
\Cref{app:fixed-label-local-certificates}.  These appendices supply the exact
arithmetic behind the displayed \(S_3\) inequalities used in the
Artin-substitution obstruction.
