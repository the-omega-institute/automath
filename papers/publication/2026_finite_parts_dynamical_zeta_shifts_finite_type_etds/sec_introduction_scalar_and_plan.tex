\subsection{Scalar finite-matrix inputs}

We record only the scalar inputs needed for notation and comparison.
They are proved later in \Cref{sec:finite-part}. After removing the
Perron factor one obtains the reduced determinant
\[
  C(A):=\det\nolimits'(I-A/\lambda)^{-1}
\]
and the reduced spectral polynomial
\[
  F_A(t):=\frac{\det(I-tA/\lambda)}{1-t}
  =\prod_{j=2}^d\left(1-\frac{\mu_j}{\lambda}t\right),
\]
where \(\mu_2,\dots,\mu_d\) are the non-Perron eigenvalues. The
Abel-regularised finite-part constant
\[
  \FP(A):=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\bigl(P_A(r)+\log(1-r)\bigr),
  \qquad
  P_A(r):=\sum_{n\ge 1}p_n(A)\lambda^{-n}r^n,
\]
measures the deviation of the primitive orbit sum from the harmonic
series.

The scalar normalisation used in the class-product theorem chain is
\begin{align*}
  \FP(A)
  &=\log C(A)
    +\sum_{k\ge 2}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}
      \log\det(I-\lambda^{-k}A)^{-1}\\
  &=\log C(A)
    +\sum_{n\ge 1}p_n(A)
      \bigl(\lambda^{-n}+\log(1-\lambda^{-n})\bigr).
\end{align*}
The determinant and primitive-orbit closed formulas for \(\FP(A)\) are proved
in \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form}; the identity
\(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\) and the scalar orbit-Mertens asymptotic
\[
  \sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_n(A)}{\lambda^n}
  =\log N+\mathcal M(A)+o(1)
\]
are \Cref{cor:finite-part-mertens-asymptotic}.  These scalar facts normalise
only the trivial Peter--Weyl channel.  They supply no non-trivial class
coordinate, no eta-gap certificate, and no additional hypothesis for Main
Theorems~I--III; those data come from the finite twisted matrices in
\Cref{prop:eta-gap-finite-state-certificate,prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}.
The introductory roadmap statements in this subsection retain their older
\texttt{stmt:} aliases, but the numbered labels below are theorem-like
interfaces whose proofs are discharged by the body propositions, lemmas, and
corollaries cited in the proof paragraphs immediately following them.

\begin{proposition}[Roadmap: scalar finite-part computation]
\label{prop:intro-scalar-finite-part-computation}
\label{thm:intro-finite-part}
\label{stmt:intro-scalar-finite-part-computation}
\label{stmt:intro-finite-part}
For a primitive non-negative matrix \(A\) with Perron root \(\lambda>1\), the
body proves
\begin{align*}
  \FP(A)
  &=\log C(A)
    +\sum_{k\ge 2}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}
      \log\det(I-\lambda^{-k}A)^{-1} \\
  &=\log C(A)+\sum_{n\ge1}p_n(A)
    \bigl(\lambda^{-n}+\log(1-\lambda^{-n})\bigr),
\end{align*}
and hence \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\).  This is exactly the
trivial-channel normalisation in Main Theorems~II--III.  The determinant and
primitive-orbit formulae are
\Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form,cor:finite-part-mertens-asymptotic};
the fact that fixed-matrix scalar records do not determine non-trivial
fixed-label Perron values is
\Cref{thm:finite-part-scalar-interface-no-descent,cor:fixed-matrix-scalar-interface-only}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  The two displayed formulae for \(\FP(A)\) are exactly
  \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form}.  The identity
  \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\) is
  \Cref{cor:finite-part-mertens-asymptotic}.  The cited no-descent theorem and
  corollary show that these scalar fixed-matrix data supply only the trivial
  channel normalisation and cannot be used as non-trivial fixed-label Perron
  data.
\end{proof}

Under the twisted spectral gap, the body proves the quotient-shadow description
of the normalised primitive class profile in
\Cref{thm:quotient-functoriality,cor:abelian-cyclic-shadow,lem:cyclic-quotient-versus-root-layer,thm:abelian-shadow-defect,lem:cyclic-quotients-one-dimensional-characters}.
It includes scalar projection onto the one-dimensional Peter--Weyl sector,
functoriality for finite quotients, the orthogonal decomposition into abelian
shadow and genuine non-abelian defect, and exact detection of the
one-dimensional sector by cyclic quotients of the fixed cocycle.  The
root-of-unity determinant layers \(\log\det(I-\omega_q^jzA)^{-1}\) occur only
in the separate canonical cyclic-lift comparison model, not as invariants of
arbitrary cyclic quotients of the fixed cocycle.

\begin{corollary}[Quotient shadow and scalar boundary]
\label{thm:intro-quotient-shadow}
\label{stmt:intro-quotient-shadow}
Assume the hypotheses of Main Theorem~II.  For every finite quotient
\(q:G\twoheadrightarrow Q\) and every class function \(f\) on \(Q\),
the pullback \(q^\ast f\) satisfies
\[
  \mathcal L^G_{q^\ast f}(z)=\mathcal L^Q_f(z),
  \qquad
  \Pi^G_{q^\ast f}(z)=\Pi^Q_f(z)
  \quad(|z|<\lambda^{-1}).
\]
At the Perron point, the cyclic quotients of the fixed cocycle detect exactly
the one-dimensional Peter--Weyl shadow of the normalised primitive class
profile, and the orthogonal complement is the non-abelian defect.  The scalar
root-of-unity determinant layers
\(\log\det(I-\omega_q^jzA)^{-1}\) belong only to the canonical cyclic-lift
comparison model and do not give invariants of arbitrary cyclic quotients of
\(\tau\).  The scalar finite part contributes only the trivial-channel
normalisation \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The two displayed pullback identities are precisely the determinant and
primitive-channel functoriality of
\Cref{thm:quotient-functoriality}.  The cyclic detection assertion follows by
combining the cyclic-quotient readout of
\Cref{cor:abelian-cyclic-shadow} with the character-theoretic separation in
\Cref{lem:cyclic-quotients-one-dimensional-characters}; the complementary
higher-dimensional component is the non-abelian defect of
\Cref{thm:abelian-shadow-defect}.  The distinction between inherited cyclic
quotients of \(\tau\) and the scalar root-of-unity layers of the canonical
cyclic lift is exactly
\Cref{prop:canonical-cyclic-lift-root-layer,lem:cyclic-quotient-versus-root-layer}.
Finally, the trivial-channel scalar normalisation is
\Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form,cor:finite-part-mertens-asymptotic}.
\end{proof}

The second scalar component is the separate fixed-matrix lift model,
related to inverse spectral questions for SFTs
\cite{LindMarcus1995}.  In this article we use only the finite-dimensional
normalisation and comparison boundary supplied by that model.  The
branch-lifted reconstruction and one-layer Fourier recovery arguments are not
used here; the retained material only distinguishes scalar cyclic-lift records
from non-abelian class-profile invariants.  The terminology of cyclic,
torsion, and exponential spectral records is fixed in
\Cref{def:spectral-fingerprints}.

\subsection{Relation to existing work}

The natural comparison class is the symbolic-dynamics and dynamical
\(\zeta\)-function literature on prime cycles, finite extensions, and
twisted determinant factorizations.  In that literature the leading
Chebotarev distribution and Frobenius-class product asymptotics are
classical inputs.  Parry--Pollicott's Chebotarev theorem for Axiom~A
Galois coverings \cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev}, Noorani--Parry's
finite homogeneous-extension theorem for shifts
\cite{NooraniParry1992ChebotarevShifts}, Sharp's Mertens theorem for
Axiom~A flows \cite{Sharp1991Mertens}, and Mesliza--Noorani's
Frobenius-class Mertens product theorem
\cite{MeslizaNoorani1999FrobeniusMertens} supply the background against
which Main Theorems~II--III should be read.  The exponent
\(|C|/|G|\) and the existence of Frobenius-class products are therefore
not claimed as new here.  The theorem-local contribution is the finite
Adams--M\"obius determinant recovery of the class constants after that
classical leading term has been separated.

At the determinant level, the paper is closer to Fried's twisted periodic
point formulae \cite{Fried1983PeriodicPointsTwistedCoefficients,Fried1983HomologicalIdentitiesClosedOrbits}, the twisted
Perron--Frobenius framework of Adachi--Sunada
\cite{AdachiSunada1987TwistedPF}, and graph-Artin determinant theories of
Hashimoto, Sunada, Stark--Terras, and related finite-graph covering work
\cite{Hashimoto1989ZetaFiniteGraphs,Hashimoto1990ZetaLFunctionsFiniteGraphs,Hashimoto1992ArtinDensityPrimeCycles,Sunada1986LFunctionsGeometry,StarkTerras1996FiniteGraphsCoverings,StarkTerras2000FiniteGraphsCoveringsII,StarkTerras2007FiniteGraphsCoveringsIII}.  Those works encode periodic
or prime-cycle data by determinants or representation-theoretic
\(L\)-functions.  The present finite-state calculation fixes one
finite-group SFT cocycle and asks the inverse question needed for the
constants: from the full family of twisted determinants, recover the
primitive Peter--Weyl channels.  The extra operation is the finite-group
Adams correction \(\psi^m\phi(g)=\phi(g^m)\), forced by repetitions of a
primitive orbit.

Finite group actions and extensions of SFTs form the adjacent symbolic
setting.  Adler--Kitchens--Marcus \cite{AdlerKitchensMarcus1985FiniteGroupActions},
Fiebig \cite{Fiebig1993PeriodicFiniteGroupActions}, Silver--Williams
\cite{SilverWilliams2005InvariantFiniteGroupActions}, Boyle--Schmieding
\cite{BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions}, Boyle--Carlsen--Eilers
\cite{BoyleCarlsenEilers2020FlowEquivalenceGSFT}, and Matsumoto
\cite{Matsumoto2016ExtensionsSubshiftsFiniteGroups} treat finite actions,
quotients, periodic data, and classification or obstruction questions.
Here the extension is fixed; the issue is not existence or
flow-equivalence classification but determinant-level recovery of
\(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\), and the
Adams-corrected Euler-product constants.

Recent finite-data and obstruction papers give a useful comparison for what is
finite and what is invariant in related dynamical settings.  Coles's finite
data criteria for shifts, Humbert's first-Ruelle-resonance theorem for Anosov
flows with smooth potentials, O'Hare's finite-data obstructions, and
the finite-state Artin/representation-theoretic examples of
Crismale--Del Vecchio--Griseta--Rossi
\cite{Coles2025VassilievWritheAxiomA,Humbert2025FirstRuelleResonance,OHare2025FiniteDataRigidity,CrismaleDelVecchioGrisetaRossi2025NoncommutativeSkewProduct}
are comparison background rather than inputs.  The present paper uses only
finite twisted matrices, character-table orthogonality, and the stated
Perron-point gap \(\eta<\lambda\); it does not import Banach-space resonance
or reconstruction machinery outside the fixed finite-matrix setting.

The theorem-by-theorem calibration is as follows.  Main Theorem~I is new in
this finite SFT setting as an inverse determinant formula for primitive
Peter--Weyl channels: the periodic twisted determinants are classical objects,
but the Adams--M\"obius correction recovers primitive labels rather than
periodic Artin labels.  Main Theorem~II uses the classical Chebotarev/Mertens
leading input under \(\eta<\lambda\) and adds the explicit finite determinant
formula for the additive constants \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\).  Main
Theorem~III uses the classical Frobenius-class product exponent and existence
statement, but its constant layer is finite-state and Adams corrected: the
fixed-label Euler transform
\[
  F_\varrho(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\varrho(z^r)}{r},
\]
its branch-compatible determinant expression, its Fourier readout into
\(K_C\), and the obstruction to replacing it by the periodic Artin logarithm
\(\mathcal L_\varrho\).  Thus the novelty is not a new class-density exponent;
it is the determinant/Adams/fixed-label constant formula after the classical
asymptotic form is in place.

Thus this paper fixes one finite SFT cocycle and proves the determinant
recovery of primitive Peter--Weyl channels, the Perron-point class Mertens
constants, and the Adams-corrected fixed-label Euler-product constants.  The
quotient, shadow, eta-gap, and \(S_3\) statements later in the paper are
supporting corollaries and finite examples: they explain functoriality,
scalar no-descent, the boundary of the strict twisted gap, and a concrete
obstruction to replacing \(F_\varrho\) by \(\mathcal L_\varrho\).  They are
not separate classification theorems competing with Main Theorems~I--III.
\subsection{Plan of the paper}

\Cref{sec:preliminaries} fixes the symbolic, determinant, and finite-group
notation.  \Cref{sec:chebotarev} proves the main Adams--M\"obius chain: twisted
determinants give primitive Peter--Weyl channels, Perron-point evaluation gives
Frobenius-class Mertens constants, and the fixed-label Euler transform gives
the Adams-corrected product constants.  \Cref{sec:eta-gap} studies the strict
twisted gap \(\eta<\lambda\), the finite-dimensional hypothesis that permits
non-trivial channels to be evaluated at the Perron point.  \Cref{sec:shadows}
develops quotient functoriality, abelian shadows, cyclic detection, and the
non-abelian defect, while \Cref{sec:finite-part} isolates the scalar finite
part and the no-descent boundary needed for the trivial-channel normalisation.
The canonical cyclic-lift scalar results serve as fixed-matrix comparison
material rather than inputs to the finite-group determinant inversion.

The remaining compiled material is arranged by support role.  The
lower-order-tail noninterference statement
\Cref{prop:main-chain-secondary-noninterference} is part of the main
class-Mertens determinant section, where it records that compensated tails
below the Perron boundary do not alter the constants in Main Theorems~II--III.
The scalar finite-part and projection-boundary results are part of the article's
main proof support: they are proved in \Cref{sec:finite-part} and are used only
for the trivial-channel normalisation.  The appendices contain the \(S_3\)
model computations \Cref{app:s3-model-computations}, the \(S_3\) rational
interval arithmetic \Cref{app:s3-log-certificates}, and the fixed-label
rational enclosure data
\Cref{app:fixed-label-local-certificates}.  Scalar cyclic-lift comparisons are
used only in the fixed-matrix boundary form stated here, and extended
secondary-edge refinements, cyclic-lift reconstruction theorems, non-abelian
root-of-unity shadow refinements, or formalisation projects are not inputs to
the present theorem chain.
