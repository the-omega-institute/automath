\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Assuming \textup{(KN85)}, for odd-Adams-invariant extensions by any finite
abelian \(2\)-group \(G\) over two primitive bases of at most \(V\) vertices,
\(2V\lceil\log_2(4V)\rceil\) radial locations in the common open Perron
interval determine the complete primitive length--element profile, provided
that one location is algebraic.  This radial depth is independent of the rank
and exponent of \(G\), but each location supplies a full
\(\abs G\)-component vector, for \(\abs G M(V)\) scalar comparisons in total.
The bases need not coincide.  The theorem contains the elementary
\((C_2)^r\) result and covers standard extensions with genuine higher
two-power holonomy.  A
strict twisted gap permits centered, not raw, class profiles at a Perron
endpoint in the binary scalar theorem.  The determinant ratio governing the
test is canonically the ratio of the standard Artin--Mazur zeta functions of
the two binary covers.  Under the same assumption, one algebraic collision
produces a unique rational Mahler certificate.  For every fixed \(p\ge2\),
the total-degree estimate bounds the numerator-plus-denominator degree
of this multiplicative certificate, from the reduced input degree alone, by
\(b_p(D)=O_p(D\log D)\).  Its order is sharp abstractly and on realizable
standard \(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.  The explicit height and fixed-\(p\) bit
complexity estimates are useful further consequences; the finite Pad\'e
system is rational reconstruction under the resulting cap.  The exact four-vertex
pair shows why a single centered Perron-boundary vector cannot be an inverse
invariant; every corresponding raw class profile diverges to \(-\infty\).
For every \(m\), an explicit binary pair on a common \(4m+2\)-vertex positive
base has \(m\) distinct rational collisions but different periodic data,
giving the unconditional lower bound
\(\mathsf N_{C_2}(V)\ge\lfloor(V-2)/4\rfloor+1\).
For each odd prime \(\ell\), a pair on \(\ell(2m+1)\) vertices has \(m\)
rational collisions, agrees through length \(m\), and first differs at length
\(m+1\) by an explicit element vector.
The squarefree estimate
\(2(p-1)\deg\rad(AB)\le D\), the collision--jet inequality, and these
odd-prime constructions are unconditional.  Subject to the explicitly
quoted assumption \textup{(KN85)}, they also give the stronger linear
prime-primary theorem and the upper bound \(\mathsf N_{C_2}(V)\le V\).

The determinant lifting theorem is a specialization of a parity-free rational
critical \(p\)-Mahler statement.  Its coefficient recursion gives the exact
bound
\[
 (pq)^{2n-1}f_n\in\ZZ,\qquad
 \log d_h\le(2h-1)\log(pq),
\]
which is sufficient for Kumiko Nishioka's verified 1982 special-value
theorem.  The next algebraic-to-rational implication is precisely
\textup{(KN85)}.  Keiji Nishioka's 1985 bibliographic record is verified, but
the original statement is not; every result using that implication therefore
includes it in its hypotheses.  Determinant parity is a convenient strong
sufficient condition yielding integral coefficients through
Dieudonn\'e--Dwork, not an essential lifting hypothesis.  The endpoint form
still uses the strict gap only to continue through the Perron circle.

Together with \textup{(KN85)}, the full dyadic Adams--M\"obius collapse proves
the headline theorem.
Conditional on \textup{(KN85)}, the prime-primary collapse reduces each
relatively unit-Adams-invariant channel to one \(\ell\)-Mahler orbit.  The
same mechanism does not presently
yield a polynomial sample
bound for every finite group.  For \(C_3\), Adams--M\"obius inversion already couples
two character determinants at infinitely many arithmetically distributed
powers, rather than one lacunary Mahler orbit.  A general finite-group theorem
therefore requires a new special-value and effective zero theory for these
coupled Euler systems.  Generic \(C_4\) outside the relative unit-Adams-invariant
subclass has the analogous coupled obstruction.  In its absence, the proved
general statement remains unconditional analytic recovery from a profile set
with an interior accumulation point.

The novelty claim is correspondingly narrow.  No priority claim is made for
either Nishioka interface or from the antecedent search.
Frobenius-class Euler products, their character expansion,
algebraic-solution rationality for rational functional equations, and the
general periodic-data dictionary are cited background.  The original Mahler
increment is the input-only \(b_p(D)\) divisor estimate for the multiplicative
certificate, its \(\Omega_p(D\log D)\) family, and the transfer of that
sharpness to realizable \(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.  The height bound and fixed-
\(p\) integer bit complexity are useful but incremental.  Bare existence and
decidability reduce, by logarithmic differentiation, to the published
additive and linear Mahler algorithms; Pad\'e reconstruction is largely
standard after a degree cap is known.  The central conditional result remains
the cross-base odd-Adams-invariant abelian two-group inverse theorem with the
\(O(V\log V)\) radial budget.  The prime-primary linear strengthening is
conditional on \textup{(KN85)}.

The principal dynamical theorem remains an inverse theorem for the
paper-specific sampled fixed-label Euler values.  Its standard dynamical
conclusion is represented periodic-data equivalence, via the cited
determinant/periodic-data dictionary.  It does not advance conjugacy, strong
shift equivalence, cocycle cohomology, switching, Bowen--Franks or flow
equivalence, or full positivity realization.  The structural reasons are
that class counts discard marked-orbit holonomy, determinant data discard
positive matrix factorization, the relative realization lemma chooses a
large common base only after the candidate polynomials are fixed, and the
arithmetic reduction controls a scalar \(p\)-power orbit rather than the
coupled character systems required beyond the stated class.
