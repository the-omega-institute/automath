\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For a relatively unit-Adams-invariant pair of extensions by a finite abelian
\(\ell\)-group, where \(\ell\) is prime, \(K\) full radial profile vectors
and equality of the primitive data through length \(L\) determine the
complete primitive length--element profile once
\[
 K+L\ge\max\{v,v'\},
\]
provided that the radii are distinct, lie in the common open Perron interval,
and include one algebraic point.  The primitive bases may differ, and no
twisted spectral gap is required.  Thus \(V\) radial locations suffice for
bases of size at most \(V\), independently of the rank and exponent of the
prime-primary group.  For \(C_2\), the explicit common-base family gives
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{V-2}{4}\right\rfloor+1
 \le\mathsf N_{C_2}(V)\le V,
\]
so the universal radial determination number has exact linear order.

The algebraic-to-rational step no longer requires an external nonlinear
hypothesis.  The coefficient recursion for the critical product gives
\[
 (pq)^{2n-1}f_n\in\ZZ,\qquad
 \log d_h\le(2h-1)\log(pq),
\]
which verifies the arithmetic hypotheses of Kumiko Nishioka's special-value
theorem and forces algebraicity from one algebraic value.  For an algebraic
normalized solution of \(F(z)^p=H(z)F(z^p)\), logarithmic differentiation
makes \(zF'/F\) a linear \(p\)-Mahler function.  The linear
rational--transcendental dichotomy makes this logarithmic derivative
rational.  Finite monodromy then gives \(F^m\in\CC(z)\); the divisor
congruence
\[
 p\,\operatorname{ord}_\beta(F^m)
 \equiv\operatorname{ord}_{\beta^p}(F^m)\pmod m
\]
forces every order to be divisible by \(m\), since otherwise the non-zero
residue would propagate to \(p^n\) distinct preimages for every \(n\).
Consequently \(F\in\CC(z)\).  This closes the lifting argument using only
verified linear Mahler rationality and the printed special-value theorem.
Determinant parity and Dieudonn\'e--Dwork remain a stronger integrality
refinement, not a lifting hypothesis.

For a reduced input of total degree \(D\), the sharp squarefree bound
\[
 2(p-1)\deg\rad(AB)\le D,\qquad R=A/B,
\]
and the collision--jet inequality provide the linear inverse threshold.  The
separate total-degree estimate
\(\deg A+\deg B\le b_p(D)=O_p(D\log D)\) has optimal order abstractly and on
standard realizable \(C_2\)-cover zeta ratios.  The explicit height and
fixed-\(p\) bit-complexity estimates are further consequences; the finite
Pad\'e system is rational reconstruction once the degree cap is known.  The
binary determinant ratio is canonically the ratio of the ordinary
Artin--Mazur zeta functions of the two covers.

The exact four-vertex pair shows that one centered Perron-boundary vector is
not an inverse invariant; the corresponding raw class profiles diverge.
For every \(m\), a binary pair on a common \(4m+2\)-vertex positive base has
\(m\) rational collisions but different periodic data.  For every odd prime
\(\ell\), a pair on \(\ell(2m+1)\) vertices has \(m\) rational collisions,
agrees through length \(m\), and first differs at length \(m+1\) by an
explicit element vector.  These families supply the lower-bound side of the
linear theorem.

The prime-primary symmetry is substantive.  For \(C_3\), Adams--M\"obius
inversion outside the relative unit-Adams-invariant class already couples
two character determinants at infinitely many arithmetically distributed
powers.  Generic \(C_4\) pairs have the analogous obstruction.  The present
one-variable lifting and zero estimates do not provide a finite sampling
bound for those coupled Euler systems.  For arbitrary finite groups the
proved general statement therefore remains analytic recovery from a radial
profile set with an interior accumulation point.

No priority claim is made for algebraic-solution rationality, Mahler
special-value theory, Frobenius-class Euler products, their character
expansion, or the general periodic-data dictionary.  The principal new
dynamical conclusion is unconditional linear radial determination for the
relatively unit-Adams-invariant prime-primary class, together with its
realizable linear lower bounds.  The new Mahler contribution is the
input-only squarefree and total-divisor control for the multiplicative
certificate, including sharpness and its dynamically realizable transfer.
The conclusion is represented periodic-data equivalence; it does not assert
conjugacy, strong shift equivalence, cocycle cohomology, switching,
Bowen--Franks equivalence, or full positivity realization.
