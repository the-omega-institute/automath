
\subsubsection{Conventions and consequences for product coordinates}
\label{subsubsec:class-product-local-observation}

The renormalized coordinate readout is
\Cref{thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.  The next consequences are its
strict-gap, scalar-normalised specializations: they identify the finite Fourier
coordinates of the Adams-corrected positive class constants when
\Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization} reduces the Perron-boundary package to
the older \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) chart.

The fixed-coordinate equivalence, the scalar-normalised support/rank statement,
the finite truncation, and the Artin-substitution criterion are exactly the
chart and Parseval clauses of
\Cref{thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity,cor:assembled-adams-frobenius-product-formula}.
Thus equality of branch-compatible non-trivial fixed-label vectors is
equivalent, after the same scalar normalisation, to equality of all positive
class-product constants; any replacement vector has the obstruction norm
displayed in \eqref{eq:class-product-rigidity-replacement-parseval}.  This
subsection records only conventions and consequences of that chart before
turning to finite determinant packaging.
\subsubsection{Branch-normalized finite coordinate determination}
\label{subsubsec:finite-determinant-closure-classical-comparison}

We now identify the finite coordinate package that feeds the
constant map.  The determinant polynomials provide the open-disc trace data,
while the Perron-boundary values require the recorded Abel/interior branch
convention.  Product constants use the canonical real logarithm on
\(\mathbb R_{>0}\) once their positivity has been proved.

\begin{proposition}[Finite coordinate packaging for the boundary constants]
  \label{thm:renormalized-finite-data-determination}
  \label{prop:finite-data-main-chain-closure}
  \label{prop:finite-data-main-factorisation-closure}
  Assume the Perron-boundary algebraic multiplicity data of
  \Cref{def:peripheral-peter-weyl-data,lem:finite-cover-peripheral-expansion}.
  Encode the finite group by its multiplication table and let
  \[
    P_\pi(z):=\det(I-zA_\pi).
  \]
  Let \(e_G=\exp(G)\).  The open-disc primitive coefficients are determined by
  the smaller finite datum
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:open-disc-primitive-data}
    (G,\mathrm{Pow}_G,\{P_\pi(z)=\det(I-zA_\pi)\}_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}),
    \qquad
    \mathrm{Pow}_G=\{C\mapsto C^a:0\le a<e_G\}.
  \end{equation}
  This is the input used for coefficient extraction in the open disc.  The
  Perron-boundary constants use a different, larger, branch-normalized datum:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:renormalized-finite-data-tuple}
    \begin{aligned}
      \mathfrak D_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}}(A,\tau;G):=
      \Bigl(&G,\mathrm{Pow}_G,\{P_\pi(z)\}_{\pi\in\Irr(G)},\lambda,\\
      &\{\Gamma_\pi\}_{\pi\in\Irr(G)},
        \{m_{\pi,\zeta}:\zeta\in\Gamma_\pi\}_{\pi\in\Irr(G)},
        \{\Log_{\pi,\zeta}\}_{\zeta\in\Gamma_\pi,\ \pi\in\Irr(G)},
        \{\Log_{\partial,\pi}\}_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}\Bigr).
    \end{aligned}
  \end{equation}
  Here, for each \(\pi\),
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:renormalized-finite-data-factorization}
    P_\pi(t/\lambda)
    =
    \prod_{\zeta\in\Gamma_\pi}(1-t\zeta)^{m_{\pi,\zeta}}
      Q_{\partial,\pi}(t),
    \qquad Q_{\partial,\pi}(1)\ne0,
  \end{equation}
  defines \(Q_{\partial,\pi}\).  The branch
  \(\Log_{\pi,\zeta}(1-t\zeta)\) is the unique continuous branch on
  \(0\le t<1\) with value \(0\) at \(t=0\), and
  \(\Log_{\partial,\pi}Q_{\partial,\pi}(1)\) is obtained by continuing
  \(\Log Q_{\partial,\pi}(t)\), normalized at \(0\), along the same segment.
  Equivalently, for the determinant boundary evaluation the required input is
  the separated branch-normalized tuple
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:perron-boundary-data-tuple-separated}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bigl(&G,\operatorname{Pow}_G,\{P_\pi\},\lambda,\\
      &\{\Gamma_\pi\},\{m_{\pi,\zeta}\},
        \{\Log_{\pi,\zeta}\},\{\Log_{\partial,\pi}\}\bigr).
    \end{aligned}
  \end{equation}
  together with the finite group and power-map data needed for Fourier and
  Adams--M\"obius inversion.  The determinant polynomials \(P_\pi\) alone do
  not specify the Abel branches at the Perron boundary.
  Here \(\mathrm{Pow}_G\) records the conjugacy-class power maps modulo
  \(e_G\).  The finite factors \(Q_{\partial,\pi}\), the logarithmic ranks
  \(s_\pi=m_{\pi,1}\), the bounds \(\Lambda_\perp,\Lambda_\partial\), and the
  optional semisimplicity certificate \(\mathfrak s_\partial\) are finite
  algebraic consequences or verification data used in the estimates, but they
  are not additional branch coordinates.  The positive scalar/product
  logarithm is not an extra coordinate: after
  \Cref{lem:renormalized-reality-positivity} it is the real logarithm on
  \(\mathbb R_{>0}\).  The peripheral data and branch families are finite
  mathematical data in the following explicit sense.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
    \item The optional semisimplicity entry lists, for every pair
    \((\pi,\zeta)\) with \(\zeta\in\Gamma_\pi\), the exact equality
    \[
      \dim\ker(A_\pi-\lambda\zeta I)=m_{\pi,\zeta}.
    \]
    Equivalently it records that the minimal polynomial has only the first
    power of \(x-\lambda\zeta\) at that peripheral root.  This entry is a
    finite check on Jordan structure; the boundary trace and determinant
    formulae are expressed in terms of the algebraic multiplicities
    \(m_{\pi,\zeta}\) above.
    \item The branch entries record the rule that \(k=1\) is evaluated by the
    Abel path from \(t=0\): after cancelling exactly \(s_\pi\) copies of
    \(1-t\), the remaining factors are read with
    \(\Log_{\pi,\zeta}\) and \(\Log_{\partial,\pi}\).  For \(k\ge2\), one uses
    the ordinary interior power-series germ value
    \(\Log_0 P_\pi(\lambda^{-k})^{-1}\).  No sequence
    \(\{B_{k,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}\}_{k\ge1,\pi}\) is included as an input.
    Finite truncation levels, rational tail radii, and interval enclosures are
    later reproducibility outputs for approximating the derived sums; they are
    not hidden coordinates in \(\mathfrak D_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}}\).
    \item The product exponentiation convention is canonical: when
    \Cref{lem:renormalized-reality-positivity} identifies a Frobenius-class
    product constant as a positive real number, its logarithm is the real
    logarithm on \(\mathbb R_{>0}\).  No separate branch-normalization datum
    is needed.
  \end{enumerate}
  With these entries fixed, the reduced determinant coordinates are computed
  by the following finite procedure.  For \(k=1\), write
  \[
    P_\pi(t/\lambda)
    =
    \prod_{\zeta\in\Gamma_\pi}(1-t\zeta)^{m_{\pi,\zeta}}
      Q_{\partial,\pi}(t),
      \qquad Q_{\partial,\pi}(1)\ne0,
  \]
  cancel \((1-t)^{s_\pi}\), and take the Abel-branch value
  \[
    B_{1,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \mathcal B_{1,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    -\sum_{\substack{\zeta\in\Gamma_\pi\\\zeta\ne1}}
      m_{\pi,\zeta}\Log_{\pi,\zeta}(1-\zeta)
    -\Log_{\partial,\pi} Q_{\partial,\pi}(1).
  \]
  For \(k\ge2\), set
  \[
    \mathcal B_{k,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \Log_0 P_\pi(\lambda^{-k})^{-1}
  \]
  using the interior value of the normalized germ.  These
  \(\mathcal B_{k,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}\) are derived values from the finite
  datum; the sums below use them only after this computation.
  This datum determines
  all primitive class counts \(p_{n,C}\), the logarithmic densities
  \(\delta_C\), the renormalized class-Mertens constants
  \(M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\), every reduced fixed-label coordinate
  \(F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and every Frobenius-class product constant
  \(K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\).  The strict-gap tuple with \(\eta<\lambda\) is the
  specialization obtained in \Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization}; it is not
  part of the general boundary datum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The finite group component gives the conjugacy classes, the character
  table, the irreducible characters, and the Adams coefficients
  \(a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}\) through the recorded power maps.  The determinant
  polynomials determine all traces by the formal identity
  \[
    \log\det(I-zA_\pi)^{-1}
    =
    \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Tr(A_\pi^n)}{n}z^n .
  \]
  Therefore
  \Cref{cor:primitive-peter-weyl-determinant,cor:primitive-peter-weyl-trace,thm:class-count-trace-recovery}
  recover every primitive Peter--Weyl coefficient and every primitive class
  count \(p_{n,C}\) from the open-disc determinant-polynomial data and the
  finite character table.

  We next justify that the extra boundary entries in
  \(\mathfrak D_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}}\) are finite and are read from
  the stated matrix/determinant data.  The peripheral set is obtained by
  solving the finitely many characteristic equations
  \(\det(xI-A_\pi)=0\) and selecting the roots of modulus \(\lambda\).
  The multiplicity \(m_{\pi,\zeta}\) is the order of the factor
  \(x-\lambda\zeta\) in that characteristic polynomial.  If the optional
  semisimplicity entry is requested, the equality
  \(\dim\ker(A_\pi-\lambda\zeta I)=m_{\pi,\zeta}\) is a finite rank
  computation certifying absence of higher peripheral Jordan blocks.  It is
  not an input to the determinant identities or to the primitive-channel
  trace estimate of \Cref{lem:finite-cover-peripheral-expansion}.  After
  those roots are removed, \(\Lambda_\perp\) is the maximum modulus of the
  remaining finite root list and
  \(\Lambda_\partial=\max\{\Lambda_\perp,\lambda^{1/2}\}\).  Thus
  \(\Gamma_\pi\), \(m_{\pi,\zeta}\), \(s_\pi\),
  \(\Lambda_\perp\), \(\Lambda_\partial\), and
  \(\mathfrak s_\partial\), when included, are finite algebraic consequences
  of the twisted matrices together with the recorded rank checks.

  The boundary pieces in
  \(\mathfrak D_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}}(A,\tau;G)\) determine the
  non-oscillatory Perron weights
  \[
    \delta_C=
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}\chi_\pi^\ast(C)s_\pi
  \]
  directly.  They also determine the reduced determinant logarithms
  \(\mathcal B_{k,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}\): for \(k=1\) the recorded
  factorisation and Abel branch rule give
  \(B_{1,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}=\mathcal B_{1,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and for
  \(k\ge2\) the value
  \(\Log P_\pi(\lambda^{-k})^{-1}\) is an ordinary interior value on the
  recorded branch.  Hence
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-fixed-label-determinant} determines
  \[
    F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \sum_{r,m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
      \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}
        a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        B_{rm,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}
  \]
  for every \(\varrho\).

  The branch data are finite for the same reason.  At \(k=1\),
  \(B_{1,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}\) is computed by factoring
  \(P_\pi(t/\lambda)\) into the factors \(1-t\zeta\) for
  \(\zeta\in\Gamma_\pi\), cancelling exactly \(s_\pi\) copies of \(1-t\),
  and evaluating the remaining product along the Abel path \(0\le t<1\).
  At \(k\ge2\), the point \(\lambda^{-k}\) lies strictly inside every
  determinant disc since \(\rho(A_\pi)\le\lambda\), so the logarithm is the
  ordinary germ value determined by \(P_\pi\).  The series defining
  \(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\) is absolutely convergent after these branch
  choices by the majorant proved in
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-fixed-label-determinant}; hence finite
  truncations plus the displayed geometric tail determine the same unique
  value.

  It remains only to record the additive finite parts.  The same
  Adams--M\"obius determinant identity with the fixed-label repetition index
  \(r\) omitted gives, after adding \(s_\varrho\log(1-t)\) and taking
  \(t\uparrow1\),
  \[
    R_\varrho
    =
    \sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}
      \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}
        a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        \mathcal B_{m,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}.
  \]
  The proof is the dominated-convergence argument in
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-fixed-label-determinant} with the summation over
  \(r\) removed; the \(m=1\) term is the reduced boundary finite part and
  all \(m\ge2\) terms are interior determinant values.  Substitution into
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-class-mertens} determines
  \[
    M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)(s_\varrho\gamma+R_\varrho).
  \]
  Finally,
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-frobenius-product} determines \(K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\)
  from \(\delta_C\) and the coordinates \(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout} gives the inverse finite
  character readout from the positive logarithms of the constants and the
  exponent profile back to the \(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\).  Thus identical
  \(\mathfrak D_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}}(A,\tau;G)\) data give identical
  class counts, renormalized densities, renormalized additive constants,
  reduced fixed-label coordinates, and Frobenius-class product constants.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Successive finite recovery of the three families of constants]
  \label{prop:assembled-recovery-dependency-order}
  \label{rem:assembled-recovery-dependency-order}
  Under the hypotheses of \Cref{prop:finite-data-main-chain-closure}, the
  finite-data recovery factors through the following ordered sequence:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:assembled-recovery-dependency-order}
    \mathfrak D_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}}(A,\tau;G)
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal O_I
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal O_{II}
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal O_{III},
  \end{equation}
  where \(\mathcal O_I\) consists of the primitive Peter--Weyl coefficients
  and primitive class counts, \(\mathcal O_{II}\) consists of the
  renormalized densities \(\delta_C\) and class-Mertens constants
  \(M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and \(\mathcal O_{III}\) consists of the reduced
  fixed-label values \(F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}\) and Frobenius-class product
  constants \(K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\).  The first arrow uses only coefficient
  extraction, Adams coefficients, and finite Fourier inversion.  The second
  arrow is the reduced Perron-boundary evaluation supplied by the finite
  peripheral multiplicity lists and branch data.  The third arrow is the
  renormalized
  fixed-label determinant and product readout of
  \Cref{thm:perron-boundary-determinant-product,cor:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.
  Thus \Cref{prop:finite-data-main-chain-closure} gives the finite recovery
  of the primary Perron-boundary package, while the old \(\eta<\lambda\)
  tuple is the strict-gap specialization of
  \Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization}.
  In particular, equality of two branch-normalized finite coordinate packages
  \(\mathfrak D_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}}\) implies equality of all three
  ordered outputs, and the positive product constants together with the
  exponent profile recover the reduced fixed-label vector by
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
  The first arrow is the extraction step in
  \Cref{prop:finite-data-main-chain-closure}.  The second uses the recorded
  \(\Gamma_\pi\), \(m_{\pi,\zeta}\), \(s_\pi\),
  \(\Lambda_\perp\), \(\Lambda_\partial\), and reduced finite-part branches
  to evaluate
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-class-mertens}.  The third is the determinant
  evaluation of \Cref{thm:renormalized-fixed-label-determinant} followed by
  the product formula and inverse readout of
  \Cref{thm:perron-boundary-determinant-product,cor:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.
  Hence identical branch-normalized determinant-coordinate packages, namely
  identical determinant polynomials together with identical peripheral
  multiplicities, branch conventions, and scalar normalization, give identical
  outputs in all three tiers.
\end{proof}

The primary exponent and product constant are the Perron-boundary quantities
\(\delta_C\) and \(K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\) of
\Cref{thm:renormalized-frobenius-product}.  In the strict-gap specialization
of \Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization}, this becomes the classical factor
\(N^{-|C|/|G|}\), and the corresponding Frobenius-class Mertens product
asymptotic is proved in \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens} from the
additive estimate and product-tail reduction; compare the Frobenius-class product
theorem of Mesliza--Noorani \cite{MeslizaNoorani1999FrobeniusMertens},
Sharp's dynamical Mertens theorem \cite{Sharp1991Mertens}, and the Chebotarev
orbit-distribution antecedents
\cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev,NooraniParry1992ChebotarevShifts}.
Nearby finite-data and skew-product works such as
\cite{CrismaleDelVecchioGrisetaRossi2025NoncommutativeSkewProduct,OHare2025FiniteDataRigidity,Epperlein2026FreeInertGSFTs,NordinNooraniMohd2024OrbitGrowthSoficPFT,DeJong2026PeriodicOrbitLengths}
address different rigidity or extension questions.  The finite-state
primitive-label contribution needed here is the following named comparison
theorem: it is the Adams correction to the Frobenius-class Mertens product,
not merely a restatement of the classical density theorem.

\begin{remark}[Adams correction to Frobenius-class Mertens products]
  \label{prop:class-product-comparison}
  Under the hypotheses of the strict-gap specialization
  \Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization,thm:class-euler-product-mertens}, the
  following assertions hold after the classical asymptotic form and the scalar
  base constant have been separated.  For the topologically mixing SFT defined
  by \(A\), with finite group extension determined by \(\tau\), primitive
  closed orbits weighted by \(\lambda^{-|\gamma|}\), and Frobenius class
  restricted to a conjugacy class \(C\subset G\), the product existence and the
  leading exponent \(|C|/|G|\) are exactly the classical Frobenius-class
  product layer proved by Mesliza--Noorani for Mertens products in Frobenius
  classes of closed subshift orbits \cite{MeslizaNoorani1999FrobeniusMertens},
  compared with the Parry--Pollicott finite-covering and Noorani--Parry finite
  homogeneous-extension Chebotarev theorems and with Sharp's closed-orbit
  Mertens normalisation
  \cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev,NooraniParry1992ChebotarevShifts,Sharp1991Mertens}.
  The comparison is made in the primitive closed-orbit convention fixed in
  \Cref{prop:prelim-trace-primitive}: a length-\(n\) primitive orbit contributes
  the factor \((1-\lambda^{-n})\), and the Frobenius class is the conjugacy
  class of the finite return product of the edge cocycle.  Thus the entropy
  weight \(e^{-hn}\) in the flow/SFT literature is the present
  \(\lambda^{-n}\), and the strict-gap density exponent is the specialization
  \(\delta_C=|C|/|G|\) of the renormalized product theorem.  If a cited
  homogeneous-extension convention uses the opposite
  action, the return element is inverted and the comparison uses \(C^{-1}\)
  instead of \(C\); this changes neither \(|C|/|G|\) nor the existence of the
  classical product layer, and our formula fixes the chosen orientation by
  using \(\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)\) throughout.
  Throughout this paper that cited layer is treated as background: in this
  notation it is the strict-gap assertion that
  \(P_C(N)N^{|C|/|G|}\) has a positive finite limit, after the same Perron
  normalisation by \(\lambda\); the Perron-boundary theorem replaces this by
  \(P_C(N)N^{\delta_C}\to K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\).  The sharper finite-dimensional gap hypotheses
  of \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit} give the explicit
  \(1+O(N^{-1})\) remainder used here.  This proposition does not assert the
  Frobenius-class Mertens asymptotic itself as new.  The finite-state assertion of this
  proposition is the remaining fixed-coordinate statement: once that classical
  layer and the scalar base \(\gamma+\log C(A)\) are fixed, the determinant
  constant is exactly controlled by the fixed-label values
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), these values are recovered from finite twisted
  determinants by Adams--M\"obius inversion, and every proposed replacement is
  measured by the zero-mean Peter--Weyl error vector below.  Thus the new
  datum in the specialization is the Adams-corrected fixed-label determinant
  constant and its obstruction to Artin substitution; in the primary theorem
  it is the renormalized vector \(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\) recovered by
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.  The substitution test is
  downstream of the Perron-boundary theorem package.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Alph*)}]
    \item The three descriptions of the logarithmic constant are equivalent:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item the primitive product tail expression
    \[
      -\log K_C=
      \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)
      +\sum_{r\ge2}\frac{\Pi_{\mathbf1_C}(\lambda^{-r})}{r};
    \]
    \item the fixed-label class-character expression
    \[
      -\log K_C=
      \frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \left(
        \gamma+\log C(A)
        +\sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
          \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
      \right);
    \]
    \item the determinant expression obtained from
    \eqref{eq:fixed-label-determinant-perron} after substituting
    \[
      F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
      =
      \sum_{r,m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
        \sum_{\substack{\pi\in\Irr(G)\\(r,m,\pi)\ne(1,1,\mathbf1)}}
          a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
          \Log\det(I-\lambda^{-rm}A_\pi)^{-1}.
      \]
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Define the normalised logarithmic boundary vector
    \[
      \mathfrak K(C)
      :=-
      \frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C-(\gamma+\log C(A)).
    \]
    Then
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison-fixed-label-inverse}
      \mathfrak K(C)=
      \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}),
      \qquad
      F_\pi(\lambda^{-1})=
      \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathfrak K(C)\chi_\pi(C)
    \end{equation}
    for every non-trivial \(\pi\).  Hence, after scalar normalisation, the
    full class-product constant vector is equivalent to the non-trivial
    fixed-label Perron vector.
    \item If the fixed-label values are replaced by the regular periodic
    Artin logarithms \(\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), define
    \[
      K_C^{\mathrm{Art}}
      :=
      \exp\!\left(
        -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
        \left(
          \gamma+\log C(A)
          +\sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
            \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
        \right)
      \right).
    \]
    Then the naive constants satisfy
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison-artin-error}
      \log\frac{K_C}{K_C^{\mathrm{Art}}}
      =
      -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
        \sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
          \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
          \left(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-
          \mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\right),
    \end{equation}
    and all class constants are unchanged by this substitution if and only if
    \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})=\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) for every
    non-trivial \(\varrho\).  Thus the novelty calibrated by this proposition
    is exactly the fixed-label Adams--M\"obius determinant constant and the
    Peter--Weyl obstruction to replacing it by an ordinary Artin logarithm.
    \item The preceding obstruction is quantitatively sharp in the following
    sense.  Let \(B=(B_\varrho)_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\) be any proposed
    non-trivial Peter--Weyl boundary vector and set
    \[
      K_C(B):=
      \exp\!\left(
        -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
        \left(
          \gamma+\log C(A)
          +\sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
            \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)B_\varrho
        \right)
      \right).
    \]
    With the same logarithm branch as above, define the normalised classwise
    error
    \[
      \mathscr E_B(C):=-\frac{|G|}{|C|}
        \log\frac{K_C}{K_C(B)}.
    \]
    Then
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison-parseval-bound}
      \begin{aligned}
      \mathscr E_B(C)
      &=
      \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
        \left(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-B_\varrho\right),\\
      \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\abs{\mathscr E_B(C)}^2
      &=
      \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
        \abs{F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-B_\varrho}^2 .
      \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    Consequently a branch-compatible formula with the classical exponent
    \(|C|/|G|\) and scalar base \(\gamma+\log C(A)\) gives the true constants
    for every conjugacy class if and only if
    \(B_\varrho=F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) for every non-trivial
    \(\varrho\).  No non-zero non-trivial Peter--Weyl error can be absorbed
    into the classical exponent, the existence statement, or the scalar base
    constant.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
  This is the comparison with the existing Frobenius-class Mertens product
  layer for the strict-gap specialization of the renormalized product theorem.
  The ordered assertions below separate the inherited and finite-label parts
  of that specialization.  Mesliza--Noorani's Frobenius-class Mertens product
  theorem gives the classical model for existence of the Frobenius-class
  product constant and the exponent \(|C|/|G|\) in the closed-subshift
  normalisation.  The Chebotarev comparison is with Parry--Pollicott's
  finite-covering theorem for Axiom~A flows and Noorani--Parry's finite
  homogeneous-extension theorem for shifts, and the scalar Mertens scale is the
  one fixed by Sharp.  The stronger explicit
  error term in \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens} is obtained internally
  from \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit} and
  \Cref{lem:class-euler-product-tail-bound}; no step in the proof reclaims
  the classical comparison layer.  What remains to prove is
  that, after the scalar baseline is removed, the branch-compatible class
  constants are equivalent to the non-trivial fixed-label Perron vector, that
  this vector is the Adams--M\"obius determinant vector, and that every
  substitute vector has exactly the Peter--Weyl error stated in
  \textup{(C)}--\textup{(D)}.  Since \textup{(C)} and \textup{(D)} are obtained
  only after subtracting a proposed replacement vector from the fixed-label
  vector, they are downstream tests rather than inputs to the proof of
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.

  The equivalence between \textup{(A)(i)} and \textup{(A)(ii)} follows by
  expanding the fixed-label tail once, so we recall the normalisation.  The
  additive class-count formula gives
  \[
    \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)
    =\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathcal M(A)
      +\frac{|C|}{|G|}
       \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
       \Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}).
  \]
  For the product tail, character orthogonality gives, for each \(r\ge2\),
  \[
    \Pi_{\mathbf1_C}(\lambda^{-r})
    =\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \left(
        \Pi_{\mathbf1}(\lambda^{-r})
        +\sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
          \Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-r})
      \right).
  \]
  The scalar finite-part normalisation identifies the scalar contribution
  \(\mathcal M(A)+\sum_{r\ge2}\Pi_{\mathbf1}(\lambda^{-r})/r\) with
  \(\gamma+\log C(A)\).  Combining the preceding displays therefore turns
  the primitive product tail in \textup{(A)(i)} into
  \[
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}
    \left(
      \gamma+\log C(A)
      +\sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
        \sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-r})}{r}
    \right),
  \]
  which is \textup{(A)(ii)} because the inner sum is
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\).  For \textup{(A)(iii)}, substitute the
  determinant formula of \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron} into
  the non-trivial summands in \textup{(A)(ii)}. The exceptional scalar Perron
  term is absent there because, for \(\varrho\neq\mathbf1\),
  \(a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}=0\) by character orthogonality; all remaining
  logarithms use the regular Abel branch specified in
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}. Hence the new datum is a finite
  twisted-determinant evaluation of fixed-primitive-label Euler transforms.

  This proves \textup{(A)}.  Taking logarithms in
  \eqref{eq:class-euler-product-k-f} gives the first formula in
  \eqref{eq:comparison-fixed-label-inverse}. Multiplying that identity by
  \((|C|/|G|)\chi_\pi(C)\), summing over conjugacy classes, and applying
  character orthogonality gives the inverse formula for every
  \(\pi\ne\mathbf1\).  Hence \textup{(B)} follows.

  Finally \Cref{thm:fixed-label-euler-transform} identifies
  \[
    F_\varrho(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\varrho(z^r)}{r}
  \]
  as the fixed-label Euler transform.  The ordinary Artin logarithm is
  \[
    \mathcal L_\varrho(z)=
    \sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_{\psi^r\varrho}(z^r)}{r}.
  \]
  The distinction comes from
  repetitions of primitive labels, which apply the power map
  \(g\mapsto g^r\) in the periodic logarithm but not in the fixed-label
  primitive Euler product. Subtracting the logarithm of the Artin-replaced
  formula from the corrected logarithmic formula gives
  \eqref{eq:comparison-artin-error}.  If the right-hand side vanishes for
  every conjugacy class, then the non-trivial character expansion has all
  coefficients zero by orthogonality; conversely, if every coefficient
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-
  \mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) is zero, the displayed error vanishes
  classwise.  This proves \textup{(C)}.

  For \textup{(D)}, subtract the branch-compatible logarithm defining
  \(K_C(B)\) from the true logarithm in \eqref{eq:class-euler-product-k-f}.
  After multiplying by \(-|G|/|C|\), the scalar term
  \(\gamma+\log C(A)\) cancels and gives the first identity in
  \eqref{eq:comparison-parseval-bound}.  The irreducible characters form an
  orthonormal basis of class functions with inner product
  \[
    \langle f,h\rangle_G=
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}f(C)\overline{h(C)}.
  \]
  Applying Parseval to the class function \(\mathscr E_B\) gives the second
  identity.  The right-hand side is zero exactly when
  \(B_\varrho=F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) for every non-trivial
  \(\varrho\), and otherwise the normalised error is non-zero for at least
  one conjugacy class.  This proves the uniqueness and non-absorption claims
  in \textup{(D)}.

\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Consequence: classical exponent and Adams-coordinate separation]
  \label{cor:class-product-priority-boundary-unique}
  \label{thm:class-product-novelty-boundary}
  \label{cor:class-product-classical-layer-uniqueness}
  In the strict-gap specialization, the exponent and existence scale are the
  classical Frobenius-product layer:
  \[
    P_C(N)=K_C N^{-|C|/|G|}(1+O(N^{-1})).
  \]
  Hence any second expansion
  \(P_C(N)=\widetilde K_CN^{-\alpha_C}(1+o(1))\) has
  \(\alpha_C=|C|/|G|\) and \(\widetilde K_C=K_C\).  After this scalar
  exponent layer is fixed, the only remaining non-trivial coordinate is the
  branch-compatible fixed-label Peter--Weyl vector.  The exact inverse
  transform and replacement-vector obstruction are
  \eqref{eq:comparison-fixed-label-inverse},
  \eqref{eq:comparison-artin-error}, and
  \eqref{eq:comparison-parseval-bound}.  Thus a branch-compatible vector
  \(B=(B_\varrho)_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\) gives the true constants for every
  conjugacy class if and only if
  \(B_\varrho=F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) for every non-trivial \(\varrho\);
  the periodic Artin replacement is valid precisely when the corresponding
  discrepancies vanish.
\end{remark}

Thus the main result proceeds from finite determinant-to-primitive inversion to
the Perron-boundary Adams--M\"obius renormalized constant package.  The
strict-gap additive and product formulae are computational specializations of
that package.
