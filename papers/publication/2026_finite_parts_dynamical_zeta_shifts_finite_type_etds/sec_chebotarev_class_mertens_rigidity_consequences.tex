
\subsubsection{Local equivalence of product coordinates}
\label{subsubsec:class-product-local-observation}

The renormalized coordinate readout is
\Cref{thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.  The next consequences are its
strict-gap, scalar-normalised specializations: they identify the finite Fourier
coordinates of the Adams-corrected positive class constants when
\Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization} reduces the Perron-boundary package to
the older \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) chart.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-coordinate equivalence after scalar normalisation]
  \label{prop:class-product-fixed-coordinate-equivalence}
  Under the hypotheses and notation of
  \Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization,thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity},
  consider two finite
  determinant-coordinate data sets with the same finite group \(G\), the same
  character table and Adams tensors, the same scalar normalisation \(C(A)\),
  and the same normalized Abel/ordinary branch convention of
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}.  Thus equality of a coordinate
  always means equality of the corresponding branch-compatible logarithmic
  determinant value, not merely equality of an exponentiated determinant.  If
  their non-trivial fixed-label Perron vectors agree,
  \[
    F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})=F'_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
    \qquad(\varrho\ne\mathbf1),
  \]
  then they give the same Frobenius-class product constants \(K_C=K'_C\) for
  every conjugacy class \(C\).  Conversely, if the constants agree for every
  \(C\) and the same scalar normalisation \(C(A)\) is used, then the
  non-trivial fixed-label Perron vectors agree.  Thus the recovered invariant
  in this strict-gap specialization is the fixed branch-normalised
  determinant-coordinate vector modulo this finite Fourier equivalence; the
  corresponding Perron-boundary invariant is the renormalized vector recovered
  in \Cref{thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  With \(C(A)\) fixed, equality of the fixed-label Perron vectors makes the
  right-hand side of \eqref{eq:class-product-fourier-coordinates} identical for
  every class.  Substitution in the exponential formula for \(K_C\) in
  \Cref{thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity} therefore gives
  \(K_C=K'_C\) for all \(C\).  Conversely, equality of the positive constants
  and equality of \(C(A)\) imply equality of the normalised logarithmic class
  functions \(H_K\) defined in \eqref{eq:class-product-log-vector}.  Applying
  the inverse character formula in
  \eqref{eq:class-product-fourier-coordinates} gives
  \[
    F_\pi(\lambda^{-1})
    =\sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}H_K(C)\chi_\pi(C)
    =\sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}H_{K'}(C)\chi_\pi(C)
    =F'_\pi(\lambda^{-1})
  \]
  for every non-trivial \(\pi\).  Every step uses only the listed finite
  character table and the fixed scalar/branch normalisation.  In particular,
  the implication is false as a branch-free statement: changing logarithmic
  lifts can change the Perron vector even when exponentiated determinant
  values are unchanged, as in the standard fixed-matrix ambiguity of choosing logarithmic branches.
  Thus no conclusion
  about alternative presentations, gauges, folds, branches, or extensions is
  introduced.
\end{proof}




\begin{corollary}[Fixed-coordinate support consequence for scalar-normalised products]
  \label{thm:class-product-minimal-obstruction-skeleton}
  Fix a finite group \(G\) and a real scalar normalisation \(\alpha\).  In the
  dynamical application below, \(\alpha\) is the product scalar
  normalisation
  \(\mathcal M(A)+\sum_{r\ge2}\Pi_{\mathbf1}(\lambda^{-r})/r
  =\gamma+\log C(A)\), not the additive scalar
  \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\).  This
  corollary is a finite linear-algebra statement for the strict-gap
  branch-normalised determinant-coordinate chart obtained from
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-frobenius-product,thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout,cor:strict-gap-specialization}
  and used in the Adams-obstruction theorem.  It compares
  already-proved scalar-normalised product constants after the finite
  determinant coordinates and their branches have been fixed; it is not a
  finite-coordinate inversion statement from arbitrary observations or from
  branch-free data.
  Let
  \(q\) be the number of conjugacy classes of \(G\), and let
  \[
    \mathcal H_G:=
    \left\{H:G\to\RR\;\middle|\;
      H\text{ is a class function and }
      \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}H(C)=0
    \right\}.
  \]
  For \(H\in\mathcal H_G\), put
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-coordinate-support-product-map}
    \mathcal K_\alpha(H)_C:=
    \exp\!\left(-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\bigl(\alpha+H(C)\bigr)\right),
    \qquad C\subset G .
  \end{equation}
  Then \(\mathcal K_\alpha\) is an injective parametrisation of the positive
  scalar-normalised product hyperplane
  \[
    \left\{(L_C)_C:\ L_C>0,
      \prod_{C\subset G}L_C=e^{-\alpha}\right\}.
  \]
  Consequently, if \(R:\mathcal H_G\to W\) is a real linear record, formed in
  these fixed determinant coordinates after \(\alpha\) has been fixed, with
  the property that
  \[
    R(H)=R(H')\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \mathcal K_\alpha(H)=\mathcal K_\alpha(H')
    \qquad(H,H'\in\mathcal H_G),
  \]
  then \(R\) is injective and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-coordinate-support-rank-bound}
    \operatorname{rank}R\ge \dim\mathcal H_G=q-1 .
  \end{equation}
  The bound is attained within this fixed coordinate space.  Every
  non-injective such fixed-coordinate record has a concrete kernel ambiguity
  family:
  for every non-zero \(V\in\ker R\), the whole affine line \(H+tV\),
  \(t\in\RR\), has constant record, while the positive product vectors
  \(\mathcal K_\alpha(H+tV)\) are pairwise distinct.  Conversely every
  ambiguity between two points of \(\mathcal H_G\) with the same record is of
  this form, with difference vector in \(\ker R\).  Thus, inside this fixed
  chart, a proper fixed-coordinate record fails because it leaves a non-zero
  zero-mean class-function direction invisible, and that direction exponentiates
  to a genuine one-parameter deformation of the product constants.  The
  non-trivial Peter--Weyl coordinate map
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-coordinate-support-peter-weyl-record}
    H\longmapsto
    \left(
      \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}H(C)\chi_\varrho(C)
    \right)_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
  \end{equation}
  is an isometric complete coordinate record in this chart, after
  imposing the usual contragredient conjugation relations for real class
  functions.  In particular the zero-mean class-function space
  \(\mathcal H_G\), of dimension \(q-1\), is the finite coordinate space used
  by the strict-gap specialization of the renormalized product readout: a
  rank-deficient fixed-coordinate record misses a non-zero vector in
  \(\mathcal H_G\), and the full Peter--Weyl record misses none in this chart.

  Applied with \(\alpha=\gamma+\log C(A)\) and
  \(H(C)=H_K(C)\) from \eqref{eq:class-product-log-vector}, the actual
  Adams-corrected Frobenius-class product constants therefore have the
  following fixed-chart interpretation and no broader one: the non-trivial
  fixed-label determinant boundary coordinates in
  \Cref{thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity} supply a complete
  record of the scalar-normalised product constants in this fixed coordinate
  chart.  In particular, for
  any branch-compatible substitute boundary
  \((B_\varrho)_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\), the normalised classwise error
  \[
    E_B(C):=
    \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
      \bigl(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-B_\varrho\bigr)
  \]
  satisfies the exact Parseval identity
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-coordinate-support-error-bound}
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}|E_B(C)|^2
    =
    \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
      |F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-B_\varrho|^2.
  \end{equation}
  Hence a non-zero non-trivial boundary error cannot be hidden, inside these
  scalar-normalised product constants, by changing notation, by keeping the
  classical exponent \(|C|/|G|\), or by using only scalar fixed-matrix data.  A
  partial fixed-coordinate record of rank less than \(q-1\) necessarily admits
  the affine kernel family above; the full Adams-corrected Peter--Weyl
  determinant boundary is the coordinate record used to close this finite
  linear-algebra failure.  This statement is a structural consequence
  subordinate to the renormalized product/readout theorem package and its
  strict-gap specialization
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-frobenius-product,thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout,cor:strict-gap-specialization,prop:class-product-downstream-coordinate-status}:
  it assumes the scalar-normalised product constants and their Adams-corrected
  Peter--Weyl determinant boundary have already been identified, and it adds no
  new product asymptotic, determinant evaluation, general observation-system
  theory,
  claim, or Perron hypothesis.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  First observe that \(\dim\mathcal H_G=q-1\), because the real vector space
  of class functions has dimension \(q\) and the displayed weighted-mean
  condition is one non-zero real linear equation.  The image of
  \(\mathcal K_\alpha\) lies in the stated product hyperplane: multiplying
  \eqref{eq:fixed-coordinate-support-product-map} over all conjugacy classes gives
  \[
    \prod_C\mathcal K_\alpha(H)_C
    =
    \exp\!\left(-\sum_C\frac{|C|}{|G|}(\alpha+H(C))\right)
    =e^{-\alpha}.
  \]
  Conversely, if \((L_C)_C\) is a positive vector in that hyperplane, then
  \[
    H_L(C):=-\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log L_C-\alpha
  \]
  has weighted mean zero, exactly because \(\prod_C L_C=e^{-\alpha}\), and
  \(\mathcal K_\alpha(H_L)=L\).  Thus \(\mathcal K_\alpha\) parametrises the
  whole hyperplane.  It is injective because equality of the positive
  vectors \(\mathcal K_\alpha(H)=\mathcal K_\alpha(H')\), followed by the
  ordinary real logarithm in each coordinate, gives
  \((|C|/|G|)(H(C)-H'(C))=0\) for every class \(C\), hence \(H=H'\).

  Now let \(R:\mathcal H_G\to W\) have the stated determining property.  If
  \(V\in\ker R\), then \(R(V)=R(0)\), so the determining property gives
  \(\mathcal K_\alpha(V)=\mathcal K_\alpha(0)\).  By injectivity of
  \(\mathcal K_\alpha\), \(V=0\).  Thus \(R\) is injective, and its rank is at
  least \(\dim\mathcal H_G=q-1\), proving
  \eqref{eq:fixed-coordinate-support-rank-bound}.

  Conversely, if a fixed-coordinate linear record is not injective, choose a
  non-zero \(V\in\ker R\).  For every \(H\in\mathcal H_G\) and every
  \(t\in\RR\), linearity gives \(R(H+tV)=R(H)\).  If
  \(s\ne t\), then \(H+sV\ne H+tV\); by the injectivity of
  \(\mathcal K_\alpha\), already proved above,
  \(\mathcal K_\alpha(H+sV)\ne\mathcal K_\alpha(H+tV)\).  Thus every non-zero
  kernel vector produces the advertised one-parameter ambiguity family of
  indistinguishable fixed-coordinate records with distinct positive product
  constants.  Taking, for instance, \(H=0\), \(s=0\), and \(t=1\) gives the
  stated bad pair.  Conversely, if two points \(H,H'\in\mathcal H_G\) have the
  same record, then \(V:=H'-H\) lies in \(\ker R\) and the pair belongs to the
  affine family \(H+tV\).  This proves that, in the fixed linear chart under
  discussion, the displayed kernel lines account for all failures of a linear
  record to distinguish the scalar-normalised product vectors.  It also shows
  that the rank lower bound is precisely the finite-dimensional injectivity
  condition needed in this determinant-coordinate chart.

  The finite character orthogonality relations identify the non-trivial
  irreducible characters with an orthonormal basis of the zero-mean
  class-function space, with the standard conjugation symmetry when the
  original class function is real.  Therefore the Peter--Weyl record
  \eqref{eq:fixed-coordinate-support-peter-weyl-record} is an isometric injection
  from \(\mathcal H_G\) onto a real coordinate space of dimension \(q-1\), so
  it attains the lower bound.

  For the dynamical constants, \Cref{thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity}
  identifies the coordinate tuple of \(H_K\) with
  \((F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}))_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\), and
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron} evaluates those coordinates
  from the finite twisted determinant family.  This gives the claimed
  fixed-coordinate support for the determinant data used in the strict-gap
  specialization of the renormalized product theorem.
  Finally,
  \(E_B\) is the zero-mean class function whose non-trivial Fourier
  coordinates are
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-B_\varrho\).  Applying Parseval in the same
  zero-mean Peter--Weyl space proves
  \eqref{eq:fixed-coordinate-support-error-bound}.  If the right-hand side is
  non-zero, some classwise error is non-zero, so the substitute boundary
  cannot give all scalar-normalised product constants; scalar data live in
  the trivial one-dimensional summand and have already been fixed by
  \(\alpha\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Artin substitution after scalar normalisation]
  \label{cor:class-product-artin-obstruction-recovery}
  \label{cor:class-product-artin-obstruction-readout}
  Assume the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit}.  After the
  product scalar normalisation
  \(\alpha_A=\gamma+\log C(A)
  =\mathcal M(A)+\sum_{r\ge2}\Pi_{\mathbf1}(\lambda^{-r})/r\), any positive class-product vector satisfying
  \eqref{eq:class-product-normalisation-constraint} determines a unique
  zero-mean Peter--Weyl coordinate vector by
  \[
    \widehat H(\pi)
    =
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \left(-\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C-\bigl(\gamma+\log C(A)\bigr)
      \right)\chi_\pi(C),
    \qquad \pi\ne\mathbf1 .
  \]
  For the dynamical vector of \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}, these
  coordinates are exactly
  \(\widehat H(\pi)=F_\pi(\lambda^{-1})\).  The scalar baseline
  \(K_C=\exp(-|C|\alpha_A/|G|)\) for all \(C\) occurs if and only if
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})=0\) for every non-trivial \(\varrho\).  If the
  displayed fixed-label values are replaced by regular periodic Artin
  logarithms \(\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), then the resulting class
  constants agree with the true constants for every conjugacy class if and
  only if
  \[
    F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})=
    \mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
    \qquad(\varrho\ne\mathbf1).
  \]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Define
  \[
    \mathfrak K(C):=
    -\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C-\bigl(\gamma+\log C(A)\bigr).
  \]
  Taking logarithms in \eqref{eq:class-euler-product-k-f} for the dynamical
  product vector gives
  \[
    \mathfrak K(C)=
    \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
      \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}).
  \]
  Multiplying by \(|C|\chi_\pi(C)/|G|\), summing over conjugacy classes,
  and using Hermitian character orthogonality gives the displayed inverse
  formula for the coordinate \(\widehat H(\pi)\).  For an arbitrary positive
  vector satisfying \eqref{eq:class-product-normalisation-constraint}, the
  same orthogonality applies to the zero-mean profile \(\mathfrak K\), so
  the coordinates are unique.  For the actual dynamical vector the preceding
  Fourier identity identifies this coordinate with
  \(F_\pi(\lambda^{-1})\), \(\pi\ne\mathbf1\).

  The scalar-baseline assertion follows from
  \eqref{eq:class-product-parseval}: the displayed norm vanishes if and
  only if all non-trivial fixed-label coordinates vanish, and then
  \eqref{eq:class-product-log-vector} gives
  \(K_C=\exp(-|C|\alpha_A/|G|)\) for every class \(C\).  Finally,
  \Cref{thm:adams-obstruction-class-product} compares the true fixed-label
  constants with the naive constants obtained by substituting
  \(\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\).  Its ratio formula shows that all
  class constants agree if and only if the non-trivial Peter--Weyl expansion
  of the discrepancy is zero.  Since the irreducible characters form an
  orthonormal basis of class functions, this is equivalent to
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})=\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) for every
  non-trivial \(\varrho\).
\end{proof}
\subsubsection{Finite determinant determination of the constants}
\label{subsubsec:finite-determinant-closure-classical-comparison}

We now identify the determinant-to-constant map: the Adams-corrected
fixed-label coordinates are computable from the finite twisted determinant
family with the stated branch convention.

\begin{theorem}[Renormalized finite-data determination of the boundary constants]
  \label{thm:renormalized-finite-data-determination}
  \label{prop:finite-data-main-chain-closure}
  \label{prop:finite-data-main-factorisation-closure}
  Assume the Perron-boundary data and finite semisimplicity certificate of
  \Cref{def:peripheral-peter-weyl-data,lem:finite-cover-peripheral-expansion}.
  Encode the finite group by its multiplication table and let
  \[
    P_\pi(z):=\det(I-zA_\pi).
  \]
  The primary boundary datum is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:renormalized-finite-data-tuple}
    \begin{aligned}
      \mathfrak D_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}}(A,\tau;G):=
      \Bigl(&G,\mathrm{Pow}_m,\{P_\pi(z)\}_{\pi\in\Irr(G)},\lambda,\\
      &\{\Gamma_\pi\}_{\pi\in\Irr(G)},
        \{m_{\pi,\zeta}:\zeta\in\Gamma_\pi\}_{\pi\in\Irr(G)},
        \{s_\pi\}_{\pi\in\Irr(G)},\\
      &\Lambda_\perp,\Lambda_\partial,
        \mathfrak s_\partial,\mathfrak b_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}},
        \mathfrak n_\partial\Bigr).
    \end{aligned}
  \end{equation}
  Here \(\mathrm{Pow}_m\) records the conjugacy-class power maps,
  \(\mathfrak s_\partial\) is the finite semisimplicity certificate,
  \(\mathfrak b_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}}\) records the reduced
  determinant finite-part branches at \(k=1\) and the ordinary interior
  logarithm branches at \(k\ge2\), and \(\mathfrak n_\partial\) records the
  positive scalar/product logarithm normalization.  This datum determines
  all primitive class counts \(p_{n,C}\), the logarithmic densities
  \(\delta_C\), the renormalized class-Mertens constants
  \(M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\), every reduced fixed-label coordinate
  \(F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and every Frobenius-class product constant
  \(K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\).  The strict-gap tuple with \(\eta<\lambda\) is the
  specialization obtained in \Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization}; it is not
  part of the general boundary datum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The finite group component gives the conjugacy classes, the character
  table, the irreducible characters, and the Adams coefficients
  \(a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}\) through the recorded power maps.  The determinant
  polynomials determine all traces by the formal identity
  \[
    \log\det(I-zA_\pi)^{-1}
    =
    \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Tr(A_\pi^n)}{n}z^n .
  \]
  Therefore
  \Cref{cor:primitive-peter-weyl-determinant,cor:primitive-peter-weyl-trace,thm:class-count-trace-recovery}
  recover every primitive Peter--Weyl coefficient and every primitive class
  count \(p_{n,C}\) from the finite determinant data and the finite
  character table.

  The boundary pieces in
  \(\mathfrak D_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}}(A,\tau;G)\) determine the
  non-oscillatory Perron weights
  \[
    \delta_C=
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}\chi_\pi^\ast(C)s_\pi
  \]
  directly.  They also determine the reduced determinant logarithms
  \(\mathcal B_{k,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}\): for \(k=1\) the recorded reduced
  finite-part branch gives \(\mathcal B_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and for
  \(k\ge2\) the value
  \(\Log P_\pi(\lambda^{-k})^{-1}\) is an ordinary interior value on the
  recorded branch.  Hence
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-fixed-label-determinant} determines
  \[
    F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \sum_{r,m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
      \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}
        a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        \mathcal B_{rm,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}
  \]
  for every \(\varrho\).

  It remains only to record the additive finite parts.  The same
  Adams--M\"obius determinant identity with the fixed-label repetition index
  \(r\) omitted gives, after adding \(s_\varrho\log(1-t)\) and taking
  \(t\uparrow1\),
  \[
    R_\varrho
    =
    \sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}
      \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}
        a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        \mathcal B_{m,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}.
  \]
  The proof is the dominated-convergence argument in
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-fixed-label-determinant} with the summation over
  \(r\) removed; the \(m=1\) term is the reduced boundary finite part and
  all \(m\ge2\) terms are interior determinant values.  Substitution into
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-class-mertens} determines
  \[
    M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}
    =
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)(s_\varrho\gamma+R_\varrho).
  \]
  Finally,
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-frobenius-product} determines \(K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\)
  from \(\delta_C\) and the coordinates \(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout} gives the inverse finite
  character readout from the positive logarithms of the constants back to the
  \(F_\varrho^{\mathrm{ren}}\).  Thus identical
  \(\mathfrak D_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}}(A,\tau;G)\) data give identical
  class counts, renormalized densities, renormalized additive constants,
  reduced fixed-label coordinates, and Frobenius-class product constants.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Successive finite recovery of the three families of constants]
  \label{prop:assembled-recovery-dependency-order}
  \label{rem:assembled-recovery-dependency-order}
  Under the hypotheses of \Cref{prop:finite-data-main-chain-closure}, the
  finite-data recovery factors through the following ordered sequence:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:assembled-recovery-dependency-order}
    \mathfrak D_{\partial}^{\mathrm{ren}}(A,\tau;G)
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal O_I
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal O_{II}
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal O_{III},
  \end{equation}
  where \(\mathcal O_I\) consists of the primitive Peter--Weyl coefficients
  and primitive class counts, \(\mathcal O_{II}\) consists of the
  renormalized densities \(\delta_C\) and class-Mertens constants
  \(M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and \(\mathcal O_{III}\) consists of the reduced
  fixed-label values \(F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}\) and Frobenius-class product
  constants \(K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\).  The first arrow uses only coefficient
  extraction, Adams coefficients, and finite Fourier inversion.  The second
  arrow is the reduced Perron-boundary evaluation supplied by the finite
  semisimplicity certificate.  The third arrow is the renormalized
  fixed-label determinant and product readout of
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-fixed-label-determinant,thm:renormalized-frobenius-product,thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.
  Thus \Cref{prop:finite-data-main-chain-closure} gives the finite recovery
  of the primary Perron-boundary package, while the old \(\eta<\lambda\)
  tuple is the strict-gap specialization of
  \Cref{cor:strict-gap-specialization}.
  In particular, equality of two finite determinant data implies equality
  of all three ordered outputs, and the positive product constants recover
  the reduced fixed-label vector by
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The first arrow is the extraction step in
  \Cref{prop:finite-data-main-chain-closure}.  The second uses the recorded
  \(\Gamma_\pi\), \(m_{\pi,\zeta}\), \(s_\pi\),
  \(\Lambda_\perp\), \(\Lambda_\partial\), semisimplicity certificate, and
  reduced finite-part branches to evaluate
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-class-mertens}.  The third is the determinant
  evaluation of \Cref{thm:renormalized-fixed-label-determinant} followed by
  the product formula and inverse readout of
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-frobenius-product,thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.
  Hence identical renormalized finite determinant data, branch conventions,
  and scalar normalization give identical outputs in all three tiers.
\end{proof}

The factor \(N^{-|C|/|G|}\) and the Frobenius-class Mertens product asymptotic
are proved in \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens} from the additive estimate
and product-tail reduction; compare the Frobenius-class product
theorem of Mesliza--Noorani \cite{MeslizaNoorani1999FrobeniusMertens},
Sharp's dynamical Mertens theorem \cite{Sharp1991Mertens}, and the Chebotarev
orbit-distribution antecedents
\cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev,NooraniParry1992ChebotarevShifts}.
Nearby finite-data and skew-product works such as
\cite{CrismaleDelVecchioGrisetaRossi2025NoncommutativeSkewProduct,OHare2025FiniteDataRigidity}
address different rigidity or extension questions.  The finite-state
primitive-label contribution needed here is the following named comparison
theorem: it is the Adams correction to the Frobenius-class Mertens product,
not merely a restatement of the classical density theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Adams correction to Frobenius-class Mertens products]
  \label{prop:class-product-comparison}
  Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}, the
  following assertions hold after the classical asymptotic form and the scalar
  base constant have been separated.  For the topologically mixing SFT defined
  by \(A\), with finite group extension determined by \(\tau\), primitive
  closed orbits weighted by \(\lambda^{-|\gamma|}\), and Frobenius class
  restricted to a conjugacy class \(C\subset G\), the product existence and the
  leading exponent \(|C|/|G|\) are exactly the classical Frobenius-class
  product layer proved by Mesliza--Noorani for Mertens products in Frobenius
  classes of closed subshift orbits \cite{MeslizaNoorani1999FrobeniusMertens},
  compared with the Parry--Pollicott finite-covering and Noorani--Parry finite
  homogeneous-extension Chebotarev theorems and with Sharp's closed-orbit
  Mertens normalisation
  \cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev,NooraniParry1992ChebotarevShifts,Sharp1991Mertens}.
  The comparison is made in the primitive closed-orbit convention fixed in
  \Cref{prop:prelim-trace-primitive}: a length-\(n\) primitive orbit contributes
  the factor \((1-\lambda^{-n})\), and the Frobenius class is the conjugacy
  class of the finite return product of the edge cocycle.  Thus the entropy
  weight \(e^{-hn}\) in the flow/SFT literature is the present
  \(\lambda^{-n}\), and the density exponent is the class density
  \(|C|/|G|\).  If a cited homogeneous-extension convention uses the opposite
  action, the return element is inverted and the comparison uses \(C^{-1}\)
  instead of \(C\); this changes neither \(|C|/|G|\) nor the existence of the
  classical product layer, and our formula fixes the chosen orientation by
  using \(\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)\) throughout.
  Throughout this paper that cited layer is treated as background: in this
  notation it is the assertion that
  \(P_C(N)N^{|C|/|G|}\) has a positive finite limit, after the same Perron
  normalisation by \(\lambda\); the sharper finite-dimensional gap hypotheses
  of \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit} give the explicit
  \(1+O(N^{-1})\) remainder used here.  This proposition does not assert the
  Frobenius-class Mertens asymptotic itself as new.  The finite-state assertion of this
  proposition is the remaining fixed-coordinate statement: once that classical
  layer and the scalar base \(\gamma+\log C(A)\) are fixed, the determinant
  constant is exactly controlled by the fixed-label values
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), these values are recovered from finite twisted
  determinants by Adams--M\"obius inversion, and every proposed replacement is
  measured by the zero-mean Peter--Weyl error vector below.  Thus the new
  datum is the Adams-corrected fixed-label determinant constant and its
  obstruction to Artin substitution, not the Frobenius-class Mertens
  asymptotic itself.  The substitution test is downstream and is not a
  hypothesis for Main Theorems~I--III.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Alph*)}]
    \item The three descriptions of the logarithmic constant are equivalent:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item the primitive product tail expression
    \[
      -\log K_C=
      \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)
      +\sum_{r\ge2}\frac{\Pi_{\mathbf1_C}(\lambda^{-r})}{r};
    \]
    \item the fixed-label class-character expression
    \[
      -\log K_C=
      \frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \left(
        \gamma+\log C(A)
        +\sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
          \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
      \right);
    \]
    \item the determinant expression obtained from
    \eqref{eq:fixed-label-determinant-perron} after substituting
    \[
      F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
      =
      \sum_{r,m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
        \sum_{\substack{\pi\in\Irr(G)\\(r,m,\pi)\ne(1,1,\mathbf1)}}
          a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
          \Log\det(I-\lambda^{-rm}A_\pi)^{-1}.
      \]
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Define the normalised logarithmic boundary vector
    \[
      \mathfrak K(C)
      :=-
      \frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C-(\gamma+\log C(A)).
    \]
    Then
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison-fixed-label-inverse}
      \mathfrak K(C)=
      \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}),
      \qquad
      F_\pi(\lambda^{-1})=
      \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathfrak K(C)\chi_\pi(C)
    \end{equation}
    for every non-trivial \(\pi\).  Hence, after scalar normalisation, the
    full class-product constant vector is equivalent to the non-trivial
    fixed-label Perron vector.
    \item If the fixed-label values are replaced by the regular periodic
    Artin logarithms \(\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), define
    \[
      K_C^{\mathrm{Art}}
      :=
      \exp\!\left(
        -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
        \left(
          \gamma+\log C(A)
          +\sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
            \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
        \right)
      \right).
    \]
    Then the naive constants satisfy
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison-artin-error}
      \log\frac{K_C}{K_C^{\mathrm{Art}}}
      =
      -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
        \sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
          \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
          \left(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-
          \mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\right),
    \end{equation}
    and all class constants are unchanged by this substitution if and only if
    \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})=\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) for every
    non-trivial \(\varrho\).  Thus the novelty calibrated by this proposition
    is exactly the fixed-label Adams--M\"obius determinant constant and the
    Peter--Weyl obstruction to replacing it by an ordinary Artin logarithm.
    \item The preceding obstruction is quantitatively sharp in the following
    sense.  Let \(B=(B_\varrho)_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\) be any proposed
    non-trivial Peter--Weyl boundary vector and set
    \[
      K_C(B):=
      \exp\!\left(
        -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
        \left(
          \gamma+\log C(A)
          +\sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
            \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)B_\varrho
        \right)
      \right).
    \]
    With the same logarithm branch as above, define the normalised classwise
    error
    \[
      \mathscr E_B(C):=-\frac{|G|}{|C|}
        \log\frac{K_C}{K_C(B)}.
    \]
    Then
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison-parseval-bound}
      \begin{aligned}
      \mathscr E_B(C)
      &=
      \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
        \left(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-B_\varrho\right),\\
      \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\abs{\mathscr E_B(C)}^2
      &=
      \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
        \abs{F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-B_\varrho}^2 .
      \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    Consequently a branch-compatible formula with the classical exponent
    \(|C|/|G|\) and scalar base \(\gamma+\log C(A)\) gives the true constants
    for every conjugacy class if and only if
    \(B_\varrho=F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) for every non-trivial
    \(\varrho\).  No non-zero non-trivial Peter--Weyl error can be absorbed
    into the classical exponent, the existence statement, or the scalar base
    constant.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  This is the comparison with the existing Frobenius-class Mertens product
  layer for the strict-gap specialization of the renormalized product theorem.
  The ordered assertions below separate the inherited and finite-label parts
  of that specialization.  Mesliza--Noorani's Frobenius-class Mertens product
  theorem gives the classical model for existence of the Frobenius-class
  product constant and the exponent \(|C|/|G|\) in the closed-subshift
  normalisation.  The Chebotarev comparison is with Parry--Pollicott's
  finite-covering theorem for Axiom~A flows and Noorani--Parry's finite
  homogeneous-extension theorem for shifts, and the scalar Mertens scale is the
  one fixed by Sharp.  The stronger explicit
  error term in \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens} is obtained internally
  from \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit} and
  \Cref{lem:class-euler-product-tail-bound}; no step in the proof reclaims
  the classical comparison layer.  What remains to prove is
  that, after the scalar baseline is removed, the branch-compatible class
  constants are equivalent to the non-trivial fixed-label Perron vector, that
  this vector is the Adams--M\"obius determinant vector, and that every
  substitute vector has exactly the Peter--Weyl error stated in
  \textup{(C)}--\textup{(D)}.  Since \textup{(C)} and \textup{(D)} are obtained
  only after subtracting a proposed replacement vector from the fixed-label
  vector, they are downstream tests rather than inputs to the proof of
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.

  The equivalence between \textup{(A)(i)} and \textup{(A)(ii)} follows by
  expanding the fixed-label tail once, so we recall the normalisation.  The
  additive class-count formula gives
  \[
    \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)
    =\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathcal M(A)
      +\frac{|C|}{|G|}
       \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
       \Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}).
  \]
  For the product tail, character orthogonality gives, for each \(r\ge2\),
  \[
    \Pi_{\mathbf1_C}(\lambda^{-r})
    =\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \left(
        \Pi_{\mathbf1}(\lambda^{-r})
        +\sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
          \Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-r})
      \right).
  \]
  The scalar finite-part normalisation identifies the scalar contribution
  \(\mathcal M(A)+\sum_{r\ge2}\Pi_{\mathbf1}(\lambda^{-r})/r\) with
  \(\gamma+\log C(A)\).  Combining the preceding displays therefore turns
  the primitive product tail in \textup{(A)(i)} into
  \[
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}
    \left(
      \gamma+\log C(A)
      +\sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
        \sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-r})}{r}
    \right),
  \]
  which is \textup{(A)(ii)} because the inner sum is
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\).  For \textup{(A)(iii)}, substitute the
  determinant formula of \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron} into
  the non-trivial summands in \textup{(A)(ii)}. The exceptional scalar Perron
  term is absent there because, for \(\varrho\neq\mathbf1\),
  \(a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}=0\) by character orthogonality; all remaining
  logarithms use the regular Abel branch specified in
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}. Hence the new datum is a finite
  twisted-determinant evaluation of fixed-primitive-label Euler transforms.

  This proves \textup{(A)}.  Taking logarithms in
  \eqref{eq:class-euler-product-k-f} gives the first formula in
  \eqref{eq:comparison-fixed-label-inverse}. Multiplying that identity by
  \((|C|/|G|)\chi_\pi(C)\), summing over conjugacy classes, and applying
  character orthogonality gives the inverse formula for every
  \(\pi\ne\mathbf1\).  Hence \textup{(B)} follows.

  Finally \Cref{thm:fixed-label-euler-transform} identifies
  \[
    F_\varrho(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\varrho(z^r)}{r}
  \]
  as the fixed-label Euler transform.  The ordinary Artin logarithm is
  \[
    \mathcal L_\varrho(z)=
    \sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_{\psi^r\varrho}(z^r)}{r}.
  \]
  The distinction comes from
  repetitions of primitive labels, which apply the power map
  \(g\mapsto g^r\) in the periodic logarithm but not in the fixed-label
  primitive Euler product. Subtracting the logarithm of the Artin-replaced
  formula from the corrected logarithmic formula gives
  \eqref{eq:comparison-artin-error}.  If the right-hand side vanishes for
  every conjugacy class, then the non-trivial character expansion has all
  coefficients zero by orthogonality; conversely, if every coefficient
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-
  \mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) is zero, the displayed error vanishes
  classwise.  This proves \textup{(C)}.

  For \textup{(D)}, subtract the branch-compatible logarithm defining
  \(K_C(B)\) from the true logarithm in \eqref{eq:class-euler-product-k-f}.
  After multiplying by \(-|G|/|C|\), the scalar term
  \(\gamma+\log C(A)\) cancels and gives the first identity in
  \eqref{eq:comparison-parseval-bound}.  The irreducible characters form an
  orthonormal basis of class functions with inner product
  \[
    \langle f,h\rangle_G=
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}f(C)\overline{h(C)}.
  \]
  Applying Parseval to the class function \(\mathscr E_B\) gives the second
  identity.  The right-hand side is zero exactly when
  \(B_\varrho=F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) for every non-trivial
  \(\varrho\), and otherwise the normalised error is non-zero for at least
  one conjugacy class.  This proves the uniqueness and non-absorption claims
  in \textup{(D)}.

\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniqueness of the priority boundary]
  \label{cor:class-product-priority-boundary-unique}
  Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}, every
  statement in this paper that compares the Frobenius-class product constants
  with earlier symbolic-dynamical Mertens product theorems factors through the
  following two data:
  \[
    \left(\frac{|C|}{|G|},\ P_C(N)N^{|C|/|G|}\to K_C\right)
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \bigl(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\bigr)_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}.
  \]
  The first datum is the classical Frobenius-class product layer used only for
  comparison of exponent, positivity, and normalisation.  The second datum is
  the branch-compatible Adams--M\"obius fixed-label determinant coordinate
  obtained by specializing
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-fixed-label-determinant,thm:renormalized-coordinate-readout}.
  Consequently no downstream corollary may change the
  exponent, absorb the scalar normalisation, or replace
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) by a periodic Artin logarithm without producing
  the explicit Peter--Weyl error vector of
  \eqref{eq:comparison-artin-error}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The product asymptotic and positive constants are supplied by
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.  The comparison with the existing
  Frobenius-class Mertens product theorem is isolated in
  \Cref{prop:class-product-comparison}: its opening paragraph identifies the
  exponent and product-existence layer as classical comparison data, while
  item~\textup{(A)} gives the two equivalent formulas for the same constant
  \(K_C\).  Item~\textup{(B)} then identifies the scalar-normalised logarithmic
  class vector with the non-trivial fixed-label Perron vector by finite
  character orthogonality.  Item~\textup{(C)} gives exactly the error incurred
  by substituting the periodic Artin logarithm, and item~\textup{(D)} gives the
  Parseval norm of any replacement vector.  Hence any later comparison can use
  only the separated classical layer and the unique fixed-label vector; any
  attempted replacement is measured by the displayed Peter--Weyl error.  This
  proves the stated factorisation and uniqueness of the priority boundary.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Consequence: classical exponent and Adams-coordinate separation]
  \label{thm:class-product-novelty-boundary}
  Assume the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.  For each
  conjugacy class \(C\subset G\), let \(P_C(N)\) and \(K_C\) be the product and
  positive constant of \eqref{eq:class-euler-product-asymptotic}.  Then the
  classical product scale and the finite determinant coordinate separate as
  follows.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
    \item If, for some real number \(\alpha_C\) and some positive number
    \(\widetilde K_C\), the same product has an asymptotic expansion
    \[
      P_C(N)=\widetilde K_C N^{-\alpha_C}(1+o(1)),
    \]
    then
    \[
      \alpha_C=\frac{|C|}{|G|},
      \qquad
      \widetilde K_C=K_C.
    \]
    Thus any cited Frobenius-class Mertens product theorem can calibrate only
    the already fixed exponent/existence scale; it cannot absorb a change in
    the determinant formula for \(K_C\).
    \item Fix the scalar Perron base \(\gamma+\log C(A)\).  For any
    branch-compatible non-trivial Peter--Weyl vector
    \(B=(B_\varrho)_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\), define
    \[
      K_C(B):=
      \exp\!\left(
        -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
        \left(
          \gamma+\log C(A)+
          \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)B_\varrho
        \right)
      \right).
    \]
    Then the constants \(K_C(B)\) equal the true constants \(K_C\) for every
    conjugacy class if and only if
    \[
      B_\varrho=F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
      \qquad(\varrho\ne\mathbf1).
    \]
    More precisely, with
    \[
      \mathscr E_B(C):=-\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log\frac{K_C}{K_C(B)},
    \]
    one has the exact Parseval identity
    \[
      \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\abs{\mathscr E_B(C)}^2
      =
      \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}
        \abs{F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-B_\varrho}^2 .
    \]
    Hence the Adams--M\"obius fixed-label vector is the unique
    scalar-normalised non-trivial coordinate vector behind the strict-gap
    product constants.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The strict-gap specialization of
  \Cref{thm:renormalized-frobenius-product} gives
  \[
    P_C(N)=K_C N^{-|C|/|G|}(1+O(N^{-1}))
  \]
  with \(K_C>0\).  Dividing this estimate by the putative expansion in
  \textup{(a)} gives
  \[
    \frac{K_C}{\widetilde K_C}
    N^{\alpha_C-|C|/|G|}(1+o(1))=1.
  \]
  Taking logarithms along sufficiently large \(N\), dividing by \(\log N\), and
  letting \(N\to\infty\) gives \(\alpha_C=|C|/|G|\).  Substituting this equality
  back into the displayed ratio gives \(K_C/\widetilde K_C=1\), hence
  \(\widetilde K_C=K_C\).  This proves \textup{(a)} and shows that the exponent
  and existence scale is not a receptacle for determinant-coordinate changes.

  For \textup{(b)}, subtract the branch-compatible logarithm defining
  \(K_C(B)\) from the fixed-label logarithmic formula for \(K_C\) in
  \Cref{prop:class-product-comparison}\textup{(A)(ii)}.  The scalar base
  \(\gamma+\log C(A)\) cancels, and multiplication by \(-|G|/|C|\) gives
  \[
    \mathscr E_B(C)=
    \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
    \bigl(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-B_\varrho\bigr).
  \]
  Character orthogonality on class functions, for the inner product
  \[
    \langle f,h\rangle_G
    =\sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}f(C)\overline{h(C)},
  \]
  gives the stated Parseval identity.
  Its right-hand side vanishes exactly when every non-trivial coordinate
  \(B_\varrho\) equals \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\).  This is equivalent to
  \(\mathscr E_B(C)=0\) for all classes, which is exactly
  \(K_C(B)=K_C\) on the chosen branch.  The uniqueness of the Adams--M\"obius
  coordinate vector follows.
\end{proof}

The preceding corollary is the single novelty boundary used below: the
Frobenius-class product exponent and existence scale are classical comparison
data, while the branch-compatible Adams--M\"obius fixed-label vector is the
finite determinant coordinate identified in this paper.  The remaining
comparison paragraph records where the surrounding literature enters; it is a
calibration of references and conventions, not another priority claim.

\begin{corollary}[Classical-layer uniqueness of the Adams coordinate]
  \label{cor:class-product-classical-layer-uniqueness}
  Assume the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}, and fix the
  classical Frobenius-class product layer for every conjugacy class: the
  primitive-orbit product exists with exponent \(|C|/|G|\), and the scalar
  Perron normalisation is the fixed number \(\gamma+\log C(A)\).  Let
  \(B=(B_\varrho)_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\) be any branch-compatible non-trivial
  Peter--Weyl boundary vector, and let \(K_C(B)\) be the corresponding constants
  from \Cref{prop:class-product-comparison}.  If
  \[
    K_C(B)=K_C
    \qquad\text{for every conjugacy class }C\subset G,
  \]
  while the exponent and scalar normalisation are kept fixed, then
  \[
    B_\varrho=F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
    \qquad(\varrho\ne\mathbf1).
  \]
  Equivalently, once the common exponent and scalar baseline have been fixed, the only branch-compatible determinant coordinate vector that
  produces the true class-product constants is the Adams--M\"obius fixed-label
  vector.  In particular the Artin-periodic replacement gives the same constants
  for all classes if and only if
  \(\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})=F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) for every
  non-trivial irreducible representation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Apply the Parseval identity \eqref{eq:comparison-parseval-bound} in
  \Cref{prop:class-product-comparison}.  The assumed equality
  \(K_C(B)=K_C\), with the same positive logarithm branch and the same scalar
  normalisation, gives \(\mathscr E_B(C)=0\) for every conjugacy class.  Hence
  the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:comparison-parseval-bound} is zero, and so
  every summand
  \(\abs{F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-B_\varrho}^2\) on the right-hand side is zero.
  Therefore \(B_\varrho=F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) for all non-trivial
  \(\varrho\).  Taking
  \(B_\varrho=\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) gives the final assertion,
  which is also the equality criterion in \eqref{eq:comparison-artin-error}.
\end{proof}
