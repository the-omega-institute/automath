Dear Editors,

We submit "Finite radial determination under a Mahler rationality hypothesis
for odd-Adams-invariant abelian two-group extensions of shifts of finite type"
for consideration as a Research Article in Ergodic Theory and Dynamical
Systems.

Assuming the explicitly quoted statement (KN85), the paper proves finite
radial determination for odd-Adams-invariant extensions by arbitrary finite
abelian two-groups over primitive edge shifts,
allowing the two base shifts to be different. The number of radial locations
is independent of the group rank and exponent. The class contains every
elementary two-group extension and standard extensions with genuine holonomy
of order 4, 8, and beyond. The paper also gives an exact boundary collision, a
realizable linear sampling lower bound, and the obstruction to extending the
finite bound to arbitrary finite groups. Its arithmetic engine includes a
strong supporting Mahler theorem: an input-only O(D log D) divisor estimate
for the multiplicative certificate itself, a matching lower-bound family, and
transfer of that sharpness to standard realizable C2-cover zeta ratios.
Explicit height and fixed-base bit-complexity estimates complete the effective
treatment.

The headline finite-sampling theorem, the algebraic-collision upper bound, and
the stronger prime-primary linear theorem all explicitly assume the
algebraic-to-rational implication (KN85). The paper separately proves an
unconditional sharp squarefree certificate bound, a collision--jet
inequality, odd-prime collision constructions, and a realizable linear
sampling lower bound. No unconditional sampling upper bound is claimed.

The priority claim is deliberately narrow. Ostrowski treats the linear
multiplicative equation. Kumiko Nishioka's 1982 special-value statement was
checked directly. The bibliographic record for Keiji Nishioka's 1985 paper is
verified, but its original theorem statement is not: the pages are
subscription-only, and a zbMATH review is only a secondary restatement. The
precise implication needed for algebraic-to-rational descent is therefore
quoted as assumption (KN85) in every result that uses it. Bare existence and
decidability of the rational certificate are prior through published additive
and linear Mahler algorithms,
and the finite Pade step is largely standard rational reconstruction once a
degree bound is known. The claimed Mahler increment is therefore the
input-only multiplicative-certificate bound, its sharpness, and the supporting
integer height and fixed-base bit analysis, not a new general Mahler decision
algorithm. No novelty or priority claim is made from the antecedent search.

ETDS is the coherent home because the submission is led by the dynamical
inverse theorem and its realizable dynamical lower bounds, at an interface
with number theory and combinatorics. The Mahler component improves
completeness and effectivity rather than the journal-tier case. We considered
but declined splitting the paper: a symbolic-computation venue would not fit
the present whole, and the realizable C2-cover family gives the supporting
Mahler theorem its strongest meaning inside the unified dynamics paper.

The submission includes separately compiled Supplementary Material containing
the Frobenius-class product-constant companion results, quotient-cover
refinements, and an exact S3 witness. It also includes a reproducibility
archive with all verification scripts, all unit tests, readable archived
outputs, exact certificates, literature audit, checksums, and reproduction
instructions. We ask that both attachments be made available to the referees.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have
approved the submission. The authors declare no competing interests. No
research datasets were generated or analysed.
