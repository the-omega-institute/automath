\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The problem addressed in this paper is primitive orbit reconstruction
for finite-group extensions of mixing subshifts of finite type. Let
\((X_A^+,\sigma)\) be a mixing subshift with primitive adjacency matrix
\(A\), let \(\lambda=\rho(A)\), and let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a cocycle
into a finite group. For every irreducible representation \(\varrho\)
of \(G\), the twisted transition matrix \(A_\varrho\) records periodic
labels through its traces. These traces are periodic data. The
primitive labels are subtler: when a primitive orbit with label \(g\)
is repeated \(m\) times, its label becomes \(g^m\). Thus primitive
extraction is not ordinary M\"obius inversion in the non-abelian
setting; it must be coupled to the Adams operations
\(\psi^m\phi(g)=\phi(g^m)\) on class functions.

The centre of the paper is the Adams--M\"obius passage from twisted
dynamical determinants to primitive Peter--Weyl channels and then to the
constants in Frobenius-class Euler products.  The classical
Frobenius-class Mertens asymptotic supplies the exponent
\(|C|/|G|\):
\[
  P_C(N)=K_C N^{-|C|/|G|}(1+o(1)).
\]
The finite-state problem addressed here is the constant \(K_C\).  Under
the strict twisted gap it is governed by the fixed-label Euler transform
\[
  F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
  =\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-r})}{r},
\]
not by the periodic Artin logarithm \(\mathcal L_\varrho\).  Replacing
\(F_\varrho\) by \(\mathcal L_\varrho\) is false in the explicit
\(S_3\) certificate proved in \Cref{app:s3-model-computations}.  Thus the new
symbolic-dynamics/dynamical-\(\zeta\) contribution is the
Adams-corrected fixed-label determinant formula for \(K_C\), together
with its finite determinant recovery and obstruction certificate, rather
than a new claim of priority for the classical class-Mertens exponent.

The finite-group spine has three steps: Main Theorem~I linearises
twisted determinants and performs primitive Adams--M\"obius inversion;
Main Theorem~II evaluates the resulting non-trivial primitive series at
\(z=\lambda^{-1}\), after imposing the twisted spectral gap
\(\eta<\lambda\), and obtains conjugacy-class Mertens constants; Main
Theorem~III identifies the Euler-product constants, while the Fourier
readout of the corresponding Perron-point primitive channels is a
structural corollary.  The readout of these constants is the Hermitian
character-orthogonality formula. Its forward class coefficient is
\(\chi_\varrho^\ast(C):=\overline{\chi_\varrho(C)}\); for unitary
characters this is equivalently \(\chi_\varrho(C^{-1})\).

The scalar finite part of \(\zeta_A\) and the cyclic root-of-unity layer
have narrower roles. The finite part supplies only the trivial-channel
normalisation \(\mathcal M(A)\), while root-of-unity sampling belongs
only to the canonical cyclic lift, where every edge is labelled by the
generator of \(\ZZ/q\ZZ\). It is not the same as taking arbitrary cyclic
quotients of the fixed cocycle. The logical direction of the proofs is
\[
  \begin{array}{c}
    \text{twisted determinants}
    \xrightarrow{\text{trace}}
    \text{periodic channels} \\[2pt]
    \xrightarrow{\text{Adams--M\"obius}}
    \text{primitive channels} \\[2pt]
    \xrightarrow[\eta<\lambda]{z=\lambda^{-1}}
    \text{class Mertens constants}
    \xrightarrow{\text{orthogonality}}
    \text{class profile}.
  \end{array}
\]
Here the Adams--M\"obius arrow is the primitive extraction step; the last
arrow is ordinary character orthogonality after the Perron-point values
have been constructed. The formal input--output statements and the scalar
normalisation are collected in the next subsection.

Main Theorem~I below is a front-matter statement of the determinant
recovery package proved in
\Cref{lem:twisted-trace-identity,thm:class-function-linearisation,%
cor:class-function-adams-mobius,cor:primitive-peter-weyl-determinant,%
cor:primitive-peter-weyl-trace,thm:class-count-trace-readout}. Main
Theorem~II below is the Perron-point class-Mertens package proved in
\Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit,prop:class-mertens-deviations,%
thm:class-mertens-fourier}. Main Theorem~III below is the
Adams-corrected Euler-product constant theorem proved in
\Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}. Its determinant evaluation,
readout rigidity, Artin-obstruction test, and finite model are subordinate
consequences rather than additional main theorems.
The eta-gap rigidity package used to test the strict-gap hypothesis is
\Cref{thm:eta-gap-rigidity,cor:eta-gap-boundary-classifier,%
thm:eta-gap-diagnostic-interface}. The quotient and cyclic-detection results
of \Cref{sec:shadows}, including the scalar projection boundary
\Cref{thm:scalar-normalisation-projection-boundary}, are structural
consequences of these two theorem statements.
The remaining fixed-matrix scalar statements are collected separately in
\Cref{sec:finite-part,app:cyclic-lift-package}. The scalar dynamical
\(\zeta\)-function
\[
  \zeta_A(z)
  =\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{\Tr(A^n)}{n}z^n\right)
  =\det(I-zA)^{-1}
\]
enters only through the trivial representation channel. Its rationality
and orbit-counting role are classical, going back to
Bowen--Lanford \cite{BowenLanford1970Zeta} and Manning
\cite{Manning1971Axiom}, with further development through Ruelle's
thermodynamic formalism
\cite{Ruelle1976ZetaExpanding,Ruelle1978Thermodynamic} and the
Parry--Pollicott transfer-operator programme
\cite{ParryPollicott1990Zeta}. The finite-part proposition below is a
classical Euler-product/M\"obius computation at this channel, while the
one-layer DFT corollary is polynomial coefficient recovery from enough
root-of-unity samples. Neither statement generates non-abelian primitive
information.

\subsection{Finite-group main theorem spine}

Let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a cocycle into a finite group, and let
\(A_\varrho\) be the twisted transition matrices. For a class function
\(\phi:G\to\CC\) write
\[
  T_{\phi,n}:=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n)}
    \phi(\tau_n(x)),
  \qquad
  \mathcal L_\phi(z):=\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{T_{\phi,n}}{n}z^n,
\]
and
\[
  \Pi_\phi(z):=\sum_{\gamma\in\cP}\phi(\tau(\gamma))z^{|\gamma|}.
\]
For a conjugacy class \(C\subset G\), let \(p_{n,C}\) denote the number
of primitive orbits of length \(n\) whose cocycle label lies in \(C\).
The scalar orbit-Mertens constant \(\mathcal M(A)\) is the
trivial-channel constant defined in \Cref{def:abel-finite-part}; the
  finite-matrix formula behind this normalisation is the calculation in
  \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form}.

\subsection{Scope}\label{sec:intro-scope-of-results}

\paragraph{Roadmap of results.}
The argument has three finite-group steps, followed by an auxiliary scalar
layer.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Main Theorem~I recovers the primitive Peter--Weyl series from the
  finite group character table together with the power maps
  \(C\mapsto C^m\) on conjugacy classes, equivalently the Adams
  operations on \(R(G)\), and the twisted determinant logarithms on the
  open Perron disc \(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\).
\item Main Theorem~II begins only after imposing \(\lambda>1\) and the
  twisted spectral gap
  \[
    \eta:=\max_{\varrho\neq\mathbf 1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda;
  \]
  if \(G\) is trivial, the maximum is empty and by convention
  \(\eta=0\).
  This hypothesis is used to evaluate the non-trivial primitive
  Peter--Weyl channels at \(z=\lambda^{-1}\) and hence define the class
  Mertens constants. By
  \Cref{lem:twisted-gap-skew-product-peripheral}, it is already a
  finite-state condition on the skew-product adjacency matrix
  \(\widetilde A\): it says that the peripheral spectral multiset of
  \(\widetilde A\) is exactly the single simple eigenvalue \(\lambda\),
  or equivalently that \(\widetilde A\) has a simple strictly dominant
  Perron eigenvalue \(\lambda\); if \(\widetilde A\) is irreducible,
  this is equivalent to primitivity of \(\widetilde A\).
\item Main Theorem~III is the retargeted symbolic-dynamics product step.
  It starts from the class constants of Main Theorem~II, forms the
  Frobenius-class Euler products, and proves that their constants are
  controlled by the fixed-label transform
  \(F_\phi(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\Pi_\phi(z^r)/r\), not the periodic Artin
  logarithm
  \(\mathcal L_\phi(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\Pi_{\psi^r\phi}(z^r)/r\). Thus the
  classical part is the exponent and existence of a Frobenius-class product
  constant, while the new finite-state statement is the Adams-corrected
  determinant recovery and the exact Artin-substitution obstruction.
\item The scalar results of \Cref{sec:finite-part} have a narrower role.
  The scalar finite-part statement below gives \(\mathcal M(A)\), while
  the appendix statements
  \Cref{thm:finite-part-cyclic-lift-spectrum-identifiability,%
  thm:finite-part-cyclic-lift-single-q-torsion-reconstruction}
  record the independent fixed-matrix cyclic-lift and one-layer Fourier
  reconstructions proved in \Cref{app:cyclic-lift-package}.
  They are not used in Main Theorem~I,
  Main Theorem~II, or the quotient/shadow consequence. The formal
  no-descent boundary in
  \Cref{thm:finite-part-scalar-interface-no-descent,cor:fixed-matrix-record-no-descent}
  records the precise obstruction: fixed-matrix cyclic-lift records
  determine a family of primitive class profiles only when the
  non-trivial Perron-point channels are already constant on that family.
\end{enumerate}
All quantities \(T_{\phi,n}\), \(\mathcal L_\phi\), and \(\Pi_\phi\)
are deterministic observables of the fixed cocycle. Any identification
of these fixed-matrix records with an external detector or shell
observable would require additional hypotheses.

For orientation, all results below are finite-state statements. The
primitive-channel identities, class Mertens constants, Adams-corrected
Euler-product constants, quotient and shadow identities, scalar
finite-part statements, cyclic-lift reconstructions, and the conditional
secondary spectral-edge refinement use only the base matrix \(A\), the
finite group data consisting of the character table and power maps
\(g\mapsto g^m\), and the determinant family
\(\{\det(I-zA_\varrho):\varrho\in\Irr(G)\}\), together with the spectral
hypotheses stated at the point where they are used. Main Theorem~I works
on the open Perron disc \(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\). Main Theorem~II adds the
finite-dimensional gap
\(\max_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda\) in order to take
Perron-point values. The scalar and cyclic-lift statements use the same
fixed matrix \(A\) with their explicitly stated finite-matrix
constructions. Thus the introductory statements below are restatements
or summaries of finite-matrix theorems proved later; no Banach-space
transfer-operator theorem is used in the proof of either main theorem.

The theorem chain in this paper is finite-state.  For each
\(\varrho\in\Irr(G)\), \Cref{lem:twisted-trace-identity} identifies
\(\Tr(A_\varrho^n)\) with a finite character sum over \(n\)-periodic
points, and \Cref{prop:twisted-spectral-radius-bound} gives
\(\rho(A_\varrho)\le \lambda\).  Hence the logarithms used on
\(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\) are ordinary finite-matrix determinant logarithms.
The passage to primitive channels is the finite Peter--Weyl expansion and
Adams--M\"obius inversion of
\Cref{thm:class-function-linearisation,cor:class-function-adams-mobius,%
cor:primitive-peter-weyl-determinant,cor:primitive-peter-weyl-trace,%
thm:class-count-trace-readout}.  The Perron-point results add only the
finite-dimensional gap
\(\eta=\max_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda\), equivalently
the corresponding peripheral-spectrum separation for \(\widetilde A\) in
\Cref{lem:twisted-gap-skew-product-peripheral}.  Thus every dependency in
the article's theorem spine is a finite-dimensional matrix identity, a
finite-group character identity, Adams--M\"obius inversion over the
integers, or Perron--Frobenius theory for finite non-negative matrices;
no H\"older Banach-space transfer theorem or Ruelle resonance theorem is
a hidden premise of the main results.

\begin{remark}[Finite-state split from H\"older/Ruelle transfer
  theory]
  \label{prop:intro-finite-state-transfer-split}
  For the finite-group extension of the primitive edge shift fixed in this
  paper, all analytic inputs used in Main Theorems~I--III factor through
  the finite matrices
  \[
    A_\varrho=\bigl(\sum_{e:i\to j}\varrho(\tau(e))\bigr)_{i,j\in V},
    \qquad \varrho\in\Irr(G),
  \]
  and the Adams operations \(\psi^m\phi(g)=\phi(g^m)\) on class functions.
  More precisely:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\rm(\alph*)]
  \item On the open Perron disc \(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\), every determinant
    logarithm appearing in Main Theorem~I is the ordinary finite-matrix
    logarithm \(\log\det(I-zA_\varrho)^{-1}\), and its periodic trace
    coefficients are the fixed-point sums of
    \Cref{lem:twisted-trace-identity}.
  \item The passage from periodic determinant data to primitive
    fixed-label series is exactly the finite Peter--Weyl expansion followed
    by Adams--M\"obius inversion of
    \Cref{thm:class-function-linearisation,cor:class-function-adams-mobius,%
    cor:primitive-peter-weyl-determinant,cor:primitive-peter-weyl-trace,%
    thm:class-count-trace-readout}; it does not use a H\"older
    Banach-space transfer theorem.
  \item The Perron-point evaluations in Main Theorems~II--III use only the
    finite-dimensional gap
    \(\eta=\max_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda\). By
    \Cref{lem:twisted-gap-skew-product-peripheral}, this is equivalent to
    the statement that the skew-product adjacency matrix \(\widetilde A\)
    has the single simple peripheral eigenvalue \(\lambda\), or,
    equivalently, a simple strictly dominant Perron eigenvalue
    \(\lambda\); if \(\widetilde A\) is irreducible, it is equivalent to
    primitivity of \(\widetilde A\).
  \end{enumerate}
  Consequently the H\"older/Ruelle transfer-operator chain and the
  finite-state chain are split in the proof architecture of this paper:
  the former supplies historical context and comparison language, whereas
  the latter supplies all labelled proof interfaces used by the main
  theorem spine.
\end{remark}

Indeed, for \(\varrho\in\Irr(G)\),
\Cref{prop:twisted-spectral-radius-bound} gives
\(\rho(A_\varrho)\le\lambda\). Hence \(I-zA_\varrho\) is invertible for
\(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\), and the power-series identity for a finite matrix
gives
\[
  \log\det(I-zA_\varrho)^{-1}
  =\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Tr(A_\varrho^n)}{n}z^n
\]
there.  The equality between these traces and the periodic fixed-point
character sums is precisely \Cref{lem:twisted-trace-identity}; summing
against the finite character-table coefficients gives
\Cref{thm:class-function-linearisation}. This verifies item (a).

The primitive-orbit step is algebraic. Repetitions of a primitive orbit
replace a label \(g\) by \(g^m\), so the operation on class functions is
the finite Adams operation \(\psi^m\). Applying ordinary M\"obius inversion
to this relation gives the primitive Peter--Weyl determinant and trace
formulas cited in (b).

Finally, the Perron-point assertions evaluate these finite series at
\(z=\lambda^{-1}\) after separating the trivial channel. The only
non-trivial analytic condition is the strict spectral inequality
\(\eta<\lambda\), equivalently the finite skew-product peripheral statement
from \Cref{lem:twisted-gap-skew-product-peripheral}. This verifies item (c)
and the stated split.

The paper's main contribution is recorded in the following three numbered
headline theorems.  They are concise front-matter versions of the detailed
body theorem packages cited in the proof guide above, but they have different
roles in the theorem spine.  Main Theorem~I is the open-disc determinant
inversion theorem: it converts periodic twisted determinants into primitive
Peter--Weyl channels before any Perron-boundary evaluation.  Main
Theorem~II is the Perron-point class-constant theorem: after the strict
twisted gap, it evaluates those primitive channels at
\(z=\lambda^{-1}\) and gives the additive Frobenius-class Mertens constants.
Main Theorem~III is the retargeted symbolic-dynamics novelty of the paper:
it identifies the Adams-corrected fixed-label Euler-product constants and
proves that the ordinary periodic Artin logarithm is not the right primitive
class-product substitute.  The literature calibration is therefore:
Parry--Pollicott, Noorani--Parry, Sharp, and Mesliza--Noorani supply the
classical prime-orbit, Chebotarev, and Frobenius-class Mertens background;
the published ETDS articles of Coles, Humbert, O'Hare, and
Crismale--Del Vecchio--Griseta--Rossi are comparison points for modern
zeta, resonance, and skew-product questions, but are not inputs to the
proofs below;
the new material here is the fixed-label Adams--M\"obius Euler transform,
its finite determinant recovery, the Hermitian class readout, and the
\(S_3\) obstruction showing that the periodic Artin logarithm gives the
wrong product constant.

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem I: finite determinant recovery of primitive
  channels]
  \phantomsection\label{stmt:main-theorem-i-adams-mobius-linearisation}
  \label{thm:intro-adams-mobius-linearisation}
  Let \(A\) be primitive and let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be a finite-group edge
  cocycle.  For every class function \(\phi:G\to\CC\), the primitive
  label series \(\Pi_\phi(z)\) on \(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\) is determined by
  the finite family of twisted determinant polynomials
  \[
    \{\det(I-zA_\varrho):\varrho\in\Irr(G)\}
  \]
  together with the Adams operations \(\psi^m\phi(g)=\phi(g^m)\).  More
  precisely,
  \[
    \Pi_\phi(z)=
    \sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\,
      \mathcal L_{\psi^m\phi}(z^m),
    \qquad
    \mathcal L_\phi(z)=
    \sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}\widehat\phi(\varrho)
      \log\det(I-zA_\varrho)^{-1}.
  \]
  Consequently the primitive Frobenius-class counts \(p_{n,C}\) are
  obtained by finite character-table operations and coefficient
  extraction from the same determinant family.
\end{theorem}

Proof is by the body package
\Cref{lem:twisted-trace-identity,thm:class-function-linearisation,%
cor:class-function-adams-mobius,cor:primitive-peter-weyl-determinant,%
thm:primitive-peter-weyl-trace,thm:class-count-trace-readout}.

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem II: Perron-point class Mertens constants]
  \phantomsection\label{stmt:main-theorem-ii-class-mertens-constants}
  \label{thm:intro-class-mertens-constants}
  Assume \(\lambda>1\) and the twisted spectral gap
  \(\eta=\max_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda\).  Then for
  every conjugacy class \(C\subset G\),
  \[
    \sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
    =
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}\log N+\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)+O(N^{-1}),
  \]
  where
  \[
    \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)=
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}\biggl(\mathcal M(A)+
      \sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
        \Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\biggr).
  \]
  The non-trivial Perron-point values
  \(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) are the values supplied by Main
  Theorem~I after analytic continuation through the strict finite-matrix
  gap.  Their Hermitian class Fourier transform recovers the full
  normalised deviation profile and gives the Parseval identity proved in
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-fourier}.
\end{theorem}

Proof is by the body package
\Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit,prop:class-mertens-deviations,%
thm:class-mertens-fourier}.

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem III: Adams-corrected Frobenius-class
  Euler-product constants]
  \phantomsection\label{stmt:main-theorem-iii-adams-frobenius-products}
  \label{thm:intro-adams-frobenius-products}
  Under the hypotheses of Main Theorem~II, define the fixed-label Euler
  transform
  \[
    F_\varrho(z):=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Pi_\varrho(z^r)}{r}.
  \]
  This is distinct from the Artin periodic logarithm
  \(\mathcal L_\varrho(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\Pi_{\psi^r\varrho}(z^r)/r\) in
  general.  For every conjugacy class \(C\),
  \[
    P_C(N):=
    \prod_{\substack{\gamma\ \mathrm{primitive}\; |\gamma|\le N\\
      \tau(\gamma)\in C}}
      (1-\lambda^{-|\gamma|})
    =K_C\,N^{-|C|/|G|}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr),
  \]
  with
  \[
    K_C=\exp\biggl(-\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \biggl(\gamma+\log C(A)+
        \sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
          F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\biggr)\biggr).
  \]
  Each \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) is computable from the same finite
  twisted determinant data as Main Theorem~I by substituting the
  Adams--M\"obius determinant formula into the displayed series.  Thus
  the leading exponent and the existence of such Frobenius-class products
  are the classical part of the statement; the new assertion is the
  primitive-label fixed-Euler determinant recovery of \(K_C\) and the
  resulting obstruction to replacing \(F_\varrho\) by the periodic Artin
  logarithm.
\end{theorem}

Proof is by the body theorem \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}; the
determinant and readout consequences are recorded afterwards.

\paragraph{Proof guide.}
  The trace identity underlying Main Theorem~I is
  \Cref{lem:twisted-trace-identity}; the determinant linearisation and
  Adams--M\"obius primitive inversion are
  \Cref{thm:class-function-linearisation,cor:class-function-adams-mobius}.
  The determinant-germ formula and coefficient forms are
  \Cref{cor:primitive-peter-weyl-determinant,thm:primitive-peter-weyl-trace,%
  thm:class-count-trace-readout}.  Main Theorem~II is proved by
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit,prop:class-mertens-deviations,%
  thm:class-mertens-fourier}.  Main Theorem~III is
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}. The determinant evaluation in
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron} and the readout corollaries
  following it are consequences used to expose the constant, not separate
  entries in the main theorem hierarchy.
  The eta-gap classifier \Cref{thm:eta-gap-rigidity} explains when the
  strict-gap hypothesis fails; it is a diagnostic boundary theorem, not a
  separate source of product constants.

As a structural consequence of Main Theorem~II, quotient maps
\(q:G\twoheadrightarrow Q\) intertwine both the periodic logarithms and
primitive series,
\[
  \mathcal L_{q^\ast f}^{G}(z)=\mathcal L_f^{Q}(z),
  \qquad
  \Pi_{q^\ast f}^{G}(z)=\Pi_f^{Q}(z)
  \qquad(|z|<\lambda^{-1}).
\]
For
\[
  \mathcal B_{A,\tau;G}(C):=\frac{|G|}{|C|}\Delta_C(A,\tau;G),
\]
the Peter--Weyl decomposition splits the Perron-point class profile into
its one-dimensional sector and its higher-dimensional sector. The span of
all cyclic quotient observables is exactly the one-dimensional sector;
therefore its projection is the abelian shadow, orthogonal to the genuine
non-abelian defect. Under \(\eta<\lambda\), the full primitive class
profile is generated by cyclic quotient shadows exactly when every
higher-dimensional primitive channel
\(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) vanishes. These assertions are proved in
\Cref{thm:quotient-functoriality,cor:abelian-cyclic-shadow,%
thm:abelian-shadow-defect,thm:cyclic-detection-boundary}.

Similarly, a scalar record formed only from the fixed matrix \(A\), such
as \(\FP(A)\), \(\mathcal M(A)\), the reduced polynomial \(F_A\), the
branch-lifted cyclic-lift record \(\{\Psi_q(A)\}_{q\ge1}\), or a torsion
layer \(\{F_A(\omega_q^j)\}_{0\le j<q}\), cannot by itself recover the
non-trivial finite-group profile for a family of cocycles over the same
base matrix. The precise projection boundary is
\Cref{thm:scalar-normalisation-projection-boundary}: scalar data determine
a single normalised class profile across such a family exactly when all
non-trivial Perron-point channels
\(\Pi_\varrho^\tau(\lambda^{-1})\), \(\varrho\neq\mathbf1\), are already
independent of \(\tau\). Thus the scalar finite-part and cyclic-lift
statements below are fixed-matrix inputs and do not enlarge the
finite-group theorem spine; the two-cocycle no-descent case is
\Cref{cor:fixed-matrix-record-no-descent}.

An explicit \(S_3\)-extension separates the two obstruction types:
the sign-character trace vanishes but its primitive value is non-zero by
the quadratic Adams mechanism, while the standard two-dimensional
channel gives the certified higher-dimensional defect. This is a
finite-state obstruction model, not a new periodic-orbit distribution
theorem.

The quotient/shadow consequence is proved in
\Cref{thm:quotient-functoriality,%
thm:scalar-normalisation-projection-boundary,%
cor:fixed-matrix-record-no-descent,%
cor:abelian-cyclic-shadow,%
prop:canonical-cyclic-lift-root-layer,%
thm:abelian-shadow-defect,%
thm:cyclic-detection-boundary,%
thm:s3-certified-nonabelian-defect}.

\subsection{Scalar finite-matrix inputs}

We record only the scalar inputs needed for notation and comparison.
They are proved later in \Cref{sec:finite-part}. After removing the
Perron factor one obtains the reduced determinant
\[
  C(A):=\det\nolimits'(I-A/\lambda)^{-1}
\]
and the reduced spectral polynomial
\[
  F_A(t):=\frac{\det(I-tA/\lambda)}{1-t}
  =\prod_{j=2}^d\left(1-\frac{\mu_j}{\lambda}t\right),
\]
where \(\mu_2,\dots,\mu_d\) are the non-Perron eigenvalues. The
Abel-regularised finite-part constant
\[
  \FP(A):=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\bigl(P_A(r)+\log(1-r)\bigr),
  \qquad
  P_A(r):=\sum_{n\ge 1}p_n(A)\lambda^{-n}r^n,
\]
measures the deviation of the primitive orbit sum from the harmonic
series.

\begin{mainproposition}[Scalar finite-part computation at the
  Perron pole]
  Let \(A\) be a primitive non-negative real matrix with Perron root
  \(\lambda=\rho(A)>1\), reduced determinant \(C(A)\), and M\"obius
  coefficients \(p_n(A)\) defined by \eqref{eq:primitive-mobius}. Then
  \[
    \FP(A)
    =\log C(A)
      +\sum_{k\ge 2}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}
        \log\det(I-\lambda^{-k}A)^{-1}.
  \]
  Equivalently,
  \[
    \FP(A)
    =\log C(A)
      +\sum_{n\ge 1}p_n(A)
        \bigl(\lambda^{-n}+\log(1-\lambda^{-n})\bigr).
  \]
  In particular, the orbit-Mertens constant
  \(\mathcal M(A)=\gamma+\FP(A)\) satisfies
  \[
    \sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_n(A)}{\lambda^n}
    =\log N+\mathcal M(A)+o(1).
  \]
\end{mainproposition}

\paragraph{Proof location.}
  This is the introductory restatement of the scalar finite-part
  computation proved in \Cref{sec:finite-part}.
  The closed formula for the Abel finite part is
  \Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form}. The identity with the
  reduced determinant follows there from the determinant--M\"obius
  expansion at the Perron pole, and the orbit-Mertens asymptotic is
  \Cref{cor:finite-part-mertens-asymptotic}. These two proved results
  give exactly the displayed formulas.


This is the trivial-channel scalar input singled out in
\Cref{sec:intro-scope-of-results};
  the scalar statement above is proved in
\Cref{lem:finite-part-primitive-closed-form,cor:finite-part-mertens-asymptotic}.

\subsection{Relation to existing work}

The natural comparison class is the symbolic-dynamics and dynamical
\(\zeta\)-function literature on prime cycles, finite extensions, and
twisted determinant factorizations. In that setting the classical input
is the existence of Chebotarev and Frobenius-class product asymptotics;
the issue addressed here is which finite determinant data recover the
constant once repetitions of primitive labels have been separated by
Adams operations.

The table isolates the precise increment. Main Theorem~I is a
determinant-level primitive inversion theorem for one fixed
finite-group SFT cocycle; Main Theorem~II turns the same finite-state
data into the classwise harmonic constants \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\)
under the finite-matrix condition \(\eta<\lambda\); Main Theorem~III
then gives the Adams-corrected Frobenius-class Euler-product constant;
and the Fourier readout isolates the character-orthogonality readout.
  The scalar cyclic-lift section remains a finite-dimensional
comparison. The secondary spectral-edge material is kept in the appendix as a
conditional finite-matrix tail refinement above the square-root barrier; it is
not a third main theorem and it does not alter the Adams-corrected product
constants.

The closest older literature is adjacent theorem-by-theorem but does
not contain this combination of operations. Fried's twisted-coefficient
and closed-orbit identities provide determinant and trace antecedents,
but they do not apply the finite-group Adams operation
\(\psi^m\phi(g)=\phi(g^m)\) inside a full class-function ring to invert
repetitions of primitive labels. Parry--Pollicott and Noorani--Parry
prove leading Chebotarev-type orbit distributions for finite extensions,
and Mesliza--Noorani prove a Frobenius-class Mertens product theorem for
closed orbits in Frobenius classes. Here the leading density and the
existence of the product asymptotic are treated as classical; the new
output is the finite-state determinant formula for the corrected
constant
\[
  \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)
  =
  \frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathcal M(A)
  +\frac{|C|}{|G|}
    \sum_{\varrho\ne\mathbf 1}
      \overline{\chi_\varrho(C)}\,\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}),
\]
where the non-trivial harmonic values are obtained from
\[
  \Pi_\phi(z)=
  \sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\mathcal L_{\psi^m\phi}(z^m)
\]
and then evaluated at \(z=\lambda^{-1}\) under \(\eta<\lambda\). For the
Euler product itself the constant uses instead the fixed-label transform
\(F_\varrho(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\Pi_\varrho(z^r)/r\), whose determinant
formula is supplied by Main Theorem~III, while the Fourier readout
records the inverse character readout.
Hashimoto and Stark--Terras develop graph-Artin determinant
factorisations and prime-cycle density results for finite graph
coverings; the present theorem instead fixes an SFT cocycle and
recovers primitive Peter--Weyl channels from the whole twisted
determinant family. Boyle--Schmieding analyse finite group extensions
through classification and obstruction theory; the theorem below is an
inverse determinant calculation for a fixed extension, not an existence
or flow-equivalence classification theorem.

The dynamical \(\zeta\)-function for SFTs goes back to
Artin--Mazur \cite{ArtinMazur1965PeriodicPoints} and Bowen--Lanford
\cite{BowenLanford1970Zeta,Manning1971Axiom}. The prime-orbit
asymptotic for shifts of finite type is classical: Parry
\cite{Parry1983PrimeOrbit} proved the analogue of the prime number
theorem for closed orbits of SFTs and their suspensions. Lalley
\cite{Lalley1987DistributionPeriodicOrbits} developed the broader
periodic-orbit distribution theory for symbolic and Axiom~A systems,
and the existence of Mertens-type asymptotics for Axiom~A flows was
established by Sharp \cite{Sharp1991Mertens}. For zeta functions
attached to broader group-action orbit data, Lind's \(\ZZ^d\)-action
zeta function is the standard reference \cite{Lind1996ZetaZdActions}.
Against this background, the present paper contributes a
determinant-level primitive reconstruction theorem for one fixed
finite-group cocycle. By Adams--M\"obius inversion it recovers the
primitive Peter--Weyl channels from twisted determinant data, and under
\(\eta<\lambda\) the same framework extracts the class-indexed
constants \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\). The scalar finite-part identity at
the Perron pole supplies the trivial-channel normalisation and a direct
comparison with the classical Mertens constant.

The transfer-operator approach is developed systematically in
\cite{Ruelle1976ZetaExpanding,Ruelle1978Thermodynamic,%
ParryPollicott1990Zeta,Baladi2000PositiveTransfer}. Those references
provide background only: H\"older or Banach-space extensions are not treated here; the proofs below use only finite matrices attached to one SFT cocycle.
Twisted determinant and \(L\)-function constructions for dynamical
systems and finite-group extensions also appear in the twisted
Perron--Frobenius framework of Adachi--Sunada
\cite{AdachiSunada1987TwistedPF}. These references place the paper on
the determinant and twisted-zeta side of the subject rather than in the
broader transfer-operator literature.

Twisted periodic-point formulas and closed-orbit identities also appear
in Fried's work on periodic points with twisted coefficients
\cite{Fried1983PeriodicPointsTwistedCoefficients} and homological
identities for closed orbits
\cite{Fried1983HomologicalIdentitiesClosedOrbits}. These references are
closer to the present determinant-level use of twisted coefficients than
the broader transfer-operator literature and provide useful antecedents
for the twisted determinant formalism used here. Their focus, however,
is not the finite-state primitive extraction carried out below: the
present paper fixes one finite-group cocycle, tracks the repetition
effect \(g\mapsto g^m\) through Adams operations, and then applies
M\"obius cancellation at the class-function level to recover primitive
Peter--Weyl channels from twisted determinant data. For
skew-product extensions and compact-group cocycles one should also
compare Parry's compact Lie group skew-product paper
\cite{Parry1997SkewProductsCompactLie} and the
  compact-group Liv\v{s}ic theorem of Parry--Pollicott
  \cite{ParryPollicott1997LivsicCompactLie}. The leading Chebotarev-type
  asymptotics for finite extensions are classical: Parry--Pollicott treat
  Axiom~A Galois coverings \cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev}, and
  Noorani--Parry prove the
  corresponding theorem for finite homogeneous extensions of shifts
  \cite{NooraniParry1992ChebotarevShifts}. Mesliza--Noorani then give
  the older Frobenius-class product comparator, a Mertens theorem for
  closed orbits in Frobenius classes
  \cite{MeslizaNoorani1999FrobeniusMertens}. Assuming the twisted gap
  \(\eta<\lambda\), the present paper identifies, for each conjugacy
  class \(C\subset G\), the secondary harmonic constant term
  \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) and the Euler-product constant \(K_C\) for
  one fixed cocycle in terms of twisted determinants. This is a
  finite-state determinant recovery of the Adams-corrected constant after
  the leading Frobenius-class product asymptotic has been separated, not
  a new leading-density theorem or a new Tauberian Mertens theorem of the
  kind proved by Sharp \cite{Sharp1991Mertens}. For
  homogeneous-extension
  background beyond the leading asymptotic,
  including ergodicity and weak-mixing criteria with applications to
  Markov shifts, compare Noorani
  \cite{Noorani1997HomogeneousExtensionsMarkov}. For
  dynamical analogues of Bauer's theorem and Artin reciprocity for closed
  orbits of skew products compare
  \cite{ParryPollicott2008BauerSkewProducts,%
  PollicottSharp2008ArtinReciprocitySkewProducts}. For orbit counting in
homology classes and related constrained equidistribution problems
compare
\cite{KatsudaSunada1988HomologyClosedGeodesics,%
KatsudaSunada1990ClosedOrbitsHomology,%
Sharp1993ClosedOrbitsHomologyAnosov,%
PollicottSharp2007Chebotarev}. Recent work of Coles
\cite{Coles2025VassilievWritheAxiomA} studies asymptotic averages of
knot-theoretic observables over periodic orbits of Axiom~A flows; that
is a geometric orbit-distribution problem rather than a finite-group
primitive inversion problem. For the non-abelian Liv\v{s}ic context
compare \cite{Parry1999LivsicNonAbelian,%
ParryPollicott1997LivsicCompactLie,%
BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions}. The determinant-level novelty
here is the explicit Adams--M\"obius extraction and readout of the
constant term from twisted finite-state data.

Finite group actions on SFTs provide the closest symbolic-dynamics
context. Adler--Kitchens--Marcus
\cite{AdlerKitchensMarcus1985FiniteGroupActions}, Fiebig
\cite{Fiebig1993PeriodicFiniteGroupActions}, and Silver--Williams
\cite{SilverWilliams2005InvariantFiniteGroupActions} study finite
actions, periodic data, quotient systems, and reduced-shift invariants.
Boyle--Schmieding
\cite{BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions} and
Boyle--Carlsen--Eilers \cite{BoyleCarlsenEilers2020FlowEquivalenceGSFT}
analyse finite group extensions through obstruction theory,
\(K\)-theory, and equivariant flow equivalence. Matsumoto's
finite-group extension theory for subshifts via skew products of
\(\lambda\)-graph systems gives another symbolic-dynamics comparison
point \cite{Matsumoto2016ExtensionsSubshiftsFiniteGroups}. Those works address
existence, classification, and periodic-data invariants. Here the cocycle
\(\tau:E_A\to G\) and its twisted matrices are fixed, and the question is
inverse-determinantal: which primitive Peter--Weyl channels and which
class-indexed constants \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) are forced by the
twisted determinants? The new
finite-group step is to undo repetitions \(g\mapsto g^m\) by
Adams--M\"obius inversion. The \(S_3\)-example later in the paper makes
this increment concrete by separating a one-dimensional quadratic Adams
effect from an independent higher-dimensional non-abelian defect inside
one explicit finite-state model.

Tauberian refinements and closed-orbit asymptotics are treated in
\cite{Pollicott1992AgmonTauberian} and the monograph
\cite{ParryPollicott1990Zeta}. For the functorial orbit-counting
perspective underlying Section~\ref{sec:shadows} one should also compare
\cite{PakapongpunWard2009FunctorialOrbit}.
For graph coverings and graph \(L\)-functions, the closest determinant
comparison is the Ihara--Bass/Hashimoto framework for finite graphs and
regular coverings. Hashimoto's determinant formulas and
graph Artin \(L\)-functions
\cite{Hashimoto1989ZetaFiniteGraphs,%
Hashimoto1990ZetaLFunctionsFiniteGraphs,%
Hashimoto1992ArtinDensityPrimeCycles}, Sunada's \(L\)-function
viewpoint and finite-graph determinant treatment
\cite{Sunada1986LFunctionsGeometry,KotaniSunada2000ZetaFiniteGraphs},
the Stark--Terras covering theory of graph Artin \(L\)-functions
\cite{StarkTerras1996FiniteGraphsCoverings,%
StarkTerras2000FiniteGraphsCoveringsII,%
StarkTerras2007FiniteGraphsCoveringsIII}, and the covering
factorisations of Mizuno--Sato
\cite{MizunoSato2000ZetaGraphCoverings} all encode prime-cycle data of
finite graph coverings by determinant or \(L\)-function formalisms
attached to representation data. Directed or weighted variants continue
this line
\cite{Bass1992Ihara,%
MizunoSato2001ZetaDigraphs,%
Horton2007IharaDigraphs,%
ChoeKwakParkSato2007Bartholdi,%
Sato2008WeightedBartholdiCoverings}. These works are directly adjacent
to the present paper at the determinant level, but their main outputs
are factorisation, covering, and prime-cycle density statements for
graph zeta or graph \(L\)-functions. The present paper instead fixes one
finite-group cocycle on an SFT and uses Adams operations together with
M\"obius inversion to recover primitive Peter--Weyl channels and the
class-indexed constants \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) from the twisted
determinant family itself.
For recent perspectives on dynamical zeta functions and dynamical
determinants beyond the classical symbolic-dynamics sources, compare
Baladi's modern monograph \cite{Baladi2018ZetaDeterminants}, the
parameter-regularity results of J\'ez\'equel
\cite{Jezequel2019ParameterRegularity}, and Shen's work on twisted
Ruelle zeta functions on locally symmetric spaces
\cite{Shen2023ComplexValuedAnalyticTorsion}. Our results stay in the
finite-state symbolic setting but fit naturally into this determinant
programme.

Group-extension spectral-radius criteria form a related but different
line of work. Stadlbauer's Kesten-type criterion for topological Markov
chains \cite{Stadlbauer2013KestenTopologicalMarkov}, Jaerisch's
Perron--Frobenius spectral-radius characterisation for group-extended
Markov systems \cite{Jaerisch2015GroupExtendedMarkov}, and the
Dougall--Sharp work on growth rates for covers and group extensions of
subshifts \cite{DougallSharp2021AnosovCoversGroupExtensions} concern
amenability, pressure, or orbit-growth rates for typically countable
group extensions. The finite-group gap condition used here is instead a
finite-dimensional Peter--Weyl spectral hypothesis for one fixed
cocycle.

The present finite-state calculation should also be distinguished from
the orbit-counting and reciprocity literature for skew products and
homology classes. Lalley's distribution results for symbolic and
Axiom~A flows \cite{Lalley1987DistributionPeriodicOrbits},
Katsuda--Sunada's homology-class orbit counting
\cite{KatsudaSunada1988HomologyClosedGeodesics,%
KatsudaSunada1990ClosedOrbitsHomology}, Parry--Pollicott's
Bauer-type theorem for skew products
\cite{ParryPollicott2008BauerSkewProducts}, and Pollicott--Sharp's
Artin reciprocity addendum
\cite{PollicottSharp2008ArtinReciprocitySkewProducts} address density,
equidistribution, or reciprocity questions for global orbit classes.
Here the cocycle is fixed and finite-state, and the target is not a new
density theorem but determinant-level primitive recovery: extracting the
primitive Peter--Weyl channels and the class-indexed constants
\(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) from its twisted determinant data.

\subsection{Plan of the paper}

\Cref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation for primitive orbit counts,
reduced determinants, cyclic lifts, and finite-group extensions.
\Cref{sec:chebotarev} develops the Adams--M\"obius mechanism for
finite-group extensions, derives the primitive Peter--Weyl determinant
series, and establishes the class Mertens theorem under a twisted
spectral gap. The appendix \Cref{sec:secondary-term} records the
finite-tail refinement available at an isolated secondary spectral edge
and verifies that this refinement leaves the Perron-point constants fixed.
\Cref{sec:eta-gap} explains the rigidity of
the boundary case \(\eta=\lambda\). \Cref{sec:shadows} proves quotient
functoriality, identifies the scalar projection boundary, the abelian
shadow, and the exact boundary of cyclic detection inside the
finite-group Peter--Weyl decomposition. A minimal \(C_3\)
conjugation-convention test (\Cref{prop:c3-class-indicator-check})
precedes the \(S_3\) model separating the quadratic sign-channel Adams
effect from the standard-channel non-abelian defect.
\Cref{sec:finite-part} then records the scalar normalisation and the
fixed-matrix lift comparison already delimited in
\Cref{sec:intro-scope-of-results}. The appendices contain the cyclic-lift proofs, the scalar
boundary remarks for omitted scalar directions, and the compact exact
certification behind the \(S_3\) Perron-point windows.
