\section{Discussion and open problems}\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Summary of the main contribution}

For a finite-group extension of a mixing subshift of finite type, the
twisted determinant family records periodic labels. The main point of
this paper is that the corresponding primitive labels are recovered by a
finite Adams--M\"obius calculation in Peter--Weyl coordinates. This is
the finite-state correction forced by the repetition rule
\(g\mapsto g^m\) for primitive orbits.  The boundary theorem keeps every
peripheral twisted eigenvalue \(\lambda\zeta\), records
\(\Gamma_\pi\), \(m_{\pi,\zeta}\), \(s_\pi=m_{\pi,1}\),
\(\Lambda_\perp\), and \(\Lambda_\partial\), and obtains the peripheral
primitive-channel asymptotic from these algebraic multiplicities.  A finite
semisimplicity certificate can be recorded separately when one wants to rule
out higher peripheral Jordan blocks.  The non-oscillatory weights determine
\[
  \delta_C=
  \frac{|C|}{|G|}
    \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}\chi_\pi^\ast(C)s_\pi,
\]
and the reduced primitive finite parts \(R_\pi\) determine the renormalized
class-Mertens constants \(M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\).

The Euler-product constants use the fixed-label Euler transform, not the
periodic Artin logarithm.  The reduced determinant finite parts
\(\mathcal B_{k,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}\) give
\(F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and the product theorem proves
\[
  P_C(N)=K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}N^{-\delta_C}(1+O(N^{-1})).
\]
The coordinate readout
\[
  -\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}
  =
  \sum_\pi\chi_\pi^\ast(C)(s_\pi\gamma+F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}})
\]
recovers the shifted fixed-label vector from the positive product constants,
and the exponent inversion
\[
  s_\pi=\sum_C\delta_C\chi_\pi(C)
\]
then recovers the reduced fixed-label vector from
\((K_C^{\mathrm{ren}})_C\) together with \((\delta_C)_C\).  The strict gap
\(\eta<\lambda\) is the specialization in
which the only Perron collision is the scalar trivial channel, so
\(\delta_C=|C|/|G|\) and the older \(F_\pi(\lambda^{-1})\) constants reappear.
The abelian-shadow and cyclic-quotient material is supporting orthogonal
projection: it identifies the one-dimensional part of the primitive profile
and records that cyclic quotient pullbacks span precisely that sector. The
\(S_3\) example shows the boundary concretely: the sign line detects an Adams
correction invisible to the Artin logarithm, while the standard
two-dimensional representation gives a genuine non-abelian component.

The scalar finite-part and cyclic-lift calculations supply the
trivial-channel normalisation and fixed-matrix comparison model described in
\Cref{subsec:scope-normalizations-support}.  The projection statement marks
the corresponding scalar boundary:
after normalising the class constants, scalar data give the constant
class-function component \(\mathcal M(A)\mathbf 1_G\), whereas the
non-trivial primitive profile is the Peter--Weyl vector
\(\mathcal B_{A,\tau;G}\).
