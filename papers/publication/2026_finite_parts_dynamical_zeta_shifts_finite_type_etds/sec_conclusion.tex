\section{Discussion and open problems}\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Summary of the main contribution}

For a finite-group extension of a mixing subshift of finite type, the
twisted determinant family records periodic labels. The main point of
this paper is that the corresponding primitive labels are recovered by a
finite Adams--M\"obius calculation in Peter--Weyl coordinates. This is
the finite-state correction forced by the repetition rule
\(g\mapsto g^m\) for primitive orbits. Under the twisted gap
\(\eta<\lambda\), the recovered primitive channels have Perron-point
values and hence determine the class-indexed constants
\(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) in the Frobenius-class Mertens sums.

The Euler-product constants require the same distinction. The underlying
Frobenius-class product existence and leading exponent \(|C|/|G|\) are the
classical layer recalled in the comparison theorem. Once that layer has been
separated, the non-trivial coordinates of the constant are the fixed-label Euler transform values
\(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), not the periodic Artin logarithms
\(\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\). The determinant formula therefore
gives
\[
  K_C=
  \exp\!\left(
    -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
    \left(
      \gamma+\log C(A)
      +\sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
    \right)
  \right),
\]
with the positive real branch
(\Cref{cor:class-function-adams-mobius,thm:class-mertens-explicit,thm:class-euler-product-mertens,prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,cor:class-product-constant-rigidity,thm:class-mertens-fourier}). Thus the paper refines the determinant readout of the already normalised class-product constant, rather than reproving or claiming novelty for the Frobenius-class Mertens asymptotic: by
\Cref{cor:class-product-classical-layer-uniqueness}, once that classical
existence/exponent layer and the scalar normalisation are held fixed, the
Adams--M\"obius fixed-label vector is the unique branch-compatible
non-trivial coordinate vector producing the true constants. Quotient
functoriality identifies the
abelianised primitive profile, and cyclic quotient observables span
precisely the one-dimensional Peter--Weyl shadow. The \(S_3\) example
shows both phenomena concretely: the sign line detects an Adams
correction invisible to the Artin logarithm, while the standard
two-dimensional representation gives a genuine non-abelian component.

The scalar finite-part and cyclic-lift calculations supply only the
trivial-channel normalisation and a fixed-matrix comparison model. The
projection theorem
\Cref{thm:scalar-normalisation-projection-boundary} marks the boundary:
after normalising the class constants, scalar data give the constant
class-function component \(\mathcal M(A)\mathbf 1_G\), whereas the
non-trivial primitive profile is the Peter--Weyl vector
\(\mathcal B_{A,\tau;G}\).

\subsection{Open directions}

The results proved here are finite-state statements for a fixed mixing SFT and
a finite-group cocycle.  Several adjacent mathematical extensions are
deliberately left outside the theorem chain.  One direction is analytic: to
build H\"older/Ruelle transfer-operator analogues of the Adams--M\"obius
primitive inversion with their own determinant conventions.  A second direction
is asymptotic: to refine the class-product formula by secondary spectral edges
after the leading Perron constant has been removed.  A third direction is
structural: to make the eta-boundary rigidity mechanism itself the central
object, rather than using the finite eta-gap verification only as a checkable
hypothesis for the fixed input.

There are also inverse questions not pursued here.  The cyclic and scalar
finite-part results in this paper are boundary statements: inherited cyclic
quotient data sees only the one-dimensional Peter--Weyl sector, and the
canonical cyclic-lift root layer is a fixed-matrix comparison device.  A
separate project could ask whether fixed-matrix scalar records support
cyclic-lift reconstruction or one-layer DFT recovery, while a structural shadow
project could develop quotient functoriality and cyclic detection beyond the
consequence-level statements retained here.  The exact rational certificates in
the appendices also suggest a reusable verifier substrate, but in this article
they remain local support for the finite \(S_3\) witness and fixed-label product
constant recovery.  Finally, completed zeta or \(\xi\)-type objects would require
new analytic structure and are not part of the present finite determinant
submission.  Thus the split directions adjacent to this article are precisely
H\"older/Ruelle transfer determinants, secondary spectral-edge constants,
fixed-matrix cyclic-lift reconstruction, zeta completions, finite rational
certificate normal forms, eta-boundary rigidity, and quotient/cyclic detection
boundaries.  None of these directions is used as a premise for Main
Theorems~I--III.
