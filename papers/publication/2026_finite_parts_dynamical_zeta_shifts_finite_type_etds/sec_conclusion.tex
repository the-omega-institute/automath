\section{Discussion and open problems}\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Summary of the main contribution}

For a finite-group extension of a mixing subshift of finite type, the
twisted determinant family records periodic labels. The main point of
this paper is that the corresponding primitive labels are recovered by a
finite Adams--M\"obius calculation in Peter--Weyl coordinates. This is
the finite-state correction forced by the repetition rule
\(g\mapsto g^m\) for primitive orbits. Under the twisted gap
\(\eta<\lambda\), the recovered primitive channels have Perron-point
values and hence determine the class-indexed constants
\(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) in the Frobenius-class Mertens sums.

The Euler-product constants require the same distinction. Once the
classical leading exponent \(|C|/|G|\) has been separated, the constant is
governed by the fixed-label Euler transform
\(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), not by the periodic Artin logarithm
\(\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\). The determinant formula therefore
gives
\[
  K_C=
  \exp\!\left(
    -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
    \left(
      \gamma+\log C(A)
      +\sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
    \right)
  \right),
\]
with the positive real branch
(\Cref{cor:class-function-adams-mobius,%
thm:class-mertens-explicit,%
thm:class-euler-product-mertens,%
prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,%
cor:class-product-constant-rigidity,%
thm:class-mertens-fourier}). This is a determinant-level refinement of
the classical Chebotarev/Mertens orbit-counting picture, rather than a
new leading-density theorem. Quotient functoriality identifies the
abelianised primitive profile, and cyclic quotient observables span
precisely the one-dimensional Peter--Weyl shadow. The \(S_3\) example
shows both phenomena concretely: the sign line detects an Adams
correction invisible to the Artin logarithm, while the standard
two-dimensional representation gives a genuine non-abelian component.

The scalar finite-part and cyclic-lift calculations supply the
trivial-channel normalisation and a fixed-matrix comparison model. The
projection theorem
\Cref{thm:scalar-normalisation-projection-boundary} marks the boundary:
after normalising the class constants, scalar data give the constant
class-function component \(\mathcal M(A)\mathbf 1_G\), whereas the
non-trivial primitive profile is the Peter--Weyl vector
\(\mathcal B_{A,\tau;G}\).

\subsection{Open problems}

We close with three directions that are deliberately not part of the
theorem chain proved here.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Structural criteria beyond direct skew-product spectral
  testing.} The
  condition
  \(\eta=\max_{\varrho\neq\mathbf 1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda\) is
  assumed in \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit}. By
  \Cref{lem:twisted-gap-skew-product-peripheral}, this is already a
  finite-dimensional condition in the present setting: it is equivalent
  to saying that the skew-product adjacency \(\widetilde A\) has no
  non-trivial peripheral spectrum, equivalently that the peripheral
  spectral multiset of \(\widetilde A\) is the single simple eigenvalue
  \(\lambda\), or equivalently that \(\widetilde A\) has a simple
  strictly dominant Perron eigenvalue \(\lambda\); if \(\widetilde A\)
  is irreducible, it is equivalent to primitivity of \(\widetilde A\).
  Thus one direct verification route is simply to build the
  skew-product matrix (or the twisted blocks \(A_\varrho\)) and test
  strict Perron dominance. The canonical cyclic lift of
  \Cref{rem:abelian-toy-model-cyclic,prop:canonical-cyclic-lift-root-layer}
  already gives such a failure:
  \(A_{\chi_j}^{\tau_q}=\omega_q^jA\) and
  \(\rho(A_{\chi_j}^{\tau_q})=\lambda\) for every \(j\neq0\), so for
  \(q>1\) this comparison model lies outside the twisted-gap regime.
  For non-abelian extensions the analogous condition was also assumed
  without derivation in \cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev}. Thus the
  open problem is not needed for the present theorems: it is the separate
  search for structural or easily checkable hypotheses on the cocycle
  \(\tau:E_A\to G\) that guarantee the strict gap without first carrying
  out the full finite-matrix test. Noorani's analysis of homogeneous
  extensions and Markov-shift applications
  \cite{Noorani1997HomogeneousExtensionsMarkov}, the analysis of Parry and
  Liv\v{s}ic obstructions in
  \cite{BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions}, the finite-action
  periodic-data viewpoint of \cite{Fiebig1993PeriodicFiniteGroupActions},
  the \(G\)-SFT flow-equivalence framework of
  \cite{BoyleCarlsenEilers2020FlowEquivalenceGSFT}, and Parry's
  non-abelian periodic-point theorem \cite{Parry1999LivsicNonAbelian}
  give context for such a future project. The \(S_3\)-model of
  \Cref{prop:s3-example-channels}, where \(\rho(A_\rho)=1<2=\lambda\), is
  only a local verification example.

\item \emph{Non-abelian analogues of cyclic arithmetic.} The scalar
  root-of-unity comparison in \Cref{sec:finite-part} is abelian in
  nature and belongs to the canonical cyclic-lift model, not to cyclic
  quotients of a fixed non-abelian cocycle. The current paper proves only
  this boundary. A systematic study of cyclic sampling functors or
  residue-class analogues in higher-dimensional twisted channels would be
  a separate project.

\item \emph{Operator-theoretic finite parts.} Analogues beyond
  finite-state matrices, for example in H\"older/Ruelle transfer-operator
  settings, are excluded from the present article. Any such extension
  would need its own Banach space, determinant convention, pressure
  normalization, and Perron-point hypotheses before an Adams--M\"obius
  finite-part formula could be compared with the finite-state formula
  proved here.
\end{enumerate}
