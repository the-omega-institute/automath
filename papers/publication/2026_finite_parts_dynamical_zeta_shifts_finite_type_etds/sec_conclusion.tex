\section{Discussion and open problems}\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Summary of the main contribution}

For a finite-group extension of a mixing subshift of finite type, the
twisted determinant family records periodic labels. The main point of
this paper is that the corresponding primitive labels are recovered by a
finite Adams--M\"obius calculation in Peter--Weyl coordinates. This is
the finite-state correction forced by the repetition rule
\(g\mapsto g^m\) for primitive orbits.  The boundary theorem keeps every
peripheral twisted eigenvalue \(\lambda\zeta\), records
\(\Gamma_\pi\), \(m_{\pi,\zeta}\), \(s_\pi=m_{\pi,1}\),
\(\Lambda_\perp\), and \(\Lambda_\partial\), and uses a finite
semisimplicity certificate to obtain the peripheral primitive-channel
asymptotic.  The non-oscillatory weights determine
\[
  \delta_C=
  \frac{|C|}{|G|}
    \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}\chi_\pi^\ast(C)s_\pi,
\]
and the reduced primitive finite parts \(R_\pi\) determine the renormalized
class-Mertens constants \(M_C^{\mathrm{ren}}\).

The Euler-product constants use the fixed-label Euler transform, not the
periodic Artin logarithm.  The reduced determinant finite parts
\(\mathcal B_{k,\pi}^{\mathrm{ren}}\) give
\(F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}}\), and the product theorem proves
\[
  P_C(N)=K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}N^{-\delta_C}(1+O(N^{-1})).
\]
The coordinate readout
\[
  -\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C^{\mathrm{ren}}
  =
  \sum_\pi\chi_\pi^\ast(C)(s_\pi\gamma+F_\pi^{\mathrm{ren}})
\]
and its inverse recover the reduced fixed-label vector from the positive
product constants.  The strict gap \(\eta<\lambda\) is the specialization in
which the only Perron collision is the scalar trivial channel, so
\(\delta_C=|C|/|G|\) and the older \(F_\pi(\lambda^{-1})\) constants reappear.
Quotient functoriality identifies the abelianised primitive profile, and
cyclic quotient observables span precisely the one-dimensional Peter--Weyl
shadow. The \(S_3\) example shows both phenomena concretely: the sign line
detects an Adams correction invisible to the Artin logarithm, while the
standard two-dimensional representation gives a genuine non-abelian component.

The scalar finite-part and cyclic-lift calculations supply the
trivial-channel normalisation and a fixed-matrix comparison model. The
projection theorem marks the corresponding scalar boundary:
after normalising the class constants, scalar data give the constant
class-function component \(\mathcal M(A)\mathbf 1_G\), whereas the
non-trivial primitive profile is the Peter--Weyl vector
\(\mathcal B_{A,\tau;G}\).

\subsection{Further directions}

Several natural extensions remain beyond the finite-state determinant setting
treated here, but none is used in the theorem chain above.  One direction is a
secondary spectral-edge theory for Frobenius-class Mertens formulae, preserving
the Adams--M\"obius separation between periodic and primitive labels while
adding edge-isolation hypotheses.  Another is to give structural criteria for
the strict twisted gap \(\eta<\lambda\), for instance in terms of mixing
properties of the skew product or representation-theoretic expansion of the
cocycle.  A third direction is to extend the determinant formula to
H\"older/Ruelle transfer operators, where determinant conventions, branch
choices, continuation, and Perron residues would have to be controlled
analytically rather than by finite matrices.

There are also inverse problems suggested by the quotient calculations.  Cyclic
quotient observations see only the one-dimensional Peter--Weyl sector, while the
canonical cyclic-lift root layer gives a fixed-matrix comparison model rather
than an invariant of the original finite-group cocycle.  Spectrum
reconstruction or one-layer DFT recovery from lift data is therefore a separate
inverse problem: it would require an observability theory with explicit descent
hypotheses before it could be compared with cocycle class profiles.  The
rational \(S_3\) enclosures in the appendices exhibit the Adams correction and
the failure of the naive Artin substitution in a concrete finite model.
