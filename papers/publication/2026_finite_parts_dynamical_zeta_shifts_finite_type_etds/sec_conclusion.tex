\section{Discussion and open problems}\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Summary of the main contribution}

The paper has two main finite-group results, with a product-constant
refinement. First, twisted determinant data in the Peter--Weyl channels
are converted to primitive orbit data by Adams--M\"obius inversion.
Second, under the gap hypothesis this primitive determinant calculus
yields the class-indexed harmonic constants
\(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) at the Perron point.  The same Adams-corrected
calculus also identifies the Frobenius-class Euler-product constants
\(K_C\): after the classical leading exponent \(|C|/|G|\) is separated,
the determinant contribution is not the ordinary Artin logarithm but the
fixed-label Euler transform \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), with its
Perron-boundary value recovered from the finite twisted determinant
family.  Equivalently,
\[
  K_C=
  \exp\!\left(
    -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
    \left(
      \gamma+\log C(A)
      +\sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
    \right)
  \right),
\]
with the positive real branch
(\Cref{cor:class-function-adams-mobius,%
thm:class-mertens-explicit,%
thm:class-euler-product-mertens,%
prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,%
thm:class-product-constant-rigidity,%
thm:class-mertens-fourier}). Quotient functoriality then identifies the
abelianised primitive profile, and the linear span of cyclic quotient
observables is the abelian shadow of the full primitive class profile at
the Perron point, under \(\eta<\lambda\).
The scalar material has the role fixed in
\Cref{sec:intro-scope-of-results}: it supplies the trivial-channel
normalisation \(\mathcal M(A)\) and a separate fixed-matrix comparison
model. The projection theorem
\Cref{thm:scalar-normalisation-projection-boundary} makes this boundary
literal: after normalising the class constants, the scalar input is the
constant class-function component \(\mathcal M(A)\mathbf 1_G\), and the
non-trivial primitive profile is exactly the Peter--Weyl vector
\(\mathcal B_{A,\tau;G}\).

\subsection{Open problems}

We close with three problems suggested by this framework.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Structural criteria beyond direct skew-product spectral
  testing.} The
  condition
  \(\eta=\max_{\varrho\neq\mathbf 1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda\) is
  assumed in \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit}. By
  \Cref{lem:twisted-gap-skew-product-peripheral}, this is already a
  finite-dimensional condition in the present setting: it is equivalent
  to saying that the skew-product adjacency \(\widetilde A\) has no
  non-trivial peripheral spectrum, equivalently that the peripheral
  spectral multiset of \(\widetilde A\) is the single simple eigenvalue
  \(\lambda\), or equivalently that \(\widetilde A\) has a simple
  strictly dominant Perron eigenvalue \(\lambda\); if \(\widetilde A\)
  is irreducible, it is equivalent to primitivity of \(\widetilde A\).
  Thus one direct verification route is simply to build the
  skew-product matrix (or the twisted blocks \(A_\varrho\)) and test
  strict Perron dominance. The canonical cyclic lift of
  \Cref{rem:abelian-toy-model-cyclic,prop:canonical-cyclic-lift-root-layer}
  already gives such a failure:
  \(A_{\chi_j}^{\tau_q}=\omega_q^jA\) and
  \(\rho(A_{\chi_j}^{\tau_q})=\lambda\) for every \(j\neq0\), so for
  \(q>1\) this comparison model lies outside the twisted-gap regime.
  For non-abelian extensions the analogous condition was also assumed
  without derivation in \cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev}. The open
  problem is therefore not computability of the condition itself but
  the search for structural or easily checkable hypotheses on the
  cocycle \(\tau:E_A\to G\) that guarantee it without first carrying
  out the full finite-matrix test. Noorani's analysis of homogeneous
  extensions and Markov-shift applications
  \cite{Noorani1997HomogeneousExtensionsMarkov}, the analysis of Parry and
  Liv\v{s}ic obstructions in
  \cite{BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions}, together with
  the finite-action periodic-data viewpoint of
  \cite{Fiebig1993PeriodicFiniteGroupActions} and the \(G\)-SFT
  flow-equivalence framework of
  \cite{BoyleCarlsenEilers2020FlowEquivalenceGSFT}, gives a partial
  structural context. Parry's non-abelian periodic-point theorem
  \cite{Parry1999LivsicNonAbelian} gives another piece of this picture,
  and the \(S_3\)-model of \Cref{prop:s3-example-channels} (where
  \(\rho(A_\rho)=1<2=\lambda\)) provides one explicit verification.

\item \emph{Non-abelian analogues of cyclic arithmetic.} The scalar
  root-of-unity comparison in \Cref{sec:finite-part} is abelian in
  nature and belongs to the canonical cyclic-lift model, not to cyclic
  quotients of a fixed non-abelian cocycle. It remains open whether
  boundary-multiplicity or residue-class data have a natural
  higher-dimensional twisted-channel analogue.

\item \emph{Operator-theoretic finite parts.} The finite-state scope set
  in \Cref{sec:intro-scope-of-results} deliberately leaves H\"older/Ruelle
  or other non-finite-state determinant theories to a separate project.
  Any such extension would need its own Banach space, determinant
  convention, pressure normalization, and Perron-point hypotheses before
  an Adams--M\"obius finite-part formula could be compared with the one
  proved here.
\end{enumerate}
