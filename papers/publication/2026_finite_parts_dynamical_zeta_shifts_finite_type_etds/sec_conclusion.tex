\section{Discussion and open problems}\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Summary of the main contribution}

For a finite-group extension of a mixing subshift of finite type, the
twisted determinant family records periodic labels. The main point of
this paper is that the corresponding primitive labels are recovered by a
finite Adams--M\"obius calculation in Peter--Weyl coordinates. This is
the finite-state correction forced by the repetition rule
\(g\mapsto g^m\) for primitive orbits. Under the twisted gap
\(\eta<\lambda\), the recovered primitive channels have Perron-point
values and hence determine the class-indexed constants
\(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) in the Frobenius-class Mertens sums.

The Euler-product constants require the same distinction. Once the
classical leading exponent \(|C|/|G|\) has been separated, the non-trivial
coordinates of the constant are the fixed-label Euler transform values
\(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), not the periodic Artin logarithms
\(\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\). The determinant formula therefore
gives
\[
  K_C=
  \exp\!\left(
    -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
    \left(
      \gamma+\log C(A)
      +\sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
    \right)
  \right),
\]
with the positive real branch
(\Cref{cor:class-function-adams-mobius,thm:class-mertens-explicit,thm:class-euler-product-mertens,prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron,cor:class-product-constant-rigidity,thm:class-mertens-fourier}). This is a determinant-level refinement of
the classical Chebotarev/Mertens orbit-counting picture, rather than a
new leading-density theorem: by
\Cref{cor:class-product-classical-layer-uniqueness}, once that classical
existence/exponent layer and the scalar normalisation are held fixed, the
Adams--M\"obius fixed-label vector is the unique branch-compatible
non-trivial coordinate vector producing the true constants. Quotient
functoriality identifies the
abelianised primitive profile, and cyclic quotient observables span
precisely the one-dimensional Peter--Weyl shadow. The \(S_3\) example
shows both phenomena concretely: the sign line detects an Adams
correction invisible to the Artin logarithm, while the standard
two-dimensional representation gives a genuine non-abelian component.

The scalar finite-part and cyclic-lift calculations supply only the
trivial-channel normalisation and a fixed-matrix comparison model. The
projection theorem
\Cref{thm:scalar-normalisation-projection-boundary} marks the boundary:
after normalising the class constants, scalar data give the constant
class-function component \(\mathcal M(A)\mathbf 1_G\), whereas the
non-trivial primitive profile is the Peter--Weyl vector
\(\mathcal B_{A,\tau;G}\).

\subsection{Further directions}

Several natural extensions require hypotheses different from the
finite-dimensional theorem chain proved here.  A H\"older/Ruelle
transfer-operator version would require Banach-space hypotheses and determinant
convention comparisons not used in the finite-matrix proof.  Completed
\(\xi\)- or \(\zeta\)-type finite-part theories would introduce a different
analytic object.  Refined secondary spectral-edge terms beyond the
Perron-boundary constants require their own separation hypotheses; here we use
only the noninterference statement proved in
\Cref{prop:main-chain-secondary-noninterference}.  Broad sufficient criteria
for the strict twisted gap would be a cocycle-classification problem, whereas
the present eta-gap material is a finite Perron-point test and boundary
obstruction.  Non-abelian cyclic-arithmetic refinements, fixed-matrix
cyclic-lift reconstruction, scalar-boundary finite-observation limits, and
inverse problems from partial class observations are separate directions, not
suppressed hypotheses for the present results.

The appendix material follows the same convention.  This article retains the
scalar normalisation and finite reproducibility records needed by the
finite-group determinant constants: the scalar constant \(C(A)\), the finite
part \(\FP(A)\), the no-descent warning, the fixed-label rational enclosures,
and the explicit \(S_3\) obstruction calculation.  The arguments above use only
the finite interfaces proved here: the eta-gap criterion, the robustness
statement \Cref{prop:main-chain-secondary-noninterference}, the fixed-label
records, and the fixed-matrix scalar boundary theorem.  Further cyclic-lift,
finite-observation, secondary-asymptotic, zeta-completion, and external
artifact-validation statements require additional theorems not assumed in this
paper.
