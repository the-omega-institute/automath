\section{Discussion and open problems}\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Summary of the main contribution}

For a finite-group extension of a mixing subshift of finite type, the
twisted determinant family records periodic labels. The main point of
this paper is that the corresponding primitive labels are recovered by a
finite Adams--M\"obius calculation in Peter--Weyl coordinates. This is
the finite-state correction forced by the repetition rule
\(g\mapsto g^m\) for primitive orbits. Under the twisted gap
\(\eta<\lambda\), the recovered primitive channels have Perron-point
values and hence determine the class-indexed constants
\(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) in the Frobenius-class Mertens sums.

The Euler-product constants require the same distinction.  The classical
Frobenius-class product theorem supplies the existence statement and the leading
exponent \(|C|/|G|\).  Once that layer and the scalar normalisation are fixed,
the remaining non-trivial coordinates of the constants are the fixed-label
Euler values \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), not the periodic Artin logarithms
\(\mathcal L_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\).  Thus the contribution is not a new proof
of the Frobenius density exponent, but a determinant formula for the normalised
constant and a rigidity statement saying that the Adams--M\"obius fixed-label
vector is the branch-compatible coordinate vector that produces the true
constants.  Quotient functoriality identifies the abelianised primitive profile,
and cyclic quotient observables span precisely the one-dimensional Peter--Weyl
shadow. The \(S_3\) example shows both phenomena concretely: the sign line
detects an Adams correction invisible to the Artin logarithm, while the standard
two-dimensional representation gives a genuine non-abelian component.

The scalar finite-part and cyclic-lift calculations supply the
trivial-channel normalisation and a fixed-matrix comparison model. The
projection theorem marks the corresponding scalar boundary:
after normalising the class constants, scalar data give the constant
class-function component \(\mathcal M(A)\mathbf 1_G\), whereas the
non-trivial primitive profile is the Peter--Weyl vector
\(\mathcal B_{A,\tau;G}\).

\subsection{Open directions}

Several natural extensions remain beyond the finite-state determinant setting
treated here.  One direction is to obtain secondary-term refinements of the
Frobenius-class Mertens formula while preserving the Adams--M\"obius separation
between periodic and primitive labels.  Another is to give structural criteria
for the strict twisted gap \(\eta<\lambda\), for instance in terms of mixing
properties of the skew product or representation-theoretic expansion of the
cocycle.  A third direction is to extend the determinant formula to
H\"older/Ruelle transfer operators, where branch choices and Perron residues
would have to be controlled analytically rather than by finite matrices.

There are also inverse problems suggested by the quotient calculations.  Cyclic
quotient observations see only the one-dimensional Peter--Weyl sector, while the
canonical cyclic-lift root layer gives a fixed-matrix comparison model rather
than an invariant of the original finite-group cocycle.  Reconstruction from
restricted observations therefore asks for an observability theory with explicit
descent hypotheses.  The rational \(S_3\) enclosures in the appendices exhibit
the Adams correction and the failure of the naive Artin substitution in a
concrete finite model.
