\section{Conditional secondary spectral-edge tail refinement}
\label{sec:secondary-term}

This appendix records a retained conditional tail refinement after the main
constants have already been constructed.  Main Theorems~I--III do not use the
hypotheses introduced here: the values
\(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), the class constants
\(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\), the fixed-label Euler values
\(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), and the product constants \(K_C\) are all
fixed before this section is invoked.  The results below do not alter any
of those quantities.  If, in addition to \(\lambda>1\) and
\(\eta<\lambda\), one has \(R>\lambda^{1/2}\) and, for the sharper tail
estimate, \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}<R\), then the finite Peter--Weyl trace
formula identifies a first oscillating remainder around those constants.
Without these extra separations no secondary expansion is asserted, and no
general secondary-term theory is claimed.  The section is included as a
conditional supporting refinement rather than as part of the main theorem
spine; any broader theory of secondary spectral-edge detection, sharpness,
or examples is deliberately left to a separate project.

\begin{theorem}[Appendix quarantine for the secondary package]
  \label{thm:secondary-companion-quarantine}
  The results in this companion section are logically downstream from Main
  Theorems~I--III.  More precisely, no statement in this section is used to
  define or prove any of
  \[
    \Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}),\qquad
    \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G),
    \qquad F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}),
    \qquad K_C,
  \]
  and the only exported product-level datum under the additional secondary
  hypotheses is a vanishing finite-length tail factor.  Consequently this
  retained package can be read after the main theorem chain without changing
  Main Theorems~I--III or any constant appearing in them.  Equivalently, the
  appendix is an instance of the main-spine non-interference principle
  \Cref{thm:main-spine-secondary-noninterference}, not an additional route to
  the Adams--M\"obius/Frobenius-product determinant chain.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The definitions used below start with the already constructed finite
  matrices \(A_\varrho\), the already imposed gap \(\eta<\lambda\), and the
  additional edge quantities \(R\), \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf1}\),
  \(\mathcal E_\varrho\), and \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}\).  None of these edge
  quantities occurs in the hypotheses or conclusions of Main Theorems~I--III
  or in the formulas defining \(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\),
  \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\), \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), or \(K_C\).
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-secondary-term} gives only a pointwise secondary
  remainder and a compensated harmonic-tail identity after those constants
  have been named.  \Cref{prop:secondary-tail-only-refinement} then inserts
  that identity into the product expansion and shows that the additional
  factor tends to one.  Thus the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:main-spine-secondary-noninterference} are met with
  \(S_C(n)=\mathfrak s_C(n)\) and \(T_C(N)=\mathfrak T_C(N)\).  The isolation
  theorem \Cref{thm:secondary-edge-isolation} records the same
  non-interference with the main constants.  Hence the section contributes
  only the conditional tail refinement, not an input to the finite determinant
  recovery, Perron-point constants, or Adams-corrected product constants of
  the main spine.
\end{proof}

For every finite matrix \(M\) and every eigenvalue
\(\alpha\in\spec(M)\), let \(m_M(\alpha)\) denote the algebraic
multiplicity of \(\alpha\). Assume throughout this section that
\(\lambda>1\), that
\[
  \eta:=\max_{\varrho\neq\mathbf 1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda,
  \qquad
  R:=\max\{\Lambda(A),\eta\}>\lambda^{1/2}.
\]
If \(G\) is trivial, the maximum defining \(\eta\) is empty and
\(\eta=0\).
Define the spectral edge sets
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathcal E_{\mathbf 1}
    &:=\{\mu\in\spec(A)\setminus\{\lambda\}:|\mu|=R\}, \\
    \mathcal E_\varrho
    &:=\{\beta\in\spec(A_\varrho):|\beta|=R\}
    \quad (\varrho\neq\mathbf 1),
  \end{aligned}
\]
and, with empty maxima interpreted as \(0\),
\[
  \Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}
  :=
  \max\left\{
    \lambda^{1/2},
    \max_{\mu\in\spec(A)\setminus(\{\lambda\}\cup\mathcal E_{\mathbf 1})}
      |\mu|,
    \max_{\varrho\neq\mathbf 1}
      \max_{\beta\in\spec(A_\varrho)\setminus\mathcal E_\varrho}
        |\beta|
  \right\}.
\]
For each conjugacy class \(C\subset G\), set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:class-secondary-profile}
  \mathfrak s_C(n)
  :=
  \frac{|C|}{|G|}
  \left(
    \sum_{\mu\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf 1}}m_A(\mu)\mu^n
    +
    \sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf 1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
      \sum_{\beta\in\mathcal E_\varrho}m_{A_\varrho}(\beta)\beta^n
  \right)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:class-secondary-tail}
  \mathfrak T_C(N)
  :=
  \sum_{n>N}\frac{\mathfrak s_C(n)}{n\lambda^n}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Finite edge skeleton under secondary separation]
  \label{prop:secondary-edge-skeleton}
  Assume the standing hypotheses \(\lambda>1\), \(\eta<\lambda\), and
  \(R>\lambda^{1/2}\) from above.  Define
  \[
    E_{\mathbf 1}(n)
    :=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf 1}}m_A(\mu)\mu^n,
    \qquad
    E_\varrho(n)
    :=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal E_\varrho}
      m_{A_\varrho}(\beta)\beta^n
    \quad(\varrho\neq\mathbf 1),
  \]
  and let
  \[
    \mathfrak b_n(C):=\frac{|G|}{|C|}\mathfrak s_C(n)
  \]
  be the normalised edge profile on conjugacy classes. Then, for every
  \(n\ge1\), the class profile is exactly the Peter--Weyl transform of
  the finite edge skeleton:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:secondary-edge-forward}
    \mathfrak b_n(C)
    =
    E_{\mathbf 1}(n)+
    \sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf 1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)E_\varrho(n).
  \end{equation}
  Conversely the edge skeleton is recovered from the normalised class
  profile by
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:secondary-edge-inverse}
    E_{\mathbf 1}(n)
    =\sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathfrak b_n(C),
    \qquad
    E_\pi(n)
    =\sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathfrak b_n(C)\chi_\pi(C)
    \quad(\pi\neq\mathbf 1).
  \end{equation}
  Hence this conditional secondary correction is completely exhausted by
  the finite list of edge exponential sums \(E_\varrho(n)\). In
  particular, if \(\mathfrak s_C(n)=0\) for every conjugacy class \(C\)
  and every \(n\ge1\), then all edge sums \(E_\varrho(n)\) vanish
  identically; equivalently, every set \(\mathcal E_\varrho\) is empty.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Equation \eqref{eq:secondary-edge-forward} is the definition of
  \(\mathfrak s_C(n)\), after multiplying by \(|G|/|C|\) and writing the
  two displayed edge sums as \(E_{\mathbf1}(n)\) and \(E_\varrho(n)\).
  For the inverse formulas, average
  \eqref{eq:secondary-edge-forward} over conjugacy classes with weights
  \(|C|/|G|\). The non-trivial character averages vanish, so the first
  formula in \eqref{eq:secondary-edge-inverse} follows. Multiplying
  \eqref{eq:secondary-edge-forward} by \(\chi_\pi(C)\), averaging over
  conjugacy classes, and using the Hermitian character orthogonality
  relation
  \[
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)\chi_\pi(C)=\delta_{\varrho\pi}
  \]
  gives the second formula.

  The preceding identities show that the class data and the edge
  skeleton determine one another for each fixed \(n\). If
  \(\mathfrak s_C(n)=0\) for every \(C\) and \(n\), then
  \eqref{eq:secondary-edge-inverse} gives \(E_\varrho(n)=0\) for every
  \(\varrho\) and every \(n\). For a fixed \(\varrho\), this is a finite
  exponential sum
  \(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal E_\varrho}m_{A_\varrho}(\alpha)\alpha^n\)
  that vanishes for all positive \(n\). The Vandermonde matrix attached
  to the distinct non-zero frequencies in \(\mathcal E_\varrho\) is
  invertible, so all coefficients must be zero. The coefficients are
  algebraic multiplicities; therefore \(\mathcal E_\varrho\) is empty.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Retained conditional secondary-tail refinement]
  \label{thm:class-mertens-secondary-term}
  Assume \(\lambda>1\), \(\eta<\lambda\), and \(R>\lambda^{1/2}\), and assume
  the additional separation \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}<R\).  These additional
  hypotheses are not used to establish the constants in Main
  Theorems~II--III.  The secondary edge gives only the
  following refinement above the square-root barrier
  \(R>\lambda^{1/2}\): it gives a pointwise first remainder and the
  corresponding compensated harmonic-tail identity.  It does not redefine
  \(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\),
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), or \(K_C\).  More precisely, the
  tail series \(\mathfrak T_C(N)\) converges absolutely for every \(C\), and
  for every conjugacy class \(C\subset G\),
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:class-secondary-pointwise}
    p_{n,C}
    =
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}\frac{\lambda^n}{n}
    +\frac{\mathfrak s_C(n)}{n}
    +O\!\left(\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}^n}{n}\right).
  \end{equation}
  Moreover,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:class-secondary-harmonic}
    \sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
    =
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}H_N
    +\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\gamma
    -\mathfrak T_C(N)
    +O\!\left(\frac{(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}/\lambda)^N}{N}\right).
  \end{equation}
  The implied constants are uniform in the conjugacy class \(C\), in
  \(n\), and in \(N\).  No class-Mertens constant or Adams-corrected
  Euler-product constant is redefined by this theorem; the only
  product-level effect is the vanishing tail factor in
  \Cref{prop:secondary-tail-only-refinement}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The strict inequality \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}<R\) is an additional
  hypothesis of the theorem, and \(R>\lambda^{1/2}\) is part of the standing
  hypothesis of this appendix.

  The trace of a finite matrix equals the sum of its eigenvalues with
  algebraic multiplicity. Therefore
  \[
    \Tr(A^n)
    =
    \lambda^n
    +\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf 1}}m_A(\mu)\mu^n
    +O(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}^n),
  \]
  and, for every non-trivial \(\varrho\in\Irr(G)\),
  \[
    \Tr(A_\varrho^n)
    =
    \sum_{\beta\in\mathcal E_\varrho}m_{A_\varrho}(\beta)\beta^n
    +O(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}^n).
  \]
  The implied constants are uniform in \(\varrho\) because \(\Irr(G)\)
  is finite.

  Inserting the first identity into \eqref{eq:primitive-mobius} yields
  \[
    p_n(A)
    =
    \frac{\lambda^n}{n}
    +\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf 1}}m_A(\mu)\mu^n
    +O\!\left(\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}^n}{n}\right),
  \]
  because the divisor \(d=1\) contributes the spectral-edge sum displayed
  above. The divisors \(d>1\) contribute
  \[
    O\!\left(
      \frac{1}{n}\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\ d>1}}\lambda^{n/d}
    \right)
    =
    O\!\left(\frac{\lambda^{n/2}}{n}\right)
    =
    O\!\left(\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}^n}{n}\right).
  \]

  Fix next \(\varrho\neq\mathbf 1\). By
  \Cref{thm:primitive-peter-weyl-trace},
  \[
    \pi_{n,\varrho}(A,\tau)
    =
    \frac{1}{n}\Tr(A_\varrho^n)
    +\frac{1}{n}
      \sum_{\substack{m\mid n\\ m>1}}\mu(m)
        \sum_{\kappa\in\Irr(G)}
          a_{\varrho,\kappa}^{(m)}\Tr(A_\kappa^{n/m}).
  \]
  The first term gives the contribution from \(\mathcal E_\varrho\). For
  the divisor sum, the same argument as in
  \Cref{lem:nontrivial-peter-weyl-channel-bound}, using
  \Cref{lem:adams-coefficients-bounded} and
  \Cref{prop:twisted-spectral-radius-bound}, yields
  \[
    \sum_{\substack{m\mid n\\ m>1}}
      \left|
        \sum_{\kappa\in\Irr(G)}
          a_{\varrho,\kappa}^{(m)}\Tr(A_\kappa^{n/m})
      \right|
    =
    O(\lambda^{n/2}),
  \]
  uniformly in \(\varrho\). Hence
  \[
    \pi_{n,\varrho}(A,\tau)
    =
    \frac{1}{n}
      \sum_{\beta\in\mathcal E_\varrho}m_{A_\varrho}(\beta)\beta^n
    +O\!\left(\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}^n}{n}\right)
  \]
  uniformly for all non-trivial \(\varrho\).

  Substituting the last two expansions into
  \Cref{lem:class-primitive-trivial-split} gives
  \eqref{eq:class-secondary-pointwise}. The uniformity in \(C\) follows
  from the finiteness of the conjugacy classes and the bounds
  \(|\chi_\varrho(C)|\le \dim\varrho\).

  Dividing \eqref{eq:class-secondary-pointwise} by \(\lambda^n\) yields
  \[
    \frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
    =
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}\frac{1}{n}
    +\frac{\mathfrak s_C(n)}{n\lambda^n}
    +O\!\left(\frac{(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}/\lambda)^n}{n}\right).
  \]
  Since \(R<\lambda\), the definition of \(\mathfrak s_C(n)\) shows
  that \(\mathfrak T_C(N)\) converges absolutely. Summing the preceding
  identity over \(n>N\) and applying
  \Cref{lem:geometric-harmonic-tail} gives
  \[
    \sum_{n>N}
      \left(
        \frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
        -\frac{|C|}{|G|}\frac{1}{n}
      \right)
    =
    \mathfrak T_C(N)
    +O\!\left(\frac{(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}/\lambda)^N}{N}\right).
  \]
  On the other hand, \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit} implies
  \[
    \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\gamma
    =
    \sum_{n\ge 1}
      \left(
        \frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
        -\frac{|C|}{|G|}\frac{1}{n}
      \right).
  \]
  Subtracting the tail beyond \(N\) from this identity yields
  \eqref{eq:class-secondary-harmonic}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Secondary-edge refinement of the product tail]
  \label{prop:secondary-tail-only-refinement}
  Assume the full secondary hypotheses \(\lambda>1\),
  \(\eta<\lambda\), \(R>\lambda^{1/2}\), and
  \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}<R\), equivalently the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-secondary-term}.  Let \(P_C(N)\) and \(K_C\)
  be the Frobenius-class Euler product and Adams-corrected constant of
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}. The secondary data refine only
  the remainder terms while keeping the Perron-point constants fixed and
  without adding any determinant coordinate to Main Theorems~I--III. For
  every conjugacy class
  \(C\subset G\), the secondary tail satisfies
  \[
    \mathfrak T_C(N)=O\!\left(\frac{(R/\lambda)^N}{N}\right),
    \qquad N\to\infty,
  \]
  and therefore tends to zero. Moreover the compensated Mertens
  approximants
  \[
    \mathcal M_{C,N}^{\mathrm{sec}}
    :=
    \sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
    -\frac{|C|}{|G|}H_N
    +\frac{|C|}{|G|}\gamma
    +\mathfrak T_C(N)
  \]
  satisfy
  \[
    \mathcal M_{C,N}^{\mathrm{sec}}
    =
    \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)
    +O\!\left(\frac{(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}/\lambda)^N}{N}\right).
  \]
  Finally the Frobenius-class product has the same leading constant
  \(K_C\) as in \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}, with finite-length
  secondary correction only in the vanishing factor
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:secondary-product-tail-only}
  \begin{aligned}
    P_C(N)
    &=
    K_C\,N^{-|C|/|G|}
    \exp\!\left(\mathfrak T_C(N)-\frac{|C|}{|G|}(H_N-\log N-\gamma)
    \right)\\
    &\quad\times
    \exp\!\left(
      O\!\left(\frac{(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}/\lambda)^N+\lambda^{-N}}{N}\right)
    \right).
  \end{aligned}
  \end{equation}
  In particular the isolated secondary spectral edge, under the additional
  separation hypotheses of this appendix, changes neither the class
  Mertens constant \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) nor the Adams-corrected
  Euler-product constant \(K_C\). It refines a finite-length remainder
  after the main theorem chain has supplied the constants.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Since every frequency in \(\mathfrak s_C(n)\) has modulus \(R<\lambda\)
  and there are only finitely many such frequencies, there is a constant
  \(B_C\) such that \(|\mathfrak s_C(n)|\le B_C R^n\). Hence
  \[
    |\mathfrak T_C(N)|
    \le
    B_C\sum_{n>N}\frac{(R/\lambda)^n}{n}
    =O\!\left(\frac{(R/\lambda)^N}{N}\right),
  \]
  which proves the first assertion. The formula for
  \(\mathcal M_{C,N}^{\mathrm{sec}}\) is exactly
  \eqref{eq:class-secondary-harmonic} rearranged.

  It remains to identify the product constant. Taking logarithms in the
  definition of \(P_C(N)\) from \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}
  gives
  \[
    \log P_C(N)
    =
    -\sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
    -\sum_{r\ge2}\frac1r\sum_{n\le N}p_{n,C}\lambda^{-rn}.
  \]
  The second term differs from
  \(-\sum_{r\ge2}\Pi_{\mathbf 1_C}(\lambda^{-r})/r\) by
  \(O(\lambda^{-N}/N)\). Indeed, \(p_{n,C}\le p_n(A)\), and
  \Cref{lem:primitive-orbit-asymptotic} gives
  \(p_n(A)=O(\lambda^n/n)\), so the omitted tail is bounded by a constant
  times
  \[
    \sum_{r\ge2}\frac1r\sum_{n>N}\frac{\lambda^n}{n}\lambda^{-rn}
    \le
    \sum_{n>N}\frac{\lambda^{-n}}{n}
    =O\!\left(\frac{\lambda^{-N}}{N}\right).
  \]
  Substituting \eqref{eq:class-secondary-harmonic} into the logarithm and
  using
  \[
    \log K_C
    =
    -\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)
    -\sum_{r\ge2}\frac{\Pi_{\mathbf 1_C}(\lambda^{-r})}{r}
  \]
  from \eqref{eq:class-euler-product-k-mc}, we obtain
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    \log P_C(N)
    &=
    \log K_C
    -\frac{|C|}{|G|}\log N
    +\mathfrak T_C(N)
    -\frac{|C|}{|G|}(H_N-\log N-\gamma)\\
    &\quad
    +O\!\left(\frac{(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}/\lambda)^N+\lambda^{-N}}{N}\right).
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  Exponentiating this identity proves
  \eqref{eq:secondary-product-tail-only}. Since
  \(\mathfrak T_C(N)\to0\), \(H_N-\log N-\gamma\to0\), and the displayed
  error tends to zero, the limiting class-product constant remains
  exactly \(K_C\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Isolation of the retained secondary edge]
  \label{thm:secondary-edge-isolation}
  Assume the full secondary hypotheses \(\lambda>1\),
  \(\eta<\lambda\), \(R>\lambda^{1/2}\), and
  \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}<R\), equivalently the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-secondary-term}.  The conditional secondary
  refinement is separated from Main Theorems~I--III in the following
  precise sense.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
    \item Its only inputs beyond Main Theorems~I--III are the finite edge
    sets \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf1}\), \(\mathcal E_\varrho\) and the strict
    separations \(R>\lambda^{1/2}\), \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}<R\).
    \item It does not change any value
    \(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\),
    \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), or \(K_C\) constructed in the main theorem
    spine.
    \item After those main constants are fixed, all class-level secondary
    information is equivalent, for each \(n\ge1\), to the
    finite Peter--Weyl edge skeleton
    \((E_{\mathbf1}(n),(E_\varrho(n))_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1})\) of
    \Cref{prop:secondary-edge-skeleton}; hence it introduces no additional
    determinant, branch, cyclic-lift, or product-constant coordinate.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The first assertion is exactly the hypothesis list at the beginning of
  this section together with the additional strict inequality in
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-secondary-term}.  The main theorem sequence supplies
  the determinant formula, Abel-path Perron values, Mertens constants, and
  product constants before any spectral-edge set is introduced here.

  For (ii), \Cref{prop:secondary-tail-only-refinement} proves that the
  secondary contribution enters the finite product only through the factor
  displayed in \eqref{eq:secondary-product-tail-only}.  The tail
  \(\mathfrak T_C(N)\) tends to zero, and the error term in that formula
  tends to zero.  Equivalently,
  \Cref{thm:main-spine-secondary-noninterference} applies to the present
  package, so the limiting constants remain the same
  \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) and \(K_C\) obtained from
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit,thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.  The
  fixed-label Perron values are not altered either, because they are
  supplied by the normalized determinant evaluation of
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron} before the secondary tail is
  formed.

  For (iii), \Cref{prop:secondary-edge-skeleton} gives the forward and
  inverse Peter--Weyl transforms
  \eqref{eq:secondary-edge-forward}--\eqref{eq:secondary-edge-inverse}
  between the normalized class profile \(\mathfrak b_n(C)\) and the finite
  list of edge sums.  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-secondary-term} uses only
  those profiles to form the pointwise correction and its tail, and
  \Cref{prop:secondary-tail-only-refinement} substitutes that tail into a
  vanishing remainder factor.  Thus the class-level secondary data and the
  finite edge skeleton determine each other at every length, while all
  determinant branches, cyclic-lift records, and product constants remain
  the already fixed main-theorem objects.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Product-level consequence of the secondary refinement]
  \label{cor:secondary-only-exported-consequence}
  Assume the full secondary hypotheses \(\lambda>1\),
  \(\eta<\lambda\), \(R>\lambda^{1/2}\), and
  \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}<R\).  The only
  information from the secondary spectral edge that is contributed to the
  finite-group product theory is the vanishing tail factor in
  \Cref{prop:secondary-tail-only-refinement}.  In particular, after the
  main theorem chain has fixed \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\), \(K_C\), and the
  non-scalar determinant coordinates
  \((F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}))_{\varrho\ne\mathbf1}\), the data
  \(\mathcal E_\varrho\), \(\mathfrak s_C(n)\), and
  \(\mathfrak T_C(N)\) determine neither a new Perron-point coordinate nor a
  new product constant.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-secondary-term} expresses the secondary data only
  through \(\mathfrak s_C(n)\) in the pointwise remainder
  \eqref{eq:class-secondary-pointwise} and through the tail
  \(\mathfrak T_C(N)\) in \eqref{eq:class-secondary-harmonic}.  By
  \Cref{prop:secondary-edge-skeleton}, these quantities are equivalent to
  the finite edge exponential sums and contain no additional component
  outside the Peter--Weyl class profile already used in the displayed
  identities.  \Cref{prop:secondary-tail-only-refinement} then substitutes
  \eqref{eq:class-secondary-harmonic} into the class product and shows that
  \(\mathfrak T_C(N)\to0\), so the limiting constants remain exactly
  \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) and \(K_C\) from Main Theorems~II--III.  The
  determinant coordinates of \(K_C\) are already identified by
  \Cref{thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity}; since the secondary terms
  occur only in the displayed vanishing tails, they introduce no further
  product-theoretic datum.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Supporting conditional detection of the secondary modulus]
  \label{cor:class-secondary-term-detects-spectrum}
  Assume the full secondary hypotheses \(\lambda>1\),
  \(\eta<\lambda\), \(R>\lambda^{1/2}\), and
  \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}<R\), equivalently the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-secondary-term}.  Then
  \[
    R
    =
    \max_{C\subset G}
      \limsup_{n\to\infty}
      \left|
        n\,p_{n,C}-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\lambda^n
      \right|^{1/n},
  \]
  where the maximum is taken over the conjugacy classes of \(G\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  By \eqref{eq:class-secondary-pointwise},
  \[
    n\,p_{n,C}-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\lambda^n
    =
    \mathfrak s_C(n)+O(\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}^n)
    \qquad (n\to\infty),
  \]
  so
  \[
    \limsup_{n\to\infty}
      \left|
        n\,p_{n,C}-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\lambda^n
      \right|^{1/n}
    \le R
  \]
  for every \(C\).

  Assume that the displayed maximum were strictly smaller than \(R\).
  Since the set of conjugacy classes is finite, there would then exist
  \(\Gamma_0<R\) such that
  \[
    n\,p_{n,C}-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\lambda^n
    =O(\Gamma_0^n)
    \qquad\text{uniformly in } C.
  \]
  Together with \eqref{eq:class-secondary-pointwise}, this would imply
  \[
    \mathfrak s_C(n)=O(\Gamma_1^n)
    \qquad\text{for every } C,
  \]
  where \(\Gamma_1:=\max\{\Gamma_0,\Gamma_{\mathrm{sec}}\}<R\).
  Multiplying by \(|G|/|C|\) gives the class function
  \[
    \mathfrak b_n(C)
    :=
    \frac{|G|}{|C|}\mathfrak s_C(n)
    =
    \sum_{\mu\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf 1}}m_A(\mu)\mu^n
    +
    \sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf 1}\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)
      \sum_{\beta\in\mathcal E_\varrho}m_{A_\varrho}(\beta)\beta^n,
  \]
  which would also satisfy \(\mathfrak b_n(C)=O(\Gamma_1^n)\)
  uniformly in \(C\).

  Averaging over conjugacy classes yields
  \[
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathfrak b_n(C)
    =
    \sum_{\mu\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf 1}}m_A(\mu)\mu^n,
  \]
  because the average of every non-trivial irreducible character
  vanishes. Likewise, for every non-trivial \(\pi\in\Irr(G)\),
  character orthogonality gives
  \[
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathfrak b_n(C)\chi_\pi(C)
    =
    \sum_{\beta\in\mathcal E_\pi}m_{A_\pi}(\beta)\beta^n.
  \]
  The assumed bound \(\mathfrak b_n(C)=O(\Gamma_1^n)\) would therefore
  force each of these spectral-edge sums to be \(O(\Gamma_1^n)\).

  By definition of \(R\), at least one among the finitely many sums
  \[
    \sum_{\mu\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf 1}}m_A(\mu)\mu^n,
    \qquad
    \sum_{\beta\in\mathcal E_\pi}m_{A_\pi}(\beta)\beta^n
    \quad (\pi\neq\mathbf 1),
  \]
  is a non-zero finite exponential sum whose frequencies all have
  modulus \(R\). \Cref{lem:finite-part-exponential-sum-limsup} then
  gives limsup \(R\), contradicting the bound \(O(\Gamma_1^n)\) with
  \(\Gamma_1<R\). Hence the maximum cannot be smaller than \(R\), and
  equality follows.
\end{proof}
