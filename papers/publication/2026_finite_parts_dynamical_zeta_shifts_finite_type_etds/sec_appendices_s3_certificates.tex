\section[Exact rational verification for the S3 example]{Exact
  rational verification for the \texorpdfstring{$S_3$}{S3}
  example}
\label{app:s3-log-certificates}

This appendix gives the finite rational arithmetic for the \(S_3\) obstruction used in
\Cref{thm:adams-obstruction-class-product,prop:s3-naive-artin-obstruction}.
The main text uses only the coarse rational non-vanishing windows from
\Cref{lem:s3-example-perron-certificates}; the pages below display the exact
finite truncations, tail estimates, and interval enclosures that certify those
windows.  The material is not a catalogue of finite-group examples and not a
general finite-verification theory for arbitrary finite groups.  Each displayed
certificate is stated as an exact rational record: after denominators are
cleared, verification reduces to integer comparisons and to the listed rational
logarithm enclosures.  Independent mechanical replay should therefore check the
same finite list of inequalities, but no analytic statement in the paper relies
on a floating-point calculation or on an unrecorded numerical oracle.

\begin{proposition}[Compact rational replay record for the \(S_3\) example]
\label{prop:s3-compact-certificate-protocol}
  The four rational windows used in the obstruction and product-constant chain
  are checked by the following finite rational data.  The underlying model is the
  full two-shift matrix
  \(
    A=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{psmallmatrix}
  \)
  with the edge labels in \eqref{eq:s3-example-edge-labels}.  The exact twisted
  blocks are
  \[
    A_\varepsilon=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    A_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      \frac12&\frac32&1&0\\
      \frac12&-\frac12&0&1\\
      -1&0&-\frac12&-\frac32\\
      0&1&\frac12&-\frac12
    \end{pmatrix},
  \]
  and their determinant polynomials are
  \(
    \det(I-zA)=1-2z,
    \det(I-zA_\varepsilon)=1,
    \det(I-zA_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}})=1+z
  \).
  Each row records the mathematical input, branch convention, finite
  truncation, rational tail, and output window; no floating-point datum or
  unrecorded limiting enumeration is part of the certificate.
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{L{0.18\textwidth}L{0.75\textwidth}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Coordinate} & \textbf{Certificate record}\\
    \midrule
    \(\Pi_\varepsilon(1/2)\) &
      Input \(E_7\) from \eqref{eq:s3-example-epsilon-certificate-sum} with
      rational brackets \(L_3(q)<\log q<U_3(q)\); positive real logarithm
      branch; odd primitive window \(r=1,3,5,7\); tail
      \(\abs{\Theta_\varepsilon}\le1/1105920\); output
      \((-342/1000,-341/1000)\).\\[0.6ex]
    \(\Pi_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)\) &
      Input \(R_{15}\) from \eqref{eq:s3-example-rho-certificate-sum} with
      finite power-map coefficients and the same \(L_3/U_3\) brackets; positive
      real logarithm branch; squarefree primitive window \(m\le15\); tail
      \(\abs{\Theta_{\mathrm{std}}}\le9/524288\); output
      \((-719/1000,-718/1000)\).\\[0.6ex]
    \(F_\varepsilon(1/2)\) &
      Input sign-representation Adams--M\"obius coefficients and rational
      endpoints \(Q_8^\pm\); scalar Perron triple deleted; Abel branch for the
      non-trivial boundary terms; finite powers through \(k=8\); tail
      \(B_\varepsilon=1/34359738360\); output
      \((-381/1000,-380/1000)\).\\[0.6ex]
    \(F_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)\) &
      Input Adams--M\"obius coefficients \((A_k,B_k)\), listed logarithm
      arguments, and rational endpoints \(S_{32}^\pm\); scalar Perron triple
      deleted; Abel branch for legal \(rm=1\) terms and interior branch for
      \(rm\ge2\); non-zero listed \(k\le32\); tail
      \(B_{\mathrm{std}}=(5/33)2^{-32}\); output \((-923/1000,-922/1000)\).\\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  The first two rows supply the coarse Perron-channel windows used in
  \Cref{lem:s3-example-perron-certificates}.  The last two rows are valid
  fixed-label enclosures in the sense of
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-branch-tail-record,cor:main-iii-branch-tail-enclosure}
  and therefore supply the \(S_3\) input to
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens,thm:main-iii-certificate-chain}.
  Equivalently, a replay program for this proposition has a finite input tape:
  the rational endpoints, the rational tails, the finite Adams coefficients,
  and the integer numerator tests obtained after clearing denominators in the
  cited inequalities.  Its role is evidentiary rather than mathematical: the
  proposition asserts the certificate format and the exact comparisons that any
  such replay must reproduce.
For every rational logarithm argument \(q>1\), the endpoints
  \(L_3(q)\) and \(U_3(q)\) are rational and enclose \(\log q\) by
  \eqref{eq:s3-log-lower-upper-bounds}.  Multiplying by a positive rational
  coefficient preserves the endpoint order, while multiplying by a negative
  coefficient reverses it.  Thus each finite signed sum in the table has a
  rational lower endpoint and a rational upper endpoint obtained by the stated
  deterministic endpoint-selection rule.

  The truncation and tail entries in the first two rows are exactly
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-epsilon-tail-certificate} and
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-rho-tail-certificate}; the proof of
  \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-sharp-certificates} shows that adding those rational
  radii to the finite intervals places \(\Pi_\varepsilon(1/2)\) and
  \(\Pi_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)\) in the displayed windows.  For the last two rows,
  \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-f-level-certificates} gives the fixed-label finite
  sums, the deleted scalar Perron convention, the listed Adams coefficients,
  and the rational tails \(B_\varepsilon\) and \(B_{\mathrm{std}}\).  The denominator-cleared
  inequalities in that lemma place the resulting rational intervals in the two
  fixed-label output windows.

  Finally, the fixed-label branch and deletion data in the last two rows are
  precisely the legal branch--tail record of
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-branch-tail-record}: the singular scalar triple is not
  evaluated, every remaining boundary term has the Abel tag, and every
  \(rm\ge2\) term is an interior determinant logarithm.  Applying
  \Cref{cor:main-iii-branch-tail-enclosure} then propagates these two verified
  fixed-label windows through the finite Hermitian Fourier transform to the
  product constants of Main Theorem~III.  Hence the table is a reusable finite
  exact-arithmetic check for the displayed \(S_3\) example.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The table specifies a finite list of rational logarithm arguments, rational
  coefficients, branch tags, and rational tail radii.  For each listed rational
  argument \(q>1\), \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-sharp-certificates} proves the
  rational enclosure \(L_3(q)<\log q<U_3(q)\).  Multiplication by a positive
  rational coefficient preserves the order of the endpoints, and multiplication
  by a negative rational coefficient reverses it.  Thus the endpoint-selection
  rule in the table gives rational lower and upper endpoints for each of the
  four finite truncations.

  For \(\Pi_\varepsilon(1/2)\) and \(\Pi_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)\), the tails are exactly the
  rational radii in
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-epsilon-tail-certificate} and
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-rho-tail-certificate}.  For
  \(F_\varepsilon(1/2)\) and \(F_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)\), the tails and the deleted scalar
  Perron convention are exactly those proved in
  \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-f-level-certificates}.  Enlarging the four finite
  rational intervals by those printed rational radii therefore contains the
  exact four target values.  The final comparisons with the displayed output
  windows are rational inequalities; clearing positive denominators gives the
  positive margins printed in
  \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-sharp-certificates,lem:appendix-s3-f-level-certificates}.
  Hence the finite table deterministically replays the four certified windows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact rational bounds for the \(S_3\)-channels]
  \label{lem:appendix-s3-sharp-certificates}
  Define
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:s3-example-epsilon-certificate-sum}
    \Pi_\varepsilon(1/2)=E_7+\Theta_\varepsilon,
  \end{equation}
  where
  \[
    E_7
    =
    -\frac12\log 2
    +\frac16\log\frac{32}{31}
    +\frac1{10}\log\frac{512}{511}
    +\frac1{14}\log\frac{8192}{8191},
  \]
  and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:s3-example-epsilon-tail-certificate}
    \abs{\Theta_\varepsilon}\le \frac{1}{1105920}.
  \end{equation}
  Also define
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:s3-example-rho-certificate-sum}
    \Pi_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)=R_{15}+\Theta_{\mathrm{std}},
  \end{equation}
  where
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    R_{15}
    ={}&
    -\log\frac32
    -\frac12\log\frac85
    -\frac13\log\frac43
    +\frac15\log\frac{33}{32}
    +\frac13\log\frac{32}{31}
    +\frac17\log\frac{129}{128} \\
    &+\frac1{10}\log\frac{512\cdot 1024}{511\cdot 1025}
    +\frac1{11}\log\frac{2049}{2048}
    +\frac1{13}\log\frac{8193}{8192} \\
    &+\frac1{14}\log\frac{8192\cdot 16384}{8191\cdot 16385}
    +\frac1{15}\log\frac{16384}{16383},
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:s3-example-rho-tail-certificate}
    \abs{\Theta_{\mathrm{std}}}\le \frac{9}{524288}.
  \end{equation}
  Consequently,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:s3-example-certified-intervals-sharp}
    -\frac{341079}{10^6}-\frac{1}{1105920}
    <\Pi_\varepsilon(1/2)<
    -\frac{341071}{10^6}+\frac{1}{1105920},
  \end{equation}
  and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:s3-example-certified-intervals-sharp-rho}
    -\frac{718352}{10^6}-\frac{9}{524288}
    <\Pi_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)<
    -\frac{718351}{10^6}+\frac{9}{524288}.
  \end{equation}
  Hence the coarse windows of
  \Cref{lem:s3-example-perron-certificates} follow.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The expression for \(E_7\) is obtained from
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-pi-eps} by retaining the odd indices
  \(r=1,3,5,7\). For the tail, put
  \(x_r=2^{1-2r}\). Since \(r\ge 9\) implies \(x_r\le 2^{-17}\),
  \[
    -\log(1-x_r)\le \frac{x_r}{1-x_r}\le 2x_r.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    \abs{\Theta_\varepsilon}
    \le
    \sum_{\substack{r\ge 9\\ r\ \mathrm{odd}}}\frac{2^{1-2r}}{r}
    \le
    \frac19\sum_{j\ge 0}2^{1-2(9+2j)}
    =
    \frac{1}{1105920}.
  \]

  The expression for \(R_{15}\) is obtained from
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-pi-rho} by retaining the squarefree indices
  \(m\le 15\). For \(m\ge 16\), set \(y_m=2^{1-m}\). Then
  \(y_m\le 2^{-15}\), and
  \[
    \log(1-y_m)^{-1}\le 2y_m,
    \qquad
    \log(1+2^{-m})\le 2^{-m}.
  \]
  Since \(0\le \alpha(m)\le 2\) and \(0\le \beta(m)\le 1\), this gives
  \[
    \abs{\Theta_{\mathrm{std}}}
    \le
    \sum_{m\ge 16}\frac{9\,2^{-m}}{m}
    \le
    \frac{9}{16}\sum_{m\ge 16}2^{-m}
    =
    \frac{9}{524288}.
  \]

  For each rational \(q>1\) appearing in \(E_7\) or \(R_{15}\), put
  \[
    u_q:=\frac{q-1}{q+1},
    \qquad
    L_3(q):=2\sum_{j=0}^{3}\frac{u_q^{2j+1}}{2j+1},
  \]
  and
  \[
    U_3(q):=L_3(q)+\frac{2u_q^9}{9(1-u_q^2)}.
  \]
  Since
  \[
    \log q
    =
    2\sum_{j\ge 0}\frac{u_q^{2j+1}}{2j+1}
    =
    L_3(q)+2\sum_{j\ge 4}\frac{u_q^{2j+1}}{2j+1},
  \]
  the tail is positive and satisfies
  \[
    0<\log q-L_3(q)
    <
    \frac{2u_q^9}{9}\sum_{j\ge 0}u_q^{2j}
    =
    \frac{2u_q^9}{9(1-u_q^2)}.
  \]
  Therefore
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:s3-log-lower-upper-bounds}
    L_3(q)<\log q<U_3(q).
  \end{equation}
  Equivalently, for every real coefficient \(c\),
  \[
    c>0:\qquad cL_3(q)<c\log q<cU_3(q),
  \]
  and
  \[
    c<0:\qquad cU_3(q)<c\log q<cL_3(q).
  \]
  Applying these inequalities termwise gives exact rational endpoints
  \begin{align*}
    E_7^-
    :={}&
    -\frac12\,U_3(2)
    +\frac16\,L_3(32/31)
    +\frac1{10}\,L_3(512/511) \\
    &+\frac1{14}\,L_3(8192/8191), \\
    E_7^+
    :={}&
    -\frac12\,L_3(2)
    +\frac16\,U_3(32/31)
    +\frac1{10}\,U_3(512/511) \\
    &+\frac1{14}\,U_3(8192/8191),
  \end{align*}
  and
  \begin{align*}
    R_{15}^-
    :={}&
    -U_3(3/2)
    -\frac12\,U_3(8/5)
    -\frac13\,U_3(4/3)
    +\frac15\,L_3(33/32)
    +\frac13\,L_3(32/31) \\
    &+\frac17\,L_3(129/128)
    +\frac1{10}\,L_3(524288/523775)
    +\frac1{11}\,L_3(2049/2048) \\
    &+\frac1{13}\,L_3(8193/8192)
    +\frac1{14}\,L_3(134217728/134209535) \\
    &+\frac1{15}\,L_3(16384/16383), \\
    R_{15}^+
    :={}&
    -L_3(3/2)
    -\frac12\,L_3(8/5)
    -\frac13\,L_3(4/3)
    +\frac15\,U_3(33/32)
    +\frac13\,U_3(32/31) \\
    &+\frac17\,U_3(129/128)
    +\frac1{10}\,U_3(524288/523775)
    +\frac1{11}\,U_3(2049/2048) \\
    &+\frac1{13}\,U_3(8193/8192)
    +\frac1{14}\,U_3(134217728/134209535) \\
    &+\frac1{15}\,U_3(16384/16383).
  \end{align*}
  Then \(E_7^-<E_7<E_7^+\) and \(R_{15}^-<R_{15}<R_{15}^+\). Since
  every \(L_3(q)\) and \(U_3(q)\) is rational, the displayed formulas
  are an exact rational interval verification. Expanding them and
  subtracting the claimed decimal rational endpoints gives positive
  rational margins. Clearing the positive denominators in the rational
  formulas for \(E_7^\pm\) and \(R_{15}^\pm\) gives the following
  exact lower bounds for those margins:
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|c|c}
    \text{comparison} & \text{exact positive margin} & \text{rational lower bound} \\
    \hline
    E_7^-+\frac{341079}{10^6} & >0 & >\frac{94363}{10^{11}}\\
    -\frac{341071}{10^6}-E_7^+ & >0 & >\frac{35281}{5\cdot10^{10}}\\
    R_{15}^-+\frac{718352}{10^6} & >0 & >\frac{2693}{25\cdot10^{9}}\\
    -\frac{718351}{10^6}-R_{15}^+ & >0 & >\frac{1}{2\cdot10^{12}}.
  \end{array}
  \]
  The rightmost column is only a compact way to print the exact
  integer comparisons after denominators have been cleared; no decimal
  approximation is used in the proof of the signs.  Explicitly, each entry in
  the middle column is the difference of two rational numbers obtained by
  substituting the printed \(L_3/U_3\) endpoints into the displayed finite
  logarithmic sum; multiplying by the least common positive denominator gives
  the positive integer certified by the lower bound in the rightmost column.
  Therefore
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:s3-example-finite-sum-windows}
    \begin{aligned}
      -\frac{341079}{10^6}
      &<E_7^-<E_7<E_7^+<-\frac{341071}{10^6}, \\
      -\frac{718352}{10^6}
      &<R_{15}^-<R_{15}<R_{15}^+<-\frac{718351}{10^6}.
    \end{aligned}
  \end{equation}
  In particular, still by exact rational arithmetic,
  \[
    E_7^+ +\frac1{1105920}<-\frac{341}{1000},
    \qquad
    R_{15}^+ +\frac9{524288}<-\frac{718}{1000},
  \]
  while
  \[
    -\frac{342}{1000}<E_7^- -\frac1{1105920},
    \qquad
    -\frac{719}{1000}<R_{15}^- -\frac9{524288}.
  \]
  Combining these finite-sum bounds with
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-epsilon-tail-certificate} and
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-rho-tail-certificate} gives
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-certified-intervals-sharp} and
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-certified-intervals-sharp-rho}. These sharp
  intervals lie respectively in
  \[
    \left(-\frac{342}{1000},-\frac{341}{1000}\right)
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \left(-\frac{719}{1000},-\frac{718}{1000}\right),
  \]
  which is exactly the certification quoted in
  \Cref{lem:s3-example-perron-certificates}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact rational bounds for the \(S_3\) product-level fixed-label values]\label{lem:appendix-s3-f-level-certificates}
  In the \(S_3\)-example of \Cref{sec:shadows}, the product-level
  obstruction coordinates in \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens} are
  verified by the printed rational inequalities below.  The finite record
  consists only of rational logarithm brackets, rational coefficients, rational
  tail bounds, and the final cleared-denominator comparisons.  In particular,
  these are windows for the Adams-corrected fixed-label Euler transform
  entering \Cref{thm:main-iii-certificate-chain}, not windows for the periodic
  Artin logarithm: the coefficients below come from the finite power maps
  \(g\mapsto g^m\), the Adams tensors, and the deletion of the singular scalar
  Perron triple before the boundary value is read.  Thus the rational
  inequalities certify the finite-state mechanism that creates the product
  obstruction, rather than a numerical approximation to a classical leading
  Mertens term.  In particular,
  the following interval inclusions are verified inside the article:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:s3-f-epsilon-appendix-window}
    -\frac{381}{1000}<F_\varepsilon(1/2)<-\frac{380}{1000},
  \end{equation}
  and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:s3-f-rho-appendix-window}
    -\frac{923}{1000}<F_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)<-\frac{922}{1000}.
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  For rational \(q>1\), we use the same rational logarithm bounds as in
  \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-sharp-certificates}:
  \[
    L_3(q)<\log q<U_3(q),
  \]
  where
  \[
    L_3(q)=2\sum_{j=0}^{3}\frac{u_q^{2j+1}}{2j+1},
    \qquad
    U_3(q)=L_3(q)+\frac{2u_q^9}{9(1-u_q^2)},
    \qquad
    u_q=\frac{q-1}{q+1}.
  \]
  Thus all endpoints below are rational numbers.

  From \eqref{eq:s3-example-pi-eps},
  \[
    F_\varepsilon(1/2)
    =-\frac12\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{1}{2^j}
      \log\left(1-2^{1-2^{j+1}}\right)^{-1}.
  \]
  Let
  \[
    Q_8:=-\frac12\log2-\frac14\log\frac87
      -\frac18\log\frac{128}{127}
      -\frac1{16}\log\frac{32768}{32767}.
  \]
  The remaining terms are negative, and, since
  \(-\log(1-x)\le2x\) for \(0<x\le2^{-31}\), their absolute value is at
  most
  \[
    B_\varepsilon:=\frac{1}{16}\,2^{-31}\frac{1}{1-2^{-32}}.
  \]
  Termwise substitution of \(L_3\) and \(U_3\), with inequalities reversed
  on negative coefficients, gives rational numbers
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    Q_8^- &:=-\frac12U_3(2)-\frac14U_3(8/7)
      -\frac18U_3(128/127)-\frac1{16}U_3(32768/32767),\\
    Q_8^+ &:=-\frac12L_3(2)-\frac14L_3(8/7)
      -\frac18L_3(128/127)-\frac1{16}L_3(32768/32767),
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  with
  \[
    Q_8^- - B_\varepsilon < F_\varepsilon(1/2)<Q_8^+.
  \]
  The rational endpoint values used here are
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|c}
    \text{endpoint} & \text{exact rational value}\\
    \hline
    Q_8^- &
    -\dfrac{77669633184570530219883191591202892933297454719}
      {203890011879382796622109825339358911119360000000}\\[1ex]
    Q_8^+ &
    -\dfrac{13843924721083839036686064834355526}
      {36342196007488170547184558429765625}\\[1ex]
    B_\varepsilon & \dfrac{1}{34359738360}.
  \end{array}
  \]
  Clearing denominators in these rational expressions gives the positive
  margins
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|c|c}
    \text{comparison} & \text{exact positive margin} &
      \text{rational lower bound}\\
    \hline
    Q_8^- -B_\varepsilon +\frac{381}{1000} & >0 & >\frac{1}{10^6}\\
    -\frac{380}{1000}-Q_8^+ & >0 & >\frac{9}{10^4}.
  \end{array}
  \]
  Hence
  \[
    -\frac{381}{1000}<Q_8^- - B_\varepsilon,
    \qquad
    Q_8^+<-\frac{380}{1000},
  \]
  proving \eqref{eq:s3-f-epsilon-appendix-window}.

  For the standard representation, combine
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-pi-rho} with the fixed-label transform. Grouping by
  \(k=rm\) gives
  \[
    F_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)=
    \sum_{k\ge1}\frac{1}{k}
      \left(
        A_k\log(1-2^{1-k})^{-1}
        -B_k\log(1+2^{-k})
      \right),
  \]
  where the first logarithm is absent for \(k=1\), and
  \[
    A_k:=\sum_{m\mid k}\mu(m)\alpha(m),
    \qquad
    B_k:=\sum_{m\mid k}\mu(m)\beta(m).
  \]
  The only non-zero pairs with \(k\le32\) are
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|ccccccccc}
    k&1&2&3&4&8&9&16&27&32\\ \hline
    A_k&0&-1&-1&-1&-1&-1&-1&-1&-1\\
    B_k&1&0&1&0&0&1&0&1&0.
  \end{array}
  \]
  Let \(S_{32}\) be the corresponding finite sum. For \(k\ge33\), the
  elementary bounds \(\abs{A_k}\le2\), \(\abs{B_k}\le1\),
  \(\log(1-2^{1-k})^{-1}\le2^{2-k}\), and
  \(\log(1+2^{-k})\le2^{-k}\) give the rational tail bound
  \[
    B_{\mathrm{std}}:=\frac{5}{33}\,2^{-32}.
  \]
  Applying the same \(L_3/U_3\) interval substitution to the finitely many
  logarithms in \(S_{32}\) gives the rational endpoints
  \(S_{32}^-<S_{32}<S_{32}^+\)
  and hence
  \[
    S_{32}^- -B_{\mathrm{std}} < F_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)<S_{32}^+ +B_{\mathrm{std}}.
  \]
  The verification is completely rational at this point.  For reference,
  the finitely many logarithmic arguments entering \(S_{32}\) are
  \[
  \begin{array}{ccc}
    3/2, & 2, & 4/3,\\
    9/8, & 8/7, & 128/127,\\
    256/255, & 513/512, & 32768/32767,\\
    67108864/67108863, & 134217729/134217728, &
    2147483648/2147483647,
  \end{array}
  \]
  with coefficients respectively
  \[
    -1,
    -\frac12,
    -\frac13,
    -\frac13,
    -\frac14,
    -\frac18,
    -\frac19,
    -\frac19,
    -\frac1{16},
    -\frac1{27},
    -\frac1{27},
    -\frac1{32}.
  \]
  These displayed rational bounds are the rational check used here: for
  each term one inserts the lower or upper endpoint according to the sign of
  the rational coefficient, sums the resulting rational numbers, and then
  compares the final rational endpoints with the rational thresholds stated
  below.  No decimal approximation is used, and the main text depends only on
  the displayed rational endpoints, coefficients, and inequalities.
  Substituting \(L_3\) or \(U_3\) according to the sign of these
  coefficients and adding the rational tail \(B_{\mathrm{std}}\), denominator
  clearing gives the explicit positive checks
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    10^6\left(S_{32}^- -B_{\mathrm{std}} +\frac{923}{1000}\right) &> 789,\\
    10^6\left(-\frac{922}{1000}-(S_{32}^+ +B_{\mathrm{std}})\right) &> 204.
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  Equivalently, the two margins are larger than \(789/10^6\) and
  \(204/10^6\). They are obtained by clearing the positive denominators in
  the displayed rational logarithmic arguments: after every signed coefficient
  has selected the appropriate \(L_3\) or \(U_3\) endpoint, the two final
  comparisons are differences of rational numbers, and multiplying by their
  common positive denominators gives positive integers.  Thus the interval
  check is contained in these rational inequalities. The shorter numerical
  consequences are
  \[
  \begin{array}{c|c|c}
    \text{comparison} & \text{exact positive margin} &
      \text{rational lower bound}\\
    \hline
    S_{32}^- -B_{\mathrm{std}} +\frac{923}{1000} & >0 & >\frac{7}{10^4}\\
    -\frac{922}{1000}-(S_{32}^+ +B_{\mathrm{std}}) & >0 & >\frac{2}{10^4}.
  \end{array}
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    -\frac{923}{1000}<S_{32}^- -B_{\mathrm{std}},
    \qquad
    S_{32}^+ +B_{\mathrm{std}}<-\frac{922}{1000},
  \]
  proving \eqref{eq:s3-f-rho-appendix-window}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Displayed rational interval windows]
  \label{cor:s3-displayed-rational-certificate-windows}
  The rational logarithm brackets, finite truncation levels, and tail bounds
  printed in \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-sharp-certificates,lem:appendix-s3-f-level-certificates}
  imply exactly the four coarse windows used in the main text:
  \[
    -\frac{342}{1000}<\Pi_\varepsilon(1/2)<-\frac{341}{1000},
    \qquad
    -\frac{719}{1000}<\Pi_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)<-\frac{718}{1000},
  \]
  and
  \[
    -\frac{381}{1000}<F_\varepsilon(1/2)<-\frac{380}{1000},
    \qquad
    -\frac{923}{1000}<F_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)<-\frac{922}{1000}.
  \]
  In particular the \(S_3\) example gives an exact rational verification of
  the primitive-channel windows \(\Pi_\varepsilon,\Pi_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}\) and for the
  Adams-corrected fixed-label product windows \(F_\varepsilon,F_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}\), not an
  undocumented floating-point approximation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  For \(\Pi_\varepsilon(1/2)\),
  \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-sharp-certificates} gives
  \[
    -\frac{341079}{10^6}-\frac{1}{1105920}
    <\Pi_\varepsilon(1/2)<
    -\frac{341071}{10^6}+\frac{1}{1105920}.
  \]
  The left endpoint is greater than \(-342/1000\) because
  \[
    \frac{342}{1000}-\frac{341079}{10^6}-\frac{1}{1105920}>0,
  \]
  and the right endpoint is less than \(-341/1000\) because
  \[
    \frac{341071}{10^6}-\frac{1}{1105920}-\frac{341}{1000}>0.
  \]
  The same lemma gives
  \[
    -\frac{718352}{10^6}-\frac{9}{524288}
    <\Pi_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)<
    -\frac{718351}{10^6}+\frac{9}{524288},
  \]
  and the rational inequalities
  \[
    \frac{719}{1000}-\frac{718352}{10^6}-\frac{9}{524288}>0,
    \qquad
    \frac{718351}{10^6}-\frac{9}{524288}-\frac{718}{1000}>0
  \]
  place this interval inside
  \((-719/1000,-718/1000)\).

  For the fixed-label coordinates,
  \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-f-level-certificates} proves directly, by the printed
  denominator-cleared comparisons, that
  \[
    -\frac{381}{1000}<Q_8^- -B_\varepsilon
    <F_\varepsilon(1/2)<Q_8^+<-\frac{380}{1000}
  \]
  and
  \[
    -\frac{923}{1000}<S_{32}^- -B_{\mathrm{std}}
    <F_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)<S_{32}^+ +B_{\mathrm{std}}<-\frac{922}{1000}.
  \]
  These implications use only the displayed rational endpoints
  \(Q_8^\pm,S_{32}^\pm\), the printed rational tails
  \(B_\varepsilon,B_{\mathrm{std}}\), and the stated positive rational margins.  Hence all
  four main-text windows follow from exact rational interval arithmetic.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite exact-arithmetic replay.}
  The mathematical verification in this appendix is the displayed rational
  arithmetic in
  \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-sharp-certificates,lem:appendix-s3-f-level-certificates}.
  The following finite rational procedure reproduces the four displayed
  certificate windows without floating-point input: compute every rational
  endpoint \(L_3(q)\le\log q\le U_3(q)\) for the rational logarithm arguments
  occurring in \(E_7\), \(R_{15}\), \(Q_8\), and \(S_{32}\); select the lower
  endpoint for each positive signed coefficient and the upper endpoint for each
  negative signed coefficient when forming a lower bound, with the opposite
  selections for the upper bound; add the rational tails
  \(1/1105920\), \(9/524288\), \(B_\varepsilon\), and \(B_{\mathrm{std}}\); and clear
  positive denominators in the four final comparisons with the printed
  rational windows.  This procedure verifies exactly the inclusions in
  \Cref{cor:s3-displayed-rational-certificate-windows}.
  For every rational argument \(q>1\),
  \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-sharp-certificates} proves
  \(L_3(q)<\log q<U_3(q)\) by the positive remainder of the series for
  \(2\operatorname{arctanh}((q-1)/(q+1))\).  Therefore multiplication by a
  positive rational coefficient preserves the order of the interval and
  multiplication by a negative rational coefficient reverses it.  Applying this
  rule term-by-term to the finite signed sums defining
  \(E_7\), \(R_{15}\), \(Q_8\), and \(S_{32}\) gives rational lower and upper
  endpoints for those four finite sums.  The remaining primitive and
  fixed-label tails are the rational quantities displayed in
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-epsilon-tail-certificate},
  \eqref{eq:s3-example-rho-tail-certificate}, and in the definitions of
  \(B_\varepsilon\) and \(B_{\mathrm{std}}\) in
  \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-f-level-certificates}.  Adding these rational tails
  enlarges the four finite-sum intervals by rational amounts and hence still
  gives rational intervals.

  The last step is purely algebraic.  Each assertion that one of these rational
  intervals lies in its printed window is an inequality between rational
  numbers; multiplying by the product of the positive denominators is an
  equivalent integer comparison.  The required positive margins are displayed in
  the proofs of
  \Cref{lem:appendix-s3-sharp-certificates,lem:appendix-s3-f-level-certificates}
  for \(\Pi_\varepsilon(1/2)\), \(\Pi_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)\),
  \(F_\varepsilon(1/2)\), and \(F_{\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}}(1/2)\).  Consequently the finite rational
  procedure recovers exactly the four windows collected in
  \Cref{cor:s3-displayed-rational-certificate-windows}.
