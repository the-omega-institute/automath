\subsubsection{Determinant evaluation of fixed-label Perron values}
\label{subsubsec:determinant-only-fixed-label-values}

This is the determinant-evaluation part of Main Theorem~III.  The preceding
product blocks reduce the product constants to the fixed-label numbers
\(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\); this block evaluates those numbers from the same
finite twisted determinant data used in Main Theorem~I.  The branch convention
is the one announced in the subsection overview and made precise in the first
lemma below: Abel continuation for the regular non-trivial boundary terms,
ordinary interior-disc logarithms for \(rm\ge2\), and deletion of the unique
singular scalar Perron triple.

Throughout this subsubsection the following convention is in force.  The
symbol \(\Log\det(I-zA_\pi)^{-1}\) denotes the normalized logarithmic germ
equal to \(0\) at \(z=0\), continued only along the Abel segment that is
explicitly present in the formula.  For \(rm=1\) and \(\pi\ne\mathbf1\), the
strict gap \(\rho(A_\pi)<\lambda\) makes this continuation regular at
\(z=\lambda^{-1}\).  For \(rm\ge2\), the point
\(z=\lambda^{-rm}\) lies strictly inside the scalar Perron disc and the
ordinary convergent power-series logarithm is used.  The scalar Perron value
\(\Log\det(I-\lambda^{-1}A_{\mathbf1})^{-1}\) is never assigned a finite part
in a non-trivial fixed-label coordinate; the corresponding triple
\((1,1,\mathbf1)\) has coefficient zero by character orthogonality and is
deleted before the boundary limit is taken.

\begin{lemma}[Singular-triple deletion and branch convention]
  \label{lem:fixed-label-singular-triple-deletion}
  Assume the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit}, and fix a
  non-trivial irreducible representation \(\varrho\in\Irr(G)\).  For
  \(0<t<1\), the Abel-regularized fixed-label determinant expression is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-label-singular-deletion-abel}
    F_\varrho(t\lambda^{-1})
    =
    \sum_{r,m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
      \sum_{\substack{\pi\in\Irr(G)\\(r,m,\pi)\neq(1,1,\mathbf1)}}
        a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        \Log\det(I-t^{rm}\lambda^{-rm}A_\pi)^{-1},
  \end{equation}
  where every logarithm is the normalized germ at the origin.  In the limit
  \(t\uparrow1\), the deleted triple \((r,m,\pi)=(1,1,\mathbf1)\) is the
  only scalar Perron singularity. Its coefficient is
  \(a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}=0\), so the singular logarithm is absent
  before the boundary limit is taken.  All remaining \(rm=1\) terms are
  non-trivial twisted branches regular at \(z=\lambda^{-1}\), and all
  \(rm\ge2\) terms, including scalar terms arising from Adams powers, are
  ordinary logarithms inside the Perron disc.  The deleted-series convention
  therefore has a unique Abel limit, and the convergence to that limit is
  justified by an absolute majorant independent of \(t\in(0,1)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  For \(0<t<1\), both the fixed-label Euler transform and every determinant
  logarithm are evaluated inside their discs of convergence. Substituting
  \eqref{eq:class-logarithm-determinant-linearisation} into
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-euler-transform-adams-mobius} gives the same
  formula as \eqref{eq:fixed-label-singular-deletion-abel} with the scalar
  triple included.  Its coefficient is
  \[
    a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}
    =\langle\psi^1\chi_\varrho,\mathbf1\rangle_G
    =\langle\chi_\varrho,\mathbf1\rangle_G=0,
  \]
  by finite-character orthogonality, because \(\varrho\neq\mathbf1\).
  Thus deleting that summand changes no Abel value for \(0<t<1\), and no
  finite part is assigned to
  \(\Log\det(I-\lambda^{-1}A_{\mathbf1})^{-1}\).
  This also proves that there is no hidden singular branch left to choose:
  the only triple for which the determinant has a Perron zero at the Abel
  endpoint has zero Adams coefficient in the non-trivial fixed-label
  coordinate and has been removed before the limit is formed.  Scalar terms
  with \(rm\ge2\) are different triples and lie strictly inside the Perron
  disc, so they are retained with the ordinary power-series logarithm.

  If \(rm=1\) and \(\pi\neq\mathbf1\), then \(r=m=1\) and the strict twisted
  spectral gap gives \(\rho(A_\pi)<\lambda\).  The finite set of non-trivial
  irreducibles therefore has a common zero-free neighbourhood of the closed
  Abel segment \(\{\theta\lambda^{-1}:0\le\theta\le1\}\), so each normalized
  logarithmic germ has a finite Abel-path boundary value. If \(rm\ge2\), then
  for every \(\pi\in\Irr(G)\),
  \[
    \rho(\lambda^{-rm}A_\pi)
    \le \lambda^{1-rm}<1,
  \]
  so the normalized logarithm is the ordinary value of the convergent
  determinant power series at \(\lambda^{-rm}\).  The double tail after the
  finitely many \(rm=1\) terms is dominated by
  \Cref{lem:fixed-label-perron-double-majorant}, applied with
  \(\phi=\chi_\varrho\): there is a constant \(C_\varrho\) such that, uniformly
  in \(0<t<1\), the absolute value of the non-deleted tail at level \((r,m)\)
  with \(rm\ge2\) is bounded by
  \[
    C_\varrho\frac{\lambda^{1-rm}}{rm}.
  \]
  Since
  \(\sum_{r,m\ge1,\,rm\ge2}\lambda^{1-rm}/(rm)
    =\sum_{k\ge2}\mathrm d(k)\lambda^{1-k}/k<\infty\), this is a summable
  majorant independent of \(t\).  Therefore the Abel limit may be passed
  through the non-deleted determinant series, and the resulting value has
  exactly the branch convention stated above.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Determinant formula for fixed-label Perron values]
  \label{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}
  Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit}, namely
  \(\lambda>1\) and \(\eta<\lambda\), every
  non-trivial \(\varrho\in\Irr(G)\) satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-label-determinant-perron}
    F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
    =
    \sum_{r,m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
      \sum_{\substack{\pi\in\Irr(G)\\(r,m,\pi)\neq(1,1,\mathbf1)}}
        a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        \Log\det(I-\lambda^{-rm}A_\pi)^{-1}.
  \end{equation}
  The branch convention is local to this proposition. For each displayed
  determinant, start with the analytic germ
  \(\Log\det(I-zA_\pi)^{-1}=0\) at \(z=0\).  If \(rm=1\) and
  \(\pi\neq\mathbf1\), its value is the Abel-path continuation to
  \(z=\lambda^{-1}\) along \(z=t\lambda^{-1}\),
  \(0\le t\uparrow1\), through the zero-free region supplied by the
  twisted spectral gap.  If \(rm\ge2\), the value is the ordinary value of
  the same normalized germ at \(z=\lambda^{-rm}\), where
  \(\rho(\lambda^{-rm}A_\pi)<1\).
  If \(\varrho\neq\mathbf1\), an Adams power \(\psi^m\chi_\varrho\) may
  contain the trivial character for \(m\ge2\).  The corresponding scalar
  determinant terms in \eqref{eq:fixed-label-determinant-perron} are part
  of the formula whenever \(rm\ge2\); they are regular ordinary
  determinant logarithms because \(\lambda^{-rm}A_{\mathbf1}\) is evaluated
  strictly inside the Perron disc.
  For non-real irreducible representations the coefficients
  \(a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}\) and the determinant terms may be complex.  No
  termwise realness is asserted; real class constants result from pairing
  conjugate irreducibles in the Hermitian class expansion.
  The summation sign is a local exclusion: the scalar boundary term
  \((r,m,\pi)=(1,1,\mathbf1)\), and only that term, is not part of the
  expression at \(z=\lambda^{-1}\). It would be
  \(a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}
  \Log\det(I-\lambda^{-1}A_{\mathbf1})^{-1}\), but
  \(a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}=0\), so no scalar Perron logarithm occurs
  in the non-trivial fixed-label value.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  For \(0<t<1\), the open-disc identity
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-singular-deletion-abel} gives
  \[
    F_\varrho(t\lambda^{-1})
    =
    \sum_{r,m\ge1}\frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
      \sum_{\substack{\pi\in\Irr(G)\\(r,m,\pi)\neq(1,1,\mathbf1)}}
        a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        \Log\det(I-t^{rm}\lambda^{-rm}A_\pi)^{-1}.
  \]
  The exclusion is already made in the open disc.  The only possible Perron
  singularity at the endpoint is the scalar triple
  \((r,m,\pi)=(1,1,\mathbf1)\), and its coefficient is
  \(a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}=0\) by non-trivial-character orthogonality.
  Thus no finite part, branch, or cancellation is assigned to
  \(\Log\det(I-\lambda^{-1}A_{\mathbf1})^{-1}\); the term is absent before
  the Abel limit is formed.  Scalar terms with \(rm\ge2\) are different
  triples and remain in the formula, because
  \(\rho(\lambda^{-rm}A_{\mathbf1})<1\).

  For the remaining boundary terms with \(rm=1\), the strict twisted gap gives
  \(\rho(A_\pi)<\lambda\) for \(\pi\ne\mathbf1\), so their normalized logarithms
  have ordinary Abel limits along \(t\lambda^{-1}\).  For every term with
  \(rm\ge2\), the point \(\lambda^{-rm}A_\pi\) is inside the Perron disc.  The
  local majorant in \Cref{lem:fixed-label-singular-triple-deletion} bounds
  the whole \(rm\ge2\) tail by the summable series
  \(C_\varrho\sum_{rm\ge2}\lambda^{1-rm}/(rm)\), independently of \(t\).
  Dominated convergence therefore permits termwise passage to
  \(t\uparrow1\) in the non-deleted series.  The left-hand side tends to
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) by the Perron-point convergence statement in
  \Cref{thm:fixed-label-euler-transform}.  This gives
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-determinant-perron} with exactly the displayed local
  exclusion and branch choices.
\end{proof}

The determinant form of Main Theorem~III uses one fixed normalization.  The
sign of \(K_C\) is the outer minus sign in
\eqref{eq:class-euler-product-k-f}, equivalently the positive exponential of
the negative logarithmic constant in
\eqref{eq:class-product-constant-compatibility}.  Class indicators use the
coefficient \((|C|/|G|)\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)\), with inverse Fourier recovery
performed by summing against \(\chi_\sigma(C)\).  In
\(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), the single scalar boundary triple
\((r,m,\pi)=(1,1,\mathbf1)\) is omitted before evaluation because its
coefficient is zero for \(\varrho\ne\mathbf1\).  Non-trivial \(rm=1\)
determinants use the Abel boundary value of the normalized germ at the origin;
all \(rm\ge2\) determinants use the ordinary interior-disc value.  These
conventions are exactly those proved in
\Cref{lem:class-product-sign-normalisation-conventions,lem:class-product-hermitian-normalisation,lem:fixed-label-singular-triple-deletion,prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}, and the absolute majorant in
\Cref{lem:fixed-label-singular-triple-deletion} justifies the displayed order
of summation and Abel limiting.

\begin{corollary}[Convention compatibility for Main Theorem III]\label{cor:main-iii-convention-compatibility}
  In Main Theorem~III, the following product-constant conventions are forced
  and mutually compatible.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
    \item The class-indicator coefficient of a non-trivial irreducible
    character is \((|C|/|G|)\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)\), where
    \(\chi_\varrho^\ast(C)=\overline{\chi_\varrho(C)}\).
    \item The product constant uses the single outer minus sign in
    \eqref{eq:class-euler-product-k-f}, with scalar base
    \(\gamma+\log C(A)\).
    \item The determinant formula for
    \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), \(\varrho\ne\mathbf1\), deletes only the
    scalar Perron triple \((r,m,\pi)=(1,1,\mathbf1)\) before evaluation;
    non-trivial boundary terms use the Abel branch from the germ at the
    origin and all \(rm\ge2\) terms use ordinary interior-disc values.
    \item With these choices, the character formula for every \(K_C\) is the
    positive real Euler-product constant of \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens},
    and the scalar-normalised product vector is Fourier-equivalent to the
    non-trivial fixed-label Perron vector.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Item~\textup{(i)} is exactly the Hermitian class-indicator normalisation of
  \Cref{lem:class-product-hermitian-normalisation}.  Item~\textup{(ii)} is
  \Cref{lem:class-product-sign-normalisation-conventions}.  Item~\textup{(iii)}
  combines the scalar-triple deletion rule of
  \Cref{lem:fixed-label-singular-triple-deletion} with the determinant
  evaluation and branch statement in \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}.
  The real positive branch in item~\textup{(iv)} is
  \Cref{lem:class-product-positive-real-branch}, while the Fourier equivalence
  is \Cref{prop:class-product-comparison,thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity,thm:main-iii-certificate-chain}.
  Hence all convention choices used by Main Theorem~III are consequences of
  previously proved finite-state statements and cannot be varied independently.
  Conversely, equality of the two positive product-constant formulas is
  equality of their real logarithms; subtracting the scalar normalisation
  yields the same fixed-label coordinate identity.  Thus the compatibility
  statement fixes both the sign and the global \(|C|/|G|\) factor used in Main
  Theorem~III, rather than adding an independent normalisation choice.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite determinant enclosure for fixed-label Perron constants]
  \label{prop:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm}
  Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit}, namely
  \(\lambda>1\) and \(\eta<\lambda\), fix a
  non-trivial irreducible representation \(\varrho\in\Irr(G)\) and a tolerance
  \(\epsilon>0\).  There is a deterministic finite enclosure, computed only
  from the twisted determinant polynomials and finite character data in
  \textup{(a)}, which returns an explicit finite determinant sum \(F_\varrho^{(M)}\) and
  a verified radius \(E_M\le\epsilon\) such that
  \[
    F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
    \in F_\varrho^{(M)}+\{w\in\CC:|w|\le E_M\}.
  \]
  The enclosure is canonical up to the requested numerical radii: two finite
  records with the same determinant polynomials, class-power maps, character
  table, Perron root, branch tags, and tail bound have intersecting verified
  discs containing the same Perron value.  Consequently every
  Frobenius-class product constant in Main Theorem~III is a limit-free finite
  determinant consequence of the twisted matrices, the scalar normalisation
  \(C(A)\), and the Abel branch; no primitive-orbit enumeration or limiting
  orbit count is an input.  The enclosure is constructed as follows.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*]
    \item \emph{Input.}  The determinant inputs are finite: the character table of \(G\),
      the class-power maps \(C\mapsto C^m\) on conjugacy classes, the finite
      determinant polynomials \(D_\pi(z):=\det(I-zA_\pi)\),
      \(\pi\in\Irr(G)\), the Perron root \(\lambda>1\), the twisted
      spectral-gap inequality \(\eta<\lambda\), and an explicit majorant
      constant \(C_\varrho\) furnished by
      \Cref{lem:fixed-label-perron-double-majorant} for
      \(\phi=\chi_\varrho\).
    \item \emph{Finite coefficients.}  For each \(m\ge1\), compute the Adams coefficients
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-label-algorithm-adams-coefficients}
        a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
        =\bigl\langle \psi^m\chi_\varrho,\chi_\pi\bigr\rangle_G,
        \qquad
        (\psi^m\chi_\varrho)(g)=\chi_\varrho(g^m),
      \end{equation}
      by the finite character-table inner product.  These coefficients may be
      complex for non-real irreducible pairs; the final class constants are
      real by conjugation symmetry of the full character sum, not by termwise
      realness.
    \item \emph{Branch and deletion rule.}  Omit the single scalar Perron term
      \((r,m,\pi)=(1,1,\mathbf1)\) before any logarithm is evaluated.  Its
      coefficient is
      \(a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}=0\), so the algorithm never assigns a
      finite part or a branch value to
      \(\Log\det(I-\lambda^{-1}A_{\mathbf1})^{-1}\).
      Normalize every remaining logarithm from the germ
      \(\Log\det(I-zA_\pi)^{-1}=0\) at \(z=0\).  For \(rm=1\) this means
      Abel continuation along \(z=t\lambda^{-1}\), \(0\le t\uparrow1\), and
      necessarily \(\pi\neq\mathbf1\).  For \(rm\ge2\) it means the ordinary
      value of the same germ at \(z=\lambda^{-rm}\), where the determinant
      is strictly inside its Perron radius.  Thus every non-deleted triple has
      exactly one legal branch tag:
      \[
        \mathsf B(r,m,\pi)=
        \begin{cases}
          \mathsf{Abel},& rm=1\text{ and }\pi\ne\mathbf1,\\
          \mathsf{Interior},& rm\ge2,
        \end{cases}
      \]
      while \((1,1,\mathbf1)\) has the separate tag \(\mathsf{Deleted}\).
    \item \emph{Truncation rule.}  Choose rational numbers \(\theta<1\) and
      \(Q_\varrho\) satisfying \(\lambda^{-1}<\theta\) and
      \(C_\varrho\lambda<Q_\varrho\), and then choose \(M\) so that the rational
      tail bound
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-rational-tail-choice}
        R_{M,\varrho}:=Q_\varrho\frac{\theta^{M+1}}{1-\theta}\le \epsilon/2
      \end{equation}
      holds.  Equivalently, this finite rational check implies the computable
      analytic tail bound
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-tail-choice}
        C_\varrho
        \sum_{k>M}\frac{\mathrm d(k)}{k}\lambda^{1-k}
        \le \epsilon/2 .
      \end{equation}
      This finite search terminates because \(\lambda>1\).  Form the finite
      truncation
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-truncation}
        F_\varrho^{(M)}
        :=
        \sum_{\substack{r,m\ge1\\rm\le M}}
        \frac{\mu(m)}{rm}
        \sum_{\substack{\pi\in\Irr(G)\\(r,m,\pi)\neq(1,1,\mathbf1)}}
          a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}
          \Log\det(I-\lambda^{-rm}A_\pi)^{-1},
      \end{equation}
      where the scalar Perron term \((1,1,\mathbf1)\) has already been
      omitted, the only possible \(rm=1\) terms use the non-trivial Abel
      branch from \textup{(c)}, and all \(rm\ge2\) terms use the ordinary germ value
      inside the Perron disc.  The truncation error is the effective tail
      estimate
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-error}
        \abs{F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})-F_\varrho^{(M)}}
        \le
        C_\varrho
        \sum_{k>M}\frac{\mathrm d(k)}{k}\lambda^{1-k},
      \end{equation}
      for a constant \(C_\varrho\) depending only on the finite determinant
      collection and \(\varrho\).  The rational tail bound gives
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-label-rational-tail-envelope}
        C_\varrho
        \sum_{k>M}\frac{\mathrm d(k)}{k}\lambda^{1-k}
        \le
        Q_\varrho\frac{\theta^{M+1}}{1-\theta},
      \end{equation}
      because \(\mathrm d(k)\le k\).  Thus the truncations converge absolutely
      and exponentially, and the same finite procedure gives verified
      interval enclosures by truncating after the displayed rational tail
      bound.
    \item \emph{Enclosure guarantee.}  If
      the finitely many logarithms occurring in
      \eqref{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-truncation} are each
      enclosed on the specified branch with radius at most \(\epsilon_0\), then
      the total enclosure radius is bounded by the tail in
      \eqref{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-error} plus
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-roundoff}
        R_{\mathrm{num},\varrho}:=\epsilon_0
        \sum_{\substack{r,m\ge1\\rm\le M}}
          \frac{|\mu(m)|}{rm}
          \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}|a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}| .
      \end{equation}
      Choosing \(\epsilon_0\) so that
      \eqref{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-roundoff} is at most
      \(\epsilon/2\) gives a verified enclosure of radius at most
      \(\epsilon\) by finite interval arithmetic.  This disc is the resulting finite enclosure.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  The proof shows that this finite record is an exact-arithmetic form of the
  Adams-corrected determinant formula, not an additional analytic input.

  Items (a) and (b) are finite because \(G\) has finitely many conjugacy
  classes and irreducible representations, and
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-algorithm-adams-coefficients} is the standard
  finite character inner product defining the Adams multiplicities.  Item
  (c) has two parts.  The deletion rule follows from
  \(a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}=\langle\chi_\varrho,\mathbf1\rangle_G=0\)
  for \(\varrho\neq\mathbf1\); this is the omitted singular term rule in
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}.  The branch rule is exactly the
  branch normalization stated there.  The twisted spectral gap makes the
  non-trivial \(rm=1\) determinants invertible along the Abel path, and
  \(rm\ge2\) gives \(\rho(\lambda^{-rm}A_\pi)\le\lambda^{1-rm}<1\), so the
  ordinary germ value is defined by its absolutely convergent logarithmic
  power series.

  With these conventions, \eqref{eq:fixed-label-determinant-perron} is the
  limit of the finite truncations
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-truncation}.  The tail with
  \(rm>M\) is bounded by
  \Cref{lem:fixed-label-perron-double-majorant}, applied to
  \(\phi=\chi_\varrho\), and regrouping the pairs \((r,m)\) by
  \(k=rm\) gives
  \[
    C_\varrho
    \sum_{\substack{r,m\ge1\\rm>M}}\frac{\lambda^{1-rm}}{rm}
    =
    C_\varrho\sum_{k>M}\frac{\mathrm d(k)}{k}\lambda^{1-k}.
  \]
  This proves \eqref{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-error}.  Choose
  rationals \(\theta\) and \(Q_\varrho\) with
  \(\lambda^{-1}<\theta<1\) and \(C_\varrho\lambda<Q_\varrho\); existence is by
  density of \(\QQ\), and the strict inequalities are finite algebraic
  comparisons against the determinant data.  Since
  \(Q_\varrho\theta^{M+1}/(1-\theta)\to0\), the search in
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-rational-tail-choice}
  terminates.  The rational envelope
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-rational-tail-envelope} follows from
  \(\mathrm d(k)\le k\), from
  \(\lambda^{1-k}=\lambda(\lambda^{-1})^k\le Q_\varrho\theta^k/C_\varrho\),
  and from summing the geometric tail.  Therefore the analytic tail check
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-tail-choice} is a consequence
  of the rational record; no infinite decimal exploration is part of the
  enclosure.

  The canonicity assertion is immediate from this inclusion.  Any finite
  record with the same determinant polynomials, class-power maps, character
  table, Perron root, branch tags, and tail bound is a finite truncation of the
  same absolutely convergent determinant series
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-determinant-perron}.  Each verified disc therefore
  contains the unique value \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\); in particular two such
  discs intersect in that value, regardless of harmless choices in interval
  subdivision or in the order of finite summation.

  It remains only to justify the stated finite-data format.  For fixed
  \(M\), the set of triples \((r,m,\pi)\) with \(rm\le M\) is finite, all
  Adams coefficients in it are computed by finite character-table sums, and
  each determinant logarithm is evaluated on the branch fixed in item (c).
  Replacing each such logarithm by an interval of radius \(\epsilon_0\) changes
  the weighted finite sum by at most the triangle-inequality bound in
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-roundoff}.  Choosing
  \(\epsilon_0\) so that this finite bound is at most the remaining budget
  after the rational tail radius in
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-rational-tail-choice}, and
  choosing \(M\) by that rational inequality, gives a rigorous enclosure interval
  of radius at most \(\epsilon\).  Thus the input, branch convention, finite
  enclosure, and error bound are all finite and explicit.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Boundary determinant convention for Main Theorem III]
  \label{cor:main-iii-boundary-determinant-convention}
  Every determinant logarithm entering the Main Theorem~III product constants
  through the fixed-label coordinates satisfies exactly one of the following
  mutually exclusive alternatives:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
    \item it is the scalar Perron triple
      \((r,m,\pi)=(1,1,\mathbf1)\), and it is deleted before any boundary
      evaluation is made;
    \item it has \(rm=1\) and \(\pi\ne\mathbf1\), and its value is the Abel
      continuation of the normalized determinant germ along
      \(t\lambda^{-1}\), \(0<t<1\), to \(t\uparrow1\);
    \item it has \(rm\ge2\), possibly with \(\pi=\mathbf1\), and its value is
      the ordinary convergent logarithm at \(z=\lambda^{-rm}\).
  \end{enumerate}
  Consequently no formula in the Main Theorem~III chain evaluates
  \(\Log\det(I-\lambda^{-1}A_{\mathbf1})^{-1}\), assigns it a finite part, or
  recovers it by cancellation after the Abel limit.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The alternatives are exhaustive for the triples \((r,m,\pi)\) appearing in
  the Adams--Mobius determinant expansion.  The first alternative is the
  singular-triple deletion of
  \Cref{lem:fixed-label-singular-triple-deletion,prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}:
  in a non-trivial fixed-label coordinate its coefficient is
  \(a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}=0\), so the summand is absent before the Abel
  endpoint is formed.  The same two results prove the second and third
  alternatives: the strict twisted spectral gap gives a regular Abel branch
  for \(rm=1\), \(\pi\ne\mathbf1\), while \(rm\ge2\) gives
  \(\rho(\lambda^{-rm}A_\pi)<1\), so the logarithm is the ordinary interior
  value.  The finite algorithm
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm} uses precisely these branch
  tags and the same deletion rule in
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm-truncation}.

  Main Theorem~III receives determinant data only through these fixed-label
  coordinates: \Cref{prop:main-iii-fixed-label-proof-spine} identifies the
  fixed-label coordinate with the determinant boundary value, and
  \Cref{cor:main-iii-convention-compatibility} fixes the sign, Fourier
  normalisation, positive branch, and omitted-triple convention used in the
  product constants.  Therefore every determinant logarithm in the Main
  Theorem~III product formula has already passed through one of the three
  alternatives above.  In particular, the scalar boundary logarithm in the
  trivial channel is never evaluated in the theorem chain; the only scalar
  terms that remain have \(rm\ge2\) and are ordinary interior-disc values.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite branch--tail enclosure criterion]
  \label{prop:fixed-label-branch-tail-record}
  Assume the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit}, fix
  \(\varrho\ne\mathbf1\), and consider finite enclosure data for
  \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\).  The enclosure is justified by the determinant
  data if and only if the following finite verification conditions all hold.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
    \item The deleted set is exactly \(\{(1,1,\mathbf1)\}\), and its coefficient
      is verified to be
      \(a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}=\langle\chi_\varrho,\mathbf1\rangle_G=0\).
    \item Every non-deleted triple \((r,m,\pi)\) with \(rm\le M\) carries the
      unique branch tag
      \[
        \mathsf B(r,m,\pi)=
        \begin{cases}
          \mathsf{Abel},& rm=1\text{ and }\pi\ne\mathbf1,\\
          \mathsf{Interior},& rm\ge2,
        \end{cases}
      \]
      and no other tag satisfies the stated convention.  In particular scalar terms with
      \(\pi=\mathbf1\) are legal precisely when \(rm\ge2\), where they are
      interior-disc determinant logarithms.
    \item The tail estimate records rational data
      \((C_\varrho,Q_\varrho,\theta_\varrho,M_\varrho)\) satisfying
      \[
        \lambda^{-1}<\theta_\varrho<1,
        \qquad C_\varrho\lambda<Q_\varrho,
        \qquad
        R_{M,\varrho}:=
        Q_\varrho\frac{\theta_\varrho^{M_\varrho+1}}{1-\theta_\varrho},
      \]
      and this number dominates the whole omitted determinant tail
      \[
        C_\varrho
        \sum_{k>M_\varrho}\frac{\mathrm d(k)}{k}\lambda^{1-k}
        \le R_{M,\varrho}.
      \]
    \item If each branch-tagged logarithm in the finite window is enclosed
      with radius at most \(\epsilon_0\), the numerical enclosure data give
      \[
        R_{\mathrm{num},\varrho}:=
        \epsilon_0
        \sum_{\substack{r,m\ge1\\rm\le M_\varrho}}
          \frac{|\mu(m)|}{rm}
          \sum_{\pi\in\Irr(G)}|a_{\varrho,\pi}^{(m)}|,
      \]
      and the verified disc
      \[
        I_\varrho+
        \{w\in\CC:|w|\le R_{M,\varrho}+R_{\mathrm{num},\varrho}\}
      \]
      contains the unique Abel-boundary value \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\).
  \end{enumerate}
  Hence branch choice, deleted scalar singularity, analytic tail, and numerical
  propagation are controlled by finite deterministic data.  Two verified
  enclosures for the same determinant data and branch convention have discs
  with a common point, namely \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Item~\textup{(i)} is the singular-triple deletion proved in
  \Cref{lem:fixed-label-singular-triple-deletion}: for
  \(\varrho\ne\mathbf1\), finite-character orthogonality gives
  \(a_{\varrho,\mathbf1}^{(1)}=0\), so the scalar Perron logarithm is removed
  before any branch or finite part is assigned.  That lemma and
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron} also prove item~\textup{(ii)}.
  If \(rm=1\), then \(r=m=1\); after the deleted scalar triple is removed,
  only \(\pi\ne\mathbf1\) remains, and \(\eta<\lambda\) gives the Abel-path
  zero-free continuation.  If \(rm\ge2\), then
  \(\rho(\lambda^{-rm}A_\pi)\le\lambda^{1-rm}<1\) for every \(\pi\), including
  \(\pi=\mathbf1\), so the ordinary normalized germ is the only branch used.

  For item~\textup{(iii)},
  \Cref{lem:fixed-label-perron-double-majorant} gives the omitted-tail bound
  by
  \[
    C_\varrho
    \sum_{k>M_\varrho}\frac{\mathrm d(k)}{k}\lambda^{1-k}.
  \]
  Since \(\mathrm d(k)\le k\) and
  \(C_\varrho\lambda<Q_\varrho\), while
  \(\lambda^{-1}<\theta_\varrho\), each summand satisfies
  \[
    C_\varrho\frac{\mathrm d(k)}{k}\lambda^{1-k}
    \le C_\varrho\lambda(\lambda^{-1})^k
    \le Q_\varrho\theta_\varrho^k .
  \]
  Summing the resulting geometric tail gives the displayed rational radius
  \(R_{M,\varrho}\).  All comparisons are finite algebraic or rational
  comparisons because \(C_\varrho\), \(Q_\varrho\), \(\theta_\varrho\), and
  \(M_\varrho\) are recorded explicitly.

  For item~\textup{(iv)}, replace each finitely many branch-tagged logarithms
  by a disc of radius \(\epsilon_0\) on its prescribed branch.  The weighted
  finite sum changes by at most the triangle-inequality radius
  \(R_{\mathrm{num},\varrho}\).  Combining this finite numerical radius with
  the tail radius from item~\textup{(iii)} and the determinant identity
  \eqref{eq:fixed-label-determinant-perron} proves that the verified disc
  contains \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\).  Conversely, any finite enclosure data satisfying
  \Cref{prop:fixed-label-perron-finite-algorithm} supplies exactly the same
  deletion rule, branch tags, rational tail radius, and finite numerical
  propagation, so it satisfies the four verification conditions.  Thus such
  finite data verify the same Abel-boundary determinant value, and two
  verified discs intersect at that value.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Product-vector rigidity after scalar normalisation}
\label{subsubsec:class-product-vector-rigidity}

After the determinant-only evaluation of \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\), the
remaining statements in this unit concern the finite class vector
\((K_C)_C\).  They are rigidity and observation results for the already
constructed product constants: they record the scalar hyperplane constraint,
Fourier inversion on conjugacy classes, and the finite coordinate fibre of
the whole positive product vector inside the branch-compatible determinant
chart.  They do not assert recovery of the cocycle or any branch-free inverse
spectral datum.

\begin{corollary}[Rigidity of the Adams-corrected class-product constants]
  \label{cor:class-product-constant-rigidity}
  Assume the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit}.  For each
  conjugacy class \(C\subset G\), let \(K_C\) be the positive constant in
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}, and set
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:class-product-normalised-log-vector}
    \mathfrak K(C)
    :=
    -\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C-\bigl(\gamma+\log C(A)\bigr).
  \end{equation}
  Then, as a structural consequence of the Adams-corrected constant formula
  and finite character Fourier inversion, the class-product constants are
  not a free family of classwise constants.  After the scalar normalisation
  \(C(A)\) and the branch-compatible determinant-coordinate convention, they
  are exactly equivalent to the non-trivial fixed-label Perron-boundary vector:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:class-product-rigidity-forward}
    \mathfrak K(C)
    =
    \sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
      \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}),
  \end{equation}
  the normalised logarithmic vector lies in the non-trivial Peter--Weyl
  summand,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:class-product-rigidity-hyperplane}
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathfrak K(C)=0,
    \qquad
    \prod_{C\subset G}K_C=
      \exp\!\bigl(-\gamma-\log C(A)\bigr),
  \end{equation}
  and the Fourier recovery is norm-preserving:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:class-product-rigidity-parseval}
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\abs{\mathfrak K(C)}^2
    =
    \sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
      \abs{F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})}^2.
  \end{equation}
  Moreover, for every non-trivial \(\pi\in\Irr(G)\),
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:class-product-rigidity-inverse}
    F_\pi(\lambda^{-1})
    =
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathfrak K(C)\chi_\pi(C).
  \end{equation}
  Consequently the finite determinant data
  \(\{\det(I-zA_\pi):\pi\in\Irr(G)\}\), the finite character table and
  Adams tensors, the normalized Abel branches, and the scalar normalisation
  \(C(A)\) determine the whole scalar-normalised Frobenius-class Euler-product
  vector \((K_C)_C\) in this fixed branch-compatible determinant chart.
  Conversely, within the same listed finite input data, scalar normalisation,
  and branch convention, the vector \((K_C)_C\) and \(C(A)\) determine all
  fixed-label values \(F_\pi(\lambda^{-1})\), \(\pi\neq\mathbf1\). These are the
  fixed-label Euler-transform values, not the periodic Artin logarithms
  \(\mathcal L_\pi(\lambda^{-1})\). Thus, subject to the single scalar
  constraint in \eqref{eq:class-product-rigidity-hyperplane}, no two
  non-trivial fixed-label Perron-boundary vectors can give the same
  normalised class-product constants.  The converse just described is only
  a coordinate expression in the fixed branch-compatible determinant model;
  equality of these
  constants is not asserted to imply equality, conjugacy, or cohomology of
  the underlying cocycles or extensions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  This is the Fourier-inversion corollary of the constant formula in
  \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}; it is not an additional
  Chebotarev or Mertens asymptotic.
  Since each \(K_C\) is positive by
  \Cref{lem:class-euler-product-real-branch}, the logarithm in
  \eqref{eq:class-product-normalised-log-vector} is the ordinary real
  logarithm.  Taking the logarithm of
  \eqref{eq:class-euler-product-k-f} gives
  \[
    -\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C-\bigl(\gamma+\log C(A)\bigr)
    =
    \sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
      \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}),
  \]
  which is \eqref{eq:class-product-rigidity-forward}.  Since the trivial
  character is orthogonal to every non-trivial irreducible character,
  summing \eqref{eq:class-product-rigidity-forward} with weights
  \(|C|/|G|\) gives the first identity in
  \eqref{eq:class-product-rigidity-hyperplane}.  Substituting the
  definition of \(\mathfrak K(C)\) then gives
  \begin{align*}
    0
    &=
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \left(-\frac{|G|}{|C|}\log K_C
        -\bigl(\gamma+\log C(A)\bigr)\right) \\
    &=-\sum_{C\subset G}\log K_C-\gamma-\log C(A),
  \end{align*}
  which is the product constraint in
  \eqref{eq:class-product-rigidity-hyperplane}.  Applying Parseval's
  identity in the orthonormal character basis of class functions gives
  \eqref{eq:class-product-rigidity-parseval}.

  Multiplying \eqref{eq:class-product-rigidity-forward}
  identity by \(|C|\chi_\pi(C)/|G|\), summing over conjugacy classes, and
  using the Hermitian character orthogonality relation
  \[
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)\chi_\pi(C)=\delta_{\varrho\pi}
  \]
  gives \eqref{eq:class-product-rigidity-inverse} for every
  \(\pi\neq\mathbf1\).

  By \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron}, each non-trivial value
  \(F_\pi(\lambda^{-1})\) is obtained from the finite twisted determinant
  family by the branch-compatible Adams--M\"obius determinant formula,
  while the scalar contribution is the single normalisation \(C(A)\).
  Substituting those determinant values into
  \eqref{eq:class-product-rigidity-forward} and then into
  \eqref{eq:class-euler-product-k-f} determines every \(K_C\).  The
  inverse formula \eqref{eq:class-product-rigidity-inverse} proves the
  converse determination from the normalised product constants and shows
  injectivity of the fixed-label vector.  This proves the rigidity
  statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Lower-order-tail stability of the Perron-point constants]
  \label{prop:main-chain-secondary-noninterference}
  Assume the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit}.  The constants in
  Main Theorems~II--III depend only on the Perron-boundary determinant
  coordinates.  Lower-order exponential contributions with vanishing
  compensated weighted sums do not change those constants.
  More precisely, let finitely many complex numbers of modulus strictly smaller
  than \(\lambda\) be arranged in class-function exponential sums \(S_C(n)\),
  and suppose that the weighted tails
  \[
    T_C(N):=\sum_{n>N}\frac{S_C(n)}{n\lambda^n}
  \]
  converge and satisfy \(T_C(N)\to0\) for every conjugacy class \(C\).  If a
  refined counting formula has the form
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:secondary-noninterference-refined-count}
    p_{n,C}
    =
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}\frac{\lambda^n}{n}
    +\frac{S_C(n)}{n}+r_C(n)
  \end{equation}
  and its compensated summatory form is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:secondary-noninterference-refined-sum}
    \sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
    =
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}H_N
    +\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\gamma
    -T_C(N)+o(1),
  \end{equation}
  then the lower-order tail lies below the Perron-boundary level and does not
  change the Frobenius-class product constant.  More explicitly, the
  main constants remain exactly
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:secondary-noninterference-mc}
    \mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)
    =
    \frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathcal M(A)
    +\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
        \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
  \end{equation}
  and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:secondary-noninterference-k}
    K_C=
    \exp\!\left(
      -\frac{|C|}{|G|}
      \left(
        \gamma+\log C(A)
        +\sum_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}
          \chi_\varrho^\ast(C)F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
      \right)
    \right),
  \end{equation}
  with the positive branch of \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.  Hence
  the finite determinant data
  \(\{\det(I-zA_\pi):\pi\in\Irr(G)\}\), the scalar normalisation \(C(A)\),
  and the Adams--M\"obius fixed-label transform determine the entire main
  product vector independently of any lower-order tail satisfying the displayed
  comparison condition.  In
  particular, any two refinements satisfying the displayed tail
  condition give the same \((\mathcal M_C)_C\), the same \((K_C)_C\), and the
  same recovered vector \((F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}))_{\varrho\neq\mathbf1}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The assertion is a limit comparison at the Perron-boundary scale.  The formulas
  \eqref{eq:secondary-noninterference-mc} and
  \eqref{eq:secondary-noninterference-k} are exactly the class-Mertens and
  class-product formulas already proved in
  \Cref{thm:class-mertens-explicit,thm:class-euler-product-mertens}.  In the
  refined summatory identity \eqref{eq:secondary-noninterference-refined-sum}
  the lower-order contribution is the single compensated tail \(-T_C(N)\).
  Since the hypotheses require \(T_C(N)\to0\), passing to the constant term
  in the normalized partial sums leaves \(\mathcal M_C(A,\tau;G)\) unchanged.

  The Euler-product proof of \Cref{thm:class-euler-product-mertens} depends
  on the counting data only through this constant term and through the
  absolutely convergent \(r\ge2\) primitive tail.  Replacing the pointwise
  count by \eqref{eq:secondary-noninterference-refined-count} changes the
  normalized logarithm only by the same vanishing tail and by a remainder
  which is already absorbed in the displayed \(o(1)\) term.  Therefore the
  limiting positive product constant is still the value in
  \eqref{eq:secondary-noninterference-k}.

  Finally, \Cref{prop:fixed-label-determinant-perron} determines each
  non-trivial value \(F_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) from the finite determinant
  data and the fixed Abel branch independently of any lower-order comparison
  term.
  The inverse Fourier recovery of
  \Cref{thm:class-product-determinant-rigidity} then recovers the same
  fixed-label vector from the normalized product constants.  Thus any two
  refinements satisfying the tail condition have identical main
  Mertens constants, product constants, and recovered fixed-label Perron
  values.
\end{proof}
