\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Let \(\Gamma\) be a finite directed multigraph with primitive adjacency
matrix \(A\), Perron root \(\lambda>1\), and one-sided edge shift
\(X_\Gamma^+\).  A map \(\tau:E(\Gamma)\to G\) to a finite group defines a
one-step skew-product cocycle.  If \(\gamma\) is a primitive periodic orbit,
the ordered product of its edge labels is defined up to conjugacy; write
\([g_\gamma]\) for that conjugacy class.  For a conjugacy class \(C\subset G\)
we consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-class-product}
  P_C(N):=
  \prod_{\substack{\gamma\text{ primitive}\\
                    |\gamma|\leq N,\ [g_\gamma]=C}}
       \left(1-\lambda^{-|\gamma|}\right).
\end{equation}
The dynamical-zeta and periodic-orbit framework is classical
\cite{BowenLanford1970Zeta,Parry1983PrimeOrbit,ParryPollicott1990Zeta}.
For a finite-group extension with the required extension-level aperiodicity,
the exponent in the Mertens asymptotic for \eqref{eq:intro-class-product} is
\(|C|/|G|\).  Mixing of the base alone does not supply that hypothesis.  The
correction below concerns both this missing hypothesis and the constant that
is obtained once it holds.

For a class function \(\phi\) on \(G\), put
\[
  \Pi_\phi(z):=\sum_{\gamma\text{ primitive}}
     \phi(g_\gamma)z^{|\gamma|}.
\]
There are two natural logarithmic transforms of this primitive series:
\begin{align}
  L_\phi(z)
    &:=\sum_{r\geq1}\frac{\Pi_{\psi^r\phi}(z^r)}{r},
    & (\psi^r\phi)(g)&:=\phi(g^r),
    \label{eq:intro-artin}\\
  F_\phi(z)
    &:=\sum_{r\geq1}\frac{\Pi_\phi(z^r)}{r}.
    \label{eq:intro-fixed}
\end{align}
The first is the periodic Artin logarithm: the \(r\)-fold traversal changes
the primitive label from \(g_\gamma\) to \(g_\gamma^r\).  The second is the
fixed-label Euler transform: the scalar factor in
\(\log(1-\lambda^{-|\gamma|})\) is repeated, but membership of the primitive
orbit in \(C\) is not re-tested after powering its label.  Accordingly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-difference}
  F_\phi(z)-L_\phi(z)
  =\sum_{r\geq2}\frac{
       \Pi_\phi(z^r)-\Pi_{\psi^r\phi}(z^r)}{r}.
\end{equation}
This elementary distinction is the source of the correction theorem.

\subsection{Main result and correction of the 1999 constant}

Let \(A_\rho\) be the finite twisted adjacency matrix in an irreducible
unitary representation \(\rho\) of \(G\), and assume the strict twisted gap
\[
  \max_{\rho\neq\mathbf1}\rad(A_\rho)<\lambda.
\]
The gap is a finite, checkable hypothesis; it is not inferred from mixing of
the base alone.  Define
\[
  C(A):=\lim_{t\uparrow1}(1-t)\det(I-tA/\lambda)^{-1}>0.
\]
The central theorem, proved in \Cref{sec:product-correction}, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main-constant}
  P_C(N)=K_C N^{-|C|/|G|}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-kc}
  K_C=
  \exp\!\left\{-\frac{|C|}{|G|}
    \left(\gamma+\log C(A)
      +\sum_{\rho\neq\mathbf1}
        \overline{\chi_\rho(C)}F_{\chi_\rho}(\lambda^{-1})
    \right)\right\}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, each non-trivial fixed-label value in \eqref{eq:intro-kc} is
recovered from the finite determinant family by Adams--M\"obius inversion.
Thus the new content is not the exponent or the existence scale of the
product, but the corrected finite-state coordinate of its constant.

Mohamed and Noorani state the corresponding exponent and constant under
mixing of the base, with the constant expressed through Artin values
\(L(e^{-h},\chi)\), where \(e^h=\lambda\)
\cite[Theorem~1 and its proof on pp.~124--125]{MeslizaNoorani1999FrobeniusMertens}.
Their character-orthogonality step starts from the correct fixed class
condition.  The next identification with \(\log L(e^{-h},\chi)\), however,
changes the \(r\)-th logarithmic summand from
\(\chi(g_\gamma)\lambda^{-r|\gamma|}/r\) to
\(\chi(g_\gamma^r)\lambda^{-r|\gamma|}/r\).  That substitution is generally
false.  Base mixing also fails to imply uniform Frobenius density: the trivial
\(C_2\)-cocycle in \Cref{rem:trivial-cocycle-counterexample} has a mixing base
but no orbit in the non-identity class.  \Cref{thm:formal-correction} therefore
states two independent repairs.  Under the strict twisted gap, the \(S_3\)
construction in \Cref{sec:s3-witness} gives a direct contradiction to the
constant: the sign Artin logarithm is zero, while the sign fixed-label
coordinate is strictly negative.

\subsection{Scope of the background appendix}

Equality of all represented spectra is the finite-group gain-graph notion of
\(G\)-cospectrality, and its translation to conjugacy-class periodic data is
established context
\cite{CavaleriDAngeliDonno2021BalanceGainGraphs,
CavaleriDonno2022Cospectrality,BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions}.
Switching and spanning-tree normalization are likewise standard
\cite{Zaslavsky1989BiasedGraphs,GrossTucker1987TopologicalGraphTheory};
non-switching-equivalent cospectral constructions form an active gain-graph
literature \cite{AbiadBelardoKhramova2024Switching}.

Appendix~\ref{sec:inverse-background} records only the determinant/periodic-data
dictionary and the standard finite fundamental-cycle switching certificate.
No novelty or dynamical inverse classification is claimed for these facts.

\subsection{Position in the literature}

The determinant formula for shifts of finite type and the general
periodic-orbit/zeta formalism are standard
\cite{BowenLanford1970Zeta,Parry1983PrimeOrbit,ParryPollicott1990Zeta}.
Likewise, M\"obius inversion between primitive Euler coordinates and periodic
ghost coordinates is classical necklace/Witt calculus
\cite{MetropolisRota1983Necklaces}.  Noorani's Artin-function treatment
\cite{Noorani1995ChebotarevFiniteExtensions} precedes the explicit
Frobenius-class product formula of Mohamed and Noorani
\cite{MeslizaNoorani1999FrobeniusMertens}.  The contribution here is the
identification of the fixed primitive-label coordinate selected by that
product, followed by application of the classical transform to the finite
family of twisted determinant blocks.  The distinction is also relevant when
the 1999 theorem is invoked in later orbit-growth work; the precise effect on
\cite{NordinNooraniMohd2024OrbitGrowthSoficPFT} is stated in
\Cref{rem:post-1999-scope}.  The graph-covering Artin-zeta context may be
found in \cite{StarkTerras1996FiniteGraphsCoverings}.
