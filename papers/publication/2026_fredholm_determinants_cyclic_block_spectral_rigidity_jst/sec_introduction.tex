\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Classical finite-state zeta identities motivate the cyclic products considered
here.  If \(A\) is a primitive finite \(0\)-\(1\) matrix and \(p_n(A)\)
denotes the number of primitive orbits of length \(n\), then
\[
  \zeta_A(z)=\det(I-zA)^{-1}
  =\prod_{n\ge1}(1-z^n)^{-p_n(A)}
\]
as a germ at the origin.  This paper does not develop symbolic dynamics.  It
uses only the finite-state identity as a source of examples and then studies
the finite-subset positive-exponent cyclic products
\[
  D_F(z)=\prod_{j\in F}(1-\beta_j z^{n_j})^{m_j},
  \qquad F\Subset J,
\]
for prescribed countable cyclic data
\(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\), with
\(n_j,m_j\in\NN\) and \(\beta_j\ne0\).  The symbol \(D_{\mathcal D}\) is used
only after the finite-subset net has a locally uniform limit, or as shorthand
for equality of that net with a Fredholm determinant.  In this convention a
reciprocal Euler product means only \(D_{\mathcal D}^{-1}\) on a chosen
zero-free neighborhood of the origin after such a limit is available.

The central question is when the finite-subset net \((D_F)_{F\Subset J}\)
converges locally uniformly to the Fredholm determinant of a trace-class
operator, and what that determinant then remembers about the operator.  The
answer is governed by the single trace budget
\[
  b_1(\mathcal D)=\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j|\beta_j|^{1/n_j}.
\]
At this threshold the cyclic factors have a canonical normal model obtained by
placing, for each \(j\), the \(n_j\) roots of \(x^{n_j}=\beta_j\) on a diagonal
block, repeated \(m_j\) times.  The determinant, however, does not remember the
chosen cyclic presentation.  It remembers precisely the collapsed non-zero
spectral multiplicity function
\[
  M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)=
  \sum_{\{j:\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\}}m_j,
  \qquad \lambda\in\CC^\times .
\]
Thus different cyclic factorizations with the same collapsed root multiset give
the same normal determinant fiber.

The main theorem, \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization}, proves the exact
criterion
\[
  b_1(\mathcal D)<\infty
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  D_F\longrightarrow\det(I-zT)
  \text{ locally uniformly for some }T\in\mathcal S_1.
\]
When this holds, normal realizations are classified by
\(M_{\mathcal D}\), up to adjoining a zero summand.  More importantly, the same
normal cyclic model is variationally rigid in the full, possibly nonnormal,
trace-class determinant fiber:
\[
  \|T\|_1\ge b_1(\mathcal D),
\]
and equality forces unitary equivalence to the normal cyclic model, again up to
a zero summand.  Beyond this equality case, the paper proves a genuinely
infinite-dimensional, but tail-localized, orbit-distance theorem:
\Cref{thm:tail-localized-orbit-distance} gives an explicit trace-norm distance
bound to the normal minimizer orbit for near-minimizers once a Ringrose--Schur
tail modulus is supplied.  Thus the distance statement is analytic and
infinite-dimensional, while its hypotheses make clear that the determinant and
the scalar trace defect alone do not control arbitrary Schur tails.
The equality statement above is the only determinant-intrinsic global rigidity
assertion in the paper.
Nonnormal quasinilpotent directions at the spectral point zero are
Fredholm-determinant invisible and are not classified.

The Schatten extension has the same deliberately narrow scope.  For
\(1<p<\infty\), \(r=\lceil p\rceil\), and
\[
  b_p(\mathcal D)=\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j|\beta_j|^{p/n_j},
\]
\Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality} records the corresponding
prescribed-budget statement for \(\det_r\).  Within the localized class where
the finite-subset regularized cyclic products \(P_F^{(r)}\) are part of the data,
\(b_p\) is the sharp
\(p\)-Schatten budget and equality again identifies the normal cyclic model.
The theorem is not an unrestricted natural-domain classification of all
\(\det_r\)-fibers.

\subsection{Relation to standard tools}

The operator-theoretic inputs behind the determinant and lower-bound parts are
classical.  Fredholm and regularized
determinants provide the zero-divisor/eigenvalue correspondence
\cite{Simon2005TraceIdeals,Simon1977InfiniteDeterminants,GohbergGoldbergKrupnik1996TracesDeterminants}.  The lower bounds
come from Weyl--Ky Fan and Schatten Weyl--Fan inequalities
\cite{Weyl1949EigenvalueInequalities,Fan1951MaximumProperties,GohbergKrein1969NonSelfAdjoint,GohbergGoldbergKaashoek1990ClassesI,Bhatia1997MatrixAnalysis}.  Equality is
read in a Ringrose triangularization \cite{Ringrose1971CompactNonSelfAdjoint}:
near-minimal or minimal trace budget pins the Schur off-diagonal entries in the
chosen root-space order.  The added tail-localized orbit-distance theorem is the
point where this finite-window pinching is assembled with explicit
infinite-dimensional Schur-tail hypotheses to produce a trace-norm distance
estimate to the minimizer orbit.  In finite dimension, the singular-value consequences
are exactly the usual Weyl--Horn body \cite{Horn1954DoublyStochastic}.  The
regularized determinant convention follows the standard modified Fredholm
determinant product formulae and normalizations for Schatten ideals
\cite{Simon2005TraceIdeals,GesztesyMakarov2004ModifiedFredholm,GesztesyLatushkinMitreaZinchenko2005,BritzCareyGesztesyNicholsSukochevZanin2021}.
The comparison with recent work in \emph{Integral Equations and Operator
Theory} is theorem-by-theorem as follows; the word ``novelty'' in the last
column is meant only in this restricted cyclic-divisor sense.

\normalsize

Thus the paper's contribution is deliberately limited.  Except for the
tail-localized estimate under an explicit Schur-tail hypothesis, the novelty is
the cyclic-divisor package: the exact trace-class threshold, the collapse from
cyclic presentation to non-zero root multiset, the sharp trace-norm minimizer
in the full determinant fiber, and the prescribed-budget Schatten analogue.
The determinant identities, perturbation-determinant technology,
Schatten-ideal estimates, singular-value majorization, and Toeplitz determinant
asymptotics cited above are used as standard background, not superseded.

\subsection{Reader's guide to the focused version}

The paper is organized around one spine.  The main theorem gives the
trace-class threshold, the normal realization, and the trace-norm equality
case.  Everything else is subordinate to that spine.  The finite-state
paragraphs explain why cyclic factors are natural but do not supply hypotheses
for the Fredholm theorem.  The stability discussion explains what the equality
proof also gives on a fixed Schur window, but it is not promoted to a global
orbit-distance result.  The finite-dimensional Horn paragraph records the
standard matrix shadow of the same collapsed eigenvalue data.  The appendix
then isolates the finite non-zero Jordan bookkeeping which the determinant
leaves unresolved.

This focused presentation deliberately suppresses several tempting but
misleading reformulations.  It does not state separate theorems for every
intermediate implication in the proof of the main theorem; those implications
are included in the proof.  It does not recast the cyclic data as a unique
factorization theorem, because the determinant only sees the collapsed roots.
It does not state a classification of nonnormal determinant fibers, because
quasinilpotent zero summands are invisible.  It also does not state a natural
\(\det_r\)-domain classification in Schatten ideals; the Schatten result is the
prescribed-budget analogue needed for the same cyclic divisor problem.

The resulting article should be read as a compact determinant-fiber theorem
with controlled boundary statements.  The mathematical content is concentrated
in the trace-class equivalence and minimizer rigidity, the prescribed-budget
Schatten analogue, and the appendix's finite skeleton.  The remaining
paragraphs are included only to prevent overinterpretation of these results and
to identify precisely which classical facts are being repackaged.
\subsection{Organization}

\Cref{sec:preliminaries} recalls the finite-state zeta identity and explains
why nontrivial primitive symbolic Euler data do not satisfy the trace-class
cyclic budget.  \Cref{sec:fredholm} proves the main theorem, records the
stability and finite-dimensional consequences, and gives the Schatten
prescribed-budget extension.  \Cref{sec:conclusion} summarizes the resulting
realization and rigidity picture.  The appendix gives the finite non-zero
Jordan-skeleton bookkeeping and states explicitly why the quasinilpotent zero
sector is excluded from the classification.
