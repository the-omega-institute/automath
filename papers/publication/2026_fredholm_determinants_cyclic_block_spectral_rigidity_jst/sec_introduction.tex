\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Classical finite-state zeta identities motivate the cyclic products below:
for a primitive finite matrix \(A\),
\[
  \zeta_A(z)=\det(I-zA)^{-1}
  =\prod_{n\ge1}(1-z^n)^{-p_n(A)}
\]
as a germ at the origin.  This paper does not develop the symbolic-dynamical
primitive-orbit theory.  Its determinant convention is instead the
positive-exponent Fredholm product
\[
  D_{\mathcal D}(z)=\prod_j(1-\beta_jz^{n_j})^{m_j},
\]
while a reciprocal Euler product means only \(D_{\mathcal D}^{-1}\) after a
connected zero-free neighborhood of the origin has been chosen.  The
finite-state symbolic product is retained only as boundary motivation: its
threshold relevant for the prescribed-cyclic-zero-divisor theorem below
becomes
\[
  \sum_{n\ge1} n\,p_n(A)<\infty,
\]
and \Cref{cor:primitive-symbolic-summability-exclusion} shows that this happens only
for the trivial one-symbol fixed-point shift \(A=[1]\).  Thus the
primitive symbolic product is not part of the theorem chain except as a
short finite-state reference point; no twisted orbit, cocycle, or
Peter--Weyl refinement is used in the arguments below.

The article therefore studies the trace-class realization problem for
prescribed cyclic zero divisors. Given countable Euler data
\[
  \mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J},
\]
we ask when the product
\[
  \prod_{j\in J}(1-\beta_j z^{n_j})^{m_j}
\]
is the Fredholm determinant of a trace-class operator and what geometry is
forced if one restricts to normal realizations. The main point is that in
the normal trace-class category the cyclic divisor of the determinant
already determines the canonical block model, the closed non-zero spectral
support, and the exact trace budget. Beyond the normal category, the same
determinant still carries a sharp variational statement: among all
trace-class realizations with that determinant, the canonical cyclic model
has minimal trace norm, and equality forces a return to that same normal
cyclic model up to a zero summand, even though the full determinant fiber
is trace-norm unbounded once quasinilpotent nonnormal directions are
adjoined.

The Fredholm--Witt bridge and determinant rigidity enter as classical
tools, but the present arguments use them only as the starting point for the
cyclic-divisor classification.
\Cref{prop:fredholm-regularized-zero-divisor-correspondence} fixes the
background convention used throughout: for both ordinary Fredholm
determinants and regularized determinants \(\det_r\), the zero at
\(z=\lambda^{-1}\) has order equal to the algebraic multiplicity of the
non-zero eigenvalue \(\lambda\). The determinant first reduces the problem
to the collapsed non-zero spectral multiplicity function
\[
  M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
  =\sum_{\{j:\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\}}m_j,
  \qquad \lambda\in\CC^\times .
\]
The main results then describe what remains after this bookkeeping is
extracted.  The cyclic normal model is the trace-norm minimizer class in the
full determinant fiber; near-minimal trace budget controls fixed Schur entries
and, with chosen tail data, distance to the diagonal minimizer window.  Finite
collapsed support gives a fixed-dimensional Weyl--Horn singular-value body and
a finite non-zero skeleton quotient.  For every \(1<p<\infty\), the same
cyclic data also have a sharp prescribed-budget \(\mathcal S_p\) theorem for
\(\det_r\), \(r=\lceil p\rceil\): the product fixes the cyclic divisor, the
finite number \(\sum_jm_jn_j|\beta_j|^{p/n_j}\) is forced by every
\(\mathcal S_p\) realization carrying that product, and equality is rigid
inside this prescribed cyclic-product fiber.

\subsection{Roadmap and hierarchy of results}

The main theorem chain is trace-class and Fredholm-theoretic.  It first fixes
the positive-exponent determinant convention and proves the exact summability
threshold
\[
  \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}<\infty .
\]
At precisely this threshold the canonical cyclic-block operator
\(L_{\mathcal D}\) is trace class.  The determinant then forgets the chosen
Euler-factor presentation and retains only the collapsed non-zero spectral
multiplicity function
\[
  M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
  =\sum_{\{j:\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\}}m_j,
  \qquad \lambda\in\CC^\times .
\]
The core trace-class theorem
\Cref{thm:main-trace-class-rigidity} states the resulting rigidity: normal
realizations are classified by this collapsed multiset, and in the full
trace-class determinant fiber the normal cyclic model is the sharp trace-norm
minimizer.  Equality in the trace-norm bound forces unitary equivalence to
\(L_{\mathcal D}\) up to a zero summand.  The closing hierarchy theorem,
\Cref{thm:trace-class-rigidity-consequences}, records that every later package
uses only the determinant-intrinsic data
\((M_{\mathcal D},\Sigma_{\mathcal D},\tau_{\mathcal D})\).

The consequences are organized in four subordinate packages.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \emph{Chosen-Schur stability.}  After a Ringrose triangularization has
  been chosen, trace-defect control pinches fixed Schur entries and gives
  finite-window estimates.  The infinite-dimensional statement is a chosen-tail
  criterion with explicit Schur-tail and determinant-tail hypotheses, not a
  basis-free orbit-distance theorem.
  \item \emph{Fixed-dimensional Weyl--Horn geometry.}  When the collapsed
  support is finite and a dimension \(N\ge d_{\mathcal D}\) is fixed, the
  singular-value image of
  \(\{T\in M_N(\CC):\det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)\}\) is the Weyl--Horn body
  \(\operatorname{HWH}(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N})\) of the zero-padded collapsed
  eigenvalue vector.  This is a finite matrix consequence of the classical
  Horn--Li--Mathias theorem after the Fredholm theorem chain has fixed the
  collapsed eigenvalues; no cross-dimensional Horn inverse system or
  stabilization theorem is asserted.
  \item \emph{Prescribed-budget Schatten theory.}  For \(1<p<\infty\), the
  regularized determinant \(\det_{\lceil p\rceil}\) is treated inside a fixed
  finite-subset cyclic-product \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget fiber.  The sharp budget
  is \(\sum_jm_jn_j|\beta_j|^{p/n_j}\), and the lower-bound and equality
  statements of \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality} hold in that
  prescribed fiber.  Natural-domain \(\det_r\) fibers outside such prescribed
  budget data are retained only as boundary obstructions.
  \item \emph{Finite non-zero skeletons.}  The algebraic nonnormal results
  record the finite Jordan-profile freedom left away from the spectral point
  zero.  The trace-class quasinilpotent zero sector is determinant-invisible;
  the paper discards it for the skeleton quotient rather than classifying it.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the central IEOT narrative is the trace-class determinant-fiber rigidity
of the cyclic model.  Schur stability, fixed-\(N\) Horn bodies,
prescribed-budget Schatten rigidity, and finite non-zero skeletons are
consequences or supporting supplements of that theorem chain, not independent
global classifications.

These limitations are mathematical rather than formal.  The article treats
trace-class Fredholm determinants and fixed-dimensional Horn consequences for
finite collapsed support.  It also treats prescribed finite-subset
cyclic-product \(\det_r\) fibers under the stated \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget.  It
does not assert classification of all natural-domain regularized fibers, of
determinant-invisible quasinilpotent zero sectors, a basis-free orbit-distance
modulus in infinite dimension, or an inverse-limit theory of Horn bodies.  Those
questions require extra invariants, topology, descent maps, or stabilization
data not present in a determinant germ.
\subsection{Novelty and imported tools}

The proof deliberately separates standard operator-theoretic inputs from
the new cyclic-divisor consequences.  The classical inputs are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item Fredholm and regularized determinant theory for trace and Schatten
  ideals, including continuity and the zero-divisor/eigenvalue
  correspondence;
  \item Weyl--Ky Fan majorization, used to turn determinant-forced
  eigenvalue data into trace and Schatten lower bounds;
  \item Horn's finite-dimensional converse to Weyl's multiplicative
  inequalities, used only to identify the exact singular-value body after the
  determinant has fixed the collapsed finite eigenvalue vector;
  \item Ringrose triangularization for compact operators, used only after a
  Schur gauge has been chosen; and
  \item the finite-dimensional closure theory of nilpotent conjugacy
  orbits, used to read the closure order of the algebraic skeleton
  quotient.
\end{enumerate}
The new contribution is the operator-theoretic structure forced by cyclic
divisors: the exact summability threshold, the canonical cyclic-block normal
realization at the threshold, the trace-norm minimum and equality-case rigidity in the
full trace-class determinant fiber, the forced finite-budget obstruction and
prescribed-budget \(\det_r\) rigidity theorem in the \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget
range, the square-root sharp
finite-dimensional trace-stability obstruction, and the finite-support
quotient of the nonnormal fiber obtained by discarding precisely the
trace-class quasinilpotent zero sector after the Riesz skeleton projection.
Thus the paper identifies what the classical Fredholm, Weyl--Ky Fan,
Weyl--Horn, Ringrose, and nilpotent-orbit tools force for the cyclic
zero-divisor realization problem.  In particular, after a Ringrose
triangularization has been chosen, the infinite-dimensional trace-distance
criterion requires separate Schur-compression and determinant-forced diagonal
tail control.  \Cref{thm:uniform-chosen-schur-tail-modulus} gives the
corresponding uniform criterion when such compatible tail data are supplied.

\subsection{Previous work}

The dynamical \(\zeta\)-function viewpoint goes back at least to
Artin--Mazur \cite{ArtinMazur1965}. In the symbolic finite-state setting,
the rationality of these \(\zeta\)-functions goes back to
Bowen--Lanford and Manning
\cite{BowenLanford1970Zeta,Manning1971Axiom}; see also
\cite{Ruelle1976ZetaExpanding,Ruelle1978Thermodynamic,ParryPollicott1990Zeta,LindMarcus1995,Kitchens1998}.
The necklace and Witt-vector algebra behind the M\"obius inversion formulas
used here is classical; see Metropolis--Rota \cite{MetropolisRota1983}.
The theory of Fredholm determinants for trace-class operators, and more
generally for operators in trace ideals, originates with Grothendieck
\cite{Grothendieck1956}, Simon's earlier account of infinite determinants
\cite{Simon1977InfiniteDeterminants}, the continuity-based treatment of
Gohberg--Goldberg--Krupnik
\cite{GohbergGoldbergKrupnik1996TracesDeterminants}, and the monograph of
Simon \cite{Simon2005TraceIdeals}; see also the non-self-adjoint
operator theory of Gohberg--Krein \cite{GohbergKrein1969NonSelfAdjoint}
and the determinant framework of
Gesztesy--Latushkin--Mitrea--Zinchenko
\cite{GesztesyLatushkinMitreaZinchenko2005}. Perturbation and zeta
determinants in adjacent settings are treated, for example, by
Gesztesy--Makarov \cite{GesztesyMakarov2007} and Hartmann--Lesch
\cite{HartmannLesch2018}. The product representation and zero-set
description for trace-class Fredholm determinants that underlie the
spectral-rigidity discussion are standard in
\cite{Simon1977InfiniteDeterminants,Simon2005TraceIdeals,
GohbergGoldbergKrupnik1996TracesDeterminants,
GesztesyLatushkinMitreaZinchenko2005};
for regularized determinant product formulas see also
Britz--Carey--Gesztesy--Nichols--Sukochev--Zanin
\cite{BritzCareyGesztesyNicholsSukochevZanin2021}.
The eigenvalue summability, trace formula, and eigenvalue/s-number
comparison underlying the global trace-norm theorem go back to Lidskii
\cite{Lidskii1959} and the eigenvalue inequalities of Weyl and Fan
\cite{Weyl1949EigenvalueInequalities,Fan1951MaximumProperties}; see also
\cite{Konig1986EigenvalueDistribution,GohbergGoldbergKaashoek1990ClassesI}
and \cite[Chapter~1]{Simon2005TraceIdeals}. Recent work on Schatten
perturbation trace formulas and determinant-adjacent spectral
invariants, including van Nuland--Skripka
\cite{VanNulandSkripka2022} and Chattopadhyay--Coine--Giri--Pradhan
\cite{ChattopadhyayCoineGiriPradhan2025}, is concerned with perturbation
expansions, higher-order differentiability, and trace formulas rather than
the fixed zero-divisor fibers and minimizer geometry considered here.
Golinskii's work
\cite{Golinskii2022} is another recent trace-ideal contribution; it is
different in focus from the present determinant-fiber rigidity problem and its
normal collapsed-multiset classification. The finite-support non-zero-skeleton
quotient
closure order uses the classical closure theorem for nilpotent conjugacy
classes, due to Gerstenhaber and Kraft--Procesi
\cite{Gerstenhaber1961Dominance,KraftProcesi1982Closures}. The
nonnormal and finite-dimensional instability phenomena discussed below
are related in spirit to spectral-instability themes such as those in
B{\"o}ttcher \cite{Bottcher2024}, but our quotient degeneration result
concerns fixed determinant fibers and trace-norm minimization. The
transfer-operator literature studies dynamical determinants whose zeros
and poles encode spectra of transfer operators on Banach or anisotropic
spaces. In the Axiom~A setting, Pollicott
\cite{Pollicott1986Meromorphic} and then Ruelle
\cite{Ruelle1990FredholmExtension} developed Fredholm determinant
formalisms for transfer operators, while Keller
\cite{Keller1989Fredholm} and Baladi--Keller
\cite{BaladiKeller1990ZetaTransfer} treated piecewise invertible and
piecewise monotone interval maps, Rugh
\cite{Rugh1996GeneralizedFredholm} treated Axiom~A systems, and
Baladi--Tsujii \cite{BaladiTsujii2008DynamicalDeterminants} treated smooth
hyperbolic diffeomorphisms on anisotropic spaces; see also Baladi's
monograph \cite{Baladi2018DynamicalZeta}. Those works begin with a given
dynamical system and a corresponding transfer operator, and then use the
resulting determinant or zeta function to study analytic continuation,
spectral data, orbit-growth asymptotics, or resonance structure. By
contrast, the present paper does not construct a transfer operator from
dynamics and does not pursue meromorphic continuation for a prescribed
system.  The dynamical literature is used here only as motivation and
context for Fredholm determinant products, not as an additional source of
twisted, cocycle, Peter--Weyl, or transfer-operator refinements.  Instead,
it fixes an abstract cyclic Euler product germ and asks
which trace-class operators can realize it, which normal model is forced
by that determinant, and how the entire determinant fiber behaves under
trace-norm minimization, equality-case rigidity, and spectral-rigidity
constraints.

\subsection{Organization}

\Cref{sec:preliminaries} recalls the finite-state \(\zeta\)-function,
proves the primitive-orbit identities used later as background, and
shows that nontrivial primitive symbolic Euler data fall outside the
trace-class summability threshold.
\Cref{sec:fredholm} contains the Fredholm--Witt bridge and the main
trace-class determinant-fiber theorem chain: cyclic-block realization, normal
classification by the collapsed multiset, full-fiber trace-norm minimality,
and equality rigidity.  The same section then records the further consequences:
chosen-Schur stability, fixed-dimensional Weyl--Horn geometry with the stated
gauge-minimizer taxonomy, controlled finite-subset prescribed-budget Schatten regularized determinants, and finite non-zero-skeleton algebraic quotient results.
\Cref{sec:conclusion} summarizes the resulting realization, variational,
and rigidity picture.
