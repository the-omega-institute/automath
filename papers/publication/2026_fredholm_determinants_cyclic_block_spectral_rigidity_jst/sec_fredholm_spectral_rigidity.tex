\section{Determinant-Fiber Rigidity and Consequences}\label{sec:trace-class-determinant-fiber-rigidity}

This section proves the core trace-class variational theorem for a fixed
Fredholm determinant and then derives the consequences used later in the
paper.  The logical order is
\[
  \begin{gathered}
  D_{\mathcal D}\longmapsto M_{\mathcal D}
  \longmapsto \textup{threshold/classification},\\
  M_{\mathcal D}\longmapsto \tau_{\mathcal D}
  \longmapsto \textup{normal minimizers}
  \longmapsto \textup{consequences},
  \end{gathered}
\]
with determinant-intrinsic independence of the chosen cyclic presentation.
The subsections follow this order and should be read as consequences of the
same packaged determinant-fiber statement, not as independent global
classification programs.
\Cref{sec:determinant-fiber-stability} is a stability consequence of the
trace-norm minimization theorem and a chosen triangular representative.
\Cref{sec:fixed-dimensional-weyl-horn-consequences} is a fixed-ambient-dimension
Horn consequence after the collapsed eigenvalue tuple has been fixed.
  \Cref{sec:schatten-prescribed-budget-rigidity} treats regularized
  determinants under a prescribed finite-subset cyclic product and finite
  \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget; its product-realized and determinant-equality
  classes are identified by
\Cref{prop:schatten-finite-budget-localization} and is not the full natural
\(\det_r\)-domain.
\Cref{sec:finite-nonzero-nonnormal-skeletons} retains only the finite non-zero
Riesz root-space skeleton; determinant-invisible trace-class quasinilpotent
zero-sector data are outside that finite algebraic quotient.  The final
corollary records this dependency pattern: threshold, collapsed classification,
trace minimum, and the subordinate stability, Horn, Schatten, and skeleton
consequences.

\subsection{Triangular and pinching inputs}

The first results in this subsection are interfaces to classical operator
and matrix theory, not new classification theorems of the present paper.  We
state them with the exact zero-padding, compactness, and Schatten hypotheses
needed later so that the subsequent determinant-fiber arguments can cite them
as imported tools.

The determinant rigidity lemma used here was stated before
\Cref{thm:normal-realizability-threshold}. In the next theorem, the lower
bound itself is the immediate combination of that lemma with the classical
Weyl--Ky Fan inequality; the additional content is the equality and
near-equality analysis, which shows how the trace-norm defect controls
the Schur off-diagonal entries of a triangular representative.

\begin{lemma}[Classical ordered Weyl--Ky Fan--Lidskii inequality]
  \label{lem:weyl-ky-fan-trace-class}
Let \(T\in\sone(H)\). Let \((\lambda_k(T))_{k\ge1}\) be the eigenvalues
of \(T\), repeated according to algebraic multiplicity and ordered so
that
\[
  \abs{\lambda_1(T)}\ge \abs{\lambda_2(T)}\ge \cdots,
\]
padding by zeros once the non-zero spectrum is exhausted. Let
\((s_k(T))_{k\ge1}\) be the singular values of \(T\), ordered so that
\[
  s_1(T)\ge s_2(T)\ge \cdots \ge 0.
\]
Then for every \(N\ge1\),
\[
  \sum_{k=1}^N \abs{\lambda_k(T)}
  \le \sum_{k=1}^N s_k(T).
\]
Consequently,
\[
  \sum_{k\ge1}\abs{\lambda_k(T)}\le \|T\|_1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the classical Weyl--Ky Fan majorization inequality for compact
operators, used here only in its partial-sum form for the non-zero eigenvalue
sequence with algebraic multiplicities and zero padding.  It is the compact
operator inequality of Weyl and Ky Fan
\cite{Weyl1949EigenvalueInequalities,Fan1951MaximumProperties}, in the exact
form for compact operators stated by Simon
\cite[Chapter~1, Theorem~1.15]{Simon2005TraceIdeals}: eigenvalues are listed
with algebraic multiplicities in non-increasing modulus, zeros are appended
after the non-zero spectrum when necessary, singular values are listed in
non-increasing order, and the asserted finite partial-sum inequality follows
for the exponent \(p=1\).  The same compact-operator interface is formulated
for eigenvalues and singular numbers in K\"onig
\cite[Chapter~2, Section~2.a]{Konig1986EigenvalueDistribution} and in the
standard \(s\)-number treatment
\cite[Chapter~VI, Section~1]{GohbergGoldbergKaashoek1990ClassesI}.  The
trace-class summability of the eigenvalue sequence is the corresponding
consequence of Lidskii's theorem \cite[p.~485]{Lidskii1959}.
Since \(T\in\sone(H)\), the
singular values are summable and
\(\sum_{k\ge1}s_k(T)=\|T\|_1\). Passing to the monotone limit in the
partial-sum inequality therefore yields the final claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Classical Schatten \texorpdfstring{\(p\)}{p}-sum Weyl--Fan inequality]
  \label{lem:weyl-fan-schatten-p-sum}
Let \(0<p<\infty\) and let \(T\in\mathcal S_p(H)\). Let
\((\lambda_k(T))_{k\ge1}\) be the eigenvalues of \(T\), repeated according
to algebraic multiplicity, ordered by non-increasing modulus, and padded by
zeros. Let \((s_k(T))_{k\ge1}\) be the singular values in non-increasing
order. Then, for every \(N\ge1\),
\[
  \sum_{k=1}^N\abs{\lambda_k(T)}^p
  \le
  \sum_{k=1}^N s_k(T)^p,
\]
and consequently
\[
  \sum_{k\ge1}\abs{\lambda_k(T)}^p\le \norm{T}_p^p .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  No part of this lemma is new; it records the cited Weyl--Horn and
  Weyl--Fan comparison theorem in the exact Schatten form needed later for
  determinant-fiber consequences.  In infinite dimension the statement is
  precisely Simon's compact-operator Weyl inequality
  \cite[Chapter~1, Theorem~1.15]{Simon2005TraceIdeals}, with eigenvalues
  counted algebraically, ordered by modulus, and zero-padded, and with singular
  values ordered decreasingly.  That theorem gives the displayed finite
  partial-sum inequality for every exponent \(p>0\); the assumption
  \(T\in\mathcal S_p\) then permits the monotone passage to the infinite sum.

  For reference, this is also the finite Weyl--Horn comparison behind the
  finite-dimensional Horn--Li--Mathias theorem used later, but no sufficiency
  part of that theorem is needed here.
  The finite-dimensional Weyl multiplicative majorization theorem says that,
  for each finite matrix and each \(1\le k\le N\), the product of the \(k\)
  largest eigenvalue moduli is at most the product of the \(k\) largest
  singular values; the terminal equality is equality of determinant moduli.
  This is the Weyl--Horn theorem in the form of Horn
  \cite[Theorem~1]{Horn1954DoublyStochastic} and Li--Mathias
  \cite[Theorem~1.1, p.~115]{LiMathias2001Construction}; for compact
  operators it is the Weyl inequality for eigenvalues and singular numbers,
  as recorded for example in K\"onig
  \cite[Chapter~2, Section~2.a]{Konig1986EigenvalueDistribution}, Simon
  \cite[Chapter~1, Theorem~1.15]{Simon2005TraceIdeals}, and
  Gohberg--Goldberg--Kaashoek
  \cite[Chapter~VI, Section~1]{GohbergGoldbergKaashoek1990ClassesI}.

  We spell out the finite-dimensional passage from multiplicative
  majorization to the stated
  \(p\)-sum form. For a finite matrix put
  \(x_k=\abs{\lambda_k}^p\) and \(y_k=s_k^p\), both arranged in decreasing
  order. Multiplicative majorization of \((\abs{\lambda_k})\) by
  \((s_k)\) is equivalent to weak log-majorization of \((x_k)\) by
  \((y_k)\). Ky Fan's dominance theorem applied to the convex increasing
  function \(t\mapsto e^t\) on logarithms, equivalently the standard fact
  that weak log-majorization implies weak majorization for non-negative
  decreasing vectors, gives
  \(\sum_{k=1}^N x_k\le\sum_{k=1}^N y_k\). For compact \(T\), use the cited
  compact Weyl inequality directly, with the same algebraic multiplicity and
  zero-padding conventions, and pass to the monotone limit over the non-zero
  eigenvalues. Since \(T\in\mathcal S_p\),
  \(\sum_k s_k(T)^p=\norm{T}_p^p<\infty\), and the asserted infinite-sum
  inequality follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Ordered non-zero root-space flag]
  \label{lem:ordered-nonzero-root-flag}
  Let \(K\) be a compact operator on a separable Hilbert space, and let
  \((\mu_q)_{q\in Q}\), where \(Q=\{1,\ldots,m\}\) or \(Q=\NN\), be any
  ordering of the non-zero eigenvalues of \(K\), each repeated according
  to algebraic multiplicity. Then there is an increasing family
  \[
    0=E_0\subset E_1\subset E_2\subset\cdots
  \]
  of finite-dimensional \(K\)-invariant subspaces, indexed by \(q\in Q\),
  such that \(\dim E_q=q\) and the induced operator on
  \(E_q/E_{q-1}\) is multiplication by \(\mu_q\). If
  \(E:=\overline{\bigcup_q E_q}\), then \(E\) contains every non-zero
  Riesz root space of \(K\), and the compact operator induced by \(K\) on
  the quotient space \(H/E\) has no non-zero eigenvalues.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  For each non-zero eigenvalue \(\lambda\), let \(G_\lambda\) be the
  Riesz root space of \(K\). This space is finite-dimensional, invariant,
  and has dimension equal to the algebraic multiplicity of \(\lambda\).
  Choose once and for all a complete invariant flag
  \[
    0=G_{\lambda,0}\subset G_{\lambda,1}\subset\cdots
    \subset G_{\lambda,m_{\mathrm{alg}}(\lambda)}=G_\lambda
  \]
  for \(K|_{G_\lambda}\). Each one-dimensional quotient
  \(G_{\lambda,r}/G_{\lambda,r-1}\) carries multiplication by
  \(\lambda\).

  For \(q\in Q\), let \(r_\lambda(q)\) be the number of occurrences of
  \(\lambda\) among \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_q\), and define
  \[
    E_q:=
    \bigoplus_{\lambda\in\{\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_q\}}
      G_{\lambda,r_\lambda(q)} .
  \]
  Only finitely many eigenvalues occur in this sum. The Riesz root spaces
  for distinct non-zero eigenvalues are linearly independent, so \(E_q\)
  has dimension \(q\). Each summand is invariant, hence \(E_q\) is
  invariant. Passing from \(E_{q-1}\) to \(E_q\) adds precisely the next
  one-dimensional quotient in the flag for \(G_{\mu_q}\), and therefore
  the quotient diagonal entry is \(\mu_q\).

  The closure \(E=\overline{\bigcup_q E_q}\) contains every
  \(G_\lambda\), because the sequence \((\mu_q)\) lists all non-zero
  eigenvalues with their full algebraic multiplicities. Let \(\widehat K\)
  denote the operator induced by \(K\) on \(H/E\). If
  \(\lambda\ne0\), the Riesz projection of \(\widehat K\) at \(\lambda\)
  is the quotient induced by the Riesz projection of \(K\) at \(\lambda\);
  the algebraic range of the latter projection is \(G_\lambda\subset E\).
  Hence the quotient
  projection is zero, so \(\lambda\) is not a characteristic value of
  \(\widehat K\). Thus \(\widehat K\) has no non-zero eigenvalues.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Prescribed-order compact Ringrose triangularization]
  \label{lem:compact-prescribed-ringrose-triangularization}
  Let \(K\) be a compact operator on a separable complex Hilbert space. Let
  \((\mu_q)_{q\in Q}\), where \(Q=\{1,
  \ldots,m\}\) or \(Q=\NN\), be any prescribed ordering of the non-zero
  eigenvalues of \(K\), each repeated according to algebraic multiplicity.
  Then there is an orthonormal triangular representation of \(K\), with
  diagonal sequence \((d_k)\), such that the non-zero subsequence of
  \((d_k)\) is exactly \((\mu_q)_{q\in Q}\) in the prescribed order, and
  every diagonal entry not belonging to this subsequence is zero. In the
  finite-dimensional case the same statement is read with a finite
  orthonormal basis. If \(K\) has no non-zero eigenvalues, the prescribed
  list is empty and the assertion says that all diagonal entries are zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The only external input is Ringrose's compact maximal-chain theorem
  \cite[Chapter~1, Theorem~3, pp.~6--8]{Ringrose1971CompactNonSelfAdjoint}.
  The prescribed order is obtained before that theorem is invoked.

  If the prescribed list is empty, take \(E=0\). Then \(K\) itself has no
  non-zero eigenvalues, and there are no prescribed finite-dimensional
  quotients. Otherwise, apply \Cref{lem:ordered-nonzero-root-flag} to the
  prescribed list \((\mu_q)\). Thus there is an increasing invariant flag
  \[
    0=E_0\subset E_1\subset E_2\subset\cdots
  \]
  with \(\dim E_q=q\) and with \(K\) acting on
  \(E_q/E_{q-1}\) as multiplication by \(\mu_q\). Let
  \(E:=\overline{\bigcup_q E_q}\). The same lemma says that \(E\)
  contains every non-zero Riesz root space of \(K\), and that the induced
  compact operator \(\widehat K\) on \(H/E\) has no non-zero eigenvalues.

  Consider the chain consisting of the spaces \(E_q\) when they exist,
  together with \(E\) and \(H\). By Zorn's lemma, extend it to a maximal
  chain of closed \(K\)-invariant subspaces. Ringrose's theorem applied to
  this maximal chain gives an orthonormal basis adapted to the chain in
  which \(K\) is upper triangular. When the prescribed list is non-empty,
  \(E_q/E_{q-1}\) is one-dimensional, so no closed subspace can lie
  strictly between \(E_{q-1}\) and \(E_q\). Hence every maximal chain
  containing the flag has these as consecutive quotients. If \(e_q\) is
  the basis vector representing the quotient \(E_q/E_{q-1}\), then
  \[
    Ke_q-\mu_q e_q\in E_{q-1},
  \]
  and therefore the corresponding diagonal entry is
  \(\langle Ke_q,e_q\rangle=\mu_q\). These quotients occur in the same
  order as the nested flag, so the diagonal entries contributed below
  \(E\) are precisely \(\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots\), with no extra entries between
  successive terms of the list. If the prescribed list is empty, this
  below-\(E\) contribution is empty. If the prescribed list is infinite,
  the corresponding quotient vectors form an orthonormal basis of
  \(E\), because \(E=\overline{\bigcup_q E_q}\); hence no additional
  diagonal entries occur inside \(E\).

  It remains to identify the diagonal entries contributed by the part of
  the chain above \(E\). Since every member of the maximal chain is
  comparable with \(E\), the members that contain \(E\) descend to a
  maximal invariant chain for the quotient operator \(\widehat K\) on
  \(H/E\). The diagonal entries of this quotient part are exactly the
  remaining diagonal entries of the triangular representation of \(K\).
  Ringrose's theorem identifies the non-zero diagonal entries in a compact
  triangular representation with the non-zero characteristic values,
  counted according to algebraic multiplicity. But \(\widehat K\) has no
  non-zero eigenvalues. Consequently every diagonal entry coming from the
  quotient part \(H/E\) is zero.

  Combining the two pieces, the non-zero diagonal subsequence is exactly
  the prescribed algebraic list \((\mu_q)\), and all remaining diagonal
  entries are zero. This proves the asserted prescribed-order
  triangularization; it does not prescribe the positions occupied by the
  zero entries and it makes no classification statement about the
  quasinilpotent part at the spectral point \(0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Classical Ringrose triangularization in Schatten ideals]
  \label{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal}
  Let \(1\le p<\infty\) and let \(T\in\mathcal S_p(H)\). After replacing \(H\)
  by the reducing subspace
  \[
    H_T:=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{u_r,v_r:r\ge1\}
  \]
  coming from any singular-value expansion
  \(T=\sum_{r\ge1}s_r(T)\langle\,\cdot\,,u_r\rangle v_r\), the operator
  acts on a separable Hilbert space and vanishes on \(H_T^\perp\).
  On this separable reducing part there is an orthonormal basis
  \((e_k)_{k\ge1}\) in which the matrix of \(T\) is upper triangular. The
  non-zero diagonal entries may be chosen so that their non-zero
  subsequence occurs in any prescribed ordering of the non-zero
  eigenvalues of \(T\), each repeated according to algebraic multiplicity.
  Every other diagonal entry is zero. Equivalently, the diagonal sequence
  \((d_k)_{k\ge1}\) has non-zero subsequence equal to the prescribed
  ordering of the non-zero algebraic eigenvalue multiset of \(T\), and all
  entries outside that subsequence are zero. In particular,
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge1}\abs{d_k}^p
    =
    \sum_{\lambda\in\spec(T)\setminus\{0\}}
      \abs{\lambda}^p\,m_{\mathrm{alg}}(\lambda)
    <\infty .
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The separable reduction follows directly from the singular-value
  expansion: both \(H_T\) and \(H_T^\perp\) reduce \(T\), and
  \(T|_{H_T^\perp}=0\). We therefore work on \(H_T\). The compact
  triangularization with the prescribed non-zero diagonal subsequence and
  zero remaining diagonal entries is exactly
  \Cref{lem:compact-prescribed-ringrose-triangularization} applied to
  \(T|_{H_T}\). The Schatten assumption is not part of the Ringrose input;
  it enters only to make the diagonal \(p\)-sum finite and to justify the
  later Schatten pinching arguments. No convergence of Schur truncations is
  imported from Ringrose: the later arguments use finite coordinate
  compressions inside the original operator \(T\in\mathcal S_p\), together
  with the diagonal \(p\)-summability obtained below.

  Denote the prescribed non-zero algebraic eigenvalue ordering by
  \((\mu_q)\). Because every diagonal entry outside the prescribed
  non-zero algebraic eigenvalue list is zero, the equality of diagonal
  sums is term-by-term:
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge1}\abs{d_k}^p
    =
    \sum_q \abs{\mu_q}^p
    =
    \sum_{\lambda\in\spec(T)\setminus\{0\}}
      \abs{\lambda}^p\,m_{\mathrm{alg}}(\lambda).
  \]
  The final finiteness follows from \Cref{lem:weyl-fan-schatten-p-sum},
  \[
    \sum_{\lambda\in\spec(T)\setminus\{0\}}
      \abs{\lambda}^p\,m_{\mathrm{alg}}(\lambda)
    \le
    \sum_{k\ge1}s_k(T)^p
    =
    \norm{T}_p^p<\infty,
  \]
  with eigenvalues repeated according to algebraic multiplicity and padded
  by zeros.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Two-by-two trace-norm pinching identity]
  \label{lem:two-by-two-trace-norm-pinching}
  For \(a,b,c\in\CC\), let
  \[
    B=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\0&b\end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  Then
  \[
    \|B\|_1^2=(\abs{a}+\abs{b})^2+\abs{c}^2.
  \]
  In particular,
  \[
    \|B\|_1\ge \abs{a}+\abs{b},
  \]
  and equality holds if and only if \(c=0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Let \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2\ge0\) be the singular values of \(B\). Then
  \[
    \|B\|_1^2=(\sigma_1+\sigma_2)^2
    =\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2+2\sigma_1\sigma_2.
  \]
  The squared singular values are the eigenvalues of \(B^*B\), so
  \[
    \sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2=\Tr(B^*B)
    =\abs{a}^2+\abs{b}^2+\abs{c}^2,
  \]
  and
  \[
    \sigma_1\sigma_2=\sqrt{\det(B^*B)}
    =\abs{\det B}
    =\abs{ab}.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    \|B\|_1^2
    =\abs{a}^2+\abs{b}^2+\abs{c}^2+2\abs{a}\,\abs{b}
    =(\abs{a}+\abs{b})^2+\abs{c}^2.
  \]
  The equality characterization follows immediately, since the square
  root is strictly increasing on \([0,\infty)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Two-by-two Schatten pinching rigidity]
  \label{lem:two-by-two-schatten-pinching}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\). For \(a,b,c\in\CC\), put
  \[
    B=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\0&b\end{pmatrix}
  \]
  and define
  \[
    \Phi_p(a,b,c):=\|B\|_p^p-\abs a^p-\abs b^p .
  \]
  Then
  \[
    \Phi_p(a,b,c)\ge0,
  \]
  Equality holds if and only if \(c=0\). For fixed \(a,b\), if
  \[
    \Phi_p(a,b,c_\nu)\longrightarrow0,
  \]
  then \(c_\nu\to0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Let \(\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge0\) be the singular values of \(B\), and let
  \(\mu_1\ge\mu_2\ge0\) be the decreasing rearrangement of
  \(\abs a,\abs b\). The two eigenvalues of \(B\) are \(a\) and \(b\), so
  Weyl--Ky Fan majorization gives
  \[
    (\mu_1,\mu_2)\prec_w(\sigma_1,\sigma_2).
  \]
  Since \(t\mapsto t^p\) is increasing and convex on
  \([0,\infty)\), weak majorization implies
  \[
    \mu_1^p+\mu_2^p\le \sigma_1^p+\sigma_2^p=\|B\|_p^p.
  \]
  This is exactly \(\Phi_p(a,b,c)\ge0\).

  It remains to identify the equality case by a direct \(2\times2\)
  computation.  Write \(x_1\ge x_2\) for the decreasing rearrangement of
  \((|a|,|b|)\).  The singular-value product and Hilbert--Schmidt identities
  for this block are
  \[
    \sigma_1\sigma_2
    =\sqrt{\det(B^*B)}
    =\abs{\det B}
    =\abs{ab}
    =x_1x_2
  \]
  and
  \[
    \sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2
    =\Tr(B^*B)
    =\abs a^2+\abs b^2+\abs c^2
    =x_1^2+x_2^2+\abs c^2 .
  \]
  If \(x_1x_2=0\), then \(\sigma_2=0\) and
  \(\sigma_1^2=x_1^2+\abs c^2\).  Hence \(c\ne0\) gives
  \(\sigma_1>x_1\), and therefore
  \(\|B\|_p^p=\sigma_1^p>x_1^p+x_2^p=\abs a^p+\abs b^p\).
  If \(x_1x_2>0\), put \(P=x_1x_2=\sigma_1\sigma_2\).  There are
  \(u,v\ge0\) such that
  \[
    \sigma_1=\sqrt P e^u,
    \qquad
    \sigma_2=\sqrt P e^{-u},
    \qquad
    x_1=\sqrt P e^v,
    \qquad
    x_2=\sqrt P e^{-v}.
  \]
  The squared-sum identity says
  \[
    P(e^{2u}+e^{-2u})
    =P(e^{2v}+e^{-2v})+\abs c^2.
  \]
  Since \(t\mapsto e^{2t}+e^{-2t}
  =2\cosh(2t)\) is strictly increasing on \([0,\infty)\), the last display
  forces \(u>v\) whenever \(c\ne0\).  The function
  \[
    t\longmapsto P^{p/2}(e^{pt}+e^{-pt})
  \]
  is strictly increasing for \(t>0\).  If \(c\ne0\), then either
  \(v>0\), giving strict increase directly from \(u>v\), or \(v=0\), in
  which case \(u>0\) and the same strict increase gives
  \[
    \|B\|_p^p
    =P^{p/2}(e^{pu}+e^{-pu})
    >P^{p/2}(e^{pv}+e^{-pv})
    =x_1^p+x_2^p
    =\abs a^p+\abs b^p .
  \]
  Thus equality in \(\Phi_p(a,b,c)\ge0\) forces \(c=0\). The converse is
  immediate because \(B\) is then diagonal.

  For the final assertion, \(\Phi_p(a,b,c_\nu)\to0\) makes
  \(\|B(a,b,c_\nu)\|_p^p\) bounded.  In finite dimension this bounds
  \((c_\nu)\).  Every convergent subsequence has a limit \(c\), and
  continuity of singular values gives \(\Phi_p(a,b,c)=0\); the equality
  case just proved gives \(c=0\).  Thus the only subsequential limit is
  zero, and \(c_\nu\to0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform two-by-two Schatten pinching modulus]
  \label{lem:uniform-two-by-two-schatten-modulus}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\), let \(E\subset\CC\) be finite, and let \(R>0\). For
  \(a,b,c\in\CC\), set
  \[
    B(a,b,c):=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\0&b\end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    \Phi_p(a,b,c):=\|B(a,b,c)\|_p^p-\abs a^p-\abs b^p .
  \]
  Define
  \[
    \Theta_{p,E,R}(\varepsilon)
    :=
    \sup\{\abs c:
      a,b\in E,
      \|B(a,b,c)\|_p\le R,
      \Phi_p(a,b,c)\le\varepsilon\},
  \]
  with the convention that the supremum of the empty set is \(0\). Then
  \(\Theta_{p,E,R}\) is a finite nondecreasing function on
  \([0,\infty)\) and
  \[
    \Theta_{p,E,R}(\varepsilon)\downarrow0
    \qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
  \]
  Consequently, whenever \(a,b\in E\), \(\|B(a,b,c)\|_p\le R\), and
  \(\Phi_p(a,b,c)\le\varepsilon\), one has
  \(\abs c\le\Theta_{p,E,R}(\varepsilon)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The function is nondecreasing because the defining set increases with
  \(\varepsilon\). It is finite since each admissible matrix satisfies
  \(\abs c\le \|B(a,b,c)\|_2\), while finite rank norm comparison gives
  \[
    \|B(a,b,c)\|_2
    \le 2^{\max\{1/2-1/p,0\}}\|B(a,b,c)\|_p
    \le 2^{\max\{1/2-1/p,0\}}R .
  \]

  Suppose that \(\Theta_{p,E,R}(\varepsilon)\) does not tend to \(0\) as
  \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Then there are \(\rho>0\), numbers
  \(\varepsilon_\nu\downarrow0\), and triples
  \((a_\nu,b_\nu,c_\nu)\) with \(a_\nu,b_\nu\in E\),
  \(\|B(a_\nu,b_\nu,c_\nu)\|_p\le R\),
  \(\Phi_p(a_\nu,b_\nu,c_\nu)\le\varepsilon_\nu\), and
  \(\abs{c_\nu}\ge\rho\). Passing to a subsequence, the finiteness of
  \(E\) makes \(a_\nu=a\) and \(b_\nu=b\) constant. The preceding bound on
  \(c_\nu\) gives a further subsequence with \(c_\nu\to c\). Continuity of
  singular values gives \(\Phi_p(a,b,c)=0\), so
  \Cref{lem:two-by-two-schatten-pinching} implies \(c=0\), contradicting
  \(\abs c\ge\rho\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Standard trace-norm convergence of Schur compressions]
  \label{lem:trace-norm-schur-compressions}
  Let \(T\in\sone(H)\), and let \((P_N)_{N\ge1}\) be finite-rank
  orthogonal projections with \(P_N\to I\) strongly. Then
  \[
    P_NTP_N\longrightarrow T
    \qquad\text{in }\sone\text{-norm}.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Fix \(\varepsilon>0\), and choose a finite-rank operator \(F\) with
  \(\|T-F\|_1<\varepsilon\). Since multiplication by contractions is
  contractive on \(\sone\),
  \[
    \|T-P_NTP_N\|_1
    \le 2\|T-F\|_1+\|F-P_NFP_N\|_1.
  \]
  It remains to treat \(F\). Write \(F=\sum_{\ell=1}^r x_\ell\otimes
  y_\ell\), where \((x\otimes y)h=\langle h,y\rangle x\). For rank-one
  operators, \(\|x\otimes y\|_1=\|x\|\,\|y\|\), and therefore
  \[
    \|x_\ell\otimes y_\ell-P_Nx_\ell\otimes P_Ny_\ell\|_1
    \le \|x_\ell-P_Nx_\ell\|\,\|y_\ell\|
      +\|P_Nx_\ell\|\,\|y_\ell-P_Ny_\ell\|.
  \]
  Strong convergence \(P_N\to I\) makes the right-hand side tend to zero
  for each \(\ell\). Hence \(P_NFP_N\to F\) in trace norm.  The displayed
  estimate gives \(\limsup_N\|T-P_NTP_N\|_1\le 2\varepsilon\). Since
  \(\varepsilon\) is arbitrary, the claim follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Standard Schatten-norm convergence of Schur compressions]
  \label{lem:schatten-schur-compressions}
  Let \(1\le p<\infty\), let \(T\in\mathcal S_p(H)\), and let
  \((P_N)_{N\ge1}\) be finite-rank orthogonal projections with
  \(P_N\to I\) strongly. Then
  \[
    P_NTP_N\longrightarrow T
    \qquad\text{in }\mathcal S_p\text{-norm}.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Use the same finite-rank approximation argument as in
  \Cref{lem:trace-norm-schur-compressions}. Given \(\varepsilon>0\),
  choose a finite-rank operator \(F\) with \(\|T-F\|_p<\varepsilon\).
  Multiplication by contractions is contractive on every Schatten class,
  and hence
  \[
    \|T-P_NTP_N\|_p
    \le 2\|T-F\|_p+\|F-P_NFP_N\|_p.
  \]
  Writing \(F\) as a finite sum of rank-one operators
  \(x_\ell\otimes y_\ell\), and using
  \(\|x\otimes y\|_p=\|x\|\,\|y\|\), strong convergence of \(P_N\) gives
  \(P_NFP_N\to F\) in \(\mathcal S_p\)-norm. Letting
  \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Spectral rigidity for regularized determinants]
  \label{lem:regularized-determinant-spectral-rigidity}
  Let \(1\le r<\infty\), and let \(T_1,T_2\in\mathcal S_r\). If
  \[
    \det_r(I-zT_1)=\det_r(I-zT_2)
  \]
  as entire functions, then the non-zero spectra of \(T_1\) and \(T_2\)
  coincide with algebraic multiplicity.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  For finite-rank approximants the regularized determinant is the ordinary
  Fredholm determinant multiplied by the Simon--Gohberg--Krein regularizing
  exponential recorded in
  \Cref{prop:fredholm-regularized-zero-divisor-correspondence}.  That
  exponential is zero-free on \(\CC\), and the cited continuity and
  zero-multiplicity theorem passes this divisor statement to Schatten-class
  limits. Hence the zero divisor of \(\det_r(I-zT_i)\) is exactly the divisor
  formed by the reciprocals of the non-zero eigenvalues of \(T_i\), with
  algebraic multiplicity. Equality of the two entire functions therefore gives
  equality, with order, of their zero divisors, and hence equality of the two
  non-zero spectral multisets.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Trace-class triangular equality and entry pinching]
  \label{lem:trace-triangular-equality-pinching}
  Let \(T\in\sone(H)\), and choose a Ringrose basis from
  \Cref{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal}. Write \(A=[a_{ij}]\) and let
  \(d_k=a_{kk}\). Suppose that
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge1}\abs{d_k}=\norm{T}_1.
  \]
  For \(P_N\) the projection onto
  \(\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_N\}\), put \(A_N=P_NTP_N\) and
  \[
    \delta_N:=\norm{A_N}_1-\sum_{k=1}^N\abs{d_k}.
  \]
  Then \(\delta_N\ge0\), \(\delta_N\to0\), and for every
  \(1\le i<j\le N\),
  \[
    \abs{a_{ij}}^2\le 2\norm{A_N}_1\,\delta_N.
  \]
  Consequently every strict Schur entry \(a_{ij}\), \(i<j\), vanishes.
  Thus equality between the trace norm and the absolute sum of the
  Ringrose diagonal forces the triangular representative to be diagonal.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The projections \(P_N\) are the initial-segment projections of the chosen
  Ringrose basis, and therefore \(P_N\to I\) strongly.  Hence
  \Cref{lem:trace-norm-schur-compressions} applies to this particular Schur
  basis; no unconditional-basis or truncation invariance is being used.
  The compression \(A_N\) is upper triangular with diagonal
  \(d_1,\ldots,d_N\). The finite-dimensional Weyl--Ky Fan inequality gives
  \(\sum_{k=1}^N\abs{d_k}\le\norm{A_N}_1\), hence \(\delta_N\ge0\).
  The convergence \(P_NTP_N\to T\) in trace norm, recorded in
  \Cref{lem:trace-norm-schur-compressions}, gives
  \(\norm{A_N}_1\to\norm{T}_1\). Since the assumed diagonal identity is the
  absolute convergence statement
  \(\sum_{k\ge1}\abs{d_k}=\norm{T}_1\), the partial diagonal sums converge
  to the same limit. Therefore \(\delta_N\to0\).

  Fix \(i<j\le N\). Let \(R_{ij}\) be the projection onto
  \(\operatorname{span}\{e_i,e_j\}\), let \(S_{ij}=P_N-R_{ij}\), and set
  \(U_{ij}=R_{ij}-S_{ij}\) on \(P_NH\). The identity
  \[
    R_{ij}A_NR_{ij}\oplus S_{ij}A_NS_{ij}
    =\frac12(A_N+U_{ij}A_NU_{ij})
  \]
  is a block-pinching identity. Convexity and unitary invariance of the
  trace norm give
  \[
    \norm{A_N}_1
    \ge \norm{R_{ij}A_NR_{ij}}_1+\norm{S_{ij}A_NS_{ij}}_1.
  \]
  The second block is upper triangular with diagonal entries
  \(d_k\), \(k\ne i,j\), so the same finite-dimensional lower bound gives
  \[
    \delta_N\ge
    \left\|
      \begin{pmatrix}d_i&a_{ij}\\0&d_j\end{pmatrix}
    \right\|_1-\abs{d_i}-\abs{d_j}.
  \]
  Applying \Cref{lem:two-by-two-trace-norm-pinching} to the displayed
  \(2\times2\) block, and using the preceding lower bound, yields
  \[
    \abs{a_{ij}}^2
    \le 2\norm{A_N}_1\delta_N .
  \]
  For fixed \(i<j\), the estimate holds for every \(N\ge j\).  The sequence
  \((\norm{A_N}_1)_N\) is bounded because it converges to \(\norm{T}_1\),
  while \(\delta_N\to0\).  Letting \(N\to\infty\) therefore forces
  \(a_{ij}=0\).  Since this applies to each strict Schur entry separately,
  the triangular matrix has no strict upper-triangular part.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Zero-sector Schur elimination in an equality fiber]
  \label{lem:zero-sector-schur-elimination-equality-fiber}
  Let \(T\in\sone(H)\), and suppose that
  \[
    \|T\|_1=
    \sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(T)\setminus\{0\}}
      \abs{\lambda}\,m_a(\lambda),
  \]
  where \(m_a(\lambda)\) denotes algebraic multiplicity. After discarding the
  zero reducing summand coming from a singular-value expansion, choose the
  Ringrose basis of \Cref{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal} for any prescribed
  ordering of the non-zero eigenvalues and complete it through the quotient on
  which the only spectral value is \(0\). If \(A=[a_{ij}]\) is the Schur matrix
  of \(T\) in this completed basis, then \(a_{ij}=0\) for every \(i<j\). In
  particular, equality in the trace lower bound eliminates Schur entries inside
  the non-zero root-space flag, inside the zero spectral sector, and across the
  interface between the two.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Choose a singular-value expansion
  \[
    T=\sum_{r\ge1}s_r(T)\,\langle\,\cdot\,,u_r\rangle v_r
  \]
  and set \(H_T=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{u_r,v_r:r\ge1\}\). Then \(H_T\) is
  separable, reduces \(T\), and \(T\) vanishes on \(H_T^\perp\): both \(T\) and
  \(T^*\) are generated by the displayed singular-value expansion. Passing from
  \(H\) to this reducing subspace preserves the non-zero algebraic spectrum and
  the trace norm, so it is harmless for the equality question.

  On the separable reducing subspace, \Cref{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal}
  first triangularizes the ordered non-zero root-space flag and then completes
  the orthonormal basis by triangularizing the quotient on which no non-zero
  eigenvalue remains. The resulting Schur diagonal contains exactly the
  non-zero eigenvalues, with algebraic multiplicity, together with zero
  diagonal entries from the quotient. Hence the assumed equality is precisely
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge1}\abs{a_{kk}}=\norm{T}_1 .
  \]
  Let \(P_N\) be the initial-segment projections of this completed Ringrose
  basis. Since it is a genuine orthonormal basis of the separable reducing
  subspace, \(P_N\to I\) strongly.  Thus the initial-segment compressions are
  exactly the compressions covered by
  \Cref{lem:trace-norm-schur-compressions}, so \(P_NTP_N\to T\) in trace norm.
  Therefore the finite Schur defects
  \[
    \delta_N=\norm{P_NTP_N}_1-
      \sum_{k=1}^N\abs{a_{kk}}
  \]
  are non-negative and tend to zero, by the finite-dimensional Weyl--Ky Fan
  lower bound applied to the triangular compression and by the absolute
  convergence of the displayed diagonal sum.

  Fix any pair \(i<j\), with no restriction on whether the corresponding
  diagonal entries lie in the non-zero root-space flag, in the zero quotient,
  or on opposite sides of that interface. For every \(N\ge j\), pinch
  \(P_NTP_N\) to the coordinate plane spanned by the \(i\)-th and \(j\)-th basis
  vectors. The proof of \Cref{lem:trace-triangular-equality-pinching}, using
  \Cref{lem:two-by-two-trace-norm-pinching}, applies to all three cases and gives
  \[
    \abs{a_{ij}}^2\le 2\norm{P_NTP_N}_1\delta_N .
  \]
  The norms \(\norm{P_NTP_N}_1\) converge to \(\norm{T}_1\), while
  \(\delta_N\to0\). Letting \(N\to\infty\) gives \(a_{ij}=0\). Since the pair was
  arbitrary in the completed Ringrose basis, the argument eliminates the strict
  Schur entries in the non-zero sector, in the zero quotient sector, and across
  their boundary.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Schatten triangular equality and entry pinching]
  \label{lem:schatten-triangular-equality-pinching}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\), let \(T\in\mathcal S_p(H)\), and choose a Ringrose
  basis from \Cref{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal}. Write
  \(A=[a_{ij}]\) and \(d_k=a_{kk}\). Suppose that
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge1}\abs{d_k}^p=\norm{T}_p^p.
  \]
  For \(P_N\) the projection onto
  \(\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_N\}\), put \(A_N=P_NTP_N\) and
  \[
    \Delta_{N,p}:=\norm{A_N}_p^p-\sum_{k=1}^N\abs{d_k}^p.
  \]
  Then \(\Delta_{N,p}\ge0\), \(\Delta_{N,p}\to0\), and for every
  \(1\le i<j\le N\),
  \[
    \left\|
      \begin{pmatrix}d_i&a_{ij}\\0&d_j\end{pmatrix}
    \right\|_p^p-\abs{d_i}^p-\abs{d_j}^p
    \le \Delta_{N,p}.
  \]
  Consequently every strict Schur entry \(a_{ij}\), \(i<j\), vanishes.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  This is the Schatten analogue of
  \Cref{lem:trace-triangular-equality-pinching}. Weyl--Ky Fan, applied to
  the finite-dimensional upper triangular compression \(A_N\), gives
  \(\Delta_{N,p}\ge0\), and
  \Cref{lem:schatten-schur-compressions} plus the assumed equality gives
  \(\Delta_{N,p}\to0\).

  For fixed \(i<j\le N\), use the same projections
  \(R_{ij}\), \(S_{ij}\), and \(U_{ij}=R_{ij}-S_{ij}\). Convexity of
  \(X\mapsto\norm{X}_p^p\), unitary invariance, and the block-pinching
  identity give
  \[
    \norm{A_N}_p^p
    \ge \norm{R_{ij}A_NR_{ij}}_p^p+\norm{S_{ij}A_NS_{ij}}_p^p.
  \]
  Applying the finite-dimensional eigenvalue--singular-value inequality
  to the second block leaves exactly the displayed two-by-two defect
  bound. By the elementary \(2\times2\) rigidity in
  \Cref{lem:two-by-two-schatten-pinching}---proved from the singular-value
  product and Hilbert--Schmidt identities, rather than from a general
  equality case for weak majorization---the left-hand side is nonnegative
  and can tend to zero for fixed \(d_i,d_j\) only when \(a_{ij}\to0\).
  Since \(\Delta_{N,p}\to0\), each fixed strict Schur entry is zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Notation for determinant fibers and collapsed spectra}

We collect the collapsed determinant-fiber notation used in the main
trace-class rigidity theorem and in the finite-dimensional consequences.
For cyclic data
\(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) satisfying the trace-class
summability hypothesis, \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities} gives the
following two displayed definitions; the subscript \(\mathcal D\) is part of the
notation in both lines:
\[
  M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
  =\sum_{\{j:\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\}}m_j,
  \qquad
  \Sigma_{\mathcal D}=\{\lambda\in\CC^\times:
  M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)>0\}.
\]
The trace and Schatten budgets are
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \tau_{\mathcal D}
  &:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
    \abs{\lambda}\, M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j},\\
  \tau_{\mathcal D,p}
  &:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
    \abs{\lambda}^{p}\, M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\]
The displayed equality for \(\tau_{\mathcal D}\) uses only the elementary fact
that every solution of \(\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\) has modulus
\(\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}\), counted with its multiplicity \(m_j\).
When \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) is finite, put
\[
  d_{\mathcal D}:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
    M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda).
\]
For \(N\ge d_{\mathcal D}\), let \(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N}\) be any
\(N\)-tuple obtained by listing each \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) with
multiplicity \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\) and adjoining
\(N-d_{\mathcal D}\) zeros.  Let
\(a_{\mathcal D,N}=(a_1,\ldots,a_N)\), \(a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_N\ge0\), be the
decreasing rearrangement of \((\abs\mu)_{\mu\in\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N}}\).
Finally,
\[
\begin{split}
  \operatorname{HWH}(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N})
  :=\{\,s_1\ge\cdots\ge s_N\ge0:
  &\ \prod_{i=1}^k a_i\le\prod_{i=1}^k s_i\quad(1\le k\le N),\\
  &\ \prod_{i=1}^N a_i=\prod_{i=1}^N s_i\,\}.
\end{split}
\]
Products containing a zero factor are read as zero.  Thus terminal zero
padding is part of the fixed-dimensional data, and
\(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}=\sum_{i=1}^N a_i^p\) for every \(N\ge d_{\mathcal D}\).

\subsection{Trace-class determinant-fiber minimization}

For ease of verification, the proof of the next theorem uses the following
dependency chain and no hidden compact-operator input. The determinant germ is
converted to the collapsed non-zero algebraic spectrum by
\Cref{lem:fredholm-spectral-rigidity,prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}.
The trace-norm lower bound is then only the classical ordered Weyl--Ky Fan
inequality of \Cref{lem:weyl-ky-fan-trace-class}. The equality case is the new
pinching step: \Cref{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal} supplies a Schur gauge,
\Cref{lem:trace-norm-schur-compressions} justifies passage from finite Schur
compressions back to the trace-class operator, and
\Cref{lem:trace-triangular-equality-pinching,lem:zero-sector-schur-elimination-equality-fiber}
eliminate the strict upper-triangular part. Finally the normal classification
already proved in \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization} identifies the diagonal
normal minimizer with the cyclic model, up to zero summands.

\begin{theorem}[Global trace-norm minimum and minimizer classification in a Fredholm-determinant fiber]
  \label{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}
  Let \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) satisfy the
  summability hypothesis of \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization}, and
  write
  \[
    D_{\mathcal D}(z)
    :=\prod_{j\in J}(1-\beta_j z^{n_j})^{m_j}.
  \]
  Let \(M_{\mathcal D}\) and \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) be the collapsed
  multiplicity function and its support from
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}. If \(T\in \sone(H)\)
  satisfies
  \[
    \det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)
  \]
  as a holomorphic germ at \(z=0\), then
  \[
    \|T\|_1
    \ge \sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_j n_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}.
  \]
  The cyclic model \(L_{\mathcal D}\) from
  \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization} attains equality. Consequently,
  \[
    \inf\bigl\{\|T\|_1:T\in \sone,\ \det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)
      \text{ near }0\bigr\}
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_j n_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}.
  \]
  Moreover, equality
  \[
    \|T\|_1
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_j n_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}
  \]
  holds if and only if \(T\) is normal and there exist a Hilbert space
  \(H_0\) and a unitary \(U:H\to H_0\oplus H_{\mathcal D}\) such that
  \[
    UTU^{-1}=0_{H_0}\oplus L_{\mathcal D}.
  \]
  Thus the canonical cyclic model is the entire trace-norm minimizer class
  in the full determinant fiber, not only in the normal category.
\end{theorem}

The proof separates four inputs.  First, Fredholm determinant rigidity fixes
the non-zero algebraic eigenvalue multiset from the germ.  Second, the
Weyl--Ky Fan lower bound converts that multiset into the trace-norm budget
\(\sum_{\lambda}\abs\lambda M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\). Third, Ringrose
triangularization supplies a Schur gauge whose non-zero diagonal subsequence is
the determinant-forced eigenvalue list, with zero positions not prescribed.
The new step is the equality analysis: finite-compression pinching uses only
the absolute diagonal-sum identity and shows that equality in the global trace
lower bound kills every strict upper-triangular Schur entry, so a full-fiber
minimizer is forced back into the normal cyclic model class.

\begin{proof}
  By \Cref{lem:fredholm-spectral-rigidity}, the non-zero eigenvalues of
  \(T\), counted with algebraic multiplicity, are exactly the non-zero
  eigenvalues encoded by \(D_{\mathcal D}\). Equivalently, by
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}, the non-zero eigenvalue
  multiset of \(T\) is the multiset in which each
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) appears with multiplicity
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\).

  Let \((\lambda_k(T))_{k\ge1}\) be the eigenvalues of \(T\), repeated
  according to algebraic multiplicity and ordered so that
  \[
    \abs{\lambda_1(T)}\ge \abs{\lambda_2(T)}\ge \cdots,
  \]
  with zeros appended after the non-zero spectrum, and let
  \((s_k(T))_{k\ge1}\) be the singular values ordered so that
  \(s_1(T)\ge s_2(T)\ge \cdots\). By the ordered Weyl--Ky Fan lemma just
  stated, for every \(N\ge1\),
  \[
    \sum_{k=1}^N \abs{\lambda_k(T)}
    \le \sum_{k=1}^N s_k(T);
  \]
  Passing to the monotone limit \(N\to\infty\) yields
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge1}\abs{\lambda_k(T)}
    \le \sum_{k\ge1}s_k(T)
    =\|T\|_1.
  \]
  Applying this to the fixed eigenvalue multiset above gives
  \[
    \|T\|_1
    \ge \sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda).
  \]
  The second equality is the collapsed trace-budget identity.  Indeed,
  substituting the definition of \(M_{\mathcal D}\) gives
  \[
    \sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    =
    \sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}
      \sum_{\{j:\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\}}m_j
    =
    \sum_{j\in J}m_j
      \sum_{\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j}\abs{\lambda}
    =
    \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}.
  \]
  Here the last step uses only that all \(n_j\) roots of
  \(\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\) have modulus \(\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}\).

  For the cyclic model \(L_{\mathcal D}\), normality implies that its
  singular values are exactly the moduli of its non-zero eigenvalues,
  counted with multiplicity. Hence
  \[
    \|L_{\mathcal D}\|_1
    =\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_j n_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}.
  \]
  The last equality is again
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}; equivalently, one may
  read off the same formula from
  \Cref{prop:sharp-trace-budget-support-obstruction}.
  Hence the lower bound is sharp and equals the stated infimum.

  Suppose now that equality holds:
  \[
    \|T\|_1
    =\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda).
  \]
  Since the determinant identity has already identified the non-zero algebraic
  spectrum of \(T\) with the collapsed multiset, the displayed equality is the
  equality hypothesis of
  \Cref{lem:zero-sector-schur-elimination-equality-fiber}.  That lemma first
  reduces by a singular-value expansion to the separable reducing subspace on
  which \(T\) acts non-trivially, then uses the completed Ringrose basis from
  \Cref{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal}.  In that completed basis the
  non-zero root-space flag is followed by a Schur basis for the quotient whose
  only spectral value is \(0\), and
  \Cref{lem:trace-norm-schur-compressions} gives trace-norm convergence of the
  Schur compressions.  The finite-compression pinching defects therefore tend
  to zero and force every strict Schur entry to vanish, including entries wholly
  in the zero sector and entries crossing into it.  Hence the operator on the
  separable reducing part is diagonal in an orthonormal basis, and the original
  operator is normal after reattaching the zero reducing summand.

  Now \(T\) is a normal trace-class operator with
  \(\det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)\). Applying
  \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization} yields a Hilbert space \(H_0\)
  and a unitary \(U:H\to H_0\oplus H_{\mathcal D}\) such that
  \[
    UTU^{-1}=0_{H_0}\oplus L_{\mathcal D}.
  \]
  Concretely, a diagonal compact representative of \(T\) has exactly the
  collapsed multiset forced by the determinant: the points of
  \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\), with multiplicities \(M_{\mathcal D}\), followed by
  zero entries.  By the diagonal form of \(L_{\mathcal D}\) in
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}, this diagonal operator is
  unitarily equivalent to \(L_{\mathcal D}\) plus an arbitrary zero summand.
  The converse implication is immediate from the already proved equality
  \(\|L_{\mathcal D}\|_1=\sum_jm_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}\).
\end{proof}

Under the hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}, the following diagram records
the dependency structure.  The horizontal path up to trace minimality is the
core determinant-fiber package; the outgoing arrows mark consequences of the same
collapsed spectral invariant: stability estimates, fixed-dimensional Horn
geometry, prescribed-budget Schatten rigidity, and finite-support residual
skeleton quotients.
  \[
  \begin{gathered}
  \boxed{D_{\mathcal D}}
  \xrightarrow{\ \textup{spectral}\ }
  \boxed{M_{\mathcal D}}
  \xrightarrow{\ \textup{pinching}\ }
  \boxed{\tau_{\mathcal D}\textup{ and normal minimizers}}\\
  \xrightarrow{\ \textup{finite skeleton, when finite}\ }
  \boxed{\textup{nonnormal quotient profiles}} .
  \end{gathered}
  \]
More explicitly, the determinant-to-multiset step is supplied by
\Cref{lem:fredholm-spectral-rigidity} and
\Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities};
\Cref{thm:cyclic-data-classification} then removes dependence on the chosen
cyclic Euler-factor presentation.  The full trace-class determinant fiber is
minimized only in \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}, whose lower
bound is the common invariant
\(\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
\abs{\lambda}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\) and whose equality statement gives the
normal minimizer class.  The finite-skeleton step has the extra hypothesis
that \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) is finite and is exactly the quotient analysis of
\Cref{thm:finite-support-quasinilpotent-quotient}; it is not an input to the
trace-norm lower bound.  The stability, Schatten, and nonnormal sections use
the collapsed multiplicity function and the sharp trace budget as inputs and
are recorded as consequences in \Cref{cor:trace-class-rigidity-consequences}.

The stability estimates immediately below are deliberately downstream of
the trace-class rigidity route just described. They quantify how the pinching
part of the main chain behaves after a Ringrose--Schur gauge and finite-tail
window have been chosen.  In the infinite-dimensional assertions, the needed
Schur-tail modulus is external data: it is not recovered from the determinant or
from the scalar trace defect, and the resulting statements are not intrinsic
global orbit-distance theorems.

\begin{corollary}[Schur-entry stability for trace-norm near-minimizers]
  \label{cor:schur-entry-stability}
  Under the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}, let \(T\in\sone(H)\)
  satisfy
  \[
    \det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)
  \]
  near \(0\), and put
  \[
    \tau_{\mathcal D}
    :=\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda).
  \]
  Choose a Ringrose basis from
  \Cref{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal} whose diagonal has non-zero
  subsequence equal to the determinant-forced algebraic
  eigenvalue multiset, with the remaining zero entries in the unspecified
  positions supplied by the triangularization. Write
  \(A=[a_{ij}]\) for the resulting upper triangular matrix. For
  \(P_N\) the projection onto
  \(\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_N\}\), set
  \[
    \delta_N(T):=\|P_NTP_N\|_1-\sum_{k=1}^N\abs{a_{kk}}.
  \]
  Then \(\delta_N(T)\ge0\),
  \[
    \limsup_{N\to\infty}\delta_N(T)
    \le \|T\|_1-\tau_{\mathcal D},
  \]
  and for every \(1\le i<j\le N\),
  \[
    \abs{a_{ij}}^2
    \le 2\|P_NTP_N\|_1\,\delta_N(T).
  \]
  In particular, if \(\|T\|_1\le\tau_{\mathcal D}+\varepsilon\), then for
  each fixed \(i<j\),
  \[
    \abs{a_{ij}}
    \le \sqrt{2(\tau_{\mathcal D}+\varepsilon)\varepsilon}
    \qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow0 \text{ along any near-minimizing family}).
  \]
  This fixed-entry conclusion is the maximal consequence asserted without
  extra tail data: converting it into trace-distance convergence requires a
  chosen finite window and explicit control of the omitted diagonal and
  compression tails.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The proof is the quantitative part of the preceding argument, without
  setting the final defect equal to zero. The compressions
  \(P_NTP_N\to T\) in trace norm by
  \Cref{lem:trace-norm-schur-compressions}, and the prescribed diagonal
  is absolutely summable with total \(\tau_{\mathcal D}\). Hence
  \[
    \limsup_{N\to\infty}\delta_N(T)
    \le \|T\|_1-\tau_{\mathcal D}.
  \]
  The pinching estimate applied to the \(i,j\) coordinates of
  \(P_NTP_N\) gives
  \[
    \delta_N(T)
    \ge
    \left\|
      \begin{pmatrix}a_{ii}&a_{ij}\\0&a_{jj}\end{pmatrix}
    \right\|_1-\abs{a_{ii}}-\abs{a_{jj}}.
  \]
  Using \Cref{lem:two-by-two-trace-norm-pinching} and
  \(\|B\|_1+\abs{a_{ii}}+\abs{a_{jj}}\le2\|P_NTP_N\|_1\) yields the
  displayed entry estimate. If
  \(\|T\|_1\le\tau_{\mathcal D}+\varepsilon\), then
  \(\|P_NTP_N\|_1\le\|T\|_1\) and, after taking \(N\to\infty\) with
  \(N\ge j\), the bound for the fixed entry follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite chosen-Schur-window distance bound]
  \label{prop:finite-chosen-schur-window-distance-bound}
  Under the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}, let
  \(T\in\sone(H)\) satisfy
  \[
    \det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)
  \]
  near \(0\). Choose a Ringrose basis as in
  \Cref{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal}, write \(A=[a_{ij}]\), let
  \(P_N\) be the projection onto the first \(N\) basis vectors, and put
  \[
    A_N=P_NTP_N,
    \qquad
    D_N=\operatorname{diag}(a_{11},\ldots,a_{NN}),
  \]
  on \(P_NH\). With
  \[
    \delta_N(T):=\|A_N\|_1-\sum_{k=1}^N\abs{a_{kk}},
  \]
  one has
  \[
    \|A_N-D_N\|_1
    \le
    N\sqrt{(N-1)\|A_N\|_1\,\delta_N(T)} .
  \]
  In particular, if
  \[
    \sum_{k>N}\abs{a_{kk}}\le\eta,
    \qquad
    \|T-P_NTP_N\|_1\le\eta,
  \]
  and \(\varepsilon:=\|T\|_1-\tau_{\mathcal D}\), then
  \[
    \|A_N-D_N\|_1
    \le
    N\sqrt{(N-1)(\|T\|_1+\eta)(\varepsilon+2\eta)} .
  \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The quantity \(\delta_N(T)\) is non-negative by the Weyl--Ky Fan lower
  bound applied to the finite matrix \(A_N\). By
  \Cref{cor:schur-entry-stability}, for every \(1\le i<j\le N\),
  \[
    \abs{a_{ij}}^2\le 2\|A_N\|_1\delta_N(T).
  \]
  Since \(A_N-D_N\) is strictly upper triangular,
  \[
    \|A_N-D_N\|_2^2
    =\sum_{1\le i<j\le N}\abs{a_{ij}}^2
    \le N(N-1)\|A_N\|_1\delta_N(T).
  \]
  The rank of \(A_N-D_N\) is at most \(N\), hence
  \(\|A_N-D_N\|_1\le\sqrt N\|A_N-D_N\|_2\), and the first displayed bound
  follows.

  Under the two chosen-tail hypotheses,
  \[
    \|A_N\|_1\le\|T\|_1+\eta
  \]
  and
  \[
    \delta_N(T)
    \le \|T\|_1+\eta-(\tau_{\mathcal D}-\eta)
    =\varepsilon+2\eta .
  \]
  Substitution gives the second estimate. This is the finite-window
  estimate that the following chosen-tail theorem globalizes after the two
  tails have been imposed in the chosen Schur gauge. Without such tail data,
  the preceding Schur-entry estimate remains a local pinching statement rather
  than a basis-free global orbit-distance estimate.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stability near the determinant-fiber minimizer}\label{sec:determinant-fiber-stability}

The minimization theorem gives more than equality rigidity, but the
infinite-dimensional estimates require a chosen Ringrose--Schur gauge.  This
subsection is therefore a stability layer over the trace-norm theorem.  It
records fixed-entry, finite-window, chosen-tail, and finite-dimensional
consequences of the same pinching mechanism; it does not promote Schur
coordinates to determinant-intrinsic invariants.

\begin{theorem}[Chosen-Schur-basis tail-controlled trace-norm estimate]
  \label{thm:tail-controlled-trace-distance}
  Under the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}, let \(T\in\sone(H)\)
  satisfy
  \[
    \det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)
  \]
  near \(0\), and let
  \[
    \tau_{\mathcal D}
    :=\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda),\qquad
    \varepsilon:=\|T\|_1-\tau_{\mathcal D}.
  \]
  Choose a Ringrose basis as in
  \Cref{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal}, write
  \(A=[a_{ij}]\), and let \(P_N\) be the projection onto
  \(\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_N\}\). Suppose that, for some
  \(N\ge1\), the diagonal tail and the compression tail satisfy
  \[
    \sum_{k>N}\abs{a_{kk}}\le \eta,
    \qquad
    \norm{T-P_NTP_N}_1\le \eta .
  \]
  Let
  \[
    D_N:=\operatorname{diag}(a_{11},\ldots,a_{NN})
  \]
  on \(P_NH\), and let \(S_N\) be the normal diagonal operator
  \(D_N\oplus0\) on \(P_NH\oplus P_NH^\perp\), transported back to \(H\)
  by the chosen basis. Then \(S_N\) is a finite truncation of the normal
  minimizer \(0\oplus L_{\mathcal D}\), and
  \[
    \norm{T-S_N}_1
    \le
    \eta+
    N\sqrt{(N-1)(\|T\|_1+\eta)(\varepsilon+2\eta)} .
  \]
  The tail hypotheses are part of the conclusion's content: this is the
  largest stability statement proved in this chain without adding an intrinsic
  compactness or uniform-tail assumption beyond the determinant and the scalar
  trace defect.
  Consequently, if \(T_\nu\) is a family in the same determinant fiber
  with \(\|T_\nu\|_1\downarrow\tau_{\mathcal D}\) and if there are
  integers \(N_\nu\to\infty\) and numbers \(\eta_\nu\downarrow0\) such
  that the two displayed tail bounds hold for \(T_\nu\) and
  \[
    N_\nu\sqrt{N_\nu-1}\sqrt{\varepsilon_\nu+2\eta_\nu}
    \longrightarrow0,
    \qquad
    \varepsilon_\nu:=\norm{T_\nu}_1-\tau_{\mathcal D},
  \]
  then
  \[
    \dist_1(T_\nu,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D})
    \longrightarrow0,
  \]
  where \(\mathcal M_{\mathcal D}\) is the unitary equivalence class of
  the normal minimizers \(0_{H_0}\oplus L_{\mathcal D}\).

  The hypotheses are part of the statement: they refer to the chosen
  Ringrose basis, the chosen initial segment \(P_NH\), and the two
  displayed tail inequalities. They are not invariant consequences of the
  determinant fiber or of the trace-norm defect alone. No basis-free
  infinite-dimensional trace-norm distance theorem is asserted here, and no
  such theorem is used later.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Set \(A_N=P_NTP_N\) and
  \[
    \delta_N:=\norm{A_N}_1-\sum_{k=1}^N\abs{a_{kk}}.
  \]
  The Weyl--Ky Fan lower bound gives \(\delta_N\ge0\). The assumed tails
  imply
  \[
    \norm{A_N}_1\le\norm{T}_1+\eta
  \]
  and
  \[
    \delta_N
    \le \norm{T}_1+\eta-(\tau_{\mathcal D}-\eta)
    =\varepsilon+2\eta .
  \]
  The same two-by-two pinching argument used in
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality} gives, for
  \(1\le i<j\le N\),
  \[
    \abs{a_{ij}}^2
    \le2\norm{A_N}_1\delta_N
    \le2(\norm{T}_1+\eta)(\varepsilon+2\eta).
  \]
  Since \(A_N-D_N\) is strictly upper triangular,
  \[
    \norm{A_N-D_N}_1
    \le \sqrt N\,\norm{A_N-D_N}_2
    \le
    N\sqrt{(N-1)(\norm{T}_1+\eta)(\varepsilon+2\eta)}.
  \]
  Finally,
  \[
    \norm{T-S_N}_1
    \le \norm{T-A_N}_1+\norm{A_N-D_N}_1
  \]
  and the first term is at most \(\eta\) by hypothesis. The operator
  \(S_N\) is diagonal with entries drawn from the determinant-forced
  eigenvalue multiset and zeros elsewhere, hence it is a finite truncation
  of a normal minimizer. For the final assertion, choose the corresponding
  full normal diagonal minimizer \(S_\nu^\ast\) by restoring the omitted
  diagonal tail. Then
  \[
    \norm{S_\nu^\ast-S_{N_\nu}}_1
    =\sum_{k>N_\nu}\abs{a_{kk}^{(\nu)}}\le\eta_\nu,
  \]
  The norms \(\norm{T_\nu}_1+\eta_\nu\) are bounded because
  \(\norm{T_\nu}_1\to\tau_{\mathcal D}\) and \(\eta_\nu\to0\). Hence the
  displayed estimate, plus the restored diagonal-tail error
  \(\eta_\nu\), tends to zero under the stated compatibility hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Chosen-tail stability criterion]
  \label{cor:chosen-tail-stability-criterion}
  Under the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}, let
  \((T_\nu)_\nu\subset\sone(H_\nu)\) be a family in the same determinant
  fiber,
  \[
    \det(I-zT_\nu)=D_{\mathcal D}(z),
    \qquad
    \varepsilon_\nu:=\norm{T_\nu}_1-\tau_{\mathcal D}\longrightarrow0 .
  \]
  For each \(\nu\), choose a Ringrose basis as in
  \Cref{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal}; write the associated triangular
  matrix as \(A^{(\nu)}=[a_{ij}^{(\nu)}]\) and let \(P_{N_\nu}\) be the
  projection onto the first \(N_\nu\) vectors. Suppose that
  \(N_\nu\to\infty\) and that the two chosen-tail errors
  \[
    d_\nu:=\sum_{k>N_\nu}\abs{a_{kk}^{(\nu)}} ,
    \qquad
    c_\nu:=\norm{T_\nu-P_{N_\nu}T_\nu P_{N_\nu}}_1
  \]
  satisfy
  \[
    d_\nu+c_\nu\longrightarrow0,
    \qquad
    N_\nu\sqrt{N_\nu-1}
      \sqrt{\varepsilon_\nu+2\max\{d_\nu,c_\nu\}}
    \longrightarrow0 .
  \]
  Then
  \[
    \dist_1(T_\nu,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D})\longrightarrow0 .
  \]
  Thus infinite-dimensional trace-distance stability in this package
  is a tail-localized criterion in a chosen Schur gauge: the determinant
  and the scalar trace defect supply the entrywise pinching, while the two
  displayed tail conditions are the additional data that convert it into an
  orbit-distance conclusion.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Set \(\eta_\nu=\max\{d_\nu,c_\nu\}\). The hypotheses give
  \(\eta_\nu\to0\), the two tail inequalities required in
  \Cref{thm:tail-controlled-trace-distance}, and exactly the compatibility
  condition appearing there. Applying that theorem to each \(T_\nu\) gives
  finite truncations of normal minimizers whose distances from \(T_\nu\)
  tend to zero. Restoring the omitted diagonal tail costs at most
  \(d_\nu\le\eta_\nu\) in trace norm, so the distance from \(T_\nu\) to the
  full minimizer class \(\mathcal M_{\mathcal D}\) also tends to zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform chosen-Schur-tail stability modulus]
  \label{thm:uniform-chosen-schur-tail-modulus}
  Under the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}, let
  \(\mathfrak C\) be a class of trace-class determinant-fiber elements
  \(T\) for which one Ringrose basis has been chosen for each \(T\).  Write
  \(P_N^T\) for the projection onto the first \(N\) vectors in the chosen
  basis for \(T\), write \(a_{kk}^T\) for the corresponding diagonal, and
  assume that \(\mathfrak C\) has a uniform chosen-Schur-tail modulus
  \[
    \omega(N):=
    \sup_{T\in\mathfrak C}
    \max\left\{
      \sum_{k>N}\abs{a_{kk}^T},
      \norm{T-P_N^TTP_N^T}_1
    \right\}
    \longrightarrow0 .
  \]
  Put
  \[
    B:=\sup_{T\in\mathfrak C}\norm{T}_1<\infty,
    \qquad
    \varepsilon(T):=\norm{T}_1-\tau_{\mathcal D}.
  \]
  For each \(N\ge1\) and each defect level \(\delta\ge0\) define the
  chosen-gauge orbit modulus
  \[
    \Omega_{\mathfrak C}(N,\delta)
    :=2\omega(N)+N\sqrt{(N-1)(B+
      \omega(N))(\delta+2\omega(N))}.
  \]
  Then every \(T\in\mathfrak C\) and every \(N\ge1\) satisfy the explicit
  estimate
  \begin{equation*}
    \dist_1(T,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D})
    \le \Omega_{\mathfrak C}(N,\varepsilon(T)).
  \end{equation*}
  Equivalently, if
  \[
    \Omega_{\mathfrak C}^{\ast}(\delta)
    :=\inf_{N\ge1}\Omega_{\mathfrak C}(N,\delta),
  \]
  then
  \[
    \sup_{\substack{T\in\mathfrak C\\ \varepsilon(T)\le\delta}}
      \dist_1(T,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D})
    \le \Omega_{\mathfrak C}^{\ast}(\delta).
  \]
  If, moreover, this chosen-tail modulus is compatible with the
  finite-window pinching loss, namely
  \begin{equation*}
    N\sqrt{(N-1)(B+\omega(N))\omega(N)}\longrightarrow0 .
  \end{equation*}
  then \(\Omega_{\mathfrak C}^{\ast}(\delta)\to0\) as
  \(\delta\downarrow0\), and this is a uniform chosen-gauge stability
  modulus for the class \(\mathfrak C\):
  \[
    \sup_{\substack{T\in\mathfrak C\\ \varepsilon(T)\le\delta}}
      \dist_1(T,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D})
    \longrightarrow0
    \qquad(\delta\downarrow0).
  \]
  Consequently, for any sequence \((T_\nu)\subset\mathfrak C\) with
  \(\varepsilon(T_\nu)\to0\), one has
  \[
    \dist_1(T_\nu,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D})\longrightarrow0 .
  \]
  This is a genuine infinite-dimensional stability statement only after the
  uniform chosen-basis tail modulus has been supplied; the modulus is extra
  structure and is not read from the Fredholm determinant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Fix \(T\in\mathfrak C\) and \(N\ge1\).  In
  \Cref{thm:tail-controlled-trace-distance}, take
  \(\eta=\omega(N)\), use the chosen Ringrose basis attached to \(T\), and
  use the projection \(P_N^T\).  The hypotheses defining \(\omega(N)\) give
  exactly the two tail inequalities required there, while
  \(\norm T_1\le B\).  The theorem gives a truncation \(S_N(T)\) of a normal
  minimizer such that
  \[
    \norm{T-S_N(T)}_1
    \le
    \omega(N)+
    N\sqrt{(N-1)(B+\omega(N))
      (\varepsilon(T)+2\omega(N))}.
  \]
  Restoring the omitted diagonal tail of the corresponding full normal
  minimizer costs at most
  \(\sum_{k>N}\abs{a_{kk}^T}\le\omega(N)\) in trace norm.  This proves the
  displayed estimate for \(\dist_1(T,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D})\).  Taking
  the infimum over \(N\) gives the bound by
  \(\Omega_{\mathfrak C}^{\ast}(\varepsilon(T))\), and taking the supremum
  over all \(T\) with \(\varepsilon(T)\le\delta\) gives the displayed uniform
  bound because \(\Omega_{\mathfrak C}(N,\delta)\) is nondecreasing in
  \(\delta\).

  Assume the compatibility condition. Given \(\rho>0\), choose \(N\) so large
  that
  \(2\omega(N)<\rho/2\) and
  \[
    N\sqrt{(N-1)(B+\omega(N))2\omega(N)}<\rho/4 .
  \]
  This is possible because \(N\) is fixed after the choice and
  the displayed compatibility hypothesis holds.  With this fixed \(N\),
  \(\Omega_{\mathfrak C}(N,\delta)\) tends as \(\delta\downarrow0\) to the
  same bound with \(\delta=0\), so it is less than \(\rho\) for all
  sufficiently small \(\delta\).  Thus
  \(\Omega_{\mathfrak C}^{\ast}(\delta)\le
  \Omega_{\mathfrak C}(N,\delta)<\rho\) for small \(\delta\), which proves
  \(\Omega_{\mathfrak C}^{\ast}(\delta)\to0\). The sequence assertion follows
  from the uniform bound with \(\delta=\varepsilon(T_\nu)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-dimensional trace stability near the minimizer class]
  \label{thm:finite-dimensional-trace-stability}
  Let \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) satisfy the trace-class
  summability hypothesis, and let \(M_{\mathcal D}\) and
  \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) be the collapsed multiplicity function and support
  from \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}. Assume that
  \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) is finite. Put
  \[
    D_{\mathcal D}(z)
    :=\prod_{j\in J}(1-\beta_j z^{n_j})^{m_j},
  \]
  and
  \[
    d_{\mathcal D}
    :=\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda),
    \qquad
    \tau_{\mathcal D}
    :=\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda).
  \]
  Let \(N\ge d_{\mathcal D}\), and let \(T\in M_N(\CC)\) satisfy
  \[
    \det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z).
  \]
  Set
  \[
    \varepsilon(T):=\|T\|_1-\tau_{\mathcal D}.
  \]
  Then \(\varepsilon(T)\ge0\). Moreover there is a normal matrix
  \(S\in M_N(\CC)\), unitarily equivalent to
  \[
    \operatorname{diag}\bigl(
      \lambda I_{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}:
      \lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\bigr)\oplus0_{N-d_{\mathcal D}},
  \]
  such that
  \[
    \|T-S\|_1
    \le
    N\sqrt{(N-1)\|T\|_1\,\varepsilon(T)}.
  \]
  In particular, in this fixed finite-dimensional fiber, if
  \(\|T\|_1\le\tau_{\mathcal D}+\varepsilon\), then
  \[
    \operatorname{dist}_1(T,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D,N})
    \le
    N\sqrt{(N-1)(\tau_{\mathcal D}+\varepsilon)\varepsilon},
  \]
  where \(\mathcal M_{\mathcal D,N}\) denotes the above unitary orbit of
  finite-dimensional minimizers with \(N-d_{\mathcal D}\) zero summands.

  The exponent \(1/2\) cannot be improved uniformly, even when
  \(N=2\) and the determinant has a non-zero double root; the explicit
  obstruction is \Cref{prop:two-by-two-square-root-sharpness}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The determinant identity fixes the non-zero eigenvalues of \(T\), with
  algebraic multiplicity, as the multiset in which
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) appears
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\) times. Since \(T\) is \(N\)-dimensional,
  the remaining \(N-d_{\mathcal D}\) eigenvalues are zero. Hence the
  Weyl--Ky Fan lower bound gives
  \[
    \|T\|_1\ge\tau_{\mathcal D},
  \]
  so \(\varepsilon(T)\ge0\).

  Choose a Schur triangularization
  \[
    U^*TU=A=[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^N
  \]
  whose diagonal lists the prescribed algebraic eigenvalue multiset:
  the non-zero eigenvalues are repeated according to
  \(M_{\mathcal D}\), and the remaining \(N-d_{\mathcal D}\) diagonal
  entries are zero. Let
  \(D=\operatorname{diag}(a_{11},\ldots,a_{NN})\) and set
  \(S=UDU^*\). Then \(S\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal D,N}\), and
  \[
    \sum_{i=1}^N\abs{a_{ii}}=\tau_{\mathcal D}.
  \]
  Thus
  \[
    \delta:=\|A\|_1-\sum_{i=1}^N\abs{a_{ii}}
    =\varepsilon(T).
  \]

  Fix \(1\le i<j\le N\). Let \(R_{ij}\) be the projection onto
  \(\operatorname{span}\{e_i,e_j\}\) and \(S_{ij}=I-R_{ij}\). The same
  pinching argument used in the proof of
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality} gives
  \[
    \delta
    \ge
    \left\|
      \begin{pmatrix}
        a_{ii} & a_{ij}\\
        0 & a_{jj}
      \end{pmatrix}
    \right\|_1-\abs{a_{ii}}-\abs{a_{jj}} .
  \]
  By \Cref{lem:two-by-two-trace-norm-pinching},
  \[
    \abs{a_{ij}}^2
    \le
    2\|A\|_1\,\delta
    =
    2\|T\|_1\,\varepsilon(T).
  \]
  Since \(A-D\) is strictly upper triangular,
  \[
    \|A-D\|_2^2
    =
    \sum_{1\le i<j\le N}\abs{a_{ij}}^2
    \le N(N-1)\|T\|_1\,\varepsilon(T).
  \]
  The rank of \(A-D\) is at most \(N\), so
  \[
    \|T-S\|_1
    =\|A-D\|_1
    \le \sqrt N\,\|A-D\|_2
    \le N\sqrt{(N-1)\|T\|_1\,\varepsilon(T)}.
  \]
  This proves the asserted distance estimate. The optimality of the
  exponent is exactly \Cref{prop:two-by-two-square-root-sharpness}.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Fixed-dimensional Weyl--Horn consequences}\label{sec:fixed-dimensional-weyl-horn-consequences}

The fixed-dimensional singular-value geometry adds no new determinant-fiber
invariant.  Once \(\Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}\) has
identified the determinant-forced eigenvalue list \(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N}\),
the answer is exactly the finite Horn--Li--Mathias theorem applied to that
length-\(N\) list.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-dimensional Weyl--Horn image]
  \label{cor:fixed-dimensional-weyl-horn-image}
  \label{def:finite-weyl-horn-body}
  \label{lem:zero-inclusive-finite-horn-theorem}
  \label{lem:zero-inclusive-log-majorization-fan-dominance}
  \label{thm:finite-dimensional-weyl-horn-cyclic-determinant-fiber}
  \label{prop:finite-horn-subordinate-trace-chain}
  \label{cor:universal-finite-dimensional-symmetric-gauge-minimization}
  \label{prop:strict-weak-majorization-gauge-rigidity}
  \label{cor:finite-dimensional-gauge-minimizer-taxonomy}
  \label{prop:arbitrary-gauge-taxonomy-sharpness}
  \label{cor:exact-fixed-dimensional-schatten-budget-range}
  \label{cor:horn-boundary-trace-stability}
  Let \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) satisfy the trace-class
  summability hypothesis, assume that \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) is finite, and
  fix \(N\ge d_{\mathcal D}\).  Then
  \[
    \{s(T):T\in M_N(\CC),\ \det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)\}
    =\operatorname{HWH}(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N}),
  \]
  where singular values are listed decreasingly.  Hence every unitarily
  invariant matrix norm is minimized on this fiber by the normal diagonal model
  with eigenvalue list \(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N}\), and this finite matrix
  geometry depends only on the collapsed multiplicity function
  \(M_{\mathcal D}\).

  More explicitly, for every \(1\le p<\infty\), the attainable fixed-\(N\)
  \(p\)-Schatten budgets are exactly
  \[
    \left\{
      \sum_{i=1}^N s_i^p:
      s\in\operatorname{HWH}(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N})
    \right\},
  \]
  with minimum
  \(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
  \abs\lambda^pM_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)=\sum_i a_{\mathcal D,N,i}^p\).  The
  trace boundary point is \(a_{\mathcal D,N}\): for
  \(s\in\operatorname{HWH}(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N})\),
  \[
    \sum_{i=1}^N a_{\mathcal D,N,i}\le\sum_{i=1}^Ns_i.
  \]
  Equality-rigidity for minimizers is only asserted for the strict
  weak-majorization gauges, the trace norm, and the strictly convex Schatten
  cases covered by \(\Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-trace-stability}\); arbitrary
  non-strict gauges can have non-normal minimizers outside the normal orbit.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  If \(T\in M_N(\CC)\) satisfies \(\det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)\), comparison
  with the collapsed determinant identity
  \[
    D_{\mathcal D}(z)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      (1-\lambda z)^{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}
  \]
  shows that the eigenvalue \(N\)-tuple of \(T\), counted with algebraic
  multiplicity, is \(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N}\).  The zero padding accounts for
  the remaining \(N-d_{\mathcal D}\) eigenvalues.  The classical finite
  Horn--Li--Mathias theorem
  \cite[Theorem~1.1]{LiMathias2001Construction}, equivalently Horn's finite
  Weyl--Horn theorem in this zero-inclusive form
  \cite[Theorem~1]{Horn1954DoublyStochastic}, therefore says exactly that the
  possible singular-value lists are the vectors
  \(s_1\ge\cdots\ge s_N\ge0\) satisfying
  \[
    \prod_{i=1}^k a_{\mathcal D,N,i}
    \le \prod_{i=1}^k s_i\quad(1\le k\le N),
    \qquad
    \prod_{i=1}^N a_{\mathcal D,N,i}=
    \prod_{i=1}^N s_i,
  \]
  which is precisely \(\operatorname{HWH}(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N})\).  The cited
  theorem is stated for fixed finite lists with non-negative singular values and
  arbitrary complex eigenvalues, so zero entries are included directly.

  The dependence on \(M_{\mathcal D}\) is immediate from the definition of
  \(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N}\).  The norm-minimization assertion follows from the
  standard implication from weak log-majorization to weak majorization together
  with Ky Fan dominance for symmetric gauge norms
  \cite[Chapter~II]{Bhatia1997MatrixAnalysis}.  Thus
  \(a_{\mathcal D,N}\) minimizes every symmetric gauge over the Horn body, and
  evaluating the same body by \(s\mapsto\sum_i s_i^p\) gives the displayed
  Schatten-budget range and minimum.  The trace-boundary inequality is the
  case \(p=1\), and \(\Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-trace-stability}\) supplies
  the stated trace-norm equality rigidity and square-root stability.  The
  remaining rigidity qualifications are exactly the classical strict-gauge
  alternatives and the elementary obstruction supplied by non-strict gauges
  such as the operator norm, for which nilpotent off-diagonal blocks can remain
  invisible at the minimizing value.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two-by-two square-root sharpness]
  \label{prop:two-by-two-square-root-sharpness}
  Fix \(\lambda\in\CC\setminus\{0\}\). For \(c\in\CC\), set
  \[
    A_c=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&c\\0&\lambda\end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  Then each \(A_c\) lies in the determinant fiber
  \[
    \det(I-zA_c)=(1-\lambda z)^2,
  \]
  whose two-dimensional minimizer class is \(\{\lambda I_2\}\), and
  \[
    \|A_c\|_1-2\abs\lambda
    =
    \frac{\abs c^2}
    {\sqrt{4\abs{\lambda}^2+\abs c^2}+2\abs{\lambda}}
    =\frac{\abs c^2}{4\abs\lambda}+O(\abs c^4).
  \]
  Moreover
  \[
    \operatorname{dist}_1(A_c,\{\lambda I_2\})=\abs c.
  \]
  Hence no estimate
  \(\operatorname{dist}_1(T,\mathcal M)=O(\varepsilon(T)^\alpha)\),
  with \(\alpha>1/2\), can hold uniformly near the minimizer class in
  finite-dimensional determinant fibers.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The determinant identity is immediate because \(A_c\) is triangular with
  diagonal entries \(\lambda,\lambda\). The normal matrices in this fiber
  have the single eigenvalue \(\lambda\), and therefore are equal to
  \(\lambda I_2\); hence the minimizer class is the singleton
  \(\{\lambda I_2\}\) by
  \Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-trace-stability}, or directly by normality.

  To compute the trace norm, write
  \[
    A_c^*A_c=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      \abs\lambda^2 & \overline\lambda c\\
      \lambda\overline c & \abs\lambda^2+\abs c^2
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  Its trace is \(2\abs\lambda^2+\abs c^2\) and its determinant is
  \(\abs\lambda^4\). For a positive \(2\times2\) matrix with eigenvalues
  \(s_1^2,s_2^2\), one has
  \((s_1+s_2)^2=s_1^2+s_2^2+2s_1s_2\). Therefore
  \[
    \|A_c\|_1^2
    =2\abs\lambda^2+\abs c^2+2\abs\lambda^2
    =4\abs\lambda^2+\abs c^2,
  \]
  and the displayed formula for \(\|A_c\|_1-2\abs\lambda\) follows by
  rationalizing the difference. Finally,
  \[
    A_c-\lambda I_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&c\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
  \]
  has rank one and singular value \(\abs c\), so its trace norm is
  \(\abs c\). Since
  \(\|A_c\|_1-2\abs\lambda\sim \abs c^2/(4\abs\lambda)\), the distance is
  a non-zero constant multiple of the square root of the defect to first
  order. This rules out every uniform exponent \(\alpha>1/2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Collapsed multiplicities under a Schatten budget]
  \label{lem:schatten-collapsed-multiplicities}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\), and let
  \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) satisfy
  \[
    n_j,m_j\in\NN,\qquad
    \beta_j\in\CC\setminus\{0\},\qquad
    \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}<\infty .
  \]
  Define
  \[
    M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    :=\sum_{\{j\in J:\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\}}m_j,
    \qquad
    \Sigma_{\mathcal D}
    :=\{\lambda\neq0:M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\neq0\}.
  \]
  Then each \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\) is finite,
  \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) is countable, and
  \[
    \sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}^pM_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}.
  \]
  Moreover the non-zero eigenvalue multiset of the cyclic
  \(\mathcal S_p\)-model \(L_{\mathcal D}\) is exactly the multiset in
  which \(\lambda\) occurs with multiplicity \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\).
  Thus \(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\) is the prescribed finite-subset cyclic-product budget
  attached to \(\mathcal D\), not a quantity determined by equality of a
  regularized determinant throughout the full natural \(\det_r\)-domain alone.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Choose roots \(\alpha_j^{n_j}=\beta_j\). The block
  \(C(n_j,\alpha_j)\otimes I_{m_j}\) has eigenvalues
  \(\alpha_j\omega\), \(\omega^{n_j}=1\), each repeated \(m_j\) times.
  Hence it contributes to the \(\lambda\)-eigenspace precisely when
  \(\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\), and the multiplicity contributed is \(m_j\).
  This gives the stated multiset description.

  Since every solution of \(\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\) has
  \(\abs{\lambda}^p=\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}\), summing over the multiset
  gives
  \[
    \sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}^pM_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    =
    \sum_{j\in J}\sum_{\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j}
      m_j\abs{\lambda}^p
    =
    \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}.
  \]
  The right-hand side is finite, so no non-zero \(\lambda\) can have
  infinite multiplicity and the support is countable.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Schatten regularized prescribed-budget rigidity}\label{sec:schatten-prescribed-budget-rigidity}

This subsection is a prescribed-budget regularized-determinant analogue of the
trace-class minimization theorem.  For \(1<p<\infty\), both the finite-subset
cyclic product and the \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget are part of the data.  We keep
two classes distinct.  The product-realized class is defined by locally uniform
convergence of the prescribed finite-subset regularized cyclic products to
\(\det_r(I-zT)\).  The determinant-equality class is defined, once
\(L_{\mathcal D}\in\mathcal S_p\) has been constructed, by equality with
\(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\).  The localization result below proves their
equivalence only after the finite \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget has been forced or
prescribed.  The natural-domain obstruction at the end marks where this method
stops: it is not a classification of all \(\det_r\)-fibers on the full
\(\mathcal S_r\)-domain.
\begin{theorem}[Finite-subset prescribed-budget cyclic-product \(\mathcal S_p\)-fiber rigidity]
  \label{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\), put \(r=\lceil p\rceil\), and let
  \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) satisfy
  \[
    \tau_{\mathcal D,p}
    :=\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}<\infty .
  \]
  This is the Schatten analogue only after both the finite-subset cyclic
  regularized product and the \(\mathcal S_p\) budget have been prescribed.
  Choose roots \(\alpha_j^{n_j}=\beta_j\), and let \(L_{\mathcal D}\) be the
  cyclic \(\mathcal S_p\)-model from
  \Cref{thm:schatten-cyclic-model-membership}. Define the finite-budget
  determinant-equality class attached to \(\mathcal D\) by the following
  display, where the superscript \(\mathrm{cyc}\) is only a label for the
  prescribed cyclic localization:
  \[
    \mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{cyc}}(H)
    :=
    \left\{
      \begin{array}{l|l}
        T\in\mathcal S_p(H)&
        \begin{array}{l}
          \det_r(I-zT)=\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\\
          \text{as entire functions of }z
        \end{array}
      \end{array}
    \right\}.
  \]
  All assertions of this theorem are assertions inside this finite-budget
  determinant-equality class.  The finite-subset cyclic product, the collapsed
  non-zero eigenvalue multiset encoded by \(\mathcal D\), and the finite
  \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget are part of the input; by
  \Cref{prop:schatten-finite-budget-localization} this class is the same as the
  corresponding product-realized class only under that finite-budget
  hypothesis.  In particular the statement is not made on the full natural
  \(\mathcal S_r\)-domain of \(\det_r\). If
  \(T\in\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{cyc}}(H)\), then
  \[
    \|T\|_p^p\ge \tau_{\mathcal D,p}.
  \]
  Equality holds if and only if \(T\) is normal and there exist a Hilbert
  space \(H_0\) and a unitary \(U:H\to H_0\oplus H_{\mathcal D}\) such
  that
  \[
    UTU^{-1}=0_{H_0}\oplus L_{\mathcal D}.
  \]
  Thus the cyclic model is the unique \(\mathcal S_p\)-norm minimizer class in
  the finite-budget determinant-equality problem localized by the prescribed
  finite-subset cyclic product, namely the prescribed part in which the non-zero
  eigenvalue multiset is the collapsed multiset forced by \(\mathcal D\). As in
  \Cref{prop:regularized-domain-boundary}, this is not a classification of the
  full natural \(\mathcal S_r\)-domain of \(\det_r\), nor of the full
  \(\det_r\)-fiber without the explicit prescribed finite-subset cyclic-product
  and \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget restrictions.

  More quantitatively, let \(M_{\mathcal D}\) and
  \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) be the collapsed multiplicity function and its
  support from \Cref{lem:schatten-collapsed-multiplicities}. Choose a
  Ringrose basis from
  \Cref{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal} whose diagonal has non-zero
  subsequence equal to the prescribed algebraic eigenvalue multiset, and
  whose remaining zero entries occupy only the positions supplied by that
  triangularization. Write \(A=[a_{ij}]\). For
  \(P_N\) the projection onto
  \(\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_N\}\), set
  \[
    \Delta_{N,p}(T)
    :=\|P_NTP_N\|_p^p-\sum_{k=1}^N\abs{a_{kk}}^p .
  \]
  Then \(\Delta_{N,p}(T)\ge0\),
  \[
    \limsup_{N\to\infty}\Delta_{N,p}(T)
    \le \|T\|_p^p-\tau_{\mathcal D,p},
  \]
  and for every \(1\le i<j\le N\),
  \[
    \left\|
      \begin{pmatrix}
        a_{ii} & a_{ij}\\
        0 & a_{jj}
      \end{pmatrix}
    \right\|_p^p-\abs{a_{ii}}^p-\abs{a_{jj}}^p
    \le \Delta_{N,p}(T).
  \]
  Consequently, along any family in this finite-budget determinant-equality
  class, equivalently in the corresponding product-realized class after
  \Cref{prop:schatten-finite-budget-localization},
  with \(\|T\|_p^p\downarrow\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\), every fixed Schur
  off-diagonal entry tends to \(0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Here \(T\) lies in the finite-budget determinant-equality class fixed by
  \(\mathcal D\). By the regularized determinant
  spectral-rigidity lemma,
  \Cref{lem:regularized-determinant-spectral-rigidity}, the identity
  \[
    \det_r(I-zT)=\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})
  \]
  and \Cref{lem:schatten-collapsed-multiplicities} show that the non-zero
  eigenvalue multiset of \(T\) is exactly the multiset in which
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) occurs
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\) times.

  \Cref{lem:weyl-fan-schatten-p-sum} gives
  \[
    \sum_k\abs{\lambda_k(T)}^p
    \le \sum_ks_k(T)^p
    =\|T\|_p^p.
  \]
  The fixed eigenvalue multiset gives
  \[
    \sum_k\abs{\lambda_k(T)}^p
    =\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}^pM_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}
    =\tau_{\mathcal D,p},
  \]
  The middle equality is \Cref{lem:schatten-collapsed-multiplicities}.
  This proves the lower bound.

  The normal cyclic model attains the bound by
  \Cref{thm:schatten-cyclic-model-membership}. Suppose conversely
  that \(\|T\|_p^p=\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\). Apply the Schatten-class
  triangularization lemma, \Cref{lem:ringrose-prescribed-diagonal}, to
  \(T\) after the separable reduction coming from a singular-value
  expansion.
  In the resulting upper triangular matrix \(A=[a_{ij}]\), the diagonal
  has non-zero subsequence equal to the prescribed algebraic eigenvalue
  multiset, with all other diagonal entries equal to zero in their chosen
  Ringrose positions. Therefore
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge1}\abs{a_{kk}}^p=\tau_{\mathcal D,p}=\|T\|_p^p.
  \]

  \Cref{lem:schatten-triangular-equality-pinching} applies to this
  triangular representation. Hence every strict upper-triangular entry of
  \(A\) vanishes, so \(T\) is diagonal in an orthonormal basis and is
  normal.

  A normal compact operator is classified, up to unitary equivalence and
  arbitrary zero summands, by its non-zero eigenvalues with multiplicity.
  Since this multiset is the one encoded by \(L_{\mathcal D}\), the
  claimed unitary equivalence follows. Notice that this step uses only the
  non-zero compact spectral multiset; a diagonal normal zero-sector summand is
  necessarily the zero operator on its Hilbert space and is absorbed into
  \(0_{H_0}\).  The converse equality implication has already been noted for
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\).

  Finally, the same compression argument without assuming equality gives
  the stated estimates. Indeed
  \(P_NTP_N\to T\) in \(\mathcal S_p\) and the prescribed diagonal has
  \(p\)-sum \(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\), so
  \[
    \limsup_{N\to\infty}\Delta_{N,p}(T)
    \le \|T\|_p^p-\tau_{\mathcal D,p}.
  \]
  The same block-pinching estimate proved in
  \Cref{lem:schatten-triangular-equality-pinching}, without taking the
  zero-defect limit, gives the displayed two-by-two defect bound. If a family has
  \(\|T\|_p^p-\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\to0\), then the corresponding
  two-by-two defects tend to \(0\) for every fixed pair \(i<j\), and
  \Cref{lem:two-by-two-schatten-pinching} forces the fixed Schur
  off-diagonal entries to tend to \(0\).
\end{proof}


\begin{theorem}[Forced finite budget for prescribed regularized cyclic products]
  \label{thm:schatten-forced-prescribed-budget-obstruction}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\), put \(r=\lceil p\rceil\), and let
  \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) be countable cyclic data,
  with no summability hypothesis imposed in advance.  Assume that there is an
  operator \(T\in\mathcal S_p(H)\) such that the finite-subset regularized
  cyclic products
  \[
    \prod_{j\in F}
      (1-\beta_jz^{n_j})^{m_j}
      \exp\!\left(
        m_j\sum_{1\le q\le (r-1)/n_j}
          \frac{\beta_j^qz^{qn_j}}{q}
      \right),
      \qquad F\Subset J,
  \]
  converge locally uniformly on \(\CC\) to \(\det_r(I-zT)\).  Then the
  prescribed \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget is automatically finite and forced by
  \(T\):
  \[
    \tau_{\mathcal D,p}
    :=\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}
    \le \|T\|_p^p<\infty .
  \]
  Consequently the cyclic model \(L_{\mathcal D}\) belongs to
  \(\mathcal S_p\), the same product is exactly
  \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\), and
  \(T\in\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{cyc}}(H)
  \) in the sense of \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}.  If
  the forced budget is sharp, \(\|T\|_p^p=\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\), then
  \(T\) is normal and is unitarily equivalent to
  \(0_{H_0}\oplus L_{\mathcal D}\) for some Hilbert space \(H_0\).

  Thus the obstruction to an unrestricted Schatten analogue is intrinsic:
  an \(\mathcal S_p\) operator carrying a prescribed regularized cyclic
  product must already pay the cyclic-product budget, and the rigidity theorem
  applies only after this finite budget has been forced or prescribed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The hypothesis is exactly the product-side hypothesis in the converse part
  of \Cref{thm:schatten-cyclic-regularized-determinant}, except that the
  finiteness of \(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\) is not assumed here.  That theorem
  proves directly from the zero-divisor of \(\det_r(I-zT)\), the collapsed
  cyclic multiplicities, and Weyl--Fan \(p\)-summability that
  \[
    \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}
    \le \|T\|_p^p.
  \]
  Since \(T\in\mathcal S_p\), the right-hand side is finite, so the
  prescribed budget is finite.

  With this finiteness now established,
  \Cref{thm:schatten-cyclic-model-membership} gives
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\in\mathcal S_p\) and
  \(\|L_{\mathcal D}\|_p^p=\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\).  The forward part of
  \Cref{thm:schatten-cyclic-regularized-determinant} identifies the same
  finite-subset net of regularized cyclic products, directed by
  \(F\Subset J\), with \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\).  By the assumed
  locally uniform convergence of that identical net to \(\det_r(I-zT)\), the
  two regularized determinants are equal.  Thus the product-realized hypothesis
  and determinant-equality condition agree after the budget has been forced, so
  \(T\) lies in the localized finite-budget class used in
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}.  If equality
  \(\|T\|_p^p=\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\) holds, the equality-rigidity conclusion of
  that theorem gives the asserted normality and unitary equivalence to the
  zero-padded cyclic model.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Finite Schur freezing in Schatten fibers]
  \label{cor:prescribed-schatten-finite-skeleton-freezing}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\), put \(r=\lceil p\rceil\), and let \(\mathcal D\)
  satisfy the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}.  Let
  \(T\in\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{cyc}}(H)\), choose a Ringrose
  basis as in \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}, and write its
  upper triangular matrix as \(A=[a_{ij}]\).  Put
  \[
    \varepsilon_p(T):=\|T\|_p^p-\tau_{\mathcal D,p}.
  \]
  Fix \(N\ge2\), a number \(R\ge\|T\|_p\), and a finite set
  \(E\subset\CC\) containing \(a_{11},\ldots,a_{NN}\).  If
  \(D_N=\operatorname{diag}(a_{11},\ldots,a_{NN})\) and
  \(A_N=[a_{ij}]_{1\le i,j\le N}\), then
  \[
    \|A_N-D_N\|_p
    \le
    N^{\max\{1/p-1/2,0\}}
    \sqrt{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}\,
    \Theta_{p,E,R}\bigl(\varepsilon_p(T)\bigr),
  \]
  where \(\Theta_{p,E,R}\) is the inverse two-by-two pinching modulus from
  \Cref{lem:uniform-two-by-two-schatten-modulus}.  Consequently, for any
  family in the same localized finite-budget class
  with \(\|T_\nu\|_p\le R\),
  \(\varepsilon_p(T_\nu)\to0\), and first \(N\) Schur diagonal entries
  contained in one fixed finite set \(E\), the whole strict upper triangular
  \(N\times N\) Schur skeleton tends to zero in \(\mathcal S_p\)-norm.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The lower bound in
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality} gives
  \(\varepsilon_p(T)\ge0\).  For \(1\le i<j\le N\), the two-by-two
  compression estimate in that theorem gives, after taking the limit superior
  over Schur compressions containing the \((i,j)\)-coordinates,
  \[
    \left\|
      \begin{pmatrix}a_{ii}&a_{ij}\\0&a_{jj}\end{pmatrix}
    \right\|_p^p
    -\abs{a_{ii}}^p-\abs{a_{jj}}^p
    \le \varepsilon_p(T).
  \]
  The same two-dimensional compression has \(\mathcal S_p\)-norm at most
  \(\|T\|_p\le R\), and its diagonal entries lie in \(E\).  Hence
  \Cref{lem:uniform-two-by-two-schatten-modulus} yields
  \[
    \abs{a_{ij}}
    \le
    \Theta_{p,E,R}\bigl(\varepsilon_p(T)\bigr)
    \qquad(1\le i<j\le N).
  \]
  Since \(A_N-D_N\) is strictly upper triangular,
  \[
    \|A_N-D_N\|_2
    \le
    \sqrt{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}\,
    \Theta_{p,E,R}\bigl(\varepsilon_p(T)\bigr).
  \]
  Its rank is at most \(N\), so finite-rank norm comparison gives
  \[
    \|A_N-D_N\|_p
    \le
    N^{\max\{1/p-1/2,0\}}\|A_N-D_N\|_2,
  \]
  proving the displayed estimate.  The final convergence assertion follows
  because \(\Theta_{p,E,R}(\varepsilon)\downarrow0\) as
  \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) by
  \Cref{lem:uniform-two-by-two-schatten-modulus}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact scope of the prescribed Schatten theorem chain]
  \label{prop:prescribed-schatten-chain-scope}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\), put \(r=\lceil p\rceil\), and let
  \(\mathcal D\) satisfy the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}.  Define the finite-budget
  determinant-equality class
  \[
    \mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}
    :=\{T\in\mathcal S_p:
      \det_r(I-zT)=\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})
      \text{ as entire functions}\}.
  \]
  By \Cref{prop:schatten-finite-budget-localization}, this class agrees with
  the product-realized class defined by the finite-subset products attached to
  \(\mathcal D\) because the hypotheses include
  \(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}<\infty\).  Then the following finite-budget alternative
  is exactly the part of the
  regularized determinant theory proved in this paper; each clause is tied to
  an earlier numbered result, and the complementary natural-domain problem is
  used only as a boundary obstruction.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item On \(\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}\), the non-zero algebraic
    eigenvalue multiset is the collapsed multiset encoded by
    \(M_{\mathcal D}\), and
    \[
      \inf_{T\in\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}}\|T\|_p^p
      =
      \tau_{\mathcal D,p}.
    \]
    The equality class is exactly the zero-padded normal cyclic model from
    \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}.
    This is the localized minimization statement of
    \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}, after the product-side
    forcing theorem
    \Cref{thm:schatten-forced-prescribed-budget-obstruction}: the finiteness of
    \(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\) is not cosmetic, because every \(\mathcal S_p\)
    operator whose \(\det_r\) is obtained from the prescribed regularized cyclic
    product already satisfies
    \(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\le\|T\|_p^p\).
    \item If \(p<r\), the boundary construction of
    \Cref{prop:regularized-domain-boundary,prop:schatten-natural-domain-budget-boundary}
    shows that the natural \(\mathcal S_r\)-domain of \(\det_r\) contains
    cyclic models whose \(\det_r\) is defined but whose prescribed
    \(\mathcal S_p\) budget is infinite.  Such models lie outside every
    finite-budget determinant-equality, equivalently product-realized, class
    \(\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}\) of the preceding clause.
  \end{enumerate}
  Consequently the Schatten results form the dependency chain from the cyclic
  construction theorem
  \Cref{thm:schatten-cyclic-regularized-determinant}, through the forced-budget
  obstruction theorem
  \Cref{thm:schatten-forced-prescribed-budget-obstruction}, through the
  localization proposition \Cref{prop:schatten-finite-budget-localization}, and
  finally to the minimizer theorem
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}, with the natural-domain
  examples retained only as the obstruction supplied by
  \Cref{prop:schatten-natural-domain-budget-boundary}.  They are not a
  classification of all natural-domain \(\det_r\)-fibers: without a prescribed
  finite \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget there is no lower-bound or equality-rigidity
  statement of the form proved here.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  For \textup{(i)}, the regularized determinant identity defining
  \(\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}\) is precisely the hypothesis class of
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}.  Its proof starts from
  \Cref{lem:regularized-determinant-spectral-rigidity,lem:schatten-collapsed-multiplicities},
  which identify the non-zero eigenvalue multiset with
  \(M_{\mathcal D}\).  Weyl--Fan majorization then gives
  \(\|T\|_p^p\ge\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\), while the normal cyclic model
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\) belongs to \(\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}\) by
  \Cref{thm:schatten-cyclic-regularized-determinant} and attains that value
  by \Cref{thm:schatten-cyclic-model-membership}.  The equality class is the
  equality-rigidity conclusion of
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}.

  For \textup{(ii)}, use the data constructed in
  \Cref{prop:regularized-domain-boundary} and recorded here in
  \Cref{prop:schatten-natural-domain-budget-boundary}: when \(p<r\), take
  \(J=\{j\ge2\}\), \(n_j=m_j=1\), and \(\beta_j=j^{-1/p}\).  Then
  \(\sum_j m_jn_j|\beta_j|^{r/n_j}<\infty\), so the normal cyclic model lies
  in \(\mathcal S_r\) and has defined \(\det_r\).  However,
  \(\sum_j m_jn_j|\beta_j|^{p/n_j}=\infty\), so that natural-domain
  example is not an element of any finite-budget \(\mathcal S_p\) fiber of the
  kind in \textup{(i)}.  Combining the two clauses gives exactly the displayed
  dependency chain and proves the asserted scope alternative.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Prescribed-budget boundary for Schatten minimizer rigidity]
  \label{cor:schatten-prescribed-budget-boundary}
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality} is a sharp lower-bound
  and equality-rigidity theorem only in the finite-budget
  determinant-equality class, which is also the prescribed finite-subset
  product-realized class under the finite-budget hypothesis,
  \[
    \mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}
    :=\{T\in\mathcal S_p:
      \det_r(I-zT)=\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\},
    \qquad r=\lceil p\rceil .
  \]
  Here the displayed equality defines the determinant-equality class; its
  equivalence with the prescribed finite-subset product-realized class is the
  finite-budget localization of \Cref{prop:schatten-finite-budget-localization}.
  It does not extend to a classification of the full natural
  \(\mathcal S_r\)-domain of \(\det_r\). More precisely, when \(p<r\)
  there are cyclic data \(\mathcal E\) and a normal cyclic model
  \(L_{\mathcal E}\in\mathcal S_r\setminus\mathcal S_p\) for which
  \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal E})\) is defined and has the prescribed cyclic
  zero divisor without the finite \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget, but
  \(L_{\mathcal E}\notin\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal E}\) and
  the \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget
  \(\sum_jm_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}\) is infinite.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The definition of \(\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}\) is exactly the
  determinant-equality hypothesis class in
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}; the theorem
  proves \(\|T\|_p^p\ge\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\) on that class and identifies
  precisely the equality case. This is the asserted prescribed-budget
  statement.

  To see that the boundary is substantive when \(p<r\), use the explicit
  data from \Cref{prop:regularized-domain-boundary}: take
  \(J=\{j\ge2\}\), \(n_j=m_j=1\), and \(\beta_j=j^{-1/p}\). Then
  \[
    \sum_{j\ge2}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}
    =\sum_{j\ge2}j^{-1}=\infty,
  \]
  whereas
  \[
    \sum_{j\ge2}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{r/n_j}
    =\sum_{j\ge2}j^{-r/p}<\infty .
  \]
  Hence the corresponding normal cyclic model belongs to
  \(\mathcal S_r\) and has a standard regularized determinant
  \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal E})\), but it does not belong to
  \(\mathcal S_p\). Thus it lies in the natural domain of \(\det_r\) while
  being outside the prescribed \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget fiber to which
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Natural \texorpdfstring{\(\det_r\)}{det-r} domain boundary for Schatten budgets]
  \label{prop:schatten-natural-domain-budget-boundary}
  This is only a boundary statement separating the prescribed-budget
  \(\mathcal S_p\) theorem from the larger natural domain of the regularized
  determinant.  Let \(1<p<\infty\), put \(r=\lceil p\rceil\), and let
  \(\mathcal E=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) be cyclic data such that
  \[
    \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{r/n_j}<\infty .
  \]
  Let \(L_{\mathcal E}\) be the corresponding normal cyclic model in
  \(\mathcal S_r\), and set
  \[
    \mathfrak G_{r,\mathcal E}
    :=\{T\in\mathcal S_r:
      \det_r(I-zT)=\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal E})
      \text{ as entire functions}\}.
  \]
  Then
  \[
    L_{\mathcal E}\in\mathfrak G_{r,\mathcal E},
    \qquad
    L_{\mathcal E}\in\mathcal S_p
    \Longleftrightarrow
    \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}<\infty .
  \]
  Consequently, if the displayed \(p\)-budget is infinite, then
  \(\mathfrak G_{r,\mathcal E}\) contains an operator with no finite
  \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget.  Thus no \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget lower-bound or
  equality-rigidity statement can hold on all of
  \(\mathfrak G_{r,\mathcal E}\).  In the integral case \(p=r\), this
  particular domain enlargement disappears; the theorem then concerns the
  fixed regularized-determinant fiber inside \(\mathcal S_p\), not an
  additional classification of unrelated \(\det_p\)-fibers.  No invariant,
  minimization principle, or complete list for arbitrary natural-domain
  \(\det_r\)-fibers is asserted or used later.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  By \Cref{thm:schatten-cyclic-model-membership}, the normal cyclic model
  satisfies
  \[
    \norm{L_{\mathcal E}}_q^q
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{q/n_j}
  \]
  for every \(q\ge1\) for which the right-hand side is finite, and belongs
  to \(\mathcal S_q\) exactly in that case.  The assumed \(r\)-summability
  therefore gives \(L_{\mathcal E}\in\mathcal S_r\), so its regularized
  determinant \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal E})\) is defined and
  \(L_{\mathcal E}\in\mathfrak G_{r,\mathcal E}\).  The same membership
  formula with \(q=p\) proves the equivalence with the finite
  \(p\)-budget.

  If that budget is infinite, \(L_{\mathcal E}\) itself is an element of the
  natural \(\det_r\)-fiber but not of \(\mathcal S_p\), so
  \(\norm{L_{\mathcal E}}_p^p\) is not a finite quantity.  A theorem whose
  conclusion is a lower bound, minimizer, or equality condition for finite
  \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget therefore cannot be stated on the whole of
  \(\mathfrak G_{r,\mathcal E}\); it must first restrict to the prescribed
  finite-budget subfiber.  If \(p=r\), the natural domain of \(\det_r\) is
  already \(\mathcal S_p\), so this specific separation between
  \(\mathcal S_r\) and \(\mathcal S_p\) is absent.  The determinant-fiber
  label nevertheless remains fixed by the chosen entire function
  \(\det_p(I-zL_{\mathcal E})\), and says nothing about other regularized
  determinant fibers.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite-budget localization of the Schatten theorem]
  \label{prop:schatten-finite-budget-localization}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\), put \(r=\lceil p\rceil\), and let
  \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) be countable cyclic data, with
  no \(\mathcal S_p\)-summability assumed a priori.  For finite
  \(F\Subset J\), set
  \[
    P_F^{(r)}(z):=
    \prod_{j\in F}
      (1-\beta_jz^{n_j})^{m_j}
      \exp\!\left(
        m_j\sum_{1\le q\le (r-1)/n_j}
          \frac{\beta_j^qz^{qn_j}}{q}
      \right).
  \]
  Here \(P_F^{(r)}\) is the finite-subset regularized cyclic product attached
  to \(\mathcal D\).  Define the product-realized Schatten fiber
  \[
    \mathcal P_{p,\mathcal D}(H)
    :=\{T\in\mathcal S_p(H):
      P_F^{(r)}\to\det_r(I-zT)
      \text{ locally uniformly on }\CC\}
  \]
  and, when the conditions in \textup{(ii)} below hold so that
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\in\mathcal S_p\), define the determinant-equality Schatten
  fiber
  \[
    \mathcal F_{p,\mathcal D}(H)
    :=\{T\in\mathcal S_p(H):
      \det_r(I-zT)=\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})
      \text{ as entire functions}\}.
  \]
  For an operator \(T\in\mathcal S_p(H)\), the following are equivalent.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=1.7em]
    \item The net \(P_F^{(r)}\), directed by finite subsets of \(J\), converges
    locally uniformly on \(\CC\) to \(\det_r(I-zT)\).
    \item The prescribed cyclic budget is finite,
    \[
      \tau_{\mathcal D,p}:=
      \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}<\infty,
    \]
    the cyclic model \(L_{\mathcal D}\) belongs to \(\mathcal S_p\), the same
    net \(P_F^{(r)}\) converges locally uniformly to
    \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\), and
    \[
      \det_r(I-zT)=\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})
      \qquad\text{as entire functions.}
    \]
  \end{enumerate}
  Hence, if \(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}<\infty\), then
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\in\mathcal S_p\), the net \(P_F^{(r)}\) converges locally
  uniformly to \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\), and the product-realized and
  determinant-equality Schatten fibers coincide:
  \[
    \mathcal P_{p,\mathcal D}(H)
    =
    \mathcal F_{p,\mathcal D}(H)
    =
    \{S\in\mathcal S_p(H):
      \det_r(I-zS)=\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})
      \text{ as entire functions}\},
  \]
  Conversely, if \(\mathcal P_{p,\mathcal D}(H)\ne\varnothing\), then
  \(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}<\infty\); in that case the preceding equality of
  fibers applies.  Thus non-emptiness of the prescribed product-realized fiber
  is exactly the product-side finite-budget condition.
  This common class is the localized finite-budget Schatten fiber denoted
  \(\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{cyc}}(H)\) in the minimization theorem.
  On this localized fiber the sharp lower bound and equality class are those of
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}.  If \(p<r\), this
  localization is a proper restriction of the natural \(\mathcal S_r\)-domain
  of \(\det_r\): the natural-domain fiber in
  \Cref{prop:schatten-natural-domain-budget-boundary} contains a normal cyclic
  model whenever its \(r\)-budget is finite, and may contain such a model with
  infinite prescribed \(p\)-budget.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Assume first \textup{(i)}.  This is precisely the hypothesis of
  \Cref{thm:schatten-forced-prescribed-budget-obstruction}.  That theorem gives
  \(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\le\|T\|_p^p<\infty\), hence the prescribed budget is
  finite.  It also gives \(L_{\mathcal D}\in\mathcal S_p\), identifies the same
  product net with \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\), and concludes that
  \(\det_r(I-zT)=\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\).  Thus \textup{(ii)} follows.

  Conversely, assume \textup{(ii)}.  The locally uniform convergence of
  \(P_F^{(r)}\) to \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\) is part of the statement, and
  the determinant-equality condition identifies that limit with
  \(\det_r(I-zT)\).  Thus \(T\) belongs to the product-realized class, proving
  \textup{(i)}.

  Under these equivalent conditions, membership in the product-realized class
  and membership in the determinant-equality class are the same condition; this
  common condition is the membership condition for
  \(\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{cyc}}(H)\), so
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality} supplies
  \(\|S\|_p^p\ge\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\) on the fiber and identifies equality
  precisely with the zero-padded normal cyclic model.  Finally,
  \Cref{prop:schatten-natural-domain-budget-boundary} gives cyclic data with
  finite \(r\)-budget and infinite \(p\)-budget when \(p<r\).  For those data
  the normal cyclic model belongs to the natural \(\mathcal S_r\)-domain fiber
  but to no finite-budget \(\mathcal S_p\)-localized fiber.  Hence the
  localization is an actual domain restriction, not a reformulation of the full
  natural-domain problem.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Dependency spine of the prescribed Schatten extension]
  \label{prop:schatten-extension-dependency-spine}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\), put \(r=\lceil p\rceil\), and let
  \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) be countable cyclic data.  The
Schatten regularized determinant theorem in this article is exactly the
  following finite-budget extension of the trace-class determinant-fiber spine.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=1.7em]
    \item If some \(T\in\mathcal S_p(H)\) is obtained as the locally uniform
    finite-subset limit of the prescribed regularized cyclic products attached
    to \(\mathcal D\), then
    \[
      \tau_{\mathcal D,p}
      :=\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}<\infty,
      \qquad
      \,\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\le\norm{T}_p^p.
    \]
    The same product is then \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\), and
    \(T\) lies in the prescribed finite-budget fiber
    \(\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{cyc}}(H)\).
    \item On that localized fiber the sharp minimum and equality class are
    \[
      \inf_{S\in\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{cyc}}(H)}
      \norm{S}_p^p
      =\tau_{\mathcal D,p},
    \]
    with equality exactly for the zero-padded normal cyclic model
    \(0_{H_0}\oplus L_{\mathcal D}\).  The proof uses the same structural
    spine as the trace-class theorem: regularized determinant zeros determine
    the collapsed non-zero spectral multiset, Weyl--Fan majorization gives the
    budget lower bound, and triangular equality forces normality.
    \item If \(p<r\), the preceding two clauses cannot be extended to the whole
    natural \(\mathcal S_r\)-domain of \(\det_r\).  There are cyclic data
    \(\mathcal E\) for which \(L_{\mathcal E}\in\mathcal S_r\), the function
    \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal E})\) is defined, and the prescribed
    \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget is infinite; hence this natural-domain element is
    outside every finite-budget \(\mathcal S_p\) fiber of the preceding kind.
  \end{enumerate}
  Consequently the Schatten chain is subordinate to the article's Fredholm
  determinant-fiber rigidity theorem: it is a controlled regularized extension
  after finite \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget localization, not an independent
  classification of natural-domain \(\det_r\)-fibers.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Clause \textup{(i)} is exactly
  \Cref{thm:schatten-forced-prescribed-budget-obstruction}.  The hypothesis in
  that theorem is the finite-subset product-side realization of
  \(\det_r(I-zT)\).  It proves the displayed bound
  \(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\le\norm{T}_p^p\), hence the finiteness of the
  prescribed budget, and then invokes
  \Cref{thm:schatten-cyclic-model-membership,thm:schatten-cyclic-regularized-determinant}
  to identify the same product with \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\).  This is
  precisely the membership condition for
  \(\mathfrak F_{p,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{cyc}}(H)\).

  Clause \textup{(ii)} is
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality} applied on the fiber just
  isolated in \textup{(i)}.  Its proof begins with
  \Cref{lem:regularized-determinant-spectral-rigidity}, which identifies the
  non-zero algebraic spectrum from the zero divisor of the regularized
  determinant, and with \Cref{lem:schatten-collapsed-multiplicities}, which
  evaluates the corresponding collapsed \(p\)-budget as
  \(\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\).  The Weyl--Fan inequality then gives the lower
  bound, while equality in the triangular Schatten estimate eliminates all
  strict upper-triangular entries and hence gives the normal zero-padded cyclic
  model.  These are the same three proof interfaces used in
  \Cref{thm:main-trace-class-rigidity}: determinant spectral rigidity,
  eigenvalue-versus-singular-value majorization, and equality-case normality.

  Clause \textup{(iii)} is the boundary assertion of
  \Cref{prop:schatten-natural-domain-budget-boundary}.  For the explicit data
  used there, namely \(J=\{j\ge2\}\), \(n_j=m_j=1\), and
  \(\beta_j=j^{-1/p}\), one has
  \[
    \sum_{j\ge2}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{r/n_j}<\infty,
    \qquad
    \sum_{j\ge2}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}=\infty,
  \]
  because \(r/p>1\).  Thus the normal cyclic model is in the natural
  \(\mathcal S_r\)-domain of \(\det_r\), but it is not in \(\mathcal S_p\) and
  has no finite prescribed \(p\)-budget.  This contradicts any attempted
  extension of the finite \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget lower-bound or equality
  theorem to the whole natural domain.  The stated subordination and boundary
  conclusion follow by combining the three clauses.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-dimensional prescribed-budget Schatten stability]
  \label{thm:finite-dimensional-schatten-stability}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\), put \(r=\lceil p\rceil\), and let
  \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) satisfy
  \[
    \tau_{\mathcal D,p}
    :=\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}<\infty .
  \]
  Let \(M_{\mathcal D}\) and \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) be the collapsed
  multiplicity function and support from
  \Cref{lem:schatten-collapsed-multiplicities}, and assume that
  \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) is finite. Put
  \[
    d_{\mathcal D}
    :=\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda).
  \]
  Fix \(N\ge d_{\mathcal D}\), and let
  \(\mathcal M_{\mathcal D,N}^{(p)}\) be the unitary orbit in
  \(M_N(\CC)\) of
  \[
    \operatorname{diag}\bigl(
      \lambda I_{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}:
      \lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\bigr)\oplus0_{N-d_{\mathcal D}}.
  \]
  Put \(E_{\mathcal D}:=\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\cup\{0\}\). For every
  \(R>\tau_{\mathcal D,p}^{1/p}\) define
  \[
    \omega_{N,p,R,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{Sch}}(\varepsilon)
    :=
    N^{\max\{1/p-1/2,0\}}
    \sqrt{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}\,
    \Theta_{p,E_{\mathcal D},R}(\varepsilon),
  \]
  where \(\Theta_{p,E_{\mathcal D},R}\) is the two-by-two inverse
  pinching modulus of
  \Cref{lem:uniform-two-by-two-schatten-modulus}. Then
  \(\omega_{N,p,R,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\) is finite,
  nondecreasing, and tends to \(0\) at the origin. This is a stability
  statement inside the finite-dimensional localized finite-budget class, where
  the prescribed product-realized and determinant-equality conditions have been
  identified, not in the full natural \(\mathcal S_r\)
  domain of \(\det_r\). In particular, the cyclic data \(\mathcal D\) and its
  \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget are fixed throughout the statement. Every
  \(T\in M_N(\CC)\) satisfying
  \[
    \det_r(I-zT)=\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D}),
    \qquad
    \norm{T}_p\le R,
  \]
  obeys
  \[
    \dist_p(T,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D,N}^{(p)})
    \le
    \omega_{N,p,R,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
      \bigl(\norm{T}_p^p-\tau_{\mathcal D,p}\bigr).
  \]
  Thus, in fixed dimension and on bounded \(\mathcal S_p\)-sets,
  convergence of the regularized determinant-fiber budget to its sharp
  value forces convergence in \(\mathcal S_p\)-distance to the whole cyclic
  minimizer orbit with the displayed inverse-pinching modulus.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Let
  \[
    \varepsilon_p(T):=\norm{T}_p^p-\tau_{\mathcal D,p}.
  \]
  By \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality},
  \(\varepsilon_p(T)\ge0\). Choose a Schur triangularization
  \(U^*TU=A=[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^N\) whose diagonal lists the determinant-forced
  algebraic eigenvalue multiset: the elements of \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\)
  occur with multiplicities \(M_{\mathcal D}\), and the remaining
  \(N-d_{\mathcal D}\) entries are zero. Let
  \(D=\operatorname{diag}(a_{11},\ldots,a_{NN})\) and put \(S=UDU^*\). Then
  \(S\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal D,N}^{(p)}\) and
  \[
    \sum_{i=1}^N\abs{a_{ii}}^p=\tau_{\mathcal D,p}.
  \]

  For \(1\le i<j\le N\), compressing to the \(i,j\) coordinates and using
  the same block-pinching estimate as in
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality} gives
  \[
    \left\|
      \begin{pmatrix}a_{ii}&a_{ij}\\0&a_{jj}\end{pmatrix}
    \right\|_p^p-
    \abs{a_{ii}}^p-\abs{a_{jj}}^p
    \le \varepsilon_p(T).
  \]
  Moreover the two-by-two compression has \(\mathcal S_p\)-norm at most
  \(\norm{T}_p\le R\), and \(a_{ii},a_{jj}\in E_{\mathcal D}\). Therefore
  \Cref{lem:uniform-two-by-two-schatten-modulus} yields
  \[
    \abs{a_{ij}}
    \le
    \Theta_{p,E_{\mathcal D},R}\bigl(\varepsilon_p(T)\bigr)
    \qquad(1\le i<j\le N).
  \]

  Since \(A-D\) is strictly upper triangular,
  \[
    \|A-D\|_2
    \le
    \sqrt{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}\,
    \Theta_{p,E_{\mathcal D},R}\bigl(\varepsilon_p(T)\bigr).
  \]
  The rank of \(A-D\) is at most \(N\), so finite-rank norm comparison gives
  \[
    \|T-S\|_p
    =\|A-D\|_p
    \le
    N^{\max\{1/p-1/2,0\}}\|A-D\|_2 .
  \]
  Combining the last two displays gives the asserted distance bound. The
  finiteness, monotonicity, and vanishing at the origin of
  \(\omega_{N,p,R,\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\) follow directly from
  \Cref{lem:uniform-two-by-two-schatten-modulus}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Classical inputs and new consequence.}
  The lower bound in
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality} is exactly the standard
  combination of \Cref{lem:fredholm-spectral-rigidity} with the classical
  inequality \(\sum_k\abs{\lambda_k(T)}\le \|T\|_1\). The additional
  trace-norm information is the quantitative pinching estimate in
  \Cref{cor:schur-entry-stability}: near-minimal defect forces every fixed
  Schur off-diagonal entry in the prescribed Ringrose triangularization to
  be small, and zero defect forces all such entries to vanish. The normal
  classification theorem then identifies the equality case with the cyclic
  model up to zero summands. The Schatten theorem
  \Cref{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality} proves the analogous
  statement for the localized finite-budget regularized determinant classes in
  every \(\mathcal S_p\), \(1<p<\infty\): equality in the sharp
  \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget is again rigid, the same Schur-compression
  mechanism gives a fixed-entry near-minimizer constraint, and
  \Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-schatten-stability} upgrades this
  entrywise constraint to a finite-dimensional bounded-set modulus
  statement, with the modulus supplied by the inverse two-by-two Schatten
  pinching function. No infinite-dimensional
  \(\mathcal S_p\)-distance-to-orbit estimate is asserted without an
  additional tail hypothesis.

\subsection{Determinant-intrinsic rigidity}\label{sec:determinant-intrinsic-classification}

The following determinant-intrinsic formulation removes the dependence on a
chosen cyclic presentation.  This closing subsection returns to the central
Fredholm narrative after the stability, Horn, and prescribed-budget Schatten
packages.  It fixes the collapsed spectral multiset and its support dimension
before any minimization statement is invoked; the final summary theorem then
records exactly which consequences belong to this article and which boundary
questions remain non-assertions.
\begin{theorem}[Determinant rigidity by collapsed spectral data]
  \label{thm:cyclic-data-classification}
  Let \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) and
  \(\mathcal D'=\{(n_k',\beta_k',m_k')\}_{k\in K}\) satisfy the summability
  hypothesis of \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization}, and choose roots
  \(\alpha_j^{n_j}=\beta_j\) and \((\alpha_k')^{n_k'}=\beta_k'\). Define
  \[
    M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    :=\sum_{\{j\in J:\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\}} m_j,
    \qquad
    M_{\mathcal D'}(\lambda)
    :=\sum_{\{k\in K:\lambda^{n_k'}=\beta_k'\}} m_k',
  \]
  and write
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\), \(L_{\mathcal D'}\) for the associated cyclic models,
  and let
  \[
    \Lambda_{\mathcal D}
    :=
    \bigl\{\!\bigl\{\alpha_j\omega:
      j\in J,\ \omega^{n_j}=1,\text{ repeated }m_j\text{ times}
    \bigr\}\!\bigr\},
  \]
  with \(\Lambda_{\mathcal D'}\) defined analogously. Then the following are
  equivalent:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item \(\det(I-zL_{\mathcal D})=\det(I-zL_{\mathcal D'})\) as holomorphic
      germs at \(z=0\).
    \item \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)=M_{\mathcal D'}(\lambda)\) for every
      \(\lambda\in\CC\setminus\{0\}\).
    \item \(\Lambda_{\mathcal D}=\Lambda_{\mathcal D'}\) as multisets.
    \item There exist Hilbert spaces \(H_0\), \(H_0'\) and a unitary
      \(V:H_0\oplus H_{\mathcal D}\to H_0'\oplus H_{\mathcal D'}\) such that
      \[
        V(0_{H_0}\oplus L_{\mathcal D})V^{-1}
        =0_{H_0'}\oplus L_{\mathcal D'}.
      \]
  \end{enumerate}
  In this situation the determinant germ determines, in the normal
  trace-class category, a unique block-cyclic realization class up to zero
  summands. In particular,
  \[
    \dim \mathsf S(L_{\mathcal D})
    =\dim \mathsf S(L_{\mathcal D'}),
  \]
  with dimension interpreted as a Hilbert-space dimension. Since \(J\) and
  \(K\) are countable, this common value lies in
  \(\NN\cup\{\aleph_0\}\). Separately,
  \[
    \|L_{\mathcal D}\|_1=\|L_{\mathcal D'}\|_1,
    \qquad
    \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}
    =\sum_{k\in K}m_k'n_k'\abs{\beta_k'}^{1/n_k'}.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The implication \textup{(iv)}\(\Rightarrow\)\textup{(i)} is immediate,
  because unitary equivalence and zero summands do not change the Fredholm
  determinant. For \textup{(i)}\(\Rightarrow\)\textup{(ii)}, apply
  \Cref{lem:fredholm-spectral-rigidity} to \(L_{\mathcal D}\) and
  \(L_{\mathcal D'}\). Their non-zero spectra therefore agree with
  algebraic multiplicity. By
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}, the multiplicity of each
  \(\lambda\neq0\) in those spectra is exactly
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\) and \(M_{\mathcal D'}(\lambda)\),
  respectively. Hence \(M_{\mathcal D}=M_{\mathcal D'}\).

  The equivalence
  \textup{(ii)}\(\Leftrightarrow\)\textup{(iii)} is exactly the multiset
  reformulation supplied by
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}: \(\lambda\) appears in
  \(\Lambda_{\mathcal D}\) with multiplicity \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\),
  and similarly for \(\mathcal D'\).

  Assume \textup{(ii)}. By
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}, both cyclic models are
  unitarily equivalent to the same diagonal operator
  \[
    \bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma}\lambda I_{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)},
    \qquad
    \Sigma:=\{\lambda\neq0:M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\neq0\}.
  \]
  After adjoining arbitrary zero summands, this gives \textup{(iv)}.

  The closing statements are the support-dimension and trace-norm formulas
  from \Cref{prop:sharp-trace-budget-support-obstruction} applied to either
  model. Thus
  \[
    \dim \mathsf S(L_{\mathcal D})=\dim \mathsf S(L_{\mathcal D'})
  \]
  is a Hilbert-space dimension identity with value in
  \(\NN\cup\{\aleph_0\}\), while
  \[
    \|L_{\mathcal D}\|_1=\|L_{\mathcal D'}\|_1
  \]
  and the displayed trace-budget equality are genuine numerical identities
  in \([0,\infty)\).
\end{proof}


The following final corollary is a synthesis of earlier results, not an
additional hidden proof engine.  The threshold/classification inputs are
\Cref{thm:normal-realizability-threshold,thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization,thm:cyclic-data-classification},
the determinant-to-collapsed-spectrum interface is
\Cref{lem:fredholm-spectral-rigidity,prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities},
and the minimization input is the equality-case Schur pinching in
\Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}. The stability, finite
Weyl--Horn, Schatten, and finite-skeleton statements which follow are summary
consequences and are not inputs to the trace-class minimum.

\begin{corollary}[Final trace-class cyclic-divisor package]
  \label{thm:main-trace-class-rigidity}
  \label{thm:trace-class-rigidity-consequences}
  \label{cor:trace-class-rigidity-consequences}
  Let \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) be cyclic data with
  \(n_j,m_j\in\NN\) and \(\beta_j\in\CC^\times\).  Interpret
  \[
    \det(I-zT)=\prod_{j\in J}(1-\beta_jz^{n_j})^{m_j}
  \]
  in the locally uniform finite-subproduct sense of
  \Cref{thm:normal-realizability-threshold}.  There is a trace-class
  realization precisely when
  \[
    \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}<\infty .
  \]
  At this threshold the determinant determines only the collapsed non-zero
  spectral multiplicity function
  \[
    M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    =\sum_{\{j:\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\}}m_j,
    \qquad \lambda\in\CC^\times,
  \]
  and not a finer cyclic Euler-factor packaging.  If
  \(D_{\mathcal D}\) denotes the determinant germ and
  \[
    \mathfrak F(D_{\mathcal D})
    =\{T\in\sone:\det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)\text{ near }0\},
  \]
  then
  \[
    \min_{T\in\mathfrak F(D_{\mathcal D})}\norm T_1
    =\tau_{\mathcal D}
    :=\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs\lambda M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}.
  \]
  The equality cases are exactly the collapsed normal model class: every
  choice of roots \(\alpha_j^{n_j}=\beta_j\) gives a cyclic presentation
  \[
    L_{\mathcal D}=
    \bigoplus_{j\in J}\bigl(C(n_j,\alpha_j)\otimes I_{m_j}\bigr),
  \]
  which is unitarily equivalent to the collapsed diagonal normal operator
  \[
    \mathcal N_{\mathcal D}:=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \lambda I_{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)} .
  \]
  A member \(T\in\mathfrak F(D_{\mathcal D})\) satisfies
  \(\norm T_1=\tau_{\mathcal D}\) if and only if \(T\) is unitarily equivalent
  to \(0_{H_0}\oplus \mathcal N_{\mathcal D}\), equivalently to
  \(0_{H_0}\oplus L_{\mathcal D}\) after the above collapse, for some zero
  summand.  In particular the determinant determines this single collapsed
  normal model after zero summands, and different summable cyclic
  presentations of the same determinant germ give unitarily equivalent cyclic
  models only after passing to the collapsed multiset.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The threshold assertion is \Cref{thm:normal-realizability-threshold}. Its
  necessity uses the Fredholm zero-divisor correspondence together with the
  Weyl--Ky Fan eigenvalue lower bound, while its sufficiency is the cyclic
  block construction; the displayed normal model is exactly the construction
  recorded there.

  The collapse assertion follows from
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities} and
  \Cref{lem:fredholm-spectral-rigidity}: the zeros of the determinant at
  \(z=\lambda^{-1}\) determine the algebraic multiplicity of each non-zero
  eigenvalue \(\lambda\), hence determine \(M_{\mathcal D}\). Conversely the
  product
  \(\prod_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}(1-\lambda z)^{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}\)
  is the same determinant, so no finer Euler packaging can be recovered from
  the germ alone. The presentation-independence statement is precisely
  \Cref{thm:cyclic-data-classification}.

  The normal classification is the normal part of
  \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization}; equivalently it is the collapsed
  normal classification of
  \Cref{thm:collapsed-multiset-normal-realization} after applying
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}. The final presentation
  independence again follows from \Cref{thm:cyclic-data-classification}.

  The trace minimum and equality-case rigidity are
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}. The value of the minimum is
  rewritten using the trace-budget identity in
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}; the equality-class assertion
  is exactly the pinching/equality case in that theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Determinant-intrinsic trace budget and minimizer class]
  \label{cor:determinant-intrinsic-trace-budget}
  Let \(\mathcal D\) and \(\mathcal D'\) be summable cyclic data as in
  \Cref{thm:cyclic-data-classification}. If
  \[
    \det(I-zL_{\mathcal D})=\det(I-zL_{\mathcal D'})
  \]
  as holomorphic germs at \(0\), then the determinant germ fixes the same
  trace budget in both presentations:
  \[
    \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}
    =
    \sum_{k\in K}m_k'n_k'\abs{\beta_k'}^{1/n_k'}.
  \]
  Moreover this common number is the infimum of \(\|T\|_1\) over the full
  trace-class determinant fiber
  \[
    \{\,T\in\sone:\det(I-zT)=\det(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\text{ near }0\,\},
  \]
  and the equality cases are exactly the normal cyclic model class
  \(0_{H_0}\oplus L_{\mathcal D}\), equivalently
  \(0_{H_0'}\oplus L_{\mathcal D'}\), up to unitary equivalence and zero
  summands.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  By \Cref{thm:cyclic-data-classification}, equality of the determinant
  germs is equivalent to equality of the collapsed multiplicity functions
  \(M_{\mathcal D}=M_{\mathcal D'}\), and it gives unitary equivalence of
  the two cyclic models after adjoining zero summands. The same theorem
  gives the trace-budget identity displayed above. Applying
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality} to either presentation of
  the common determinant germ identifies this common trace budget with the
  full trace-class infimum and classifies equality exactly as the normal
  cyclic model, with arbitrary zero summands. The unitary equivalence from
  \Cref{thm:cyclic-data-classification} makes the two descriptions of the
  minimizer class identical.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite-dimensional specialization.}
  Let \(A\) be a finite matrix and let \(T\in \sone\) satisfy
  \[
    \det(I-zT)=\det(I-zA).
  \]
  Then \(T\) and \(A\) have the same non-zero eigenvalues with
  algebraic multiplicity. Thus cyclic-block constructions may enlarge
  the ambient operator category, but they do not create spectral
  freedom once the determinant is prescribed. This is just
  \Cref{lem:fredholm-spectral-rigidity} applied to the finite-rank
  operator \(A\).
