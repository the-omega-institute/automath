\section{Boundary Examples: Finite Non-Zero Riesz Skeletons}\label{sec:finite-nonzero-nonnormal-skeletons}

This appendix records the boundary information not used in the proof of
\Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}.  The determinant fixes the
collapsed non-zero algebraic multiplicities \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\), but it
does not fix Jordan form on the corresponding finite-dimensional Riesz root
spaces and it does not see the quasinilpotent sector at zero.  Thus the finite
non-zero skeleton is the product, over non-zero roots, of the usual nilpotent
similarity classes on those root spaces; the zero sector enters only as a
boundary obstruction.

\begin{proposition}[Finite Riesz skeleton]
  \label{thm:nonnormal-jordan-classification}
  \label{thm:finite-support-quasinilpotent-quotient}
  Let \(\mathcal D\) satisfy the summability hypothesis of
  \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization}, and let
  \(D_{\mathcal D}(z):=\det(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\).  Let
  \(M_{\mathcal D}\) and \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) be the collapsed multiplicity
  function and support from \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}.
  On each non-zero Riesz root space, the determinant fiber modulo trace-class
  quasinilpotent zero summands has exactly the following residual data:
  for every \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\), a nilpotent similarity class on
  \(\CC^{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}\), equivalently a Jordan partition of
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\).  Every such product of nilpotent classes is
  realized by a trace-class operator with determinant \(D_{\mathcal D}\), and
  every trace-class realization gives one such product.  In each fixed product
  class,
  \[
    \inf \|T\|_1
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j},
  \]
  and the infimum is attained precisely for the zero nilpotent class on every
  non-zero root space.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  If \(T\) realizes \(D_{\mathcal D}\),
  \Cref{lem:fredholm-spectral-rigidity,prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}
  identify the non-zero algebraic spectrum: the Riesz root space at
  \(\lambda\) has dimension \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\).  The only remaining
  finite-dimensional similarity datum on that root space is the nilpotent part
  of \((T-\lambda I)|\Ran P_\lambda(T)\), hence a nilpotent Jordan class.
  Conversely, for prescribed nilpotents \(N_\lambda\) on
  \(\CC^{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}\), choose positive scalars \(t_\lambda\) so
  small that
  \(\sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}t_\lambda\|N_\lambda\|_1<\infty\), and
  form
  \[
    T=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      (\lambda I_{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}+t_\lambda N_\lambda).
  \]
  Each block contributes \((1-z\lambda)^{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}\), so the
  determinant is \(D_{\mathcal D}\).  The trace-norm lower bound is
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}; letting the scalars
  \(t_\lambda\downarrow0\) gives the displayed infimum.  Attainment is exactly
  the equality case of \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}: a
  minimizer is normal, hence diagonal on each non-zero root space, so all
  nilpotent parts vanish; the normal cyclic model attains the bound in that
  case.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Boundary interpretation of the finite skeleton]
  Equivalently, for finite support the non-zero skeleton quotient is the product
  \[
    \prod_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \{\text{nilpotent similarity classes on }
        \CC^{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}\}.
  \]
  This quotient records similarity classes, not unitary or metric data; the
  usual Riesz decomposition supplies the finite non-zero root spaces.  Direct
  sums with trace-class quasinilpotent zero-sector operators preserve the
  quotient and the Fredholm determinant, but may change trace norm and are not
  classified.  Within each finite root space the classical nilpotent closure
  order is the dominance order on partitions, and diagonal similarity can drive
  a non-zero nilpotent part to trace-norm zero without attaining the normal
  equality case.  At zero, even finite nilpotent Jordan data are invisible to
  the determinant.
\end{remark}
