\section{Finite Non-Zero Skeleton Supplement}\label{sec:finite-nonzero-nonnormal-skeletons}

The determinant fixes the collapsed non-zero spectral multiplicities but does
not force diagonalizability on the corresponding Riesz root spaces.  This
supplement treats the residual finite-support nonnormal information as a
supporting consequence of the trace-class determinant-fiber theorem: finite Jordan
profiles are classified away from zero, while quasinilpotent zero summands are
kept separate.  All classification here is finite-support, non-zero-skeleton
classification after discarding trace-class quasinilpotent zero summands; it
is not a full trace-class determinant-fiber similarity or topology theorem.

Trace-norm minimality fixes the normal minimizers, but the determinant
still permits arbitrary nilpotent Jordan structure at each prescribed
non-zero eigenvalue, subject only to algebraic multiplicity. The next
result records this residual freedom and shows that it can be approached
at the sharp trace-norm bound, while it is attained only in the diagonal
case. The whole discussion in this subsection concerns the finite non-zero
Riesz skeleton; trace-class quasinilpotent summands at zero are either
carried as a separate obstruction or explicitly ignored, and no
classification modulo all quasinilpotent perturbations is asserted.  The
appendix is therefore used only as support for the main determinant-fiber
rigidity theorem, not as a standalone theory of all determinant-invisible
zero sectors.

\begin{theorem}[Jordan profiles away from zero]
  \label{thm:nonnormal-jordan-classification}
  Let \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) satisfy the
  summability hypothesis of \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realisation}, and
  let \(L_{\mathcal D}\) be the associated block-cyclic model. Set
  \[
    D_{\mathcal D}(z):=\det(I-zL_{\mathcal D}).
  \]
  Let \(M_{\mathcal D}\) and \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) be the collapsed
  multiplicity function and its support from
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}. For each
  \(\lambda\in \Sigma_{\mathcal D}\), let
  \[
    \pi_\lambda=(r_{\lambda,1},\dots,r_{\lambda,\ell_\lambda})
  \]
  be a finite tuple of positive integers. Then the following are
  equivalent:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item There exists \(T\in \sone(H)\) such that
      \[
        \det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)
      \]
      near \(z=0\), and for every \(\lambda\in \Sigma_{\mathcal D}\)
      the restriction of \(T\) to the generalized eigenspace at
      \(\lambda\) has Jordan block sizes exactly \(\pi_\lambda\).
    \item For every \(\lambda\in \Sigma_{\mathcal D}\),
      \[
        M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
        =\sum_{q=1}^{\ell_\lambda}r_{\lambda,q}.
      \]
  \end{enumerate}
  If these conditions hold, then
  \[
    \inf\Bigl\{\|T\|_1:T\text{ satisfies \textup{(i)}}\Bigr\}
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}.
  \]
  For this fixed profile \((\pi_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}\),
  this sharp infimum is attained precisely when every prescribed block has
  size one, i.e. precisely when \(r_{\lambda,q}=1\) for every
  \(\lambda\in \Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) and every
  \(1\le q\le \ell_\lambda\). If the fixed profile contains a nontrivial
  Jordan block, then the displayed bound is approached by the
  approximating operators constructed in the proof but is not attained
  within that fixed profile class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Assume \textup{(i)}. By \Cref{lem:fredholm-spectral-rigidity}, the
  non-zero spectrum of \(T\), counted with algebraic multiplicity, agrees
  with that of \(L_{\mathcal D}\). By
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}, the algebraic
  multiplicity of each \(\lambda\in \Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) in
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\) is \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\). On the
  finite-dimensional generalized eigenspace at \(\lambda\), the sum of
  the Jordan block sizes is exactly the algebraic multiplicity. Therefore
  \textup{(ii)} follows.

  Assume now that \textup{(ii)} holds. Let \(N_r\) denote the nilpotent
  Jordan block of size \(r\), with ones on the superdiagonal and zeros
  elsewhere. Since
  \[
    \sum_{\lambda\in \Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\abs{\lambda}
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}
    <\infty
  \]
  by \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}, the set
  \[
    \mathcal B
    :=\{(\lambda,q):\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D},
      \ 1\le q\le \ell_\lambda\}
  \]
  is countable. Fix \(\varepsilon>0\), and choose numbers
  \(\delta_{\lambda,q}>0\) such that
  \[
    \sum_{(\lambda,q)\in\mathcal B}\delta_{\lambda,q}<\varepsilon.
  \]
  For each \((\lambda,q)\in\mathcal B\), continuity of the trace norm on
  the finite-dimensional matrix algebra \(M_{r_{\lambda,q}}(\CC)\) yields
  a number \(t_{\lambda,q}\ge 0\) such that
  \[
    t_{\lambda,q}>0 \text{ if } r_{\lambda,q}\ge 2,
    \qquad
    \left\|\lambda I_{r_{\lambda,q}}
      +t_{\lambda,q}N_{r_{\lambda,q}}\right\|_1
    \le r_{\lambda,q}\abs{\lambda}+\delta_{\lambda,q}.
  \]
  Set
  \[
    B_{\lambda,q}
    :=\lambda I_{r_{\lambda,q}}+t_{\lambda,q}N_{r_{\lambda,q}}.
  \]
  If \(r_{\lambda,q}\ge 2\), then \(B_{\lambda,q}\) is similar to the
  Jordan block \(J_{r_{\lambda,q}}(\lambda)=\lambda I_{r_{\lambda,q}}
  +N_{r_{\lambda,q}}\), because
  \[
    D_t^{-1}J_r(\lambda)D_t=\lambda I_r+tN_r,
    \qquad
    D_t:=\operatorname{diag}(1,t,\dots,t^{r-1}),
  \]
  for every \(t>0\). If \(r_{\lambda,q}=1\), then \(B_{\lambda,q}\) is
  the \(1\times 1\) Jordan block \((\lambda)\).

  Define
  \[
    H_\varepsilon
    :=\bigoplus_{(\lambda,q)\in\mathcal B}\CC^{r_{\lambda,q}},
    \qquad
    T_\varepsilon
    :=\bigoplus_{(\lambda,q)\in\mathcal B}B_{\lambda,q}.
  \]
  The estimate above and \textup{(ii)} give
  \begin{align*}
    \|T_\varepsilon\|_1
    &\le \sum_{(\lambda,q)\in\mathcal B}
      \left(r_{\lambda,q}\abs{\lambda}+\delta_{\lambda,q}\right) \\
    &= \sum_{\lambda\in \Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\abs{\lambda}
      +\sum_{(\lambda,q)\in\mathcal B}\delta_{\lambda,q} \\
    &< \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}+\varepsilon.
  \end{align*}
  Hence \(T_\varepsilon\in \sone(H_\varepsilon)\). By construction, for
  each \(\lambda\in \Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) the generalized eigenspace of
  \(T_\varepsilon\) at \(\lambda\) is the direct sum of the blocks
  \(B_{\lambda,q}\), so the Jordan block sizes are exactly
  \(\pi_\lambda\).

  Let \(\mathcal B_N\subset \mathcal B\) be an increasing exhaustion by
  finite sets, and set
  \[
    T_\varepsilon^{(N)}
    :=\bigoplus_{(\lambda,q)\in\mathcal B_N}B_{\lambda,q}.
  \]
  Then
  \[
    \|T_\varepsilon-T_\varepsilon^{(N)}\|_1\to 0.
  \]
  Since each \(B_{\lambda,q}\) is upper triangular with diagonal entry
  \(\lambda\), one has
  \[
    \det(I-zB_{\lambda,q})=(1-\lambda z)^{r_{\lambda,q}}.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    \det(I-zT_\varepsilon^{(N)})
    =\prod_{(\lambda,q)\in\mathcal B_N}(1-\lambda z)^{r_{\lambda,q}}.
  \]
  By trace-norm continuity of the Fredholm determinant
  \cite[Theorem~3.4]{Simon2005TraceIdeals},
  \[
    \det(I-zT_\varepsilon)
    =\prod_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      (1-\lambda z)^{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}
  \]
  as a holomorphic germ at \(z=0\). The same product is
  \(\det(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\), by
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}. Thus
  \[
    \det(I-zT_\varepsilon)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)
  \]
  near \(z=0\). This proves \textup{(i)}.

  The construction above gives, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), an operator
  \(T_\varepsilon\) satisfying \textup{(i)} and
  \[
    \|T_\varepsilon\|_1
    <\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}+\varepsilon.
  \]
  On the other hand, every operator satisfying \textup{(i)} obeys the
  lower bound from \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}.
  Consequently,
  \[
    \inf\Bigl\{\|T\|_1:T\text{ satisfies \textup{(i)}}\Bigr\}
    =\sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}.
  \]

  If \(r_{\lambda,q}=1\) for every \(\lambda\) and \(q\), then
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\) itself satisfies \textup{(i)} and attains the
  infimum by \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realisation}. Conversely, assume
  that the infimum is attained by some operator \(T\) satisfying
  \textup{(i)}. Then \(\|T\|_1\) equals the lower bound from
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}, so \(T\) is normal. For
  each \(\lambda\in \Sigma_{\mathcal D}\), the restriction of \(T\) to
  the generalized eigenspace at \(\lambda\) is a finite-dimensional normal
  operator with the single eigenvalue \(\lambda\), hence equals
  \(\lambda I\). Therefore every Jordan block has size \(1\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Freedom at zero.}
  If \(Q\in \sone(K)\) is quasinilpotent, then
  \(\det(I-zQ)\equiv 1\), so \(T\oplus Q\) has the same determinant as
  \(T\).  Thus the nonnormal Jordan classification theorem describes only the
  non-zero algebraic Jordan geometry; see
  \Cref{thm:nonnormal-jordan-classification}.  The quasinilpotent zero sector
  remains free, and the result is not a unitary classification of root-space
  nilpotents; see \Cref{rem:algebraic-partitions-not-unitary-classes}.
\begin{proposition}[Zero-part freedom in determinant fibers]
  \label{prop:zero-part-freedom-interface}
  Let \(T\in\sone(H)\). Let \(Q\in\sone(K)\) be quasinilpotent. Then
  \[
    \det(I-z(T\oplus Q))=\det(I-zT)
  \]
  as entire functions, and the non-zero Riesz root-space restrictions of
  \(T\oplus Q\) are canonically the same as those of \(T\). Conversely,
  suppose \(\mathcal D\) satisfies the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}, the collapsed support
  \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) is finite, and \(S\in\sone(H_S)\) satisfies
  \[
    \det(I-zS)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)
  \]
  near \(0\). Then there are finite-dimensional operators
  \(S_\lambda\) on spaces of dimension \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\),
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\), and a trace-class quasinilpotent
  operator \(Q_S\) such that \(S\) is similar to
  \[
    \left(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}S_\lambda\right)
      \oplus Q_S,
  \]
  where each \(S_\lambda-\lambda I\) is nilpotent. The determinant fixes
  only the dimensions of these non-zero root spaces; the trace-class
  quasinilpotent zero summand \(Q_S\) is arbitrary and invisible to the
  determinant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Multiplicativity of Fredholm determinants under trace-class direct sums
  and
  \Cref{prop:fredholm-regularized-zero-divisor-correspondence} give
  \[
    \det(I-z(T\oplus Q))=
    \det(I-zT)\det(I-zQ)=\det(I-zT).
  \]
  If \(\lambda\ne0\), then
  \[
    (T\oplus Q-\lambda I)^k
    =(T-\lambda I)^k\oplus(Q-\lambda I)^k.
  \]
  Since \(Q-\lambda I\) is invertible for \(\lambda\ne0\), the Riesz root
  space of \(T\oplus Q\) at \(\lambda\) is exactly the root space of
  \(T\) at \(\lambda\), embedded as the first summand. Thus zero
  quasinilpotent stabilization changes neither the determinant nor the
  non-zero root-space restrictions.

  Conversely, under the finite-support hypothesis, apply
  \Cref{thm:finite-support-quasinilpotent-quotient}. It gives the Riesz
  decomposition of \(S\) into the finite-dimensional non-zero root-space
  restrictions
  \(S_\lambda=S|_{\mathcal R_\lambda(S)}\), with
  \(\dim\mathcal R_\lambda(S)=M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\), and a complementary
  trace-class quasinilpotent restriction \(Q_S\). On each root space,
  \(S_\lambda-\lambda I\) is nilpotent by definition of the generalized
  eigenspace. Finally, the first paragraph shows that replacing \(Q_S\) by
  any trace-class quasinilpotent leaves both the determinant and all
  non-zero root-space restrictions unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Algebraic partitions are not unitary classes]
  \label{rem:algebraic-partitions-not-unitary-classes}
  Jordan partitions classify finite-dimensional nilpotent root-space
  restrictions only up to algebraic similarity. They do not classify them
  up to unitary equivalence. For example, for \(t\ne0\) put
  \[
    N_t=\begin{pmatrix}0&t\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  The matrices \(N_t\) and \(N_s\) have the same Jordan partition \((2)\)
  whenever \(s,t\ne0\). They are unitarily equivalent only when
  \(\abs{s}=\abs{t}\), because the non-zero singular value of \(N_t\) is
  \(\abs{t}\). Thus the algebraic quotient below is classified by
  partitions, while the stable unitary skeleton retains the full unitary
  equivalence class of each finite-dimensional nilpotent root-space
  restriction.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Stable unitary and algebraic root-space quotients]
  \label{def:root-space-equivalence-relations}
  Let \(\mathcal D\) satisfy the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}. For a determinant-fiber
  element \(T\), put
  \[
    \mathcal R_\lambda(T)
    :=\bigcup_{k\ge1}\ker((T-\lambda I)^k),
    \qquad
    N_\lambda(T):=(T-\lambda I)|_{\mathcal R_\lambda(T)}
  \]
  for \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\).

  Two fiber elements \(T,T'\) are \emph{stably unitarily equivalent away
  from zero}, written \(T\sim_{\mathrm u}T'\), if there exist trace-class
  quasinilpotents \(Q,Q'\) such that \(T\oplus Q\) and \(T'\oplus Q'\)
  are unitarily equivalent. This is the unitary relation used for the
  inherited Hilbert-space root-space operators \(N_\lambda(T)\). It records
  only the non-zero root-space restrictions that survive after adjoining
  trace-class quasinilpotent zero sectors; it is not a full unitary-orbit
  classification of the determinant fiber.  The added summands \(Q,Q'\)
  are stabilizing zero-sector noise: their unitary equivalence class, Jordan
  model, or any finer quasinilpotent structure is intentionally not recorded.

  When \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) is finite, two fiber elements are
  \emph{algebraically equivalent modulo the zero quasinilpotent sector},
  written \(T\sim_{\mathrm{alg}}T'\), if, for every
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\),
  \[
    N_\lambda(T)\quad\text{and}\quad N_\lambda(T')
  \]
  are similar as nilpotent matrices. Equivalently, after the Riesz
  decomposition away from zero, trace-class quasinilpotent summands at
  \(0\) are ignored and only the algebraic \(GL\)-orbits of the non-zero
  root-space nilpotents are retained. This relation is deliberately not a
  quotient topology on the full trace-class determinant fiber and is not a
  unitary equivalence relation; see
  \Cref{rem:algebraic-partitions-not-unitary-classes} for the separation of
  algebraic partitions from unitary root-space classes.  Thus both
  \(\sim_{\mathrm u}\) and \(\sim_{\mathrm{alg}}\) classify only data away
  from zero after adjoining or discarding trace-class quasinilpotent
  zero-sector summands; they do not classify the invisible zero sector itself.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Riesz decomposition with quasinilpotent complement]
  \label{lem:compact-riesz-root-space-quasinilpotent-complement}
  Let \(T\in\sone(H)\), and let \(F\subset\sigma(T)\setminus\{0\}\)
  be a finite set containing all non-zero spectral points of \(T\). For
  \(\lambda\in F\), let
  \[
    \mathcal R_\lambda(T)=\bigcup_{k\ge1}\ker((T-\lambda I)^k)
  \]
  be the Riesz root space. Then each \(\mathcal R_\lambda(T)\) is
  finite-dimensional, and there is a closed \(T\)-invariant subspace
  \(K_T\) such that
  \[
    H=\left(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in F}\mathcal R_\lambda(T)\right)
      \dotplus K_T
  \]
  algebraically and topologically. With respect to this decomposition,
  \(T\) is similar to
  \[
    \left(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in F}T|_{\mathcal R_\lambda(T)}\right)
      \oplus Q_T,
    \qquad Q_T:=T|_{K_T},
  \]
  where \(Q_T\in\sone(K_T)\) is quasinilpotent. The summands and
  projections are the standard Riesz spectral summands for compact
  operators \cite[Chapter~1]{Ringrose1971CompactNonSelfAdjoint}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Since \(T\) is compact, every non-zero spectral point is an isolated
  eigenvalue of finite algebraic multiplicity, and the corresponding Riesz
  projection has finite-dimensional range. Choose pairwise disjoint small
  positively oriented circles \(\Gamma_\lambda\), \(\lambda\in F\), each
  enclosing \(\lambda\) and no other point of \(\sigma(T)\), and none
  enclosing \(0\). Set
  \[
    P_\lambda=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_\lambda}
      (\zeta I-T)^{-1}\,d\zeta .
  \]
  The Riesz calculus gives commuting finite-rank idempotents with
  \(\Ran P_\lambda=\mathcal R_\lambda(T)\), and
  \(P_\lambda P_\mu=0\) for \(\lambda\ne\mu\). Put
  \[
    P=\sum_{\lambda\in F}P_\lambda,
    \qquad K_T=\Ran(I-P).
  \]
  Then \(P\) is a bounded projection commuting with \(T\), so \(K_T\) is
  closed and \(T\)-invariant, and the displayed topological direct sum
  follows. The block diagonal similarity is simply the representation of
  \(T\) on the range of \(P\) and the range of \(I-P\).

  Because \(Q_T=T|_{K_T}\) is the restriction of a trace-class operator to
  a complemented invariant subspace, it is trace-class on \(K_T\): indeed
  the trace-class ideal is stable under bounded left and right multiplication,
  and, after identifying \(K_T\) with \(\Ran(I-P)\), the restriction is the
  compressed operator \((I-P)T(I-P)|_{K_T}\). The
  spectrum of \(Q_T\) is the part of \(\sigma(T)\) outside the Riesz
  spectral sets enclosed by the curves \(\Gamma_\lambda\), together with
  the possible point \(0\). Since \(F\) contains all non-zero spectral
  points of \(T\), this spectrum is contained in \(\{0\}\). Thus the
  spectral radius of \(Q_T\) is zero, so \(Q_T\) is quasinilpotent.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quasinilpotent stabilization preserves non-zero root-space invariants]
  \label{thm:quasinilpotent-stabilization-nonzero-invariants}
  Let \(T\in\sone(H)\), \(T'\in\sone(H')\), and let
  \(Q\in\sone(K)\), \(Q'\in\sone(K')\) be quasinilpotent. Suppose that
  there is a unitary
  \[
    U:H\oplus K\longrightarrow H'\oplus K'
  \]
  such that
  \[
    U(T\oplus Q)U^{-1}=T'\oplus Q'.
  \]
  Then for every \(\lambda\in\CC\setminus\{0\}\) and every \(k\ge1\),
  \(U\) maps
  \[
    \ker((T-\lambda I)^k)\oplus\{0\}
  \]
  unitarily onto
  \[
    \ker((T'-\lambda I)^k)\oplus\{0\},
  \]
  and the restricted root-space operators are unitarily equivalent:
  \[
    U\bigl((T-\lambda I)|_{\ker((T-\lambda I)^k)}\oplus0\bigr)U^{-1}
    =
    (T'-\lambda I)|_{\ker((T'-\lambda I)^k)}\oplus0 .
  \]
  Consequently the stabilized unitary-equivalence class preserves each
  nilpotent restriction at non-zero eigenvalues, and therefore also every
  Jordan block profile. In particular, a determinant-fiber element with a
  nontrivial block at some \(\lambda\neq0\) cannot become unitarily equivalent
  to the normal cyclic model \(L_{\mathcal D}\), even after adjoining
  trace-class quasinilpotent summands at zero.  No assertion is made about
  distinguishing, parametrizing, or classifying those quasinilpotent
  summands themselves.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Since \(Q\) and \(Q'\) are quasinilpotent,
  \[
    \sigma(Q)=\sigma(Q')=\{0\}.
  \]
  Hence \(Q-\lambda I\) and \(Q'-\lambda I\) are invertible for every
  \(\lambda\neq0\). Therefore, for every \(k\ge1\),
  \[
    \ker((T\oplus Q-\lambda I)^k)
    =
    \ker((T-\lambda I)^k)\oplus\{0\},
  \]
  and similarly
  \[
    \ker((T'\oplus Q'-\lambda I)^k)
    =
    \ker((T'-\lambda I)^k)\oplus\{0\}.
  \]
  The intertwining relation
  \(U(T\oplus Q)=(T'\oplus Q')U\) implies
  \[
    U(T\oplus Q-\lambda I)^k
    =(T'\oplus Q'-\lambda I)^kU.
  \]
  Thus \(U\) carries the first kernel onto the second. Since \(U\) is unitary,
  its restriction is a unitary
  between these finite-dimensional root spaces with their inherited inner
  products.

  The same intertwining relation, restricted to the displayed kernels,
  gives the asserted unitary equivalence of the nilpotent root-space
  restrictions. For compact operators, every non-zero root space is
  finite-dimensional and stabilizes after finitely many powers.  The kernel
  dimensions
  \[
    \dim\ker((T-\lambda I)^k),\qquad k\ge1,
  \]
  equivalently the restricted nilpotent on the stabilized root space, determine
  the Jordan partition at \(\lambda\). Hence
  these profiles survive quasinilpotent stabilization at zero. The final
  assertion follows because the normal cyclic model has only one-by-one
  Jordan blocks at its non-zero eigenvalues.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stable unitary finite non-zero skeleton under quasinilpotent stabilization]
  \label{thm:stable-nonzero-skeleton-classification}
  Let \(T\in\sone(H)\), \(T'\in\sone(H')\), and suppose that
  \[
    \det(I-zT)=\det(I-zT')
  \]
  as germs at \(0\). For \(\lambda\neq0\), write
  \[
    \mathcal R_\lambda(T)
    :=\bigcup_{k\ge1}\ker((T-\lambda I)^k),
    \qquad
    N_\lambda(T):=(T-\lambda I)|_{\mathcal R_\lambda(T)} .
  \]
  Then \(\mathcal R_\lambda(T)\) and \(\mathcal R_\lambda(T')\) are
  finite-dimensional spaces of the same dimension. If
  \(T\sim_{\mathrm u}T'\) in the sense of
  \Cref{def:root-space-equivalence-relations}, then for every
  \(\lambda\neq0\) the nilpotent root-space restrictions
  \(N_\lambda(T)\) and \(N_\lambda(T')\), with their inherited Hilbert
  norms, are unitarily equivalent.

  For the cyclic determinant fiber over \(D_{\mathcal D}\), this is only a
  finite non-zero-skeleton statement: after ignoring the trace-class
  quasinilpotent zero sector, the determinant-forced
  dimension of the non-zero root space at
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) is \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\). The
  stable unitary skeleton preserves the full unitary equivalence class of
  each finite-dimensional nilpotent root-space restriction, but only in the
  non-zero skeleton just defined. Separately, at
  the algebraic similarity level, every partition of
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\) occurs as the Jordan profile at
  \(\lambda\), and different algebraic profiles remain distinct after
  arbitrary trace-class quasinilpotent stabilization at zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Equality of Fredholm determinants fixes the non-zero spectrum with
  algebraic multiplicity by \Cref{lem:fredholm-spectral-rigidity}. Hence
  the root spaces at a fixed \(\lambda\neq0\) are finite-dimensional and
  have the same dimension for \(T\) and \(T'\). If
  \(T\sim_{\mathrm u}T'\), then
  \Cref{thm:quasinilpotent-stabilization-nonzero-invariants} applies and
  gives unitary equivalence of the restricted nilpotent root-space
  operators at every non-zero \(\lambda\).

  Now assume the determinant is \(D_{\mathcal D}\). The dimension formula
  \[
    \dim\mathcal R_\lambda(T)=M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
  \]
  follows from \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}. On this
  finite-dimensional root space, \(T-\lambda I\) is nilpotent. Conversely,
  \Cref{thm:nonnormal-jordan-classification} constructs determinant-fiber
  elements with every prescribed partition of
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\) at each non-zero \(\lambda\). The same
  stabilization-invariance theorem then shows that different full unitary
  classes of the nilpotent root-space restrictions cannot become
  unitarily equivalent after adding quasinilpotent summands at zero. In
  particular, different Jordan profiles remain distinct.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite-support quotient and closure order}

The quotient statements that follow formalize exactly this limited residual
object.  They classify only the finite tuple of nilpotent restrictions on the
determinant-forced non-zero root spaces and then study its finite-dimensional
orbit-closure order after the invisible quasinilpotent zero sector has been
discarded.

\begin{definition}[Finite-support non-zero-skeleton quotient]
  \label{def:finite-support-skeleton-quotient}
  Assume that \(\mathcal D\) satisfies the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality} and that
  \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) is finite. Write
  \[
    \mathcal F_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{fin}}
    :=\{T\in\sone(H):\det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)
      \text{ as a germ at }0\},
  \]
  where the Hilbert space \(H\) is allowed to vary. Put
  \[
    \mathcal E_{\mathcal D}
    :=\prod_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \mathrm{Nil}_{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}(\CC),
    \qquad
    G_{\mathcal D}:=
      \prod_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      GL(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda),\CC),
  \]
  with the product Euclidean topology on the finite-dimensional affine
  space \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal D}\), and let
  \(G_{\mathcal D}\) act by componentwise conjugacy. For
  \(T\in\mathcal F_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{fin}}\), the Riesz root space
  \(\mathcal R_\lambda(T)\) has dimension
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\). After choosing linear identifications
  \(\mathcal R_\lambda(T)\cong\CC^{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}\), the tuple
  \[
    \bigl((T-\lambda I)|_{\mathcal R_\lambda(T)}
      \bigr)_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
  \]
  defines a point of \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal D}/G_{\mathcal D}\); changing
  the identifications changes the tuple only by the
  \(G_{\mathcal D}\)-action. This point is denoted
  \(\operatorname{Skel}_{\mathcal D}(T)\).

  The algebraic relation \(T\sim_{\mathrm{alg}}T'\) used below means
  \(\operatorname{Skel}_{\mathcal D}(T)=
  \operatorname{Skel}_{\mathcal D}(T')\). The topology and
  closure language on the quotient are always those transported from the
  finite-dimensional orbit quotient
  \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal D}/G_{\mathcal D}\). No quotient topology on the
  full trace-class fiber, no zero-sector invariant, and no unitary quotient,
  is intended.  The equivalence relation deliberately ignores the trace-class
  quasinilpotent zero sector; it does not classify that sector.  In particular,
  two representatives with different
  trace-class quasinilpotent complements at \(0\) have the same image in this
  quotient whenever their finite non-zero skeletons agree; the complements are
  ignored rather than classified.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-support finite non-zero-skeleton quotient after ignoring trace-class quasinilpotents]
  \label{thm:finite-support-quasinilpotent-quotient}
  Let \(\mathcal D\) satisfy the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}, and assume that
  \(\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) is finite. Let \(T\in\sone(H)\) satisfy
  \[
    \det(I-zT)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)
  \]
  near \(0\). For each \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\), let
  \[
    \mathcal R_\lambda(T)
    :=\bigcup_{k\ge1}\ker((T-\lambda I)^k),
    \qquad
    T_\lambda:=T|_{\mathcal R_\lambda(T)} .
  \]
  Then
  \[
    \dim\mathcal R_\lambda(T)=M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    \qquad(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}),
  \]
  and there is a closed \(T\)-invariant subspace \(K_T\) such that
  \[
    H=\left(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \mathcal R_\lambda(T)\right)\dotplus K_T
  \]
  algebraically and topologically, and the restriction
  \(Q_T:=T|_{K_T}\) is trace-class and quasinilpotent. Consequently \(T\)
  is similar to
  \[
    \left(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}T_\lambda\right)
      \oplus Q_T .
  \]
  Consequently, after ignoring the trace-class quasinilpotent zero summand
  and retaining only the finite non-zero Riesz skeleton needed in the present
  theorem chain, the map
  \[
    [T]_{\sim_{\mathrm{alg}}}
    \longmapsto
    \bigl([N_\lambda(T)]_{GL(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda),\CC)}
      \bigr)_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
  \]
  is the skeleton map of
  \Cref{def:finite-support-skeleton-quotient} and is a bijection
  \[
    \mathcal F_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{fin}}/\!\sim_{\mathrm{alg}}
    \cong
    \prod_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \mathrm{Nil}_{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}(\CC)/
      GL(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda),\CC),
  \]
  where the quotient is the finite-dimensional non-zero-skeleton quotient defined
  above and \(\mathrm{Nil}_m(\CC)\) denotes nilpotent \(m\times m\)
  matrices. This bijection records only the finite non-zero skeleton and
  deliberately discards the trace-class quasinilpotent zero summand; it is
  not a classification of the full determinant fiber modulo arbitrary
  quasinilpotent perturbations, and it does not attach an invariant to the
  discarded zero-sector summand. It is also not a unitary-orbit statement; the
  algebraic/unitary separation is the one fixed in
  \Cref{def:root-space-equivalence-relations,rem:algebraic-partitions-not-unitary-classes}.
  At the
  Jordan-profile level, the quotient is equivalently the product of the
  partition sets of \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\). This is an algebraic
  similarity quotient, not a unitary quotient; the stable unitary relation
  \(\sim_{\mathrm u}\) preserves the full unitary class described in
  \Cref{thm:stable-nonzero-skeleton-classification}. These algebraic
  quotient classes have the same
  sharp trace-norm infimum
  \[
    \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j},
  \]
  and this infimum is attained in a quotient class if and only if all of
  its non-zero root-space nilpotents are zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Determinant rigidity and
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities} show that the non-zero
  spectrum of \(T\), counted with algebraic multiplicity, is exactly the
  multiset in which each
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) appears
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\) times. Thus
  \(\dim\mathcal R_\lambda(T)=M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\) for each
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\), and there are no other non-zero
  spectral points.

  Applying
  \Cref{lem:compact-riesz-root-space-quasinilpotent-complement} with
  \(F=\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) gives the displayed complemented direct-sum
  decomposition, the similarity
  \[
    T\sim
    \left(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}T_\lambda\right)
      \oplus Q_T,
  \]
  and the fact that \(Q_T\) is trace-class and quasinilpotent.

  The preceding decomposition shows that the algebraic relation
  \(\sim_{\mathrm{alg}}\) is exactly equality of the finite-dimensional
  nilpotent similarity classes \(N_\lambda(T)\), with the zero
  quasinilpotent summand ignored. This is an algebraic quotient by bounded
  similarities on the Riesz summands, not a metric, topological, or unitary
  quotient of the full trace-class fiber. Hence the displayed map
  is well defined and injective by definition. It is surjective because
  \Cref{thm:nonnormal-jordan-classification} realizes, independently for
  every \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\), every nilpotent Jordan
  partition of size \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\), while a nilpotent
  similarity class is determined by its Jordan partition. The statement
  about the sharp infimum and its attainment is exactly the fixed-profile
  infimum and equality statement in
  \Cref{thm:nonnormal-jordan-classification}.
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}[Finite zero-sector invisibility obstruction]
  \label{prop:zero-sector-invisibility-obstruction}
  Assume the finite-support hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:finite-support-quasinilpotent-quotient}.  Let
  \[
    S_{\mathcal D}:=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \lambda I_{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}
  \]
  be the finite non-zero normal skeleton, and let \(N\) be a
  finite-dimensional nilpotent matrix.  Then
  \[
    S_{\mathcal D}\oplus N\in\mathcal F_{\mathcal D}^{\mathrm{fin}},
    \qquad
    \operatorname{Skel}_{\mathcal D}(S_{\mathcal D}\oplus N)
    =\operatorname{Skel}_{\mathcal D}(S_{\mathcal D}).
  \]
  If \(N\) is non-zero, then \(S_{\mathcal D}\oplus N\) is not similar to
  \(S_{\mathcal D}\oplus0_{\dim N}\).  Thus the quotient of
  \Cref{thm:finite-support-quasinilpotent-quotient} deliberately forgets a
  determinant-invisible zero-sector invariant even in finite dimension.  This
  proposition is only a scope obstruction for the finite non-zero skeleton and
  is not a complete similarity classification of the full trace-class
  determinant fiber.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  A nilpotent matrix has no non-zero eigenvalues, hence
  \(\det(I-zN)=1\).  Multiplicativity of finite-rank Fredholm determinants
  under direct sums gives
  \[
    \det(I-z(S_{\mathcal D}\oplus N))
    =\det(I-zS_{\mathcal D})\det(I-zN)=D_{\mathcal D}(z),
  \]
  where the last equality is the collapsed spectral multiplicity identity
  from \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}.  Hence
  \(S_{\mathcal D}\oplus N\) belongs to the same determinant fiber.  Since
  the spectrum of \(N\) is \(\{0\}\), the Riesz projection at every
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) is unchanged by adding \(N\).  The
  non-zero root spaces and their nilpotent restrictions are therefore those
  of the diagonal skeleton \(S_{\mathcal D}\), proving equality of the finite
  non-zero skeletons.

  Similarity preserves the dimensions of kernels.  Because
  \(S_{\mathcal D}\) is invertible on its finite non-zero skeleton, the kernel
  of \(S_{\mathcal D}\oplus0_{\dim N}\) has dimension \(\dim N\).  If
  \(N\neq0\) is nilpotent on a finite-dimensional space, then
  \(\ker N\) is a proper subspace, so
  \[
    \dim\ker(S_{\mathcal D}\oplus N)=\dim\ker N<\dim N .
  \]
  The two operators therefore cannot be similar.  The determinant and the
  finite non-zero skeleton both miss this zero-sector information.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Classical closure order inside the finite non-zero skeleton]
  \label{lem:finite-support-quotient-closure-order}
  Assume the finite-support hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:finite-support-quasinilpotent-quotient}. For
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\), let
  \(\pi_\lambda\) and \(\rho_\lambda\) be partitions of
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\), and let
  \(\mathcal O_\pi\) and \(\mathcal O_\rho\) be the corresponding
  finite-dimensional quotient strata, represented by determinant-fiber
  elements whose Jordan profiles at \(\lambda\) are respectively
  \(\pi_\lambda\) and \(\rho_\lambda\). Then
  \[
    \mathcal O_\rho\subseteq\overline{\mathcal O_\pi}
  \]
  in the finite-dimensional non-zero skeleton if and only if, for every
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\),
  \[
    \sum_{q=1}^r\rho_{\lambda,q}
    \le
    \sum_{q=1}^r\pi_{\lambda,q}
    \qquad(r\ge1),
  \]
  where missing parts are read as \(0\). Thus the quotient closure
  relation is the product, over the prescribed non-zero eigenvalues, of
  the usual dominance order on nilpotent Jordan partitions. In
  particular the diagonal normal minimizer stratum, corresponding to the
  partitions \((1,\ldots,1)\), is the unique closed stratum in the
  finite-support quotient, and it lies in the closure of every quotient
  stratum. The determinant-fiber input here is only the reduction to the
  finite non-zero skeleton; the closure-order statement itself is the
  classical finite-dimensional nilpotent-orbit theorem cited below.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The non-zero skeleton is the finite direct sum of root spaces
  \[
    \bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \mathcal R_\lambda,
    \qquad
    \dim\mathcal R_\lambda=M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda),
  \]
  and a quotient stratum is the product of the nilpotent similarity
  orbits of the restrictions
  \((T-\lambda I)|_{\mathcal R_\lambda}\). The scalar summands
  \(\lambda I\) do not affect orbit closures, and the spectral summands
  for distinct \(\lambda\)'s are separated by the Riesz decomposition.
  Hence the closure relation in the non-zero skeleton is the product of
  the closure relations for nilpotent conjugacy orbits on the spaces
  \(\mathcal R_\lambda\).

  For nilpotent matrices, the closure order of conjugacy classes is the
  dominance order on Jordan partitions: the orbit with partition
  \(\rho\) is contained in the Zariski, equivalently Euclidean, closure
  of the orbit with partition \(\pi\) precisely when
  \[
    \sum_{q=1}^r\rho_q\le\sum_{q=1}^r\pi_q
    \qquad(r\ge1).
  \]
  This is exactly the imported finite-dimensional
  Gerstenhaber--Kraft--Procesi closure theorem for nilpotent orbits; see
  Gerstenhaber's dominance theorem
  \cite[pp.~324--348]{Gerstenhaber1961Dominance} and the Kraft--Procesi
  closure theorem for classical nilpotent classes
  \cite[pp.~539--602]{KraftProcesi1982Closures}. Applying it
  independently on each \(\mathcal R_\lambda\) gives the displayed
  product criterion.

  The partition \((1,\ldots,1)\) is dominated by every partition of
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\), and its nilpotent orbit consists only of
  the zero nilpotent. Therefore the corresponding product stratum is
  contained in every quotient-stratum closure and is itself closed. Since
  any other partition has a non-zero nilpotent orbit whose closure also
  contains the zero nilpotent, no other quotient stratum can be closed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Degeneration of finite non-zero-skeleton quotient classes at the sharp budget]
  \label{thm:nonnormal-quotient-degeneration}
  Assume the finite-support hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:finite-support-quasinilpotent-quotient}. For each
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\), fix a nilpotent matrix
  \(N_\lambda\) on a Hilbert space of dimension
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\), and put
  \[
    J_\lambda:=\lambda I+N_\lambda,\qquad
    J:=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}J_\lambda .
  \]
  Then the similarity class of \(J\) has determinant
  \(D_{\mathcal D}\) and
  \[
    \inf\{\|SJS^{-1}\|_1:S \text{ invertible}\}
    =
    \tau_{\mathcal D}
    :=
    \sum_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \abs{\lambda}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda).
  \]
  This infimum is attained if and only if every \(N_\lambda=0\).

  More precisely, suppose some \(N_\lambda\neq0\). If
  \(S_\nu\) is a sequence of invertible matrices on the finite-dimensional
  space of \(J\) such that
  \[
    \|S_\nu JS_\nu^{-1}\|_1\longrightarrow \tau_{\mathcal D},
  \]
  then the similarity condition numbers must diverge:
  \[
    \|S_\nu\|\,\|S_\nu^{-1}\|\longrightarrow\infty .
  \]
  Equivalently, trace-norm minimization inside a nonnormal quotient class
  reaches the normal minimizer orbit only outside every bounded-similarity
  gauge.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The determinant of \(I-zJ_\lambda\) is
  \((1-\lambda z)^{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}\), because
  \(J_\lambda\) has the sole eigenvalue \(\lambda\) with algebraic
  multiplicity \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\). Multiplying over
  \(\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}\) and using
  \Cref{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities} gives determinant
  \(D_{\mathcal D}\). Similarity does not change the determinant.
  Therefore the global lower bound in
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality} gives
  \[
    \|SJS^{-1}\|_1\ge\tau_{\mathcal D}
  \]
  for every invertible \(S\).

  We next show that the lower bound is approachable within the fixed
  similarity class. Since only the similarity class is being considered,
  first conjugate the finite-dimensional nilpotents \(N_\lambda\) into
  Jordan form. On a Jordan block
  \(J_r(\lambda)=\lambda I_r+N_r\), where \(N_r\) has ones on the
  superdiagonal, let
  \[
    D_{\varepsilon,r}
    :=
    \operatorname{diag}(1,\varepsilon^{-1},\ldots,
      \varepsilon^{-(r-1)})
    \qquad(0<\varepsilon<1).
  \]
  Then
  \[
    D_{\varepsilon,r}J_r(\lambda)D_{\varepsilon,r}^{-1}
    =
    \lambda I_r+\varepsilon N_r .
  \]
  Taking the direct sum of these diagonal scalings over all Jordan blocks
  gives invertibles \(D_\varepsilon\) such that
  \(D_\varepsilon JD_\varepsilon^{-1}\) has the same diagonal part as
  \(J\) and nilpotent part tending to \(0\) in operator norm. Hence
  \[
    D_\varepsilon JD_\varepsilon^{-1}
    \longrightarrow
    \bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\mathcal D}}
      \lambda I_{M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)}
  \]
  in trace norm as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). The trace norm of the
  limit is \(\tau_{\mathcal D}\), so the infimum is exactly
  \(\tau_{\mathcal D}\).

  If the infimum is attained at some \(SJS^{-1}\), then
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality} implies that
  \(SJS^{-1}\) is normal and therefore diagonalizable. Similarity
  preserves Jordan block sizes, so all nilpotent parts \(N_\lambda\) must
  be zero. Conversely, if every \(N_\lambda=0\), then \(J\) is already the
  normal diagonal minimizer and the infimum is attained.

  It remains to prove the condition-number statement. Suppose, to the
  contrary, that some \(N_\lambda\neq0\) and that a minimizing sequence
  \(S_\nu JS_\nu^{-1}\) has
  \[
    \|S_\nu\|\,\|S_\nu^{-1}\|\le C .
  \]
  Multiplying \(S_\nu\) by a non-zero scalar does not change
  \(S_\nu JS_\nu^{-1}\), so normalize \(\|S_\nu\|=1\). Then
  \(\|S_\nu^{-1}\|\le C\). In finite dimension, after passing to a
  subsequence, \(S_\nu\to S\) in operator norm. The inverse bound gives
  \[
    \|Sx\|=\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\|S_\nu x\|
    \ge C^{-1}\|x\|
  \]
  for every vector \(x\), so \(S\) is invertible. Consequently
  \(S_\nu JS_\nu^{-1}\to SJS^{-1}\) in trace norm. The limit has trace
  norm \(\tau_{\mathcal D}\), so the preceding attainment argument forces
  all \(N_\lambda\) to vanish, contradicting the hypothesis. Thus the
  condition numbers diverge along every minimizing sequence in a
  nonnormal quotient class.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Nonnormal determinant fibers are trace-norm unbounded]
  \label{cor:nonnormal-fiber-unbounded}
  Under the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}, let
  \[
    \tau_{\mathcal D}
    :=\sum_{j\in J}m_j n_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}.
  \]
  Then for every \(r>0\) there exists a trace-class nonnormal operator
  \(T_r\) such that
  \[
    \det(I-zT_r)=D_{\mathcal D}(z)
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \|T_r\|_1=\tau_{\mathcal D}+r.
  \]
  In particular, the determinant fiber over \(D_{\mathcal D}\) is
  unbounded in trace norm once nonnormal realisations are allowed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Let \((e_n)_{n\ge1}\) be the standard orthonormal basis of
  \(\ell^2(\NN)\), and define the weighted shift \(Q\) by
  \[
    Qe_n:=2^{-n}e_{n+1}
    \qquad (n\ge1).
  \]
  Then \(Q^*Qe_n=4^{-n}e_n\), so \(|Q|e_n=2^{-n}e_n\) and hence
  \[
    \|Q\|_1=\Tr|Q|=\sum_{n\ge1}2^{-n}=1.
  \]
  Thus \(Q\in\sone\). Moreover,
  \[
    Q^m e_n
    =2^{-(n+(n+1)+\cdots+(n+m-1))}e_{n+m}
    =2^{-(mn+\frac{m(m-1)}2)}e_{n+m},
  \]
  whence
  \[
    \|Q^m\|
    \le 2^{-\frac{m(m+1)}2}
    \qquad (m\ge1).
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    r(Q)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\|Q^m\|^{1/m}=0,
  \]
  so \(\sigma(Q)=\{0\}\). Since \(Q^m e_n\perp e_n\) for every \(m\ge1\)
  and every \(n\), one has \(\Tr(Q^m)=0\) for all \(m\ge1\). The
  Fredholm--Witt expansion from \Cref{prop:fredholm-witt-bridge} therefore gives
  \[
    \log\det(I-zQ)^{-1}
    =\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\Tr(Q^m)}{m}z^m=0
  \]
  near \(z=0\), hence \(\det(I-zQ)=1\) on all of \(\CC\). Now set
  \(Q_r:=rQ\). Then \(Q_r\) is trace-class and nonnormal, with
  \[
    \sigma(Q_r)=\{0\},\qquad
    \det(I-zQ_r)=1,\qquad
    \|Q_r\|_1=r.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    T_r:=L_{\mathcal D}\oplus Q_r
  \]
  is trace-class and nonnormal, and multiplicativity of Fredholm
  determinants under direct sums gives
  \[
    \det(I-zT_r)
    =\det(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\det(I-zQ_r)
    =D_{\mathcal D}(z).
  \]
  Since trace norm is additive on orthogonal direct sums,
  \[
    \|T_r\|_1=\|L_{\mathcal D}\|_1+\|Q_r\|_1=\tau_{\mathcal D}+r,
  \]
  using \Cref{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality} for
  \(\|L_{\mathcal D}\|_1=\tau_{\mathcal D}\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Persistence in the full determinant fiber.}
  The concrete weighted-shift example was already given in
  \Cref{rem:nonnormal-obstruction-after-realisation}. The present
  corollary strengthens that observation: after adjoining the scaled
  quasinilpotent shifts \(Q_r=rQ\), one obtains nonnormal realisations of
  the same determinant with arbitrarily large trace norm. Thus the
  normal classifications in
  \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realisation,thm:cyclic-data-classification}
  cannot extend to all trace-class operators.

The finite non-zero-skeleton results identify only the algebraic residue left by
\(M_{\mathcal D}\).  They also show why trace-class quasinilpotent zero
summands prevent this article from claiming a larger determinant-fiber
classification.
