\section{Fredholm determinants and cyclic divisors}\label{sec:fredholm}

We collect the determinant facts needed for the paper in one place.  The
point is not a new Fredholm determinant construction, but the bookkeeping that
occurs when the zero divisor is presented by cyclic factors.

\subsection{The cyclic model}

\begin{proposition}[Fredholm and regularized zero-divisor correspondence]
  \label{prop:fredholm-regularized-zero-divisor-correspondence}
  \label{lem:fredholm-spectral-rigidity}
  For \(T\in\mathcal S_1\), the zero of \(\det(I-zT)\) at
  \(z=\lambda^{-1}\) has order equal to the algebraic multiplicity of the
  non-zero eigenvalue \(\lambda\).  The same zero-divisor statement holds for
  \(\det_r(I-zT)\) when \(T\in\mathcal S_r\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  This is the standard Fredholm product over non-zero eigenvalues; the
  regularizing exponential factor in \(\det_r\) is zero-free and therefore
  does not change the non-zero zero divisor.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fredholm--Witt bridge]
  \label{prop:fredholm-witt-bridge}
  The logarithm of \(\det(I-zT)^{-1}\) has Witt coordinates determined by
  \(\Tr(T^n)\) whenever \(T\in\mathcal S_1\).  In this paper these coordinates
  are used only to motivate the cyclic divisor presentation, not as extra
  invariants beyond the collapsed zero divisor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  For small \(z\), expand
  \(-\log\det(I-zT)=\sum_{n\ge1}\Tr(T^n)z^n/n\) and compare with the standard
  Witt product expansion.  The comparison is purely formal after the analytic
  identity is known near the origin.
\end{proof}

For a trace-class operator \(T\) on a separable Hilbert space we use
\[
  \det(I-zT)=\prod_{\lambda\in\spec(T)\setminus\{0\}}
       (1-z\lambda)^{a_T(\lambda)},
\]
where \(a_T(\lambda)\) is algebraic multiplicity; the product converges
locally uniformly because
\(\sum_{\lambda}a_T(\lambda)\abs{\lambda}<\infty\).  Conversely, a multiset
\(\Lambda\subset\CC\setminus\{0\}\) with multiplicities \(M(\lambda)\) is the
non-zero spectrum of a normal trace-class operator if and only if
\(\sum_{\lambda}M(\lambda)\abs{\lambda}<\infty\).  These are the standard Fredholm
facts used below; see, for instance, \cite[Chs.~3--4]{Simon2005TraceIdeals}.
For \(T\in\mathcal S_r\), the regularized determinant of order \(r\) is written
unambiguously as follows.  In words, \(\det_r\) means ``the regularized
Fredholm determinant of order \(r\)''.  We use the standard modified
Fredholm determinant convention, as in the classical Schatten-ideal
normalizations and the modified-determinant literature
\cite{Simon2005TraceIdeals,GesztesyMakarov2004ModifiedFredholm,GesztesyLatushkinMitreaZinchenko2005,BritzCareyGesztesyNicholsSukochevZanin2021}:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \det_{r}(I-zT)
  :=
  \prod_{\lambda\ne0}
  \left(
    (1-z\lambda)
    \exp\!\left(\sum_{q=1}^{r-1}\frac{(z\lambda)^q}{q}\right)
  \right)^{a_T(\lambda)} .
\end{aligned}
\]
The exponential factors are entire and zero-free, so this regularized product
has the same non-zero zero divisor as the ordinary Fredholm product whenever
both are interpreted in their standard domains.

Let \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) be countable data with
\(n_j,m_j\in\NN\) and \(\beta_j\in\CC^\times\).  Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
  b_1(\mathcal D)
  &:=\sum_{j\in J}m_j n_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j},\\
  M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
  &:=\sum_{\{j:\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\}}m_j .
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(M_{\mathcal D}\) denotes the collapsed multiplicity function, not a
choice of individual cyclic blocks.
For each finite subset \(F\Subset J\) put
\[
  D_F(z):=\prod_{j\in F}(1-\beta_j z^{n_j})^{m_j}.
\]
The net \((D_F)_{F\Subset J}\) is always defined; no infinite product is being
used before its convergence has been proved.  When this net converges locally
uniformly on \(\CC\), its limit is denoted by \(D_{\mathcal D}\).  Equivalently,
in assertions involving a trace-class operator \(T\), the phrase
\(D_{\mathcal D}(z)=\det(I-zT)\) means precisely that the finite-subset net
\((D_F)_{F\Subset J}\), directed by inclusion, converges locally uniformly to
\(\det(I-zT)\).
The associated trace budget can be read directly from the roots:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \sum_{\lambda\ne0}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\abs{\lambda}
  &=
  \sum_{\lambda\ne0}\abs{\lambda}
    \sum_{\{j:\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\}}m_j \\
  &=
  \sum_{j\in J}m_j
    \sum_{\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j}\abs{\lambda}
  =
  \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Only the fact that all roots of \(\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j\) have modulus
\(\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}\) is used in this identity.
The product-side zero divisor is the collapsed multiset
\(M_{\mathcal D}\), since
\[
  (1-\beta z^n)=\prod_{\lambda^n=\beta}(1-z\lambda).
\]
For each triple choose once and for all the roots of \(x^{n_j}=\beta_j\) and
let \(L_{j}\) be the diagonal normal block containing those roots, repeated
\(m_j\) times.  The cyclic normal model is
\(L_{\mathcal D}=\bigoplus_{j\in J}L_j\); changing the chosen roots only
unitarily relabels finite-dimensional summands.  Its non-zero spectral
multiplicity is \(M_{\mathcal D}\) and
\[
  \|L_{\mathcal D}\|_1=b_1(\mathcal D).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Cyclic Fredholm determinant fiber]
  \label{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization}
  \label{thm:cyclic-data-classification}
  \label{thm:determinant-fiber-trace-minimality}
  Let \(\mathcal D=\{(n_j,\beta_j,m_j)\}_{j\in J}\) be as above, with finite
  products \(D_F\) defined for \(F\Subset J\).
  The following are equivalent.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item \(b_1(\mathcal D)<\infty\).
    \item The cyclic model \(L_{\mathcal D}\) is trace class and
    the finite-subset net \((D_F)_{F\Subset J}\) converges locally uniformly on
    \(\CC\) to \(\det(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\).
    \item There exists a trace-class operator \(T\) with
    the finite-subset net \((D_F)_{F\Subset J}\) converging locally uniformly on
    \(\CC\) to \(\det(I-zT)\).
  \end{enumerate}
  When these conditions hold, every trace-class realization has non-zero
  eigenvalue multiplicities \(M_{\mathcal D}\).  Hence the normal realizations
  are exactly the normal operators unitarily equivalent to
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\oplus0\), after collapsing repeated roots and adding an
  arbitrary zero summand.  Moreover, in the full trace-class determinant fiber,
  \[
    \|T\|_1\ge \sum_{\lambda\ne0}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\abs{\lambda}
    =b_1(\mathcal D),
  \]
  and equality holds if and only if \(T\) is unitarily equivalent to
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\oplus0\); the equality assertion uses the corrected
  Ringrose triangular representation in
  \Cref{lem:ordered-ringrose-triangular-representation}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Ringrose triangular representation with zero sector]
  \label{lem:ordered-ringrose-triangular-representation}
  Let \(T\) be compact on a separable Hilbert space. There is an orthonormal
  basis in which \(T\) is upper triangular and whose diagonal \((d_k)\) has the
  following property: for every \(\lambda\ne0\), the set
  \(\{k:d_k=\lambda\}\) has cardinality equal to the algebraic multiplicity of
  \(\lambda\), and every remaining diagonal entry is zero. No assertion is made
  that all non-zero entries precede all zero entries unless the non-zero
  spectrum has finite algebraic support or the zero sector is absent. In
  particular, the assertion applies to every \(T\in\mathcal S_1\), and then
  \[
    \sum_k |d_k|=
    \sum_{\lambda\ne0}a_T(\lambda)|\lambda|<\infty .
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  This is Ringrose's triangular representation theorem for compact operators,
  applied after separating the finite-dimensional Riesz root spaces for the
  non-zero eigenvalues from the residual quotient; see
  \cite[Chapter~1, Theorem~3, pp.~6--8]{Ringrose1971CompactNonSelfAdjoint}.
  Each non-zero Riesz root space contributes its eigenvalue on the diagonal,
  repeated by algebraic multiplicity.  The quotient by the closed span of all
  non-zero root spaces has no non-zero characteristic values, so Ringrose's
  diagonal there consists only of zeros.  If both pieces are infinite, the
  resulting basis may interlace the non-zero and zero diagonal positions; only
  the displayed diagonal multiset is used below.  For trace-class \(T\), Weyl's
  eigenvalue--singular-value inequality gives the final summability.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}
  If \(b_1(\mathcal D)<\infty\), then the direct sum defining
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\) is trace class and its trace norm is the displayed sum.
  Multiplicativity of the finite-dimensional determinant on finite partial
  sums gives
  \[
    \det(I-zL_F)=\prod_{j\in F}(1-\beta_jz^{n_j})^{m_j}.
  \]
  Since \(L_F\to L_{\mathcal D}\) in trace norm, standard determinant
  continuity gives the asserted locally uniform limit, proving
  \textup{(i)}\(\Rightarrow\)\textup{(ii)}\(\Rightarrow\)\textup{(iii)}.

  Conversely, assume \textup{(iii)}.  The finite products, rather than an
  a priori infinite product, are the only product-side objects used here.  Fix
  \(\lambda\ne0\).  If infinitely many cyclic factors contribute to the root
  \(\lambda\), then for every \(r\) all sufficiently large finite subsets
  \(F\) make \(D_F\) divisible by \((1-z\lambda)^r\).  Local uniform
  convergence, hence convergence of derivatives at \(z=\lambda^{-1}\), would
  force \(\det(I-zT)\) to have a zero of arbitrarily large order there, which is
  impossible for a nonzero entire function.  Thus \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)<
  \infty\).  Once the finite set of contributing factors is included in \(F\),
  the order contributed at \(z=\lambda^{-1}\) is exactly
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\), and passing to the cofinal subnet gives a zero
  of \(\det(I-zT)\) of at least that order.  The Fredholm zero-divisor theorem
  therefore gives
  \(M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\le a_T(\lambda)\) for every \(\lambda\ne0\).
  Weyl's inequality for compact operators gives
  \(\sum_{\lambda}a_T(\lambda)\abs{\lambda}\le\|T\|_1<\infty\).  Therefore
  \[
    b_1(\mathcal D)=\sum_{\lambda\ne0}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\abs{\lambda}
    \le \sum_{\lambda\ne0}a_T(\lambda)\abs{\lambda}
    \le \norm{T}_1,
  \]
  so \textup{(iii)} implies \textup{(i)} and also gives the lower bound for
  every realization.  With \textup{(i)} now known, the first paragraph of the
  proof shows that the same finite-subset net converges to
  \(\det(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\).  Hence
  \(\det(I-zT)=\det(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\), and the Fredholm zero-divisor theorem
  identifies the non-zero eigenvalue multiplicities of \(T\) exactly with
  \(M_{\mathcal D}\).

  If \(T\) is normal, the spectral theorem identifies it, up to a zero
  summand, with the diagonal normal operator whose non-zero diagonal multiset is
  \(M_{\mathcal D}\).  For the equality case in the lower bound, choose the
  Ringrose triangularization supplied by
  \Cref{lem:ordered-ringrose-triangular-representation}.  Its
  diagonal sequence \((d_k)\) is the collapsed non-zero multiset, counted with
  algebraic multiplicity, with zeros in all remaining diagonal positions.
  Equality in the displayed lower bound is therefore exactly
  \(\|T\|_1=\sum_k |d_k|\).  The trace-class triangular equality lemma below,
  \Cref{lem:trace-class-triangular-equality-pinching}, then shows that every
  strictly upper triangular Schur entry is zero.  Thus \(T\) is diagonal in
  this Schur basis; the zero spectral part has zero diagonal and is the zero
  operator.  Hence \(T\) is normal with precisely the collapsed non-zero
  spectral multiset and is unitarily equivalent to
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\oplus0\).  The converse equality is immediate for the
  normal cyclic model.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Trace-class triangular equality pinching]
  \label{lem:trace-class-triangular-equality-pinching}
  Let \(T\in\mathcal S_1(H)\), and let \((e_k)_{k\ge1}\) be an orthonormal
  basis in which \(T\) is upper triangular, with diagonal entries
  \(d_k=\langle Te_k,e_k\rangle\).  If
  \[
    \|T\|_1=\sum_{k\ge1}|d_k|,
  \]
  then \(T\) is diagonal in this basis.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We first recall the diagonal estimate used below.  If \(A\in\mathcal S_1\)
  and \((f_k)\) is any orthonormal basis, then
  \(\sum_k|\langle Af_k,f_k\rangle|\le\|A\|_1\).  Indeed, for a finite set
  \(F\) choose phases \(\omega_k\), \(|\omega_k|=1\), such that
  \(\omega_k\langle Af_k,f_k\rangle=|\langle Af_k,f_k\rangle|\).  The finite-rank
  diagonal contraction \(U_F f_k=\omega_k f_k\) for \(k\in F\), and
  \(U_F f_k=0\) otherwise, gives
  \[
    \sum_{k\in F}|\langle Af_k,f_k\rangle|
    =|\Tr(U_FA)|\le\|A\|_1.
  \]
  Taking the supremum over finite \(F\) gives the claim.

  Fix two indices \(i<j\), and let \(Q\) be the projection onto
  \(\operatorname{span}\{e_i,e_j\}\).  Pinching by the orthogonal
  decomposition \(H=QH\oplus(1-Q)H\) is contractive on trace class, so
  \[
    \|QTQ\|_1+\|(1-Q)T(1-Q)\|_1\le\|T\|_1.
  \]
  Applying the diagonal estimate to the two diagonal blocks gives the reverse
  lower bound
  \[
    |d_i|+|d_j|+
    \sum_{k\ne i,j}|d_k|
    \le
    \|QTQ\|_1+\|(1-Q)T(1-Q)\|_1.
  \]
  Since the left side is \(\sum_k|d_k|=\|T\|_1\), both inequalities are
  equalities, and in particular
  \[
    \|QTQ\|_1=|d_i|+|d_j|.
  \]
  In the ordered basis \((e_i,e_j)\), upper triangularity gives
  \[
    QTQ=\begin{pmatrix}d_i&c_{ij}\\0&d_j\end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  For a two-by-two upper triangular matrix
  \(B=\begin{psmallmatrix}a&c\\0&b\end{psmallmatrix}\), with singular values
  \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2\), one has
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    \|B\|_1^2
    &=(\sigma_1+\sigma_2)^2
      =\Tr(B^*B)+2\sqrt{\det(B^*B)} \\
    &=|a|^2+|b|^2+|c|^2+2|ab|
      =(|a|+|b|)^2+|c|^2 .
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  Hence \(\|B\|_1=|a|+|b|\) if and only if \(c=0\).  Applying this to
  \(QTQ\) gives \(c_{ij}=0\).  Since \(i<j\) were arbitrary, every strictly
  upper triangular matrix coefficient of \(T\) is zero, and the lower
  triangular coefficients were already zero.  Thus \(T\) is diagonal.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite Schur-window pinching lemma]
  \label{lem:finite-schur-window-compactness-modulus}
  Fix a window size \(N\ge1\) and a trace-norm bound \(R>0\).  Let
  \(\mathcal U_N\) denote the strictly upper triangular \(N\times N\)
  matrices, and define
  \[
    \Phi_{N,R}(\delta)
    :=
    \sup\left\{
      \|U\|_1:
      \begin{array}{l}
        D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_N),\ U\in\mathcal U_N,\\[2pt]
        \|D+U\|_1\le R,\\[2pt]
        0\le \|D+U\|_1-
        \sum_{k=1}^N |d_k|\le\delta
      \end{array}
    \right\},
  \]
  with the supremum over the empty set interpreted as \(0\).  Then
  \(\Phi_{N,R}(\delta)<\infty\), it is nondecreasing in \(\delta\), and
  \[
    \Phi_{N,R}(\delta)\longrightarrow0
    \qquad(\delta\downarrow0).
  \]
  Hence, on this specified finite Schur window and within the specified
  trace-norm bound, an excess
  \(\|D+U\|_1-
  \sum_{k=1}^N|d_k|\le\delta\) controls the whole strict upper triangular
  window by the modulus \(\Phi_{N,R}\).  The modulus depends on \(N\) and
  \(R\); no dimension-free, determinant-intrinsic, or global orbit-distance
  modulus is asserted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Weyl--Ky Fan gives
  \(\sum_k|d_k|\le\|D+U\|_1\), so any matrix in the defining set satisfies
  \(\|D\|_1\le R\) and hence
  \(\|U\|_1\le\|D+U\|_1+
  \|D\|_1\le2R\).  Thus all feasible pairs lie in a compact subset of the
  finite-dimensional space of diagonal-plus-strictly upper triangular
  matrices.

  Let
  \[
    E(D,U):=\|D+U\|_1-
      \sum_{k=1}^N |d_k| .
  \]
  This function is continuous and nonnegative.  If \(E(D,U)=0\), then the
  finite-dimensional version of the two-block pinching argument in
  \Cref{lem:trace-class-triangular-equality-pinching} applies to each pair of
  coordinates: the diagonal estimate and trace-norm contractivity of the
  two-block pinching force equality on every two-by-two compression, and the
  identity
  \[
    \left\|\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\0&b\end{pmatrix}\right\|_1^2
    =(|a|+|b|)^2+|c|^2
  \]
  then forces each strict upper triangular entry \(c\) to vanish.  Hence
  \(U=0\) on the zero-excess set.

  If \(\Phi_{N,R}(\delta)\not\to0\), there are
  \(\rho>0\), \(\delta_m\downarrow0\), and feasible pairs \((D_m,U_m)\) with
  \(\|U_m\|_1\ge\rho\).  Compactness gives a convergent subsequence with limit
  \((D,U)\).  Continuity gives \(E(D,U)=0\), hence \(U=0\), contradicting
  \(\|U\|_1\ge\rho\).  Monotonicity follows directly from the definition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Schatten triangular equality pinching]
  \label{lem:schatten-triangular-equality-pinching}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\).  Let \(T\in\mathcal S_p(H)\), and let
  \((e_k)_{k\ge1}\) be an orthonormal basis in which \(T\) is upper
  triangular, with diagonal entries \(d_k=\langle Te_k,e_k\rangle\).  Then
  \[
    \sum_{k\ge1}|d_k|^p\le \|T\|_p^p.
  \]
  If equality holds, then \(T\) is diagonal in this basis.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The displayed inequality is the Schatten Weyl--Fan diagonal inequality,
  applied to the triangular representation.  Equivalently, the diagonal
  sequence is weakly majorized by the singular-value sequence and the convex
  function \(t\mapsto t^p\) gives the stated \(p\)-sum bound.

  It remains to record the equality case without appealing to an abstract
  equality theorem for weak majorization.  First consider a two-by-two upper
  triangular matrix
  \[
    B=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\0&b\end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  Let \(x_1\ge x_2\) be the decreasing rearrangement of \((|a|,|b|)\), and
  let \(\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\) be the singular values of \(B\).  Then
  \[
    \sigma_1\sigma_2=|ab|=x_1x_2,
    \qquad
    \sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2=x_1^2+x_2^2+|c|^2.
  \]
  If \(x_1x_2=0\), then \(\sigma_2=0\) and
  \(\sigma_1^2=x_1^2+|c|^2\), so \(c\ne0\) gives
  \(\|B\|_p^p>x_1^p+x_2^p=|a|^p+|b|^p\).  If \(x_1x_2>0\), put
  \(P=x_1x_2\) and write
  \[
    \sigma_1=\sqrt P e^u,
    \quad \sigma_2=\sqrt P e^{-u},
    \qquad
    x_1=\sqrt P e^v,
    \quad x_2=\sqrt P e^{-v},
  \]
  with \(u,v\ge0\).  The squared-sum identity says
  \(2P\cosh(2u)=2P\cosh(2v)+|c|^2\), hence \(c\ne0\) implies \(u>v\).
  Since
  \[
    t\longmapsto P^{p/2}(e^{pt}+e^{-pt})
  \]
  is strictly increasing for \(t>0\) and is larger at every positive
  \(t\) than at \(0\), again \(c\ne0\) gives
  \(\|B\|_p^p>|a|^p+|b|^p\).  Thus for every \(1<p<\infty\),
  \[
    \left\|\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\0&b\end{pmatrix}\right\|_p^p
    \ge |a|^p+|b|^p,
  \]
  with equality if and only if \(c=0\).

  Now assume \(\|T\|_p^p=\sum_k|d_k|^p\).  Fix \(i<j\), and let \(Q\) be
  the projection onto \(\operatorname{span}\{e_i,e_j\}\).  Pinching by the
  orthogonal decomposition \(H=QH\oplus(1-Q)H\) is contractive on
  \(\mathcal S_p\), and the Schatten \(p\)-power of a block diagonal operator
  is the sum of the block \(p\)-powers, so
  \[
    \|QTQ\|_p^p+\|(1-Q)T(1-Q)\|_p^p\le \|T\|_p^p.
  \]
  Applying the diagonal inequality to the two diagonal blocks gives the reverse
  lower bound
  \[
    |d_i|^p+|d_j|^p+
    \sum_{k\ne i,j}|d_k|^p
    \le
    \|QTQ\|_p^p+\|(1-Q)T(1-Q)\|_p^p.
  \]
  Both inequalities are therefore equalities.  In particular,
  \[
    \|QTQ\|_p^p=|d_i|^p+|d_j|^p.
  \]
  In the ordered basis \((e_i,e_j)\), upper triangularity gives
  \[
    QTQ=\begin{pmatrix}d_i&c_{ij}\\0&d_j\end{pmatrix},
  \]
  and the two-by-two equality case forces \(c_{ij}=0\).  Since \(i<j\) were
  arbitrary, every strictly upper triangular coefficient is zero.  Thus \(T\)
  is diagonal in the chosen basis.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Collapsed spectral multiplicities]
  \label{cor:trace-class-rigidity-consequences}
  \label{prop:collapsed-spectral-multiplicities}
  Under the equivalent hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization}, the Fredholm determinant remembers
  precisely the collapsed non-zero spectral multiplicity function
  \(M_{\mathcal D}\).  Different cyclic presentations with the same
  \(M_{\mathcal D}\) therefore give the same determinant, the same trace
  budget, and the same normal minimizer class \(L_{\mathcal D}\oplus0\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  This is the zero-divisor and normal-classification part of
  \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization}; the budget identity follows from the
  root-sum computation preceding that theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Nonnormality after realization]
  \label{rem:nonnormal-obstruction-after-realization}
  The determinant fixes algebraic multiplicities away from zero but not the
  non-zero Jordan partitions or any quasinilpotent zero summand.  These are
  boundary data only; the compact finite-skeleton statement is isolated in
  \Cref{sec:finite-nonzero-nonnormal-skeletons}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Stability and finite-dimensional shadows}

Two standard consequences are useful, but they should not obscure the main
statement.  First, if a Ringrose Schur basis is fixed and
\(\|T\|_1\le b_1(\mathcal D)+\varepsilon\), then every fixed finite block of
strictly upper triangular Schur entries is controlled by a finite-window
modulus only after the window size and a trace-norm bound have been fixed.  In
particular, for a two-by-two compression
\(
  \begin{psmallmatrix}a&x\\0&b\end{psmallmatrix}
\)
one has
\[
  \left\|\begin{pmatrix}a&x\\0&b\end{pmatrix}\right\|_1
  \ge |a|+|b|,
\]
with equality only when \(x=0\).  More generally,
\Cref{lem:finite-schur-window-compactness-modulus} gives, for each specified
window size \(N\) and bound \(R\), a modulus \(\Phi_{N,R}\) controlling the
strict Schur window by the finite-window excess.  The dependence on
\((N,R)\) is part of the statement.  The next theorem records the corresponding
infinite-dimensional distance-to-orbit result.  Its hypotheses deliberately
include an externally supplied Schur-tail modulus; consequently the theorem is
a genuine conditional orbit-distance theorem, not an intrinsic consequence of
the determinant alone.

\begin{theorem}[Tail-localized infinite-dimensional orbit distance]
  \label{thm:tail-localized-orbit-distance}
  Assume the equivalent conditions of
  \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization}, and let
  \(\mathcal M_{\mathcal D}\) be the unitary orbit of the normal minimizer class
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\oplus0\).  Let \((T_\nu)_\nu\) be trace-class operators,
  possibly acting on different separable Hilbert spaces, such that
  \[
    \det(I-zT_\nu)=D_{\mathcal D}(z),
    \qquad
    \varepsilon_\nu:=\|T_\nu\|_1-b_1(\mathcal D)\longrightarrow0 .
  \]
  For each \(\nu\), choose a Ringrose upper triangular basis whose diagonal has
  the determinant-forced collapsed non-zero multiset and zeros in every
  remaining position; the non-zero and zero positions need not be ordered by
  sector.  Write the triangular matrix as \(A^{(\nu)}=[a_{ij}^{(\nu)}]\), let
  \(P_N^{(\nu)}\) be the projection onto the first \(N\) chosen basis vectors,
  and suppose that there are integers \(N_\nu\to\infty\) for which
  \[
    d_\nu:=\sum_{k>N_\nu}|a_{kk}^{(\nu)}|\longrightarrow0,
    \qquad
    c_\nu:=\|T_\nu-P_{N_\nu}^{(\nu)}T_\nu P_{N_\nu}^{(\nu)}\|_1
      \longrightarrow0 .
  \]
  Put \(\eta_\nu:=\max\{d_\nu,c_\nu\}\).  Then, for every \(\nu\),
  \[
  \begin{split}
    \dist_1(T_\nu,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D})
    \le{}&2\eta_\nu \\
    &+N_\nu\sqrt{(N_\nu-1)(\|T_\nu\|_1+
      \eta_\nu)(\varepsilon_\nu+2\eta_\nu)} .
  \end{split}
  \]
  In particular, if
  \[
    N_\nu\sqrt{N_\nu-1}
      \sqrt{\varepsilon_\nu+2\eta_\nu}\longrightarrow0,
  \]
  then
  \[
    \dist_1(T_\nu,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D})\longrightarrow0 .
  \]
  More generally, if a class \(\mathfrak C\) of operators in the same
  determinant fiber is equipped with chosen Ringrose bases and constants
  \(B<\infty\), \(\omega(N)\downarrow0\) such that
  \[
    \|T\|_1\le B,
    \qquad
    \sum_{k>N}|a_{kk}^T|\le\omega(N),
    \qquad
    \|T-P_N^TT P_N^T\|_1\le\omega(N)
  \]
  for all \(T\in\mathfrak C\), then every
  \(T\in\mathfrak C\) satisfies the uniform bound
  \[
  \begin{split}
    \dist_1(T,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D})
    \le{}&2\omega(N)\\
    &+N\sqrt{(N-1)(B+
      \omega(N))(\|T\|_1-b_1(\mathcal D)+2\omega(N))}
  \end{split}
  \]
  for every \(N\ge1\).  Hence, if
  \[
    N\sqrt{(N-1)(B+
      \omega(N))\omega(N)}\longrightarrow0,
  \]
  then
  \[
    \sup_{\substack{T\in\mathfrak C\\
      \|T\|_1-b_1(\mathcal D)\le\delta}}
      \dist_1(T,\mathcal M_{\mathcal D})\longrightarrow0
    \qquad(\delta\downarrow0).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Fix one operator \(T=T_\nu\) and suppress \(\nu\).  Let \(N=N_\nu\),
  \(P=P_N^{(\nu)}\), \(A=[a_{ij}]\), and \(\eta=\eta_\nu\).  Put
  \(A_N=PTP\) and
  \[
    \delta_N:=\|A_N\|_1-\sum_{k=1}^N|a_{kk}| .
  \]
  The diagonal Weyl--Fan estimate gives \(\delta_N\ge0\).  The compression-tail
  hypothesis gives \(\|A_N\|_1\le\|T\|_1+
  \eta\), while the diagonal-tail hypothesis gives
  \[
    \sum_{k=1}^N|a_{kk}|
    \ge b_1(\mathcal D)-\eta .
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    0\le\delta_N\le \|T\|_1+
      \eta-(b_1(\mathcal D)-\eta)
    =\varepsilon+2\eta .
  \]

  For \(1\le i<j\le N\), pinch \(A_N\) to the two-dimensional coordinate block
  on \(\{i,j\}\) and the remaining one-dimensional coordinate blocks.  Trace-norm
  contractivity of pinching gives
  \[
    \left\|
      \begin{pmatrix}a_{ii}&a_{ij}\\0&a_{jj}\end{pmatrix}
    \right\|_1
    +\sum_{\substack{k\le N\\k\ne i,j}}|a_{kk}|
    \le\|A_N\|_1 .
  \]
  Hence the two-by-two excess is at most \(\delta_N\).  The identity
  \[
    \left\|
      \begin{pmatrix}a&x\\0&b\end{pmatrix}
    \right\|_1^2=(|a|+|b|)^2+|x|^2
  \]
  implies
  \[
    |a_{ij}|^2
    \le 2\|A_N\|_1\delta_N
    \le 2(\|T\|_1+
      \eta)(\varepsilon+2\eta).
  \]
  If \(D_N=\operatorname{diag}(a_{11},\ldots,a_{NN})\), then
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    \|A_N-D_N\|_1
    &\le \sqrt N\,\|A_N-D_N\|_2 \\
    &\le
    N\sqrt{(N-1)(\|T\|_1+
      \eta)(\varepsilon+2\eta)} .
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  The diagonal entries of the chosen Ringrose representation consist of the
  determinant-forced collapsed eigenvalues, counted algebraically, and zeros
  elsewhere. Hence the diagonal operator
  \(S^\ast:=\operatorname{diag}(a_{11},a_{22},\ldots)\), after reattaching any
  discarded zero reducing summand, is a normal minimizer in
  \(\mathcal M_{\mathcal D}\).  Its initial diagonal truncation is
  \(S_N:=D_N\oplus0\), and
  \[
    \|T-S_N\|_1
    \le \|T-A_N\|_1+
      \|A_N-D_N\|_1
    \le \eta+
      N\sqrt{(N-1)(\|T\|_1+
      \eta)(\varepsilon+2\eta)} .
  \]
  The omitted diagonal tail satisfies
  \(\|S^\ast-S_N\|_1=\sum_{k>N}|a_{kk}|=d_\nu\le\eta\).  Therefore adding that
  tail gives an element of \(\mathcal M_{\mathcal D}\) and proves the displayed
  distance bound.
  Since \(\|T_\nu\|_1\to b_1(\mathcal D)\), the stated compatibility condition
  forces the right-hand side to tend to zero.

  The uniform assertion is the same estimate with
  \(\eta=\omega(N)\), \(\varepsilon=
  \|T\|_1-b_1(\mathcal D)\), and \(\|T\|_1\le B\).  Given
  \(\rho>0\), choose \(N\) so large that the two terms involving
  \(\omega(N)\) are less than \(\rho/2\) when \(\delta=0\); this is possible
  by the displayed compatibility condition.  With that \(N\) fixed, the same
  bound is less than \(\rho\) for all sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), proving
  the final limit.
\end{proof}

Second, when the collapsed support is finite, the possible singular-value
vectors in any fixed dimension are exactly the classical Weyl--Horn body for
the zero-padded eigenvalue vector.  Thus all fixed-dimensional symmetric-gauge
minimization statements reduce to Horn's theorem and its usual equality cases;
there is no cross-dimensional Horn inverse system in this paper.



\subsection{Conventions for fibers and zero summands}

The unrestricted trace-class fiber of a function \(D\) is the class of all
\(T\in\mathcal S_1\) satisfying \(D(z)=\det(I-zT)\).  This is the large fiber in
which \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization} proves the trace-norm lower bound
and its equality case.  It contains normal diagonal realizations, non-zero
Jordan perturbations, and trace-class quasinilpotent summands at zero; the
theorem identifies only the equality class in this large fiber.

Three smaller quotients will be mentioned, and none is an additional
determinant invariant.  Imposing normality reduces the data to the non-zero
collapsed multiset \(M_{\mathcal D}\), with arbitrary zero summands appended.
Keeping the cyclic presentation \(\mathcal D\) records more than the determinant
sees, because \(\mathcal D\mapsto M_{\mathcal D}\) is many-to-one.  The appendix
then forgets the zero sector and records only the finite non-zero Riesz
skeleton, namely nilpotent similarity classes on the non-zero root spaces.

The Schatten result below is narrower in a different way: it is a
prescribed-budget theorem for the regularized finite products actually written
there, not a natural-domain classification of all \(\det_r\)-fibers.
Accordingly, cross-dimensional Horn systems, global orbit-distance estimates
without tail control, unrestricted regularized determinant domains, and the
quasinilpotent zero sector remain boundary topics rather than extra main
theorems.
\subsection{Examples of collapse and sharpness}

We include a few elementary examples because they make clear why the theorem is
stated in terms of the collapsed multiplicity function rather than the original
list of cyclic factors.  If
\[
  (1-\beta z^2)(1+\beta z^2)=1-\beta^2z^4,
\]
then the two displayed cyclic presentations need not be remembered separately
by the determinant.  What is remembered is the multiset of roots of
\(x^2=\beta\) together with the roots of \(x^2=-\beta\), which is the same as
the multiset of roots of \(x^4=\beta^2\), with the appropriate multiplicities.
The canonical normal operator is therefore attached to this collapsed root
multiset.  A choice of square or fourth roots is only a basis labelling of the
same normal diagonal summand.

A second example shows why the summability condition is not cosmetic.  Suppose
that the data consist of factors \((1-\beta_jz)\), each with multiplicity one.
Then the finite-subset products \(\prod_{j\in F}(1-\beta_jz)\) converge locally
uniformly to a trace-class Fredholm determinant exactly when
\(\sum_j|\beta_j|<\infty\).  If this sum diverges, the formal product may still
make sense in a smaller analytic or formal category, but it cannot be the
Fredholm determinant of a trace-class operator: the non-zero eigenvalues would
have to include the \(\beta_j\), counted with multiplicity, and Weyl's
inequality would force their absolute sum to be finite.  The general cyclic
threshold is the same observation after replacing each factor by all roots of
\(x^{n_j}=\beta_j\).

The equality statement is also sharp.  For a single factor
\((1-\beta z)^m\), the diagonal normal block \(\beta I_m\) and the Jordan block
\(J_m(\beta)\) have the same determinant factor.  If \(m>1\), the latter is
nonnormal and has larger trace norm unless the nilpotent part vanishes.  Thus
minimal trace norm selects the diagonal block, while the determinant alone
allows the whole finite Jordan family.  Appending a nilpotent block at zero
makes the trace norm arbitrarily large without changing the determinant at all.
This is why the paper separates determinant-fiber minimization from any claim
of full nonnormal classification.

Finally, the finite-state symbolic motivation lies at the boundary of the
trace-class theorem.  For a nontrivial primitive finite shift, primitive orbit
counts grow fast enough that \(\sum_n n p_n(A)=\infty\).  The reciprocal zeta
identity is still a meaningful finite-state dynamical identity, but it is not a
summable cyclic zero divisor of the kind classified by
\Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization}.  This separation prevents the paper
from importing dynamical claims that are irrelevant to the Fredholm determinant
fiber under study.

\subsection{Proof details behind the trace-class package}

We spell out the two points in \Cref{thm:cyclic-fredholm-realization} where the
cyclic notation can otherwise obscure the classical argument.  The first is
convergence.  If \(F\subset J\) is finite and \(L_F=\bigoplus_{j\in F}L_j\),
then the trace-norm tail satisfies
\[
  \|L_{\mathcal D}-L_F\|_1
  =\sum_{j\notin F}m_jn_j|\beta_j|^{1/n_j}.
\]
Thus finite partial cyclic products form a Cauchy net in the trace-norm
Fredholm determinant topology.  The standard estimate
\[
  |\det(I+A)-\det(I+B)|
  \le \|A-B\|_1
      \exp(1+\|A\|_1+\|B\|_1)
\]
for trace-class perturbations, applied to \(A=-zL_F\) and
\(B=-zL_{\mathcal D}\) on compact \(z\)-sets, gives the required locally
uniform convergence.  No summation over a preferred ordering of \(J\) is
involved; the determinant is the limit over finite subsets.

The second point is the equality case.  Let \((d_k)\) be the diagonal sequence
of a Ringrose triangular representation of \(T\) as supplied by
\Cref{lem:ordered-ringrose-triangular-representation}.  Its non-zero diagonal subsequence is
the collapsed non-zero eigenvalue multiset, counted with algebraic
multiplicity, and every remaining diagonal entry is zero; no ordering of the
zero positions is prescribed.  Weyl--Ky Fan gives
\[
  \sum_k |d_k| \le \sum_k s_k(T)=\|T\|_1.
\]
Equality in the global determinant-fiber bound is exactly
\(\|T\|_1=\sum_k|d_k|\).  The equality conclusion is not obtained by an
unproved passage from the global Weyl--Ky Fan inequality to equality in every
finite compression; it is the separate trace-class pinching statement
\Cref{lem:trace-class-triangular-equality-pinching}.  Its proof uses the
contractivity of two-block Schur pinching in \(\mathcal S_1\) to isolate any
coordinate pair and then the elementary identity, for
\(B=\begin{psmallmatrix}a&c\\0&b\end{psmallmatrix}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \|B\|_1^2
  &=(\sigma_1+\sigma_2)^2
    =\Tr(B^*B)+2\sqrt{\det(B^*B)} \\
  &=\abs{a}^2+\abs{b}^2+\abs{c}^2+2\abs{ab}
    =(\abs{a}+\abs{b})^2+\abs{c}^2 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus equality \(\|B\|_1=\abs a+\abs b\) holds exactly when \(c=0\), and the
lemma removes every strictly upper triangular Schur coefficient of \(T\).  The
remaining zero spectral summand is diagonal with zero diagonal, hence is the
zero operator.  This is why equality in the trace budget is stronger than
equality of determinants: it rules out all nonnormal determinant-invisible
directions.

The same discussion explains the near-minimal statements used later.  The
precise finite-dimensional form is
\Cref{lem:finite-schur-window-compactness-modulus}: after fixing a window size
\(N\) and a trace-norm bound \(R\), the strict Schur window
\(U\) satisfies
\[
  \|U\|_1\le
  \Phi_{N,R}\left(
    \|D+U\|_1-
    \sum_{k=1}^N |d_k|
  \right),
  \qquad
  \Phi_{N,R}(\delta)\to0.
\]
Thus the modulus is explicitly tied to the chosen finite window and the bounded
domain \(\|D+U\|_1\le R\).  This is a chosen-basis statement.  Without an
external condition controlling the Schur tail, it does not become an intrinsic
global operator-distance estimate to the unitary orbit of the normal model.

\subsection{A prescribed-budget Schatten extension}

For \(1<p<\infty\) let \(r=\lceil p\rceil\) and set
\[
  b_p(\mathcal D):=\sum_{j\in J}m_j n_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}.
\]
For each finite \(F\Subset J\), define the finite-subset
\emph{regularized} cyclic product
\[
  P_F^{(r)}(z):=
  \prod_{j\in F}
  \prod_{\lambda^{n_j}=\beta_j}
  \left[
    (1-z\lambda)
    \exp\!\left(\sum_{q=1}^{r-1}\frac{(z\lambda)^q}{q}\right)
  \right]^{m_j}.
\]
Equivalently, this is \(\det_r(I-zL_F)\) for the finite cyclic model
attached to \(F\).  Collapsing the roots in each cyclic block gives the shorter
formula with the exponential
\(\exp(m_j\sum_{1\le q\le (r-1)/n_j}\beta_j^qz^{qn_j}/q)\), but the rootwise
display above is the definition used for the convergence argument.  Thus
\(P_F^{(1)}(z)=\prod_{j\in F}(1-\beta_jz^{n_j})^{m_j}\), while for
\(r>1\) the exponential factors are part of the prescribed finite product.
The product-realized prescribed Schatten class is
\[
  \mathcal P_{p,\mathcal D}(H)
  :=\left\{T\in\mathcal S_p(H):
  P_F^{(r)}\longrightarrow \det_r(I-zT)
  \text{ locally uniformly on }\CC\right\}.
\]
This definition is made without assuming \(b_p(\mathcal D)<\infty\); it uses
only the prescribed finite products and the regularized determinant of the
candidate \(\mathcal S_p\)-operator.  The finite-budget hypothesis below is a
sufficient hypothesis under which the cyclic model exists in \(\mathcal S_p\)
and the product-realized class agrees with the corresponding model determinant
fiber.
The symbol \(\mathcal F_{p,\mathcal D}(H)\) is used only under the finite-budget
hypothesis.  Indeed, if \(b_p(\mathcal D)<\infty\), then the diagonal cyclic
model satisfies \(L_{\mathcal D}\in\mathcal S_p\).  Since
\(r=\lceil p\rceil\ge p\), this also gives
\(L_{\mathcal D}\in\mathcal S_r\), because the non-zero root sequence is bounded
and a finite \(p\)-sum implies a finite \(r\)-sum after separating the finitely
many roots of modulus greater than \(1\).  Hence the model determinant
\(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\) is well-defined, and only then we define
\[
  \mathcal F_{p,\mathcal D}(H)
  :=\left\{T\in\mathcal S_p(H):
  \det_r(I-zT)=\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\right\}.
\]
The theorem below proves that, under the same hypothesis, the prescribed finite
products converge to \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\), and hence that these two
classes coincide:
\[
  \mathcal P_{p,\mathcal D}(H)=\mathcal F_{p,\mathcal D}(H).
\]
The point of the product-realized class is that the finite products are the
regularized products \(P_F^{(r)}\), not the ordinary products
\(\prod_{j\in F}(1-\beta_jz^{n_j})^{m_j}\) unless \(r=1\).
The following theorem is intentionally localized to the finite-subset
regularized cyclic-product class with finite prescribed budget.  It is not a
classification of the whole natural \(\det_r\)-domain.

\begin{theorem}[Prescribed-budget Schatten extension]
  \label{thm:schatten-determinant-fiber-minimality}
  Let \(1<p<\infty\), \(r=\lceil p\rceil\), and assume
  \(b_p(\mathcal D)<\infty\).  Then the cyclic model
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\) belongs to \(\mathcal S_p\),
  \[
    \sum_{\lambda\ne0}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\abs{\lambda}^{p}
    =b_p(\mathcal D),
  \]
  the net \(P_F^{(r)}\) converges locally uniformly to
  \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\), whose zero divisor here means the collapsed
  multiplicity function rather than an ordinary \(D_{\mathcal D}\)-limit, the classes
  \(\mathcal P_{p,\mathcal D}(H)\) and
  \(\mathcal F_{p,\mathcal D}(H)\) coincide, and this regularized determinant
  has the non-zero zero divisor prescribed by the collapsed multiplicity
  function \(M_{\mathcal D}\).

  Within this common finite-budget class, every realization
  satisfies
  \[
    \|T\|_p^p\ge b_p(\mathcal D).
  \]
  Equality holds if and only if \(T\) is unitarily equivalent to
  \(L_{\mathcal D}\oplus0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The membership statement follows directly from the diagonal model: each root
  of \(x^{n_j}=\beta_j\) has modulus \(|\beta_j|^{1/n_j}\), so the
  \(p\)-Schatten sum is exactly \(b_p(\mathcal D)\).  The same summability
  makes the collapsed root sequence bounded, since for every \(\eta>0\) only
  finitely many collapsed roots can satisfy \(\abs\lambda\ge\eta\).  Since
  \(r=\lceil p\rceil\ge p\), boundedness and the finite \(p\)-sum imply
  \[
    \sum_{\lambda\ne0}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\abs{\lambda}^{r}<\infty,
  \]
  after separating finitely many roots with \(\abs\lambda>1\).  Fix \(R>0\).  Separate the
  finite set of roots for which \(R\abs\lambda>1/2\).  On the remaining tail,
  put
  \[
    E_r(w):=(1-w)\exp\!\left(\sum_{q=1}^{r-1}\frac{w^q}{q}\right).
  \]
  For \(\abs w\le1/2\), using the principal logarithm,
  \[
    \log E_r(w)
    =\log(1-w)+\sum_{q=1}^{r-1}\frac{w^q}{q}
    =-\sum_{q=r}^{\infty}\frac{w^q}{q},
  \]
  so \(\abs{\log E_r(w)}\le C_r\abs w^r\).  Therefore, uniformly for
  \(\abs z\le R\),
  \[
    \sum_{\lambda\text{ in the tail}}
    M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\abs{\log E_r(z\lambda)}
    \le C_{r,R}
    \sum_{\lambda\text{ in the tail}}
    M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\abs\lambda^r,
  \]
  and the right side tends to zero with the tail.  The finite-subset
  logarithmic sums are thus uniformly absolutely Cauchy on compact sets, and
  exponentiation gives
  \(P_F^{(r)}\to\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\) locally uniformly.
  Consequently \(L_{\mathcal D}\in\mathcal P_{p,\mathcal D}(H)\).  Moreover,
  if \(T\in\mathcal P_{p,\mathcal D}(H)\), the defining limit equals both
  \(\det_r(I-zT)\) and \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\), so
  \(T\in\mathcal F_{p,\mathcal D}(H)\); the reverse inclusion is immediate from
  the just-proved convergence to \(\det_r(I-zL_{\mathcal D})\).
  The definition of \(\det_r\) shows that its zeros away from the origin are exactly
  \(z=\lambda^{-1}\), with order equal to algebraic multiplicity, hence the
  same collapsed divisor \(M_{\mathcal D}\).

  For a realization \(T\) in the common class, the same zero-divisor comparison gives
  the non-zero algebraic multiplicities \(M_{\mathcal D}\).  The Schatten
  Weyl--Fan inequality gives
  \[
    \sum_{\lambda\ne0}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\abs{\lambda}^{p}
    \le \|T\|_p^p,
  \]
  proving the lower bound.  If equality holds, choose a Ringrose
  triangularization as supplied by \Cref{lem:ordered-ringrose-triangular-representation}:
  the non-zero diagonal subsequence is the collapsed non-zero eigenvalue
  multiset, counted with algebraic multiplicity, and every remaining diagonal
  entry is zero; no ordering of the zero positions is prescribed.  The diagonal
  \(p\)-sum in this triangularization is exactly
  \(b_p(\mathcal D)\), so equality is precisely
  \(\|T\|_p^p=\sum_k|d_k|^p\).  By the Schatten triangular equality pinching
  lemma, \Cref{lem:schatten-triangular-equality-pinching}, all strictly upper
  triangular Schur coefficients vanish.  Thus the non-zero root spaces are
  diagonal normal blocks, and the zero spectral part is a diagonal operator
  with zero diagonal, hence the zero operator.  Therefore equality is exactly
  the normal cyclic model up to a zero summand.  Conversely, that model plainly
  attains the displayed budget.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Finite-dimensional and regularized perspectives}

We close the main section by making explicit how the finite-dimensional and
regularized statements should be read.  These paragraphs replace the longer
chain of corollaries one might otherwise state separately.

When \(M_{\mathcal D}\) has finite support, choose an integer \(N\) at least as
large as \(\sum_{\lambda}M_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)\).  The eigenvalue vector in dimension \(N\)
is obtained by listing the non-zero roots with their collapsed multiplicities
and padding by zeros.  Horn's theorem describes exactly which singular-value
vectors can occur for \(N\times N\) matrices with that eigenvalue vector.  In
particular, every unitarily invariant norm is minimized at the normal diagonal
matrix with those eigenvalues.  This is not an additional infinite-dimensional
classification: it is the fixed-dimensional matrix shadow of the determinant
fiber.  If \(N\) changes, one is changing the ambient Horn problem by adding
more zero eigenvalues, and no projective compatibility across all dimensions is
asserted.

The same fixed-dimensional viewpoint clarifies the finite non-zero skeleton.
In a finite matrix fiber, the determinant fixes only the characteristic
polynomial away from zero.  The algebraic similarity classes over each
non-zero eigenvalue are then indexed by partitions of the corresponding
algebraic multiplicity.  The normal minimizer is the partition into ones at
every non-zero eigenvalue.  Other partitions are valid determinant-fiber
points, but they carry nilpotent Jordan directions and therefore lose the
trace-norm equality case.  This is precisely the algebraic bookkeeping isolated
in the appendix.

For \(\det_r\), the reason for keeping the word ``prescribed'' in the
Schatten theorem is that a regularized determinant is not merely the ordinary
Fredholm product with a different convergence condition.  The factor attached
to an eigenvalue \(\lambda\) is
\[
  (1-z\lambda)
  \exp\left(\sum_{q=1}^{r-1}\frac{(z\lambda)^q}{q}\right),
\]
so the zero divisor is unchanged but the analytic function also contains
regularizing exponential data.  The theorem uses only the zero-divisor part for
rigidity and keeps the finite cyclic product realization as part of the
hypothesis.  This avoids the stronger and generally different problem of
classifying all operators in the natural domain of \(\det_r\) with a given
regularized determinant.

The budget condition \(b_p(\mathcal D)<\infty\) is exactly the Schatten analogue
of the trace-class threshold.  Each cyclic block contributes
\(m_j n_j|\beta_j|^{p/n_j}\) to \(\|L_{\mathcal D}\|_p^p\), because all roots of
\(x^{n_j}=\beta_j\) have modulus \(|\beta_j|^{1/n_j}\).  Conversely, if an
operator in the prescribed class has the same regularized zero divisor, the
Schatten Weyl--Fan inequality forces the same collapsed eigenvalue
\(p\)-sum to be no larger than \(\|T\|_p^p\).  Equality again means equality in
the triangular pinching argument, hence normality on the non-zero root spaces
and no Schatten mass in the zero sector.

This is the sense in which the Schatten result is parallel to the trace-class
main theorem while remaining local.  It gives the sharp budget and equality
class for the regularized cyclic-product class that the paper studies.  It deliberately
leaves open, and does not need, a natural-domain theorem that would compare
regularized determinants with the same zeros but different exponential
regularization data.

\begin{proposition}[Scope of the regularized determinant assertion]
  \label{prop:regularized-domain-boundary}
  The preceding theorem is a finite-budget localization theorem.  It does not
  assert that every element of the natural \(\det_r\)-domain with the same
  regularized determinant belongs to the prescribed cyclic-product class, nor
  does it classify determinant-invisible quasinilpotent zero summands.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Regularized determinants encode the non-zero zero divisor, but the
  exponential regularization also introduces lower-order trace data and is
  naturally defined on a larger Schatten domain.  The theorem therefore keeps
  the finite cyclic product data as part of the hypothesis.  Quasinilpotent
  zero-sector summands have no non-zero zero divisor and so remain outside the
  asserted classification, exactly as in the trace-class case.
\end{proof}
