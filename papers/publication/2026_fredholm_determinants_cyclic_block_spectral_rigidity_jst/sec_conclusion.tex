\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The paper's central object is the trace-class Fredholm determinant fiber of a
prescribed cyclic divisor.  The first main conclusion is the sharp threshold
\[
  \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}<\infty .
\]
At this threshold the normal cyclic-block model exists, and the determinant
collapses the divisor to the non-zero spectral multiplicity function
\(M_{\mathcal D}\).  Normal realisations are classified by this collapsed
multiset, up to zero summands.

The same collapsed data control the full trace-class fiber.  Any trace-class
operator with determinant \(D_{\mathcal D}\) has trace norm at least the trace
budget of the cyclic model.  Equality is rigid: the operator is unitarily
equivalent to the normal cyclic model, again up to a zero summand.  Thus the
main theorem chain identifies both the determinant-fiber minimum and the
minimizer class.

The stability results quantify this rigidity only in the stated gauges.  Near
minimality pinches fixed Schur entries after a Ringrose triangularisation has
been chosen.  The finite chosen-Schur-window estimate gives a truncated
operator-distance bound.  The infinite-dimensional distance criterion requires
both Schur-tail and determinant-tail control, as in
\Cref{thm:tail-controlled-trace-distance,cor:chosen-tail-stability-criterion,thm:uniform-chosen-schur-tail-modulus}; without such tails, no basis-free
global orbit-distance modulus is claimed.  In each fixed finite dimension,
\Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-trace-stability} gives the corresponding
square-root trace-distance estimate, and the exponent \(1/2\) is sharp.

When the collapsed support is finite, the fixed-dimensional matrix fibers have
a classical Weyl--Horn description.
\Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-weyl-horn-cyclic-determinant-fiber} identifies
the singular-value image with the Horn body of the zero-padded collapsed
eigenvalue vector \(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N}\).  Consequently
\Cref{cor:universal-finite-dimensional-symmetric-gauge-minimization,cor:exact-fixed-dimensional-schatten-budget-range}
recover universal unitarily invariant norm minimization by the normal model
and the exact fixed-dimensional Schatten-budget range.  These are
fixed-ambient-dimension consequences, not a stable inverse system of Horn
bodies.

The finite non-zero-skeleton results describe the residual nonnormal freedom
left by the determinant.  Away from zero, finite Jordan profiles are exactly
the algebraic partitions compatible with the collapsed multiplicities.  Such
profiles can approach the sharp trace-norm budget, but a nontrivial profile
cannot attain it.  In finite collapsed support,
\Cref{thm:finite-support-quasinilpotent-quotient} gives an algebraic quotient
by discarding the trace-class quasinilpotent zero sector, and
\Cref{lem:finite-support-quotient-closure-order} identifies the resulting
closure order with product dominance of nilpotent Jordan partitions.
\Cref{thm:nonnormal-quotient-degeneration} shows that approaching the normal
budget from a nonnormal quotient class requires similarities with condition
numbers tending to infinity.

The zero sector is intentionally not classified.  Trace-class
quasinilpotents do not change the Fredholm determinant and make determinant
fibers trace-norm unbounded.  They also do not erase the non-zero root-space
geometry: \Cref{thm:quasinilpotent-stabilization-nonzero-invariants} and
\Cref{thm:stable-nonzero-skeleton-classification} record the stable non-zero
Riesz-skeleton invariant.  The obstruction in
\Cref{prop:zero-sector-invisibility-obstruction} explains why the finite
quotient is not a full similarity classification of the whole trace-class
fiber.

Finally, the Schatten theory is a prescribed-budget regularized consequence.  For every
\(1<p<\infty\), the cyclic model satisfies the sharp threshold
\[
  \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}<\infty,
\]
with the corresponding regularized determinant factors for
\(\det_{\lceil p\rceil}\).  The minimization and equality statements hold in
the prescribed cyclic-product \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget fiber, as formalized in
\Cref{cor:regularized-controlled-extension-scope,cor:schatten-prescribed-budget-boundary,prop:schatten-natural-domain-budget-boundary}.  They are not a classification
of the whole natural \(\mathcal S_{\lceil p\rceil}\)-domain of the
regularized determinant.  In finite dimension,
\Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-schatten-stability} gives the associated
bounded-set stability modulus, preserving the same collapsed spectral
interpretation and variational rigidity as the trace-class theorem.
