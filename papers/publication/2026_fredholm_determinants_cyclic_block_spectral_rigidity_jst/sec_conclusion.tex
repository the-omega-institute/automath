\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The preceding sections separate three layers of the same Fredholm
determinant problem. The cyclic divisor first imposes the exact
trace-class threshold
\[
  \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}<\infty
\]
and, in the normal category, determines the cyclic-block model up to
unitary equivalence and zero summands. In the full trace-class category,
the same determinant fixes the sharp trace-norm infimum and the full
minimizer class, while the Schur-entry estimate records a quantitative
near-minimizer constraint in a Ringrose triangularisation. For finite
ambient dimension, this becomes an optimal square-root estimate for the
trace-norm distance to the unitary orbit of minimizers. Away from zero,
the determinant fixes only algebraic multiplicities; the possible finite
Jordan profiles are exactly the partitions of those multiplicities.
These profiles can be approached arbitrarily closely at the sharp
trace-norm budget, but a nontrivial profile cannot attain the budget.

The zero spectral point has a different role. Trace-class quasinilpotents
do not change the Fredholm determinant and make every determinant fiber
trace-norm unbounded, but they also do not erase the non-zero root-space
geometry: by
\Cref{thm:quasinilpotent-stabilization-nonzero-invariants}, stabilization
by trace-class quasinilpotents preserves the unitary equivalence class of
each non-zero generalized eigenspace restriction. The stable-skeleton
formulation in \Cref{thm:stable-nonzero-skeleton-classification} records
the resulting invariant of the whole non-zero root-space collection.
When the determinant-forced non-zero support is finite,
\Cref{thm:finite-support-quasinilpotent-quotient} turns this into a
quotient statement: after splitting off the Riesz root spaces at the
prescribed non-zero eigenvalues, the remaining summand is trace-class and
quasinilpotent, and the quotient by that zero sector is exactly the
collection of finite-dimensional nilpotent root-space classes. Thus the
determinant fiber is rigid at the level of the collapsed non-zero
spectral multiset, flexible at the level of allowed nonnormal Jordan
profiles, and still retains those profiles under quasinilpotent
stabilization at zero.

The Schatten theorem gives the corresponding sharp cyclic-block threshold
for every \(1<p<\infty\),
\[
  \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}<\infty,
\]
together with the explicit regularized determinant factors for
\(\det_{\lceil p\rceil}\). The companion Schatten minimizer theorem shows
that this is not only an existence threshold: in the full regularized
determinant fiber, equality in the sharp \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget again
forces normality and unitary equivalence to the cyclic model up to a zero
summand, and near equality forces the fixed Schur off-diagonal entries to
be small. In finite dimension,
\Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-schatten-stability} upgrades this entrywise
constraint to an orbit-level bounded-set stability theorem: within a
fixed regularized determinant fiber, \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget defect
tending to zero forces \(\mathcal S_p\)-distance to the whole minimizer
orbit to tend to zero. This extends the cyclic-block realization picture
beyond trace class, while preserving the same collapsed spectral
interpretation and the same variational rigidity of the prescribed
non-zero divisor.
