\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The preceding sections separate three layers of the same Fredholm
determinant problem. The cyclic divisor first imposes the exact
trace-class threshold
\[
  \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}<\infty
\]
and, in the normal category, determines the cyclic-block model up to
unitary equivalence and zero summands. In the full trace-class category,
the same determinant fixes the sharp trace-norm infimum and the full
minimizer class, while the Schur-entry estimate and
\Cref{prop:finite-chosen-schur-window-distance-bound} record quantitative
near-minimizer constraints in a chosen Ringrose triangularisation. With the
additional chosen-tail hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:tail-controlled-trace-distance,cor:chosen-tail-stability-criterion}
this entrywise pinching becomes an infinite-dimensional distance criterion.
For each fixed finite ambient dimension, it becomes a square-root estimate
for the trace-norm distance to the unitary orbit of minimizers, and the
exponent \(1/2\) cannot be improved uniformly in that fixed-dimensional
regime. In finite collapsed support there is also an exact
Weyl--Horn description of the same fixed-dimensional matrix fibers:
\Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-weyl-horn-cyclic-determinant-fiber} identifies
their singular-value image with the Horn body of the padded collapsed
eigenvalue vector, and
\Cref{cor:universal-finite-dimensional-symmetric-gauge-minimization,cor:exact-fixed-dimensional-schatten-budget-range}
record the resulting universal unitarily invariant norm minimization and
the exact fixed-dimensional Schatten-budget range. Away from zero, the
determinant fixes only algebraic multiplicities; the possible finite
Jordan profiles are exactly the algebraic partitions of those
multiplicities.
These profiles can be approached arbitrarily closely at the sharp
trace-norm budget, but a nontrivial profile cannot attain the budget.

The zero spectral point has a different role. Trace-class quasinilpotents
do not change the Fredholm determinant and make every determinant fiber
trace-norm unbounded, but they also do not erase the non-zero root-space
geometry: by
\Cref{thm:quasinilpotent-stabilization-nonzero-invariants}, stabilization
by trace-class quasinilpotents preserves the unitary equivalence class of
each non-zero generalized eigenspace restriction. The stable-skeleton
formulation in \Cref{thm:stable-nonzero-skeleton-classification} records
the resulting stable unitary invariant of the whole non-zero root-space
collection.
When the determinant-forced non-zero support is finite,
\Cref{thm:finite-support-quasinilpotent-quotient} turns this into a
formal algebraic finite non-zero-skeleton quotient statement: after splitting
off the Riesz root spaces at the prescribed non-zero eigenvalues, the
remaining summand is trace-class and quasinilpotent, and after ignoring that
zero sector the finite skeleton is exactly the product of finite-dimensional
nilpotent \(GL\)-orbit classes. Thus the
determinant fiber is rigid at the level of the collapsed non-zero
spectral multiset, flexible at the level of allowed nonnormal Jordan
profiles, and still retains those profiles under quasinilpotent
stabilization at zero. The finite-support quotient also has a variational
boundary and an explicit closure order:
\Cref{thm:finite-support-quotient-closure-order} identifies the stratum
closures with the product dominance order on the nilpotent Jordan
partitions, while \Cref{thm:nonnormal-quotient-degeneration} shows that a
nonnormal quotient class has the same sharp trace-norm infimum as the
normal class, but every sequence approaching that infimum must pass
through similarities with condition numbers tending to infinity. Hence
the normal minimizer is not simply another representative of a nonnormal
class; it is the unique closed quotient stratum and the boundary limit of
the remaining strata in an unbounded similarity gauge.

The tail-controlled trace-norm theorem is conditional: distance to the
normal minimizer class is controlled only after choosing a Ringrose basis and
controlling both the Schur compression tail and the determinant-forced diagonal
tail in that basis. Without such tail hypotheses, the paper asserts
fixed-entry Schur stability, the finite chosen-Schur-window estimate, and the
fixed-dimensional trace estimate, not a basis-free global
infinite-dimensional orbit-distance modulus.

The Schatten theorem gives the corresponding sharp cyclic-block threshold
for every \(1<p<\infty\),
\[
  \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}<\infty,
\]
together with the explicit regularized determinant factors for
\(\det_{\lceil p\rceil}\). As formalized in
\Cref{cor:regularized-controlled-extension-scope}, this is the exact
\(\mathcal S_p\)-membership condition for the cyclic model and the sharp
lower bound for the prescribed cyclic-product \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget
regularized determinant problem; it is not a classification of when an
arbitrary \(\mathcal S_{\lceil p\rceil}\)-operator has a regularized
determinant, nor of the full \(\det_{\lceil p\rceil}\)-fiber without the
prescribed product and budget restrictions. The companion boundary statement
\Cref{cor:schatten-minimizer-prescribed-budget-scope} makes the same
restriction explicit for Schatten minimizer rigidity. The companion
Schatten minimizer theorem shows that, once the
prescribed cyclic divisor and \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget are fixed, equality in
that prescribed cyclic-product regularized determinant problem's sharp
\(\mathcal S_p\)-budget again
forces normality and unitary equivalence to the cyclic model up to a zero
summand, and near equality forces the fixed Schur off-diagonal entries to
be small. In finite dimension,
\Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-schatten-stability} upgrades this entrywise
constraint to a finite-dimensional bounded-set modulus theorem:
within a fixed finite-dimensional prescribed cyclic-product regularized
determinant fiber and an \(\mathcal S_p\)-bounded set, \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget defect tending to
zero forces \(\mathcal S_p\)-distance to the whole minimizer orbit to
tend to zero through the inverse two-by-two Schatten pinching modulus. This
extends the cyclic-block realization picture
beyond trace class, while preserving the same collapsed spectral
interpretation and the same variational rigidity of the prescribed
non-zero divisor.
