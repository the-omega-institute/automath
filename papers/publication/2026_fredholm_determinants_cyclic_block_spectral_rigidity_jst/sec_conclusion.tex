\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The paper's central object is the trace-class Fredholm determinant fiber of a
prescribed cyclic divisor.  The first main conclusion is the sharp threshold
\[
  \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{1/n_j}<\infty .
\]
At this threshold the normal cyclic-block model exists, and the determinant
collapses the divisor to the non-zero spectral multiplicity function
\(M_{\mathcal D}\).  Normal realisations are classified by this collapsed
multiset, up to zero summands.

The same collapsed data control the full trace-class fiber.  Any trace-class
operator with determinant \(D_{\mathcal D}\) has trace norm at least the trace
budget of the cyclic model.  Equality is rigid: the operator is unitarily
equivalent to the normal cyclic model, again up to a zero summand.  Thus the
main theorem chain identifies both the determinant-fiber minimum and the
minimizer class.

The stability results quantify this rigidity only in the stated gauges.  Near
minimality pinches fixed Schur entries after a Ringrose triangularisation has
been chosen.  The finite chosen-Schur-window estimate gives a truncated
operator-distance bound.  The infinite-dimensional distance criterion requires
both Schur-tail and determinant-tail control, as in
\Cref{thm:tail-controlled-trace-distance},
\Cref{cor:chosen-tail-stability-criterion}, and
\Cref{thm:uniform-chosen-schur-tail-modulus}; without such tails, no
basis-free global orbit-distance modulus is claimed.  In each fixed finite dimension,
\Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-trace-stability} gives the corresponding
square-root trace-distance estimate, and the exponent \(1/2\) is sharp.

When the collapsed support is finite, the fixed-dimensional matrix fibers have
a classical Weyl--Horn description.
\Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-weyl-horn-cyclic-determinant-fiber} identifies
the singular-value image with the Horn body of the zero-padded collapsed
eigenvalue vector \(\Lambda_{\mathcal D,N}\), and
\Cref{prop:finite-horn-subordinate-trace-chain} records that this body is
subordinate to the same collapsed multiplicity function used in the
trace-class minimum.  Consequently
\Cref{cor:universal-finite-dimensional-symmetric-gauge-minimization}
and \Cref{cor:exact-fixed-dimensional-schatten-budget-range} recover universal
unitarily invariant norm minimization by the normal model and the exact
fixed-dimensional Schatten-budget range. In that fixed-dimension
statement, empty fibers give empty ranges, the non-empty scalar fiber is the
only bounded case, and every non-empty fiber in dimension at least two is
unbounded above. These are fixed-ambient-dimension consequences, not a stable
inverse system of Horn bodies.

The finite non-zero-skeleton results describe the residual nonnormal freedom
left by the determinant.  Away from zero, finite Jordan profiles are exactly
the algebraic partitions compatible with the collapsed multiplicities.  Such
profiles can approach the sharp trace-norm budget, but a nontrivial profile
cannot attain it.  The trace-class quasinilpotent zero sector is deliberately
not classified: it does not change the determinant, makes fibers trace-norm
unbounded, and serves only as the stabilizing obstruction needed to isolate
the non-zero root-space skeleton.

Finally, the Schatten theory is a prescribed-budget consequence.
For every \(1<p<\infty\), the cyclic model satisfies the sharp threshold
\[
  \sum_{j\in J}m_jn_j\abs{\beta_j}^{p/n_j}<\infty,
\]
with the corresponding regularized determinant factors for
\(\det_{\lceil p\rceil}\).  Conversely, an \(\mathcal S_p\) operator whose
regularized determinant is obtained from this prescribed cyclic product must
already have this finite budget bounded by its \(\mathcal S_p\)-norm.  The
minimization and equality statements therefore hold in the prescribed
cyclic-product \(\mathcal S_p\)-budget fiber, as formalized in
\Cref{cor:regularized-controlled-extension-scope},
\Cref{thm:schatten-forced-prescribed-budget-obstruction},
\Cref{prop:prescribed-schatten-chain-scope},
\Cref{cor:schatten-prescribed-budget-boundary}, and
\Cref{prop:schatten-natural-domain-budget-boundary}.  The cited statements
keep this prescribed-budget hypothesis separate from the larger natural
\(\mathcal S_{\lceil p\rceil}\)-domain of the regularized determinant.  In
finite dimension,
\Cref{thm:finite-dimensional-schatten-stability} gives the associated
bounded-set stability modulus, preserving the same collapsed spectral
interpretation and variational rigidity as the trace-class theorem.  For
ordinary symmetric-gauge minimization, the equality claims are exactly those
of \Cref{cor:finite-dimensional-gauge-minimizer-taxonomy}: arbitrary gauges
give attainment, while trace and strictly convex Schatten norms give the
rigid normal minimizer class.

Thus the central conclusion is determinant-fiber rigidity: Fredholm
determinant germs collapse cyclic data to an intrinsic non-zero spectral
multiset, that multiset fixes the sharp trace budget, equality in the full
trace-class fiber forces the normal cyclic model, and fixed-dimensional
Weyl--Horn geometry describes the associated singular-value bodies.
