\subsection{Zeckendorf value and the numerical realization of stable addresses}\label{subsec:emergent-arithmetic-zeckendorf-value-address}

For \(w=w_1\cdots w_m\in X_m\), define its Zeckendorf value by
\[
V_m(w)=\sum_{k=1}^m w_k F_{k+1}.
\]
When \(0\le N\le F_{m+2}-1\), the Zeckendorf expansion \(Z(N)=(c_1,c_2,\dots)\) has no nonzero digits beyond the \(m\)-th place by Lemma \ref{lem:zeckendorf-range-bijection}. Hence
\[
Z_m(N):=\pi_m(Z(N))=(c_1,\dots,c_m)\in X_m
\]
is well defined, and
\[
N=V_m\bigl(Z_m(N)\bigr).
\]
Thus \(X_m\) may be regarded as a canonical address space for the integers in the range \(0\le N<F_{m+2}\).

\subsubsection{Finite-resolution arithmetic and modular structure}
To turn finite-resolution readout into a closed algebraic object, we define the operations on \(X_m\) by pulling modular arithmetic back along the value map. The next theorem records the quotient reason for the modulus \(F_{m+2}\): at depth \(m\), adding the single local period relation that kills the next Fibonacci scale collapses the free configuration monoid exactly to residues modulo \(F_{m+2}\), and \(X_m\) is the canonical residue-fold transversal.

\begin{definition}[Finite-resolution arithmetic]\label{def:finite-resolution-arithmetic}
For any \(w,v\in X_m\), define
\[
w\boxplus_m v:=Z_m\Bigl((V_m(w)+V_m(v))\bmod F_{m+2}\Bigr),
\qquad
w\boxtimes_m v:=Z_m\Bigl((V_m(w)\cdot V_m(v))\bmod F_{m+2}\Bigr),
\]
and write \(0_m:=Z_m(0)\), \(1_m:=Z_m(1)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Modular structure of finite-resolution arithmetic]
\label{thm:finite-resolution-mod}
The map \(V_m:X_m\to\ZZ/(F_{m+2}\ZZ)\) is an isomorphism of commutative rings
\[
(X_m,\boxplus_m,\boxtimes_m)\cong\bigl(\ZZ/(F_{m+2}\ZZ),+,\times\bigr).
\]
In particular, finite-resolution arithmetic on \(X_m\) is uniquely determined by modularizing values and then pulling back the canonical representatives.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma \ref{lem:zeckendorf-range-bijection} and the definition of \(Z_m(N)\), the map \(V_m\) is a bijection from \(X_m\) onto \(\{0,1,\dots,F_{m+2}-1\}\), hence may be identified with a bijection onto \(\ZZ/(F_{m+2}\ZZ)\). The definitions of \(\boxplus_m\) and \(\boxtimes_m\) immediately give
\[
V_m(w\boxplus_m v)\equiv V_m(w)+V_m(v)\pmod{F_{m+2}},
\qquad
V_m(w\boxtimes_m v)\equiv V_m(w)\,V_m(v)\pmod{F_{m+2}}.
\]
Since these operations are transported from the residue ring along a bijection, they endow \(X_m\) with a commutative ring structure for which \(V_m\) is a ring isomorphism. Uniqueness follows from bijectivity: any operations making \(V_m\) into a ring homomorphism must coincide with the pullback formulas above.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite quotient and residue-fold classification]\label{thm:finite-quotient-residue-fold-classification}
Fix \(m\ge 1\) and set \(M_m:=F_{m+2}\). Let \(\equiv_m\) be the smallest congruence on the commutative monoid \(\mathcal{D}\) generated by the Fibonacci relations of Definition \ref{def:fib-congruence} together with the depth-\(m\) period relation
\[
e_{m+1}\equiv_m 0.
\]
Then the residue value map
\[
\overline{\mathrm{Val}}_m:\mathcal{D}/\!\equiv_m\longrightarrow \ZZ/(M_m\ZZ),
\qquad
[a]_m\longmapsto [\mathrm{Val}(a)]_{M_m},
\]
is an isomorphism of commutative monoids. Moreover, every \(\equiv_m\)-class contains a unique representative in \(X_m\), namely
\[
\Fold_m(a):=Z_m\bigl(\mathrm{Val}(a)\bmod M_m\bigr).
\]
Under this canonical transversal, addition of quotient classes is exactly \(\boxplus_m\). If multiplication of finite classes is required to be compatible with residue multiplication through \(\overline{\mathrm{Val}}_m\), then it is forced and is exactly \(\boxtimes_m\). Consequently the finite-resolution ring on \(X_m\) is the unique fold-aware arithmetic descending from the local Fibonacci relations plus the single period obstruction \(e_{m+1}\equiv_m0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The ordinary Fibonacci relations preserve \(\mathrm{Val}\) by Proposition \ref{prop:val-invariant}. The extra relation also preserves value modulo \(M_m\), because
\[
\mathrm{Val}(e_{m+1})=F_{m+2}=M_m\equiv0\pmod {M_m}.
\]
Thus \(\overline{\mathrm{Val}}_m\) is a well-defined homomorphism of commutative monoids.

Let \(\varepsilon:=[e_1]_m\). As in Theorem \ref{thm:monoid-quotient-is-N}, the Fibonacci relations imply
\[
[e_k]_m=F_{k+1}\varepsilon\qquad(k\ge1).
\]
The additional relation gives \(M_m\varepsilon=[e_{m+1}]_m=0\). Hence the map
\[
\alpha:
\ZZ/(M_m\ZZ)\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}/\!\equiv_m,
\qquad
[n]_{M_m}\longmapsto n\varepsilon,
\]
is well defined. It is a homomorphism, and
\[
\overline{\mathrm{Val}}_m(\alpha([n]_{M_m}))=[n]_{M_m},
\]
since \(\mathrm{Val}(e_1)=F_2=1\). Conversely, for any \(a=\sum_k a_ke_k\in\mathcal{D}\),
\[
[a]_m
=\sum_k a_k[e_k]_m
=\left(\sum_k a_kF_{k+1}\right)\varepsilon
=\mathrm{Val}(a)\varepsilon
=\alpha\bigl([\mathrm{Val}(a)]_{M_m}\bigr).
\]
Thus \(\alpha\) and \(\overline{\mathrm{Val}}_m\) are inverse isomorphisms.

By Lemma \ref{lem:zeckendorf-range-bijection}, the map \(V_m:X_m\to\{0,1,\dots,M_m-1\}\) is bijective. Therefore \(Z_m(r)\) is defined and unique for each residue representative \(0\le r<M_m\). For a configuration \(a\), the word \(\Fold_m(a)=Z_m(\mathrm{Val}(a)\bmod M_m)\) lies in the same \(\equiv_m\)-class because the two configurations have the same image under the isomorphism \(\overline{\mathrm{Val}}_m\). If two words \(w,v\in X_m\) lie in the same class, then \(V_m(w)\equiv V_m(v)\pmod {M_m}\). Both values lie between \(0\) and \(M_m-1\), hence they are equal; the bijectivity of \(V_m\) gives \(w=v\). This proves that \(X_m\) is the unique canonical transversal.

For \(w,v\in X_m\), quotient addition followed by the transversal sends their classes to the unique word of residue
\[
V_m(w)+V_m(v)\bmod M_m,
\]
which is exactly the definition of \(w\boxplus_m v\). The same argument with residue products shows that any multiplication compatible with \(\overline{\mathrm{Val}}_m\) must send \((w,v)\) to the unique word of residue \(V_m(w)V_m(v)\bmod M_m\), namely \(w\boxtimes_m v\). Hence the finite ring operations are forced by the quotient skeleton and the canonical residue-fold representative.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite local normalization modulo \texorpdfstring{$F_{m+2}$}{F m+2}]\label{prop:finite-local-normalization-mod-F}
Fix \(m\ge 1\), set \(M_m:=F_{m+2}\), and let \(\equiv_m\) be generated by the Fibonacci moves together with \(e_{m+1}\equiv_m0\), as in Theorem \ref{thm:finite-quotient-residue-fold-classification}. For every configuration \(a\in\mathcal D\) there is a finite sequence of elementary symmetric local rewrites
\[
e_2\longleftrightarrow 2e_1,
\qquad
e_{j}\longleftrightarrow e_{j-1}+e_{j-2}\quad(j\ge3),
\qquad
e_{m+1}\longleftrightarrow 0,
\]
applied in additive contexts, which starts at \(a\) and ends at
\[
\Fold_m(a)=Z_m\bigl(\mathrm{Val}(a)\bmod M_m\bigr)\in X_m.
\]
Thus the residue-fold representative in Theorem \ref{thm:finite-quotient-residue-fold-classification} is not only algebraically classified: it is reached by a finite local resolution procedure using exactly the Fibonacci relations and the depth-\(m\) period relation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(N:=\mathrm{Val}(a)\). First expand every occurrence of each basis vector in \(a\) down to the first scale, exactly as in Proposition \ref{prop:finite-local-fold-normalization}: replace \(e_2\) by \(2e_1\), and for \(j\ge3\) replace \(e_j\) by \(e_{j-1}+e_{j-2}\), recursively. This strictly decreases the largest index of the occurrence being expanded, hence terminates, and it sends \(a\) by finitely many local Fibonacci moves to \(Ne_1\).

Divide \(N=qM_m+r\), with \(q\ge0\) and \(0\le r<M_m\). The same downward expansion sends \(e_{m+1}\) to \(M_me_1\), because \(\mathrm{Val}(e_{m+1})=F_{m+2}=M_m\). Reversing that finite expansion gives a finite local sequence from \(M_me_1\) to \(e_{m+1}\). Hence, inside the additive context \((q-1)M_me_1+re_1\), one may rewrite one block \(M_me_1\) to \(e_{m+1}\) and then use the period move \(e_{m+1}\to0\). Repeating this operation \(q\) times gives a finite sequence from \(Ne_1\) to \(re_1\).

Let \(c:=Z_m(r)\). Since \(0\le r<M_m\), Lemma \ref{lem:zeckendorf-range-bijection} gives \(c\in X_m\) and \(V_m(c)=r\). Expanding the finitely many nonzero basis vectors of \(c\) downward gives a finite Fibonacci rewrite sequence from \(c\) to \(re_1\); reversing it gives a finite sequence from \(re_1\) to \(c\). Concatenating the three finite sequences
\[
a\rightsquigarrow Ne_1\rightsquigarrow re_1\rightsquigarrow c
\]
proves the claimed local reachability. By definition, \(c=Z_m(r)=Z_m(\mathrm{Val}(a)\bmod M_m)=\Fold_m(a)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Universal residue-fold factorization and operation rigidity]\label{thm:residue-fold-factorization-rigidity}
Fix \(m\ge1\), set \(M_m:=F_{m+2}\), and let \(\Fold_m:\mathcal D\to X_m\) be the residue-fold map from Theorem \ref{thm:finite-quotient-residue-fold-classification}. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{(no hidden finite fold invariants)} for every set \(Y\), composition with \(\Fold_m\) gives a bijection between maps \(g:X_m\to Y\) and maps \(f:\mathcal D\to Y\) that are constant on \(\equiv_m\)-classes:
\[
g\longmapsto g\circ\Fold_m,
\qquad
\{g:X_m\to Y\}\cong\{f:\mathcal D\to Y:\ a\equiv_m b\Rightarrow f(a)=f(b)\};
\]
  \item \textbf{(finite operation classification)} for every arity \(r\ge1\), composition with \(V_m\) gives a bijection between operations \(T:X_m^r\to X_m\) and residue operations \(\tau:(\ZZ/(M_m\ZZ))^r\to\ZZ/(M_m\ZZ)\), namely
\[
V_m\bigl(T(w_1,\dots,w_r)\bigr)
=\tau\bigl(V_m(w_1),\dots,V_m(w_r)\bigr);
\]
  \item under this classification, the only binary operations compatible with quotient addition and quotient multiplication are respectively \(\boxplus_m\) and \(\boxtimes_m\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, a fixed-resolution table or automaton on \(X_m\) has mathematical content here only through its compatibility with the residue fold; the classified quotient skeleton, rather than transport along an arbitrary bijection, is what forces the finite arithmetic used in the sequel.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First note that \(\Fold_m\) is the identity on \(X_m\). Indeed, if \(w\in X_m\), then \(V_m(w)\in\{0,\dots,M_m-1\}\) and Lemma \ref{lem:zeckendorf-range-bijection} gives
\[
\Fold_m(w)=Z_m(V_m(w))=w.
\]

Let \(f:\mathcal D\to Y\) be constant on \(\equiv_m\)-classes. By Theorem \ref{thm:finite-quotient-residue-fold-classification}, every \(a\in\mathcal D\) satisfies \(a\equiv_m\Fold_m(a)\). Hence
\[
f(a)=f(\Fold_m(a)).
\]
Thus \(f=g\circ\Fold_m\), where \(g\) is the restriction of \(f\) to \(X_m\). This factorization is unique because \(\Fold_m\) is the identity on \(X_m\). Conversely, if \(f=g\circ\Fold_m\) and \(a\equiv_m b\), then Theorem \ref{thm:finite-quotient-residue-fold-classification} gives \(\Fold_m(a)=\Fold_m(b)\), so \(f(a)=f(b)\). This proves the first assertion.

For the operation classification, use the bijection \(V_m:X_m\to\ZZ/(M_m\ZZ)\) from Theorem \ref{thm:finite-resolution-mod}. Given \(T:X_m^r\to X_m\), define
\[
\tau(\alpha_1,
\dots,\alpha_r)
:=V_m\bigl(T(Z_m(\alpha_1),\dots,Z_m(\alpha_r))\bigr),
\]
where \(Z_m(\alpha)\) denotes the unique element of \(X_m\) with residue \(\alpha\). This \(\tau\) is forced by the displayed compatibility identity, because the entries \(w_i\) may be chosen as \(Z_m(\alpha_i)\). Conversely, given \(\tau\), the formula
\[
T_\tau(w_1,
\dots,w_r)
:=Z_m\bigl(\tau(V_m(w_1),\dots,V_m(w_r))\bigr)
\]
defines the unique operation inducing \(\tau\). This proves the second assertion.

If a binary operation is required to realize quotient addition, its residue operation is necessarily \((x,y)\mapsto x+y\) in \(\ZZ/(M_m\ZZ)\), so the preceding bijection gives exactly \(\boxplus_m\). If it is required to realize quotient multiplication, its residue operation is necessarily \((x,y)\mapsto xy\), and the same bijection gives exactly \(\boxtimes_m\). The final sentence is the stated interpretation of this classification.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite windows are residue folds, not stable truncations]\label{prop:finite-stable-compatibility-warning}
Fix \(m\ge 1\) and write \(M_m:=F_{m+2}\). Define the fold-aware residue projection
\[
\Pi_m:X_\infty\longrightarrow X_m,
\qquad
\Pi_m(c):=Z_m\bigl(\mathrm{Val}(c)\bmod M_m\bigr).
\]
Then \(\Pi_m\) is a semiring homomorphism from \((X_\infty,\oplus,\otimes)\) to \((X_m,\boxplus_m,\boxtimes_m)\). By contrast, the naive digit truncation \(\pi_m:X_\infty\to X_m\) is not, in general, compatible with finite-resolution arithmetic; already for \(m=1\) it fails to preserve addition.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(c,d\in X_\infty\), Theorems \ref{thm:add-definitional} and \ref{thm:mul-definitional} give
\[
\mathrm{Val}(c\oplus d)=\mathrm{Val}(c)+\mathrm{Val}(d),
\qquad
\mathrm{Val}(c\otimes d)=\mathrm{Val}(c)\mathrm{Val}(d).
\]
Reducing these identities modulo \(M_m\) and applying the definition of \(Z_m\) gives
\[
\Pi_m(c\oplus d)
=Z_m\bigl((\mathrm{Val}(c)+\mathrm{Val}(d))\bmod M_m\bigr)
=\Pi_m(c)\boxplus_m\Pi_m(d),
\]
because \(V_m(\Pi_m(c))\equiv \mathrm{Val}(c)\pmod {M_m}\), and similarly
\[
\Pi_m(c\otimes d)
=Z_m\bigl((\mathrm{Val}(c)\mathrm{Val}(d))\bmod M_m\bigr)
=\Pi_m(c)\boxtimes_m\Pi_m(d).
\]
The zero and one elements are also preserved, since \(\Pi_m(Z(0))=Z_m(0)=0_m\) and \(\Pi_m(Z(1))=Z_m(1)=1_m\). Hence \(\Pi_m\) is a semiring homomorphism.

For the failure of naive truncation, take \(m=1\), \(c=Z(1)=e_1\), and \(d=Z(2)=e_2\). In stable arithmetic,
\[
c\oplus d=Z(3)=e_3,
\]
so \(\pi_1(c\oplus d)=0_1\). On the other hand, \(\pi_1(c)=e_1\), \(\pi_1(d)=0_1\), and therefore
\[
\pi_1(c)\boxplus_1\pi_1(d)=e_1\boxplus_1 0_1=e_1\ne 0_1.
\]
Thus digit truncation is not a semiring homomorphism from the stable semiring to the finite-resolution ring. The correct cross-resolution map is the residue fold \(\Pi_m\), which first descends through the value modulo \(F_{m+2}\) and only then chooses the finite Zeckendorf representative.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[CRT splitting: canonical product decomposition at composite resolutions]\label{cor:crt-factorization}
Let \(m\ge 1\), set \(M:=F_{m+2}\), and write its prime factorization as
\[
M=\prod_{i=1}^{r} p_i^{k_i}
\qquad
(p_i\text{ pairwise distinct}).
\]
Then there is a ring isomorphism
\[
\ZZ/(M\ZZ)\cong \prod_{i=1}^{r}\ZZ/(p_i^{k_i}\ZZ),
\]
and therefore \((X_m,\boxplus_m,\boxtimes_m)\) is isomorphic to the same direct product ring. Its unit group likewise decomposes as
\[
(\ZZ/(M\ZZ))^\times\cong \prod_{i=1}^{r}(\ZZ/(p_i^{k_i}\ZZ))^\times.
\]
In particular, if \(r\ge 2\), then \(\ZZ/(M\ZZ)\) contains nontrivial idempotents and hence admits a canonical many-factor splitting in the ring-theoretic sense.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
All statements are standard consequences of the Chinese remainder theorem. The ring decomposition is the usual CRT isomorphism, the description of the unit group follows componentwise, and nontrivial idempotents occur exactly when the modulus has at least two coprime factors.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal depth for the simultaneous \(2/3/5\) axes]\label{prop:crt-235-min-depth}
Let \(M:=F_{m+2}\). Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(2,3,5\mid M\), equivalently \(30\mid M\);
  \item \(3,4,5\mid (m+2)\);
  \item \(60\mid (m+2)\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, under the finite-resolution arithmetic isomorphism
\[
(X_m,\boxplus_m,\boxtimes_m)\cong \ZZ/(F_{m+2}\ZZ)
\]
from Theorem \ref{thm:finite-resolution-mod}, if one requires simultaneous CRT visibility of the three basic congruence axes modulo \(2\), \(3\), and \(5\) inside a single modulus ring, then the smallest admissible resolution is
\[
\boxed{\ m_{\min}=58\qquad(m_{\min}+2=60)\ }.
\]
\leanverified{crt\_235\_min\_depth}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Note that \(2=F_3\), \(3=F_4\), and \(5=F_5\). By the classical identity
\[
\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)},
\]
we have for every \(n\) and every \(d\ge 1\),
\[
F_d\mid F_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\gcd(F_d,F_n)=F_d
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
F_{\gcd(d,n)}=F_d
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
d\mid n.
\]
Taking \(d=3,4,5\) and \(n=m+2\), we obtain
\[
2\mid F_{m+2}\Longleftrightarrow 3\mid(m+2),\qquad
3\mid F_{m+2}\Longleftrightarrow 4\mid(m+2),\qquad
5\mid F_{m+2}\Longleftrightarrow 5\mid(m+2).
\]
Therefore \(30\mid F_{m+2}\) if and only if \(3,4,5\mid(m+2)\), and this is equivalent to \(60\mid(m+2)\). The smallest solution is \(m+2=60\), hence \(m_{\min}=58\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A rigid structural trichotomy: field / local / CRT-split]\label{rem:ring-phase-trichotomy}
By Theorem \ref{thm:finite-resolution-mod}, the invertible internal symmetries at resolution \(m\) are naturally encoded by the unit group
\[
(X_m^\times,\boxtimes_m)\cong (\ZZ/(M\ZZ))^\times,
\qquad
M=F_{m+2},
\]
where
\[
X_m^\times:=\{w\in X_m:\ \exists\,w^{-1}\in X_m\text{ such that }w\boxtimes_m w^{-1}=1_m\}.
\]
Hence the most rigid notion of ``unification'' or ``symmetry breaking'' is a fact about the residue ring itself, rather than an a priori choice of a continuous Lie group:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{field} (maximal uniformity): \(M\) is prime, so the arithmetic is a field by Corollary \ref{cor:field-phase-fib-prime};
  \item \textbf{local}: \(M=p^k\) is a prime power, so the ring does not split by CRT but is not a field unless \(k=1\);
  \item \textbf{CRT-split}: \(M\) has at least two distinct prime factors, so the ring and its unit group decompose canonically as in Corollary \ref{cor:crt-factorization}.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Completable cross-scale unification: a Fibonacci-adic inverse-limit ring}\label{subsubsec:fib-adic-inverse-limit}
In general, the modulus at resolution \(m\) is \(M_m:=F_{m+2}\), but the sequence \((M_m)_{m\ge 1}\) is not itself a divisibility chain. Therefore, if one wants to make cross-scale compatibility precise in the form of an inverse limit, one must choose a subtower along which the moduli do divide one another.

\begin{lemma}[Obstruction to the full adjacent-resolution inverse limit]\label{lem:adjacent-resolution-obstruction}
For integers \(a,b\ge3\), one has
\[
F_a\mid F_b\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad a\mid b.
\]
Consequently the adjacent finite-resolution moduli \(M_m=F_{m+2}\) do not form a divisibility chain: for every \(m\ge1\),
\[
F_{m+2}\nmid F_{m+3}.
\]
In particular, there is no natural adjacent reduction homomorphism
\[
\ZZ/(F_{m+3}\ZZ)\longrightarrow \ZZ/(F_{m+2}\ZZ),
\qquad [x]_{F_{m+3}}\longmapsto [x]_{F_{m+2}},
\]
except in the impossible case \(F_{m+2}\mid F_{m+3}\). The dyadic tower below is therefore a necessary divisibility-compatible skeleton, not merely a cosmetic subsequence.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We use the standard identity \(\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}\). If \(a\mid b\), then \(\gcd(a,b)=a\), hence \(F_a=\gcd(F_a,F_b)\) and \(F_a\mid F_b\). Conversely, suppose \(F_a\mid F_b\) with \(a,b\ge3\). Then
\[
F_a=\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}.
\]
The Fibonacci sequence is strictly increasing from index \(2\) onward, and \(F_a>1\) for \(a\ge3\); hence \(\gcd(a,b)=a\), so \(a\mid b\).

Taking \(a=m+2\) and \(b=m+3\), the condition \(a\mid b\) would require \(m+2\mid m+3\), equivalently \(m+2\mid1\), which is impossible for \(m\ge1\). Thus \(F_{m+2}\nmid F_{m+3}\). Finally, the displayed formula on residue classes is well defined exactly when equality modulo \(F_{m+3}\) implies equality modulo \(F_{m+2}\), which is equivalent to \(F_{m+2}\mid F_{m+3}\). The preceding paragraph rules this out at adjacent resolutions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fibonacci divisibility chain]\label{lem:fib-divisibility-chain}
If \(a\mid b\), then \(F_a\mid F_b\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is a standard property of Fibonacci numbers. It follows immediately from
\[
\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}:
\]
if \(a\mid b\), then \(\gcd(a,b)=a\), hence \(\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_a\), which is equivalent to \(F_a\mid F_b\).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Dyadic subtower and the Fibonacci-adic ring]\label{def:fib-adic-ring}
Let
\[
n_k:=2^k,\qquad m_k:=n_k-2,\qquad M_k:=F_{n_k}=F_{m_k+2}\qquad(k\ge 2).
\]
By Lemma \ref{lem:fib-divisibility-chain}, we have \(M_k\mid M_{k+1}\), so there are natural surjective ring homomorphisms
\[
\rho_{k+1\to k}:\ZZ/(M_{k+1}\ZZ)\twoheadrightarrow \ZZ/(M_k\ZZ),
\qquad
[x]_{M_{k+1}}\longmapsto [x]_{M_k}.
\]
Define the inverse-limit ring

\begin{proposition}[Transported dyadic stable-address inverse system]\label{prop:transported-dyadic-stable-address-system}
For \(k\ge 2\), let \(m_k=2^k-2\) and \(M_k=F_{2^k}=F_{m_k+2}\) as in Definition \ref{def:fib-adic-ring}. Define
\[
\rho^X_{k+1\to k}:X_{m_{k+1}}\longrightarrow X_{m_k},
\qquad
\rho^X_{k+1\to k}(w):=Z_{m_k}\bigl(V_{m_{k+1}}(w)\bmod F_{2^k}\bigr).
\]
Then the square
\[
\begin{CD}
X_{m_{k+1}} @>{\rho^X_{k+1\to k}}>> X_{m_k}\\
@V{V_{m_{k+1}}}VV @VV{V_{m_k}}V\\
\ZZ/(M_{k+1}\ZZ) @>{\rho_{k+1\to k}}>> \ZZ/(M_k\ZZ)
\end{CD}
\]
commutes after identifying \(V_{m_j}\) with the finite-resolution ring isomorphism of Theorem \ref{thm:finite-resolution-mod}. Consequently the inverse limit
\[
X_{\mathrm{Fib},2}:=\varprojlim_k (X_{m_k},\rho^X_{k+1\to k})
\]
carries a unique commutative topological ring structure for which the coordinatewise value map
\[
\widehat V:X_{\mathrm{Fib},2}\longrightarrow \ZZ_{\mathrm{Fib},2},
\qquad
(w_k)_k\longmapsto \bigl([V_{m_k}(w_k)]_{M_k}\bigr)_k
\]
is an isomorphism of topological rings.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The residue \(V_{m_{k+1}}(w)\bmod F_{2^k}\) has a unique representative in \(\{0,\dots,M_k-1\}\), so \(Z_{m_k}\) is defined by Lemma \ref{lem:zeckendorf-range-bijection}. Applying the same lemma again gives
\[
V_{m_k}\bigl(\rho^X_{k+1\to k}(w)\bigr)
=V_{m_{k+1}}(w)\bmod M_k,
\]
which is exactly the residue projection
\[
\rho_{k+1\to k}\bigl([V_{m_{k+1}}(w)]_{M_{k+1}}\bigr)
=[V_{m_{k+1}}(w)]_{M_k}.
\]
This proves commutativity of the square. Since each \(V_{m_k}\) is a ring isomorphism by Theorem \ref{thm:finite-resolution-mod}, the maps \(\rho^X_{k+1\to k}\) are precisely the residue projections transported back to stable addresses; in particular they are surjective ring homomorphisms for the finite-resolution operations \(\boxplus_{m_k}\) and \(\boxtimes_{m_k}\).

Taking inverse limits of the commuting squares gives a bijection \(\widehat V\) from compatible stable-address threads to compatible residue threads. The ring operations and topology on \(X_{\mathrm{Fib},2}\) are therefore transported coordinatewise from \(\ZZ_{\mathrm{Fib},2}\). With these transported operations, \(\widehat V\) is an isomorphism of rings; because both inverse-limit topologies are the subspace topologies inside products of finite discrete sets and each coordinate map \(V_{m_k}\) is a bijection, \(\widehat V\) is also a homeomorphism.
\end{proof}

The next proposition records standard profinite consequences after the divisibility subtower has been chosen. The paper-specific point is not compactness itself, but the fold-aware maps \(\rho^X_{k+1\to k}\) in Proposition \ref{prop:transported-dyadic-stable-address-system}, which identify the finite residue quotients with compatible stable-address threads.

\begin{proposition}[Basic properties of the inverse-limit ring: finite quotients and a compact unit group]\label{prop:fib-adic-basic}
Let \(\ZZ_{\mathrm{Fib},2}\) be as in Definition \ref{def:fib-adic-ring}. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{(profinite completeness)} \(\ZZ_{\mathrm{Fib},2}\) is an inverse limit of finite rings and therefore a compact, totally disconnected commutative topological ring in the inverse-limit topology;
  \item \textbf{(finite-resolution quotients)} for each \(k\), there is a natural surjective ring homomorphism
\[
\pi_k:\ZZ_{\mathrm{Fib},2}\twoheadrightarrow \ZZ/(F_{2^k}\ZZ),
\]
and these projections satisfy the compatibility relation \(\rho_{k+1\to k}\circ \pi_{k+1}=\pi_k\);
  \item \textbf{(compactification of the unit groups)} the unit group \(\ZZ_{\mathrm{Fib},2}^\times\) is a compact topological group, naturally isomorphic to the inverse limit of the finite unit groups:
\[
\ZZ_{\mathrm{Fib},2}^\times\cong \varprojlim_k \bigl(\ZZ/(F_{2^k}\ZZ)\bigr)^\times.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Parts (1) and (2) are standard facts about profinite inverse limits: a product of finite discrete rings is compact, the inverse limit is a closed subset of that product, and the ring operations are inherited componentwise. For (3), if \(x=(x_k)_k\in \ZZ_{\mathrm{Fib},2}\) is invertible, then each coordinate \(x_k=\pi_k(x)\) is invertible because \(\pi_k\) is a ring homomorphism. Conversely, assume that every \(x_k\) is a unit and write \(y_k:=x_k^{-1}\). Since \(\rho_{k+1\to k}(x_{k+1})=x_k\), applying \(\rho_{k+1\to k}\) to \(x_{k+1}y_{k+1}=1\) shows that \(\rho_{k+1\to k}(y_{k+1})\) is an inverse of \(x_k\); by uniqueness of inverses, \(\rho_{k+1\to k}(y_{k+1})=y_k\). Thus \((y_k)_k\) belongs to the inverse limit and is the inverse of \(x\). Hence the unit group is exactly the inverse limit of the finite unit groups and is compact as a closed subgroup of a compact product. The nonstandard input from this paper is supplied earlier by the Fibonacci divisibility tower and the fold-aware stable-address projections, not by these general profinite conclusions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation with the projective limit of admissible prefixes]\label{rem:fib-adic-vs-syntax}
The ring \(\ZZ_{\mathrm{Fib},2}\) should be distinguished from the projective limit formed by admissible Fibonacci words under truncation. The latter is a syntactic object describing compatibility of visible prefixes in the Fibonacci shift, whereas \(\ZZ_{\mathrm{Fib},2}\) is an arithmetic object describing compatibility of finite quotient rings along a divisibility tower. These are complementary limiting constructions inside the same folding framework.
\end{remark}
