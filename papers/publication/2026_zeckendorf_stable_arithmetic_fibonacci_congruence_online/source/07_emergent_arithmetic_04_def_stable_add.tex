\subsection{Addition as normalization: from normal forms to a commutative monoid}\label{subsec:emergent-arithmetic-stable-add}

The preceding definition of \(\oplus\) is algebraic transport along the bijection \(\mathrm{Val}:X_\infty\to\NN\). The point of the present subsection is to separate that tautological level from the genuinely digit-level statements about normalization and finite-state realizability.

\begin{remark}[A clarification about ``tautology'': definitional isomorphism versus observable implementation]\label{rem:arith-not-novel-as-abstract}
One should acknowledge that Definition \ref{def:stable-add} transports the operation directly from \((\NN,+)\). In that sense, the statement that \(\mathrm{Val}\) is an isomorphism is \textbf{definitional} at the level of abstract algebra: it does not produce a monoid that is nonisomorphic to \((\NN,+)\).

The reason for recording the isomorphism anyway is twofold. First, it fixes notation and emphasizes that addition need not be treated as a primitive input. Second, the real mathematical content lies in two further statements that interface directly with the ``scan and read out'' viewpoint:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Orbit/folding content.} The congruence classes generated by local Fibonacci rewriting are exactly the value fibers, and the Zeckendorf language supplies the unique transversal; this is the content of Theorem \ref{thm:monoid-quotient-is-N} and Corollary \ref{cor:fold-as-section}.
  \item \textbf{Digit-level content.} The operation may be described as digitwise superposition followed by normalization, as made explicit in Corollary \ref{cor:add-as-fold}. This turns the abstract formula \(\mathrm{Val}^{-1}\) into a concrete, auditable normalization principle.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Frougny's online normalizer in the paper's coordinates]\label{lem:online-zeckendorf-specialization}
Use the Fibonacci numeration sequence \(U_i:=F_{i+2}\) for \(i\ge 0\), so that the paper's digit \(c_k\) at weight \(F_{k+1}\) is the usual Fibonacci-numeration digit at position \(i=k-1\). Let \(L\ge 1\), let \(s_1,\dots,s_L\in\{0,1,2\}\), and read the padded word from high weight to low weight as
\[
s_L,s_{L-1},\dots,s_1.
\]
There is a finite-state online normalizer, with delay \(3\) and a bounded terminal flush, whose output is the unique admissible word \(u\in X_\infty\) satisfying
\[
\mathrm{Val}(u)=\sum_{k=1}^{L}s_kF_{k+1}.
\]
Leading zero padding at positions \(k>L\) does not change either the value or the eventual output, except for harmless leading zeros in the high-to-low stream.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We use the specific Fibonacci result rather than an unspecified Pisot black box. Frougny proves that addition in base \(\tau=(1+\sqrt5)/2\) on the digit set \(\{0,1\}\) is realized by an on-line finite automaton with delay \(3\) \cite[Proposition~13]{Frougny1999OnlineAddition}. The same paper then translates this construction to the Fibonacci numeration system and states that addition on \(\{0,1\}\) in that system is computable by an on-line finite automaton with delay \(3\), with a terminal function giving the normal Fibonacci representation \cite[Corollary~4]{Frougny1999OnlineAddition}. The input alphabet of the transducer after pointwise addition of two binary input streams is exactly \(\{0,1,2\}\), and the output alphabet is \(\{0,1\}\).

The change from Frougny's usual positions \(i=0,1,\dots\) to the paper's positions \(k=1,2,\dots\) is only the index shift \(U_{k-1}=F_{k+1}\). Reading \(s_L,s_{L-1},\dots,s_1\) is therefore exactly the most-significant-to-least-significant convention used in on-line numeration. The cited automaton supplies a finite state set, transition rule, output rule, delay \(3\), and terminal flush for this fixed alphabet and fixed numeration system.

The normalizer is value preserving in the Fibonacci numeration system. Hence its output represents \(\sum_{k=1}^{L}s_kF_{k+1}\). By Theorem \ref{thm:zeckendorf-unique}, there is only one admissible binary representative of that value, namely the stated \(u\in X_\infty\). Since prepending leading zeros contributes no value and keeps the input inside the same finite alphabet, it cannot change the normalized representative; it only shifts the same on-line computation by leading zero symbols.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Digit-level realization of Zeckendorf addition with finite delay]\label{thm:zeckendorf-add-fst}
Let \(c,d\in X_\infty\) have finite support, and let
\[
m:=\max\bigl(\{k:\ c_k=1\text{ or }d_k=1\}\cup\{0\}\bigr)
\]
be the effective resolution. Choose any padding length \(L\ge m+2\), append zeros through position \(L\), and read the finite input synchronously from high Fibonacci weight to low Fibonacci weight:
\[
(c_L,d_L),(c_{L-1},d_{L-1}),\dots,(c_1,d_1)
\in(\{0,1\}^2)^L,
\]
where \(c_i=d_i=0\) for \(i>m\). There are a finite set of states \(Q\), an initial state \(q_0\), a transition/output rule and final-output map
\[
\tau:Q\times \{0,1\}^2\longrightarrow Q\times\{0,1\}^*,
\qquad
\omega:Q\longrightarrow \{0,1\}^*,
\]
and constants \(D,B\), independent of \(m,c,d\), such that after reading through input position \(i\) the transducer has irrevocably output all result digits in positions at least \(i-D\). After the final pair \((c_1,d_1)\) is read, the final-output map \(\omega\) emits the remaining low-order suffix of length at most \(B\) and the computation halts. Hence \(c\oplus d\) is computed in \(O(m)\) digit operations without constructing \(\mathrm{Val}(c)\) or \(\mathrm{Val}(d)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma \ref{lem:online-zeckendorf-specialization} to the digitwise sum
\[
s_i:=c_i+d_i\in\{0,1,2\}\qquad(1\le i\le L).
\]
The preliminary letter map \((a,b)\mapsto a+b\) from the synchronous pair alphabet \(\{0,1\}^2\) to \(\{0,1,2\}\) is itself finite-state and memoryless. Composing this letter map with the finite-state normalizer from the lemma gives the required transducer \((Q,q_0,\tau,\omega,D,B)\). The constants \(D\) and \(B\) are those of the fixed golden-ratio normalizer, enlarged if necessary by an absolute amount to absorb the terminal flush; they are independent of \(m,c,d\).

The output is the unique admissible word of value
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{L}(c_i+d_i)F_{i+1}
=\mathrm{Val}(c)+\mathrm{Val}(d).
\]
By Corollary \ref{cor:add-as-fold}, that word is exactly \(\Fold_\infty(c+d)=c\oplus d\). Leading zero padding through any \(L\ge m+2\) leaves the value unchanged and gives the online normalizer enough trailing high-order zeros to settle carries near the most significant end before the low-order scan proceeds. Since the machine performs one transition per input pair and then emits only a bounded suffix, the number of digit operations is \(O(m)\). Concrete Zeckendorf implementations of the same finite-state normalization principle are given by Ahlbach--Usatine--Frougny--Pippenger \cite{AhlbachUsatineFrougnyPippenger2013}; the Ostrowski formulation is also recorded in \cite{HieronymiTerry2018OstrowskiAddition}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-resolution addition is a residue fold with finite-state realization]\label{thm:finite-add-residue-fold-fst}
Fix \(m\ge 1\) and set \(M_m:=F_{m+2}\). Let \(c,d\in X_m\), extend them by zeros to elements of \(X_\infty\), and put
\[
a:=c+d\in\mathcal{D}.
\]
Then finite-resolution addition is obtained by the quotient-and-fold formula
\[
c\boxplus_m d
=Z_m\bigl(\mathrm{Val}(a)\bmod M_m\bigr)
=\Pi_m\bigl(\Fold_\infty(a)\bigr).
\]
Moreover this operation has a finite-state digit realization at fixed resolution: a finite transducer, depending only on \(m\) and the Fibonacci numeration system and not on \(c,d\), computes \(c\boxplus_m d\) from the padded synchronous digit stream for \((c,d)\) by online Zeckendorf addition followed by the residue fold \(\Pi_m\). Thus the finite operation is not naive truncation of the infinite sum, but descent through the congruence quotient modulo \(F_{m+2}\) followed by the unique Zeckendorf representative in \(X_m\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By definition of \(a\),
\[
\mathrm{Val}(a)=V_m(c)+V_m(d).
\]
The definition of \(\boxplus_m\) in finite-resolution arithmetic therefore gives
\[
c\boxplus_m d
=Z_m\bigl((V_m(c)+V_m(d))\bmod M_m\bigr)
=Z_m\bigl(\mathrm{Val}(a)\bmod M_m\bigr).
\]
On the other hand \(\Fold_\infty(a)=Z(\mathrm{Val}(a))\), so the definition of the fold-aware residue projection \(\Pi_m\) from Proposition \ref{prop:finite-stable-compatibility-warning} yields
\[
\Pi_m\bigl(\Fold_\infty(a)\bigr)
=Z_m\bigl(\mathrm{Val}(\Fold_\infty(a))\bmod M_m\bigr)
=Z_m\bigl(\mathrm{Val}(a)\bmod M_m\bigr),
\]
because folding preserves value. This proves the displayed quotient-and-fold formula.

For finite-state realizability, Theorem \ref{thm:zeckendorf-add-fst} computes \(\Fold_\infty(a)=c\oplus d\) from the synchronous input stream with bounded delay and finite memory. Since \(M_m\) is fixed once the resolution is fixed, the residue of the value modulo \(M_m\) can be tracked by a finite register with states in \(\ZZ/(M_m\ZZ)\): when an output digit \(u_k\) is produced by the online normalizer, update the register by adding \(u_kF_{k+1}\) modulo \(M_m\), using the Fibonacci weights modulo \(M_m\). At the end of the stream the register contains \(\mathrm{Val}(c\oplus d)\equiv\mathrm{Val}(a)\pmod {M_m}\). The bijection \(Z_m:\{0,
\dots,M_m-1\}\to X_m\) is a fixed finite lookup table at this resolution, so appending this lookup to the finite transducer outputs \(Z_m(\mathrm{Val}(a)\bmod M_m)=c\boxplus_m d\). All state sets are finite, and the construction uses the residue fold \(\Pi_m\) rather than digit truncation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Low-to-high scans versus online numeration convention]\label{rem:online-orientation}
The stable-address indices in the rest of the paper are written from low weight to high weight because this is convenient for the value formula \(\sum c_kF_{k+1}\). The online theorem above uses the standard numeration-theoretic convention of reading most significant digits first. This is only an interface convention: for a finite support input one first chooses the effective resolution \(L\), scans downward from \(L\), and then interprets the output back in the same stable-address coordinates.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Online realizability and non-parallelizability]\label{rem:online-not-parallel}
In the scan-based semantics, the existence of an online transducer means that readout may proceed unidirectionally in time while maintaining only finite memory. This fits the folding perspective adopted in the present paper. At the same time, addition in the golden-ratio numeration system, although online, is in general not parallelizable in the strong circuit-theoretic sense. In the present framework, this reflects the fact that stable readout has finite memory but still carries cross-scale dependence through carries and normalization. See \cite{Frougny1999OnlineAddition} for precise results and discussion.
\end{remark}
