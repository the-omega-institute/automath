\section{The reversed pair shift and bulk statistics}
\label{app:pressure}

This appendix constructs the four-state presentation used in Section~\ref{sec:gauge}. We keep the low-to-high convention of Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}, but pair words are read from high Fibonacci weights to low Fibonacci weights. This is the natural orientation for the synchronized construction.

\subsection{Reversed pair words}
For \(\omega=(\omega_1,\dots,\omega_M)\in\Om_M\), write
\[
z=\Fold_M(\omega)=(z_1,\dots,z_M)\in X_M.
\]
Define the \emph{reversed pair word}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reversed-pair}
\mathcal{R}_M(\omega)
:=
\bigl((\omega_M,z_M),(\omega_{M-1},z_{M-1}),\dots,(\omega_1,z_1)\bigr)
\in (\{0,1\}^2)^M.
\end{equation}
We abbreviate a pair symbol \((x,y)\in\{0,1\}^2\) by the two-character word \(xy\in\{00,01,10,11\}\).

Let \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pair}}\) be the set of all finite factors of all reversed pair words \(\mathcal{R}_M(\omega)\), \(M\ge 1\). The corresponding bi-infinite subshift is denoted
\[
\mathcal{P}\subset (\{00,01,10,11\})^{\ZZ}.
\]
Its coordinate process is exactly the bulk pair process \((X_t,Y_t)\) of Section~\ref{sec:gauge}, but read from high weights to low weights.

\begin{lemma}[High-side zero padding]\label{lem:zero-padding}
If \(\omega\in\Om_M\) and \(L\ge 1\), then the LH word
\[
\omega 0^L:=(\omega_1,\dots,\omega_M,\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{L\text{ times}})\in\Om_{M+L}
\]
has the same first \(M\) normalized digits as \(\omega\). If \(z=Z(N(\omega))\), then \(z_j=0\) for \(j\ge M+2\); hence the added high-order part is
\[
(z_{M+1},0,\dots,0).
\]
Consequently every factor in \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pair}}\) may be preceded by arbitrarily many pair symbols \(00\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Appending zeros at higher Fibonacci sites does not change \(N(\omega)\), hence it does not change any normalized digit. Moreover,
\[
N(\omega)\le \sum_{j=1}^M\Fib_{j+1}=\Fib_{M+3}-2<\Fib_{M+3},
\]
so the normalized expansion has no digit above position \(M+1\). In high-to-low order the added part therefore consists of \(L-1\) pair symbols \(00\), followed by \((0,z_{M+1})\). Taking \(L\) arbitrarily large gives the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The carry transducer}

Consider a raw word and its full Zeckendorf normalization, both padded at high weights so that all normalized digits are visible. Read the pair word from high to low. Immediately before the digit of weight \(\Fib_k\), let
\[
P=\text{(raw value already read)}-\text{(normalized value already read)}
\]
and let \(y^-\) be the preceding normalized digit, with a virtual initial value \(y^-=0\). The four carry states are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{state}&\text{condition at the cut before }\Fib_k\\\hline
00&P=0,\ y^-=0,\\
01&P=-\Fib_{k+1},\\
10&P=-\Fib_k,\ y^-=0,\\
11&P=0,\ y^-=1.
\end{array}
\]
In state \(01\), the value of \(y^-\) need not be retained because every admissible next output digit is \(0\).

\begin{proposition}[Carry transition table]\label{prop:transitions}
The possible carry transitions, labeled by the current raw/normalized pair, are exactly
\begin{align*}
00 &\xrightarrow{00} 00, &\qquad
00 &\xrightarrow{01} 01, &\qquad
00 &\xrightarrow{11} 11,\\
01 &\xrightarrow{10} 10,\\
10 &\xrightarrow{10} 00, &\qquad
10 &\xrightarrow{00} 01, &\qquad
10 &\xrightarrow{11} 01,\\
11 &\xrightarrow{00} 00.
\end{align*}
Thus every finite normalized pair word labels a path in this four-state graph.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\delta=x-y\) for a pair label \(xy\). Reading the digit of weight \(\Fib_k\) changes the imbalance from \(P\) to \(P+\delta\Fib_k\). At the next cut the current weight is \(\Fib_{k-1}\), and the recurrence \(\Fib_{k+1}=\Fib_k+\Fib_{k-1}\) gives the displayed state updates. For example,
\[
-\Fib_{k+1}+\Fib_k=-\Fib_{k-1},
\]
so \(01\xrightarrow{10}10\), while a zero-difference label in state \(10\) leaves \(P=-\Fib_k\), which is state \(01\) at the next cut.

It remains to exclude the other labels. After the current digit, the maximum raw value still available is
\[
\sum_{j=2}^{k-1}\Fib_j=\Fib_{k+1}-2,
\]
whereas the maximum value of a normalized tail is \(\Fib_k-1\). Hence a positive imbalance \(\Fib_k\), produced by label \(10\) in state \(00\) or \(11\), cannot be cancelled by the normalized tail. In state \(01\), every label except \(10\) leaves a negative imbalance of magnitude at least \(\Fib_{k+1}\), too large for the raw tail. In state \(10\), label \(01\) leaves imbalance \(-2\Fib_k\), and
\[
2\Fib_k>\Fib_{k+1}-2,
\]
so this also cannot be cancelled. Finally, adjacent normalized \(1\)s are forbidden; this leaves only \(11\xrightarrow{00}00\) from the fourth state. These exclusions and the recurrence give exactly the stated table.

For an unpadded finite fold, append two high-order raw zeros. By \Cref{lem:zero-padding}, the original pair word is unchanged on its visible coordinates, all normalized digits now lie in the enlarged window, and the enlarged stream starts in state \(00\). The preceding argument therefore applies to every factor in \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pair}}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Carry path realization]\label{prop:sync-language}
A finite pair word belongs to \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pair}}\) if and only if it labels a path in the graph of Proposition~\ref{prop:transitions}. Every such path label is realized at arbitrarily large distance from both ends of a finite reversed pair word.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One direction is Proposition~\ref{prop:transitions}. Conversely, the graph is strongly connected: state \(00\) reaches \(01,10,11\), and every state returns to \(00\). Surround the given path by paths from \(00\) to its initial state and from its terminal state back to \(00\). Add arbitrarily many \(00\)-labeled loops at state \(00\) on both sides.

Assign consecutive Fibonacci weights to the resulting path. The carry identities in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:transitions} show that the initial and terminal imbalance are both zero, so the two coordinates of the label word have equal Fibonacci value. The second coordinate has no adjacent \(1\)s, as is immediate from the transition table. It is therefore the Zeckendorf normalization of the first coordinate. This realizes the original path label, and the added loops place it arbitrarily far from both boundaries.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Adjacency matrix and bulk Parry measure}
In the state order \((00,01,10,11)\), the adjacency matrix of Proposition~\ref{prop:transitions} is
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&0&1\\
0&0&1&0\\
1&2&0&0\\
1&0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The graph is primitive: for instance, every state reaches \(00\), and from \(00\) one reaches every state in at most two further steps.

\begin{proposition}[Finite volume to bulk]\label{prop:bulk-limit}
Let \(\omega^{(M)}\) be uniform on \(\Om_M\), and let \(\mathcal{R}_M(\omega^{(M)})\) be the reversed pair word \eqref{eq:reversed-pair}. If \(i_M\to\infty\) and \(M-i_M\to\infty\), then every fixed-length factor centered at \(i_M\) converges in law to the Parry measure of the graph above.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Propositions~\ref{prop:transitions} and \ref{prop:sync-language}, the finite reversed pair words are paths in a primitive right-resolving graph subject only to their initial and terminal carry conditions. The first coordinate of the labels recovers \(\omega\), so the uniform measure on \(\Om_M\) gives equal weight to these boundary-conditioned paths. Perron--Frobenius asymptotics for a primitive graph show that fixed boundary conditions do not affect a factor whose distance from both ends tends to infinity. Such factors therefore converge to the Parry measure of the four-state presentation; see \cite[Section~8.4]{LindMarcus1995} and \cite[Chapter~2]{Kitchens1998}.
\end{proof}

The Perron eigenvalue of \(A\) is \(2\). A convenient right/left eigenvector pair is
\[
r=(2,1,2,1)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\ell=(2,2,1,1)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\ell^{\mathsf T}r=9.
\]
Hence the Parry transition matrix is
\[
P_{ij}=\frac{A_{ij}r_j}{2r_i}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac12&\frac14&0&\frac14\\
0&0&1&0\\
\frac12&\frac12&0&0\\
1&0&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and the stationary state distribution is
\[
\pi_i=\frac{\ell_ir_i}{\ell^{\mathsf T}r}
=
\frac19(4,2,2,1).
\]

\subsection{One-site law}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:joint}]
In the graph of Proposition~\ref{prop:transitions}, the edge labels are the pair symbols \((X_t,Y_t)\in\{0,1\}^2\). Explicitly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
00 &\xrightarrow{00} 00, &
00 &\xrightarrow{01} 01,\\
00 &\xrightarrow{11} 11, &
01 &\xrightarrow{10} 10,
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
10 &\xrightarrow{10} 00, &
10 &\xrightarrow{00} 01,\\
10 &\xrightarrow{11} 01, &
11 &\xrightarrow{00} 00.
\end{aligned}
\]
For an edge \(i\to j\), the Parry edge measure is
\[
\PP(i\to j)=\pi_i\frac{r_j}{2r_i}.
\]
The only multiple edge is \(10\to 01\), which carries the two labels \(00\) and \(11\), so each copy receives half of that mass. Using
\[
\pi=\frac19(4,2,2,1),
\qquad
r=(2,1,2,1)^{\mathsf T},
\]
one obtains
\[
\PP(01)=\pi_{00}\frac{r_{01}}{2r_{00}}=\frac49\cdot\frac14=\frac19,
\]
\[
\PP(10)=\pi_{01}\cdot 1+\pi_{10}\frac{r_{00}}{2r_{10}}
=\frac29+\frac19=\frac13,
\]
\[
\PP(11)=\pi_{00}\frac{r_{11}}{2r_{00}}+\frac12\pi_{10}\frac{r_{01}}{2r_{10}}
=\frac19+\frac1{18}=\frac16,
\]
and therefore
\[
\PP(00)=1-\PP(01)-\PP(10)-\PP(11)=\frac{7}{18}.
\]
This is exactly the table in Theorem~\ref{thm:joint}. The marginals
\[
\PP(X_0\neq Y_0)=\frac49,
\qquad
\PP(Y_0=1)=\frac{5}{18}
\]
follow by summation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Tilted matrices and cumulants}
For the discrepancy observable \(\Delta_t=X_t\oplus Y_t\), only the labels \(01\) and \(10\) contribute. The tilted adjacency matrix is therefore
\[
A_\theta=
\begin{pmatrix}
1& e^\theta &0&1\\
0&0& e^\theta &0\\
e^\theta &2&0&0\\
1&0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Write \(z=e^\theta\), and let \(\rho_\Delta(\theta)\) be the Perron root. Then
\[
F_\Delta(\lambda,z)
:=
\det(\lambda I-A_\theta)
=
\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2z+1)\lambda^2+(2z-z^3)\lambda+2z.
\]
Since \(F_\Delta(2,1)=0\), implicit differentiation of
\[
F_\Delta(\rho_\Delta(\theta),e^\theta)=0
\]
at \(\theta=0\) gives
\[
F_{\Delta,\lambda}(2,1)=9,\qquad
F_{\Delta,z}(2,1)=-8,
\]
so \(\rho_\Delta'(0)=8/9\). A second differentiation yields
\[
F_{\Delta,\lambda\lambda}(2,1)=30,\qquad
F_{\Delta,\lambda z}(2,1)=-9,\qquad
F_{\Delta,zz}(2,1)=-12,
\]
hence \(\rho_\Delta''(0)=332/243\). Therefore
\[
\Lambda_\Delta(\theta):=\log \rho_\Delta(\theta)-\log 2
\]
satisfies
\[
\Lambda_\Delta'(0)=\frac49,
\qquad
\Lambda_\Delta''(0)=\frac{118}{243}.
\]
These are the mean discrepancy density and asymptotic variance used in Corollary~\ref{thm:llnclt}.

For the output-weight observable \(Y_t\), the labels with \(Y_t=1\) are \(01\) and \(11\). Weighting each such edge by \(e^\theta\) gives
\[
M(e^\theta)=
\begin{pmatrix}
1& e^\theta &0& e^\theta\\
0&0&1&0\\
1&1+e^\theta&0&0\\
1&0&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
the weighted matrix used in Section~\ref{sec:partition}. Its characteristic polynomial is
\[
F_Y(\lambda,z)
:=
\det(\lambda I-M(z))
=
\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2z+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+z(z+1).
\]
Since \(F_Y(2,1)=0\), implicit differentiation of \(F_Y(\rho_Y(\theta),e^\theta)=0\) gives
\[
F_{Y,\lambda}(2,1)=9,\qquad
F_{Y,z}(2,1)=-5,
\]
so \(\rho_Y'(0)=5/9\). Differentiating again and using
\[
F_{Y,\lambda\lambda}(2,1)=30,\qquad
F_{Y,\lambda z}(2,1)=-8,\qquad
F_{Y,zz}(2,1)=2,
\]
gives \(\rho_Y''(0)=71/243\). Hence
\[
\Lambda_Y(\theta):=\log \rho_Y(\theta)-\log 2
\]
satisfies
\[
\Lambda_Y'(0)=\frac{5}{18},
\qquad
\Lambda_Y''(0)=\frac{67}{972}.
\]

\subsection{Autocovariances and power spectrum}
For the vertex chain with transition matrix \(P\), the discrepancy observable depends only on the current transition:
\[
g(i,j)=
\begin{cases}
1,& (i,j)\in\{(00,01),(01,10),(10,00)\},\\
0,& \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Define
\[
h_j:=\sum_k P_{jk}g(j,k)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac14\\[2mm]
1\\[2mm]
\frac12\\[2mm]
0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
q_k:=\sum_j \pi_jP_{jk}g(j,k)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac19 & \frac19 & \frac29 & 0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then
\[
\EE[\Delta_0]=\pi h=\frac49,
\qquad
\EE[\Delta_0\Delta_j]=qP^{j-1}h\qquad (j\ge1).
\]
Because \(P=\tfrac12 D^{-1}AD\) with \(D=\mathrm{diag}(2,1,2,1)\), \(P\) is similar to \(A/2\). Therefore its characteristic polynomial is
\[
(\lambda-1)\left(\lambda-\frac12\right)\left(\lambda+\frac12\right)^2.
\]
The stationary covariance sequence \(c_j=\Cov(\Delta_0,\Delta_j)\) therefore has the form
\[
c_j=2^{-j}\bigl(a+(-1)^j(b+cj)\bigr)\qquad (j\ge1).
\]
Direct evaluation of \(qP^{j-1}h-(4/9)^2\) at \(j=1,2,3\) gives
\[
c_1=\frac{17}{324},\qquad
c_2=\frac{25}{648},\qquad
c_3=\frac{7}{648},
\]
from which
\[
a=\frac18,\qquad
b=\frac{7}{648},\qquad
c=\frac{1}{108}.
\]
Hence
\[
c_j = 2^{-j}\!\left(\frac{1}{8}+(-1)^j\!\left(\frac{7}{648}+\frac{j}{108}\right)\right)\qquad (j\ge1),
\]
while
\[
c_0=\Var(\Delta_0)=\frac49\left(1-\frac49\right)=\frac{20}{81}.
\]
Substituting these values into
\[
S(\omega)=c_0+2\sum_{j\ge1}c_j\cos(j\omega)
\]
and summing the resulting geometric series yields
\[
S(\omega)
=
\frac{2\bigl(32\cos^3\omega+12\cos^2\omega-116\cos\omega-105\bigr)}
{9\,(4\cos\omega-5)\,(4\cos\omega+5)^2}.
\]
At \(\omega=0\) this gives \(S(0)=118/243\), in agreement with the CLT variance.
