\section{Compatible restriction across scales}
\label{sec:multiscale}

Reversal turns the most-significant-digit-first rule \(011\to100\) into \(110\to001\) in our low-to-high convention. Each rewrite preserves \(N\) and decreases the number of \(1\)s; Appendix~\ref{app:rewriting} proves termination and confluence.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:idempotent}
For every \(m\ge1\), \(\Fold_m:\Om_m\to X_m\) is an idempotent surjection.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The fold is the unique Zeckendorf normal form, truncated to the window, and it fixes every \(x\in X_m\).
\end{proof}

For \(m_2\ge m_1\), let \(\proj_{m_2\to m_1}:X_{m_2}\to X_{m_1}\) retain the first \(m_1\) digits. These maps form an inverse system whose limit is the one-sided golden-mean shift \cite[Chapter~1]{LindMarcus1995}. On raw words let
\[
\naive_{m+1\to m}(\omega_1\cdots\omega_{m+1})=\omega_1\cdots\omega_m.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Sharp commutation set]\label{prop:sharp-commutation}
For \(m\ge1\) and \(\omega\in\Om_{m+1}\),
\[
\proj_{m+1\to m}\bigl(\Fold_{m+1}(\omega)\bigr)
=\Fold_m\bigl(\naive_{m+1\to m}(\omega)\bigr)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N(\omega)<\Fib_{m+3}.
\]
Exactly
\[
\left\lceil\frac{2^{m+2}+1}{3}\right\rceil
\]
words commute. Thus their density tends to \(2/3\), and failure has limiting density \(1/3\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(A=N(\naive_{m+1\to m}(\omega))\), \(b=\omega_{m+1}\), and \(T=\Fib_{m+2}\). Then \(N(\omega)=A+bT\), while \(A\le\Fib_{m+3}-2=T+\Fib_{m+1}-2<2T\). Hence the value represented by \(\Fold_m(\naive_{m+1\to m}(\omega))\) is
\[
R(A)=A-T\mathbf 1_{\{A\ge T\}}.
\]
If \(N(\omega)<\Fib_{m+3}\), the projected large fold represents \(N(\omega)-T\mathbf 1_{\{N(\omega)\ge T\}}=R(A)\), since
\[
\mathbf 1_{\{N(\omega)\ge T\}}=b+\mathbf 1_{\{A\ge T\}}.
\]
If \(N(\omega)\ge\Fib_{m+3}\), then \(N(\omega)\le\Fib_{m+4}-2\), so it instead represents \(N(\omega)-\Fib_{m+3}\). Its difference from \(R(A)\) is
\[
\bigl(b-1+\mathbf 1_{\{A\ge T\}}\bigr)T-\Fib_{m+1}\ne0,
\]
because the coefficient of \(T\) is \(-1\), \(0\), or \(1\), and \(T-\Fib_{m+1}=\Fib_m\). Zeckendorf uniqueness proves the equivalence.

For the count, \(b=0\) contributes all \(2^m\) words. For \(b=1\), one needs \(A<\Fib_{m+1}\). If \(B_m\) counts such length-\(m\) words, the highest digit must vanish; splitting on the next digit gives
\[
B_1=1,\qquad B_2=2,\qquad B_m=2^{m-2}+B_{m-2}.
\]
Thus \(B_m=\lceil(2^m+1)/3\rceil\), and \(2^m+B_m\) is the displayed count.
\end{proof}

Define the compatible restriction by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:re-def}
\aware_{m+1\to m}(\omega):=\proj_{m+1\to m}\bigl(\Fold_{m+1}(\omega)\bigr)\in X_m.
\end{equation}
Its values lie in \(X_m\) and \(\Fold_m\) fixes \(X_m\) pointwise, so it satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:commuting}
\Fold_m\circ\aware_{m+1\to m}=\proj_{m+1\to m}\circ\Fold_{m+1}.
\end{equation}
and, by the same token, it is the unique \(X_m\)-valued map with this property.

\begin{corollary}[Commutation at all depths]\label{cor:all-depth-commutation}
For \(\omega\in\Om_L\), every adjacent diagram commutes on every prefix if and only if \(\omega\in X_L\), in which case \(\Fold_L(\omega)=\omega\). Thus the only trajectories admitting a fully projective tower of folded prefixes are those that require no folding at all.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp-commutation}, all diagrams commute exactly when every prefix of length \(m+1\) has value below \(\Fib_{m+3}\). Every golden-mean prefix is already a Zeckendorf expansion and satisfies this bound. Conversely, if \(\omega_j=\omega_{j+1}=1\), its prefix through \(j+1\) has value at least \(\Fib_{j+1}+\Fib_{j+2}=\Fib_{j+3}\).
\end{proof}
