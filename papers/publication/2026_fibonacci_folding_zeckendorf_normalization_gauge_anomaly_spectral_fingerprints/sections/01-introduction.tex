\section{Introduction}

Zeckendorf's theorem furnishes a canonical representative among Fibonacci expansions: every nonnegative integer admits a unique expression
\[
\sum_{k\ge 1} z_k\Fib_{k+1},
\qquad z_k\in\{0,1\},\qquad z_kz_{k+1}=0,
\]
with no adjacent \(1\)s. Classical work concerns either the normalized expansion of a single integer or normalization on one-sided or two-sided digit sequences. In this paper we isolate a finite-window version of the problem. Given a binary word \(\omega\in\Om_m\), we evaluate its Fibonacci value, replace it by its Zeckendorf expansion, and retain only the original \(m\) low-weight coordinates. This defines a map
\[
\Fold_m:\Om_m\to X_m,
\]
where \(X_m\) is the length-\(m\) language of the golden-mean shift.

The point of the finite-window problem is that normalization does not commute with the obvious restriction when the window size changes. If one first truncates a word of length \(m+1\) and then folds, the result need not agree with the truncation of its \((m+1)\)-window fold. We show that there is nevertheless a canonical restriction on microstates, namely the unique map \(\aware_{m+1\to m}\) for which folding and restriction commute. The discrepancy between \(\aware_{m+1\to m}\) and naive truncation is the basic observable studied here.

At a structural level, the paper shows that this discrepancy is governed by a small sofic system. When raw and normalized digits are observed simultaneously in the bulk, one obtains a stationary pair process \((X_t,Y_t)\). A general structural theorem gives synchronized pair shifts for zero-synchronized span-\(r\) normalizers under an explicit bounded-cascade hypothesis. That hypothesis is stronger than a bound on the support of one rewrite and does not apply to the cascading Fibonacci scan. Instead, the Fibonacci pair shift is established directly by a four-state arithmetic carry transducer. A separate boundary-cancellation argument proves local invertibility of the Fibonacci window code. The probabilistic, symbolic, and thermodynamic aspects of the finite-window model remain consequences of the pair shift alone.

\subsection{Main results}

The basic finite-window properties are established in Sections~\ref{sec:preliminaries} and \ref{sec:multiscale}. In particular, \(\Fold_m\) is an idempotent projection onto \(X_m\), and the compatible restriction \(\aware_{m+1\to m}\) is characterized by the commutative relation
\[
\Fold_m\circ \aware_{m+1\to m}
\;=\;
\proj_{m+1\to m}\circ \Fold_{m+1}.
\]
The first substantive point is that the resulting discrepancy is genuinely multiscale. Proposition~\ref{prop:worst} constructs, for every \(m\), a word \(\omega^\star\in\Om_{m+1}\) for which naive truncation and compatible restriction disagree at every visible coordinate. Proposition~\ref{prop:tail-patch} shows that no rule depending only on a uniformly bounded tail can repair this failure of commutation.

The structural input begins with Theorem~\ref{thm:span-r-pair}, which shows that every zero-synchronized span-\(r\) local normalizer satisfying the bounded-cascade condition has a synchronized pair presentation whose follower memory is bounded by \(r-1\). Corollary~\ref{cor:fib-pair-criterion} uses a different argument in the Fibonacci case: an explicit carry invariant presents the interior pair language of \((X_t,Y_t)\) by a four-state synchronized right-resolving graph. Theorem~\ref{thm:discrepancy-fischer} classifies the symbolic support of the discrepancy factor: it is a strictly sofic shift with a three-state Fischer cover and topological entropy \(\log\varphi\), strictly below the pair entropy \(\log2\). Once the pair graph has been identified, the discrepancy laws are obtained from standard finite-state calculations. Theorem~\ref{thm:joint}, Corollary~\ref{thm:llnclt}, and Theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum} give the exact one-site law and the complete two-point statistics of the discrepancy process, including density \(4/9\), asymptotic variance \(118/243\), closed autocovariance formulas, and a rational power spectrum. Theorem~\ref{thm:joint-rotation} then gives a sharp deterministic relation between output density and discrepancy density: their joint rotation set is a pentagon, its five vertices have unique periodic realizations, and each of its two upper faces is carried by a mixing frontier subshift of entropy \(\log\rho_{\mathrm{pl}}\), where \(\rho_{\mathrm{pl}}\) is the plastic constant.

A second part of the paper concerns the sequence-level map induced by the window fold. For each \(m\), the map \(\Fold_m\) defines a sliding block code \(\Phi_m\) on the full two-sided shift, and Proposition~\ref{prop:sofic} identifies its image as a sofic shift. Theorem~\ref{thm:block-separation} proves a sharp whole-block statement: for \(m\ge3\), distinct binary blocks of length at least \(2m-1\) have different sequences of folded length-\(m\) windows, whereas an explicit Fibonacci-recurrence family collides at length \(2m-2\). Local recovery is shorter. Theorem~\ref{thm:window-decoder} shows that three consecutive labels determine the current microstate digit and that two do not, so the exact zero-anticipation inverse memory is \(2\). Theorem~\ref{thm:conjugacy} gives the conjugacy threshold \(m\ge3\), and Proposition~\ref{thm:m2} describes the unique exceptional fiber at \(m=2\).

The final part of the paper concerns weighted fibers. The same four-state presentation yields a transfer-matrix description of
\[
Z_m(y)=\sum_{\omega\in\Om_m} y^{|\Fold_m(\omega)|_1}.
\]
The recurrence and generating-function formulas are convenient consequences of the transfer matrix, but the conceptual statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:microcanonical-output}, which identifies the free energy with the Legendre transform of a strictly concave entropy profile on the pair shift. Corollary~\ref{thm:weight-stats} then gives the corresponding limit laws for the output Hamming weight.

\subsection{Relation to earlier work}

The numeration-theoretic background goes back to the classical work of Lekkerkerker and Zeckendorf \cite{Lekkerkerker1952,Zeckendorf1972}. For Fibonacci numeration, normalization by automata, and related weighted numeration systems we use \cite{Brown1964,Fraenkel1985,Frougny1988,Frougny1992,FrougnyConfluent1992,FrougnySolomyak1996,FrougnySakarovitch1998,AlloucheShallit2003,GrabnerKirschenhoferTichy2002,DumontSidorovThomas1999,ShallitShan2023}. On the symbolic side we appeal only to standard material on sofic shifts, sliding block codes, transfer matrices, and thermodynamic formalism \cite{Weiss1973,LindMarcus1995,Kitchens1998,Parry1964,ParryPollicott1990,Walters1982,Ruelle2004,Bowen2008}.

Closest in spirit are works on the normalized Fibonacci digit process, or equivalently on the golden-ratio Erd\H{o}s measure. Alexander and Zagier \cite{AlexanderZagier1991}, Sidorov and Vershik \cite{SidorovVershik1998}, Bezhaeva and Oseledets \cite{BezhaevaOseledets2007,BezhaevaOseledets2010,BezhaevaOseledets2Step}, and Kempton \cite{KemptonDigits2014} study the normalized digits themselves and recover, in particular, the classical density \(5/18\) for a normalized digit to equal \(1\). Our viewpoint is different. We keep raw and normalized digits in the same finite window, and the central question is how normalization interacts with restriction across scales. To the best of our knowledge, the pair observable \((X_t,Y_t)\) and the corresponding compatible-restriction problem have not been isolated in this form in the Zeckendorf literature.

The existence of finite automata for normalization in Fibonacci or Pisot numeration is classical \cite{Frougny1988,Frougny1992,FrougnyConfluent1992,FrougnySolomyak1996,FrougnySakarovitch1998}. What is specific here is not the appearance of a finite automaton as such, but the finite-window compatibility problem: raw and normalized digits are observed on the same window, and the obstruction to changing the window size is encoded by a pair shift rather than by the normalized digits alone. The four-state pair graph is derived from an explicit Fibonacci carry invariant. The conjugacy threshold uses a related arithmetic mechanism: the Fibonacci recurrence cancels the interior of three consecutive window sums and leaves boundary coefficients whose size forces local agreement. For the joint-density theorem we use the standard cycle description of rotation sets for locally constant observables on shifts of finite type \cite{Ziemian1995}; the content specific to the present model is the exact rotation polygon and the rigidity and entropy of its extremal faces.

We do not attempt to formulate a general theory of numeration automata or sofic models. The point is rather that, in this finite-window setting, three questions that at first sight are unrelated are governed by the same explicit pair shift: the bulk statistics of the restriction discrepancy, the invertibility threshold for the induced sliding block code, and the thermodynamics of weighted fibers.

\subsection{Organization}

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} introduces the finite-window fold, and Section~\ref{sec:multiscale} identifies the compatible restriction across scales. Section~\ref{sec:gauge} proves the structural pair theorem, derives the four-state Fibonacci carry presentation, classifies the discrepancy factor, computes its statistics, and determines the joint output--discrepancy rotation set. Section~\ref{sec:sequence} proves the sharp block-separation bound, the exact causal decoder radius, and the conjugacy threshold. Section~\ref{sec:partition} treats weighted fibers and the associated variational formalism. The appendices contain the rewriting argument, the carry-state proof of the four-state presentation, the graph interpretation of block separation, and the linear-algebraic calculations behind the recurrence formulas.
