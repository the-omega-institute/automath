\section{Introduction}

For a fixed window length \(m\), apply Zeckendorf normalization to each consecutive length-\(m\) block of a bi-infinite binary sequence and retain the normalized digits visible in that window. The central result of this paper is that, for every \(m\ge3\), the resulting sliding code is a topological conjugacy onto its image and has a causal inverse of exact memory \(2\). Thus three consecutive fold labels recover the current raw digit, while two do not, independently of the window length. This is not a bounded-propagation phenomenon: Fibonacci carries can cascade across arbitrarily many coordinates.

The uniform three-label decoder contrasts with a sharp window-scale threshold for a different recovery problem. To reconstruct a whole raw block from its sequence of length-\(m\) folds, blocks of length \(2m-1\) are uniquely determined, while collisions persist at length \(2m-2\). The distinction between whole-block reconstruction and one-digit causal recovery is essential: the former grows with \(m\), whereas the latter remains fixed.

The finite-window fold comes from Zeckendorf's theorem, which furnishes a canonical representative among Fibonacci expansions: every nonnegative integer admits a unique expression
\[
\sum_{k\ge 1} z_k\Fib_{k+1},
\qquad z_k\in\{0,1\},\qquad z_kz_{k+1}=0,
\]
with no adjacent \(1\)s. Classical work concerns either the normalized expansion of a single integer or normalization on one-sided or two-sided digit sequences. In this paper we isolate a finite-window version of the problem. Given a binary word \(\omega\in\Om_m\), we evaluate its Fibonacci value, replace it by its Zeckendorf expansion, and retain only the original \(m\) low-weight coordinates. This defines a map
\[
\Fold_m:\Om_m\to X_m,
\]
where \(X_m\) is the length-\(m\) language of the golden-mean shift.

Normalization does not commute with the obvious restriction when the window size changes. If one first truncates a word of length \(m+1\) and then folds, the result need not agree with the truncation of its \((m+1)\)-window fold. There is nevertheless a canonical restriction on microstates, namely the unique map \(\aware_{m+1\to m}\) for which folding and restriction commute. The discrepancy between \(\aware_{m+1\to m}\) and naive truncation provides a useful structural observable.

When raw and normalized digits are observed simultaneously in the bulk, the resulting pair language has a four-state synchronized presentation obtained from an explicit Fibonacci carry invariant. Its discrepancy factor is strictly sofic. The carry graph and the decoder arise from related Fibonacci arithmetic but from distinct arguments: the graph records a relative carry imbalance, whereas a boundary-cancellation identity proves both sharp block separation and local recovery. Once the graph is identified, the probabilistic, rotation-set, and weighted-fiber formulas are standard finite-state consequences.

\subsection{Main results}

The arithmetic backbone is Theorem~\ref{thm:block-separation}. For \(m\ge3\), distinct binary blocks of length at least \(2m-1\) have different sequences of folded length-\(m\) windows, and an explicit Fibonacci-recurrence family collides at length \(2m-2\). The same boundary identity has a stronger local consequence. Theorem~\ref{thm:window-decoder} proves that three consecutive labels determine the current microstate digit and that two do not, so the exact zero-anticipation inverse memory is \(2\), independently of \(m\). Theorem~\ref{thm:conjugacy} turns this decoder into a topological conjugacy for every \(m\ge3\); Proposition~\ref{thm:m2} describes the exceptional fiber at \(m=2\).

The structural support for this arithmetic result is the explicit pair model. Corollary~\ref{cor:fib-pair-criterion} and Theorem~\ref{thm:pair-language} present the interior raw/normalized language by a four-state synchronized right-resolving graph. Theorem~\ref{thm:discrepancy-fischer} identifies its discrepancy factor as a strictly sofic shift with a three-state Fischer cover and topological entropy \(\log\varphi\), strictly below the pair entropy \(\log2\). A brief bounded-delay criterion explains why finite pair memory is automatic for genuinely finite-delay projections; Fibonacci folding lies outside that setting and requires the separate carry invariant.

The finite-window setup also yields a compatible restriction across scales. The map \(\Fold_m\) is an idempotent projection onto \(X_m\), and \(\aware_{m+1\to m}\) is the unique stable-valued map satisfying
\[
\Fold_m\circ \aware_{m+1\to m}
\;=\;
\proj_{m+1\to m}\circ \Fold_{m+1}.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:worst} shows that naive and compatible restriction can disagree at every visible coordinate, while Proposition~\ref{prop:tail-patch} rules out repair by a uniformly bounded tail rule. These facts motivate the pair observable but are not used to prove the decoder.

The remaining exact formulas are consequences of the four-state graph. Theorem~\ref{thm:joint}, Corollary~\ref{thm:llnclt}, and Theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum} record the one-site law, density \(4/9\), asymptotic variance \(118/243\), autocovariances, and rational power spectrum. Standard cycle enumeration gives the output--discrepancy rotation pentagon in Theorem~\ref{thm:joint-rotation}. Weighting the same graph gives the partition function
\[
Z_m(y)=\sum_{\omega\in\Om_m} y^{|\Fold_m(\omega)|_1}.
\]
The recurrence, limit laws, and entropy duality in Section~\ref{sec:partition} are transfer-matrix consequences of that presentation rather than independent inputs to the decoding theorem.

\subsection{Relation to earlier work}

The numeration-theoretic background goes back to the classical work of Lekkerkerker and Zeckendorf \cite{Lekkerkerker1952,Zeckendorf1972}. For Fibonacci numeration, normalization by automata, and related weighted numeration systems we use \cite{Brown1964,Fraenkel1985,Frougny1988,Frougny1992,FrougnyConfluent1992,FrougnySolomyak1996,FrougnySakarovitch1998,AlloucheShallit2003,GrabnerKirschenhoferTichy2002,DumontSidorovThomas1999,ShallitShan2023}. On the symbolic side we appeal only to standard material on sofic shifts, sliding block codes, transfer matrices, and thermodynamic formalism \cite{Weiss1973,LindMarcus1995,Kitchens1998,Parry1964,ParryPollicott1990,Walters1982,Ruelle2004,Bowen2008}.

Closest in spirit are works on the normalized Fibonacci digit process, or equivalently on the golden-ratio Erd\H{o}s measure. Alexander and Zagier \cite{AlexanderZagier1991}, Sidorov and Vershik \cite{SidorovVershik1998}, Bezhaeva and Oseledets \cite{BezhaevaOseledets2007,BezhaevaOseledets2010,BezhaevaOseledets2Step}, and Kempton \cite{KemptonDigits2014} study the normalized digits themselves and recover, in particular, the classical density \(5/18\) for a normalized digit to equal \(1\). Our viewpoint is different. We apply the same finite-window fold along a bi-infinite raw sequence and ask for exact local recovery from the resulting label sequence. The raw/normalized pair observable then records the carry structure behind this code.

The existence of finite automata for normalization in Fibonacci or Pisot numeration is classical \cite{Frougny1988,Frougny1992,FrougnyConfluent1992,FrougnySolomyak1996,FrougnySakarovitch1998}. What is specific here is the sharp inverse theorem for the sliding code formed by overlapping finite folds. The Fibonacci recurrence cancels the interior of three consecutive window sums and leaves boundary coefficients whose size forces local agreement. This produces an inverse radius independent of \(m\), despite unbounded normalization cascades. The four-state pair graph is derived separately from an explicit carry invariant. For the rotation polygon we use the standard cycle description for locally constant observables on shifts of finite type \cite{Ziemian1995}; the pressure and limit laws likewise use standard finite-state machinery.

We do not claim a general method for numeration automata or a class-level theorem containing the Fibonacci example. The bounded-delay criterion below is an expository contrast, not a generalization of Fibonacci folding. The contribution is the sharp and complete decoding result for this explicit arithmetic code, supported by its finite carry presentation.
