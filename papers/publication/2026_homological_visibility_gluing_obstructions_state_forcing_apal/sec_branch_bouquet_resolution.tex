The preceding corollary classifies the branchwise hidden subgroups that can occur on a fixed finite nerve with $H_2\cong \mathbb Z^\beta$. The next theorem replaces that abstract nerve by an explicitly chosen bouquet good-cover family and classifies, inside that family and with the stated realization data, the finite abelian groups that occur as pure two-branch resolution kernels.

All realization data in this subsection are chosen data: a finite simplicial
model $K_\beta$, the open-star good-cover system, a trivialization of the
constant finite abelian band, and the branch cocycles or homomorphisms used in
\Cref{thm:mixed-branch-realization}. The theorem below is not a canonical
classification over all presentations of the bouquet.

\begin{lemma}[Bouquet good-cover and finite-abelian interfaces]\label{lem:bouquet-cover-classical-interface}
Let $K$ be a finite simplicial complex and let $\mathcal U^{(m)}$ be the open-star cover of $\operatorname{Sd}^m K$ on $|K|$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item every nonempty finite intersection of members of $\mathcal U^{(m)}$ is contractible, and $N(\mathcal U^{(m)})$ is canonically isomorphic to $\operatorname{Sd}^mK$;
\item the covers $\mathcal U^{(m+1)}$ refine $\mathcal U^{(m)}$, and in the pullback site generated by all such open stars, their nonempty intersections, and a strict initial object for empty intersections, the family $(\mathcal U^{(m)})_{m\ge0}$ is filtered and cofinal;
\item for every constant finite abelian group $G$, the Leray--\v{C}ech comparison identifies
\[
\varinjlim_m\check H^2(\mathcal U^{(m)},G)
\cong
H^2(|K|,G),
\]
and the comparison is compatible with the simplicial cohomology of the nerves;
\item for every integer $\beta\ge 1$ and every nonzero finite abelian group $G$, the
group $G$ admits a decomposition
\[
G=G_1\oplus G_2
\]
with $G_1,G_2$ nonzero and $d(G_i)\le\beta$ for $i=1,2$ if and only if $d(G)\le 2\beta$ and $G$ is not a cyclic $p$-group.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \textup{(i)}, the open star of a vertex of a barycentric subdivision is contractible, and a nonempty finite intersection of such open stars is the open star of the simplex spanned by the corresponding vertices. This is contractible. The nerve of the open-star cover of a barycentric subdivision is the subdivision itself; this is the standard nerve computation for open stars in a simplicial complex \cite[Secs.~2.1, 4G]{Hatcher2002}.

For \textup{(ii)}, barycentric subdivision gives a simplicial refinement of the previous subdivision, and each open star in $\operatorname{Sd}^{m+1}K$ lies in an open star of $\operatorname{Sd}^{m}K$. The generated site used here includes a strict initial object $\emptyset$ and declares the pullback of two disjoint members to be $\emptyset$; nonempty pullbacks are the corresponding finite intersections. Thus every pullback demanded by the local-reference-site axioms is represented in the site, while the \v{C}ech cochains still ignore empty intersections because the constant sheaf has only the zero section on $\emptyset$. The pretopology is generated by these covers, their pullbacks, identity covers, and the empty cover of $\emptyset$, so any covering family in the generated site is refined by a common later barycentric open-star cover. This proves filteredness and cofinality.

For \textup{(iii)}, the contractibility in \textup{(i)} makes each $\mathcal U^{(m)}$ a Leray cover for the genuine constant sheaf $G$ on the associated formal \v{C}ech site. Empty pullback objects contribute no cochains. The Leray theorem for \v{C}ech cohomology therefore identifies the \v{C}ech cohomology of these covers with the sheaf cohomology of $|K|$, and the nerve identification in \textup{(i)} identifies the same groups with simplicial cohomology of $\operatorname{Sd}^mK$ \cite[Chap.~III, Sec.~4]{Bredon1997}, \cite[Sec.~4G]{Hatcher2002}. The refinement maps are the usual subdivision maps, hence induce cohomology isomorphisms.

For \textup{(iv)}, fix an integer $\beta\ge1$. The standing nonzero hypothesis removes the trivial group from the classification; here a cyclic $p$-group means a cyclic group of order $p^n$ with $n\ge1$. First suppose such a decomposition exists. Then $G$ is a direct sum of two nonzero groups, so it is decomposable. The finite abelian indecomposable groups are exactly the cyclic groups of prime-power order \cite[Chap.~12]{DummitFoote2004}. Also
\[
d(G)\le d(G_1)+d(G_2)\le 2\beta.
\]
Conversely, assume $d(G)\le2\beta$ and $G$ is not a cyclic $p$-group. There are two cases: either the minimal number of generators is $m\ge2$, or $m=1$ and the cyclic group has non-prime-power order. If $d(G)=m\ge2$, write an invariant-factor decomposition
\[
G\cong C_{n_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{n_m},
\qquad
n_1\mid n_2\mid\cdots\mid n_m.
\]
With $t=\min\{\beta,m-1\}$, the two sums
\[
G_1=C_{n_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{n_t},
\qquad
G_2=C_{n_{t+1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{n_m}
\]
are nonzero and satisfy $d(G_1)\le\beta$ and $d(G_2)=m-t\le\beta$. If $d(G)=1$, then $G$ is cyclic. Since it is not a cyclic $p$-group, its order has at least two coprime factors $a,b>1$, and the Chinese remainder theorem gives
\[
G\cong \mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/b\mathbb Z,
\]
with both summands nonzero and singly generated. This proves the equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bouquet mixed-realization transfer]\label{lem:bouquet-mixed-realization-transfer}
Fix $\beta\ge 1$ and the bouquet site $\mathcal C_a$ built from
$K_\beta$ and the open-star covers $\mathcal U_\beta^{(m)}$ as in
\Cref{thm:bouquet-two-branch-resolution}. Let $G$ be a finite abelian
group, and let $G_1,G_2\le G$ be nonzero subgroups satisfying
\[
d(G_i)\le \beta\qquad(i=1,2).
\]
Then there exist a state $p$, an admitted reference $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$
inside a model $M$ with $a=[r]_p$, a local object functor on
$\mathcal C_a$, and a realization prestack with constant band $G$ such
that
\[
\Vis_{p,s}(r)=\{v_1,v_2\},
\qquad
K_{v_i}=G_i\quad(i=1,2).
\]
Both component gerbes are non-neutral. If, moreover,
\[
G_1\cap G_2=0,\qquad G_1+G_2=G,
\]
then
\[
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{\mathrm{br}}(r)\cong B_{\mathrm{res}}(r)\cong G,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{\mathrm{unif}}(r)=0,
\qquad
M,p\Vdash \Null_s^{\mathrm{glue}}(r).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{lem:bouquet-cover-classical-interface}(i)--(iii), the cover
$\mathcal U_\beta^{(0)}$ is a finite good cover whose nerve is
$K_\beta$, all finite intersections are contractible, the constant band
$G$ is trivial on the cover, and the generated filtered family satisfies
\textup{(H4)}. Since $K_\beta\simeq\bigvee_{j=1}^{\beta}S^2$, we have
\[
H_1\bigl(N(\mathcal U_\beta^{(0)}),\mathbb Z\bigr)=0,
\qquad
H_2\bigl(N(\mathcal U_\beta^{(0)}),\mathbb Z\bigr)\cong \mathbb Z^\beta.
\]
The inequalities $d(G_i)\le\beta$ therefore allow homomorphisms
\[
\phi_i:H_2\bigl(N(\mathcal U_\beta^{(0)}),\mathbb Z\bigr)\to G
\]
with $\operatorname{Im}(\phi_i)=G_i$.

Apply \Cref{thm:mixed-branch-realization} to the fixed finite nerve,
constant band $A:=G$, branch index set $\{1,2\}$, and homomorphisms
$\phi_1,\phi_2$. It constructs the asserted one-state realization data
and gives
\[
K_{v_i}=\operatorname{Im}(\phi_i)=G_i.
\]
The model constructed here is therefore admissible for the same reason as in
\Cref{thm:mixed-branch-realization}: it is obtained by the layer-expansion
interface of \Cref{prop:admissible-layer-expansion-interface}.
Since $G_i\ne0$, the same theorem makes both component gerbes
non-neutral.

Under the additional direct-sum hypotheses, the branch-sensitive hidden
subgroup intersection is $0$ and the branch-uniform hidden subgroup sum
is all of $G$. The quotient formulas in
\Cref{thm:branch-sensitive-visible-quotient,thm:branch-uniform-visible-quotient,thm:branch-comparison-exact-sequence}
therefore give
\[
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{\mathrm{br}}(r)\cong B_{\mathrm{res}}(r)\cong G,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{\mathrm{unif}}(r)=0.
\]
Finally, because both branches are non-neutral,
\Cref{thm:mixed-branch-realization}(iv) gives
$M,p\Vdash \Null_s^{\mathrm{glue}}(r)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-branch resolution on chosen bouquet good covers]\label{thm:bouquet-two-branch-resolution}
Fix an integer $\beta\ge 1$, let $K_\beta$ be a finite connected simplicial complex whose geometric realization is homotopy equivalent to
\[
\bigvee_{j=1}^{\beta} S^2,
\]
and write
\[
X_\beta:=|K_\beta|.
\]
For each $m\ge 0$, let
\[
\mathcal U_\beta^{(m)}
\]
be the open-star cover of the barycentric subdivision $\operatorname{Sd}^m K_\beta$, viewed as a cover of $X_\beta$. Let $\mathcal C_a$ be the pullback site whose objects are $X_\beta$, a strict initial object $\emptyset$, and all nonempty finite intersections of members of the covers $\mathcal U_\beta^{(m)}$; the pullback of two objects over a common target is their nonempty intersection when it is nonempty and is $\emptyset$ otherwise. Covering families are generated by the families $\mathcal U_\beta^{(m)}$, their pullbacks, identity covers, and the empty cover of $\emptyset$.
All model-existence assertions in this theorem are understood relative to the
well-typed admissible-expansion convention of
\Cref{prop:admissible-layer-expansion-interface}.

Then the family
\[
\bigl(\mathcal U_\beta^{(m)}\bigr)_{m\ge 0}
\]
is a filtered cofinal family satisfying \textup{(H4)} for every constant finite abelian band.

Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, and write $d(G)$ for the minimal number of generators of $G$. Then the following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item $G\ne0$, $d(G)\le 2\beta$, and $G$ is not a cyclic $p$-group, where
cyclic $p$-group means cyclic of order $p^n$ with $n\ge1$.
\item There exist a semantic model $M$, a state $p$, an admitted reference $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ with $a=[r]_p$, a local object functor on $\mathcal C_a$, and a realization prestack
\[
\mathfrak P_r\to \mathcal C_a
\]
with trivialized constant band $G$, using the finite-nerve Leray comparison
supplied by the chosen bouquet good covers, such that
\[
\Vis_{p,s}(r)=\{v_1,v_2\},
\]
both component gerbes $\mathfrak G_{v_1}$ and $\mathfrak G_{v_2}$ are non-neutral, and
\[
K_{v_1}\cap K_{v_2}=0,
\qquad
K_{v_1}+K_{v_2}=G.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Whenever these conditions hold,
\[
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{\mathrm{br}}(r)\cong B_{\mathrm{res}}(r)\cong G,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{\mathrm{unif}}(r)=0,
\qquad
M,p\Vdash \Null_s^{\mathrm{glue}}(r).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We use four explicit interfaces: the good-cover comparison, the
finite-abelian decomposition, the mixed-realization transfer, and the
branch-exactness consequence.

The good-cover interface is supplied by
\Cref{lem:bouquet-cover-classical-interface}. By clause \textup{(i)},
each $\mathcal U_\beta^{(m)}$ is a finite good cover of $X_\beta$, its
nonempty finite intersections are contractible, and
\[
N(\mathcal U_\beta^{(m)})\cong \operatorname{Sd}^{m}K_\beta.
\]
Thus the constant band $G$ is trivial on every such cover, and
\[
H^1(U_{i_0\cdots i_n},G)=0
\]
for every nonempty intersection appearing in the \v{C}ech descent
argument. Clause \textup{(ii)} makes
$\bigl(\mathcal U_\beta^{(m)}\bigr)_{m\ge 0}$ filtered and cofinal in the
generated pretopology of $\mathcal C_a$, and clause \textup{(iii)}
identifies the filtered \v{C}ech system with derived cohomology. Hence
the family satisfies \textup{(H4)} for every constant finite abelian band.

Also, every subdivision of $K_\beta$ is homotopy equivalent to $K_\beta$, hence
\[
H_1\bigl(N(\mathcal U_\beta^{(m)}),\mathbb Z\bigr)=0,
\qquad
H_2\bigl(N(\mathcal U_\beta^{(m)}),\mathbb Z\bigr)\cong \mathbb Z^\beta
\]
for every $m$ \cite[Secs.~2.1, 2.2]{Hatcher2002}. This records exactly
the finite-nerve hypotheses needed by the realization theorems.

Now assume \textup{(i)}. The finite-abelian interface is
\Cref{lem:bouquet-cover-classical-interface}(iv): it gives a direct-sum
decomposition
\[
G=G_1\oplus G_2
\]
with $G_1,G_2$ nonzero and
\[
d(G_1)\le\beta,
\qquad
d(G_2)\le\beta.
\]
The mixed-realization transfer
\Cref{lem:bouquet-mixed-realization-transfer} applied to these two
summands produces a realization with two visible branches, non-neutral
component gerbes, and $K_{v_i}=G_i$. Because the summands form an
internal direct sum, $G_1\cap G_2=0$ and $G_1+G_2=G$, so condition
\textup{(ii)} follows.

Conversely, assume \textup{(ii)} and put
\[
K_i:=K_{v_i}
\qquad
(i=1,2).
\]
For each $i$, choose a finite nerve presentation of the branch class $\omega_{v_i}$ on one of the covers $\mathcal U_\beta^{(m_i)}$. Since the good-cover interface gives
\[
H_2\bigl(N(\mathcal U_\beta^{(m_i)}),\mathbb Z\bigr)\cong \mathbb Z^\beta,
\]
the hidden subgroup
\[
K_i=\operatorname{Im}(\mathrm{ev}_{\omega_{v_i}})
\]
is generated by at most $\beta$ elements. The equalities
\[
K_1\cap K_2=0,
\qquad
K_1+K_2=G
\]
give an internal direct-sum decomposition
\[
G\cong K_1\oplus K_2
\]
with both summands nonzero. Hence $G$ is decomposable, and the finite abelian indecomposable groups are exactly the cyclic $p$-groups \cite[Chap.~12]{DummitFoote2004}. Moreover
\[
d(G)\le d(K_1)+d(K_2)\le 2\beta.
\]
Thus \textup{(i)} holds.

Finally, the exact-sequence consequences are separate from the realization step. The identities
\[
\bigcap_{i=1}^2 K_{v_i}=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^2 K_{v_i}=G
\]
give
\[
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{\mathrm{br}}(r)\cong B_{\mathrm{res}}(r)\cong G,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{\mathrm{unif}}(r)=0
\]
by \Cref{thm:branch-sensitive-visible-quotient,thm:branch-uniform-visible-quotient,thm:branch-comparison-exact-sequence}. Since both branches are non-neutral, \Cref{thm:mixed-branch-realization}(iv) gives
\[
M,p\Vdash \Null_s^{\mathrm{glue}}(r).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A two-branch sphere example separating the aggregate invariants]\label{ex:two-branch-sphere-separation}
This is the case $\beta=1$ and $G=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2$ of \Cref{thm:bouquet-two-branch-resolution}.

Let $\mathcal U$ be a finite good cover of $S^2$ with nerve
\[
N:=N(\mathcal U),
\]
and take
\[
A=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2.
\]
Since
\[
H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0,
\qquad
H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\cong \mathbb Z
\]
\cite[Secs.~2.1, 2.2]{Hatcher2002}, the universal coefficient theorem gives
\[
H^2(N,A)\cong \mathrm{Hom}(H_2(N,\mathbb Z),A)\cong A.
\]
Choose simplicial cocycles
\[
\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in Z^2(N,A)
\]
whose cohomology classes evaluate on the fundamental class $[S^2]$ as the basis vectors $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. Define the local object functor
\[
F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}:\mathcal C_a^{op}\to\mathsf{Set}
\]
by
\[
F_{p,s}(U)=\{v_1,v_2\}
\]
for every nonempty proper finite intersection $U$ of members of $\mathcal U$, by
\[
F_{p,s}(a)=\varnothing,
\]
and by the identity map on the labels $v_1,v_2$ under restriction. This local
object functor and the prestack data below are used in the generated-subsite
admissibility convention of \Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}. Then
\[
M,p\Vdash \CompSec_s(r),
\qquad
M,p\nVdash \Sec_s(r),
\]
and the sheafification has exactly two global visible classes, represented by the constant families $v_1$ and $v_2$ on the cover. For $m=1,2$, choose local realizations $x_i^{(m)}$ over the members of $\mathcal U$ with automorphism group $A$ and transition maps whose triple-overlap defect is $\alpha_m$. This is the two-label instance of the cocycle-to-prestack construction isolated in \Cref{lem:twisted-branch-prestack-model}; equivalently, it is the standard local-trivialization construction for gerbes \cite[Chap.~IV]{Giraud1971}, \cite[Sec.~5]{Breen2006}, \cite{Murray1996}. It yields a prestack presentation $\mathfrak P^{(m)}$ with
\[
\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P^{(m)})
\]
equal to the singleton subpresheaf of $F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}$ generated by $v_m$, and its stackification
\[
\mathfrak G_{v_m}:=a(\mathfrak P^{(m)})
\]
is the corresponding non-neutral $A$-banded branch gerbe.

Form the disjoint union prestack
\[
\mathfrak P_r:=\mathfrak P^{(1)}\sqcup \mathfrak P^{(2)}
\]
and let
\[
\mathfrak L_r:=a(\mathfrak P_r).
\]
Its presheaf of isomorphism classes is the disjoint union of the two singleton local-object subpresheaves supplied by $\mathfrak P^{(1)}$ and $\mathfrak P^{(2)}$, hence
\[
\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P_r)\cong F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Vis_{p,s}(r)=\{v_1,v_2\},
\]
while both component gerbes are non-neutral. Thus the two visible branches are recovered from explicit local-object data plus chosen local realization data, rather than inserted by taking gerbes as primitive input.

If $[S^2]$ denotes the fundamental class in $H_2(N,\mathbb Z)$, then
\[
\mathrm{ev}_{\omega_1}([S^2])=(1,0),
\qquad
\mathrm{ev}_{\omega_2}([S^2])=(0,1).
\]
Hence
\[
K_{v_1}=\langle(1,0)\rangle,
\qquad
K_{v_2}=\langle(0,1)\rangle,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{v_1}\cong A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{v_2}\cong \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]
Therefore
\[
\bigcap_{v} K_v=0,
\qquad
\sum_{v} K_v=A,
\qquad
\max_v |K_v|=2.
\]
By \Cref{thm:branch-sensitive-visible-quotient,thm:branch-uniform-visible-quotient,thm:multi-branch-strictification-budget},
\[
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{\mathrm{br}}(r)\cong A,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{\mathrm{unif}}(r)=0,
\qquad
\log_2\!\bigl(\max_v |K_v|\bigr)=1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
B_{\mathrm{res}}(r)\cong A,
\qquad
\log_2|B_{\mathrm{res}}(r)|=2,
\qquad
\left|\bigcap_v K_v\right|=1.
\]
Thus each branch individually hides one coordinate of $A$, but the hidden coordinates are transverse: once the branch label is known, nothing remains hidden across all branches, whereas any single character channel admissible on both branches must be trivial. In this example the entire uniform hidden budget is exhausted by branch resolution itself.
\end{example}
