\section{Homological images and aggregate quotients}
\label{sec:homological-images}

Fix a finite simplicial complex \(N\), a finite abelian group \(A\), and a
class \(\omega\in H^2(N,A)\).  The universal coefficient map is denoted by
\[
 \operatorname{ev}_\omega:
 H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow A.
\]
Define its \emph{homological image} and \emph{homological cokernel} by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:homological-image-cokernel}
 K_\omega:=\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{ev}_\omega),
 \qquad Q_\omega:=A/K_\omega.
\end{equation}
These definitions depend on the supplied finite presentation of the component
gerbe.  Refinement and retrivialization transport them by UCT naturality, but
no canonical identification along distinct comparison zigzags is asserted.
The resulting freely generated presentation groupoid and its elementary
naturality calculation are recorded in the technical appendix rather than
promoted to an invariance theorem.

For a finite family of component classes
\((\omega_v)_{v\in V}\), put \(K_v=K_{\omega_v}\) and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aggregate-quotients}
 Q_{\mathrm{lab}}:=A\Big/\bigcap_{v\in V}K_v,
 \qquad
 Q_{\mathrm{com}}:=A\Big/\sum_{v\in V}K_v.
\end{equation}
The first quotient retains enough information to map to every componentwise
quotient \(A/K_v\).  The second retains only characters which descend through
every one of them.

\begin{proposition}[UCT and aggregate subgroup package]
\label{prop:uct-aggregate-package}
With the notation above:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item The universal coefficient sequence is
      \[
      0\longrightarrow
      \operatorname{Ext}^1(H_1(N,\mathbb Z),A)
      \longrightarrow H^2(N,A)
      \xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}}
      \operatorname{Hom}(H_2(N,\mathbb Z),A)
      \longrightarrow0.
      \]
      Thus \(K_\omega=0\) exactly when \(\omega\) lies in the displayed
      Ext subgroup.  In particular, nonzero classes can have zero
      homological image when \(H_1\) has torsion.
\item The quotient map \(q_\omega:A\to Q_\omega\) is initial among group
      homomorphisms \(q:A\to B\) satisfying
      \(q\circ\operatorname{ev}_\omega=0\).  A character
      \(\chi:A\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\) factors through \(Q_\omega\)
      exactly when \(\chi(K_\omega)=0\).  Since finite Pontryagin duality
      separates points,
      \[
      K_\omega=\mathop{\bigcap}_{\chi(K_\omega)=0}\ker\chi.
      \]
\item For a nonempty finite family \((K_v)_{v\in V}\), there is a natural
      short exact sequence
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:aggregate-exact-sequence}
      0\longrightarrow
      \frac{\sum_vK_v}{\bigcap_vK_v}
      \longrightarrow Q_{\mathrm{lab}}
      \longrightarrow Q_{\mathrm{com}}
      \longrightarrow0.
      \end{equation}
      Moreover,
      \[
      \widehat{Q_{\mathrm{com}}}
      \cong\bigcap_{v\in V}K_v^\perp,
      \qquad
      \widehat{Q_{\mathrm{lab}}}
      \cong\sum_{v\in V}K_v^\perp
      \]
      inside \(\widehat A\).
\item If \(H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0\) and
      \(H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^\beta\), a subgroup
      \(K\le A\) occurs as \(K_\omega\) exactly when \(d(K)\le\beta\).
      Every finite family of such subgroups is simultaneously attained by
      the prestack of
      \Cref{thm:prescribed-component-image-construction}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Clause (i) is the cohomological universal coefficient theorem, natural in
both \(N\) and \(A\) \cite[Sec.~3.1]{Hatcher2002}.  Its middle-to-right map
evaluates a cohomology class on integral \(2\)-cycles, so its value at
\(\omega\) is \(\operatorname{ev}_\omega\).  Exactness identifies the
kernel with the Ext term.

For a simplicial map \(f:N'\to N\), naturality is the commutative square
\[
\begin{CD}
H^2(N,A) @>{\operatorname{ev}}>>
 \operatorname{Hom}(H_2(N,\mathbb Z),A)\\
@V{f^*}VV @VV{(-)\circ f_*}V\\
H^2(N',A) @>{\operatorname{ev}}>>
 \operatorname{Hom}(H_2(N',\mathbb Z),A).
\end{CD}
\]
Thus
\[
 \operatorname{ev}_{f^*\omega}
 =\operatorname{ev}_\omega\circ f_*.
\]
For a coefficient homomorphism \(\theta:A\to A'\), the corresponding
identity is
\[
 \operatorname{ev}_{\theta_*\omega}
 =\theta\circ\operatorname{ev}_\omega.
\]
These formulas are the only invariance input used for a specified
presentation comparison.  The short exact UCT sequence may split as a
sequence of groups, but no natural splitting is chosen.

For (ii), \(q\circ\operatorname{ev}_\omega=0\) says precisely that \(q\)
vanishes on \(K_\omega\).  The universal property of the quotient gives a
unique factorization through \(A/K_\omega\).  Apply the same observation to
characters.  The intersection formula follows because characters of a
finite abelian group separate points \cite{Terras1999}.

For the last assertion, pass to the finite quotient \(A/K_\omega\).  If
\(a\notin K_\omega\), then \(\bar a\ne0\) in that quotient.  Finite
Pontryagin duality gives a character
\[
 \bar\chi:A/K_\omega\longrightarrow\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z
\]
with \(\bar\chi(\bar a)\ne0\).  Its pullback \(\chi=\bar\chi q_\omega\)
annihilates \(K_\omega\) but not \(a\).  Hence no element outside
\(K_\omega\) belongs to the displayed intersection of kernels.  The reverse
containment is immediate.

The containment \(\bigcap_vK_v\subseteq\sum_vK_v\) gives the quotient map in
\eqref{eq:aggregate-exact-sequence}.  By the third isomorphism theorem its
kernel is
\[
 \frac{\sum_vK_v}{\bigcap_vK_v},
\]
which proves exactness.  For finite abelian groups, annihilators exchange sums and
intersections:
\[
 \Big(\sum_vK_v\Big)^\perp=\bigcap_vK_v^\perp,
 \qquad
 \Big(\bigcap_vK_v\Big)^\perp=\sum_vK_v^\perp.
\]
Dualizing the two quotients proves the character formulas in (iii).

The first annihilator identity does not require finiteness:
\(\chi\) kills \(\sum_vK_v\) exactly when it kills every \(K_v\).  For the
second, one inclusion is immediate:
\[
 \sum_vK_v^\perp\subseteq\Big(\bigcap_vK_v\Big)^\perp.
\]
For finite groups, equality follows from exactness of Pontryagin duality.
It may also be checked by cardinalities, using
\[
 |H^\perp|=\frac{|A|}{|H|}
\]
and, for two subgroups,
\[
 |K+L|\,|K\cap L|=|K|\,|L|.
\]
Induction gives the formula for a finite family.

Under the hypotheses of (iv), the Ext term vanishes and UCT becomes
\[
 H^2(N,A)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z^\beta,A).
\]
The image of a homomorphism from \(\mathbb Z^\beta\) is generated by the
images of the standard basis, so it has at most \(\beta\) generators.
Conversely, given generators \(k_1,\dots,k_\beta\) of \(K\), allowing zeros
when fewer are needed, the map
\[
 (n_1,\dots,n_\beta)\longmapsto\sum_jn_jk_j
\]
has image \(K\).  Applying
\Cref{thm:prescribed-component-image-construction} to a family of these maps
gives their simultaneous object-level realization on the supplied fixed
cover and band.

The number \(d(K)\) can be read prime by prime.  If
\[
 K\cong\bigoplus_p\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r_p}C_{p^{e_{p,j}}},
\]
then
\[
 d(K)=\max_p r_p.
\]
Thus the condition in (iv) is not a bound on \(|K|\) or on its exponent.
It is precisely the rank bound imposed by the \(\beta\) basis elements of
\(H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\).  Once homomorphisms with the desired images have been
chosen componentwise, simultaneous attainment is the prestack-level part
supplied by \Cref{thm:prescribed-component-image-construction}.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{How to read the aggregate quotients}

There are canonical maps
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{lab}}\longrightarrow A/K_v
 \qquad(v\in V),
\]
because \(\bigcap_wK_w\subseteq K_v\).  The product of these maps is
injective:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:labelled-product-embedding}
 A/\bigcap_vK_v
 \longrightarrow\prod_{v\in V}A/K_v,
 \qquad
 a+\bigcap_vK_v\longmapsto(a+K_v)_v.
\end{equation}
Its kernel consists of the elements lying in every \(K_v\).  Thus
\(Q_{\mathrm{lab}}\) is the part of the coefficient group retained by all
componentwise quotient values together.

In the other direction, every componentwise quotient maps onto
\(Q_{\mathrm{com}}\):
\[
 A/K_v\longrightarrow A/\sum_wK_w,
\]
because \(K_v\subseteq\sum_wK_w\).  This is the largest quotient of \(A\)
of the displayed form receiving the quotient map from every \(A/K_v\).
The map in \eqref{eq:aggregate-exact-sequence} measures the loss between
keeping the tuple of component values and keeping only their common quotient.

On characters, the directions reverse.  A character of
\(Q_{\mathrm{com}}\) must kill every \(K_v\), while a character of
\(Q_{\mathrm{lab}}\) is a finite sum of characters each killing one of the
component subgroups.  Written inside \(\widehat A\), these are precisely the
two annihilator identities in the proposition.  This interpretation uses
only the contravariance of finite duality.

\subsection*{Evaluation at chain level}

Choose a simplicial cocycle
\(\alpha\in Z^2(N,A)\) representing \(\omega\).  If
\[
 z=\sum_{\sigma\in N_2}n_\sigma\sigma
\]
is an integral \(2\)-cycle, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain-level-evaluation}
 \operatorname{ev}_\omega([z])
 =\langle\alpha,z\rangle
 =\sum_{\sigma\in N_2}n_\sigma\alpha(\sigma).
\end{equation}
Replacing \(\alpha\) by \(\alpha+\delta b\) changes the last expression by
\[
 \langle\delta b,z\rangle
 =\langle b,\partial z\rangle=0.
\]
Replacing \(z\) by \(z+\partial c\) changes it by
\[
 \langle\alpha,\partial c\rangle
 =\langle\delta\alpha,c\rangle=0.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:chain-level-evaluation} depends only on the cohomology class
and the homology class.  In the realization theorem,
\(\alpha=\alpha_\lambda\) is also the triple-overlap defect of the
\(\lambda\)-component gerbe, so the formula evaluates that defect directly
on a \(2\)-cycle of the chosen nerve.

This chain formula also explains why the coordinate subgroup
\[
 H_\alpha=\langle\alpha(\sigma):\sigma\in N_2\rangle
\]
can be larger than \(K_\omega\).  Every value in \(K_\omega\) is an integral
combination of cocycle coordinates, so \(K_\omega\subseteq H_\alpha\).
But the coefficients in \eqref{eq:chain-level-evaluation} must satisfy the
cycle equations.  An arbitrary combination of coordinates need not arise
from a cycle.  Moreover, \(H_\alpha\) can change when \(\alpha\) is altered
by a coboundary, whereas \(K_\omega\) cannot.

\subsection*{Torsion and free-homology boundary cases}

Suppose
\[
 H_1(N,\mathbb Z)
 \cong\mathbb Z^r\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^t\mathbb Z/n_j\mathbb Z.
\]
The free summand contributes nothing to the Ext term, while
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathbb Z/n_j\mathbb Z,A)
 \cong A/n_jA.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-ext-term}
 \operatorname{Ext}^1(H_1(N,\mathbb Z),A)
 \cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^t A/n_jA.
\end{equation}
If every multiplication map \(n_j:A\to A\) is surjective, the Ext term
vanishes even though \(H_1\) has torsion.  For finite \(A\), shared primary
torsion between \(H_1\) and \(A\) is what permits classes that evaluation on
\(H_2\) cannot detect.

For example, if
\[
 H_1(N,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
 \qquad H_2(N,\mathbb Z)=0,
 \qquad A=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
\]
then \(H^2(N,A)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) entirely through
\eqref{eq:explicit-ext-term}.  The nonzero class defines a non-neutral
banded gerbe, but
\[
 \operatorname{ev}_\omega:0\longrightarrow A
\]
has zero image and \(Q_\omega=A\).  The quotient does not detect neutrality
in this case.

At the opposite endpoint, if \(H_1(N,\mathbb Z)\) is free, the Ext term
vanishes.  Then
\[
 H^2(N,A)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(H_2(N,\mathbb Z),A)
\]
is an isomorphism.  A class has zero homological image only when it is zero.
The wedge-of-spheres application lies in this regime.

\subsection*{A two-component arithmetic check}

Let
\[
 A=\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z,\qquad
 K_1=\langle4\rangle,\qquad
 K_2=\langle3\rangle.
\]
Then \(K_1\) has order \(3\), \(K_2\) has order \(4\), and
\[
 K_1\cap K_2=0,\qquad K_1+K_2=A.
\]
Hence
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{lab}}=A,\qquad Q_{\mathrm{com}}=0,
\]
and the kernel in \eqref{eq:aggregate-exact-sequence} is \(A\).  Both
subgroups are cyclic, so each is the image of a homomorphism
\(\mathbb Z\to A\).  On a nerve with
\(H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\) and \(H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0\), the
prescribed-realization theorem places these supplied maps in two component
gerbes of a single prestack on that fixed cover and band.

By contrast, if \(A=\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\), any two nonzero subgroups are
comparable:
\[
 \langle4\rangle\subseteq\langle2\rangle\subseteq A.
\]
Their intersection cannot be zero.  Thus no two nonzero component images
can satisfy \(K_1\cap K_2=0\) and \(K_1+K_2=A\).  This elementary contrast
is the cyclic part of the arithmetic used in the next section.

All clauses except the simultaneous prestack realization of the supplied
maps on a fixed cover and band are standard formal consequences of UCT,
finite duality, and subgroup arithmetic.  In particular,
there is no separate information-theoretic theorem hidden in the fibres of
these quotients.  Since a quotient fibre is a coset of its kernel, the minimum
cardinality of an unrestricted auxiliary set capable of separating every
fibre is simply the kernel cardinality.  For example,
\[
 |q_\omega^{-1}(\bar a)|=|K_\omega|,
 \qquad
 \left|\ker(Q_{\mathrm{lab}}\to Q_{\mathrm{com}})\right|
 =\left|\frac{\sum_vK_v}{\bigcap_vK_v}\right|.
\]

The Ext clause also marks a limitation of the homological image.  If
\(H_2(N,\mathbb Z)=0\) and
\(\operatorname{Ext}^1(H_1(N,\mathbb Z),A)\ne0\), a nonzero gerbe class can
have \(K_\omega=0\) and \(Q_\omega=A\).  For instance, an
\(\mathbb RP^2\)-type nerve and suitable \(2\)-torsion coefficients exhibit
this behavior.  Conversely, when \(H_1(N,\mathbb Z)\) is free, the Ext term
vanishes and zero evaluation forces \(\omega=0\).  These are UCT boundary
cases, not additional classification results.
