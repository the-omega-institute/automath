\section{Component gerbes and branch obstruction classes}\label{sec:gerbe-obstruction}

From this point on the argument proceeds branchwise relative to a chosen realization prestack. When derived obstruction classes are needed, the cofinal acyclic-cover comparison data listed below are also imposed. No unconditional existence theorem for such data is sought here. This section reorganizes the gerbe-theoretic obstruction package of the companion paper \cite{MaZhang2026ConditionalGluingFailure} in the intrinsic realization-stack language used later. The first result of the section isolates the canonicity that is available once the sheafified local object functor has been lifted to a banded stack: the branch obstruction class is intrinsic to the resulting realization-stack extension, up to equivalence of extensions. Since a single reference may carry several visible value classes, the relevant obstruction objects are the corresponding component gerbes rather than a single global gerbe. The cocycle constructions used below are the standard local-trivialization constructions for gerbes \cite[Chap.~IV]{Giraud1971}, \cite[Sec.~5]{Breen2006}, together with their bundle-gerbe counterparts \cite{Murray1996,Brylinski1993}, applied branchwise in the present setting.

We begin by fixing the hypotheses used in the section. Individual statements invoke only the subcollection they require.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(H\arabic*)},nosep]
\item\label{hyp:admitted-prestack} An admitted reference $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ at a state $p$ with $a=[r]_p$, equipped with a realization prestack $\mathfrak P_r \to \mathcal C_a$ and band $\Band$ in the sense of \Cref{def:realization-prestack}.
\item\label{hyp:band-identification} The band $\Band$ is identified with a fixed abelian sheaf on the slice site $\mathcal C_{p,s}/a$.
\item\label{hyp:global-conservativity} The realization prestack $\mathfrak P_r$ is globally conservative at $a$ in the sense of \Cref{def:global-conservativity}.
\item\label{hyp:cofinal-gerbe-adapted} For every visible branch $v$, there is a filtered cofinal family
\[
\Cov_v^{\mathrm{acyc}}(a)\subseteq \Cov_{p,s}(a)
\]
such that every cover
\[
\mathcal U=\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I}\in \Cov_v^{\mathrm{acyc}}(a)
\]
has a \v{C}ech nerve in $\mathcal C_a$, satisfies
\[
H^1(U_{i_0\cdots i_n},\Band)=0
\]
for every nonempty intersection needed in the descent argument through degree $2$, and the comparison map
\[
\varinjlim_{\mathcal U\in \Cov_v^{\mathrm{acyc}}(a)} \check H^2(\mathcal U,\Band)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } H^2(\mathcal C_a,\Band)
\]
is an isomorphism, as in the standard \v{C}ech-to-derived comparison
theorems for acyclic covers \cite{Bredon1997,Jardine2015}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[\v{C}ech versus derived cohomology]\label{rem:cech-vs-derived}
Hypothesis \ref{hyp:cofinal-gerbe-adapted} is the precise comparison assumption governing the passage between \v{C}ech and derived $H^2$. Without it, the branch obstruction class lives in the filtered colimit
\[
\varinjlim_{\mathcal U\in \Cov_v^{\mathrm{acyc}}(a)} \check H^2(\mathcal U,\Band),
\]
and the non-neutrality statement in \Cref{thm:cech-bridge-compatible-realizations} is purely \v{C}ech-theoretic. With \ref{hyp:cofinal-gerbe-adapted}, this colimit is canonically identified with the derived $H^2(\mathcal C_a,\Band)$, and the obstruction class $\omega_v$ is an element of derived cohomology. All theorems in this subsection state explicitly whether they use only the \v{C}ech-level data or additionally require the comparison isomorphism.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to the companion paper]
The companion paper \cite{MaZhang2026ConditionalGluingFailure} formulates branch obstructions relative to a chosen gluing-sensitive realization prestack and its stackification. The present section isolates the three extensions needed later: the promotion of cover-relative \v{C}ech classes to derived classes $\omega_v$ under \textup{(H4)}, the reformulation of the obstruction theory for arbitrary banded realization-stack extensions of the sheafified local-object functor, and the branchwise neutrality criteria stated directly in terms of component gerbes.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Component gerbes and branch obstruction classes]\label{def:compatible-realization-stack}\label{def:cech-gluing-obstruction}
Fix an admitted reference $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ at a state $p$, write
\[
a=[r]_p,
\]
and assume that $r$ admits a realization prestack $\mathfrak P_r$ with stackification $\mathfrak L_r$ and band $\Band$ as in \Cref{def:realization-prestack}. For a visible value class
\[
v\in \Vis_{p,s}(r)\cong \pi_0(\mathfrak L_r)(a),
\]
let $\mathfrak G_v(U)$ be the full subgroupoid of $\mathfrak L_r(U)$ on those objects whose connected-component class restricts to $v$ on $U$. When \Cref{thm:component-gerbe-decomposition} applies, we call $\mathfrak G_v$ the \emph{component gerbe} of the visible branch $v$.

Assume moreover that \textup{(H4)} holds. For each visible branch $v$ and each cover
\[
\mathcal U\in \Cov_v^{\mathrm{acyc}}(a),
\]
\Cref{thm:cech-bridge-compatible-realizations} produces a \v{C}ech obstruction class
\[
[g_{\mathcal U}]\in \check H^2(\mathcal U,\Band).
\]
Refinement compatibility identifies these classes in the filtered system, and the comparison isomorphism in \textup{(H4)} therefore determines a unique derived class
\[
\omega_v\in H^2(\mathcal C_{p,s}/a,\Band),
\]
called the \emph{branch obstruction class} of $v$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Banded realization-stack extension]\label{def:realization-stack-extension}
For an admitted reference $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ at a state $p$, abbreviate
\[
a=[r]_p,
\qquad
\mathcal C_a:=\mathcal C_{p,s}/a,
\]
and fix an abelian sheaf $\Band$ on $\mathcal C_a$. Let $\mathfrak E_r$ be a
$\Band$-banded stack in groupoids on $\mathcal C_a$. A
\emph{$\Band$-banded realization-stack extension} is a pair
\[
(\mathfrak E_r,\epsilon_r)
\]
consisting of such a stack together with an isomorphism of sheaves
\[
\epsilon_r:\pi_0(\mathfrak E_r)\xrightarrow{\sim} a_{p,s}F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}
\]
on $\mathcal C_a$.

For a visible value class
\[
v\in \Vis_{p,s}(r)\cong \bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a),
\]
the \emph{$v$-component substack} of $\mathfrak E_r$ is the full substack
\[
\mathfrak E_r[v](U):=
\bigl\{x\in \mathfrak E_r(U)\mid \epsilon_{r,U}([x])=v|_U\bigr\}
\]
for objects $U$ of $\mathcal C_a$, where $\epsilon_{r,U}$ denotes the component of $\epsilon_r$ at $U$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Equivalence data and component transport]\label{lem:extension-equivalence-component-transport}
Let
\[
(\mathfrak E_r,\Band,\epsilon_r),
\qquad
(\mathfrak E'_r,\Band',\epsilon'_r)
\]
be banded realization-stack extensions on $\mathcal C_a$. Suppose an equivalence of stacks
\[
\Phi:\mathfrak E_r\to\mathfrak E'_r
\]
comes with a band isomorphism $\theta:\Band\xrightarrow{\sim}\Band'$ and satisfies
\[
\epsilon'_r\circ\pi_0(\Phi)=\epsilon_r.
\]
Then, for every visible value class $v$, $\Phi$ restricts to an equivalence of full component substacks
\[
\Phi_v:\mathfrak E_r[v]\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak E'_r[v],
\]
and this restricted equivalence is banded by $\theta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $U$ be an object of $\mathcal C_a$ and let $x\in\mathfrak E_r[v](U)$. By definition,
\[
\epsilon_{r,U}([x])=v|_U.
\]
The compatibility condition gives
\[
\epsilon'_{r,U}([\Phi(x)])
=
(\epsilon'_r\circ\pi_0(\Phi))_U([x])
=
\epsilon_{r,U}([x])
=
v|_U.
\]
Thus $\Phi(x)\in\mathfrak E'_r[v](U)$, so $\Phi$ restricts to a functor
\[
\Phi_v:\mathfrak E_r[v]\to\mathfrak E'_r[v].
\]
Since both component substacks are full, $\Phi_v$ is fully faithful because $\Phi$ is. For local essential surjectivity, take $z\in\mathfrak E'_r[v](U)$. A quasi-inverse $\Psi$ to $\Phi$ gives, after possibly refining $U$, an object $y\in\mathfrak E_r(U)$ with $\Phi(y)\cong z$. Applying $\epsilon'_r$ to this local isomorphism gives
\[
\epsilon_{r,U}([y])
=
\epsilon'_{r,U}([\Phi(y)])
=
\epsilon'_{r,U}([z])
=
v|_U,
\]
so $y$ lies locally in $\mathfrak E_r[v]$. Hence $\Phi_v$ is an equivalence of stacks. The automorphism-sheaf maps of $\Phi_v$ are the restrictions of the automorphism-sheaf maps of $\Phi$, so the band is transported by the same isomorphism $\theta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Transport of Giraud classes under banded equivalence]\label{lem:giraud-class-transport}
Let $\mathfrak G$ be a $\Band$-banded gerbe and let $\mathfrak G'$ be a $\Band'$-banded gerbe on the same site $\mathcal C$. Suppose
\[
\Phi:\mathfrak G\to\mathfrak G'
\]
is an equivalence of stacks whose induced maps on automorphism sheaves identify the bands through an isomorphism
\[
\theta:\Band\xrightarrow{\sim}\Band'.
\]
Then
\[
\theta_*[\mathfrak G]=[\mathfrak G']
\qquad\text{in}\qquad
H^2(\mathcal C,\Band').
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a cover $\{U_i\to U\}$ which locally trivializes $\mathfrak G$, objects $x_i\in\mathfrak G(U_i)$, and descent isomorphisms
\[
\varphi_{ij}:x_i|_{U_{ij}}\xrightarrow{\sim}x_j|_{U_{ij}}.
\]
The usual Giraud cocycle $c_{ijk}\in\Band(U_{ijk})$ is defined by
\[
\varphi_{ik}^{-1}\varphi_{jk}\varphi_{ij}=c_{ijk}\in\operatorname{Aut}(x_i|_{U_{ijk}})\cong\Band(U_{ijk}).
\]
Applying $\Phi$ gives local objects $\Phi(x_i)$ of $\mathfrak G'$ and comparison isomorphisms $\Phi(\varphi_{ij})$. The corresponding defect is
\[
\Phi(\varphi_{ik})^{-1}\Phi(\varphi_{jk})\Phi(\varphi_{ij})
=
\Phi(c_{ijk}),
\]
which is identified with $\theta(c_{ijk})$ under the band isomorphism. Thus the transported local trivialization of $\mathfrak G'$ has cocycle $\theta_*(c)$. Refining the cover or changing the local objects changes both cocycles by the corresponding coboundary, exactly as in the standard local-trivialization classification of banded gerbes \cite[Chap.~IV]{Giraud1971}, \cite[Tag 06NY]{StacksProject}. Hence the cohomology class of $\mathfrak G'$ is $\theta_*[\mathfrak G]$.

The functoriality invoked here is the usual naturality of Giraud's classification with respect to a morphism of bands. Equivalently, the assignment
\[
(\mathfrak G,\Band)\longmapsto [\mathfrak G]\in H^2(\mathcal C,\Band)
\]
is functorial for banded equivalences: an equivalence of gerbes whose automorphism sheaves are identified by $\theta$ transports a local object--isomorphism--defect cocycle to its pointwise image under $\theta$, and this construction is independent of all choices after passage to cohomology.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Canonical branch obstruction map under realization-stack extensions]\label{thm:intrinsic-branch-obstruction-extension}
Let $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ be admitted at a state $p$, write
\[
a=[r]_p,
\qquad
\mathcal C_a:=\mathcal C_{p,s}/a,
\]
and let
\[
(\mathfrak E_r,\Band,\epsilon_r)
\]
be a $\Band$-banded realization-stack extension in the sense of \Cref{def:realization-stack-extension}. Then the assignment
\[
\delta_{\mathfrak E_r}:\Vis_{p,s}(r)\longrightarrow H^2(\mathcal C_a,\Band),
\qquad
v\longmapsto [\mathfrak E_r[v]]
\]
is canonically defined, where $[\mathfrak E_r[v]]$ denotes the Giraud class of the $v$-component substack. More precisely:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item for every visible value class $v$, the full substack $\mathfrak E_r[v]$ is a $\Band$-banded gerbe on $\mathcal C_a$;
\item for every visible value class $v$,
\[
\delta_{\mathfrak E_r}(v)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak E_r[v]\ \text{is neutral}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak E_r[v](a)\neq\varnothing;
\]
\item if
\[
(\mathfrak E'_r,\Band',\epsilon'_r)
\]
is another banded realization-stack extension and
\[
\Phi:\mathfrak E_r\to \mathfrak E'_r
\]
is an equivalence of stacks whose induced map on automorphism sheaves identifies the bands through an isomorphism
\[
\theta:\Band\xrightarrow{\sim}\Band'
\]
and whose map on connected components satisfies
\[
\epsilon'_r\circ \pi_0(\Phi)=\epsilon_r,
\]
then for every visible value class $v$ the functor $\Phi$ restricts to an equivalence
\[
\mathfrak E_r[v]\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak E'_r[v]
\]
and
\[
\theta_*\bigl(\delta_{\mathfrak E_r}(v)\bigr)=\delta_{\mathfrak E'_r}(v)
\quad\text{in}\quad
H^2(\mathcal C_a,\Band');
\]
\item if $r$ admits a gluing-sensitive realization datum and
\[
\mathfrak E_r=\mathfrak L_r=a(\mathfrak P_r)
\]
is its stackification, then
\[
\delta_{\mathfrak E_r}(v)=\omega_v
\]
for every visible branch $v$ for which \textup{(H4)} holds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a visible value class
\[
v\in \Vis_{p,s}(r)\cong \bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a).
\]
Since $\mathfrak E_r$ is a stack in groupoids and $\mathfrak E_r[v]$ is a full substack, $\mathfrak E_r[v]$ is again a stack in groupoids.

Let $U$ be an object of $\mathcal C_a$. The section
\[
v|_U\in \bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(U)\cong \pi_0(\mathfrak E_r)(U)
\]
is, by definition of the sheaf of connected components, locally represented by objects of $\mathfrak E_r$. Hence there exists a cover
\[
\{U_i\to U\}
\]
and objects
\[
x_i\in \mathfrak E_r(U_i)
\]
with
\[
\epsilon_{r,U_i}([x_i])=v|_{U_i}.
\]
Therefore
\[
(\mathfrak E_r[v])(U_i)\neq\varnothing
\]
for every $i$, so $\mathfrak E_r[v]$ is locally nonempty.

Now let
\[
x,y\in \mathfrak E_r[v](U).
\]
Then
\[
\epsilon_{r,U}([x])=\epsilon_{r,U}([y])=v|_U.
\]
Because $\epsilon_{r,U}$ is an isomorphism, the connected-component classes $[x]$ and $[y]$ in $\pi_0(\mathfrak E_r)(U)$ coincide. By the definition of $\pi_0(\mathfrak E_r)$, there exists a cover
\[
\{U_i\to U\}
\]
such that
\[
x|_{U_i}\cong y|_{U_i}
\qquad
(\forall i).
\]
Thus any two objects of $\mathfrak E_r[v](U)$ are locally isomorphic. The full substack $\mathfrak E_r[v]$ is therefore a gerbe \cite[Chap.~IV]{Giraud1971}, \cite[Tag 06NY]{StacksProject}. Since it is full inside the $\Band$-banded stack $\mathfrak E_r$, the automorphism sheaf of every object in $\mathfrak E_r[v]$ is still identified with $\Band$. This proves \textup{(i)}.

Clause \textup{(i)} identifies $\mathfrak E_r[v]$ as a $\Band$-banded gerbe, so its Giraud class
\[
[\mathfrak E_r[v]]\in H^2(\mathcal C_a,\Band)
\]
is defined canonically \cite[Chap.~IV]{Giraud1971}, \cite[Tag 06NY]{StacksProject}. By the classification of banded gerbes, this class vanishes if and only if the gerbe is neutral. Since $a$ is the terminal object of the slice site $\mathcal C_a$, neutrality is equivalent to the existence of an object over $a$, namely
\[
\mathfrak E_r[v](a)\neq\varnothing.
\]
This proves \textup{(ii)}.

Assume now that $\Phi$ is an equivalence as in \textup{(iii)}. The compatibility condition
\[
\epsilon'_r\circ\pi_0(\Phi)=\epsilon_r
\]
first fixes the component on which the equivalence is allowed to act: an object whose component is $v|_U$ is sent to an object whose component is again $v|_U$. Hence \Cref{lem:extension-equivalence-component-transport} gives a restricted equivalence
\[
\Phi_v:\mathfrak E_r[v]\to \mathfrak E'_r[v]
\]
of the two $v$-component gerbes. This equivalence is not merely an abstract equivalence of stacks: on every local object $x$ the induced map
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathfrak E_r[v]}(x)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathfrak E'_r[v]}(\Phi_v x)
\]
is the band map $\theta:\Band\to\Band'$. Therefore the local-trivialization cocycle defining the Giraud class of $\mathfrak E_r[v]$ is transported termwise by $\theta$ to the cocycle defining the class of $\mathfrak E'_r[v]$. By the band-functorial classification recalled in \Cref{lem:giraud-class-transport},
\[
\theta_*\bigl([\mathfrak E_r[v]]\bigr)=[\mathfrak E'_r[v]]
\]
in $H^2(\mathcal C_a,\Band')$. This is exactly the displayed identity in \textup{(iii)}.

For \textup{(iv)}, let $\mathfrak E_r=\mathfrak L_r=a(\mathfrak P_r)$. By \Cref{prop:stackification-components}, the stackification carries a canonical identification
\[
\pi_0(\mathfrak L_r)\cong a_{p,s}F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a},
\]
so $\mathfrak L_r$ is a banded realization-stack extension. The $v$-component substack defined above is then exactly the component gerbe $\mathfrak G_v$ of \Cref{def:compatible-realization-stack}. On every cover
\[
\mathcal U\in \Cov_v^{\mathrm{acyc}}(a),
\]
\Cref{thm:cech-bridge-compatible-realizations} constructs the \v{C}ech class of this gerbe. The filtered colimit of those \v{C}ech classes is, by the standard classification of banded gerbes, the Giraud class of $\mathfrak G_v$, and hypothesis \textup{(H4)} identifies that colimit with the derived cohomology group
\[
H^2(\mathcal C_a,\Band).
\]
By \Cref{def:cech-gluing-obstruction}, the image of that same filtered system is precisely $\omega_v$. Therefore
\[
\delta_{\mathfrak E_r}(v)=[\mathfrak G_v]=\omega_v.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Supporting component gerbes of visible branches]\label{thm:component-gerbe-decomposition}
In the pullback-based setting of \Cref{def:compatible-realization-stack}, and using only the realization-prestack and component data fixed there, every visible branch $v\in \Vis_{p,s}(r)$ determines a full substack $\mathfrak G_v\subseteq \mathfrak L_r$ that is a banded $\Band$-gerbe over $\mathcal C_{p,s}/a$. In particular, neither global conservativity at $a$ nor the derived comparison hypothesis \textup{(H4)} is used here.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{prop:stackification-components}, the stackification
\[
\mathfrak E_r:=\mathfrak L_r
\]
is a $\Band$-banded realization-stack extension of
$a_{p,s}F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}$. The visible branch $v$ is a section of
\[
\pi_0(\mathfrak L_r)(a)\cong (a_{p,s}F_{p,s})(a),
\]
and the full substack
\[
\mathfrak G_v(U)=
\{x\in\mathfrak L_r(U)\mid [x]\ \text{restricts to}\ v|_U\}
\]
is therefore exactly the $v$-component substack
$\mathfrak E_r[v]$ of \Cref{def:realization-stack-extension}.

We recall why this is a gerbe. Because $\mathfrak L_r$ is a stack in
groupoids, every descent datum for objects whose component lies over
$v$ glues to an object whose component still lies over $v$; hence
$\mathfrak G_v$ is a full substack. Local nonemptiness follows from the
definition of a visible branch as a section of the sheaf of connected
components: after passing to a cover of any object $U$, the section
$v|_U$ is represented by objects of $\mathfrak L_r$. Local connectedness
is the local form of equality in the component sheaf: if two objects of
$\mathfrak G_v(U)$ have component $v|_U$, then they become isomorphic
after a cover of $U$. Fullness is built into the definition of
$\mathfrak G_v$, so no morphisms between objects in the component are
discarded. Finally, for every object $x$ of $\mathfrak G_v(U)$, the
automorphism sheaf of $x$ inside $\mathfrak G_v$ is the same as its
automorphism sheaf inside $\mathfrak L_r$, and this sheaf is identified
with $\Band|_{\mathcal C_a/U}$ by the banding of the realization
prestack and its stackification. Thus $\mathfrak G_v$ is a
$\Band$-banded gerbe.

This argument uses only the realization prestack, its stackification, and
the component identification. Global conservativity at $a$ concerns
whether terminal stack objects come from terminal prestack objects, and
\textup{(H4)} concerns promotion of cover-level \v{C}ech classes to
derived cohomology; neither condition enters the gerbe construction
itself.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\v{C}ech obstruction of a branch gerbe on an acyclic cover]\label{thm:cech-bridge-compatible-realizations}
Assume that $\mathfrak G_v$ is a banded component gerbe with band $\Band$ in the sense of \Cref{def:compatible-realization-stack}. Using only this gerbe and a single acyclic cover of $a$, let
\[
\mathcal U=\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I}\in \Cov_{p,s}(a)
\]
be any cover whose \v{C}ech nerve exists in $\mathcal C_{p,s}/a$, and write
\[
U_{i_0\cdots i_n}
\]
for the corresponding simplices. Assume that the cover is locally nonempty for the branch, so that objects
\[
x_i\in \mathfrak G_v(U_i)
\]
exist for all $i$, and assume
\[
H^1(U_{i_0\cdots i_n},\Band)=0
\]
for every nonempty intersection used below, including all members, pairwise overlaps, triple overlaps, and quadruple overlaps appearing in the descent calculation.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item for every pair $(i,j)$ there exists an isomorphism
\[
\theta_{ij}:x_i|_{U_{ij}}\xrightarrow{\sim} x_j|_{U_{ij}};
\]
\item on each triple overlap $U_{ijk}$, the element
\[
c_{ijk}:=\theta_{ik}^{-1}|_{U_{ijk}}\circ \theta_{jk}|_{U_{ijk}}\circ \theta_{ij}|_{U_{ijk}}
\in \Band(U_{ijk})
\]
defines a \v{C}ech $2$-cocycle $c=(c_{ijk})\in \check Z^2(\mathcal U,\Band)$;
\item the class $[c]\in \check H^2(\mathcal U,\Band)$ depends only on the branch gerbe $\mathfrak G_v$ and the chosen cover: changing the local objects or the comparison isomorphisms changes $c$ by a \v{C}ech coboundary, and refinements of acyclic covers pull this class back functorially;
\item the class $[c]$ vanishes if and only if the branch gerbe $\mathfrak G_v$ is neutral over $a$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By local nonemptiness, fix objects $x_i\in \mathfrak G_v(U_i)$. For every pair $(i,j)$, the sheaf
\[
\mathrm{Isom}(x_i|_{U_{ij}},x_j|_{U_{ij}})
\]
is a $\Band|_{U_{ij}}$-torsor. The vanishing
\[
H^1(U_{ij},\Band)=0
\]
therefore trivializes this torsor \cite[Chap.~III]{MacLaneMoerdijk1994}, \cite[Sec.~11]{StacksProject}, so we may choose a section
\[
\theta_{ij}:x_i|_{U_{ij}}\xrightarrow{\sim} x_j|_{U_{ij}}.
\]
This proves \textup{(i)}.

On a triple overlap $U_{ijk}$, the composite
\[
c_{ijk}:=\theta_{ik}^{-1}|_{U_{ijk}}\circ \theta_{jk}|_{U_{ijk}}\circ \theta_{ij}|_{U_{ijk}}
\]
is an automorphism of $x_i|_{U_{ijk}}$ and hence an element of $\Band(U_{ijk})$. On a quadruple overlap $U_{ijkl}$ one computes
\[
c_{jkl}\,c_{ijl}
=
\theta_{jl}^{-1}\theta_{kl}\theta_{jk}\,\theta_{il}^{-1}\theta_{jl}\theta_{ij}
=
\theta_{il}^{-1}\theta_{kl}\theta_{ik}^{-1}\theta_{ik}\theta_{jk}\theta_{ij}
=
c_{ikl}\,c_{ijk},
\]
where all restrictions to $U_{ijkl}$ are suppressed from the notation. This is exactly the \v{C}ech cocycle identity $\delta c=0$, so $c=(c_{ijk})$ lies in $\check Z^2(\mathcal U,\Band)$. This proves \textup{(ii)}.

For \textup{(iii)}, first keep the objects $x_i$ fixed and replace $\theta_{ij}$ by another choice $\theta'_{ij}$. Since $\mathrm{Isom}(x_i|_{U_{ij}},x_j|_{U_{ij}})$ is a $\Band|_{U_{ij}}$-torsor, there exist unique sections $h_{ij}\in \Band(U_{ij})$ with
\[
\theta'_{ij}=\theta_{ij}\circ h_{ij}^{-1}.
\]
A direct calculation on triple overlaps gives
\[
c'_{ijk}=h_{ik}\,c_{ijk}\,h_{jk}^{-1}h_{ij}^{-1},
\]
which is the multiplicative form of the statement that $c'$ and $c$ differ by the coboundary $\delta h$.

Next replace the objects $x_i$ by another family $x_i'\in \mathfrak G_v(U_i)$. Since $x_i$ and $x_i'$ lie in the same gerbe over $U_i$, the sheaf $\mathrm{Isom}(x_i,x_i')$ is a $\Band|_{U_i}$-torsor. The vanishing
\[
H^1(U_i,\Band)=0
\]
trivializes it, so choose isomorphisms
\[
\psi_i:x_i\xrightarrow{\sim} x_i'.
\]
Define
\[
\theta_{ij}':=\psi_j|_{U_{ij}}\circ \theta_{ij}\circ \psi_i^{-1}|_{U_{ij}}.
\]
Then the associated cocycle satisfies
\[
c'_{ijk}
=
\psi_i|_{U_{ijk}}\,c_{ijk}\,\psi_i^{-1}|_{U_{ijk}}
=
c_{ijk},
\]
under the band identification $\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}(x_i)\cong \Band|_{U_i}$. Thus changing local objects changes the cocycle only by the preliminary freedom in the choice of the $\psi_i$, hence only by a coboundary. If
\[
\lambda:\mathcal V\to\mathcal U
\]
is a refinement by another cover $\mathcal V\in\Cov_{p,s}(a)$ whose \v{C}ech nerve exists and whose relevant intersections satisfy the same $H^1$-vanishing hypothesis, then the same construction on $\mathcal V$ uses the pulled-back objects and isomorphisms, so the resulting cocycle is exactly $\lambda^*(c)$. Therefore the induced cohomology class is refinement compatible.

For \textup{(iv)}, suppose first that $\mathfrak G_v$ is neutral and choose a global object $x\in \mathfrak G_v(a)$. For each $i$, the sheaf $\mathrm{Isom}(x|_{U_i},x_i)$ is a $\Band|_{U_i}$-torsor and therefore trivial because $H^1(U_i,\Band)=0$. Choose
\[
\psi_i:x|_{U_i}\xrightarrow{\sim} x_i.
\]
If we set
\[
\theta_{ij}:=\psi_j|_{U_{ij}}\circ \psi_i^{-1}|_{U_{ij}},
\]
then the resulting cocycle is identically zero, so $[c]=0$.

Conversely, assume $[c]=0$. Then there is a $1$-cochain $h=(h_{ij})$ with
\[
c=\delta h.
\]
Replace $\theta_{ij}$ by
\[
\theta_{ij}':=\theta_{ij}\circ h_{ij}^{-1}.
\]
The defining equation $c=\delta h$ is exactly the statement that the $\theta_{ij}'$ satisfy the strict cocycle condition on triple overlaps. Hence $(x_i,\theta_{ij}')$ is a descent datum for the stack $\mathfrak G_v$, and since $\mathfrak G_v$ is a stack in groupoids, that descent datum is effective. The resulting glued object of $\mathfrak G_v(a)$ is a global object, so the gerbe is neutral.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[\v{C}ech calculus for branch gerbes]\label{lem:cech-branch-gerbe-calculus}
In the setting of \Cref{thm:cech-bridge-compatible-realizations}, the following four operations have the indicated effects on the cocycle
\[
c_{ijk}:=\theta_{ik}^{-1}\theta_{jk}\theta_{ij}.
\]
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item If the local objects $x_i$ are fixed and $\theta'_{ij}=\theta_{ij}\circ h_{ij}^{-1}$ for a $1$-cochain $h=(h_{ij})$ with values in $\Band$, then the new cocycle is
\[
c'_{ijk}=h_{ik}\,c_{ijk}\,h_{jk}^{-1}h_{ij}^{-1};
\]
in additive notation, $c'-c=\delta h$.
\item If local objects $x_i$ are replaced by $x_i'$ and $\psi_i:x_i\xrightarrow{\sim}x_i'$ are chosen, then
\[
\theta'_{ij}:=\psi_j\theta_{ij}\psi_i^{-1}
\]
produces the same cocycle under the band identifications. Changing the $\psi_i$ modifies the result only by a coboundary of the kind in \textup{(i)}.
\item If $\lambda:\mathcal V\to\mathcal U$ is a refinement satisfying the same acyclicity hypotheses, the cocycle constructed from the pulled-back objects and isomorphisms on $\mathcal V$ is $\lambda^*c$.
\item The class $[c]$ is zero if and only if the descent datum may be adjusted so that the comparison isomorphisms satisfy the strict cocycle condition; by effective descent for the stack $\mathfrak G_v$, this is equivalent to a global object of $\mathfrak G_v(a)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \textup{(i)}, the Isom sheaf on $U_{ij}$ is a $\Band$-torsor, so the ratio between $\theta_{ij}$ and $\theta'_{ij}$ is the unique element $h_{ij}^{-1}\in\Band(U_{ij})$ with
\[
\theta'_{ij}=\theta_{ij}\circ h_{ij}^{-1}.
\]
Substituting these three identities in
\[
c'_{ijk}=(\theta'_{ik})^{-1}\theta'_{jk}\theta'_{ij}
\]
and using that the band is abelian gives
\[
c'_{ijk}=h_{ik}\,c_{ijk}\,h_{jk}^{-1}h_{ij}^{-1}.
\]
This is precisely the multiplicative Cech coboundary formula.

For \textup{(ii)}, substituting
\[
\theta'_{ij}=\psi_j\theta_{ij}\psi_i^{-1}
\]
into the triple product gives
\[
(\theta'_{ik})^{-1}\theta'_{jk}\theta'_{ij}
=
\psi_i\theta_{ik}^{-1}\theta_{jk}\theta_{ij}\psi_i^{-1}.
\]
Under the band identification
\[
\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}(x_i)\cong\Band|_{U_i}\cong
\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}(x_i'),
\]
conjugation by $\psi_i$ is the identity on $\Band$. If the isomorphisms $\psi_i$ are changed, the new choices differ from the old ones by a $0$-cochain in the automorphism sheaves, and the resulting comparison isomorphisms differ from the displayed $\theta'_{ij}$ by the coboundary described in \textup{(i)}.

For \textup{(iii)}, a refinement map $\lambda$ sends each member of $\mathcal V$ to one member of $\mathcal U$. Pulling back the objects $x_i$ and maps $\theta_{ij}$ along these assignments gives on each triple overlap of $\mathcal V$ exactly the restriction of $c_{\lambda(i)\lambda(j)\lambda(k)}$. This is the usual pullback cocycle $\lambda^*c$.

For \textup{(iv)}, if $[c]=0$, choose $h$ with $c=\delta h$ and replace $\theta_{ij}$ by $\theta_{ij}\circ h_{ij}^{-1}$. By \textup{(i)} the new cocycle is zero, which says exactly that the modified $\theta_{ij}$ satisfy the strict descent condition on triple overlaps. Since $\mathfrak G_v$ is a stack, this descent datum is effective and glues to an object of $\mathfrak G_v(a)$. Conversely, a global object $x\in\mathfrak G_v(a)$ and local isomorphisms $x|_{U_i}\cong x_i$ produce comparison maps whose triple defect is zero; \textup{(ii)} and \textup{(i)} then show that the original cocycle class is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Derived branch classes detect neutrality]\label{prop:derived-branch-classification}
Assume \textup{(H1)}--\textup{(H4)}. For every visible branch $v$,
\[
\omega_v=0\ \text{in}\ H^2(\mathcal C_a,\Band)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak G_v\ \text{is neutral}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{def:cech-gluing-obstruction}, the derived class $\omega_v$ is the image of the refinement-compatible \v{C}ech classes supplied by \Cref{thm:cech-bridge-compatible-realizations}. The comparison isomorphism in \textup{(H4)} identifies this class with the standard Giraud class of the banded gerbe $\mathfrak G_v$. The vanishing of that derived class is equivalent to neutrality by the classification of banded gerbes by $H^2$ \cite[Chap.~IV]{Giraud1971}, \cite[Tag 06NY]{StacksProject}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Compatible local sections versus visible branches]\label{prop:compsecs-vs-visible-branches}
Assume that $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ is admitted at $p$ and admits a realization prestack in the sense of \Cref{def:realization-prestack}. Then visible branches already detect compatible local realizability, without any global-conservativity or derived-comparison hypothesis:
\[
\Vis_{p,s}(r)\neq\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M,p\Vdash \CompSec_s(r).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{thm:visible-value-components},
\[
\Vis_{p,s}(r)\cong \bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a).
\]
The implication from strict compatible local sectionability to
nonemptiness of this sheafification is
\Cref{prop:compsec-to-sheafification}. Conversely, by the definition of a
visible value class in \Cref{def:visible-value-classes}, a visible branch
is represented by local sections whose restrictions agree after the
allowed local overlap refinements. Pulling the family to those refinements
and using transitivity of the pretopology gives a strict matching family
on a cover of $a$, so \Cref{def:existence-levels}(ii) gives
$M,p\Vdash\CompSec_s(r)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Terminal-fibre nonemptiness and neutrality]\label{lem:terminal-fibre-neutrality}
Let $v\in \Vis_{p,s}(r)$. Then
\[
\mathfrak G_v(a)\neq\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak G_v\ \text{is neutral}.
\]
\leanverified{paper_gluing_failure_terminal_fibre_neutrality}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By definition of the gerbe notion used in this paper, neutrality means the existence of a global object over $a$. Therefore it is exactly the same as $\mathfrak G_v(a)\neq\varnothing$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Global sections versus neutral component gerbes]\label{prop:global-sections-vs-neutral-components}
\leanverified{paper_gluing_failure_global_sections_vs_neutral_components}
Assume that $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ is admitted at $p$, that it admits a realization prestack in the sense of \Cref{def:realization-prestack}, and that this prestack is globally conservative at $a$ in the sense of \Cref{def:global-conservativity}. Then a global realization exists exactly when some visible branch has a global object:
\[
M,p\Vdash \Sec_s(r)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists v\in \Vis_{p,s}(r)\ \text{such that}\ \mathfrak G_v(a)\neq\varnothing.
\]
Equivalently, by \Cref{lem:terminal-fibre-neutrality}, a global realization exists exactly when at least one visible branch gerbe is neutral.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From \Cref{prop:no-new-global-objects}, $M,p\Vdash \Sec_s(r)$ iff $\mathfrak L_r(a)\neq\varnothing$.

If $\mathfrak L_r(a)\neq\varnothing$, choose $x\in \mathfrak L_r(a)$ and let
\[
v=[x]\in \pi_0(\mathfrak L_r)(a)\cong \Vis_{p,s}(r).
\]
Then $x\in \mathfrak G_v(a)$, so $\mathfrak G_v(a)\neq\varnothing$.

Conversely, if $\mathfrak G_v(a)\neq\varnothing$ for some visible branch $v$, then $\mathfrak G_v(a)\subseteq \mathfrak L_r(a)$, so $\mathfrak L_r(a)\neq\varnothing$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional branched gerbe semantics of gluing-level absence]\label{thm:gerbe-null-semantics}
Assume \textup{(H1)}--\textup{(H3)} for an admitted reference $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ at $p$. Then the gluing-null condition is exactly the existence of at least one visible branch and the non-neutrality of every component gerbe:
\[
M,p\Vdash \Null^{\mathrm{glue}}_s(r)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Vis_{p,s}(r)\neq\varnothing
\ \text{and}\
\mathfrak G_v\ \text{is non-neutral for every }v\in\Vis_{p,s}(r).
\]
If, in addition, \textup{(H4)} holds, then this is equivalently
\[
M,p\Vdash \Null^{\mathrm{glue}}_s(r)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Vis_{p,s}(r)\neq\varnothing
\ \text{and}\
\omega_v\neq 0\ \text{in }H^2(\mathcal C_a,\Band)
\text{ for every }v\in\Vis_{p,s}(r).
\]
The companion theorem \cite[Theorem~5.20]{MaZhang2026ConditionalGluingFailure} is the meet-semilattice analogue of this pullback-site formulation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $r$ is admitted, the definition of gluing-level nullity gives
\[
M,p\Vdash \Null^{\mathrm{glue}}_s(r)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M,p\Vdash \CompSec_s(r)\ \text{and}\ M,p\nVdash \Sec_s(r).
\]
By \Cref{prop:compsecs-vs-visible-branches}, the compatible-local clause is equivalent to
\[
\Vis_{p,s}(r)\neq\varnothing.
\]
By \Cref{prop:global-sections-vs-neutral-components}, using global conservativity at $a$ from \textup{(H3)}, the global-section clause is equivalent to the existence of a visible branch $v$ with
\[
\mathfrak G_v(a)\neq\varnothing.
\]
By \Cref{lem:terminal-fibre-neutrality}, this is equivalent to the existence of a neutral component gerbe. Therefore
\[
M,p\nVdash \Sec_s(r)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak G_v\ \text{is non-neutral for every }v\in\Vis_{p,s}(r),
\]
provided the compatible-local clause has supplied at least one visible branch. Combining these two equivalences proves the first displayed characterization.

Assume now also \textup{(H4)}. For each visible branch $v$, \Cref{prop:derived-branch-classification} gives
\[
\omega_v=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak G_v\ \text{is neutral}.
\]
Thus non-neutrality of every component gerbe is equivalent to nonvanishing of every derived branch class $\omega_v$, and the second displayed characterization follows.
\end{proof}
