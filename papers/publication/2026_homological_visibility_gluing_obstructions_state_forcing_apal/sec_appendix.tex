\section{Presentation-comparison bookkeeping}
\label{sec:presentation-bookkeeping}

The article deliberately keeps the homological image attached to supplied
finite-site data.  This section records the limited naturality that is useful
when two such presentations have been related explicitly.  It does not assert
path independence for arbitrary zigzags and does not turn the homological
image into an invariant of an unspecified geometric object.

Fix a finite abelian group \(A\).  A \emph{finite presentation} is a pair

\[
 (N,\omega),
 \qquad N\text{ a finite simplicial complex},\quad
 \omega\in H^2(N,A).
\]

Its evaluation homomorphism and image are

\[
 e_\omega:H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow A,
 \qquad K_\omega=\operatorname{Im}(e_\omega).
\]

The notation suppresses the universal coefficient transformation.  It is
important that the source group remains visible: an equality between image
subgroups can be justified by naturality only after the relevant map on
homology and the relevant map on coefficients have both been specified.

We use three elementary comparison moves.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item A \emph{retri-vialization move} replaces a normalized cocycle
      \(\alpha\) by \(\alpha+\delta b\) on the same nerve.  It fixes the
      class \(\omega=[\alpha]\), hence fixes \(e_\omega\) and
      \(K_\omega\).
\item A \emph{simplicial move} is a simplicial map \(f:N'\to N\), carrying
      \((N,\omega)\) to \((N',f^*\omega)\).
\item A \emph{coefficient move} is an isomorphism
      \(\theta:A\xrightarrow{\sim}A'\), carrying \((N,\omega)\) to
      \((N,\theta_*\omega)\).
\end{enumerate}

Let \(\mathscr P_A\) denote the free groupoid generated by chosen invertible
instances of these moves.  Thus a morphism in \(\mathscr P_A\) remembers its
word in the selected comparison data.  Relations are imposed only for
identity moves, inverses, and literal compositions of the underlying maps.
In particular, two different refinement zigzags with the same endpoints are
not declared equal merely because they compare the same pair of nerves.

\begin{proposition}[Naturality along a specified presentation comparison]
\label{prop:presentation-comparison-naturality}
Let a morphism in the presentation groupoid be represented by a simplicial
isomorphism \(f:N'\to N\), a coefficient isomorphism
\(\theta:A\to A'\), and any number of retrivialization moves, so that

\[
 \omega'=\theta_*f^*\omega\in H^2(N',A').
\]

Then

\begin{equation}\label{eq:presentation-evaluation-square}
\begin{CD}
H_2(N',\mathbb Z) @>{e_{\omega'}}>> A'\\
@V{f_*}V{\cong}V @A{\theta}A{\cong}A\\
H_2(N,\mathbb Z) @>{e_\omega}>> A
\end{CD}
\end{equation}

commutes.  Consequently

\[
 K_{\omega'}=\theta(K_\omega),
 \qquad
 A'/K_{\omega'}\cong A/K_\omega.
\]

These identifications are functorial in the chosen word of comparison moves.
No equality between the identifications attached to distinct words is
asserted without an additional coherence argument.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The universal coefficient map is natural in the simplicial complex and in
the coefficient group.  If \(z\in H_2(N',\mathbb Z)\), then

\[
 e_{\omega'}(z)
 =e_{\theta_*f^*\omega}(z)
 =\theta\bigl(e_\omega(f_*z)\bigr),
\]

which is the commutativity of
\eqref{eq:presentation-evaluation-square}.  Since \(f_*\) and \(\theta\)
are isomorphisms, taking images gives
\(K_{\omega'}=\theta(K_\omega)\).  The induced quotient isomorphism is
\(a+K_\omega\mapsto\theta(a)+K_{\omega'}\).

A retrivialization changes a cocycle by a coboundary and hence does not
change its cohomology class.  The calculation therefore applies to each
generator.  Naturality under a composite follows by pasting the corresponding
squares.  Because the groupoid is free on the selected moves, this proves
functoriality along the recorded word and nothing stronger.
\end{proof}

The same calculation applies componentwise to the family in Theorem~4.2 of
the article.  Sums and
intersections are carried through a coefficient isomorphism, so the two
aggregate quotients in Proposition~5.1 of the article are transported
as well.  This is the entire presentation-comparison claim used by the
article.

\subsection{Cocycle changes in coordinates}

For completeness, choose a total order on the vertex set of \(N\).  A
normalized \(2\)-cochain is a function

\[
 \alpha_{ijk}\in A
 \qquad(i<j<k,\ \{i,j,k\}\in N),
\]

extended alternately to other orders and by zero to repeated indices.  Its
cocycle condition on an ordered \(3\)-simplex is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-cocycle-identity}
 \alpha_{jkl}-\alpha_{ikl}+\alpha_{ijl}-\alpha_{ijk}=0.
\end{equation}

For a \(1\)-cochain \(b=(b_{ij})\), retrivialization gives

\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-coboundary-change}
 \alpha'_{ijk}
 =\alpha_{ijk}+(\delta b)_{ijk}
 =\alpha_{ijk}+b_{jk}-b_{ik}+b_{ij}.
\end{equation}

If \(z=\sum n_{ijk}[i,j,k]\) is a simplicial \(2\)-cycle, then

\[
 \langle\delta b,z\rangle
 =\langle b,\partial z\rangle=0.
\]

Thus the coordinate calculation already shows that evaluation on homology
is unaffected by the choice of overlap arrows.  Changing local objects adds
another coboundary after conjugating the overlap arrows.  For an abelian
band, conjugation acts trivially on the coefficient sheaf, and the same
calculation applies.  This is the coordinate form of the standard
naturality statement used in the article's component-gerbe discussion.

The subgroup generated by the individual coordinates

\[
 H_\alpha=\langle\alpha_{ijk}\rangle\le A
\]

need not be unchanged by \eqref{eq:appendix-coboundary-change}.  It follows
that the coordinate quotient \(A/H_\alpha\) is not a substitute for the
homological quotient \(A/K_\omega\).  One always has

\[
 K_\omega\subseteq H_\alpha,
\]

because evaluating \(\alpha\) on a cycle forms an integral linear
combination of its coordinates, but strict containment can occur.  This is
why the article formulates its quotient through the UCT evaluation map.

\section{Finite calculations}
\label{sec:finite-calculations}

The following computations illustrate the scope and the limits of the
single UCT package in the article.  They are examples, not additional
classification results.

\subsection{A cyclic coefficient group}

Let \(A=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\), let
\(H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\), and suppose
\(H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0\).
The homomorphism

\[
 \phi(m,n)=2m\pmod 4
\]

has image \(K=\{0,2\}\).  Hence

\[
 Q=A/K\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]

Writing characters as

\[
 \chi_t(x)=\frac{tx}{4}\pmod{\mathbb Z},
 \qquad t\in\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z,
\]

the annihilator of \(K\) is

\[
 K^\perp=\{\chi_0,\chi_2\}.
\]

These are exactly the characters that factor through \(Q\).  The other two
characters distinguish elements within a quotient fibre and therefore do
not descend.

Now take two component images

\[
 K_1=\{0,2\},\qquad K_2=A.
\]

Then

\[
 \bigcap_iK_i=K_1,
 \qquad
 \sum_iK_i=A,
\]

and the aggregate exact sequence becomes

\[
 0\longrightarrow A/K_1
 \longrightarrow A/K_1
 \longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow0.
\]

This degenerate endpoint is useful as a check on directions: the labelled
quotient retains the first component's parity, while the common quotient
retains nothing.

\subsection{An Ext-only class}

Let \(N\) have the homology of \(\mathbb RP^2\), and let
\(A=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\).  Thus

\[
 H_2(N,\mathbb Z)=0,
 \qquad
 H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]

The universal coefficient sequence yields

\[
 H^2(N,A)
 \cong
 \operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)
 \cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]

For the nonzero class \(\omega\), the evaluation map has zero domain and
therefore \(K_\omega=0\).  Its homological quotient is all of \(A\), even
though the corresponding banded gerbe is non-neutral.  This example keeps
separate two questions that can otherwise be conflated:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
\item whether the Giraud class vanishes;
\item whether evaluation on integral \(2\)-cycles detects it.
\end{enumerate}

The first answer is no and the second answer is also no.  The homological
image records only the second question.

\subsection{Two complementary images}

Let \(A=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\) with basis \(e_1,e_2\), and take

\[
 K_1=\langle e_1\rangle,
 \qquad
 K_2=\langle e_2\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
 K_1\cap K_2=0,
 \qquad
 K_1+K_2=A.
\]

Thus \(Q_{\mathrm{lab}}=A\), \(Q_{\mathrm{com}}=0\), and the kernel of the
aggregate comparison is all of \(A\).  Both component images are nonzero
and proper.  A map from \(\mathbb Z^\beta\) can realize either image as soon
as \(\beta\ge1\), and the two maps can be placed in separate components by
the construction of Theorem~4.2 of the article.

For a cyclic group of prime-power order, any two nonzero subgroups are
linearly ordered.  Their intersection is therefore nonzero, so this
complementary pattern cannot occur.  For a cyclic group whose order has two
distinct prime divisors, the primary subgroups for the two primes have zero
intersection and generate the whole group.  These elementary cases account
for the exceptional clause in Corollary~6.1 of the article.

\subsection{A matrix check}

When \(H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^\beta\) and

\[
 A\cong\bigoplus_{r=1}^s\mathbb Z/n_r\mathbb Z,
\]

a homomorphism \(\phi:H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\to A\) is represented by an
\(s\times\beta\) matrix whose \(r\)-th row is read modulo \(n_r\).  The
subgroup \(K_\omega\) is the column span in \(A\).  This gives a direct
finite check of every quotient in the article:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item compute the column span for each component matrix;
\item compute intersections and sums inside the invariant-factor model of
      \(A\);
\item quotient \(A\) by the resulting subgroup;
\item check the character description by solving the corresponding modular
      annihilator equations.
\end{enumerate}

The calculation is presentation-relative because the choice of basis for
\(H_2\) and the invariant-factor coordinates of \(A\) are part of the input.
Changing either basis transports the matrices by invertible row or column
operations and leaves the abstract image subgroup unchanged through the
specified isomorphism.

\section{A narrow lower-language separation example}
\label{sec:auxiliary-separation}

This final section explains why the article accepts a presheaf lift as input
instead of claiming that arbitrary lift data arise naturally from a lower
pointwise semantics.  The example is intentionally narrow.  It establishes
only that one constant-free, one-reference-variable language cannot define a
matching-family predicate.  It does not produce a banded prestack and plays
no role in the proofs cited from the article.

Let \(L\) be a relational language with one distinguished sort \(R\), no
constants of sort \(R\), no parameters, and formulas having at most one free
variable \(x:R\).  Let \(\mathcal M\) be an \(L\)-structure and suppose a
finite family of elements of \(R^\mathcal M\) forms a single orbit under
\(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal M)\).  Automorphism invariance gives

\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit-invariance}
 \mathcal M\models\varphi(r)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal M\models\varphi(r')
\end{equation}

for every parameter-free one-variable formula \(\varphi\) and every two
elements \(r,r'\) in that orbit.

Attach to each \(r\in R^\mathcal M\) a finite pullback site
\(\mathcal C_r\) and a set-valued presheaf \(F_r\).  This attachment is
external to the language \(L\).  Choose two orbit elements \(r_0,r_1\) so
that:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item \(F_{r_0}\) has local sections on a cover whose restrictions agree on
      every pullback overlap;
\item \(F_{r_1}\) has local sections on the corresponding cover, but at least
      one pair has unequal restrictions on a nonempty overlap.
\end{enumerate}

Such data can be realized on the cover with two members and one nonempty
overlap.  Give \(F_{r_0}\) a singleton on both cover members and on the
overlap, with the unique restriction maps.  Give \(F_{r_1}\) singleton
sets on the cover members, a two-element set on the overlap, and restriction
maps selecting different elements.  In both cases the terminal value may be
empty.

Equation \eqref{eq:orbit-invariance} shows that no formula in the stated
fragment distinguishes \(r_0\) from \(r_1\), while the attached presheaf
predicate ``there exists a strict matching family'' does distinguish them.
Therefore that predicate is not definable uniformly in this fragment from
the pointwise \(L\)-structure alone.

The restrictions on the language matter.  With two free reference
variables, equality may distinguish tuples by equality pattern.  With named
parameters, a formula \(x=r_0\) isolates the selected witness.  With symbols
that directly encode restriction maps or matching families, the predicate
may be definable by construction.  The example therefore supplies no broad
undefinability theorem.  It supports only the modest interface decision in
Section~2 of the article: the finite site,
presheaf, and banded prestack
are supplied data unless a separate source construction is proved.

The article's empirical-model boundary result, Theorem~7.1, gives a
substantive instance of the
same caution.  A support presheaf canonically yields a split stack, but a
non-split banded lift and its degree-two class remain additional data.
