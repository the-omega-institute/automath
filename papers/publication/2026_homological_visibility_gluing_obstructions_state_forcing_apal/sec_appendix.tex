\appendix

We collect two auxiliary topics. The first concerns the refinement-dynamic extension together with finite-state complexity bounds for the associated information-determination notions. The second gives the GHZ/Mermin banded groupoid lift of the support presheaf mentioned in the introduction.
\section{Finite-state complexity bounds}\label{sec:appendix-complexity}

We record the finite-state upper bounds for the information-determination notions attached to the refinement-dynamic extension of the semantics.

For this appendix, assume that the indexed refinement system is finite and that a distinguished formula $\chi$ is part of the input together with polynomial-time decidable tables for the relations
\[
q\mapsto (M,q\Vdash\chi),
\qquad
q\mapsto (M,q\Vdash\neg\chi),
\qquad
q\mapsto (\mathrm{info}_j(q))_{j\in J}.
\]
The finite information maps for refinement axes $\mathrm{info}_j$ of \Cref{def:axis-information-maps} are assumed to be part of the input model; agreement of $K$-information between two states is checked by comparing $\mathrm{info}_j(q)=\mathrm{info}_j(r)$ for each $j\in K$. This is the natural finite encoding once the underlying semantic state graph has already been computed and the information-determination problems are asked over that graph. No matching lower bounds are claimed here.

We consider the decision problems \textup{\textsc{Determining}}, \textup{\textsc{NecessaryAxis}}, and \textup{\textsc{MinDetermining}} associated with \Cref{def:axis-support,def:axis-indispensability}. For \textup{\textsc{MinDetermining}} we use the standard class $\mathrm{D}^P$ in the sense of Papadimitriou and Yannakakis \cite{PapadimitriouYannakakis1984}.

\begin{proposition}[Complexity upper bounds]\label{thm:axis-complexity-upper-bounds}
Under the finite encoding above, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item \textup{\textsc{Determining}} belongs to coNP.
\item \textup{\textsc{NecessaryAxis}} belongs to NP.
\item \textup{\textsc{MinDetermining}} belongs to $\mathrm{D}^P$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \textup{\textsc{Determining}}, failure of determination is witnessed by a pair $(q,r)$ of $K$-reachable states that agree on the relevant $K$-information, both decide $\chi$, and satisfy opposite truth values. Reachability, agreement of axis information, and decision of $\chi$ are all polynomial-time checkable in the finite input, so the complement of \textup{\textsc{Determining}} is in NP.

For \textup{\textsc{NecessaryAxis}}, a witness is again a pair $(q,r)$, now only required to agree on all axes except the distinguished axis $j$ and to satisfy opposite truth values of $\chi$. This certificate is polynomially checkable, so \textup{\textsc{NecessaryAxis}} lies in NP.

For \textup{\textsc{MinDetermining}}, the condition that $K$ determines $\chi$ is in coNP by the first clause. The condition that every axis in $K$ is necessary is in NP, because one may guess one witness pair for each axis $j\in K$, and the total certificate size remains polynomial. By \Cref{prop:indispensability-implies-minimality}, the conjunction of these two conditions characterizes minimal information-determining axis sets, so \textup{\textsc{MinDetermining}} lies in $\mathrm{D}^P$.
\end{proof}

\section{Refinement dynamics}\label{sec:appendix-dynamics}

This section contains the formal refinement-dynamic material.

\subsection{Refinement dynamics and information determination}\label{sec:appendix-multiaxis-refinement}

The previous sections analyzed satisfaction at a fixed state. We now treat refinement itself as a dynamics: the satisfaction clause stays unchanged, but the set of assignments may shrink along different axes of information. The results apply to model classes in which refinement induces morphisms of slice sites and compatible maps of finite nerves.

\subsubsection{Dynamic refinement}

\begin{definition}[Indexed refinement system]\label{def:multi-axis-dynamic-structure}
Fix a model $M$. A \emph{indexed refinement system} on $M$ is a tuple
\[
\mathfrak{D}=\bigl(\St(M),\le,\tau,J,\{\rightsquigarrow_j\}_{j\in J}\bigr),
\]
where $J$ is an axis set, each $\rightsquigarrow_j$ is a single-axis refinement relation satisfying
\[
p\rightsquigarrow_j q \Longrightarrow q\le p,
\]
and $\tau:\St(M)\to T$ maps states to a linearly ordered set $T$ such that
\[
p\rightsquigarrow_j q \Longrightarrow \tau(q)\ge \tau(p).
\]
For finite $K\subseteq J$, write $p\rightsquigarrow_K q$ if $q$ is reachable from $p$ by a finite chain of refinements using only axes in $K$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Updates preserve satisfaction]\label{prop:updates-preserve-forcing}
If $p\rightsquigarrow_K q$ and $M,p\Vdash\varphi$, then $M,q\Vdash\varphi^\uparrow$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every indexed step is a refinement by \Cref{def:multi-axis-dynamic-structure}, so $q\le p$. The claim is therefore immediate from
\artresult{Persistence of universal team satisfaction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Delayed decidability]\label{def:delayed-decidability}
Let $p\rightsquigarrow_K q$. We say that $\varphi$ undergoes \emph{delayed decidability} from $p$ to $q$ if
\[
M,p\nVdash\varphi,\qquad
M,p\nVdash\neg\varphi,\qquad
\text{but}\qquad
M,q\Downarrow\varphi.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Delayed decidability does not create truth]\label{prop:delayed-no-new-truth}
If $\varphi$ undergoes delayed decidability in a transition $p\rightsquigarrow_K q$, then the passage from $p$ to $q$ acts by eliminating assignments from $R_p$ rather than by changing the truth conditions of $\varphi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $M,p\Vdash\varphi$ or $M,p\Vdash\neg\varphi$ already held, then \Cref{prop:updates-preserve-forcing} would preserve that judgment at $q$. Hence delayed decidability can occur only because the refinement $q\le p$ has discarded assignments that formerly gave incompatible outcomes. The semantic clause for $\varphi$ itself has not changed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Delayed classification is non-retrocausal]\label{prop:nonretrocausal-delayed-classification}
Let $p\rightsquigarrow_K q$, and suppose that $q$ is $\tau$-minimal among the $K$-reachable states that decide $\varphi$. Then every $u$ with
\[
p\rightsquigarrow_K u
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\tau(u)<\tau(q)
\]
is still undecided on $\varphi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If some earlier $u$ with $\tau(u)<\tau(q)$ already decided $\varphi$, then $q$ would not be $\tau$-minimal among the deciding states reachable from $p$. Thus no earlier stage carries the later decision. The decision at $q$ records newly available discriminatory power at $q$, not a change in the past.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Information determination and explicit lifting}

\begin{definition}[Finite information maps for refinement axes]\label{def:axis-information-maps}
Fix an indexed refinement system $\mathfrak{D}$ on a model $M$. For each axis $j\in J$, assume there is a finite set $I_j$ and a map
\[
\mathrm{info}_j:\{q\in\St(M)\mid p\rightsquigarrow_K q\text{ for some finite }K\ni j\}\longrightarrow I_j.
\]
For states $q$ and $r$ reachable from $p$ via $K$-refinements and a subset $L\subseteq K$, we say that $q$ and $r$ \emph{agree on $L$-information} if
\[
\mathrm{info}_j(q)=\mathrm{info}_j(r)
\qquad
\text{for every }j\in L.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Information-determining axis sets and minimality]\label{def:axis-support}
Let $K\subseteq J$ be finite. We say that $K$ \emph{determines} a formula $\varphi$ at $p$, written $K\triangleright_p\varphi$, if whenever
\[
p\rightsquigarrow_K q,\qquad
p\rightsquigarrow_K r,
\]
and both $q$ and $r$ decide $\varphi$, agreement of the $K$-information (in the sense of \Cref{def:axis-information-maps}) implies agreement of the decision. An information-determining set $K$ is \emph{minimal} if no proper subset of $K$ still determines $\varphi$ at $p$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Necessity of a refinement axis]\label{def:axis-indispensability}
Let $K\subseteq J$ be finite and let $j\in K$. We say that $j$ is \emph{necessary relative to $K$} for $\varphi$ at $p$ if there exist states $q$ and $r$ with
\[
p\rightsquigarrow_K q,\qquad
p\rightsquigarrow_K r,
\]
such that $q$ and $r$ agree on $(K\setminus\{j\})$-information (in the sense of \Cref{def:axis-information-maps}), while
\[
M,q\Vdash\varphi
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
M,r\Vdash\neg\varphi.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Necessity of a refinement axis implies minimality]\label{prop:indispensability-implies-minimality}
If $K\triangleright_p\varphi$ and every $j\in K$ is necessary relative to $K$ for $\varphi$ at $p$, then $K$ is a minimal information-determining axis set for $\varphi$ at $p$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Assume that a proper subset $L\subsetneq K$ still determined $\varphi$, and choose $j\in K\setminus L$. Any two states that agree on all axes in $K\setminus\{j\}$ automatically agree on all axes in $L$, contradicting the necessity of the refinement axis $j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Pairwise commuting refinements]\label{def:axis-orthogonality}
Two axes $j,k\in J$ are \emph{pairwise commuting at $p$} if whenever
\[
p\rightsquigarrow_j q
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
p\rightsquigarrow_k r,
\]
there exists a state $u$ such that
\[
p\rightsquigarrow_{\{j,k\}} u,
\qquad
u\le q,
\qquad
u\le r.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Common refinements and completeness]\label{def:fusion-state}
Let $K\subseteq J$ be finite, and suppose that for each $j\in K$ there is a single-axis refinement $p\rightsquigarrow_j q_j$. A state $u$ is a \emph{common refinement} for the family $(q_j)_{j\in K}$ if
\[
p\rightsquigarrow_K u
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
u\le q_j\ \text{for every }j\in K.
\]
The indexed refinement system \emph{admits all required common refinements} if every finite pairwise commuting family of single-axis refinements admits a common refinement.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Explicit lifting principle]\label{cor:explicit-lifting}
Assume that all required common refinements exist. Let $K\subseteq J$ be finite, and for each $j\in K$ let
\[
p\rightsquigarrow_j q_j
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Read_{q_j,s_j}(r_j)=v_j\neq \Indet.
\]
If the single-axis refinements are pairwise commuting at $p$, then there exists a common refinement $u$ such that
\[
\Read_{u,s_j}(r_j)=v_j
\qquad
(\forall j\in K).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By the common-refinement hypothesis there exists a common refinement $u$ with $u\le q_j$ for all $j\in K$. Since each $q_j$ carries a determined typed readout, \Cref{prop:typed-readout-persistence} transports that readout to the common refinement $u$. Thus all axis-wise readout judgments hold simultaneously at one state.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Componentwise obstruction monotonicity}

The obstruction theory of \Cref{sec:null-decomposition} becomes dynamic once global component sections and their component gerbes are transported along refinement. In the model classes just described, the basic structural fact is that refinement can only shrink the homological image subgroup and enlarge its cokernel.

\begin{proposition}[Refinement monotonicity of componentwise homological cokernels]\label{thm:branch-visibility-monotonicity}
Let $q\le p$, let $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ be admissible at both states, and write
\[
a_p=[r]_p,
\qquad
a_q=[r]_q.
\]
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item there is a morphism of slice sites
\[
f_{q,p}:\mathcal C_{q,s}/a_q\to \mathcal C_{p,s}/a_p;
\]
\item the banded groupoid lift at $q$ is equivalent to the pullback of the banded groupoid lift at $p$ along $f_{q,p}$;
\item global component sections pull back along refinement, and for a component section $w\in \Vis_{q,s}(r)$ lying above $v\in \Vis_{p,s}(r)$ the corresponding component gerbes satisfy
\[
\mathfrak G_w\simeq f_{q,p}^*(\mathfrak G_v);
\]
\item compatible finite nerve representatives have been chosen together with a simplicial refinement map
\[
N(f_{q,p}):N(\mathcal U_q)\to N(\mathcal U_p)
\]
such that the induced component obstruction classes satisfy
\[
\omega_w=N(f_{q,p})^*(\omega_v)
\]
with the same finite abelian band $A$.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
X_v\subseteq X_w,
\qquad
K_w\subseteq K_v,
\qquad
Q_w\twoheadrightarrow Q_v,
\qquad
|K_w|\le |K_v|.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The pullback of gerbes is again a gerbe, and the induced cohomology class is the pullback of the original one \cite[Tag 06NY]{StacksProject}, \cite[Theorem~3.11]{BerghSchnurer2021}. Naturality of the universal coefficient map with respect to the simplicial refinement map $N(f_{q,p})$ gives the cohomological compatibility needed below. Let $\chi\in X_v$. Then
\[
\chi_*(\omega_v)=0.
\]
Applying the pullback $N(f_{q,p})^*$ gives
\[
\chi_*(\omega_w)=\chi_*\bigl(N(f_{q,p})^*(\omega_v)\bigr)=N(f_{q,p})^*\bigl(\chi_*(\omega_v)\bigr)=0,
\]
so $\chi\in X_w$. Hence $X_v\subseteq X_w$.

By \artresult{Character-theoretic detection of the homological cokernel},
\[
K_v=\bigcap_{\chi\in X_v}\ker\chi
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
K_w=\bigcap_{\chi\in X_w}\ker\chi.
\]
Since $X_v\subseteq X_w$, intersecting over the larger family gives the smaller subgroup, so $K_w\subseteq K_v$. Therefore the quotient map $A\twoheadrightarrow A/K_v$ factors through $A/K_w$, yielding a surjection
\[
Q_w=A/K_w\twoheadrightarrow A/K_v=Q_v.
\]
For finite groups, $K_w\subseteq K_v$ implies $|K_w|\le |K_v|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A strict drop under refinement]\label{ex:refinement-drop}
Let $A=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$, and let $r$ be admissible at $q\le p$.
Assume that at $p$ there is one global component section $v$ and a component obstruction class
\[
\omega_v\in H^2(N(\mathcal U_p),A)
\]
with
\[
K_v=\langle 2\rangle\subseteq A.
\]
Hence
\[
Q_v=A/K_v\cong \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]

Suppose $r$ has a unique component section $w$ above $v$ at the refinement $q$, and the induced pullback class
\[
\omega_w=f_{q,p}^*(\omega_v)
\]
is trivial in $H^2(N(\mathcal U_q),A)$. Then
\[
K_w=\{0\},\qquad Q_w=A/K_w\cong A.
\]
By \Cref{thm:branch-visibility-monotonicity}, the map
\[
Q_w\longrightarrow Q_v
\]
is the quotient mod~$2$, so $|K_w|<|K_v|$ and the logarithmic fibre cardinality decreases strictly under this refinement.
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}[Refinement decreases presentation-relative logarithmic fibre cardinality]\label{cor:branch-budget-monotonicity}
Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:branch-visibility-monotonicity}, refinement can only enlarge the quotient and can never increase the presentation-relative minimum auxiliary register size of the componentwise quotient map:
\[
\log_2|K_w|\le \log_2|K_v|.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is the last clause of \Cref{thm:branch-visibility-monotonicity} rewritten on a logarithmic scale.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Readout-preserving rewrites}

\begin{definition}[Readout-preserving rewrite]\label{def:value-preserving-rewrite}
A \emph{readout expression} is any term whose semantic role at a state is determined by a typed readout. A rewrite
\[
E\leadsto E'
\]
between readout expressions is \emph{value preserving} if for every state $p$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item $\Read_p(E)$ is defined if and only if $\Read_p(E')$ is defined,
\item whenever defined, $\Read_p(E)=\Read_p(E')$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Readout-preserving rewrites do not create facts]\label{prop:value-preserving-no-creation}
If $E\leadsto E'$ is value preserving and
\[
\Read_p(E')=v
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
v\neq \Indet,
\]
then
\[
\Read_p(E)=v.
\]
In particular, a readout-preserving rewrite cannot turn an inadmissible or non-unique-or-absent expression into a genuinely new value claim.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{def:value-preserving-rewrite}, definedness is preserved in both directions, and whenever the readouts are defined they agree. Hence the determined readout of $E'$ gives the same determined readout for $E$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Normalization does not create facts]\label{cor:normalization-no-fact-creation}
Let
\[
E=E_0\leadsto E_1\leadsto\cdots\leadsto E_n=E'
\]
be a finite sequence of readout-preserving rewrites. If $\Read_p(E')=v\neq \Indet$, then $\Read_p(E)=v$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{prop:value-preserving-no-creation} inductively along the rewrite sequence.
\end{proof}

\section{A split GHZ/Mermin banded groupoid lift}\label{sec:appendix-ghz}

The GHZ/Mermin model provides a convenient illustration of the split lift in
\artresult{Canonical split stack and the degree obstruction}. The role of this section is
correspondingly limited: it records chosen representatives for that split
lift, not a constructed non-split finite-nerve GHZ example. In particular, it
does not supply a canonical finite nerve, a normalized $2$-cocycle, a
\v{C}ech-to-derived comparison map, or a nonzero value of $K_\omega$ for GHZ.

Let $e_{\mathrm{GHZ}}$ be the Abramsky--Brandenburger empirical model obtained from the GHZ state \cite{GreenbergerHorneZeilinger1989,Mermin1990,AbramskyBrandenburger2011}, situated in the Kochen--Specker tradition \cite{KochenSpecker1967}. Related topological and cohomological treatments of contextuality include \cite{OkayRaussendorf2016,OkayRobertsBartlettRaussendorf2017,OkayTyhurstRaussendorf2018,Aasnaess2022}. The measurement cover consists of the four contexts
\[
C_{XXX},\ C_{XYY},\ C_{YXY},\ C_{YYX},
\]
where, for instance, $C_{XYY}$ records the joint measurement in which the first observer measures $X$ and the other two measure $Y$. Each context carries outcomes in $\{\pm 1\}^3$, and the GHZ support presheaf $S_{\mathrm{GHZ}}$ selects exactly the assignments whose parity matches the GHZ constraints: $+1$ on $XXX$ and $-1$ on the three $XYY$-type contexts.

\begin{definition}[Banded groupoid lift of a support presheaf]\label{def:amplitude-lift}
Let $S_e$ be the support presheaf of a finite empirical model on a measurement-cover site $\mathcal C_a$. An \emph{banded groupoid lift of the support presheaf} of $S_e$ with band $\Band$ is a banded groupoid lift
\[
\mathfrak P_e\to \mathcal C_a
\]
together with an identification
\[
\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P_e)\cong S_e.
\]
Equivalently, such a lift is a choice of lift objects and transition
isomorphisms whose isomorphism classes recover the supported local assignments.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Support data versus a non-split banded groupoid lift]\label{rem:amplitude-lift-choice}
The support presheaf canonically determines the split lift of
\artresult{Canonical split stack and the degree obstruction}, but that lift has only neutral
component gerbes. It does not determine a non-split banded groupoid lift of the support presheaf, a
nonzero component obstruction class, or a nonzero value of $K_\omega$. Such claims
for empirical support presheaves are therefore relative to additional lift
data.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Chosen GHZ phase-line lift]\label{lem:ghz-prestack}
Let $a=[r]_p$ be the GHZ base object and $\mathcal C_a:=\mathcal C_{p,s}/a$. Fix, for each object $U$ of $\mathcal C_a$ and each supported local assignment $s\in S_{\mathrm{GHZ}}(U)$, a one-dimensional complex line $L_{U,s}$ generated by a chosen local GHZ amplitude, together with restriction isomorphisms compatible with restriction of assignments. Let $\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{GHZ}}(U)$ be the groupoid whose objects are the pairs
\[
(s,L_{U,s}),
\qquad
s\in S_{\mathrm{GHZ}}(U),
\]
and whose automorphisms are the signs $\{\pm 1\}\cong \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ acting on the chosen line. Then $\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{GHZ}}\to\mathcal C_a$ is a banded groupoid lift of the support presheaf $S_{\mathrm{GHZ}}$ with band $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. Its stackification is bandedly equivalent to
\[
\operatorname{Disc}(aS_{\mathrm{GHZ}})
\times\mathsf B(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z),
\]
so every component gerbe is neutral.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By construction, restriction along $V\to U$ sends $(s,L_{U,s})$ to the restricted supported assignment together with the chosen restricted line, so the fibres form a category fibred in groupoids over $\mathcal C_a$. The automorphism sheaf of every object is the constant sheaf $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$, acting by sign changes on the one-dimensional line. Forgetting the line remembers only the supported assignment, and two objects are isomorphic exactly when they have the same supported assignment. Hence
\[
\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{GHZ}})\cong S_{\mathrm{GHZ}},
\]
so \Cref{def:amplitude-lift} is satisfied.
The compatible choice of one line for every supported assignment gives an
equivalence with the split prestack having those assignments as components
and $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ as each automorphism sheaf. Stackification
therefore gives the displayed split stack, whose identity torsor supplies a
global object in each component.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Limit of the chosen GHZ/Mermin lift]\label{thm:ghz-lift-limit}
Let $S_{\mathrm{GHZ}}$ be the GHZ support presheaf, and fix the chosen banded groupoid lift of the support presheaf $\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{GHZ}}$ of \Cref{lem:ghz-prestack}. Suppose, in addition, that one has chosen a stackification
\[
\mathfrak L_{\mathrm{GHZ}}:=a(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{GHZ}})
\]
and a cofinal family of covers
\[
\Cov_{\mathrm{GHZ}}^{\mathrm{acyc}}(a)
\]
such that the terminal-fibre essential-surjectivity and \v{C}ech-to-derived comparison clauses of \Cref{def:gluing-sensitive-banded-lift} hold for this chosen lift. Then
\[
(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{GHZ}},\mathfrak L_{\mathrm{GHZ}},\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,\Cov_{\mathrm{GHZ}}^{\mathrm{acyc}})
\]
is a gluing-sensitive banded lift datum for the chosen split GHZ banded
groupoid lift, but every Giraud class furnished by its component gerbes is zero. In
particular, it does not recover the nonzero relative degree-one classes of
\cite{AbramskyMansfieldSoaresBarbosa2012}, and it yields no nonzero value of
$K_\omega$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:ghz-prestack} identifies the stackification as a split banded lift.
The additional assumptions are exactly the remaining clauses in
\Cref{def:gluing-sensitive-banded-lift}. The general theorems therefore
apply, but neutrality of every component gerbe forces all resulting Giraud
classes, homological evaluation maps, and homological image subgroups to vanish.
\end{proof}
