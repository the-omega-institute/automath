Overall decision
Decision on the present submission: reject and invite a substantially reframed resubmission elsewhere.
This is not a rejection on the former APAL grounds. The revised manuscript now states its limitations with unusual clarity: the principal finite-group result is explicitly tied to a chosen good-cover site, finite nerves, a constant finite band, and chosen non-split lift data; it expressly denies that the construction is an invariant of bare empirical models.  It also correctly states that the higher semantic layers are classes of supplied typed expansions, not an existence or closure theorem for pre-existing lower-layer models. 
My rejection is instead based on four remaining matters:


The priority account is not yet safe. The central “component gerbe over a global component section” construction has a direct Giraud antecedent more exact than the manuscript acknowledges.


Much of the advertised theorem structure is textbook gerbe theory, UCT naturality, finite duality, subgroup arithmetic, or fibre counting. The exact packaging may be new, but the mathematical advance is smaller than the number and presentation of the theorems suggest.


There is a real missing hypothesis in the general finite-nerve Čech–simplicial comparison. The flagship wedge theorem can be repaired without changing its conclusion, but Section 5 is presently stated too generally.


The proofs of the two claims most capable of carrying originality—the representative-rigid terminal theorem and the prescribed-image prestack construction—are in the unavailable supplement. The main article itself identifies these as supplement material.  I therefore assess their statements and priority below, but I cannot certify those proofs from main.pdf.


I do not find evidence in the displayed statements of a false flagship finite-group equivalence. The problem is priority, significance, architecture, and one correctable coefficient hypothesis—not an obvious contradiction in the finite-abelian algebra.

2. Priority audit
Bottom-line priority judgment
The priority narrative is accurate about scope but not yet accurate about mathematical ancestry.
The manuscript now correctly denies a new general classification of gerbes, a presentation-free invariant, a construction from bare empirical models, and an unconditional non-split lifting theorem. That part is satisfactory.
The unsafe part is the claim that the article’s own contribution includes the component-gerbe construction and a broad family of construction/classification results without identifying how directly those are already contained in classical stack and gerbe theory. There is one definite omitted theorem-level antecedent and several theorem families that are standard lemmas in specialized notation.
I did not find a previously published theorem stating the entire bespoke two-label wedge classification verbatim. That does not make it a substantial priority claim: exact statements manufactured by composing standard equivalences can be formally new while having little independent mathematical priority.

3. Mathematical and expository objections requiring correction
3.1 The finite-nerve Čech–simplicial comparison is missing connectedness
Proposition 5.6 identifies an ordered Čech n-cochain with one element of A for every nonempty (n+1)-fold intersection, and then identifies that with one coefficient on the corresponding simplex of the ordinary nerve. 
That is not valid merely because the band is trivialized as a constant sheaf. For a disconnected overlap Ui0​⋯in​​,
Γ(Ui0​⋯in​​,A​)≅Aπ0​(Ui0​⋯in​​),
not one copy of A. The ordinary nerve records that the overlap is nonempty but not how many connected components it has.
The open-star good covers used in the flagship theorem have contractible, hence connected, nonempty intersections. Thus this does not destroy Theorem 6.16. It does invalidate the generality claimed in Section 5.
Resolution: choose one:


add the hypothesis that every nonempty finite intersection is connected and that the coefficient trivializations identify its section group coherently with A; or


replace the ordinary nerve by the Čech simplicial set/component nerve, with one simplex for each connected component; or


formulate the comparison with the appropriate local coefficient system.


This correction is mandatory. Without it, Section 5 contains a false general assertion.

3.2 The generated-site Leray comparison needs an explicit Comparison Lemma
The proof of Lemma 6.14 passes quickly from contractible open-star intersections to sheaf cohomology of ∣K∣ on the generated site. Contractibility alone does not identify the sheaf category or derived cohomology of an abstractly generated finite-intersection site with the usual topological site.
The required statement is plausible: the open stars and their finite intersections form a basis stable under finite intersections, and the generated coverage should be the induced basis topology. But that equivalence must be stated and proved.
Resolution: add a theorem saying that:


the all-subdivision open-star/intersection category is a basis for Xβ​;


its generated topology agrees with the induced open-cover topology;


restriction induces an equivalence of sheaf categories;


the paper’s “genuine constant sheaf” corresponds to the ordinary constant sheaf;


the relevant intersections are acyclic;


the filtered Čech colimit computes derived H2.


If the supplement already proves exactly this for the all-subdivision site, cite the theorem verbatim in Lemma 6.14 rather than replacing it by two sentences. If it proves only the comparison for one finite intersection site, an additional passage to the common generated site is still needed.

3.3 The presentation-comparison groupoid currently proves too little
The groupoid is freely generated by refinements already assumed to induce H2​-isomorphisms, cocycle replacements, band retrivializations, and inverses. The preservation of Kω​ under those chosen arrows is then UCT naturality. Distinct zigzags may act by different automorphisms, and no path independence is asserted. 
This is legitimate bookkeeping, but not a meaningful invariance theorem. The category has been defined so that the subgroup transports.
Resolution: either:


prove coherence/path independence over a naturally occurring category of presentations; or


demote the groupoid to a definition and one naturality proposition, and remove it from the principal originality list.


For the present paper, the second option is the realistic one.

If an actual application imposes locality, equivariance, continuity, computability, or common encoder constraints, a nontrivial result could remain. No such constraint is presently present.
