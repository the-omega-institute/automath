\subsection{The exact interface with empirical models}

Abramsky--Brandenburger empirical models already have two categorical
reformulations close to the question considered here. Car\`u identifies the
degree-one cohomology used by Abramsky--Mansfield--Barbosa with torsors
trivialized by the measurement cover \cite[Prop.~6.3]{Caru2017}. More recently,
Barbosa, Kharoof, and Okay construct fully faithful functors from measurement
scenarios to bundle and simplicial-bundle scenarios, together with natural
isomorphisms on their empirical-model functors
\cite{BarbosaKharoofOkay2024}. Neither construction produces a banded gerbe
or a banded stack lift in the sense of
\Cref{def:banded-stack-lift}. The following comparison locates the
gap precisely.

Let a \emph{finite measurement scenario} be a finite set \(X\), finite
nonempty outcome sets \(O_x\) for \(x\in X\), and a finite antichain
\(\mathcal M\) of subsets of \(X\) whose union is \(X\). Write
\(\mathcal D_{\mathcal M}\) for the finite context category consisting of
the subcontexts of members of \(\mathcal M\), with inclusions as arrows, and
equip it with the coverage induced by the maximal contexts. We also use its
terminal augmentation, obtained by adjoining \(X\) and declaring
\(\mathcal M\) to cover \(X\). A finite empirical model
\[
e=(e_C)_{C\in\mathcal M}
\]
is a compatible family of probability distributions on
\(\prod_{x\in C}O_x\). Its support presheaf is
\[
S_e(U)=\operatorname{supp}(e_U)
\qquad(U\in\mathcal D_{\mathcal M}),
\]
where compatibility makes the marginal \(e_U\) independent of the maximal
context containing \(U\) \cite{AbramskyBrandenburger2011}. At the adjoined
terminal object we take the right Kan extension
\[
\widehat S_e(X):=
\varprojlim_{U\in\mathcal D_{\mathcal M}}S_e(U).
\]
Thus an element of \(\widehat S_e(X)\) is, by definition, a compatible family
of supported local assignments. Below \(aS_e\) denotes the sheafification of
this terminally augmented presheaf.

A \emph{context refinement} used below is a refinement
\(\rho:\mathcal M'\to\mathcal M\) on the same measurements and outcomes,
with \(C'\subseteq\rho(C')\), for which marginalization defines the refined
model \(\rho^*e\). The inclusion of downward-closed context categories induces
a functor
\[
u_\rho:\mathcal D_{\mathcal M'}\hookrightarrow
\mathcal D_{\mathcal M}
\]
which is required to be continuous for the two generated cover topologies and
to extend to their terminal augmentations. We also require the natural
base-change isomorphism
\[
S_{\rho^*e}\cong u_\rho^*S_e.
\]
This includes ordinary iterated refinements which are covering refinements
for the generated topology. No
claim is made here for resource-theoretic simulations which change the
measurement or outcome sets.

\begin{proposition}[Abramsky--Brandenburger support interface]\label{prop:ab-compatibility-translation}
For every finite empirical model \(e\), the following sets are naturally in
bijection:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item compatible families \((s_C)_{C\in\mathcal M}\) with
      \(s_C\in S_e(C)\);
\item global assignments \(s\in\prod_{x\in X}O_x\) such that
      \(s|_C\in S_e(C)\) for every \(C\in\mathcal M\);
\item global sections of the support presheaf in the standard
      Abramsky--Brandenburger sense;
\item elements of \(\widehat S_e(X)\), equivalently elements of
      \((aS_e)(X)\) on the terminally augmented context site.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, if both \(\CompSec_s(r)\) and \(\Sec_s(r)\) are interpreted
from this same support presheaf on the standard measurement cover, then
\[
M,p\Vdash\CompSec_s(r)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M,p\Vdash\Sec_s(r).
\]
In particular, strong contextuality is absence of a compatible family at the
set-valued level; it is not the matching-without-amalgamation case
\(\CompSec_s(r)\land\neg\Sec_s(r)\) of this paper.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The event presheaf
\(\mathcal E(U)=\prod_{x\in U}O_x\) is a sheaf. Hence a compatible family
\((s_C)\) glues uniquely to a global assignment \(s\), and its restrictions
remain in the prescribed supports. Conversely, restriction of such an
assignment gives a compatible supported family. This proves the first three
identifications. The fourth is the defining limit formula for
\(\widehat S_e(X)\); the plus construction for the cover
\(\mathcal M\to X\) gives the same set in the sheafification. The displayed
equivalence follows immediately.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Support versus probabilistic contextuality]\label{rem:support-vs-probabilistic-contextuality}
The proposition concerns the possibilistic support of \(e\). Probabilistic
noncontextuality asks instead for a distribution on
\(\prod_{x\in X}O_x\) whose marginals are the given distributions \(e_C\).
That is a fibre-extension problem for the distribution presheaf, and is not
determined by \(S_e\): empirical models with the same support can have
different probabilistic noncontextuality behaviour.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Canonical split stack and the degree obstruction]\label{thm:empirical-model-stack-interface}
Let \(e\) be a finite empirical model on the context site above.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item There is a canonical banded stack lift with zero band,
\[
\mathfrak E_e^0:=\operatorname{Disc}(aS_e),
\qquad
\pi_0(\mathfrak E_e^0)\cong aS_e.
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Disc}\) regards a sheaf of sets as a stack of discrete
groupoids.
\item More generally, after an abelian sheaf \(\Band\) has been supplied,
there is a canonical split \(\Band\)-banded stack lift
\[
\mathfrak E_e^{\mathrm{spl}}:=
\operatorname{Disc}(aS_e)\times\mathsf B\Band.
\]
Every component gerbe of this lift is neutral, and therefore every
Giraud class supplied by
\Cref{thm:intrinsic-branch-obstruction-extension} is zero.
\item The assignments in \textup{(i)} and \textup{(ii)} are pseudonatural
under context refinements: there are coherent banded equivalences
\[
u_\rho^{-1}\mathfrak E_e^0\simeq
\mathfrak E_{\rho^*e}^0,
\qquad
u_\rho^{-1}\mathfrak E_e^{\mathrm{spl}}\simeq
\mathfrak E_{\rho^*e}^{\mathrm{spl}}
\]
in the second formula whenever the supplied bands are identified under
pullback.
\item The AB and AMB constructions do not produce the non-split degree-two
data required by this paper. More precisely, fix
\(C_0\in\mathcal M\) and \(s\in S_e(C_0)\).
Abramsky--Mansfield--Barbosa form the free-abelian presheaf
\(\mathcal F_e=\mathbb Z[S_e]\), its relative kernel
\(\mathcal F_{e,\overline C_0}\), and a class
\[
\gamma_e(s)\in
\check H^1(\mathcal M,\mathcal F_{e,\overline C_0}).
\]
This is relative \v{C}ech cohomology of a presheaf, and the class is the
isomorphism class of an
\(\mathcal F_{e,\overline C_0}\)-torsor trivialized by
\(\mathcal M\). It is not a derived \(H^2\) Giraud class for a sheaf band
\cite{AbramskyMansfieldSoaresBarbosa2012,Caru2017}.
\item There is no comparison which recovers these AMB classes, for all finite
empirical models, from the component-section obstruction map of any banded stack lift
whose component sheaf is \(aS_e\). Indeed, if \(e\) is strongly contextual,
then
\[
(aS_e)(X)=\varnothing,
\]
so the component-section obstruction map
\[
\delta_{\mathfrak E_e}:(aS_e)(X)\longrightarrow
H^2(\mathcal D_{\mathcal M},\Band)
\]
has empty domain, whereas AMB classes are defined for the local sections
\(s\in S_e(C_0)\) and can be nonzero, as in their GHZ and
Kochen--Specker examples.
\end{enumerate}
In \textup{(i)}, \emph{canonical} means that the construction is the
composite of the universal sheafification functor and the discrete-stack
functor, with no choices, and that the displayed refinement equivalences
satisfy the usual pseudofunctor coherence. It does not mean that all
banded stack lifts of \(S_e\) are equivalent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The discrete groupoid construction preserves descent, so
\(\operatorname{Disc}(aS_e)\) is a stack and its connected-component sheaf is
exactly \(aS_e\). Its automorphism sheaves are zero, proving \textup{(i)}.
The classifying stack \(\mathsf B\Band\) has one local component and
automorphism sheaf \(\Band\). Taking its product with the discrete stack
therefore proves the band and component assertions in \textup{(ii)}. The
global object represented by the identity \(\Band\)-torsor neutralizes each
component gerbe, so its Giraud class vanishes.

Inverse image for the morphism of context sites commutes, up to its canonical
isomorphism, with associated sheaves, discrete stacks, products, and
classifying stacks. Together with
\(S_{\rho^*e}\cong u_\rho^*S_e\), this gives the equivalences and their
coherence in \textup{(iii)}.

The construction of \(\gamma_e(s)\) and its degree are those of
\cite[Sec.~4]{AbramskyMansfieldSoaresBarbosa2012}. The torsor
classification is \cite[Prop.~6.3]{Caru2017}, proving \textup{(iv)}. Finally,
\Cref{prop:ab-compatibility-translation} identifies strong contextuality with
emptiness of \((aS_e)(X)\). The component-section obstruction map of
\Cref{thm:intrinsic-branch-obstruction-extension} is indexed by precisely
that set, while \(\gamma_e(s)\) is indexed by a local section. This proves
the asserted failure in \textup{(v)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact limit of transfer to empirical models]\label{thm:unique-branch-contextuality-comparison}
For finite Abramsky--Brandenburger empirical models, the set-valued
local-to-global statements of \Cref{prop:ab-compatibility-translation}
transfer functorially. The later banded and homological theorems transfer
only after a band and a non-split banded stack lift have been
supplied as additional data. In particular:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item the canonical split lift gives only zero Giraud classes and zero
homological image subgroups;
\item the characterization
\[
d(G)\le 2\beta
\quad\text{and}\quad
G\text{ is not a cyclic }p\text{-group}
\]
remains a construction theorem for the chosen wedge-of-spheres good-cover, band, and
two-component cocycle data of
\Cref{thm:wedge-sphere-two-component-label-loss}; it is not a characterization of
groups canonically attached to empirical models;
\item without an additional degree-shifting construction, the Giraud
\(H^2\)-class neither recovers nor refines the AMB relative
\(\check H^1\)-obstruction.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first and third assertions are
\Cref{thm:empirical-model-stack-interface}(ii),(iv)--(v). The wedge-of-spheres theorem
starts from a prescribed finite nerve, a constant finite abelian band, and
chosen componentwise homomorphisms
\(H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\to A\). None of those data is selected by a bare empirical
model, so its group classification cannot be transported through the split
construction. This proves the second assertion.
\end{proof}
