\section{Finite good-cover sites and prescribed realization}
\label{sec:homological-visibility}

Let \(\mathcal U=\{U_i\}_{i\in I}\) be a finite open cover of a space
\(X\).  Its ordinary nerve \(N=N(\mathcal U)\) records which finite
intersections are nonempty.  In order to identify Cech cochains with one
copy of a coefficient group for each simplex of \(N\), we impose the
following hypothesis throughout this section:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:connected-overlap-hypothesis}
 \begin{minipage}{0.82\textwidth}
 every nonempty finite intersection \(U_J=\bigcap_{j\in J}U_j\) is
 connected, and the chosen trivializations of the locally constant
 coefficient sheaf identify \(\Gamma(U_J,\mathscr A)\) coherently with
 the same fixed group \(A\), with every restriction map identified with
 \(\operatorname{id}_A\).
 \end{minipage}
\end{equation}
Without connectedness,
\(\Gamma(U_J,\mathscr A)\cong A^{\pi_0(U_J)}\), whereas the ordinary nerve
retains only the information that \(U_J\) is nonempty.  Thus
\eqref{eq:connected-overlap-hypothesis} is essential for the ordinary-nerve
formulation below.  A component nerve or local coefficients would be needed
without it.

For a fixed cover satisfying \eqref{eq:connected-overlap-hypothesis}, define
the \emph{finite intersection site} \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\) as follows.
Its objects are a terminal object \(a\), one object \(U_J\) for every
nonempty simplex \(J\) of \(N\), and a strict initial object \(\varnothing\)
representing all empty intersections.  There is an arrow
\(U_J\to U_K\) when \(K\subseteq J\), and
\[
 U_J\times_{U_L}U_K=
 \begin{cases}
 U_{J\cup K},&J\cup K\in N,\\
 \varnothing,&J\cup K\notin N.
 \end{cases}
\]
The pretopology is generated by the vertex cover \(\{U_i\to a\}\), its
pullbacks, identity covers, and the empty cover of \(\varnothing\).  Let
\(A_{\mathcal U}\) be the restriction of the sheaf of locally constant
\(A\)-valued functions on \(X\).  Hence
\[
 A_{\mathcal U}(U_J)=A,
 \qquad A_{\mathcal U}(\varnothing)=0,
\]
under the coherent trivializations in
\eqref{eq:connected-overlap-hypothesis}.

For \(J\subseteq I\), put \(\widetilde U_J=U_J\) when \(J\in N\) and
\(\widetilde U_J=\varnothing\) otherwise.  Then
\(U_J\times_{U_L}U_K=\widetilde U_{J\cup K}\), so disjoint cover members
still have a fibre product.  Pulling the vertex cover back to a nonempty
proper intersection \(U_J\) produces a family containing the identity of
\(U_J\).  Evaluation at the terminal slice object is therefore exact, and
\[
 H^q(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/U_J,A_{\mathcal U})=0
 \qquad(q>0).
\]
This is the required Leray acyclicity on the formal intersection site; for a
topological good cover it follows from contractibility of the nonempty
intersections.

Under the fixed identifications in
\eqref{eq:connected-overlap-hypothesis}, an ordered Cech cochain
\(c\in\check C^q(\mathcal U,A_{\mathcal U})\) is the family
\[
 c_{i_0\cdots i_q}\in A
 \qquad
 (i_0<\cdots<i_q,\ U_{i_0\cdots i_q}\ne\varnothing).
\]
Its differential is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cech-simplicial-differential}
 (\delta c)_{i_0\cdots i_{q+1}}
 =\sum_{r=0}^{q+1}(-1)^r
 c_{i_0\cdots\widehat{i_r}\cdots i_{q+1}},
\end{equation}
which is exactly the simplicial coboundary of \(N\); empty intersections
contribute zero.

\begin{proposition}[Connected-overlap and basis-site comparison]
\label{prop:finite-site-comparison}
Let \(A\) be an abelian group.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item For a finite cover satisfying
      \eqref{eq:connected-overlap-hypothesis}, the ordered Cech complex of
      \(A_{\mathcal U}\) is naturally the simplicial cochain complex of the
      ordinary nerve:
      \[
      \check C^q(\mathcal U,A_{\mathcal U})
      \cong C^q(N,A).
      \]
\item If the nonempty intersections are also
      \(A_{\mathcal U}\)-acyclic, the Leray comparison gives
      \[
      H^q(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U},A_{\mathcal U})
      \cong \check H^q(\mathcal U,A_{\mathcal U})
      \cong H^q(N,A).
      \]
\item Let \(K\) be a finite simplicial complex, put \(X=|K|\), and let
      \(\mathcal U^{(m)}\) be the open-star cover of
      \(\operatorname{Sd}^mK\).  The category \(\mathcal B_K\) of all open
      stars in these covers and all their finite intersections is a basis
      for \(X\), stable under finite intersections after adjoining the empty
      set.  The topology generated on \(\mathcal B_K\) is the induced
      open-cover topology, and restriction gives an equivalence
      \[
      \mathbf {Sh}(X)\simeq\mathbf {Sh}(\mathcal B_K).
      \]
      Under this equivalence the genuine constant sheaf on
      \(\mathcal B_K\) is the restriction of the ordinary constant sheaf on
      \(X\).  Every nonempty basic intersection is contractible, the covers
      \((\mathcal U^{(m)})_{m\ge0}\) are filtered and cofinal, and
      \[
      \varinjlim_m\check H^2(\mathcal U^{(m)},A)
      \cong H^2(X,A).
      \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For (i), the coherent trivializations identify the section group on each
nonempty overlap with one copy of \(A\) and every restriction with the
identity; empty intersections contribute zero.  The ordered Cech differential
is therefore the displayed simplicial differential.  For (ii), acyclicity
collapses the Cech-to-derived spectral sequence to this row, giving the usual
Leray comparison \cite[Chap.~III, Sec.~4]{Bredon1997}.  On the formal
intersection site, the slice vanishing above gives the same collapse.

For (iii), open stars in successive barycentric subdivisions have arbitrarily
small mesh.  Given \(x\in O\) with \(O\subseteq X\) open, a sufficiently
fine subdivision has a vertex whose open star contains \(x\) and lies in
\(O\).  Hence the union of all these stars is a basis.  Adding their finite
intersections makes it stable under intersections, and a family of basis
objects covers precisely when their union covers in \(X\).  Thus its
generated topology is the topology induced from the open-set site.

To make the small-mesh assertion explicit, fix a metric on the compact
polyhedron \(X\).  Barycentric subdivision reduces the mesh of each simplex
by a uniform factor strictly less than one.  Choose
\(\varepsilon>0\) with \(B(x,\varepsilon)\subseteq O\).  At a sufficiently
late subdivision, every simplex meeting \(x\) has diameter less than
\(\varepsilon/2\).  A vertex belonging to a smallest simplex whose closure
contains \(x\) then has open star contained in \(O\).  Applying this
argument to every member of a finite open cover and using compactness shows
that a sufficiently late star cover refines that cover.

The Comparison Lemma for a basis stable under intersections now gives the
displayed equivalence of sheaf categories; explicitly, restriction is fully
faithful because sections are recovered by gluing over basis covers, and its
quasi-inverse sends a basis sheaf to the sheaf whose sections on an open set
are compatible basis-local sections.  This also shows directly that locally
constant \(A\)-valued functions restrict to the genuine constant sheaf used
on \(\mathcal B_K\) \cite[Chap.~III, Sec.~7]{MacLaneMoerdijk1994}.

A nonempty intersection of open stars is the open star of the simplex spanned
by the corresponding vertices, hence is contractible.  Barycentric
subdivision refines the preceding cover, and every finite basis cover is
refined by a sufficiently late subdivision cover; the sequence is therefore
filtered and cofinal.  The basic intersections are acyclic for the constant
sheaf, so (ii), the sheaf-category equivalence, and cofinality give
\[
 \varinjlim_m\check H^2(\mathcal U^{(m)},A)
 \cong H^2(\mathcal B_K,A)
 \cong H^2(X,A).
\]
This proves every asserted comparison, including the passage from the
all-subdivision generated site to the usual topological site.
\end{proof}

We now package supplied Cech-cocycle gerbes into a single fixed-cover,
fixed-band prestack.  The construction simultaneously controls the
component presheaf, terminal essential surjectivity, neutral and non-neutral
labels, and the images of the supplied evaluation homomorphisms.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-site realization of prescribed componentwise evaluation maps]
\label{thm:prescribed-component-image-construction}
Let \(\mathcal U=\{U_i\}_{i\in I}\) be a finite good cover satisfying
\eqref{eq:connected-overlap-hypothesis}, with connected nerve \(N\), and let
\(A\) be a finite abelian group.  Assume
\[
 H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0.
\]
Let \(\Lambda\) be a finite nonempty set and prescribe homomorphisms
\[
 \phi_\lambda:H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow A
 \qquad(\lambda\in\Lambda).
\]
Put \(\Lambda_0=\{\lambda:\phi_\lambda=0\}\).  Then there are a presheaf
\(F\) on \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\) and an
\(A_{\mathcal U}\)-banded prestack lift
\(\mathfrak P\), with stackification \(\mathfrak L\), such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item \(F(a)=\Lambda_0\), \((aF)(a)\cong\Lambda\), and
      \(\pi_0(\mathfrak L)(a)\cong\Lambda\);
\item the component gerbe \(\mathfrak G_\lambda\) has class
      \(\omega_\lambda\in H^2(N,A)\) satisfying
      \[
      \operatorname{ev}_{\omega_\lambda}=\phi_\lambda,
      \qquad
      \operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{ev}_{\omega_\lambda})
      =\operatorname{Im}(\phi_\lambda),
      \]
      and \(\mathfrak G_\lambda\) is neutral exactly when
      \(\lambda\in\Lambda_0\);
\item \(\mathfrak P(a)\to\mathfrak L(a)\) is essentially surjective, and
      its terminal objects occur exactly in the components indexed by
      \(\Lambda_0\);
\item \(F\) has a strict matching family on \(\mathcal U\), and
      \[
      \operatorname{MWA}(F,a)
      \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
      \Lambda_0=\varnothing.
      \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The universal coefficient sequence and \(H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0\) give a natural
isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uct-realization-isomorphism}
 \eta_A:H^2(N,A)\xrightarrow{\sim}
 \operatorname{Hom}(H_2(N,\mathbb Z),A).
\end{equation}
Degree-one UCT also gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degree-one-uct-terminal-vanishing}
 H^1(N;A)
 \cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_1(N,\mathbb Z),A)=0.
\end{equation}
For this fixed cover and the coherent fixed-\(A\) trivializations, the
comparison of \Cref{prop:finite-site-comparison} identifies this with
\(H^1(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U},A_{\mathcal U})=0\).  Thus the homological
hypothesis has two distinct roles: it removes the degree-two Ext ambiguity
in \eqref{eq:uct-realization-isomorphism}, and it trivializes the
degree-one Isom torsors in the neutral terminal fibres.
For every \(\lambda\), let \(\omega_\lambda\) be the inverse image of
\(\phi_\lambda\).  Choose a normalized simplicial cocycle
\(\alpha_\lambda\in Z^2(N,A)\) representing it, choosing
\(\alpha_\lambda=0\) when \(\lambda\in\Lambda_0\).  Extend it alternately
to ordered triples and set it to zero on triples with a repeated index.

The choice \(\alpha_\lambda=0\) for a zero map is legitimate because
\(\eta_A\) is injective: \(\phi_\lambda=0\) implies
\(\omega_\lambda=0\).  Conversely, a nonzero prescribed map has a nonzero
class.  For pairwise distinct indices, alternating extension means
\[
 \alpha_{\lambda,\sigma(i)\sigma(j)\sigma(k)}
 =\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\alpha_{\lambda,ijk}.
\]
On every ordered \(3\)-simplex, the cocycle equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:realization-cocycle-equation}
 \alpha_{\lambda,jkl}-\alpha_{\lambda,ikl}
 +\alpha_{\lambda,ijl}-\alpha_{\lambda,ijk}=0.
\end{equation}
These are the only coefficient identities used in the groupoid
construction.

Define the component presheaf by
\[
 F(W)=
 \begin{cases}
 \Lambda,&W\ne a,\varnothing\text{ is a nonempty intersection object},\\
 \Lambda_0,&W=a,\\
 \{*\},&W=\varnothing.
 \end{cases}
\]
Restrictions among proper nonempty intersections are identities,
restrictions from \(a\) are the inclusion
\(\Lambda_0\hookrightarrow\Lambda\), and restrictions to
\(\varnothing\) are unique.  A matching family on the vertex cover has a
single label on every connected component of the nerve.  Since \(N\) is
connected, its label is a unique element of \(\Lambda\).  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label-sheafification}
 (aF)(a)\cong\Lambda.
\end{equation}

Here is the sheafification calculation in detail.  A family
\((\lambda_i)_{i\in I}\) with \(\lambda_i\in F(U_i)=\Lambda\) is matching
exactly when
\[
 \lambda_i=\lambda_j
 \quad\text{for every edge }\{i,j\}\in N.
\]
No condition is imposed by an empty overlap because both restrictions land
in the singleton \(F(\varnothing)\).  Edgewise equality propagates along
paths in the connected \(1\)-skeleton of \(N\), so every matching family is
constant.  Conversely, each constant family is matching.  It determines a
section of \(aF\) over \(a\), and distinct constants remain distinct after
restriction because every nonempty cover member retains its label.

Every cover in the generated pretopology is obtained from the vertex cover
by pullback and transitivity.  On a nonempty proper intersection, a generated
cover contains an identity, so it neither creates nor identifies labels.
It follows that the vertex-cover calculation computes all terminal sections
of the sheafification.  On proper nonempty objects,
\((aF)(W)=\Lambda\), and on the initial object it is a singleton.

We next construct the prestack and verify its structure rather than citing a
separate realization result.  Fix \(\lambda\notin\Lambda_0\).  Put
\(\mathfrak P_\lambda(a)=\varnothing\).  For a nonempty proper intersection
\(W\), let
\[
 I(W)=\{i\in I:W\subseteq U_i\}.
\]
The groupoid \(\mathfrak P_\lambda(W)\) has objects
\(x_i^{\lambda,W}\) for \(i\in I(W)\), and
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(x_i^{\lambda,W},x_j^{\lambda,W})
 =A_{\mathcal U}(W)\cong A.
\]
Writing a morphism as \((b,i,j)\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twisted-composition}
 (c,j,k)\circ(b,i,j)
 =(b+c+\alpha_{\lambda,ijk},i,k).
\end{equation}
Normalization supplies identities and inverses.  Explicitly,
\[
 1_{x_i^{\lambda,W}}=(0,i,i),
 \qquad
 (b,i,j)^{-1}=(-b,j,i),
\]
because every cocycle coordinate with a repeated index is zero.  For three
composable arrows, the two parenthesizations are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (d,k,l)\circ\bigl((c,j,k)\circ(b,i,j)\bigr)
 &=(b+c+d+\alpha_{\lambda,ijk}
       +\alpha_{\lambda,ikl},i,l),\\
 \bigl((d,k,l)\circ(c,j,k)\bigr)\circ(b,i,j)
 &=(b+c+d+\alpha_{\lambda,jkl}
       +\alpha_{\lambda,ijl},i,l).
\end{aligned}
\]
They agree by \eqref{eq:realization-cocycle-equation}.  Associativity is
therefore exactly the cocycle identity
\[
 \alpha_{\lambda,ijk}+\alpha_{\lambda,ikl}
 =\alpha_{\lambda,jkl}+\alpha_{\lambda,ijl}.
\]
If \(V\subseteq W\), then \(I(W)\subseteq I(V)\); restriction keeps the
object index and applies the sheaf restriction to the coefficient.  These
functors compose strictly.  Over \(\varnothing\) take the terminal groupoid.

To verify the restriction assertion, write
\(\rho_{VW}:A_{\mathcal U}(W)\to A_{\mathcal U}(V)\).  The functor sends
\[
 x_i^{\lambda,W}\longmapsto x_i^{\lambda,V},
 \qquad
 (b,i,j)\longmapsto(\rho_{VW}(b),i,j).
\]
On nonempty connected intersections, the coherent trivializations identify
\(\rho_{VW}\) with the identity of \(A\).  Hence
\[
 \rho_{VW}(b+c+\alpha_{\lambda,ijk})
 =\rho_{VW}(b)+\rho_{VW}(c)+\alpha_{\lambda,ijk},
\]
so restriction preserves \eqref{eq:twisted-composition}.  If
\(Z\subseteq V\subseteq W\), the equality
\(\rho_{ZV}\rho_{VW}=\rho_{ZW}\) gives literal equality of the restriction
functors.  This is strict functoriality, not functoriality only up to a
cartesian comparison arrow.

For fixed \(i,j\in I(W)\), the presheaf of arrows between the corresponding
objects on the slice over \(W\) is
\[
 V\longmapsto A_{\mathcal U}(V).
\]
It is the restriction of the genuine coefficient sheaf, and hence is a
sheaf.  This verifies morphism descent for every twisted summand.
Translation in the coefficient coordinate identifies
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}(x_i^{\lambda,W})
 \cong A_{\mathcal U}|_{\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W}.
\]
Changing \(i\) transports this identification through an arrow
\((b,i,j)\); abelianness makes the induced conjugation on \(A\) trivial.
The identifications therefore constitute an \(A_{\mathcal U}\)-banding.

For \(\lambda\in\Lambda_0\), let \(\mathfrak P_\lambda\) be the neutral
one-object \(A_{\mathcal U}\)-banded stack on every nonempty object and the
terminal groupoid on \(\varnothing\).  This is a stack here, not merely a
one-object prestack: on the slice over a nonempty formal intersection, every
generated cover contains an identity, so the nonempty part of the topology
is trivial; the empty cover forces value zero at \(\varnothing\).  Evaluation
at the terminal slice object is exact, whence
\(H^1(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W,A_{\mathcal U})=0\), and every object
descent torsor is trivial.

Concretely, descent data for the distinguished local object on a cover
\(\{V_r\to W\}\) are arrows represented by coefficients
\(d_{rs}\in A_{\mathcal U}(V_{rs})\) satisfying
\[
 d_{rs}+d_{st}=d_{rt}.
\]
They form an \(A_{\mathcal U}\)-torsor cocycle.  The vanishing just noted
provides coefficients \(e_r\in A_{\mathcal U}(V_r)\) with
\(d_{rs}=e_s-e_r\).  Adjusting the local identifications by \(e_r\) makes
them agree and yields the global object.  Morphism descent is the ordinary
sheaf condition for \(A_{\mathcal U}\).  Thus the neutral summand is already
a stack on every relevant slice.

Set
\[
 \mathfrak P=\coprod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak P_\lambda
\]
fibrewise on nonempty objects, with a shared terminal fibre over
\(\varnothing\).  For two objects in a twisted fibre, the isomorphism
presheaf on the relevant slice is the restriction of the genuine sheaf
\(A_{\mathcal U}\); it is therefore a sheaf.  Automorphism sheaves are the
same sheaf, so \(\mathfrak P\) is an \(A_{\mathcal U}\)-banded prestack.
Every nonempty proper fibre has connected components indexed by \(\Lambda\),
the terminal fibre has components \(\Lambda_0\), and the empty fibre has one
component.  Thus
\[
 \pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P)\cong F.
\]
Stackification and \eqref{eq:label-sheafification} prove (i).

There are two points in this coproduct verification.  First, over a nonempty
object there are no arrows between different \(\lambda\)-summands, so
isomorphism descent cannot change a label.  A matching object descent datum
therefore lies in one summand on each connected part of a cover, and the
connected nerve forces a single label.  Second, all summands restrict to the
same terminal groupoid over \(\varnothing\).  This causes no identification
on a nonempty overlap: the initial object has no arrow back to a nonempty
object and its unique descent datum contributes no coefficient.

For a twisted summand, every two named objects are isomorphic, since their
Hom set is the nonempty set \(A\).  Thus each such fibre is connected.
The neutral summand has one object and is connected as well.  It follows
objectwise that the component presheaf is exactly \(F\), including its
restriction \(\Lambda_0\hookrightarrow\Lambda\) out of the terminal object.
The standard compatibility of stackification with components gives
\[
 \pi_0(\mathfrak L)\cong aF,
\]
and the terminal component sections are the labels in \(\Lambda\).

On \(U_i\), choose \(x_i^{\lambda,U_i}\); on \(U_{ij}\), choose the arrow
\((0,i,j)\).  By \eqref{eq:twisted-composition}, its triple-overlap defect is
\[
 (0,i,k)^{-1}(0,j,k)(0,i,j)
 =\alpha_{\lambda,ijk}
\]
under the band identification.  The Cech representative of the component
gerbe is therefore exactly \(\alpha_\lambda\).  For the fixed cover and
coherently trivialized fixed band, the comparison in
\Cref{prop:finite-site-comparison} identifies its Giraud class with
\(\omega_\lambda\), and
\eqref{eq:uct-realization-isomorphism} gives
\(\operatorname{ev}_{\omega_\lambda}=\phi_\lambda\).  By the standard
component-gerbe package, this gerbe is neutral exactly when its class is zero,
equivalently when \(\phi_\lambda=0\).  This proves (ii).

To check the sign in the defect, use
\[
 (0,i,k)^{-1}=(0,k,i)
\]
and apply \eqref{eq:twisted-composition} twice.  Normalization cancels the
terms with repeated indices, while alternating extension moves the remaining
term to the ordered coordinate \(\alpha_{\lambda,ijk}\).  This is the same
convention as \eqref{eq:giraud-triple-defect}.  Therefore the class obtained
by stackification is not merely cohomologous to a class with the desired
evaluation: its chosen vertex-cover representative is the initially
selected cocycle \(\alpha_\lambda\).

For \(\lambda\in\Lambda_0\), the neutral summand is already a stack and its
distinguished terminal object maps to an object of the corresponding
component of \(\mathfrak L\).  If \(\lambda\notin\Lambda_0\), the component
gerbe is non-neutral and therefore has no object over \(a\).  Hence every object of
\(\mathfrak L(a)\) lies in a zero component and comes from
\(\mathfrak P(a)\), proving (iii), including the vacuous case
\(\Lambda_0=\varnothing\).

When \(\Lambda_0\ne\varnothing\), an object of
\(\mathfrak L(a)\) has some component label
\(\lambda\in\Lambda\).  The component-gerbe package identifies the full
substack at that label with \(\mathfrak G_\lambda\).  If
\(\lambda\notin\Lambda_0\), non-neutrality says that this full substack has
no terminal object, so the assumed object cannot have that label.  If
\(\lambda\in\Lambda_0\), the distinguished terminal object of the neutral
summand of \(\mathfrak P\) maps to an object in the same component of
\(\mathfrak L\).  The Isom sheaf between its image and any other terminal
object in that component is an \(A_{\mathcal U}\)-torsor.  Its class lies in
\(H^1(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U},A_{\mathcal U})\), which vanishes by
\eqref{eq:degree-one-uct-terminal-vanishing}.  The torsor therefore has a
global section, and the two terminal objects are globally isomorphic.  This
is precisely essential surjectivity, rather than equality of object sets.

Finally, assigning one fixed \(\lambda\in\Lambda\) to every \(U_i\) is a
strict matching family: restrictions on nonempty overlaps are identities and
those on empty overlaps are unique.  It has an amalgamation precisely when
\(F(a)=\Lambda_0\) is nonempty.  This proves (iv) and completes the
construction.

\end{proof}

\subsection*{A mixed-label example}

Take a triangulated \(2\)-sphere and an open-star cover of a barycentric
subdivision.  Its nerve \(N\) satisfies
\[
 H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0,\qquad H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
Let \(A=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\), and take the label set
\[
 \Lambda=\{\mathtt n,\mathtt p,\mathtt q\}.
\]
Prescribe
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \phi_{\mathtt n}(1)&=0,\\
 \phi_{\mathtt p}(1)&=2,\\
 \phi_{\mathtt q}(1)&=3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then
\[
 \Lambda_0=\{\mathtt n\},\qquad
 K_{\mathtt n}=0,\qquad
 K_{\mathtt p}=\langle2\rangle,\qquad
 K_{\mathtt q}=\langle3\rangle.
\]
The component presheaf has \(F(a)=\{\mathtt n\}\), while its sheafification
has all three terminal component sections; only the \(\mathtt n\)-component
has a terminal stack object.  For the two nonzero labels,
\[
 Q_{\mathtt p}=A/\langle2\rangle\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
 \qquad
 Q_{\mathtt q}=A/\langle3\rangle\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
\]
and their images are complementary:
\[
 \langle2\rangle\cap\langle3\rangle=0,
 \qquad
 \langle2\rangle+\langle3\rangle=A.
\]
The zero image from \(\mathtt n\) leaves the aggregate intersection zero,
while the sum remains \(A\).  Hence
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{lab}}=A,\qquad Q_{\mathrm{com}}=0.
\]

The example has no matching-without-amalgamation predicate because
\(F(a)\ne\varnothing\), although the matching families labelled
\(\mathtt p\) and \(\mathtt q\) have no amalgamation.
