\begin{abstract}
On the chosen good-cover site of a wedge of $\beta$ $2$-spheres, a nonzero finite abelian group $G$ occurs as a two-component label-loss kernel with complementary nonzero image subgroups exactly when $d(G)\leq 2\beta$ and $G$ is not a cyclic $p$-group.  This follows from the universal coefficient theorem after fixing a finite nerve, a constant finite abelian band, and a banded groupoid lift of a presheaf.  For a component-gerbe class $\omega$, evaluation on $H_2$ gives $\operatorname{ev}_\omega:H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\to A$; its image and cokernel determine the componentwise and aggregate quotients.  When $H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0$ and $H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^\beta$, the possible image subgroups are exactly those generated by at most $\beta$ elements.  Under explicit separatedness, representability, and \v{C}ech-to-derived comparison hypotheses, matching families without global amalgamations correspond to non-neutral component gerbes.  This derived $H^2$ invariant is not the relative \v{C}ech $H^1$ obstruction of Abramsky--Mansfield--Barbosa; only the underlying support-presheaf comparison is functorial, and the finite-group characterization does not transfer to bare empirical models.
\end{abstract}
