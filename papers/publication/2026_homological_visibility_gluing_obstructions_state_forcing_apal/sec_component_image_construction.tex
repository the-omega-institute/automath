\subsection{Construction of prescribed component data}

The preceding algebra isolates the componentwise and aggregate quotients attached to a given family of homological image subgroups. The next theorem gives a converse existence statement on connected finite nerves with vanishing first homology. It is internal to the fixed finite-nerve framework of the paper: the nerve, the constant band, and the comparison data are fixed in advance, and the theorem constructs a presheaf and banded groupoid lift with the prescribed images.

The construction proofs use the following explicit typed-expansion
interface. The site is not an arbitrary full subcategory of intersections.
It is the finite intersection site associated to the cover nerve of a finite good-cover presentation, so that
the cohomological comparison used later is an actual Leray comparison rather
than a declared comparison datum.

\begin{lemma}[Finite intersection site associated to the cover nerve, Leray comparison, and one-state admissible expansion]\label{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}
Let
\[
\mathcal U=\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I}
\]
be a finite cover whose nerve $N=N(\mathcal U)$ is the nerve of a good cover
of a paracompact space $X\simeq |N|$. Let $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$ be the
finite intersection site associated to this chosen good-cover model, defined
as follows. Its objects are $a$, one strict initial object $\emptyset$, and
one object $U_J$ for each nonempty simplex $J\in N$. A geometric-realization functor
from this formal category to open subsets of \(X\) sends \(a\) to \(X\),
\(\emptyset\) to the empty open set, and \(U_J\) to the corresponding
nonempty good-cover intersection. This is a pullback-complete finite intersection site,
not the full subcategory on only nonempty intersections. The same strict
initial object represents every empty finite intersection. Thus the only
finite subsets represented by named non-initial objects are the simplices
of \(N\), but a finite subset \(J\subseteq I\) which is not a simplex is
not discarded from the site-level pullback calculus; whenever such a
subset arises as a pullback intersection, the pullback is the
distinguished object \(\emptyset\). In formulas
we write
\[
\widetilde U_J:=
\begin{cases}
U_J,& J\in N,\\
\emptyset,& J\notin N.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus empty intersections are not omitted from the site: all of them are
identified with the same strict initial pullback object $\emptyset$, and
the open set corresponding to \(\widetilde U_J\) is the actual intersection
\(\bigcap_{j\in J}U_j\), possibly empty. The order is generated by
\[
\emptyset\to U_J\to U_K\to a
\qquad (K\subseteq J,\; J,K\in N).
\]
There is a unique arrow \(\emptyset\to W\) for every object \(W\), and no
non-initial object maps to \(\emptyset\).
For any two arrows $U_J\to U_L$ and $U_K\to U_L$ with
$L\subseteq J\cap K$, their fibre product is
\[
U_J\times_{U_L}U_K=\widetilde U_{J\cup K}.
\]
If $J\cup K\notin N$, this means that the fibre product is the strict
initial object $\emptyset$. Any fibre product in which one factor is
$\emptyset$ is also $\emptyset$. The pretopology is the smallest
pretopology for which the vertex cover \(\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I}\), all of
its pullbacks along arrows of the formal category, all identity covers,
and the empty cover of \(\emptyset\) are covers. Equivalently, it is
obtained by repeatedly applying pullback stability and transitivity to
the vertex cover inside the formal category just described. For example,
the pullback of the vertex cover along \(U_J\to a\) is the family of
arrows
\[
\{\widetilde U_{J\cup\{i\}}\to U_J\}_{i\in I},
\]
with the terms for non-simplices equal to \(\emptyset\). Repeated
occurrences of \(\emptyset\) in such a pulled-back family may be
identified with the same initial object. They are harmless in matching
conditions because the coefficient sheaves used below have zero value at
\(\emptyset\), but the corresponding pullback object is still present in
the site. In particular, disjoint cover members \(U_i,U_j\to a\) have the
pullback \(U_i\times_aU_j=\emptyset\) in the site. The empty object is
omitted only from the nonempty \v{C}ech nerve and from the displayed
nonzero cochain groups, never from the local-reference-site structure.
Every pullback demanded by \Cref{def:local-reference-frame} is therefore
an object of \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\), including the empty pullback
objects produced by disjoint members of a covering family.
Thus closure under the pretopology axioms is part of the definition, not a
consequence of omitting any empty pullback object. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$ is a pullback-based reference-refinement site
with terminal object $a$; in particular, for every pair of cover members
$U_i,U_j\to a$, the fibre product is the formal object $U_{\{i,j\}}$ if
$\{i,j\}\in N$ and is the strict initial object $\emptyset$ otherwise;
\item the cover $\mathcal U$ remains a cover of $a$ in
$\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, all pullbacks of members of this cover exist
there, and its nonempty \v{C}ech nerve is the finite simplicial complex
$N$;
\item for every abelian group $A$, let $A_{\mathcal U}$ denote the genuine
constant sheaf
on \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\)
\[
A_{\mathcal U}(W)=\operatorname{LC}(|W|,A),
\]
where \(|W|\subseteq X\) is the open set represented by \(W\). This is the
restriction of the genuine constant sheaf of locally constant
\(A\)-valued functions on the chosen good-cover space. It is this
sheaf, not the identity-restriction constant presheaf on the abstract
index category, that is used as the band. With these coefficients the
\v{C}ech cochain complex of
$\mathcal U$ with coefficients in $A_{\mathcal U}$ and the finite nerve
presentation of any class in $H^2(N(\mathcal U),A)$ are the same finite
cochain data after replacing $\mathcal C_a$ with
$\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$. This is a cochain-level identification because
the cover is a chosen good cover: on connected nonempty basic
intersections \(A_{\mathcal U}\) has value \(A\), while on the strict
initial object it has value \(0\);
\item on the slice
\(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/U_J\) over every nonempty proper intersection
object, every cover of a nonempty object contains an identity, while the
empty cover forces every abelian sheaf to take value \(0\) at the strict
initial object. Consequently the abelian sheaf category on the slice is
equivalent to the presheaf category on its full nonempty subcategory.
Evaluation at the terminal object is therefore exact and
\[
H^q(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/U_J,A_{\mathcal U})=0
\qquad(q>0).
\]
Thus the vertex cover is \(A_{\mathcal U}\)-Leray for an entirely internal
finite-site reason, and the Cech-to-derived comparison gives a natural
isomorphism
\[
H^n(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U},A_{\mathcal U})\cong \check H^n(\mathcal U,A_{\mathcal U})\cong H^n(N,A)
\]
for all $n$, and the \v{C}ech cocycle on the vertex cover maps to the
corresponding derived cohomology class;
\item for every contravariant functor
\[
F:\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}^{op}\to\mathsf{Set},
\]
there is a one-state $\mathbb L_2$-model $M_2$ with state $p$ and a
distinguished admissible parameter $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ such that $[r]_p=a$, the
local slice site of $r$ is exactly $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, and the local
object functor on that slice site is $F$; this model is the admissible
$\mathbb L_2$-expansion supplied by
\artresult{Typed-expansion model-class interface for the layer chain};
\item if, in addition, a category fibred in groupoids
\[
\mathfrak P\to\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}
\]
with abelian sheaf band $\Band$ satisfies
\[
\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P)\cong F
\]
and the automorphism-sheaf condition of
\Cref{def:banded-groupoid-lift}, then $M_2$ has an admissible
$\mathbb L_3$-expansion $M_3$ in which $r$ has banded groupoid lift
$\mathfrak P$, band $\Band$, and stackification $a(\mathfrak P)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The formal index category is thin and has terminal object $a$. We first
verify the pullbacks, since they are part of the local-reference-site
structure and not merely a convention about the \v{C}ech cochain complex.
Suppose that \(U_J\to U_L\) and \(U_K\to U_L\) are two maps, so
\(L\subseteq J\cap K\). If \(J\cup K\in N\), then \(U_{J\cup K}\) maps to
both \(U_J\) and \(U_K\), and any object mapping to both over \(U_L\) is
indexed by a simplex containing \(J\cup K\), hence factors uniquely through
\(U_{J\cup K}\). If \(J\cup K\notin N\), no nonempty simplex can contain the
union, so the only object mapping to both over \(U_L\) is the strict initial
object \(\emptyset\). Pullbacks involving \(\emptyset\) are \(\emptyset\) by
strict initiality. Therefore every fibre product required by
\Cref{def:local-reference-frame} is an object of \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\).
In particular, for every pair in the covering family \(\{U_i\to a\}\) the
pullback is \(U_{\{i,j\}}\) when \(\{i,j\}\in N\) and is \(\emptyset\)
otherwise. This proves the pullback-object clause in \textup{(i)}; the
identity, pullback-stability, and transitivity pretopology axioms hold by
construction of the generated pretopology.

The covering family $\mathcal U$ is one of the generators of the
pretopology on $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$. A finite set $J\subseteq I$
spans a simplex precisely when the iterated pullback of the corresponding
$U_i$ is a nonempty object $U_J$ rather than $\emptyset$. Hence the
\v{C}ech nerve of the cover is $N$, proving \textup{(ii)}.

The sheaf \(A_{\mathcal U}\) is defined by restricting the genuine
constant sheaf of locally constant \(A\)-valued functions on \(X\) along
the geometric-realization functor \(W\mapsto |W|\). Equivalently,
\(A_{\mathcal U}(W)=\operatorname{LC}(|W|,A)\), with the usual sheaf
restriction maps for locally constant functions. Since every nonempty
formal intersection \(U_J\) is contractible, hence connected, its sections
are \(A\); over the strict initial object the section group is \(0\). This
is a sheaf before it is used as a band: no sheaf condition is being
inferred from the identity-restriction constant presheaf on the formal
index category. More explicitly, if a generated cover of an object \(W\)
contains several nonempty pieces, a matching family is a family of locally
constant functions on the corresponding open subsets which agree on the
nonempty geometric overlaps and impose no condition on empty pullbacks,
where the coefficient group is \(0\). These functions glue uniquely to a
locally constant function on \(|W|\) because they are sections of the
ambient constant sheaf restricted to the cover.
Hence the \v{C}ech $n$-cochains on
$\mathcal U$ are exactly functions from the set of nonempty
$(n+1)$-fold intersections to $A$; empty pullback terms contribute only
zero sections. Thus empty intersections are present as pullback objects
in $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, but their coefficient group is
$A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0$, so omitting them from the finite cochain
display changes neither cochains nor coboundaries. The preceding
paragraph identifies that nonzero indexing set before and after passing
to $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, and the alternating coboundary is the same.
Hence the \v{C}ech cochain complex, the simplicial cochain complex of
$N(\mathcal U)$, and the
universal-coefficient data used in the finite nerve representative are
identical. This proves \textup{(iii)}.

For \textup{(iv)}, fix a nonempty proper intersection object \(U_J\), and
consider any nonempty object \(U_K\to U_J\) of its slice. The pullback of the
vertex cover along \(U_K\to a\) is
\[
\{\widetilde U_{K\cup\{i\}}\to U_K\}_{i\in I}.
\]
For every \(k\in K\), its \(k\)-term is the identity
\(U_K=U_{K\cup\{k\}}\to U_K\). Hence this pulled-back generating cover
contains an identity. The same is true of identity covers and is preserved
under pullback and transitivity: pulling back an identity gives an identity,
and, when covers each containing an identity are composed, the composite
family again contains an identity. Induction over the generation of the
pretopology therefore shows that every covering family of every nonempty
object \(U_K\to U_J\) contains \(\operatorname{id}_{U_K}\). Equivalently,
the topology on the full nonempty subcategory of
\(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/U_J\) is trivial.

The only remaining object is the strict initial object \(\emptyset\to U_J\).
Its empty covering family forces an abelian sheaf to take the value \(0\)
there. Restrictions to that object are then unique. Thus restriction to the
full nonempty subcategory is an equivalence from abelian sheaves on the slice
to presheaves of abelian groups on that subcategory. Since \(U_J\to U_J\) is
terminal, the global section functor is evaluation there, which is exact
because kernels and epimorphisms in a presheaf category are computed
objectwise. Therefore
\[
H^q(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/U_J,A_{\mathcal U})=0
\qquad(q>0).
\]
The slice over the empty intersection is also acyclic: the empty cover forces
every sheaf of abelian groups to have zero value there. Thus all finite
intersections of members of the vertex cover are acyclic. This proves the
Leray hypothesis
directly inside the finite intersection site; contractibility of their ambient
good-cover models is used in \textup{(iii)} to identify coefficient
groups with \(A\), but not to infer this derived acyclicity.

Applying that Leray theorem to the vertex cover of the terminal object \(a\)
identifies the derived sheaf cohomology on the generated finite site with the
\v{C}ech cohomology of this cover. By \textup{(iii)}, the resulting Cech
cochain complex is the simplicial cochain complex of the nonempty nerve
\(N\) with coefficients in \(A\): nonempty intersections contribute one copy
of \(A\), and empty intersections contribute \(0\). Hence
\[
H^n(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U},A_{\mathcal U})
\cong \check H^n(\mathcal U,A_{\mathcal U})
\cong H^n(N,A)
\]
naturally in \(A\). Under this comparison a vertex-cover \v{C}ech
\(2\)-cocycle represents exactly the corresponding derived class in
\(H^2(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U},A_{\mathcal U})\). The argument uses only the
generated finite cover-basis condition just stated; it does not assert an
equivalence of topoi between \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\) and the open-set
site of \(X\).

For \textup{(v)}, build the lower structure explicitly. Let the state set be
the singleton $\{p\}$. For each object $U$ of $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, take a
reference element $r_U$ of sort $\mathsf{Ref}_s$, interpret $\Adm_s$ at $p$ as
the set of all these elements, and put $[r_U]_p=U$. This gives a surjective
reference-class assignment onto the object set of $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$.
Let $r:=r_a$ be the distinguished terminal reference. The refinement relation
and pretopology on these base objects are those of
$\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$; its identity, pullback-stability, transitivity, and
pullback-object clauses were verified in \textup{(i)}. Thus the slice site over
$[r]_p=a$ is exactly $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$. For parameter sorts not used in
the construction, take empty reference sets or the inherited background
interpretation. Assign the given functor $F$ as the presheaf of local sections on
that slice site. These data are exactly the semantic data required by
\Cref{def:reference-classes,def:local-reference-frame,def:local-object-functor};
the lower $\mathbb L_1$-reduct is the same one-state information model with
the added site and local-section symbols forgotten. Hence they define the
claimed $\mathbb L_2$-model by the presheaf expansion case of
\artresult{Typed-expansion model-class interface for the layer chain}. Thus the existence
assertions in this subsection are relative to the deliberately broad
typed-expansion semantics of that proposition, not to an
independently axiomatized smaller class of semantic models.

For \textup{(vi)}, expand this $\mathbb L_2$-model by the supplied fibred
groupoid $\mathfrak P$, the band $\Band$, and the stackification
$a(\mathfrak P)$. The displayed identification
$\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P)\cong F$ gives clause \textup{(i)} of
\Cref{def:banded-groupoid-lift}, and the assumed automorphism-sheaf condition
gives clause \textup{(ii)}. Thus $\mathfrak P$ is a banded groupoid lift for
$r$ at $p$ over the local slice site $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, and adding it
is precisely the $\mathbb L_2\preceq\mathbb L_3$ expansion described in
\artresult{Typed-expansion model-class interface for the layer chain} and
instantiated in \artresult{Concrete instantiation of the finite chain}.
This proves \textup{(v)} and \textup{(vi)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Genuine constant sheaf and isomorphism sheaves on the finite intersection site]\label{lem:formal-site-constant-sheaf-banding}
In the finite intersection site associated to the cover nerve $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$ of
\Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}, let $A_{\mathcal U}$ be the
genuine constant sheaf associated with an abelian group $A$ on the chosen
good-cover space, restricted to the finite intersection site. This is part of the
input supplied by \Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}; it is not
deduced from the identity-restriction constant presheaf on the formal
index category. Equivalently,
\[
A_{\mathcal U}(W)=\operatorname{LC}(|W|,A)
\]
for the open subset \(|W|\subseteq X\) represented by \(W\), with
\(A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0\); on connected nonempty intersections this
is identified with \(A\), but that identification is a consequence of
connectedness of the chosen good-cover pieces, not the definition of a
constant presheaf on the index category. For every object \(W\), all sheaf
assertions below are made on the slice topology
\(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W\), whose covers include the pullbacks
generated by the strict initial object for empty overlaps.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item for every nonempty basic intersection object $U_J$,
$A_{\mathcal U}(U_J)=\operatorname{LC}(|U_J|,A)\cong A$, while
$A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0$; at the terminal object \(a\), the value is
the group of locally constant functions on the whole good-cover
geometric realization, namely \(A^{\pi_0(|N|)}\), and this is \(A\) in the
connected-nerve applications below;
\item the restriction of $A_{\mathcal U}$ to every slice
$\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W$ is again a sheaf. Its value on an object
\(V\to W\) is always \(\operatorname{LC}(|V|,A)\): this is \(A\) for a
connected nonempty basic intersection, \(0\) for \(\emptyset\), and the
locally constant functions on \(|V|\) for any possibly disconnected
corresponding open set;
\item for every generated covering family
\[
\{V_\ell\to V\}_{\ell\in L}
\]
in a slice, a family \(a_\ell\in A_{\mathcal U}(V_\ell)\) is a matching
family exactly when the corresponding locally constant functions agree on
each nonempty pullback \(V_\ell\times_VV_m\), while pullbacks equal to
\(\emptyset\) impose only the unique equality in
\(A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0\). Such matching families glue uniquely in
\(A_{\mathcal U}(V)\);
\item if \(x,y\) are two objects of a fibred groupoid over \(W\) whose
isomorphism presheaf is defined on each \(V\to W\) by an explicit
identification
\[
\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}(x,y)(V)=A_{\mathcal U}(V)
\]
with restrictions those of \(A_{\mathcal U}\), then
\(\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}(x,y)\) is a sheaf on the slice
\(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W\). In particular, automorphism presheaves
defined in this way are genuine sheaves banded by \(A_{\mathcal U}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The sheaf \(A_{\mathcal U}\) has already been defined as the restriction
of the genuine constant sheaf on the chosen good-cover space. Thus
the sheaf property is inherited before any calculation of values on the
formal index category is made. Each nonempty object $U_J$ is represented
by a nonempty finite intersection of good-cover members, hence by a
connected contractible space, so locally constant $A$-valued functions on
it are just constant functions with value in $A$. The strict initial
object represents the empty open set and has the zero group of sections.
The terminal object \(a\) represents the whole good-cover space, so
its sections are locally constant functions on \(|N|\), i.e.
\(A^{\pi_0(|N|)}\). In the construction theorems that use this lemma the
nerve is connected, and this terminal value is a single copy of \(A\).
This proves \textup{(i)}.

Restriction of a sheaf to a slice site is a sheaf, so
$A_{\mathcal U}|_{\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W}$ is a sheaf on every slice.
Equivalently, if
\[
\{V_\ell\to V\}_{\ell\in L}
\]
is any covering family in the generated pretopology of
\(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W\), a matching family of sections of
$A_{\mathcal U}$ over the \(V_\ell\) glues uniquely because those sections
are locally constant functions on the corresponding good-cover
intersections. If \(|V|\) is connected and nonempty, the glued section is a
single element of \(A\), and compatibility along nonempty pullback
overlaps is exactly equality of those local constants. If \(|V|\) is
disconnected, the same sheaf axiom glues independently on the connected
components of \(|V|\), giving \(\operatorname{LC}(|V|,A)\) rather than a
single copy of \(A\).
Cover members whose pullback is \(\emptyset\) impose no equality because
\(A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0\); when \(V=\emptyset\), the only covering
is refined by the empty cover and the unique section is \(0\). This proves
\textup{(ii)} without invoking the identity-restriction constant
presheaf, and the same paragraph proves the explicit matching-family
description in \textup{(iii)}.

For \textup{(iv)}, the displayed identification is an isomorphism
\[
\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}(x,y)\cong
A_{\mathcal U}|_{\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W}
\]
of presheaves on the slice; the right-hand side is a sheaf by
\textup{(ii)}. This is the automorphism-sheaf condition used below. Thus
any later notation that writes the coefficient group as $A$ on nonempty
basic objects abbreviates sections of the sheaf $A_{\mathcal U}$, not the
constant presheaf with identity restrictions on an arbitrary site.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Slice \(H^1\)-vanishing and neutral one-object stacks]\label{lem:formal-site-neutral-stack-h1}
Let \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\) be the finite intersection site associated to the cover nerve of
\Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}, and assume that every
nonempty good-cover intersection \(|U_J|\) is contractible and that
\[
H_1(N(\mathcal U),\mathbb Z)=0.
\]
Let
\(A_{\mathcal U}\) be the genuine constant sheaf associated with an abelian
group \(A\) on the chosen good-cover space. Then for every object
\(W\in\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\),
\[
H^1(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W,A_{\mathcal U})=0.
\]
Moreover, on every slice \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W\), the prestack
\(P^0_W\) with one object \(*_V\) over each \(V\to W\), automorphism group
\(\operatorname{Aut}(*_V)=A_{\mathcal U}(V)\), and restriction functors
given by the sheaf restrictions of \(A_{\mathcal U}\), is a stack.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(W=\emptyset\), the slice has a terminal initial object with only the
empty-cover contribution and \(A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0\), so the
cohomology group is zero. If \(W=U_J\) is nonempty, the slice
\(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W\) is the formal good-cover site obtained by
pulling the chosen good cover back to the contractible intersection
\(|U_J|\). All nonempty finite intersections in that pulled-back cover are
again contractible, and the strict initial object accounts for empty
intersections. Leray's theorem for this acyclic cover identifies
\[
H^1(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W,A_{\mathcal U})
\cong
\check H^1(\mathcal U|_W,A_{\mathcal U})
\cong
H^1(|W|,A).
\]
The last group is zero because \(|W|\) is contractible. If \(W=a\), the
same comparison gives
\[
H^1(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/a,A_{\mathcal U})
\cong H^1(|N|,A).
\]
In the construction theorems that use neutral summands we assume
\(H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0\); the universal coefficient theorem then gives
\[
H^1(|N|,A)\cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_1(N,\mathbb Z),A)=0
\]
for every abelian group \(A\). Thus the displayed vanishing holds on every
relevant slice.

It remains to spell out why this vanishing, not merely the sheaf condition
for automorphisms, makes the neutral one-object prestack a stack. Let
\(\{V_i\to V\}\) be a cover in \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W\). A descent datum
for objects of \(P^0_W\) consists of the unique local lift objects together with
transition automorphisms
\[
g_{ij}\in A_{\mathcal U}(V_i\times_VV_j)
\]
satisfying \(g_{jk}+g_{ij}=g_{ik}\) on triple overlaps. This is precisely an
\(A_{\mathcal U}\)-torsor cocycle on the slice over \(V\). Since
\[
H^1(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/V,A_{\mathcal U})=0,
\]
there are sections \(h_i\in A_{\mathcal U}(V_i)\) with
\[
g_{ij}=h_j-h_i.
\]
After changing the local identifications by the \(h_i\), the descent datum
is equivalent to the trivial descent datum, which glues to the object
\(*_V\). Descent for morphisms is exactly the sheaf condition for
\(A_{\mathcal U}\), already proved in
\Cref{lem:formal-site-constant-sheaf-banding}. Hence \(P^0_W\) is a stack.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Nonempty refinements in the finite intersection site associated to the cover nerve]\label{lem:formal-site-nonempty-refinements}
Let \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\) be the finite intersection site associated to the cover nerve of
\Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}. If \(W\ne\emptyset\), then
every covering family of \(W\) has a nonempty member. Consequently,
pulling any covering family of \(a\) back to a vertex object \(U_i\)
produces a cover of \(U_i\) with at least one nonempty member.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The pretopology on \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\) is generated by the vertex
cover, its pullbacks, identity covers, transitivity, and the empty cover of
\(\emptyset\). The only generator with no nonempty member is the empty
cover of the strict initial object. Pullback stability does not turn a
cover of a nonempty object into a family consisting only of
\(\emptyset\): if \(W=U_J\ne\emptyset\), the pullback of the vertex cover
contains the term \(\widetilde U_{J\cup\{j\}}=U_J\) for every \(j\in J\).
Identity covers of nonempty objects are nonempty, and transitivity
preserves the existence of a nonempty member because a cover of a
nonempty intermediate object again has a nonempty member by the same
induction on the generation of covers. Thus every generated cover of
\(W\ne\emptyset\) has at least one nonempty member. The final assertion is
the case \(W=U_i\) after pullback.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Sheafification of the formal label presheaf]\label{lem:formal-label-sheafification}
Let \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\) be the finite intersection site associated to the cover nerve of
\Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}, and assume that the
nonempty nerve \(N(\mathcal U)\) is connected. Let \(\Lambda\) be a set and
\(\Lambda_0\subseteq\Lambda\). Define a presheaf \(F_{\Lambda_0}\) on
\(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\) by
\[
F_{\Lambda_0}(W)=
\begin{cases}
\Lambda,& W\to a\text{ a nonempty proper formal intersection object},\\
\Lambda_0,& W=a,\\
\{\ast\},& W=\emptyset,
\end{cases}
\]
with identity restrictions among nonempty proper intersection objects,
the inclusion \(\Lambda_0\hookrightarrow\Lambda\) from \(a\) to nonempty
proper objects, and the unique restriction to \(\emptyset\). Then
\[
\bigl(aF_{\Lambda_0}\bigr)(a)\cong\Lambda.
\]
Under this bijection, a label \(\lambda\in\Lambda\) is represented by the
strict matching family that assigns \(\lambda\) to every vertex object
\(U_i\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), the constant vertex assignment
\(\lambda_i=\lambda\in F_{\Lambda_0}(U_i)\) is a strict matching family:
on a nonempty pairwise overlap the two restrictions are the same label,
and on an empty pairwise overlap both restrictions are the unique element
of \(F_{\Lambda_0}(\emptyset)\). This gives a section of
\((aF_{\Lambda_0})(a)\).

Conversely, let \(\xi\in(aF_{\Lambda_0})(a)\). By
\Cref{lem:strict-sheafification-representatives}, after replacing its
plus--plus representative by a composite cover, \(\xi\) is represented by
sections of \(F_{\Lambda_0}\) whose restrictions to each pullback overlap
agree after a further cover of that overlap. Pull this cover back along
the vertex cover \(\{U_i\to a\}\). For each vertex \(i\), the resulting
cover of \(U_i\) has a nonempty member by
\Cref{lem:formal-site-nonempty-refinements}; choose one such member and
call the label carried by its section \(\lambda_i\in\Lambda\). This label
is independent of the chosen nonempty member of the pulled-back cover of
\(U_i\): two such members meet after a common covering refinement in the
plus--plus representative, and on every nonempty refinement all
restriction maps among nonempty proper objects are identities on
\(\Lambda\), while refinements through \(\emptyset\) carry no label.

If two vertices \(i\) and \(j\) are joined by an edge of \(N\), the
pullback \(U_{ij}=U_i\times_aU_j\) is a nonempty object. Pulling the
representative further to \(U_{ij}\) and using
\Cref{lem:formal-site-nonempty-refinements} gives a nonempty refinement on
which the two restrictions agree in \(F_{\Lambda_0}\). Since the
restrictions from \(U_i\) and \(U_j\) to any nonempty refinement of
\(U_{ij}\) are identity maps on \(\Lambda\), this equality forces
\(\lambda_i=\lambda_j\). The nerve is connected, so equality propagates
along edge paths and all vertices carry one common label
\(\lambda\in\Lambda\). Every nonempty piece of the composite
representative maps to some vertex and therefore carries this same label.
Thus \(\xi\) is the section represented by the constant vertex family with
label \(\lambda\).

It remains only to check that distinct labels do not become equivalent.
If the constant families labelled \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\) are equivalent
in the plus--plus construction, then after a common covering refinement
their restrictions agree on every nonempty member of that refinement.
Pulling the common refinement back to any vertex object \(U_i\) gives a
cover of \(U_i\). By \Cref{lem:formal-site-nonempty-refinements}, this
cover has a nonempty member. On that member all restrictions among
nonempty proper objects are identities on \(\Lambda\), hence
\(\lambda=\mu\). Thus the displayed map
\(\Lambda\to(aF_{\Lambda_0})(a)\) is bijective.
\end{proof}
