\subsection{Realization of prescribed branch data}

The preceding algebra isolates the branchwise and aggregate quotients attached to a given family of hidden subgroups. The next theorem gives a converse existence statement on connected finite nerves with vanishing first homology. It is internal to the fixed finite-nerve framework of the paper: the nerve, the constant band, and the comparison data are fixed in advance, and the theorem constructs local-object and prestack data realizing the prescribed images.

The realization proofs use the following explicit admissible-expansion
interface. The site is not an arbitrary full subcategory of intersections.
It is the formal \v{C}ech site of a finite good-cover presentation, so that
the cohomological comparison used later is an actual Leray comparison rather
than a declared comparison datum.

\begin{lemma}[Formal \v{C}ech site and one-state admissible expansion]\label{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}
Let
\[
\mathcal U=\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I}
\]
be a finite cover whose nerve $N=N(\mathcal U)$ is realized by a good cover
of a paracompact space $X\simeq |N|$. Let $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$ be the
formal \v{C}ech index site over this chosen good-cover realization, defined
as follows. Its objects are $a$, one strict initial object $\emptyset$, and
one object $U_J$ for each nonempty simplex $J\in N$. A realization functor
from this formal category to open subsets of \(X\) sends \(a\) to \(X\),
\(\emptyset\) to the empty open set, and \(U_J\) to the corresponding
nonempty good-cover intersection. This is a pullback-complete formal site,
not the full subcategory on only nonempty intersections. The same strict
initial object represents every empty finite intersection. Thus the only
finite subsets represented by named non-initial objects are the simplices
of \(N\), but a finite subset \(J\subseteq I\) which is not a simplex is
not discarded from the site-level pullback calculus; whenever such a
subset arises as a pullback intersection, the pullback is the
distinguished object \(\emptyset\). In formulas
we write
\[
\widetilde U_J:=
\begin{cases}
U_J,& J\in N,\\
\emptyset,& J\notin N.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus empty intersections are not omitted from the site: all of them are
identified with the same strict initial pullback object $\emptyset$, and
the realization of \(\widetilde U_J\) is the actual intersection
\(\bigcap_{j\in J}U_j\), possibly empty. The order is generated by
\[
\emptyset\to U_J\to U_K\to a
\qquad (K\subseteq J,\; J,K\in N).
\]
There is a unique arrow \(\emptyset\to W\) for every object \(W\), and no
non-initial object maps to \(\emptyset\).
For any two arrows $U_J\to U_L$ and $U_K\to U_L$ with
$L\subseteq J\cap K$, their fibre product is
\[
U_J\times_{U_L}U_K=\widetilde U_{J\cup K}.
\]
If $J\cup K\notin N$, this means that the fibre product is the strict
initial object $\emptyset$. Any fibre product in which one factor is
$\emptyset$ is also $\emptyset$. The pretopology is the smallest
pretopology for which the vertex cover \(\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I}\), all of
its pullbacks along arrows of the formal category, all identity covers,
and the empty cover of \(\emptyset\) are covers. Equivalently, it is
obtained by repeatedly applying pullback stability and transitivity to
the vertex cover inside the formal category just described. For example,
the pullback of the vertex cover along \(U_J\to a\) is the family of
arrows
\[
\{\widetilde U_{J\cup\{i\}}\to U_J\}_{i\in I},
\]
with the terms for non-simplices equal to \(\emptyset\). Repeated
occurrences of \(\emptyset\) in such a pulled-back family may be
identified with the same initial object. They are harmless in matching
conditions because the coefficient sheaves used below have zero value at
\(\emptyset\), but the corresponding pullback object is still present in
the site. In particular, disjoint cover members \(U_i,U_j\to a\) have the
pullback \(U_i\times_aU_j=\emptyset\) in the site. The empty object is
omitted only from the nonempty \v{C}ech nerve and from the displayed
nonzero cochain groups, never from the local-reference-site structure.
Every pullback demanded by \Cref{def:local-reference-frame} is therefore
an object of \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\), including the empty pullback
objects produced by disjoint members of a covering family.
Thus closure under the pretopology axioms is part of the definition, not a
consequence of omitting any empty pullback object. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$ is a pullback-based local reference site
with terminal object $a$; in particular, for every pair of cover members
$U_i,U_j\to a$, the fibre product is the formal object $U_{\{i,j\}}$ if
$\{i,j\}\in N$ and is the strict initial object $\emptyset$ otherwise;
\item the cover $\mathcal U$ remains a cover of $a$ in
$\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, all pullbacks of members of this cover exist
there, and its nonempty \v{C}ech nerve is the finite simplicial complex
$N$;
\item for every abelian group $A$, let $A_{\mathcal U}$ denote the genuine
constant sheaf
on \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\)
\[
A_{\mathcal U}(W)=\operatorname{LC}(|W|,A),
\]
where \(|W|\subseteq X\) is the open set represented by \(W\). This is the
restriction of the genuine constant sheaf of locally constant
\(A\)-valued functions on the chosen good-cover realization. It is this
sheaf, not the identity-restriction constant presheaf on the abstract
index category, that is used as the band. With these coefficients the
\v{C}ech cochain complex of
$\mathcal U$ with coefficients in $A_{\mathcal U}$ and the finite nerve
presentation of any class in $H^2(N(\mathcal U),A)$ are the same finite
cochain data after replacing $\mathcal C_a$ with
$\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$. This is a cochain-level identification because
the cover is a chosen good cover: on connected nonempty basic
intersections \(A_{\mathcal U}\) has value \(A\), while on the strict
initial object it has value \(0\);
\item for every constant abelian group $A$, the Leray comparison for the
chosen good cover and the genuine constant sheaf $A_{\mathcal U}$ gives a
natural isomorphism
\[
H^n(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U},A_{\mathcal U})\cong \check H^n(\mathcal U,A_{\mathcal U})\cong H^n(N,A)
\]
for all $n$, and the \v{C}ech cocycle on the vertex cover maps to the
corresponding derived cohomology class;
\item for every contravariant functor
\[
F:\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}^{op}\to\mathsf{Set},
\]
there is a one-state $\mathbb L_2$-model $M_2$ with state $p$ and a
distinguished admitted reference $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ such that $[r]_p=a$, the
local slice site of $r$ is exactly $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, and the local
object functor on that slice site is $F$; this model is the admissible
$\mathbb L_2$-expansion supplied by
\Cref{prop:admissible-layer-expansion-interface};
\item if, in addition, a category fibred in groupoids
\[
\mathfrak P\to\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}
\]
with abelian sheaf band $\Band$ satisfies
\[
\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P)\cong F
\]
and the automorphism-sheaf condition of
\Cref{def:realization-prestack}, then $M_2$ has an admissible
$\mathbb L_3$-expansion $M_3$ in which $r$ has realization prestack
$\mathfrak P$, band $\Band$, and stackification $a(\mathfrak P)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The formal index category is thin and has terminal object $a$. We first
verify the pullbacks, since they are part of the local-reference-site
structure and not merely a convention about the \v{C}ech cochain complex.
Suppose that \(U_J\to U_L\) and \(U_K\to U_L\) are two maps, so
\(L\subseteq J\cap K\). If \(J\cup K\in N\), then \(U_{J\cup K}\) maps to
both \(U_J\) and \(U_K\), and any object mapping to both over \(U_L\) is
indexed by a simplex containing \(J\cup K\), hence factors uniquely through
\(U_{J\cup K}\). If \(J\cup K\notin N\), no nonempty simplex can contain the
union, so the only object mapping to both over \(U_L\) is the strict initial
object \(\emptyset\). Pullbacks involving \(\emptyset\) are \(\emptyset\) by
strict initiality. Therefore every fibre product required by
\Cref{def:local-reference-frame} is an object of \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\).
In particular, for every pair in the covering family \(\{U_i\to a\}\) the
pullback is \(U_{\{i,j\}}\) when \(\{i,j\}\in N\) and is \(\emptyset\)
otherwise. This proves the pullback-object clause in \textup{(i)}; the
identity, pullback-stability, and transitivity pretopology axioms hold by
construction of the generated pretopology.

The covering family $\mathcal U$ is one of the generators of the
pretopology on $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$. A finite set $J\subseteq I$
spans a simplex precisely when the iterated pullback of the corresponding
$U_i$ is a nonempty object $U_J$ rather than $\emptyset$. Hence the
\v{C}ech nerve of the cover is $N$, proving \textup{(ii)}.

The sheaf \(A_{\mathcal U}\) is defined by restricting the genuine
constant sheaf of locally constant \(A\)-valued functions on \(X\) along
the realization functor \(W\mapsto |W|\). Equivalently,
\(A_{\mathcal U}(W)=\operatorname{LC}(|W|,A)\), with the usual sheaf
restriction maps for locally constant functions. Since every nonempty
formal intersection \(U_J\) is contractible, hence connected, its sections
are \(A\); over the strict initial object the section group is \(0\). This
is a sheaf before it is used as a band: no sheaf condition is being
inferred from the identity-restriction constant presheaf on the formal
index category. More explicitly, if a generated cover of an object \(W\)
contains several nonempty pieces, a matching family is a family of locally
constant functions on the corresponding open subsets which agree on the
nonempty realized overlaps and impose no condition on empty pullbacks,
where the coefficient group is \(0\). These functions glue uniquely to a
locally constant function on \(|W|\) because they are sections of the
ambient constant sheaf restricted to the cover.
Hence the \v{C}ech $n$-cochains on
$\mathcal U$ are exactly functions from the set of nonempty
$(n+1)$-fold intersections to $A$; empty pullback terms contribute only
zero sections. Thus empty intersections are present as pullback objects
in $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, but their coefficient group is
$A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0$, so omitting them from the finite cochain
display changes neither cochains nor coboundaries. The preceding
paragraph identifies that nonzero indexing set before and after passing
to $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, and the alternating coboundary is the same.
Hence the \v{C}ech cochain complex, the simplicial cochain complex of
$N(\mathcal U)$, and the
universal-coefficient data used in the finite nerve presentation are
identical. This proves \textup{(iii)}.

For \textup{(iv)}, let $j:X\to\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$ denote the
geometric realization comparison induced by the chosen good cover. Since
all nonempty finite intersections in a good cover are contractible, the
higher cohomology of the constant sheaf $A_{\mathcal U}$ on each member and
each nonempty finite intersection vanishes; over the strict initial object
there is no additional contribution. Leray's theorem for this acyclic cover identifies
the derived sheaf cohomology of the associated site with the \v{C}ech
cohomology of the cover \cite{Bredon1997,Jardine2015}. The nerve lemma identifies the good-cover
\v{C}ech cohomology with the simplicial cohomology of $N$; the
cochain-level identification is the ordered finite nerve comparison of
\Cref{prop:finite-nerve-comparison}. Naturality in the constant group $A$
is inherited from the functoriality of sheaf cohomology and simplicial
cochains. Therefore a vertex-cover \v{C}ech $2$-cocycle represents exactly
the corresponding derived class in
$H^2(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U},A_{\mathcal U})$, rather than a separately
declared comparison datum.

For \textup{(v)}, build the lower structure explicitly. Let the state set be
the singleton $\{p\}$. For each object $U$ of $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, take a
reference element $r_U$ of sort $\mathsf{Ref}_s$, interpret $\Adm_s$ at $p$ as
the set of all these elements, and put $[r_U]_p=U$. This gives a surjective
reference-class assignment onto the object set of $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$.
Let $r:=r_a$ be the distinguished terminal reference. The refinement relation
and pretopology on these reference classes are those of
$\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$; its identity, pullback-stability, transitivity, and
pullback-object clauses were verified in \textup{(i)}. Thus the slice site over
$[r]_p=a$ is exactly $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$. For reference sorts not used in
the construction, take empty reference sets or the inherited background
interpretation. Assign the given functor $F$ as the local object functor on
that slice site. These data are exactly the semantic data required by
\Cref{def:reference-classes,def:local-reference-frame,def:local-object-functor};
the lower $\mathbb L_1$-reduct is the same one-state information model with
the added site and local-object symbols forgotten. Hence they define the
claimed $\mathbb L_2$-model by the local-object expansion case of
\Cref{prop:admissible-layer-expansion-interface}. Thus the existence
assertions in this subsection are relative to the deliberately broad
well-typed admissible-expansion semantics of that proposition, not to an
independently axiomatized smaller class of semantic models.

For \textup{(vi)}, expand this $\mathbb L_2$-model by the supplied fibred
groupoid $\mathfrak P$, the band $\Band$, and the stackification
$a(\mathfrak P)$. The displayed identification
$\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P)\cong F$ gives clause \textup{(i)} of
\Cref{def:realization-prestack}, and the assumed automorphism-sheaf condition
gives clause \textup{(ii)}. Thus $\mathfrak P$ is a realization prestack for
$r$ at $p$ over the local slice site $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, and adding it
is precisely the $\mathbb L_2\preceq\mathbb L_3$ expansion described in
\Cref{prop:admissible-layer-expansion-interface} and instantiated in
\Cref{prop:chain-instantiation}. This proves \textup{(v)} and \textup{(vi)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Genuine constant sheaf and isomorphism sheaves on the formal site]\label{lem:formal-site-constant-sheaf-banding}
In the formal \v{C}ech site $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$ of
\Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}, let $A_{\mathcal U}$ be the
genuine constant sheaf associated with an abelian group $A$ on the chosen
good-cover realization, restricted to the formal site. This is part of the
input supplied by \Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}; it is not
deduced from the identity-restriction constant presheaf on the formal
index category. Equivalently,
\[
A_{\mathcal U}(W)=\operatorname{LC}(|W|,A)
\]
for the open subset \(|W|\subseteq X\) represented by \(W\), with
\(A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0\); on connected nonempty intersections this
is identified with \(A\), but that identification is a consequence of
connectedness of the chosen good-cover pieces, not the definition of a
constant presheaf on the index category. For every object \(W\), all sheaf
assertions below are made on the slice topology
\(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W\), whose covers include the pullbacks
generated by the strict initial object for empty overlaps.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item for every nonempty basic intersection object $U_J$,
$A_{\mathcal U}(U_J)=\operatorname{LC}(|U_J|,A)\cong A$, while
$A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0$; at the terminal object \(a\), the value is
the group of locally constant functions on the whole good-cover
realization, namely \(A^{\pi_0(|N|)}\), and this is \(A\) in the
connected-nerve applications below;
\item the restriction of $A_{\mathcal U}$ to every slice
$\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W$ is again a sheaf. Its value on an object
\(V\to W\) is always \(\operatorname{LC}(|V|,A)\): this is \(A\) for a
connected nonempty basic intersection, \(0\) for \(\emptyset\), and the
locally constant functions on \(|V|\) for any possibly disconnected
realized open set;
\item for every generated covering family
\[
\{V_\ell\to V\}_{\ell\in L}
\]
in a slice, a family \(a_\ell\in A_{\mathcal U}(V_\ell)\) is a matching
family exactly when the corresponding locally constant functions agree on
each nonempty pullback \(V_\ell\times_VV_m\), while pullbacks equal to
\(\emptyset\) impose only the unique equality in
\(A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0\). Such matching families glue uniquely in
\(A_{\mathcal U}(V)\);
\item if \(x,y\) are two objects of a fibred groupoid over \(W\) whose
isomorphism presheaf is defined on each \(V\to W\) by an explicit
identification
\[
\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}(x,y)(V)=A_{\mathcal U}(V)
\]
with restrictions those of \(A_{\mathcal U}\), then
\(\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}(x,y)\) is a sheaf on the slice
\(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W\). In particular, automorphism presheaves
defined in this way are genuine sheaves banded by \(A_{\mathcal U}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The sheaf \(A_{\mathcal U}\) has already been defined as the restriction
of the genuine constant sheaf on the chosen good-cover realization. Thus
the sheaf property is inherited before any calculation of values on the
formal index category is made. Each nonempty object $U_J$ is represented
by a nonempty finite intersection of good-cover members, hence by a
connected contractible space, so locally constant $A$-valued functions on
it are just constant functions with value in $A$. The strict initial
object represents the empty open set and has the zero group of sections.
The terminal object \(a\) represents the whole good-cover realization, so
its sections are locally constant functions on \(|N|\), i.e.
\(A^{\pi_0(|N|)}\). In the realization theorems that use this lemma the
nerve is connected, and this terminal value is a single copy of \(A\).
This proves \textup{(i)}.

Restriction of a sheaf to a slice site is a sheaf, so
$A_{\mathcal U}|_{\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W}$ is a sheaf on every slice.
Equivalently, if
\[
\{V_\ell\to V\}_{\ell\in L}
\]
is any covering family in the generated pretopology of
\(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W\), a matching family of sections of
$A_{\mathcal U}$ over the \(V_\ell\) glues uniquely because those sections
are locally constant functions on the corresponding good-cover
intersections. If \(|V|\) is connected and nonempty, the glued section is a
single element of \(A\), and compatibility along nonempty pullback
overlaps is exactly equality of those local constants. If \(|V|\) is
disconnected, the same sheaf axiom glues independently on the connected
components of \(|V|\), giving \(\operatorname{LC}(|V|,A)\) rather than a
single copy of \(A\).
Cover members whose pullback is \(\emptyset\) impose no equality because
\(A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0\); when \(V=\emptyset\), the only covering
is refined by the empty cover and the unique section is \(0\). This proves
\textup{(ii)} without invoking the identity-restriction constant
presheaf, and the same paragraph proves the explicit matching-family
description in \textup{(iii)}.

For \textup{(iv)}, the displayed identification is an isomorphism
\[
\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}(x,y)\cong
A_{\mathcal U}|_{\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W}
\]
of presheaves on the slice; the right-hand side is a sheaf by
\textup{(ii)}. This is the automorphism-sheaf condition used below. Thus
any later notation that writes the coefficient group as $A$ on nonempty
basic objects abbreviates sections of the sheaf $A_{\mathcal U}$, not the
constant presheaf with identity restrictions on an arbitrary site.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Nonempty refinements in the formal \v{C}ech site]\label{lem:formal-site-nonempty-refinements}
Let \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\) be the formal \v{C}ech site of
\Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}. If \(W\ne\emptyset\), then
every covering family of \(W\) has a nonempty member. Consequently,
pulling any covering family of \(a\) back to a vertex object \(U_i\)
produces a cover of \(U_i\) with at least one nonempty member.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The pretopology on \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\) is generated by the vertex
cover, its pullbacks, identity covers, transitivity, and the empty cover of
\(\emptyset\). The only generator with no nonempty member is the empty
cover of the strict initial object. Pullback stability does not turn a
cover of a nonempty object into a family consisting only of
\(\emptyset\): if \(W=U_J\ne\emptyset\), the pullback of the vertex cover
contains the term \(\widetilde U_{J\cup\{j\}}=U_J\) for every \(j\in J\).
Identity covers of nonempty objects are nonempty, and transitivity
preserves the existence of a nonempty member because a cover of a
nonempty intermediate object again has a nonempty member by the same
induction on the generation of covers. Thus every generated cover of
\(W\ne\emptyset\) has at least one nonempty member. The final assertion is
the case \(W=U_i\) after pullback.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Sheafification of the formal label presheaf]\label{lem:formal-label-sheafification}
Let \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\) be the formal \v{C}ech site of
\Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}, and assume that the
nonempty nerve \(N(\mathcal U)\) is connected. Let \(\Lambda\) be a set and
\(\Lambda_0\subseteq\Lambda\). Define a presheaf \(F_{\Lambda_0}\) on
\(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\) by
\[
F_{\Lambda_0}(W)=
\begin{cases}
\Lambda,& W\to a\text{ a nonempty proper formal intersection object},\\
\Lambda_0,& W=a,\\
\{\ast\},& W=\emptyset,
\end{cases}
\]
with identity restrictions among nonempty proper intersection objects,
the inclusion \(\Lambda_0\hookrightarrow\Lambda\) from \(a\) to nonempty
proper objects, and the unique restriction to \(\emptyset\). Then
\[
\bigl(aF_{\Lambda_0}\bigr)(a)\cong\Lambda.
\]
Under this bijection, a label \(\lambda\in\Lambda\) is represented by the
strict matching family that assigns \(\lambda\) to every vertex object
\(U_i\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), the constant vertex assignment
\(\lambda_i=\lambda\in F_{\Lambda_0}(U_i)\) is a strict matching family:
on a nonempty pairwise overlap the two restrictions are the same label,
and on an empty pairwise overlap both restrictions are the unique element
of \(F_{\Lambda_0}(\emptyset)\). This gives a section of
\((aF_{\Lambda_0})(a)\).

Conversely, let \(\xi\in(aF_{\Lambda_0})(a)\). By
\Cref{lem:strict-sheafification-representatives}, after replacing its
plus--plus representative by a composite cover, \(\xi\) is represented by
sections of \(F_{\Lambda_0}\) whose restrictions to each pullback overlap
agree after a further cover of that overlap. Pull this cover back along
the vertex cover \(\{U_i\to a\}\). For each vertex \(i\), the resulting
cover of \(U_i\) has a nonempty member by
\Cref{lem:formal-site-nonempty-refinements}; choose one such member and
call the label carried by its section \(\lambda_i\in\Lambda\). This label
is independent of the chosen nonempty member of the pulled-back cover of
\(U_i\): two such members meet after a common covering refinement in the
plus--plus representative, and on every nonempty refinement all
restriction maps among nonempty proper objects are identities on
\(\Lambda\), while refinements through \(\emptyset\) carry no label.

If two vertices \(i\) and \(j\) are joined by an edge of \(N\), the
pullback \(U_{ij}=U_i\times_aU_j\) is a nonempty object. Pulling the
representative further to \(U_{ij}\) and using
\Cref{lem:formal-site-nonempty-refinements} gives a nonempty refinement on
which the two restrictions agree in \(F_{\Lambda_0}\). Since the
restrictions from \(U_i\) and \(U_j\) to any nonempty refinement of
\(U_{ij}\) are identity maps on \(\Lambda\), this equality forces
\(\lambda_i=\lambda_j\). The nerve is connected, so equality propagates
along edge paths and all vertices carry one common label
\(\lambda\in\Lambda\). Every nonempty piece of the composite
representative maps to some vertex and therefore carries this same label.
Thus \(\xi\) is the section represented by the constant vertex family with
label \(\lambda\).

It remains only to check that distinct labels do not become equivalent.
If the constant families labelled \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\) are equivalent
in the plus--plus construction, then after a common covering refinement
their restrictions agree on every nonempty member of that refinement.
Pulling the common refinement back to any vertex object \(U_i\) gives a
cover of \(U_i\). By \Cref{lem:formal-site-nonempty-refinements}, this
cover has a nonempty member. On that member all restrictions among
nonempty proper objects are identities on \(\Lambda\), hence
\(\lambda=\mu\). Thus the displayed map
\(\Lambda\to(aF_{\Lambda_0})(a)\) is bijective.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Twisted branch prestack model]\label{lem:twisted-branch-prestack-model}
Let $\mathcal U=\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I}$ be a finite cover whose nerve $N$ is connected, let $A$ be a finite abelian group, let $\Lambda$ be a finite nonempty label set, and let
\[
\alpha_\lambda\in Z^2(N,A)
\qquad
(\lambda\in\Lambda)
\]
be normalized simplicial cocycles. We use their alternating extension to all
ordered triples of indices: for pairwise distinct indices, permuting an
ordered simplex multiplies the value by the sign of the permutation, and any
triple with a repeated index has value $0$. Let
\[
A_{\mathcal U}
\]
denote the genuine constant sheaf on $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, i.e. the
sheaf of locally constant $A$-valued functions on the chosen good-cover
realization restricted to the formal \v{C}ech site. Hence
$A_{\mathcal U}(U_J)=A$ for each nonempty basic intersection and
$A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0$.
Let
\[
\Lambda_0:=\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid \alpha_\lambda=0\}.
\]
On the formal \v{C}ech site $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, define $F$ by
\[
F(W)=
\begin{cases}
\Lambda,& W\to a\text{ a nonempty proper formal intersection object},\\
\Lambda_0,& W=a,\\
\{\ast\},& W=\emptyset,
\end{cases}
\]
with identity restrictions among nonempty proper intersection objects,
inclusions $\Lambda_0\hookrightarrow\Lambda$ from $a$ to nonempty proper
intersections, and the unique restriction map to $F(\emptyset)$. For each
$\lambda\notin\Lambda_0$, define a fibred groupoid $\mathfrak P_\lambda$ by
\[
\mathfrak P_\lambda(a)=\varnothing
\]
and, for every nonempty proper object $W$ of $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, put
\[
I(W):=\{i\in I\mid W\to U_i\text{ in }\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}\}.
\]
Thus $I(U_J)=J$ for a formal intersection object. Define objects
\[
x_i^{(\lambda,W)}\qquad (i\in I(W))
\]
and morphism sets
\[
\mathrm{Hom}\bigl(x_i^{(\lambda,W)},x_j^{(\lambda,W)}\bigr)
=A_{\mathcal U}(W),
\]
which is identified with \(A\) because \(W\) is a connected nonempty
basic intersection. With this identification, write morphisms as
\((a,i,j)\) with \(a\in A\), and define composition by
\[
(b,j,k)\circ(a,i,j)=(a+b+\alpha_{\lambda,ijk},i,k).
\]
For a morphism $V\to W$, one has $I(W)\subseteq I(V)$, because a map
$W\to U_i$ composes with $V\to W$. Restrictions therefore send
$x_i^{(\lambda,W)}$ to $x_i^{(\lambda,V)}$ and $(a,i,j)$ to the same
coefficient-labelled morphism after applying the restriction map of
\(A_{\mathcal U}\). Over $\emptyset$ take the terminal groupoid and let
every restriction to $\emptyset$ be the unique functor. For each
$\lambda\in\Lambda_0$, let $\mathfrak P_\lambda$ be the neutral stack with
one object over every object and automorphism group
\(A_{\mathcal U}(W)\) over \(W\), so the fibre over \(\emptyset\) is
terminal because \(A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0\). Restrictions are exactly
the restriction maps of the sheaf \(A_{\mathcal U}\). Its automorphism
sheaf is therefore the genuine constant sheaf $A_{\mathcal U}$. Set
\[
\mathfrak P:=\coprod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak P_\lambda.
\]
This denotes the fibrewise coproduct over every nonempty object, while over
$\emptyset$ the summands share the single terminal fibre and all
restrictions to $\emptyset$ are the unique functor. The band used below is
the genuine constant sheaf $A_{\mathcal U}$ on the formal site, not the
identity-restriction constant presheaf.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item each nonzero summand $\mathfrak P_\lambda$ is a category fibred in groupoids, and the pullback functors are strictly functorial;
\item the identity, inverse, and associativity laws in each twisted fibre are respectively the normalized identities
\[
\alpha_{\lambda,iij}=\alpha_{\lambda,ijj}=0,
\qquad
\alpha_{\lambda,iji}=0,
\]
and the cocycle identity
\[
\alpha_{\lambda,jkl}-\alpha_{\lambda,ikl}
+\alpha_{\lambda,ijl}-\alpha_{\lambda,ijk}=0;
\]
\item $\mathfrak P$ is a prestack with genuine constant band $A_{\mathcal U}$;
\item its presheaf of connected components is $F$;
\item after stackification, the visible components over $a$ are naturally indexed by $\Lambda$, and the component indexed by $\lambda$ has Cech cocycle $\alpha_\lambda$ on $\mathcal U$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The sheaf $A_{\mathcal U}$ is fixed as part of the formal site interface in
\Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}(iii)--(iv). Since every
nonempty object of the formal site is represented by a connected
contractible intersection of the good cover, locally constant
$A$-valued functions on it are exactly constant functions with value in
$A$; the strict initial object has only the zero section. Thus the
coefficient notation $A$ below is shorthand for the actual sheaf sections
$A_{\mathcal U}(W)$ on nonempty basic objects, not for the
identity-restriction constant presheaf on an arbitrary site; equivalently,
we are using \Cref{lem:formal-site-constant-sheaf-banding} throughout.
In particular, whenever an isomorphism or automorphism presheaf below is
written as \(A\) on nonempty objects and \(0\) on \(\emptyset\), it is
defined by the displayed identification with
\(A_{\mathcal U}|_{\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W}\). The prestack condition is
therefore a sheaf condition for a genuine sheaf on each slice, not a
claim that the identity-restriction constant presheaf on the index
category is already a sheaf.

For a nonempty proper formal intersection object $W$ and a fixed
$\lambda\notin\Lambda_0$, the displayed formula makes every morphism
invertible. The identity at $x_i^{(\lambda,W)}$ is $(0,i,i)$ because the
cocycle is normalized on repeated indices. The inverse of $(a,i,j)$ is
$(-a,j,i)$, again because the repeated-index normalization gives
$\alpha_{\lambda,iji}=0$. Associativity of
\[
(c,k,l)\circ\bigl((b,j,k)\circ(a,i,j)\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\bigl((c,k,l)\circ(b,j,k)\bigr)\circ(a,i,j)
\]
is equivalent, after cancelling $a+b+c$, to
\[
\alpha_{\lambda,ijk}+\alpha_{\lambda,ikl}
=
\alpha_{\lambda,jkl}+\alpha_{\lambda,ijl},
\]
which is the displayed cocycle identity, interpreted under the alternating
ordered-triple convention just fixed. This proves that each fibre is a
groupoid. If $V\to W$, then $I(W)\subseteq I(V)$, so every object and
morphism named over $W$ has the same name over $V$. The restriction maps
therefore preserve labels and restrict coefficients through the map
\(A_{\mathcal U}(W)\to A_{\mathcal U}(V)\). Under the connected
intersection identifications these coefficient restrictions are the
identity maps \(A\to A\) on nonempty objects; for restrictions to
\(\emptyset\) they are the unique maps to \(0\). Thus the same ordered
simplex indices are used wherever a nonempty coefficient is present, so
restrictions preserve identities, inverses, and composition. Since
inclusions \(I(W)\subseteq I(V)\) are transitive and
\(A_{\mathcal U}\) is a sheaf with functorial restrictions, restrictions
compose strictly. This proves \textup{(i)} and \textup{(ii)}.

For each pair of objects in a twisted fibre over a nonempty object $W$, the
isomorphism presheaf on the slice \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W\) is defined
objectwise by
\[
V\longmapsto A_{\mathcal U}(V),
\]
with restrictions those of the sheaf \(A_{\mathcal U}\), whenever the two
restricted labels are both defined; over \(\emptyset\) this gives the
single section of \(A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0\). On connected nonempty
basic intersections this is the coefficient notation \(A\), but the
presheaf itself is the restriction of the genuine constant sheaf
\(A_{\mathcal U}\), not the identity-restriction constant presheaf on the
index category. By
\Cref{lem:formal-site-constant-sheaf-banding}, it is therefore a sheaf.
The same argument applies to automorphisms,
where the distinguished zero coefficient gives a canonical identification
\[
\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}(x_i^{(\lambda,W)})
\cong A_{\mathcal U}|_{\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}/W}.
\]
Thus the prestack condition and the banding use the genuine constant sheaf
$A_{\mathcal U}$. The zero summands are already stacks by the same
constant-sheaf descent argument: their isomorphism and automorphism
presheaves are literally the corresponding slice restrictions of
\(A_{\mathcal U}\). Taking the finite coproduct over nonempty objects and
the shared terminal fibre over $\emptyset$ preserves the prestack
condition: over nonempty objects this is the usual finite coproduct of
prestacks in groupoids, while the strict initial object has only the unique
matching datum. This proves \textup{(iii)} without any appeal to a
constant presheaf sheaf condition.

For every nonempty proper intersection object, each twisted summand is
connected and each zero summand is one connected component; there are no
morphisms between distinct summands. Over $\emptyset$ the shared terminal
fibre has the unique empty-local object, matching $F(\emptyset)=\{\ast\}$.
Hence
\[
\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P)(U)\cong\Lambda
\]
for every nonempty proper intersection object $U\to a$. Over $a$, the
nonzero summands have empty fibre and the zero summands have one object, so
\[
\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P)(a)\cong\Lambda_0.
\]
These identifications are compatible with the restriction maps, giving \textup{(iv)}.

By \Cref{lem:formal-label-sheafification}, applied to the displayed
presheaf \(F\) and the subset \(\Lambda_0\subseteq\Lambda\), the
sheafification of \(F\) has terminal sections indexed by \(\Lambda\). By
\Cref{prop:stackification-components}, stackification identifies
\[
\pi_0(a(\mathfrak P))\cong aF,
\]
so the visible components over $a$ are indexed by $\Lambda$.

For a fixed $\lambda$, take on $U_i$ the object $x_i^{(\lambda,U_i)}$ if $\lambda\notin\Lambda_0$ and the distinguished constant object if $\lambda\in\Lambda_0$. On $U_{ij}$ take the morphism $(0,i,j)$ in the nonzero case and the identity in the zero case. The triple defect is
\[
(0,i,k)^{-1}\circ(0,j,k)\circ(0,i,j)
=
(\alpha_{\lambda,ijk},i,i)
\]
in the twisted case and is $0$ in the constant case. Under the band identification this is exactly $\alpha_{\lambda,ijk}$. Thus the Cech obstruction cocycle of the $\lambda$-component is $\alpha_\lambda$, proving \textup{(v)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Realization of prescribed branchwise hidden subgroups on a formal \v{C}ech site]\label{thm:prescribed-branch-realization}
Let
\[
\mathcal U=\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I}
\]
be a finite good-cover presentation in a pullback-based slice site $\mathcal C_a$ whose nonempty \v{C}ech nerve
\[
N:=N(\mathcal U)
\]
is connected. Equivalently, the realization of the formal cover is a
paracompact space homotopy equivalent to $|N|$ and all nonempty finite
intersections of members of $\mathcal U$ are acyclic for constant
coefficients. Let $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$ be the formal \v{C}ech site of
\Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}; it includes the strict
initial object $\emptyset$ for all empty intersections, so disjoint cover
members have a pullback in the site even though they do not contribute a
nonzero finite-nerve cochain. Let $A$ be a finite abelian group, and let
$A_{\mathcal U}$ denote the genuine constant sheaf on
$\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$ whose value on each nonempty basic intersection
is identified with $A$. View $A_{\mathcal U}$ as the automorphism band of
the realization prestack constructed below, and assume
\[
H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0.
\]
Use the Leray comparison isomorphism of
\Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}(iv). Let $\Lambda$ be a finite nonempty set, and let
\[
\phi_\lambda:H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\to A
\]
be a nonzero homomorphism for each $\lambda\in\Lambda$. Then, relative to the
admissible-expansion convention of
\Cref{prop:admissible-layer-expansion-interface}, there exist a one-state
admissible $\mathbb L_2/\mathbb L_3$ expansion $M$ with state $p$, a distinguished admitted reference
$r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ with $[r]_p=a$, a local slice site for $r$ equal to
$\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, a local object functor $F_{p,s}$ on
$\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$, and a realization prestack
\[
\mathfrak P_r\to \mathcal C_{\mathcal U}
\]
with band $A_{\mathcal U}$ such that, for $\mathfrak L_r:=a(\mathfrak P_r)$, the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item
\[
\Vis_{p,s}(r)\cong \Lambda;
\]
\item under the identification in \textup{(i)}, for each $\lambda\in\Lambda$ the corresponding component gerbe $\mathfrak G_\lambda\subseteq \mathfrak L_r$ has branch obstruction class
\[
\omega_\lambda\in H^2(N,A)
\]
satisfying
\[
\mathrm{ev}_{\omega_\lambda}=\phi_\lambda,
\qquad
K_\lambda=\operatorname{Im}(\phi_\lambda);
\]
\item $\mathfrak P_r$ is globally conservative at $a$ by the vacuous terminal-fibre case
\[
\mathfrak P_r(a)=\varnothing=\mathfrak L_r(a),
\]
and
\[
M,p\Vdash \Null^{\mathrm{glue}}_s(r);
\]
\item the aggregate visibility quotients satisfy
\[
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{\mathrm{br}}(r)\cong A\Big/\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\operatorname{Im}(\phi_\lambda),
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{vis}}^{\mathrm{unif}}(r)\cong A\Big/\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\operatorname{Im}(\phi_\lambda).
\]
\end{enumerate}
In particular, within the fixed finite-nerve framework above, every finite family of subgroups of $A$ arising as images of homomorphisms
\[
H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\to A
\]
is realized as the family of intrinsic hidden subgroups of the visible branches of one admitted reference.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since
\[
H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0,
\]
the universal coefficient sequence reduces to an isomorphism
\[
\eta_A:H^2(N,A)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Hom}(H_2(N,\mathbb Z),A).
\]
For each $\lambda\in\Lambda$, choose the unique class
\[
\omega_\lambda\in H^2(N,A)
\]
with
\[
\eta_A(\omega_\lambda)=\phi_\lambda,
\]
and choose a normalized simplicial cocycle
\[
\alpha_\lambda\in Z^2(N,A)
\]
representing $\omega_\lambda$, with the alternating ordered-triple extension
of \Cref{lem:twisted-branch-prestack-model}.

By \Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}(i)--(iv), pass to the
formal \v{C}ech site $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$ appearing in the statement.
The cover $\mathcal U$, its nerve, and the constant-band finite cochain
data are the same, and Leray comparison identifies
$H^2(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U},A_{\mathcal U})$ with $H^2(N,A)$. The derived branch class
used below is therefore the actual derived class corresponding to the
chosen finite-nerve cocycle.

Define the local object functor
\[
F_{p,s}:\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}^{op}\to\mathsf{Set}
\]
by
\[
F_{p,s}(U):=\Lambda
\qquad
\text{for every nonempty proper intersection object }U\to a,
\]
\[
F_{p,s}(a):=\varnothing,
\]
and
\[
F_{p,s}(\emptyset):=\{\ast\}.
\]
Restrictions between nonempty proper intersection objects are identities,
and every restriction to $\emptyset$ is the unique map. This is the
presheaf \(F_{\Lambda_0}\) of
\Cref{lem:formal-label-sheafification} with \(\Lambda_0=\varnothing\).
That lemma uses the plus--plus description of sheafification, not a
one-step matching-family replacement, and it treats pullbacks equal to
\(\emptyset\) as strict initial objects carrying only the unique section.
Since the nonempty nerve is connected, it gives
\[
\bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a)\cong \Lambda.
\]

For each $\lambda\in\Lambda$, define a category fibred in groupoids
\[
\mathfrak P_\lambda\to \mathcal C_{\mathcal U}
\]
by the nonzero-summand construction of
\Cref{lem:twisted-branch-prestack-model}. Thus
$\mathfrak P_\lambda(a)=\varnothing$; over every nonempty proper formal
intersection object $W$ its objects are
$x_i^{(\lambda,W)}$ for $i\in I(W):=\{i\in I\mid W\to U_i\}$; its morphism
sets are the sheaf sections $A_{\mathcal U}(W)$, identified with \(A\) on
connected nonempty \(W\); and composition is twisted by the normalized
cocycle $\alpha_\lambda$ under this identification. Over $\emptyset$ the
fibre is terminal because \(A_{\mathcal U}(\emptyset)=0\). The groupoid
laws, strict functoriality, prestack
condition, and genuine constant-band automorphism-sheaf condition are
exactly \Cref{lem:twisted-branch-prestack-model}(i)--(iii), whose banding
uses \Cref{lem:formal-site-constant-sheaf-banding}.

Let
\[
\mathfrak P_r:=\coprod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak P_\lambda.
\]
As in \Cref{lem:twisted-branch-prestack-model}, this is the fibrewise
coproduct over nonempty objects with the shared terminal fibre over
$\emptyset$.
The verification that this is a fibred prestack, that its restrictions are strictly functorial, that its automorphism sheaves are the genuine constant band $A_{\mathcal U}$, and that the normalized cocycle identities give the groupoid laws is precisely \Cref{lem:twisted-branch-prestack-model} with $\Lambda_0=\varnothing$. On every nonempty proper intersection object $U\to a$, each summand $\mathfrak P_\lambda(U)$ is connected, while there are no morphisms between distinct summands. Consequently,
\[
\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P_r)(U)\cong \Lambda
\qquad
\text{for every nonempty proper }U\to a,
\]
and
\[
\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P_r)(a)=\varnothing.
\]
Over $\emptyset$ the shared terminal fibre gives
$\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P_r)(\emptyset)\cong\{\ast\}=F_{p,s}(\emptyset)$.
Hence
\[
\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P_r)\cong F_{p,s}.
\]
By \Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}(v)--(vi), the functor
$F_{p,s}$ and the prestack $\mathfrak P_r$ determine a one-state semantic
model $M$ with admitted reference $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ at its unique state $p$,
with reference class $[r]_p=a$, local slice site $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$,
local object functor $F_{p,s}$, and realization prestack $\mathfrak P_r$ with
genuine constant band $A_{\mathcal U}$. This admissibility is exactly the expansion convention of
\Cref{prop:admissible-layer-expansion-interface}; no additional semantic
closure axiom for freely adjoining local-object or prestack data is being used.
At this point all semantic interfaces required by the statement have been checked: the admitted reference is $r$; the formal \v{C}ech site is $\mathcal C_{\mathcal U}$; the local object functor is the displayed $F_{p,s}$; the prestack band is the genuine constant sheaf $A_{\mathcal U}$; the cover $\mathcal U$ is compared with derived cohomology by the Leray isomorphism of \Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}(iv); the visible classes will be the sheafified terminal sections of $F_{p,s}$; and the forcing clauses for $\CompSec_s$, $\Sec_s$, and $\Null_s^{\mathrm{glue}}$ are read from these compatible-section and terminal-section data.
By \Cref{prop:stackification-components},
\[
\pi_0(\mathfrak L_r)\cong a_{p,s}F_{p,s},
\]
so the visible value classes at $a$ are exactly
\[
\Vis_{p,s}(r)\cong \bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a)\cong \Lambda.
\]
This proves \textup{(i)}.

For each $\lambda\in\Lambda$, let $\mathfrak G_\lambda$ be the full substack of $\mathfrak L_r$ determined by the visible branch $\lambda$. On the cover $\mathcal U$, take the local objects
\[
x_i^{(\lambda)}:=x_i^{(\lambda,U_i)}
\]
and, on each overlap $U_{ij}$, take the distinguished isomorphism
\[
\phi_{ij}^{(\lambda)}:=(0,i,j):x_i^{(\lambda)}|_{U_{ij}}\xrightarrow{\sim}x_j^{(\lambda)}|_{U_{ij}}.
\]
Then on every triple overlap $U_{ijk}$,
\[
\bigl(\phi_{ik}^{(\lambda)}\bigr)^{-1}\circ
\phi_{jk}^{(\lambda)}\circ
\phi_{ij}^{(\lambda)}
=
(\alpha_{\lambda,ijk},i,i),
\]
which identifies with $\alpha_{\lambda,ijk}\in A$ under the automorphism-band identification. Therefore the \v{C}ech cocycle produced by \Cref{thm:cech-bridge-compatible-realizations} is exactly $\alpha_\lambda$, and the branch obstruction class of $\mathfrak G_\lambda$ is
\[
\omega_\lambda=[\alpha_\lambda].
\]
This is the stackification-component compatibility clause of \Cref{lem:twisted-branch-prestack-model} written on the cover $\mathcal U$.
Thus the finite-nerve comparison uses exactly the fixed good cover
$\mathcal U$, the constant band $A$, and the normalized cocycle
$\alpha_\lambda$ supplied by the homomorphism $\phi_\lambda$; the passage
to derived $H^2(\mathcal C_{\mathcal U},A_{\mathcal U})$ is the Leray comparison of
\Cref{lem:generated-subsite-model-interface}(iv).
Since
\[
\mathrm{ev}_{\omega_\lambda}=\eta_A(\omega_\lambda)=\phi_\lambda,
\]
one gets
\[
K_\lambda=\operatorname{Im}(\phi_\lambda).
\]
This proves \textup{(ii)}.

Because each $\phi_\lambda$ is nonzero and $\eta_A$ is an isomorphism, each class $\omega_\lambda$ is nonzero. By \Cref{prop:derived-branch-classification}, every component gerbe $\mathfrak G_\lambda$ is therefore non-neutral, and \Cref{lem:terminal-fibre-neutrality} gives
\[
\mathfrak G_\lambda(a)=\varnothing
\qquad
(\forall \lambda\in\Lambda).
\]
Hence
\[
\mathfrak L_r(a)=\varnothing.
\]
Since also
\[
\mathfrak P_r(a)=\varnothing,
\]
the stackification functor on the terminal fibre is
\[
(\iota_r)_a:\mathfrak P_r(a)\to \mathfrak L_r(a),
\]
that is, the empty functor $\varnothing\to\varnothing$, and it is essentially surjective. Thus $\mathfrak P_r$ is globally conservative at $a$.

Moreover,
\[
\bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a)\cong \Lambda\neq\varnothing,
\]
and the cover $\mathcal U$ carries an explicit strict matching family:
choose any $\lambda\in\Lambda$ and put the section $\lambda\in
F_{p,s}(U_i)=\Lambda$ on every member $U_i$. Restrictions between
nonempty proper intersections are identities, and restrictions to
$\emptyset$ are unique, so the family agrees on every pullback overlap.
Hence \Cref{def:existence-levels}(ii) gives
\[
M,p\Vdash \CompSec_s(r).
\]
On the other hand,
\[
F_{p,s}(a)=\varnothing,
\]
so
\[
M,p\nVdash \Sec_s(r).
\]
Since $r$ is admitted by construction, it follows that
\[
M,p\Vdash \Null^{\mathrm{glue}}_s(r).
\]
This proves \textup{(iii)}.

Finally, \textup{(iv)} is \Cref{thm:branch-sensitive-visible-quotient,thm:branch-uniform-visible-quotient} applied to the family
\[
K_\lambda=\operatorname{Im}(\phi_\lambda)
\qquad
(\lambda\in\Lambda).
\]
The last sentence is the same statement rewritten in terms of the images of the chosen homomorphisms.
\end{proof}

The preceding realization theorem forces every branch to be non-neutral. The next theorem removes that restriction and identifies the neutral branches exactly with the zero homomorphisms.
