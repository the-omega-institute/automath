\section{The wedge-of-spheres realization corollary}
\label{sec:wedge-application}

Fix \(\beta\ge1\), and let \(K_\beta\) be a finite connected simplicial
complex with
\[
 |K_\beta|\simeq\bigvee_{j=1}^{\beta}S^2.
\]
For each \(m\ge0\), take the open-star cover of
\(\operatorname{Sd}^mK_\beta\), and let \(\mathcal B_\beta\) be the
all-subdivision basis site of
\Cref{prop:finite-site-comparison}(iii).

\subsection*{The selected geometric presentation}

The nerve of each open-star cover is canonically
\(\operatorname{Sd}^mK_\beta\).  A nonempty finite intersection of open
stars is the open star of the simplex spanned by the corresponding vertices,
so it is connected and contractible.  Consequently the selected covers
satisfy the connected-overlap hypothesis and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wedge-homology}
 H_1(N(\mathcal U^{(m)}),\mathbb Z)=0,
 \qquad
 H_2(N(\mathcal U^{(m)}),\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^\beta.
\end{equation}
The subdivision maps form the filtered cofinal system used in
\Cref{prop:finite-site-comparison}(iii).

For two component classes with homological images \(K_1,K_2\le G\), the
kernel of the aggregate comparison is
\[
 \frac{K_1+K_2}{K_1\cap K_2}.
\]
We ask when the prescribed realization gives the band itself by imposing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complementary-images}
 K_1\cap K_2=0,
 \qquad K_1+K_2=G,
 \qquad K_1,K_2\ne0.
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Two-component realization on a wedge of \(2\)-spheres]
\label{thm:wedge-sphere-two-component-label-loss}
Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group and let \(d(G)\) be its minimal number
of generators.  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item \(G\ne0\), \(d(G)\le2\beta\), and \(G\) is not cyclic of
      prime-power order;
\item on one of the selected finite open-star covers in
      \(\mathcal B_\beta\), the prestack construction of
      \Cref{thm:prescribed-component-image-construction} has two non-neutral
      component gerbes whose homological images satisfy
      \eqref{eq:complementary-images}.
\end{enumerate}
When these conditions hold,
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{lab}}\cong G,
 \qquad Q_{\mathrm{com}}=0,
 \qquad
 \ker(Q_{\mathrm{lab}}\to Q_{\mathrm{com}})\cong G,
\]
and the constructed component presheaf has matching without amalgamation.
The assertion is relative to this selected finite-site realization; it is not
a presentation-independent classification of gerbes or finite abelian groups.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Assume (ii).  By \eqref{eq:wedge-homology} and
\Cref{prop:uct-aggregate-package}(iv), each \(K_i\) is generated by at most
\(\beta\) elements.  The first two equalities in
\eqref{eq:complementary-images} give an internal direct sum
\(G=K_1\oplus K_2\), so
\[
 d(G)\le d(K_1)+d(K_2)\le2\beta.
\]
Both summands are nonzero; hence \(G\) is decomposable.  The indecomposable
finite abelian groups are exactly the cyclic groups of prime-power order
\cite[Chap.~12]{DummitFoote2004}, proving (i).

Conversely, assume (i).  If \(d(G)=r\ge2\), choose an invariant-factor
decomposition
\[
 G\cong C_{n_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{n_r},
 \qquad n_1\mid n_2\mid\cdots\mid n_r.
\]
Let \(t=\min\{\beta,r-1\}\) and put
\[
 K_1=C_{n_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{n_t},
 \qquad
 K_2=C_{n_{t+1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{n_r}.
\]
The two groups are nonzero, \(d(K_1)\le\beta\), and
\(d(K_2)=r-t\le\beta\) because \(r\le2\beta\).

If \(d(G)=1\), then \(G\) is cyclic.  Its order is not a prime power, so
there are coprime integers \(a,b>1\) with \(|G|=ab\).  The Chinese remainder
theorem gives
\[
 G\cong C_a\oplus C_b,
\]
and both summands are nonzero and singly generated.  Thus in either case
\(G=K_1\oplus K_2\) with \(d(K_i)\le\beta\).

Choose maps \(\mathbb Z^\beta\to G\) with images \(K_1\) and \(K_2\), and
transport them across \eqref{eq:wedge-homology}.  Applying
\Cref{thm:prescribed-component-image-construction} with two labels produces
the required component gerbes.  Their evaluation maps are nonzero, so both
gerbes are non-neutral, and the component presheaf has matching without
amalgamation.  Finally, \eqref{eq:aggregate-quotients} and
\eqref{eq:aggregate-exact-sequence} give the three quotient identities.
\end{proof}

For \(\beta=1\), the example
\[
 G=C_4\oplus C_2,
 \qquad K_1=C_4\oplus0,
 \qquad K_2=0\oplus C_2
\]
has two cyclic complementary images, each realized from
\(H_2\cong\mathbb Z\).  In contrast, \(C_2^3\) needs three generators and
fails the bound \(d(G)\le2\).
