\section{The wedge-of-spheres realization corollary}
\label{sec:wedge-application}

Fix \(\beta\ge1\), and let \(K_\beta\) be a finite connected simplicial
complex with
\[
 |K_\beta|\simeq\bigvee_{j=1}^{\beta}S^2.
\]
For each \(m\ge0\), take the open-star cover of
\(\operatorname{Sd}^mK_\beta\), and let \(\mathcal B_\beta\) be the
all-subdivision basis site of
\Cref{prop:finite-site-comparison}(iii).  Every nonempty finite overlap is
contractible, hence connected, and the coherent coefficient trivializations
are those of the genuine constant sheaf.  Thus the hypothesis omitted by an
ordinary-nerve formulation with disconnected overlaps is satisfied here.

\subsection*{The selected geometric presentation}

The vertices of \(\operatorname{Sd}^mK_\beta\) index its open-star cover.
A finite family of open stars has nonempty intersection exactly when the
corresponding vertices span a simplex.  The nerve of the cover is therefore
canonically isomorphic to \(\operatorname{Sd}^mK_\beta\).  In particular,
\[
 |N(\mathcal U^{(m)})|
 \cong|\operatorname{Sd}^mK_\beta|
 \cong|K_\beta|.
\]
The homology groups used below are consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wedge-homology}
 H_1(N(\mathcal U^{(m)}),\mathbb Z)=0,
 \qquad
 H_2(N(\mathcal U^{(m)}),\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^\beta.
\end{equation}

If vertices \(v_0,\dots,v_q\) span a simplex, the intersection of their open
stars is the open star of that simplex.  It deformation retracts to the
barycentre and is connected and contractible.  If they do not span a
simplex, their intersection is empty and is represented by the strict
initial object of the finite intersection site.  Hence each selected cover
satisfies the connected-overlap hypothesis literally, not only after passage
to a component nerve.

The subdivision maps provide a filtered system of refinements.  The basis
comparison does not assert that a homological image subgroup is independent
of every possible refinement zigzag.  It says that each class on the
underlying polyhedron has representatives on sufficiently fine selected
covers and that the associated derived class is the image of those
representatives.  The corollary below fixes one such cover and applies the
realization theorem there.

For two component classes with homological images \(K_1,K_2\le G\), the
kernel of the aggregate comparison is, by
\eqref{eq:aggregate-exact-sequence},
\[
 \frac{K_1+K_2}{K_1\cap K_2}.
\]
We ask when the selected realization construction makes this kernel equal to
the band itself by requiring
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complementary-images}
 K_1\cap K_2=0,
 \qquad K_1+K_2=G,
 \qquad K_1,K_2\ne0.
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Two-component realization on a wedge of \(2\)-spheres]
\label{thm:wedge-sphere-two-component-label-loss}
Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group and let \(d(G)\) be its minimal number
of generators.  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item \(G\ne0\), \(d(G)\le2\beta\), and \(G\) is not cyclic of
      prime-power order;
\item on one of the selected finite open-star covers in
      \(\mathcal B_\beta\), the prestack construction of
      \Cref{thm:prescribed-component-image-construction} has two non-neutral
      component gerbes whose homological images satisfy
      \eqref{eq:complementary-images}.
\end{enumerate}
When these conditions hold,
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{lab}}\cong G,
 \qquad Q_{\mathrm{com}}=0,
 \qquad
 \ker(Q_{\mathrm{lab}}\to Q_{\mathrm{com}})\cong G,
\]
and the constructed component presheaf has matching without amalgamation.
The assertion is relative to this selected finite-site realization; it is not
a presentation-independent classification of gerbes or finite abelian groups.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For every selected cover, the nerve is a barycentric subdivision of
\(K_\beta\).  The nerve and basis-site comparisons of
\Cref{prop:finite-site-comparison} give
\[
 H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0,
 \qquad
 H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^\beta,
\]
and identify the finite Cech representatives with the corresponding derived
classes on \(\mathcal B_\beta\).

Assume (ii).  By \Cref{prop:uct-aggregate-package}(iv), each \(K_i\) is
generated by at most \(\beta\) elements.  The complementary-image equations
give
\[
 G=K_1\oplus K_2.
\]
Hence \(d(G)\le d(K_1)+d(K_2)\le2\beta\).  Since both summands are nonzero,
\(G\) is decomposable.  The indecomposable finite abelian groups are exactly
the cyclic groups of prime-power order \cite[Chap.~12]{DummitFoote2004}, so
(i) follows.

For completeness, the generator inequality may be checked prime by prime.
For a finite abelian group \(H\), let
\[
 r_p(H)=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(H/pH).
\]
Then \(d(H)=\max_p r_p(H)\).  Direct sums give
\[
 r_p(K_1\oplus K_2)=r_p(K_1)+r_p(K_2),
\]
and hence
\[
 d(G)
 =\max_p\bigl(r_p(K_1)+r_p(K_2)\bigr)
 \le d(K_1)+d(K_2)
 \le2\beta.
\]
This proves the numerical necessity without choosing an invariant-factor
basis.

Conversely, suppose (i).  If \(d(G)=r\ge2\), choose an invariant-factor
decomposition
\[
 G\cong C_{n_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{n_r},
 \qquad n_1\mid n_2\mid\cdots\mid n_r,
\]
and let \(t=\min\{\beta,r-1\}\).  Put
\[
 K_1=C_{n_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{n_t},
 \qquad
 K_2=C_{n_{t+1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{n_r}.
\]
Both groups are nonzero.  We have \(d(K_1)\le\beta\), while
\(d(K_2)=r-t\le\beta\) because \(r\le2\beta\).

If \(d(G)=1\), then \(G\) is cyclic.  Its order is not a prime power, so it
has coprime factors \(a,b>1\).  The Chinese remainder theorem gives
\[
 G\cong C_a\oplus C_b.
\]
Taking \(K_1=C_a\) and \(K_2=C_b\) again gives two nonzero summands, each
generated by one element.  Thus in either case
\(G=K_1\oplus K_2\), with \(d(K_i)\le\beta\).

In the case \(r\ge2\), the chosen invariant-factor decomposition makes the
generator counts transparent.  The first block has \(t\le\beta\) cyclic
factors.  If \(r\le\beta\), then \(t=r-1\) and the second block is cyclic.
If \(r>\beta\), then \(t=\beta\) and
\[
 r-t=r-\beta\le\beta
\]
because \(r=d(G)\le2\beta\).  Thus neither block is empty and both satisfy
the rank constraint required by the UCT package.

In the cyclic case, write
\[
 |G|=\prod_{p\mid |G|}p^{e_p}
\]
with at least two distinct primes.  Partition the prime set into two
nonempty subsets \(P_1,P_2\), put
\[
 a=\prod_{p\in P_1}p^{e_p},
 \qquad
 b=\prod_{p\in P_2}p^{e_p},
\]
and use \(C_{ab}\cong C_a\oplus C_b\).  This gives the required summands
without assuming a particular factorization in advance.

Choose homomorphisms
\[
 \phi_i:\mathbb Z^\beta\longrightarrow G
\]
with images \(K_i\), and transport them across
\(H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^\beta\).  The finite-site realization
theorem, with label set \(\{1,2\}\), produces the two component gerbes and
their prescribed images.  Both maps are nonzero, so both gerbes are
non-neutral and the component presheaf has matching without amalgamation.
Finally, \eqref{eq:aggregate-quotients} and
\eqref{eq:aggregate-exact-sequence} give the three displayed quotient
identities.

To make the maps explicit, choose generators
\[
 K_i=\langle k_{i1},\dots,k_{i r_i}\rangle,
 \qquad r_i\le\beta,
\]
and send the first \(r_i\) standard basis vectors of \(\mathbb Z^\beta\)
to the displayed generators, sending the remaining basis vectors to zero.
The UCT isomorphism selects classes \(\omega_i\in H^2(N,G)\), and the
construction of
\Cref{thm:prescribed-component-image-construction} selects normalized
cocycles and places them in the two labelled summands of one prestack.
Since \(K_i\ne0\), neither evaluation map is zero, so both Giraud classes are
nonzero in the \(H_1=0\) regime.
\end{proof}

For \(\beta=1\), the statement says that a nonzero finite abelian group is
attained in this two-component manner precisely when it splits as a direct
sum of two nonzero cyclic groups.  This includes cyclic groups whose orders
have at least two distinct prime divisors and noncyclic two-generated groups,
but excludes every cyclic \(p\)-group.  This small case illustrates that the
criterion is the sharp arithmetic endpoint of the chosen realization, not a
new structural theorem about finite abelian groups.

\subsection*{Low-rank cases}

For \(\beta=1\), both images must be cyclic.  The admissible groups fall into
two families.

First, a cyclic group \(C_n\) is admissible exactly when \(n\) has at least
two distinct prime divisors.  If \(n=ab\) with \(\gcd(a,b)=1\) and
\(a,b>1\), take
\[
 K_1=\langle b\rangle\cong C_a,
 \qquad
 K_2=\langle a\rangle\cong C_b
\]
inside \(C_{ab}\).  They have zero intersection and generate the group.
If \(n=p^e\), the subgroup lattice is a chain and no two nonzero subgroups
have zero intersection.

Second, a noncyclic two-generated group is admissible whenever it decomposes
as a direct sum of two nonzero cyclic groups.  For example,
\[
 G=C_4\oplus C_2,\qquad
 K_1=C_4\oplus0,\qquad K_2=0\oplus C_2.
\]
Each image is generated by one element of \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z\).  On the
other hand, \(C_2^3\) requires three generators and violates
\(d(G)\le2\), even though it has many complementary subgroups.

For \(\beta=2\), groups with at most four generators are permitted, provided
they are not cyclic of prime-power order.  For instance,
\[
 G=C_2^4
\]
is realized by the complementary rank-two subgroups
\[
 K_1=\langle e_1,e_2\rangle,
 \qquad
 K_2=\langle e_3,e_4\rangle.
\]
Each is the image of \(\mathbb Z^2\).  The bound is sharp:
\(C_2^5\) cannot be written as a sum of two subgroups each generated by at
most two elements, since their sum would have rank at most four.

Mixed-primary examples do not require the same decomposition at each prime.
Take
\[
 G=C_4\oplus C_2\oplus C_3.
\]
For \(\beta=1\), the decomposition
\[
 K_1=C_4,\qquad K_2=C_2\oplus C_3\cong C_6
\]
has two cyclic summands, so the group is already admissible.  This illustrates
why \(d(G)\) is the maximum primary rank rather than the total number of
cyclic factors appearing in an arbitrary decomposition.

In every example, the conclusion remains about the selected prestack
realization.  The arithmetic chooses possible image subgroups; the
finite-site theorem supplies component gerbes having those images.  Neither
step classifies all gerbes on the wedge or assigns such data to an unrelated
semantic model.
