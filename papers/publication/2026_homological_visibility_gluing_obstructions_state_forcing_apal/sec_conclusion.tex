\section{Concluding remarks}\label{sec:conclusion}

The paper separates three stages of the local-to-global problem. At the level of information states, \Cref{thm:pointwise-irreducibility} shows that compatible local sectionability and gluing-level nullity are invisible in the automorphism-invariant, constant-free, one-variable fragment considered here. This is not intended as a general expressivity theorem, but it does isolate the first place at which the local-object layer goes beyond the lower forcing language.

At the passage from set-valued to groupoid-valued data, \Cref{thm:canonical-lift-rigidity} shows that a slice-separated representative-rigid lift can be globally conservative only when the sheafification unit at the terminal fibre is surjective; under $H^1$-vanishing and slice-separatedness on the reference slice, this is equivalent to sheafiness of the local object functor at that fibre. Thus the gerbe-theoretic part of the theory is necessarily relative to additional lift data. \Cref{thm:intrinsic-branch-obstruction-extension} identifies the maximal canonicity available without an existence theorem: once the sheafified local object functor has been lifted to a banded realization stack, the branch obstruction class is intrinsic up to equivalence of such extensions. Visible value classes are then governed by the sheaf of connected components; on slice-separated reference slices, strict gluing failure is carried by the component gerbes of the visible branches.

For a finite abelian branch obstruction class, the presentation-object
subgroup \(K_\omega=\operatorname{Im}(\mathrm{ev}_\omega)\) and quotient
\(A_{\mathrm{vis}}^\omega=A/K_\omega\) capture the homological information
that survives at a fixed finite nerve presentation; across presentations
they form a groupoid-indexed invariant transported along chosen morphisms in
the finite nerve comparison groupoid. The branchwise comparison sequence and
the realization theorems show how these quotients behave when several
visible branches are present, under the fixed finite nerve, constant-band,
explicit \v{C}ech--derived comparison package, and \(H_1=0\) hypotheses used
in the construction. When in addition
\(H_2(N(\mathcal U),\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^\beta\), the realizable hidden
subgroups are exactly the subgroups generated by at most \(\beta\) elements.
The bouquet construction supplies an explicit chosen class of good-cover
examples in which the pure two-branch resolution kernels can be described
completely: the nonzero finite abelian groups that occur are exactly those
with \(d(G)\le2\beta\) which are not cyclic \(p\)-groups. The appendix
contains only supplementary material: the refinement-dynamic extension,
finite-state complexity bounds, and the GHZ/Mermin lift used as an
illustrative chosen datum.

The hypothesis package is therefore deliberately visible: \textup{(RC)} for
local objects, \textup{(H1)}--\textup{(H4)} and global conservativity for
component gerbes, slice-separatedness whenever visible branches are used as
strict compatible local sections, slice-separatedness and $H^1$-vanishing for
terminal-fibre rigidity, and finite-nerve plus constant-band data together
with the explicit \v{C}ech--derived comparison package for homological
visibility.
This paper keeps that package in the pullback-based slice-site formulation; the
meet-semilattice presentation belongs to the companion APAL article. The
comparison between presentations is consequently groupoid-indexed and
zigzag-dependent, not a claim of presentation-free canonical representatives.
