\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The article starts with supplied finite-site data: a presheaf, an
abelian-banded prestack lift, and its stackification.  Giraud's component
construction then assigns the maximal component gerbe to each global section
of the component sheaf.  The use made of this standard input is narrow: under
slice separatedness and terminal essential surjectivity it compares the
strict presheaf predicate of matching without amalgamation with non-neutrality
of every global component.  Representative-rigidity shows why a strictly
pullback-stable choice of lift representatives is too restrictive in the
basic \(H^1\)-vanishing regime.

More sharply, stackification of a prestack is fully faithful.  Essential
surjectivity on a neutral terminal component therefore gives an equivalence
with the full neutral gerbe, whose object classes form an \(H^1\)-torsor and
whose object automorphisms are \(H^0\).  The marked terminal statement in
\Cref{thm:marked-terminal-torsor-form} retains those unavoidable ambiguities;
the pointed-atlas alternative of
\Cref{rem:pointed-atlas-terminal-form} is recorded separately and is not
combined with it.  These are sharp structural consequences of standard
torsor theory, not a presentation-localization result.

On a finite good-cover site, connected overlaps together with coherent
trivializations that identify every coefficient group with one fixed \(A\)
and every restriction map with \(\operatorname{id}_A\) permit the ordinary
nerve to carry the full constant-coefficient Cech complex.  The
all-subdivision basis comparison identifies the generated site with the
ordinary sheaf theory of the underlying polyhedron.  Within this fixed
setting, the standard Cech-cocycle gerbe construction can be packaged into a
single prestack whose component presheaf, terminal behavior, neutrality
pattern, and evaluation images are prescribed simultaneously.

UCT and elementary subgroup arithmetic then yield the aggregate quotients.
For the selected open-star covers of
\(\bigvee_{j=1}^{\beta}S^2\), the two-component realization criterion is
\[
 d(G)\le2\beta
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 G\text{ is not cyclic of prime-power order}.
\]
This is a sharp corollary of the selected realization, not a new
classification principle for finite abelian groups or gerbes.

Bare empirical models provide a support presheaf and its canonical split
stack, whose component gerbes are neutral.  The support does not determine a
preferred nonzero degree-two class: the datum is underdetermined when nonzero
classes exist and absent when the relevant \(H^2\) vanishes.  The relative
degree-one obstructions are indexed differently and are not recovered by the
component-section map.  Although that degree-one obstruction is incomplete,
the established operator-phase degree-two obstruction is subsumed by it on
their common algebraic domain.  The present article supplies no operation
from bare empirical-model data to a \(2\)-cocycle, so a non-split banded lift
and its class must be supplied before the finite-site results apply.

The resulting contribution has a definite boundary.  The following parts
are classical inputs:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{component over }v\text{ is a gerbe}
 &\text{Giraud III.2.1.5.3},\\
\text{neutrality}\Longleftrightarrow[\mathfrak G_v]=0
 &\text{abelian-banded gerbe classification},\\
\text{finite Cech class}\longrightarrow\text{UCT evaluation}
 &\text{Leray comparison and UCT},\\
\text{aggregate exact sequence}
 &\text{subgroup and quotient arithmetic.}
\end{array}
\]
They are stated together so that the hypotheses used by the applications
remain visible, not as separate advances.

The two proofs requiring construction beyond those inputs are terminal
rigidity and prescribed simultaneous realization.  The first identifies
where literal pullback stability removes a descent cocycle and where
\(H^1\)-vanishing removes the remaining Isom torsor.  The second constructs
the label presheaf and twisted groupoid fibres, verifies strict restrictions
and the genuine constant band, computes stackification components and
terminal objects, and recovers the selected Cech cocycles.  Their full proofs
make the claimed scope independently checkable within the article.

The technical appendix has a limited role.  Its presentation groupoid records
naturality only along specified comparison words and does not assert path
independence.  Its finite computations illustrate the UCT and subgroup
formulas.  Its lower-language example explains why the finite-site lift is
treated as input but does not construct that lift.

A genuinely presentation-independent theorem would require localization over
common refinements, including independence of refinement maps and zigzags and
the proof that the resulting cocycle-to-gerbe \(2\)-functor is a
biequivalence.  That full localization, rather than the terminal structural
statements above, is the substantive result not supplied by the present
article.

No crossed-module comparison is pursued: the present arguments begin after a
gerbe \(H^2\)-class and its Cech representative are supplied, and contain
neither the source-side degree-three chain model nor the comparison cochain
required to derive such a class from crossed-module data.
