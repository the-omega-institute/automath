\section{Concluding remarks}\label{sec:conclusion}

The paper separates three stages of the local-to-global problem. At the level
of information states, \Cref{thm:pointwise-irreducibility} shows that neither
existence of a matching family nor matching without amalgamation is definable
in the automorphism-invariant, constant-free, one-variable fragment considered
here. This is not a general expressivity theorem; it identifies the first
point at which the presheaf data exceed the lower language of universal team
satisfaction.

The passage from set-valued to groupoid-valued data necessarily depends on
additional lift data. By \Cref{thm:canonical-lift-rigidity}, a strictly
pullback-stable representative choice together with essential surjectivity on
the terminal fibre implies that the sheafification unit is surjective there.
Under $H^1$-vanishing and separatedness of the restriction to the slice site,
this is equivalent to the presheaf already satisfying the sheaf condition at
that fibre. For finite empirical models,
\Cref{thm:empirical-model-stack-interface} gives a canonical split banded
stack lift, but every component gerbe is neutral. The AMB obstruction is a
relative \v{C}ech $H^1$ torsor class indexed by a local section, whereas the
obstruction here is a derived Giraud $H^2$ class indexed by a global component
section. Only the support-level comparison is functorial, and neither class
recovers the other in general.

For a fixed finite nerve representative, the homological image
$K_\omega=\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{ev}_\omega)$ and homological
cokernel $Q_\omega=A/K_\omega$ record the contribution detected on $H_2$.
Across presentations they transport along chosen arrows in the
presentation-comparison groupoid; no independence of the chosen zigzag is
asserted. For several global component sections, the label-aware and
common-character quotients are related by the component-forgetting exact
sequence. Under the fixed finite-nerve, constant-band,
\v{C}ech-to-derived comparison, and $H_1=0$ hypotheses, the construction
theorems attain exactly the componentwise image subgroups permitted by
second homology.

When $H_2(N(\mathcal U),\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^\beta$, these are precisely
the subgroups generated by at most $\beta$ elements. The chosen open-star good
covers of a wedge of $\beta$ $2$-spheres therefore give the exact
classification: the nonzero finite abelian groups occurring as two-component
label-loss kernels with complementary nonzero image subgroups are those with
$d(G)\leq2\beta$ that are not cyclic $p$-groups. This construction does not
transfer to bare empirical models, whose canonical split lift has zero
homological image subgroups.

\begin{remark}[External crossed-module comparison interface]
\label{rem:crossed-module-comparison-interface}
Let
\[
1\longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow \widehat K\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow 1
\]
be a central extension, let $K\trianglelefteq G$, put $Q=G/K$, and suppose
that $G\to Q$ admits the local sections required in locally continuous or
locally smooth group cohomology and that the conjugation action lifts so that
$\widehat K\to G$ is a crossed module.  Neeb, Wagemann, and Wockel construct
a \v{C}echization homomorphism
\[
\tau:H^3_{\mathrm{loc}}(Q,Z)\longrightarrow
\check H^2(Q,\underline Z)
\]
and ask whether the image of the crossed-module characteristic class agrees,
up to a convention-dependent sign, with the lifting obstruction of the
principal $K$-bundle $G\to Q$ \cite[Problem~8.1(b)]{NeebWagemannWockel2013}.
The present paper supplies only the downstream analysis of a lifting-gerbe
$H^2$-class after a finite-nerve, constant finite-band presentation and its
\v{C}ech-to-derived comparison have been chosen.  It does not supply a chain
model for $H^3_{\mathrm{loc}}$, a representative of the crossed-module
characteristic class, or a chain homotopy comparing that representative with
the triple-overlap defect cocycle.  Consequently, neither the comparison
identity, its sign, nor its asserted naturality follows from the results
proved here.  Establishing such a comparison is an external open interface,
not a theorem or a construction begun in this paper.
\end{remark}

The hypotheses remain explicit throughout: \textup{(RC)} specifies pullbacks
of the presheaf data; \textup{(H1)}--\textup{(H4)} specify the banded lift,
representability, and \v{C}ech-to-derived comparison; separatedness is invoked
where global component sections are represented by matching families; and
$H^1$-vanishing is used for terminal-fibre rigidity. The formulation here uses
pullback-based slice sites. Comparisons of finite presentations are therefore
groupoid-indexed and zigzag-dependent, rather than presentation-free choices
of canonical representatives.
