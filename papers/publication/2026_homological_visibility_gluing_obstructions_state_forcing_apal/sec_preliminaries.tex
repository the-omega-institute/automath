\section{The finite-site interface}\label{sec:preliminaries}

The results use a site, a presheaf, and a banded prestack as input.  No
semantic construction of these data is assumed.  This section records the
small interface needed later and fixes the distinction between a matching
family and an amalgamation.

Let \(\mathcal C\) be a small site with pullbacks of covering families and
terminal object \(a\), and let
\[
 F:\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\mathbf {Set}
\]
be a presheaf.  If \(\mathcal U=\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I}\) is a cover, write
\(U_{ij}=U_i\times_aU_j\).  A \emph{strict matching family} on
\(\mathcal U\) is a family \(s_i\in F(U_i)\) satisfying
\[
 s_i|_{U_{ij}}=s_j|_{U_{ij}}
 \qquad(i,j\in I).
\]
It is an \emph{amalgamation} if there is \(s\in F(a)\) with
\(s|_{U_i}=s_i\) for every \(i\).  We use the abbreviations
\[
 \operatorname{Match}(F,a),\qquad
 \operatorname{Glob}(F,a),\qquad
 \operatorname{MWA}(F,a)
\]
for, respectively, existence of a strict matching family on some cover of
\(a\), nonemptiness of \(F(a)\), and
\[
 \operatorname{Match}(F,a)\ \text{and}\ F(a)=\varnothing.
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{MWA}\) means matching without amalgamation.  It is not
a claim about every matching family on every cover.

\subsection*{Three terminal levels}

Three sets occur at the terminal object and should not be identified without
hypotheses:
\[
 F(a),\qquad (aF)(a),\qquad
 \pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P)(a).
\]
The chosen lift identifies the first and third sets, whereas sheafification
maps the first to the second.  A member of \(F(a)\) is a strict global
presheaf section.  A member of \((aF)(a)\) is represented locally and may
have no strict representative in \(F(a)\).  A component section of the
stackification is again an element of \((aF)(a)\), but it need not be the
component of an actual object of \(\mathfrak L(a)\): the full stack over
that component can be a non-neutral gerbe.

This gives the sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-terminal-levels}
 \operatorname{Ob}(\mathfrak P(a))
 \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Ob}(\mathfrak L(a))
 \longrightarrow
 \pi_0(\mathfrak L)(a)
 \cong(aF)(a).
\end{equation}
The first map is stackification and the second takes a fibrewise isomorphism
class.  The composite on components is the sheafification unit
\[
 u_a:F(a)\longrightarrow(aF)(a).
\]
The last arrow in \eqref{eq:three-terminal-levels} need not be surjective:
a global section of a component sheaf is only a locally inhabited
component, while an object of \(\mathfrak L(a)\) is a global object in that
component.  Giraud's construction measures exactly this difference.

Terminal essential surjectivity concerns objects, not component sections.
It says that every object already present in \(\mathfrak L(a)\) comes from
\(\mathfrak P(a)\) up to isomorphism.  It does not say that every member of
\((aF)(a)\) is represented by an object of \(\mathfrak L(a)\).  The latter
assertion would force every global component gerbe to be neutral.

Write \(u:F\to aF\) for the sheafification unit.  We say that \(F\) is
\emph{separated on the slice over \(a\)} if, for every \(X\to a\), two
sections of \(F(X)\) which agree after restriction to a cover of \(X\) are
equal.  This stronger slice condition, rather than separatedness only at
\(a\), is used whenever equality in \(aF\) is returned to equality in
\(F\).

The slice qualifier is needed because a plus--plus representative of a
terminal sheaf section first gives equality only after a cover of an overlap.
That overlap is an object \(U_{ij}\to a\), and the equality must be returned
from \(aF(U_{ij})\) to \(F(U_{ij})\).  Separatedness only of
\(F(a)\to(aF)(a)\) does not provide this step.

Let \(\mathscr A\) be an abelian sheaf on \(\mathcal C\).  An
\emph{\(\mathscr A\)-banded prestack lift of \(F\)} is a prestack in
groupoids \(\mathfrak P\) with a fixed split cleavage, together with
compatible identifications
\[
 \pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P)\cong F,
 \qquad
 \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathfrak P}(x)\cong\mathscr A
\]
on every slice on which \(x\) is defined.  Let
\[
 \iota:\mathfrak P\longrightarrow\mathfrak L:=a(\mathfrak P)
\]
be stackification.  Standard compatibility of stackification with connected
components gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stackification-components-core}
 \pi_0(\mathfrak L)\cong aF,
\end{equation}
and the map induced by \(\iota\) on components is the sheafification unit.
We say that \(\iota\) is \emph{terminally essentially surjective} if
\(\mathfrak P(a)\to\mathfrak L(a)\) is essentially surjective.

The lift is \emph{representative-rigid} if there are maps
\[
 s_X:F(X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ob}(\mathfrak P(X))
\]
selecting a representative of every component and satisfying, for every
\(f:Y\to X\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict-representative-pullback}
 f^*s_X(t)=s_Y(f^*t)
\end{equation}
as a literal equality.  The equality is taken with respect to the fixed
split cleavage.  Ordinary choices of cartesian lifts are generally only
pseudofunctorial; the strictness in
\eqref{eq:strict-representative-pullback} is therefore a substantive extra
hypothesis, not a harmless choice of notation.  Split cleavages and their
relation to pseudofunctorial pullback are standard for fibred categories
\cite[Chap.~VI]{MacLaneMoerdijk1994}.

A componentwise representative choice alone would instead provide objects
\(s_X(t)\) and, for \(f:Y\to X\), some isomorphism
\[
 f^*s_X(t)\xrightarrow{\sim}s_Y(f^*t).
\]
On a composable pair \(Z\xrightarrow{g}Y\xrightarrow{f}X\), the two induced
isomorphisms from \((fg)^*s_X(t)\) to \(s_Z((fg)^*t)\) need not agree.
Their ratio is an automorphism, hence a section of the band.  On triple
overlaps these ratios can contribute a nonzero \(2\)-cocycle.  The equality
\eqref{eq:strict-representative-pullback} eliminates the comparison arrows
and their defect.  The rigidity theorem uses exactly this elimination and
does not claim that an arbitrary fibred category admits it.

\begin{proposition}[Presheaf and prestack interface]
\label{prop:finite-site-semantic-interface}
With the notation above:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item a strict matching family determines a section of \((aF)(a)\); if
      \(F\) is separated on the slice over \(a\), every section of
      \((aF)(a)\) is represented by a strict matching family on a cover of
      \(a\).  Hence, under this separatedness hypothesis,
      \[
      \operatorname{Match}(F,a)
      \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
      (aF)(a)\ne\varnothing;
      \]
\item \(\mathfrak P(a)\ne\varnothing\) if and only if \(F(a)\ne\varnothing\).
      If \(\iota\) is terminally essentially surjective, these are also
      equivalent to \(\mathfrak L(a)\ne\varnothing\);
\item under separatedness on the slice and terminal essential surjectivity,
      \(\operatorname{MWA}(F,a)\) holds exactly when \((aF)(a)\) is
      nonempty and \(\mathfrak L(a)\) is empty.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Applying \(u\) to a strict matching family gives a matching family in the
sheaf \(aF\), which has a unique amalgamation in \((aF)(a)\).  Conversely,
let \(\xi\in(aF)(a)\).  The plus--plus description of sheafification gives
a cover \(\{U_i\to a\}\) and sections \(s_i\in F(U_i)\) representing
\(\xi|_{U_i}\), with the following local equality on each overlap: there is
a cover \(\{V_{ijk}\to U_{ij}\}_k\) such that
\[
 s_i|_{V_{ijk}}=s_j|_{V_{ijk}}.
\]
Every \(U_{ij}\to a\) is an object of the slice.  Separatedness there turns
these locally witnessed equalities into
\(s_i|_{U_{ij}}=s_j|_{U_{ij}}\).  The \(s_i\) therefore form a strict
matching family.  This proves (i).

The first plus construction identifies matching families after passage to a
common covering refinement.  The second plus construction turns the
resulting separated presheaf into a sheaf.  A representative of \(\xi\) may
therefore begin with sections whose restrictions agree only locally on
\(U_{ij}\).  Pulling the witnessing covers back makes the local equalities
simultaneous.  Slice separatedness then shows that the original two
restrictions were already equal in \(F(U_{ij})\).  This is why the conclusion
produces a strict matching family on one cover rather than only a family
compatible after unrelated refinements.

The isomorphism \(\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P)(a)\cong F(a)\) proves
the first equivalence in (ii).  The forward implication from
\(\mathfrak P(a)\ne\varnothing\) to \(\mathfrak L(a)\ne\varnothing\) is
given by \(\iota\), and the converse is terminal essential surjectivity.
Finally, (iii) is obtained by combining (i), (ii), and the definition of
\(\operatorname{MWA}(F,a)\).
\end{proof}

The proposition can be read as a dependency audit:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{comparison}&\text{hypothesis used}\\ \hline
\operatorname{Match}(F,a)\Longleftrightarrow(aF)(a)\ne\varnothing
 &\text{slice separatedness}\\
\mathfrak P(a)\ne\varnothing\Longleftrightarrow F(a)\ne\varnothing
 &\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P)\cong F\\
\mathfrak L(a)\ne\varnothing\Longleftrightarrow\mathfrak P(a)\ne\varnothing
 &\text{terminal essential surjectivity.}
\end{array}
\]
No band, cohomology group, or gerbe classification is needed for these
set-level comparisons.  Those enter only after a global component section
has been fixed.

The article treats \((\mathcal C,F,\mathfrak P,\mathscr A)\) as supplied
finite-site lift data.  In particular, the fixed-cover, fixed-band realization
theorem below starts from supplied gerbe classes, equivalently their UCT
evaluation maps; it does not derive them from a lower team-semantic model.  A
narrow lower-language separation
example and the presentation bookkeeping removed from the core argument are
given in the technical appendix,
\href{run:supplement.pdf}{Appendix~\ref*{supp-sec:auxiliary-separation}}, only
as context; they do not generate the lift.
