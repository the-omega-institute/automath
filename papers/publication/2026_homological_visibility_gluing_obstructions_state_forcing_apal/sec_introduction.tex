\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

A stack in groupoids has a sheaf of connected components.  A global section
of that sheaf need not be represented by a global object of the stack, but it
does select a maximal subgerbe.  This is Giraud's component-gerbe construction
\cite[III, Prop.~2.1.5.3]{Giraud1971}.  The component is represented by a
global object precisely when its gerbe is neutral, or equivalently when its
Giraud \(H^2\)-class vanishes.  These classical facts are the starting point
of the article.

Our setting is deliberately finite and presentation-relative.  We fix a site
with terminal object, a presheaf \(F\), an abelian-banded prestack lift
\(\mathfrak P\), and its stackification \(\mathfrak L\).  The connected
components of \(\mathfrak P\) recover \(F\), while those of
\(\mathfrak L\) recover the sheafification \(aF\).  If \(F\) is separated on
the terminal slice and stackification is essentially surjective on the
terminal fibre, a strict matching family without an amalgamation occurs
exactly when \((aF)(a)\) is nonempty and every component gerbe selected by a
section of \((aF)(a)\) is non-neutral.  This is a comparison between a strict
presheaf predicate and standard gerbe theory, not a new classification of
gerbes.

The first paper-specific point is a rigidity statement for a particularly
strong kind of prestack presentation.  Suppose one selects a representative
of every presheaf component and requires those representatives to be stable
under pullback as literal equalities with respect to a split cleavage.  Under
slice separatedness, terminal essential surjectivity then forces the
sheafification unit
\[
 F(a)\longrightarrow(aF)(a)
\]
to be surjective.  Conversely, when \(H^1\) of the band vanishes,
surjectivity makes the Isom torsor between a selected representative and an
arbitrary terminal stack object trivial.  The result is a three-way
equivalence between terminal essential surjectivity, bijectivity of the
terminal sheafification unit, and the sheaf gluing condition at the terminal
object.  The proof in \Cref{thm:canonical-lift-rigidity} distinguishes the
strict-representative step from the classical \(H^1\)-torsor step.

The second point is an explicit finite-label prestack packaging.  Let
\(\mathcal U\) be a finite good cover with connected nerve \(N\), let \(A\)
be a finite abelian group, assume \(H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0\), and prescribe a
finite family
\[
 \phi_\lambda:H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow A.
\]
UCT selects classes in \(H^2(N,A)\), and the standard Cech construction
realizes each class by an \(A\)-banded gerbe
\cite{Breen2006,Moerdijk2002}.  The construction in
\Cref{thm:prescribed-component-image-construction} assembles these gerbes
into one prestack while controlling four features simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item the presheaf and sheaf of component labels;
\item terminal essential surjectivity of stackification;
\item the exact set of neutral and non-neutral labels;
\item the evaluation maps and their image subgroups.
\end{enumerate}
The full construction and all four verifications are part of the article.

There is a coefficient hypothesis which cannot be suppressed.  For an
ordinary nerve to index the Cech cochain complex with one copy of \(A\) per
simplex, every nonempty finite overlap must be connected and the coefficient
trivializations must be coherent.  On a disconnected overlap \(U_J\), the
section group is \(A^{\pi_0(U_J)}\), information which the ordinary nerve
does not contain.  We impose the connected-overlap hypothesis explicitly in
\eqref{eq:connected-overlap-hypothesis}.  The open-star covers used for the
wedge application have contractible nonempty intersections and satisfy it.

We also separate the formal algebra from the realization.  For a class
\(\omega\in H^2(N,A)\), put
\[
 K_\omega=\operatorname{Im}
 \bigl(H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}_\omega}A\bigr),
 \qquad Q_\omega=A/K_\omega.
\]
UCT naturality, quotient initiality, character annihilators, and the Ext
kernel are collected in one proposition.  For finitely many component
classes, the two aggregate quotients are
\[
 A/\bigcap_vK_v,
 \qquad A/\sum_vK_v,
\]
and their comparison is the elementary exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow
 \frac{\sum_vK_v}{\bigcap_vK_v}
 \longrightarrow
 A/\bigcap_vK_v
 \longrightarrow
 A/\sum_vK_v
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
No independent information-theoretic claim is made for the cardinalities of
these fibres.

The finite-group endpoint is correspondingly stated as a realization
corollary.  On the all-subdivision open-star basis of a finite model of
\(\bigvee_{j=1}^{\beta}S^2\), a nonzero finite abelian group \(G\) is
realized as the comparison kernel for two complementary nonzero component
images exactly when
\[
 d(G)\le2\beta
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 G\text{ is not cyclic of prime-power order}.
\]
The proof combines the prescribed-image prestack with the elementary fact
that the indecomposable finite abelian groups are the cyclic prime-power
groups.  It is not a classification attached to an arbitrary space,
presentation, or empirical model.

\subsection*{Inputs, standard steps, and paper-specific steps}

The logical order of the construction is
\[
\begin{array}{c}
(\mathcal C,F,\mathfrak P,\mathscr A)\\
\big\downarrow\text{\scriptsize stackification}\\
\mathfrak L,\ \pi_0(\mathfrak L)\cong aF\\
\big\downarrow\text{\scriptsize pull back over }v\in(aF)(a)\\
\mathfrak G_v,\ [\mathfrak G_v]\in H^2(\mathcal C,\mathscr A)\\
\big\downarrow\text{\scriptsize selected finite presentation and UCT}\\
\operatorname{ev}_{\omega_v}:H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\to A,\quad
K_v,\quad Q_v.
\end{array}
\]
The first line is supplied data.  The component gerbe in the third line is
Giraud's standard maximal subgerbe over a component section.  Its neutrality
criterion and transport under a banded equivalence are also standard.  The
last line uses standard Cech comparison and UCT after a finite presentation
has been selected.

The article-specific statements concern restrictions and simultaneous
control across this sequence.  The rigidity theorem identifies a strict
representative hypothesis that collapses a possible terminal gluing failure
when \(H^1\) vanishes.  The prescribed-realization theorem constructs one
prestack in which the component presheaf, terminal fibre, neutrality set, and
finite family of UCT evaluations are fixed together.  The wedge result is
then a corollary obtained by combining that construction with finite-group
arithmetic.  It is not presented as a separate classification mechanism.

This division also explains the theorem count.  Component-gerbe existence,
neutrality, banded naturality, UCT, duality, subgroup sums and intersections,
and quotient-fibre cardinality are consolidated rather than restated as
independent theorems.  Numbered statements are retained only where they
serve one of three roles:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item record the standard component-gerbe package with its exact Giraud
      antecedent;
\item state a paper-specific comparison, rigidity, or simultaneous
      realization assertion;
\item isolate an application boundary or the wedge realization endpoint.
\end{enumerate}

There are likewise two distinct uses of cohomology.  The Giraud class
\[
 [\mathfrak G_v]\in H^2(\mathcal C,\mathscr A)
\]
decides neutrality of the full component gerbe.  After a finite connected
good-cover presentation with constant coefficient group \(A\) is fixed, its
image under
\[
 H^2(N,A)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Hom}(H_2(N,\mathbb Z),A)
\]
measures only evaluation on integral \(2\)-cycles.  A nonzero Ext class can
be invisible to this map.  The homological image is therefore a quotient
construction applied to a supplied gerbe class, not a complete obstruction
theory for neutrality.

\subsection*{Scope and relation to the companion manuscript}

The lift data in this article are supplied.  They are not constructed from
teams, logical models, or empirical probability tables.  A bare empirical
model has a canonical split stack lift whose component gerbes are neutral.
The Abramsky--Mansfield--Barbosa obstruction is instead a relative Cech
\(H^1\)-torsor class indexed by a local section
\cite{AbramskyMansfieldSoaresBarbosa2012,Caru2017}.  The empty-domain argument
in \Cref{thm:empirical-model-boundary} prevents a universal recovery of those
classes from a global-component-section map.

The companion manuscript
\cite{MaZhang2026ConditionalGluingFailure} uses meet-semilattice sites.  The
overlap with the present pullback-site treatment is as follows.

\subsection*{Organization}

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes the minimal presheaf and prestack
interface.  Section~\ref{sec:gerbe-obstruction} records the standard Giraud
component package, proves the comparison criterion, and gives the full
terminal-rigidity proof.  Section~\ref{sec:homological-visibility} proves the
connected-overlap, basis-site, and derived comparisons before constructing
the prescribed finite-label prestack.  Section~\ref{sec:homological-images}
collects the UCT and aggregate consequences.  The wedge application and the
empirical-model boundary occupy Sections~\ref{sec:wedge-application} and
\ref{sec:empirical-boundary}.  The separately compiled technical appendix
contains only presentation bookkeeping, extended examples, and the narrow
lower-language boundary argument; the proofs needed to certify the principal
claims are all contained here.
