\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

For a stack in groupoids, a global section of the sheaf of connected
components selects a maximal subgerbe.  This is Giraud's component-gerbe
construction \cite[III, Prop.~2.1.5.3]{Giraud1971}.  Such a component has a
global object precisely when its gerbe is neutral, equivalently when its
Giraud \(H^2\)-class vanishes.  We apply this construction to an
abelian-banded prestack lift \(\mathfrak P\) of a presheaf \(F\) on a site
with terminal object \(a\), with stackification
\(\mathfrak L=a(\mathfrak P)\) and component sheaf \(aF\).

The first principal result identifies the effect of a strict choice of lift
representatives.  Representative-rigidity means that the chosen object in
each presheaf component is stable under pullback as a literal equality with
respect to a fixed split cleavage.

\begin{theoremA}
Let \(F\) be separated on the slice over \(a\), and let \(\mathfrak P\) be a
representative-rigid \(\mathscr A\)-banded prestack lift.  Every component
gerbe of \(a(\mathfrak P)\) selected by a section of \((aF)(a)\) is neutral.
Terminal essential surjectivity of \(\mathfrak P\to a(\mathfrak P)\) then
implies that \(F(a)\to(aF)(a)\) is surjective.  If
\(H^1(\mathcal C,\mathscr A)=0\), the converse holds, and the following are
equivalent: terminal essential surjectivity, bijectivity of
\(F(a)\to(aF)(a)\), and unique amalgamation in \(F(a)\) of every strict
matching family on every cover of \(a\).
\end{theoremA}

The first assertion is an obstruction theorem: a nonzero component-gerbe
class rules out a strictly pullback-stable representative system, without
any hypothesis on terminal essential surjectivity or on \(H^1\).  The proof
turns strict matching representatives into identity-valued descent data.
The converse terminal assertion then trivializes the \(\mathscr A\)-torsor
of isomorphisms by the \(H^1\)-vanishing hypothesis.  The full statement and
proof are \Cref{thm:canonical-lift-rigidity}.

The second principal result starts from prescribed cohomological data on one
finite good-cover presentation.  The connected-overlap and coherent
constant-band hypotheses ensure that the ordinary nerve carries the actual
Cech complex, rather than forgetting coefficient sections on components of
disconnected overlaps.

\begin{theoremB}
Let \(\mathcal U\) be a finite good cover with connected nerve \(N\), with
connected nonempty intersections and coherent trivializations of a fixed
finite constant band \(A\), and assume \(H_1(N,\mathbb Z)=0\).  Given a
finite nonempty set \(\Lambda\) and maps
\[
 \phi_\lambda:H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow A
 \qquad(\lambda\in\Lambda),
\]
put \(\Lambda_0=\{\lambda:\phi_\lambda=0\}\).  There are a presheaf
\(F\) and an \(A\)-banded prestack lift \(\mathfrak P\) on the finite
intersection site such that \(F(a)=\Lambda_0\),
\((aF)(a)\cong\Lambda\), stackification is terminally essentially
surjective, and the \(\lambda\)-component gerbe is neutral exactly for
\(\lambda\in\Lambda_0\) and has evaluation map \(\phi_\lambda\).  Moreover,
\(F\) has a strict matching family and has matching without amalgamation
exactly when \(\Lambda_0\) is empty.
\end{theoremB}

The construction in \Cref{thm:prescribed-component-image-construction}
defines the twisted groupoid fibres and restriction functors explicitly; its
triple-overlap defects are chosen cocycles representing the prescribed maps.
It therefore controls the component presheaf, terminal fibre, neutrality
pattern, and all evaluations in one prestack.

The vanishing of \(H_1(N,\mathbb Z)\) in Theorem B simultaneously removes
the Ext term in degree two and the terminal torsor in degree one.  Keeping
both terms gives a second realization theorem.

\begin{theoremC}
Let \(\mathcal U\) and \(A\) satisfy the finite good-cover,
connected-overlap, and coherent constant-band hypotheses, with connected
nerve \(N\), but impose no condition on \(H_1(N,\mathbb Z)\).  Every finite
family of prescribed classes \(\omega_\lambda\in H^2(N,A)\) is realized
simultaneously by one terminally essentially surjective banded prestack.  Its
terminal source components over each neutral label are indexed by
\(H^1(N,A)\), and this multiplicity is necessary in every terminally
essentially surjective realization.  More precisely, finite marked terminal
source sets are classified by having empty fibres over non-neutral labels and
cardinality \(|H^1(N,A)|\) over every neutral label.  Moreover, a nonzero
pure-Ext class gives matching without amalgamation while every homological
image and both aggregate quotients fail to detect the non-neutral component
gerbe.
\end{theoremC}

This is \Cref{thm:unrestricted-component-class-realization}, together with
\Cref{cor:pure-ext-unrestricted-realization}.  It identifies the exact cost of
removing the homological hypothesis: neutral terminal labels acquire the full
torsor of global twists, and the component presheaf ceases to be separated
precisely when that torsor is nontrivial.

For \(\omega\in H^2(N,A)\), write
\[
 K_\omega=\operatorname{Im}
 \bigl(H_2(N,\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}_\omega}A\bigr).
\]
UCT identifies the possible image subgroups on a wedge of \(2\)-spheres, and
the realization theorem supplies them as component gerbes.

\begin{corollaryD}
Fix \(\beta\ge1\) and the selected open-star covers of a finite model of
\(\bigvee_{j=1}^{\beta}S^2\).  A finite abelian group \(G\) occurs in the
construction with two non-neutral component gerbes whose nonzero homological
images \(K_1,K_2\) satisfy \(K_1\cap K_2=0\) and \(K_1+K_2=G\) if and only
if
\[
 G\ne0,\qquad d(G)\le2\beta,\qquad
 G\text{ is not cyclic of prime-power order}.
\]
In this case the aggregate comparison kernel is \(G\), and the component
presheaf has matching without amalgamation.
\end{corollaryD}

This is \Cref{thm:wedge-sphere-two-component-label-loss}.  It is the
principal concrete application of Theorem B.

The classical inputs are kept distinct from these constructions.
Giraud's component result and the classification of abelian-banded gerbes by
\(H^2\), including realization from Cech cocycles, precede the present work
\cite[Chap.~IV]{Giraud1971}\cite{Breen2006,Moerdijk2002}.  Theorems B and C
do not reclassify gerbes: they package supplied classes into marked prestacks
while simultaneously fixing their terminal and component data.  The
homological images then follow from UCT and finite subgroup arithmetic.  In
particular, an Ext class may be nonzero while its evaluation on integral
\(2\)-cycles is zero.

The finite site, band, prestack lift, and component classes are supplied
data.  Section~\ref{sec:empirical-boundary} explains once why a bare empirical
model does not select the non-split lift or \(2\)-cocycles used by Theorem B.
The component-obstruction framework, the matching/non-neutrality comparison,
and quotient initiality also overlap with the companion manuscript
\cite{MaZhang2026ConditionalGluingFailure}; no chronological priority for
those parts is inferred from their pullback-site formulation here.
