\begin{lemma}[Bijective terminal unit and the terminal sheaf axiom]\label{lem:bijective-unit-terminal-sheaf-axiom}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a site with pullbacks of covering families, let $F$
be a presheaf of sets, let $X$ be an object, and let
\[
u:F\longrightarrow aF
\]
be the sheafification unit. Assume that \(F\) is separated at every object
of the slice \(\mathcal C/X\). Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
\item the map \(u_X:F(X)\to(aF)(X)\) is bijective;
\item \(F\) satisfies the sheaf axiom at \(X\): every strict matching
family on every cover of \(X\) has a unique amalgamation in \(F(X)\).
\end{enumerate}
Here separatedness on the whole slice is essential.  In the direction from
bijectivity of \(u_X\) to the sheaf axiom, the chosen preimage of the
glued sheaf section has restrictions which agree with the prescribed
local sections only after applying \(u\) over each cover member.  Equality
in \((aF)(X_i)\) gives equality in \(F\) only after a further cover of
\(X_i\), and separatedness at \(X_i\), not merely at \(X\), is what
descends that refined equality back to \(X_i\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Separatedness on the slice at \(X\) implies injectivity of \(u_X\). Indeed, if
\(u_X(\sigma)=u_X(\tau)\), then
\Cref{lem:sheafification-unit-equality-local} gives a covering family
\(\{X_i\to X\}_i\) on which the restrictions of \(\sigma\) and \(\tau\)
are equal in \(F(X_i)\), and separatedness at \(X\) forces
\(\sigma=\tau\).

Assume first that \(u_X\) is bijective. Let
\[
\bigl(\{X_i\to X\}_{i\in I},(\sigma_i)_{i\in I}\bigr)
\]
be a strict \(F\)-matching family. Applying \(u\) to the sections
\(\sigma_i\) gives a matching family in the sheaf \(aF\), hence a unique
section \(\xi\in(aF)(X)\). By surjectivity choose
\(\sigma\in F(X)\) with \(u_X(\sigma)=\xi\). For each \(i\),
\[
u_{X_i}\bigl(\res_{X_i}^{X}\sigma\bigr)=u_{X_i}(\sigma_i)
\]
in \((aF)(X_i)\). This equality is not an equality in \(F(X_i)\). By
\Cref{lem:unit-equality-on-cover-members}, applied to the cover
\(\{X_i\to X\}_{i\in I}\), these sheafified equalities upgrade to
\[
\res_{X_i}^{X}\sigma=\sigma_i
\qquad(\forall i).
\]
Thus \(\sigma\) is an amalgamation of the original matching family.
Uniqueness of the amalgamation follows again from separatedness at \(X\).

Conversely, assume the sheaf axiom at \(X\). Let
\(\xi\in(aF)(X)\). By the plus--plus normal form
\Cref{lem:strict-sheafification-representatives}, \(\xi\) is represented
by a cover \(\{X_i\to X\}_i\) and sections
\(\sigma_i\in F(X_i)\) whose restrictions to each pullback overlap agree
after a further covering of that overlap. Because every such overlap is an
object of \(\mathcal C/X\), \Cref{lem:separated-restriction-refined-equality}
upgrades the refined equalities to a strict \(F\)-matching family. The
sheaf axiom at \(X\) gives an amalgamation \(\sigma\in F(X)\). The
sections \(u_X(\sigma)\) and \(\xi\) restrict to the same sections of
\(aF\) on the cover \(\{X_i\to X\}_i\), so separatedness of the sheaf
\(aF\) gives \(u_X(\sigma)=\xi\). Hence \(u_X\) is surjective, and it is
injective by the first paragraph.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Isomorphism torsor for a lifted component]\label{lem:canonical-lift-isom-torsor}
Let $\mathfrak P_r$ be a representative-rigid banded groupoid lift with band $\Band$ on $\mathcal C_a$, let
\[
\iota_r:\mathfrak P_r\to\mathfrak L_r:=a(\mathfrak P_r)
\]
be the stackification map, and let $x\in\mathfrak L_r(a)$ and $\sigma\in F_{p,s}(a)$ satisfy
\[
[\iota_r(s_a(\sigma))]=[x]
\quad\text{in}\quad
\pi_0(\mathfrak L_r)(a).
\]
Then the sheaf
\[
\mathcal I_{\sigma,x}:=
\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}_{\mathfrak L_r}(\iota_r(s_a(\sigma)),x)
\]
is a $\Band$-torsor on $\mathcal C_a$. If $H^1(\mathcal C_a,\Band)=0$, then $\mathcal I_{\sigma,x}$ has a global section, equivalently
\[
\iota_r(s_a(\sigma))\cong x
\quad\text{in}\quad
\mathfrak L_r(a).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
y:=s_a(\sigma).
\]
The equality of component classes in the sheaf $\pi_0(\mathfrak L_r)$ means precisely that the two objects $\iota_r(y)$ and $x$ become isomorphic after passing to a cover of $a$. Hence there is a covering family
\[
\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I}
\]
and isomorphisms
\[
\phi_i:\iota_r(y)|_{U_i}\xrightarrow{\sim}x|_{U_i}.
\]
Thus $\mathcal I_{\sigma,x}$ is locally nonempty.

For every object $U$ of $\mathcal C_a$ and every local isomorphism
\[
\phi:\iota_r(y)|_U\xrightarrow{\sim}x|_U,
\]
right composition gives a simply transitive action of
\[
\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}_{\mathfrak L_r}(\iota_r(y)|_U)
\]
on $\mathcal I_{\sigma,x}(U)$:
\[
\alpha\longmapsto \phi\circ\alpha.
\]
Because $\mathfrak L_r$ is the stackification of a $\Band$-banded prestack, the automorphism sheaf of $\iota_r(y)|_U$ is identified with $\Band|_U$; the band is abelian, so the same torsor is obtained from left composition through the automorphism sheaf of $x|_U$. Therefore $\mathcal I_{\sigma,x}$ is a $\Band$-torsor. This is the usual Isom-torsor construction for a banded stack \cite[Chap.~III]{MacLaneMoerdijk1994}.

The nonabelian cohomology set $H^1(\mathcal C_a,\Band)$ classifies $\Band$-torsors on $\mathcal C_a$ up to isomorphism. Since $\Band$ is here an abelian sheaf, the distinguished point $0\in H^1(\mathcal C_a,\Band)$ is the class of the trivial torsor. If $H^1(\mathcal C_a,\Band)=0$, every $\Band$-torsor is trivial; hence $\mathcal I_{\sigma,x}$ is isomorphic to the trivial torsor $\Band$ and has a global section. Such a section is exactly an isomorphism $\iota_r(y)\cong x$ in the terminal fibre $\mathfrak L_r(a)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Representing component sections by descent]\label{lem:component-section-descent-object}
Let $\mathfrak P_r$ be a representative-rigid banded groupoid lift on
$\mathcal C_a$, assume that $F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}$ is
separated on the slice, and let
\[
\iota_r:\mathfrak P_r\to\mathfrak L_r:=a(\mathfrak P_r)
\]
be the stackification map. Every section
\[
\xi\in\pi_0(\mathfrak L_r)(a)
\]
is represented by an object of the terminal stack fibre $\mathfrak L_r(a)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
F:=F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}.
\]
The identification
\[
\pi_0(\mathfrak L_r)\cong a_{p,s}F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}
\]
from \Cref{prop:stackification-components} writes $\xi$ as a sheafified
matching datum. We now use the compatible-representative plus--plus normal form
explicitly, because a section of \(aF\) is not, for a general presheaf, a
strict \(F\)-matching family on the first displayed cover. By
\Cref{lem:strictify-component-representatives}, applied in the slice
\(\mathcal C_a\), there is a
covering family
\[
\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I}
\]
and sections
\[
\sigma_i\in F_{p,s}(U_i)
\]
such that the sheaf sections \(u_{U_i}(\sigma_i)\) glue to \(\xi\), and
such that for every pair \(i,j\), with
\[
U_{ij}:=U_i\times_a U_j,
\]
the two restrictions agree on the original overlap:
\[
\res_{U_{ij}}^{U_i}(\sigma_i)
=
\res_{U_{ij}}^{U_j}(\sigma_j)
\qquad(\forall i,j),
\]
including the case where \(U_{ij}\) is the formal initial object
representing an empty overlap. The cited choice of compatible representatives lemma is the
point at which separatedness on the slice, rather than separatedness only at
\(a\), is needed: the plus--plus representative first gives equality only
after covers of the \(U_{ij}\), and separatedness at those overlap objects
upgrades it to equality on the original \(U_{ij}\).
Representative-rigidity supplies strict representatives
\[
y_i:=s_{U_i}(\sigma_i)\in\mathfrak P_r(U_i).
\]
On every overlap, strict functoriality gives equality of the restricted
representatives
\[
y_i|_{U_{ij}}=s_{U_{ij}}(\sigma_i|_{U_{ij}})
=s_{U_{ij}}(\sigma_j|_{U_{ij}})=y_j|_{U_{ij}}.
\]
These equalities are equalities in the fibre over the actual pullback
object \(U_{ij}\). They are not merely equalities after a further cover of
\(U_{ij}\). This is why the following identity arrows are legitimate
cover-level descent arrows; without the preceding separatedness and
compatible representatives, identity descent data on the displayed cover would not be
defined.
Taking the identity isomorphism of this common object on each
$U_{ij}$ therefore gives the overlap arrow
\[
\theta_{ij}:y_i|_{U_{ij}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}y_j|_{U_{ij}}
\]
inside $\mathfrak L_r(U_{ij})$. On a triple overlap
$U_{ijk}:=U_i\times_aU_j\times_aU_k$, the two composites
\[
\theta_{ik}|_{U_{ijk}},
\qquad
\theta_{jk}|_{U_{ijk}}\circ\theta_{ij}|_{U_{ijk}}
\]
are both the identity of the same restricted object, including when this
triple overlap is the formal initial object. Thus the cocycle condition
for descent is satisfied strictly. Since $\mathfrak L_r$ is a stack,
this descent datum glues to an object
\[
x_\xi\in\mathfrak L_r(a).
\]
By construction, the connected-component class of $x_\xi$ is the original
section $\xi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rigidity of representative-rigid lifts over slice sites on which the restricted presheaf is separated]\label{thm:canonical-lift-rigidity}
Let $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ be admissible at a state $p$, write
\[
a=[r]_p,
\qquad
\mathcal C_a:=\mathcal C_{p,s}/a,
\]
and let
\[
\mathfrak P_r\to \mathcal C_a
\]
be a representative-rigid banded groupoid lift with band $\Band$. Let
\[
u:F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}\longrightarrow a_{p,s}F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}
\]
be the sheafification unit. Assume throughout this theorem that
\[
F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}
\]
is separated on the slice in the sense of
\Cref{def:separated-restriction}; equivalently, $F_{p,s}$ is
separated at every object of the slice $\mathcal C_a$, including every
cover member, every pullback overlap, and every refinement occurring below.
Separatedness only at the terminal object $a$ is not sufficient for this
theorem, and no assertion below is made under that weaker hypothesis.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item If $\mathfrak P_r$ is essentially surjective on the terminal fibre over $a$, then
\[
u_a:F_{p,s}(a)\to \bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a)
\]
is surjective.
\item If
\[
H^1(\mathcal C_a,\Band)=0,
\]
then surjectivity of $u_a$ implies that $\mathfrak P_r$ is essentially
surjective on the terminal fibre over $a$.
\end{enumerate}
Under the same vanishing hypothesis the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
\item $\mathfrak P_r$ is essentially surjective on the terminal fibre over $a$;
\item $u_a$ is bijective;
\item $F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}$ satisfies the sheaf gluing condition at
the object $a$, that is, every matching family on every cover of $a$ has
an amalgamation in $F_{p,s}(a)$; uniqueness follows from the standing
separatedness on the slice at $a$.
\end{enumerate}
The last equivalence is not a consequence of separatedness at \(a\) alone.
It uses separatedness at all cover members, pullback overlaps, and their
refinements in the slice \(\mathcal C_a\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
\mathfrak L_r:=a(\mathfrak P_r).
\]
By \Cref{prop:stackification-component-unit-naturality}, the morphism of
component sheaves induced by the stackification map
\[
\iota_r:\mathfrak P_r\to \mathfrak L_r
\]
identifies with the sheafification unit
\[
u:F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}\to a_{p,s}F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}.
\]

Assume first that $\mathfrak P_r$ is essentially surjective on the terminal fibre over $a$, and let
\[
\xi\in \bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a)\cong \pi_0(\mathfrak L_r)(a).
\]
By \Cref{lem:component-section-descent-object}, choose an object
\[
x\in \mathfrak L_r(a)
\]
whose connected-component class is $\xi$. The cited lemma uses the
plus--plus normal form of \Cref{lem:strict-sheafification-representatives}:
first one represents $\xi$ by $F_{p,s}$-sections whose overlap equalities
hold after local refinements, then separatedness on the slice upgrades those
refined overlap equalities to a strict matching family, and only then does
effective descent in the stackification produce the terminal object.
Essential surjectivity of
\[
(\iota_r)_a:(\mathfrak P_r)_a\to (\mathfrak L_r)_a
\]
gives an object
\[
y\in \mathfrak P_r(a)
\]
with $\iota_r(y)\cong x$. Let
\[
\sigma:=[y]\in \pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P_r)(a)\cong F_{p,s}(a).
\]
Since $u_a$ is the map on connected components induced by $\iota_r$, one has
\[
u_a(\sigma)=[\iota_r(y)]=[x]=\xi.
\]
Thus $u_a$ is surjective.

Assume next that $H^1(\mathcal C_a,\Band)=0$ and that $u_a$ is surjective. Let
\[
x\in \mathfrak L_r(a).
\]
Its connected-component class
\[
[x]\in \pi_0(\mathfrak L_r)(a)\cong \bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a)
\]
lies in the image of $u_a$, so choose
\[
\sigma\in F_{p,s}(a)
\]
with
\[
u_a(\sigma)=[x].
\]
Put
\[
y:=s_a(\sigma)\in \mathfrak P_r(a).
\]
By construction, $y$ represents the class $\sigma$ in $\pi_0^{\mathrm{pre}}(\mathfrak P_r)(a)$, hence
\[
[\iota_r(y)]=u_a(\sigma)=[x]
\]
in $\pi_0(\mathfrak L_r)(a)$. By \Cref{lem:canonical-lift-isom-torsor}, the sheaf
\[
\underline{\mathrm{Isom}}_{\mathfrak L_r}(\iota_r(y),x)
\]
is a $\Band$-torsor, and the vanishing of $H^1(\mathcal C_a,\Band)$ gives a global section of it. Equivalently, there exists an isomorphism
\[
\iota_r(y)\xrightarrow{\sim} x
\]
in $\mathfrak L_r(a)$. Thus every object of $\mathfrak L_r(a)$ is in the essential image of $(\iota_r)_a$, so $\mathfrak P_r$ is essentially surjective on the terminal fibre over $a$.

The standing separatedness on the slice also gives injectivity of the unit at the
terminal object. If
\[
u_a(\sigma)=u_a(\tau)
\qquad
(\sigma,\tau\in F_{p,s}(a)),
\]
then the two global sections define the same element of the sheafification at $a$.
Applying \Cref{lem:sheafification-unit-equality-local} gives a cover
\[
\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I}
\]
such that
\[
\res_{U_i}^a(\sigma)=\res_{U_i}^a(\tau)
\qquad
(\forall i\in I).
\]
Separatedness on the slice applies in particular at the terminal object
$a$, and therefore forces $\sigma=\tau$. Thus $u_a$ is injective.

Under $H^1(\mathcal C_a,\Band)=0$, part \textup{(i)} gives
\textup{(a)}$\Rightarrow$\textup{(b)}, and part \textup{(ii)} together
with injectivity gives \textup{(b)}$\Rightarrow$\textup{(a)}. It remains
to identify \textup{(b)} with the terminal sheaf axiom, and we spell this
out because this is the point where separatedness on the slice is
used beyond the terminal object.

Assume \textup{(b)}, and let
\[
\{U_i\to a\}_{i\in I},
\qquad
\sigma_i\in F_{p,s}(U_i)
\]
be a strict matching family on a cover of \(a\). Applying \(u\) to the
\(\sigma_i\) gives a matching family in the sheaf
\(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\), hence a unique section
\[
\xi\in \bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a)
\]
whose restriction to \(U_i\) is \(u_{U_i}(\sigma_i)\). By surjectivity of
\(u_a\), choose \(\sigma\in F_{p,s}(a)\) with \(u_a(\sigma)=\xi\). For
each \(i\),
\[
u_{U_i}\bigl(\res_{U_i}^{a}\sigma\bigr)=u_{U_i}(\sigma_i)
\]
in \((a_{p,s}F_{p,s})(U_i)\). This equality is not an equality in
\(F_{p,s}(U_i)\). By \Cref{lem:sheafification-unit-equality-local},
there is a covering family
\[
\{V_{ik}\to U_i\}_{k}
\]
such that
\[
\res_{V_{ik}}^{a}\sigma=\res_{V_{ik}}^{U_i}\sigma_i
\qquad(\forall k)
\]
in \(F_{p,s}(V_{ik})\). Since \(U_i\) is an object of the slice
\(\mathcal C_a\) and the standing hypothesis is separatedness on the slice,
\(F_{p,s}\) is separated at \(U_i\). The displayed local equalities over
the cover of \(U_i\) therefore force
\[
\res_{U_i}^{a}\sigma=\sigma_i
\]
in \(F_{p,s}(U_i)\). Thus \(\sigma\) is an amalgamation of the original
matching family. Its uniqueness follows from separatedness at \(a\).
Therefore \textup{(c)} holds.

Conversely, assume \textup{(c)} and let
\[
\xi\in \bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a).
\]
By the plus--plus normal form of
\Cref{lem:strict-sheafification-representatives}, \(\xi\) is represented
on a cover \(\{U_i\to a\}\) by sections
\(\sigma_i\in F_{p,s}(U_i)\) whose restrictions to each overlap
\[
U_{ij}:=U_i\times_aU_j
\]
agree after a further covering family of \(U_{ij}\). Each \(U_{ij}\) is an
object of the slice \(\mathcal C_a\), so separatedness on the slice and
\Cref{lem:separated-restriction-refined-equality} upgrade these refined
equalities to equalities on the original overlaps. Hence the
\(\sigma_i\) form a strict matching family on the displayed cover. By the
sheaf axiom at \(a\), there is an amalgamation
\(\sigma\in F_{p,s}(a)\). The sections \(u_a(\sigma)\) and \(\xi\) have
the same restrictions to the cover \(\{U_i\to a\}\), so separatedness of
the sheaf \(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\) gives \(u_a(\sigma)=\xi\). Thus \(u_a\) is
surjective, and its injectivity was proved above. Therefore
\textup{(b)} holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Matching without amalgamation requires noncanonical lift data]\label{cor:no-rigid-gluing-null}
Let $r:\mathsf{Ref}_s$ be admissible at a state $p$, write
\[
a=[r]_p,
\qquad
\mathcal C_a:=\mathcal C_{p,s}/a,
\]
assume that $F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}$ is separated on the slice. If
\[
M,p\Vdash \NoAmalg_s(r),
\]
then no representative-rigid banded groupoid lift with band $\Band$ can be essentially surjective on the terminal fibre over $a$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The condition
\[
M,p\Vdash \NoAmalg_s(r)
\]
means that $r$ is admissible, that
\[
M,p\Vdash \CompSec_s(r),
\]
and that
\[
M,p\nVdash \Sec_s(r).
\]
By \Cref{thm:sheafification-characterization},
\[
\bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a)\neq\varnothing,
\]
whereas \Cref{def:existence-levels}(iii) gives
\[
F_{p,s}(a)=\varnothing.
\]
Hence the unit map
\[
u_a:F_{p,s}(a)\to \bigl(a_{p,s}F_{p,s}\bigr)(a)
\]
cannot be surjective. Since separatedness on the slice is part of the present
hypotheses, the contrapositive of
\Cref{thm:canonical-lift-rigidity}(i) therefore excludes essential
surjectivity on the terminal fibre over $a$ for every representative-rigid banded groupoid lift
with band $\Band$.
This is not contradicted by the vacuous terminal-fibre essential-surjectivity examples used
later: in those examples the prestack and stack terminal fibres are both
empty. Representative-rigidity is the extra hypothesis that rules out that
vacuous case when a sheafified terminal component exists. Indeed, if such a
prestack were representative-rigid, then
\Cref{lem:component-section-descent-object} would turn the sheafified terminal
component into an object of $\mathfrak L_r(a)$, contradicting the equality
$\mathfrak P_r(a)=\varnothing=\mathfrak L_r(a)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What remains conditional]\label{rem:gluing-sensitive-lifts}
\Cref{prop:split-banded-groupoid-lift} constructs a split prestack for every
presheaf \(F_{p,s}|_{\mathcal C_a}\) and every abelian sheaf \(\Band\).
Separatedness on the slice is needed exactly for this split prestack to be
separated as a prestack in groupoids. The split construction is not
gluing-sensitive: it records the prescribed component presheaf and band, but
it does not by itself supply the additional gerbe data, acyclic-cover
comparisons, or terminal-fibre essential-surjectivity hypotheses used later. The
gerbe-theoretic and homological statements are therefore relative to chosen
banded lift data satisfying those extra hypotheses.
\end{remark}
