
\subsection{Exact Parry scan-error law for periodic and reducible observers}
\label{sub:exact-parry-scan-law}

The comparison bounds in Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-state-posterior} discard the
terminal Parry weights and the posterior ambiguity of each boundary state.
For the Parry measure these quantities are finite-state data, so the loss can
be removed even when the refined ambiguous graph is periodic or reducible.

\begin{lemma}[Absorption and posterior weights]
\label{lem:parry-observer-absorption}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-state-posterior}, and assume
that the chosen presentation is $\mu$-resolving with bound $R$.  Let
$\lambda=\lambda_{\mathrm{cyl}}$, and let $\ell,r>0$ be left and right
Perron eigenvectors of the defining adjacency matrix $A$, normalized by
$\ell^{\mathsf T}r=1$.  For $v=(i,u)\in\mathcal R_\partial$, let
\[
h_v:=\mu_{i,u}(\text{the observer eventually enters }\mathcal S_1),
\qquad \eta_v:=\min\{h_v,1-h_v\}.
\]
Here $\mu_{i,u}$ denotes the Parry future law started with terminal symbol
$i$ and observer state $u$.  Then the observer enters
$\mathcal S_0\cup\mathcal S_1$ with probability one under every accessible
boundary conditional law, and
\[
0<h_v<1,\qquad
\mu(\mathsf P\mid[a])=h_{(a_{m-1},q(a))}
\quad\text{for every }[a]\in\partial_m\mathsf P.
\]
Moreover, the vector $h$ is the unique solution of the finite absorbing
Markov-chain equations
\[
h_{(i,u)}=\sum_{j:A_{ij}=1}
\frac{r_j}{\lambda r_i}
\begin{cases}
1,&T_j(u)\in\mathcal S_1,\\
0,&T_j(u)\in\mathcal S_0,\\
h_{(j,T_j(u))},&T_j(u)\in\mathcal S_\partial.
\end{cases}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every accessible ambiguous observer state $u$, the resolving hypothesis
provides a fixed continuation of length at most $R$ ending in each of
$\mathcal S_0$ and $\mathcal S_1$.  Every allowed Parry transition has
conditional probability at least
\[
p_*:=\min\{r_j/(\lambda r_i):A_{ij}=1\}>0,
\]
so, conditionally on any boundary prefix represented by $(i,u)$, the
probability of entering either decided class within the next $R$ symbols is at
least $p_*^R$.  Iterating at successive blocks of length $R$ gives a bound
$(1-p_*^R)^k$ for the probability of remaining ambiguous after $kR$ steps.
Thus the hitting time of $\mathcal S_0\cup\mathcal S_1$ is almost surely
finite.  The defining properties of a finite-state posterior presentation
identify the event, modulo the conditional null set of paths that never hit a
decided class, with eventual entry into $\mathcal S_1$.  The Markov property
then gives the displayed conditional-probability identity and the first-step
equations.  Uniqueness follows because the transient ambiguous transition
matrix has spectral radius strictly less than one: the same block estimate
gives geometric decay of its powers.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Periodic polynomial asymptotics for positive matrix coefficients]
\label{lem:periodic-polynomial-matrix-coefficients}
Let $B$ be a finite nonnegative matrix, let $s\ge0$ be nonzero, and let
$t>0$ on every state reachable from $\operatorname{supp}(s)$.  Suppose every
state of $B$ is reachable from $\operatorname{supp}(s)$, and put
$\rho=\rho(B)$.  If $B$ is nilpotent, then $sB^nt=0$ for all sufficiently large
$n$.  Otherwise, let $q$ be the largest number of strongly connected
components of spectral radius $\rho$ occurring in a directed chain reachable
from $\operatorname{supp}(s)$.  There are an integer $p\ge1$ and positive
constants $c_0,\ldots,c_{p-1}$ such that
\[
sB^nt=\rho^n\bigl(c_{n\bmod p}n^{q-1}+O(n^{q-2})\bigr)
\quad(n\to\infty),
\]
where the error term is interpreted as $O(\rho^n\vartheta^n)$ when $q=1$, for
some $0<\vartheta<1$.  One may take $p$ to be the least common multiple of
the periods of the spectral-radius components.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $B$ in Frobenius normal form.  A walk contributing to $sB^nt$ visits a
chain of strongly connected diagonal blocks in their partial order.  A chain
containing $j$ blocks of spectral radius $\rho$ contributes at most
$O(n^{j-1}\rho^n)$: this follows by expanding the block power into the
convolution over the $j-1$ transition times between the blocks and applying
the Perron--Frobenius bound to each irreducible block (see, for example,
\cite[Chs.~1--2]{Seneta2006}).  Hence the total degree is at most $q-1$.

Choose a reachable chain of $q$ spectral-radius components and fixed positive
weight paths between successive components.  In each component, the
appropriate power of the period is primitive.  Perron--Frobenius therefore
gives a uniform lower bound $c\rho^k$ for walks of every sufficiently large
allowed length in the corresponding cyclic class.  For each residue of $n$
modulo the least common multiple $p$ of the component periods, choose the
terminal cyclic class according to that residue.  The first $q-1$ residence
times may then be varied independently through $\asymp n^{q-1}$ admissible
values while the final component absorbs the remaining length; positivity of
$t$ supplies a terminal state in every cyclic class.  Thus, for every residue
class, the coefficient is bounded below by a positive multiple of
$n^{q-1}\rho^n$.

Finally, the Jordan expansion of the finite matrix $B$ is a finite sum of terms
$P_\zeta(n)\zeta^n$.  The peripheral eigenvalues of each irreducible block are
$\rho$ times roots of unity, and the preceding chain bound shows that the
largest degree is $q-1$.  Grouping the peripheral terms by $n\bmod p$ gives
the displayed constants $c_j$; the lower bound just proved makes each
$c_j$ strictly positive.  All remaining eigenvalues have modulus strictly
less than $\rho$, which gives the stated remainder when $q=1$ and is absorbed
by $O(n^{q-2}\rho^n)$ when $q\ge2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact Parry scan-error law for finite posterior observers]
\label{thm:exact-parry-scan-law}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-state-posterior}, and assume
that the chosen finite-state posterior presentation is $\mu$-resolving.  Let
$\widetilde B$ and $r_1$ be the refined ambiguous matrix and initial row vector
of that theorem.  Let $\ell,r>0$ be normalized as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:parry-observer-absorption}.  For $v=(i,u)\in\mathcal R_\partial$
define
\[
s_v:=(r_1)_v\ell_i,
\qquad
t_v:=r_i\eta_v,
\]
where $\eta_v$ is given by Lemma~\ref{lem:parry-observer-absorption}.  Then,
for every $m\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact-parry-scan-law}
\varepsilon_m(\mathsf P;\mu)
=\lambda^{-(m-1)}s\,\widetilde B^{\,m-1}t.
\end{equation}
If $\widetilde B$ is nilpotent, the scan error is eventually zero.  Otherwise
its generating function is rational:
\[
\sum_{m\ge1}\varepsilon_m(\mathsf P;\mu)z^{m-1}
=s\left(I-\frac z\lambda\widetilde B\right)^{-1}t,
\]
as an identity of formal power series (and analytically for
$|z|<\lambda/\rho(\widetilde B)$).  Consequently
$(\varepsilon_m)_{m\ge1}$ satisfies a constant-coefficient linear recurrence
of order at most $|\mathcal R_\partial|$.

In the nonnilpotent case let $q$ be the maximal number of reachable
spectral-radius strongly connected components in a directed chain of
$\widetilde B$, and let $p$ be the least common multiple of their periods.
Then there are constants $c_0,\ldots,c_{p-1}>0$ such that
\[
\varepsilon_m(\mathsf P;\mu)
=\left(\frac{\rho(\widetilde B)}\lambda\right)^{m-1}
\bigl(c_{(m-1)\bmod p}(m-1)^{q-1}
 +O((m-1)^{q-2})\bigr).
\]
For $q=1$ the error term is exponentially smaller than the leading term.
In particular, periodic and reducible observers have a complete residue-class
asymptotic, and the polynomial factor records the length of a maximal chain of
critical boundary components.  When $\widetilde B$ is primitive, $q=p=1$ and,
if $x,y>0$ are Perron eigenvectors normalized by $y^{\mathsf T}x=1$, then
\[
c_0=(s x)(y^{\mathsf T}t)>0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $a=a_0\cdots a_{m-1}$ be a boundary word and write
$v_k=(a_k,q(a_0\cdots a_k))$.  The boundary-word/path bijection of
Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary-word-refined-path} identifies boundary words with
paths $v_0\to\cdots\to v_{m-1}$ in $\widetilde B$ whose initial state is in
$\operatorname{supp}(r_1)$.  The Parry cylinder formula is
\[
\mu([a])=\ell_{a_0}r_{a_{m-1}}\lambda^{-(m-1)}.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:parry-observer-absorption}, the terminal posterior ambiguity
on this cylinder is $\eta_{v_{m-1}}$.  Substitution into the exact atomwise
Bayes decomposition gives
\[
\varepsilon_m
=\lambda^{-(m-1)}
\sum_{v_0\to\cdots\to v_{m-1}}
 (r_1)_{v_0}\ell_{a_0}
 \prod_{k=0}^{m-2}(\widetilde B)_{v_kv_{k+1}}
 r_{a_{m-1}}\eta_{v_{m-1}},
\]
which is~\eqref{eq:exact-parry-scan-law}.  The resolvent identity gives the
rational generating function, and Cayley--Hamilton gives the recurrence.

All states in $\mathcal R_\partial$ are reachable from
$\operatorname{supp}(r_1)$ by definition, while $t_v>0$ by the resolving
hypothesis and Lemma~\ref{lem:parry-observer-absorption}.  Applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:periodic-polynomial-matrix-coefficients} to
$(\widetilde B,s,t)$ gives the stated residue-class asymptotic.  In the
primitive case, Perron--Frobenius gives
$\widetilde B^n=\rho^nxy^{\mathsf T}+O(\rho_1^n)$ with $\rho_1<\rho$;
substitution into~\eqref{eq:exact-parry-scan-law} yields the displayed
constant.
\end{proof}
