\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The denominator-weighted Brocot layer exhibits a complete one-digit
condensation picture for every fixed \(s>2\).  Its nonmultiplicative
continuant weight does not place it under a general independent-sum or Gibbs
partition theorem.  The arithmetic replacement is the two-sided context
estimate: after the large digit is removed, the inverse-continuant masses of
the left and right words are summable and the central continuant is
asymptotically their product times the large digit.  This yields the product
context law in total variation and all of its defect, location, and
denominator consequences.

The same proof has a scalar output.  It gives the coefficient
\(2\{\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)\}^2\), identifies the endpoint overcount in the
earlier convolution, and transfers termwise to the odd denominator layers
used by Fibonacci generators.  The correction concerns only that leading
coefficient.  A determination of the remaining coefficients would require
uniform expansions beyond the dominated-convergence argument used here.

At the arithmetic critical exponent, the scalar output becomes an exact
regularly varying generator-cost law.  The free-generator orbit count then
turns the classical Fibonacci layer sum into a renewal sum.  The tail
constant \(b_C=8\) controls both the spectrally positive stable limit and the
fractional \(m^{3-\sigma_0}\) term.  This is the full shared mechanism:
continued-fraction contexts determine the critical tail, and renewal theory
transfers that tail to the Fibonacci partition function.

The companion manuscript \emph{Finite-window Zeckendorf thermodynamics}
starts from an exact finite-window correspondence and studies local spectra,
critical coexistence, and large deviations.  Those questions are logically
independent of the total-variation condensation theorem.  Keeping the two
settings separate also makes the scope of the present conclusion explicit:
the argument establishes a structural law for this continuant model and its
specific Fibonacci renewal application, rather than a universal mechanism
for condensation or freezing.
