\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The total-variation theorem describes the sampled continued fraction beyond
its scalar normalization.  It gives simultaneous limits for the two
contexts, the condensate defect and location, and the denominator
factorization.  The summable two-context estimate is the arithmetic step
that makes this joint conclusion possible for the nonmultiplicative
continuant weight.

Its scalar denominator-layer asymptotic supplies the critical cost tail in
Weinstein's generator coordinates.  The exact orbit count turns the
classical Fibonacci layer into a renewal sum, and the tail then produces the
unbounded sublinear term of order \(m^{3-\sigma_0}\).  The same tail yields
the ancillary stable domain-of-attraction statement for generator costs.

Two questions remain.  Quantitative rates for the total-variation context
law would require uniform control as the two context words grow.  Analogous
critical renewal corrections for other nonmultiplicative continued-fraction
families would require an arithmetic generator identity and a local tail
estimate adapted to those families.
