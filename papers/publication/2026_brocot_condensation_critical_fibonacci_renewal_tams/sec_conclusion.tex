\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The denominator-weighted Brocot layer exhibits a complete one-digit
condensation picture for every fixed \(s>2\).  Its nonmultiplicative
continuant weight does not place it under a general independent-sum or Gibbs
partition theorem.  The arithmetic replacement is the two-sided context
estimate: after the large digit is removed, the inverse-continuant masses of
the left and right words are summable and the central continuant is
asymptotically their product times the large digit.  This yields the product
context law in total variation and all of its defect, location, and
denominator consequences.  In particular, the result concerns the entire
sampled continued fraction rather than only its normalizing sum: uniqueness
of the large digit, tightness of the discarded digit mass, and convergence
of both contexts are simultaneous conclusions.

The same proof has a scalar output.  It gives the coefficient
\(2\{\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)\}^2\), identifies the endpoint overcount in the
earlier convolution, and transfers termwise to the odd denominator layers
used by Fibonacci generators.  The correction concerns only that leading
coefficient.  A determination of the remaining coefficients would require
uniform expansions beyond the dominated-convergence argument used here.
Nothing in the endpoint calculation verifies, modifies, or rules out those
remaining coefficients.  This boundary is essential because the
condensation proof uses a first-order central-continuant approximation and
summable domination; it does not produce the uniform higher-order remainder
needed for a full corrected expansion.

At the arithmetic critical exponent, the scalar output becomes an exact
regularly varying generator-cost law.  The free-generator orbit count then
turns the classical Fibonacci layer sum into a renewal sum.  The tail
constant \(b_C=8\) controls both the spectrally positive stable limit and the
fractional \(m^{3-\sigma_0}\) term.  This is the full shared mechanism:
continued-fraction contexts determine the critical tail, and renewal theory
transfers that tail to the Fibonacci partition function.

The transfer has two distinct probabilistic outputs.  For independent
critical generator letters, the explicit scale
\[
 a_n=\left(\frac{2^{\sigma_0+2}}{\sigma_0-1}n\right)^{1/(\sigma_0-1)}
\]
gives a centered spectrally positive stable limit.  For the deterministic
classical partition function, the exact orbit identity converts the same
tail into
\begin{align*}
 &\sum_{F_{m+1}-1\le N<F_{m+2}-1}R(N)^{-\sigma_0}\\
 &\qquad=\frac{2m}{\mu_C}
 +\frac{2K_C}{\mu_C^2(\sigma_0-2)(3-\sigma_0)}m^{3-\sigma_0}
 +o(m^{3-\sigma_0}).
\end{align*}
The second term is unbounded but sublinear.  It is therefore a genuine
finite-size contribution at the scale of the partition sum, rather than a
relabeling of the finitely many endpoint orbits.

Several limitations follow from the proof.  The stable statement is a
domain-of-attraction result for sums of generator-letter costs; it is not a
local limit theorem and supplies no rate of convergence.  The Fibonacci
expansion is established at the single arithmetic critical exponent and
does not assert an analogous fractional correction away from criticality.
Nor does the argument classify other continued-fraction weights: the exact
constant uses the regular/negative continued-fraction correspondence, the
two context masses, and Weinstein's particular free monoid.  Models without
those identities require separate arithmetic input.

The companion manuscript \emph{Finite-window Zeckendorf thermodynamics}
starts from an exact finite-window correspondence and studies local spectra,
critical coexistence, and large deviations.  Those questions are logically
independent of the total-variation condensation theorem, and the two
arguments are logically autonomous: no result of the companion manuscript
is used here, while its principal finite-window results do not require the
tail constant, stable law, or fractional correction proved here.  Keeping
the two settings separate also makes the scope of the present conclusion
explicit.  The argument establishes structural consequences specific to
this nonmultiplicative continuant model and its Fibonacci generator renewal;
it does not establish a new universal mechanism for condensation or
freezing.
