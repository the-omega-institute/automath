You are refereeing the attached 25-page paper again. Your earlier round rejected it, and you were
explicit that this was a rejection for INCREMENTALITY, not for a mathematical error. I am not asking
you to re-derive that. I am asking what to do with it.

(1) WHAT WOULD MAKE IT NON-INCREMENTAL? Name the specific additional theorem that would change your
verdict - not a direction, a statement. If the paper proved X, would you send it out? Give X as
precisely as you can, and say roughly how hard you expect X to be and whether the machinery already

(2) IF IT STAYS AS IT IS, where does it belong? Name the strongest journal that would plausibly
accept the paper unchanged, with a rough numeric probability, and say why you are not naming the next
one up. An incremental but correct and well-written paper usually has a home; I want to know its
name rather than keep polishing.

(3) IS ANYTHING IN IT OVERSOLD? Compare the abstract and introduction against the theorems clause by
clause, comparing hypotheses and quantifiers rather than conclusions. In sibling manuscripts of mine
this has repeatedly turned up advertised claims stronger than what is proved, including one that was
false against its own cited theorem. Assume the same is possible here.
