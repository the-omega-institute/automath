You refereed this paper twice. This is not a review request. It is a specific mathematical

THE CLAIM IN THE PAPER. Lemma "from the local denominator layer to the cost tail" asserts
  b_{2d+1}(sigma_0) ~ b_C d^{-sigma_0},   b_C = 2 (zeta(s-1)/zeta(s))^2 = 2 rho^2 = 8 at s = sigma_0,
justifying it as a product of two absolutely convergent series equal to 2 rho_s^2.

WHAT I MEASURED. Computing b_{2d+1}(sigma_0) exactly (it is a finite sum: the cost class d consists
of the 2^{d-1} negative-continued-fraction vectors e_i >= 2 with sum(e_i - 1) = d), I get for
b_{2d+1}(sigma_0) * d^{sigma_0}:
   d = 10 : 8.41      d = 15 : 11.58     d = 20 : 13.22     d = 25 : 13.86
monotonically increasing, increments decaying at ratio 0.744. It passes 8 at d = 10 and does not
come back. Fitting A + B/d over the last eight points gives A = 16.9. The value is insensitive to
sigma_0: perturbing sigma_0 by 0.01 moves it by 0.22. I used
sigma_0 = 2.478750785733960260671487261390, the root of zeta(s-1)/zeta(s) = 2 to forty digits, and I
verified the underlying bijection independently - the cost classes have exactly 2^{d-1} members and
the enumerated sum agrees with sum_{q>=2} phi(q) q^{-s} to 1e-12.

WHY I THINK THE CONSTANT IS 4 rho^2 = 16, NOT 2 rho^2 = 8.

First, an identity I verified on all 48,517 reduced fractions with q < 400 and found exact:
  d(p/q) = ( sum of the regular continued-fraction partial quotients of q/p ) - 1.
So the cost class d is exactly the set of q/p = [a_0; a_1, ..., a_k] with sum a_i = d + 1, and
b_{2d+1}(sigma) is the sum of K(a_0,...,a_k)^{-sigma} over that set, K the continuant.

For the continuant to stay of order d, one partial quotient must carry almost all of the mass while
the remaining partial quotients form BOUNDED patterns. The large quotient can sit anywhere in the
word, so it generically has a bounded pattern on EACH side, and the continuant factorises as
  K ~ a_i * K(left) * K(right).
Hence
  b_{2d+1}(sigma) ~ d^{-sigma} * S^2,   S := sum over all finite tails t of K(t)^{-sigma}.
Numerically S is at least 3.372 and rising with increments decaying near 0.7, consistent with
S = 2 rho = 4 - the factor 2 being the two continued-fraction representations of each rational. That
gives b_C = S^2 = 4 rho^2 = 16, which the data supports and 8 does not.

My reading of the error: the paper does correctly see a product of two series - the two sides of the
large quotient - but assigns the factor 2 once to the product rather than once to EACH side. Two
copies of 2 rho give 4 rho^2, not 2 rho^2.
