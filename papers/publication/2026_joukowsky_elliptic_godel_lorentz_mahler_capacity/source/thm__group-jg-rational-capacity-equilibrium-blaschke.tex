\subsection{Degree-Two Joukowsky Boundary Theory}\label{subsec:main-joukowsky-boundary-scale}

The boundary theory begins with a lower-truncated rational pullback
kernel interface and then specializes to the degree-two map
\(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\).  In that specialization the collision
relation becomes \(z=t\) or \(zt=r^{-2}\), which is disjoint from
\(\TT^2\) except on the diagonal.  This elementary skeleton is the
common source of the capacity formula, the sharp Fourier equilibrium
deficit, and the finite-Blaschke lift theorem.

\paragraph{Fixed-Scale Joukowsky Consequences.}\label{par:fixed-scale-joukowsky-package-scope}
At a fixed scale \(r>1\), the analytic conclusions eventually assembled
in Corollary \ref{thm:main-joukowsky-boundary-scale} provide the capacity
value, the ellipse, the pushed-forward equilibrium measure, the Fourier
deficit, and the finite-Blaschke Haar-lift condition.  The transport
polynomial \(Q_r(P)\), the one-scale annular shell theorem, and the
Rouch\'e selector are added later in the fixed-scale synthesis section,
after their algebraic hypotheses have been proved.

\subsection{Auxiliary lower-truncated pullback kernel}\label{subsec:degree-two-joukowsky-specialization}

The general rational-map input for this section is only the standard
lower-truncated pullback identity and the pushforward variational
principle, collected in Appendix~\ref{app:auxiliary-rational-pullbacks}.
No closed formula for \(\operatorname{cap}\) of general rational images,
no Fourier diagonalization, and no rational inverse theorem is claimed
here.
\begin{proposition}[Auxiliary lower-truncated pullback interface]\label{prop:main-rational-pullback-interface}
Let \(R=A/B\) be nonconstant, with coprime \(A,B\in\CC[z]\), and let
\(K\subset\CC\) be compact, nonpolar, and pole-free for \(R\).  Put
\[
\Psi_R(z,t):=\frac{A(z)B(t)-A(t)B(z)}{z-t},
\]
with the polynomial extension to \(z=t\).  Then for every
\(\mu\in\mathcal P(K)\),
\[
I(R_\ast\mu)=
\iint_{K^2}\log\left|
\frac{(z-t)\Psi_R(z,t)}{B(z)B(t)}
\right|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t),
\]
interpreted by lower truncation.  Writing \(R(z)-R(t)\) through this
divided difference only names the diagonal, branch, and equal-value
collision loci.  The rest of the paper uses this interface only after
the specialization \(R=J_r\).
The accompanying capacity equality is only the classical variational
principle after pushforward,
\[
\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))
=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(K)}I(R_\ast\mu).
\]
Its equality cases are exactly the lifts of the equilibrium measure of
\(R(K)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is Proposition~\ref{prop:general-rational-image-capacity-pullback}
from Appendix~\ref{app:auxiliary-rational-pullbacks}.
\end{proof}

For the map used in the paper these facts reduce immediately to one
explicit statement: for
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}=\frac{rz^2+r^{-1}}{z},\qquad r>1,
\]
the collision equation is
\[
J_r(z)=J_r(t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (z-t)(rzt-r^{-1})=0.
\]
Thus the only off-diagonal partner is the involution
\(t=r^{-2}/z\), and this graph misses \(\TT^2\).  The theorem below is
the form used in the sequel; all nonpolarity, selection, and general
rational-pushforward details have been moved out of the main line.
\begin{theorem}[Degree-two Joukowsky boundary reduction]\label{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation}
Let \(r>1\), let
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}=\frac{rz^2+r^{-1}}{z}
\]
  and let \(K\subset\CC\setminus\{0\}\) be compact and nonpolar.  Equivalently, \(J_r=A/B\) with coprime polynomials \(A(z)=rz^2+r^{-1}\) and \(B(z)=z\), so \(K\) avoids the unique pole.  All energies below are lower-truncated extended logarithmic energies, and the displayed pullback integral is the single truncated kernel \(\log|J_r(z)-J_r(t)|\) before any separation into singular and bounded factors.  Then \(J_r(K)\) is compact and nonpolar and
\[
\log\operatorname{cap}(J_r(K))
=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(K)}\iint_{K\times K}\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|\,
\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t),
\]
where the integral is the lower-truncated extended value; equivalently the displayed supremum is \(\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(K)}I((J_r)_\ast\mu)\).  Measures with infinite negative pushed-forward energy simply contribute \(-\infty\), while maximizers necessarily have finite energy.  The complete collision set on \(K\times K\) is
\[
\{(z,t):z=t\}\ \cup\ \{(z,t):zt=r^{-2}\}.
\]
Thus every collision is either the ordinary logarithmic diagonal or the single involutive partner \(t=r^{-2}/z\).  Each vertical collision section contains at most the two points \(z\) and \(r^{-2}/z\), with coalescence only at the critical fixed points \(z=\pm r^{-1}\).  The maximizers are exactly the lifts of the equilibrium measure of \(J_r(K)\):
\[
\mathcal E_{J_r}(\mu)=\log\operatorname{cap}(J_r(K))
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(J_r)_\ast\mu=\nu_{J_r(K)}.
\]
In particular the off-diagonal graph is disjoint from \(\TT\times\TT\).  Hence the boundary map \(J_r:\TT\to E_r:=J_r(\TT)\) is a homeomorphism, every \(\sigma\in\mathcal P(E_r)\) has a unique source measure \(\eta\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) with \(J_{r\ast}\eta=\sigma\), and
\[
I(\sigma)=
\iint_{\TT^2}
\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|
\,\dd\eta(z)\dd\eta(t).
\]
Thus the elliptic-boundary equilibrium problem in Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} has only the ordinary diagonal collision singularity on the source circle.

Moreover, this theorem is the capacity bridge used in the sequel.  Proposition \ref{prop:general-rational-image-capacity-pullback} enters only as infrastructure for the lower-truncated rational kernel: after the specialization \(A(z)=rz^2+r^{-1}\), \(B(z)=z\), all nontrivial singular information descends to the two explicit relations \(z=t\) and \(zt=r^{-2}\).  In particular the source-circle boundary problem, the sharp Fourier deficit, and the finite Blaschke-cover equilibrium theorem depend on no further rational-map ambiguity beyond this degree-two skeleton.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(A(z)=rz^2+r^{-1}\) and \(B(z)=z\), the difference quotient in Proposition \ref{prop:general-rational-image-capacity-pullback} is
\[
\Psi_{J_r}(z,t)=\frac{(rz^2+r^{-1})t-(rt^2+r^{-1})z}{z-t}
=rzt-r^{-1}.
\]
Substitution into the pullback kernel gives
\[
\log|z-t|+
\log|rzt-r^{-1}|-
\log|z|-
\log|t|
=
\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|.
\]
The off-diagonal collision relation is \(rzt-r^{-1}=0\), equivalently \(zt=r^{-2}\).  If \(|z|=|t|=1\), this relation cannot hold for \(r>1\).  The maximizer statement is the specialization of Proposition \ref{prop:general-rational-image-capacity-pullback} to \(R=J_r\).  The section bound follows from the displayed factorization: for fixed \(z\), a collision point \(t\) must be either \(z\) or \(r^{-2}/z\), and these coincide exactly when \(z^2=r^{-2}\), that is, when \(z=\pm r^{-1}\).

It remains to record the boundary descent, since this is the degree-two form used later.  If \(z,t\in\TT\), then the relation \(J_r(z)=J_r(t)\) forces either \(z=t\) or \(zt=r^{-2}\).  The second alternative is impossible on \(\TT^2\), so \(J_r\) is injective on \(\TT\).  It is continuous from the compact space \(\TT\) onto the Hausdorff space \(E_r\), hence is a homeomorphism.  Therefore every \(\sigma\in\mathcal P(E_r)\) has the unique source \(\eta=(J_r^{-1})_\ast\sigma\).  Substituting \(K=\TT\) in the kernel identity above and pushing the lower-truncated logarithmic integral through this homeomorphism gives the displayed boundary energy formula.  Thus the final assertion follows by specializing the general singularity description in Proposition \ref{prop:general-rational-image-capacity-pullback} to the unit circle.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Degree-two theorem controls the later equilibrium chain]\label{cor:primary-joukowsky-capacity-chain}
Let \(r>1\), let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product, and let \(R_{B,r}=J_r\circ B\).  For every pair of Borel probability measures \(\mu_1,\mu_2\) on \(\TT\), if
\[
B_\ast\mu_1=B_\ast\mu_2,
\]
then
\[
I((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu_1)=I((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu_2)
\]
as lower-truncated extended energies.  Hence the covered Joukowsky equilibrium problem factors through the single boundary measure \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\) and through the degree-two kernel
\[
\log\left|(u-v)\left(r-r^{-1}u^{-1}v^{-1}\right)\right|,
\qquad u,v\in\TT.
\]
Consequently the finite Blaschke-cover theorem, the sharp Joukowsky equilibrium stability theorem, and the later fixed-scale classification have no independent rational-pullback input: their capacity normalization is exactly Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation} together with the boundary descent in Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation} gives the complete boundary kernel for \(J_r\) on \(\TT\): the off-diagonal partner relation \(uv=r^{-2}\) is disjoint from \(\TT^2\), and the boundary energy of any pushforward \(J_{r\ast}\eta\) is the lower-truncated integral of the displayed kernel against \(\eta\times\eta\).  Since \((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu)\), equality of \(B_\ast\mu_1\) and \(B_\ast\mu_2\) gives equality of the two energies by substituting the same measure \(\eta\) into this kernel formula.  The final sentence is just the same factorization read through the hypotheses of the subsequent results: Theorem \ref{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity} diagonalizes this boundary kernel on \(\TT\), Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} applies it after the finite cover \(B\), and the fixed-scale classification uses the resulting scale \(\log r\).  No part of this argument invokes a general rational map after the degree-two Joukowsky specialization has been made.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Degree-two boundary collision reduction]\label{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity}
Let \(r>1\).  On the source circle \(\TT\), the degree-two Joukowsky map has no branch-graph collision except the ordinary diagonal:
\[
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:J_r(z)=J_r(t)\}=\{(z,z):z\in\TT\}.
\]
Consequently the boundary kernel on \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\) is exactly
\[
\log|J_r(z)-J_r(t)|=
\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|,
\qquad z,t\in\TT,
\]
where the second factor is continuous and nonzero on \(\TT^2\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation}, the full collision set for \(J_r\) is the union of the diagonal \(z=t\) and the involutive partner relation \(zt=r^{-2}\).  On \(\TT^2\) the second relation is impossible, because \(|zt|=1\) while \(r^{-2}<1\).  Hence the only boundary collision for \(J_r\) is the ordinary diagonal.  The displayed identity is the same theorem specialized to \(K=\TT\), and the factor \(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\) cannot vanish on \(\TT^2\), since vanishing would again imply \(|zt|=r^{-2}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Capacity normalization of \(E_r\)]\label{lem:joukowsky-ellipse-capacity-normalization}
Let \(r>1\), put \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), and let \(z_+(w)\) denote the branch
of the inverse of \(J_r\) satisfying \(|z_+(w)|>1\) near infinity.  Then
\[
z_+(w)=\frac{w}{r}+O(w^{-1}),\qquad w\to\infty,
\]
and consequently
\[
\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r,\qquad
\nu_{E_r}=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The inverse relation \(w=J_r(z)\) is
\[
r z^2-wz+r^{-1}=0.
\]
The root with modulus \(>1\) for large \(w\) is
\[
z_+(w)=\frac{w+\sqrt{w^2-4}}{2r},
\]
where the square root is chosen so that \(\sqrt{w^2-4}=w+O(w^{-1})\)
at infinity.  Hence \(z_+(w)=w/r+O(w^{-1})\).

On \(\{|z|>1\}\), the derivative \(J_r'(z)=r-r^{-1}z^{-2}\) has no zero,
since its critical points \(z=\pm r^{-1}\) lie in the unit disk.  The
collision relation \(J_r(z)=J_r(t)\) implies \(z=t\) or \(zt=r^{-2}\);
the second alternative is impossible when \(|z|,|t|>1\).  Thus the
exterior branch is univalent and maps \(\{|z|>1\}\) conformally onto
\(\CC\setminus E_r\), with inverse \(z_+\).  Pulling back the Green
function of the exterior unit disk gives
\[
g_{\CC\setminus E_r}(w)=\log|z_+(w)|
=\log|w|-\log r+o(1),\qquad w\to\infty.
\]
For a compact nonpolar set \(K\), the Green function with pole at infinity
has the normalization
\[
g_{\CC\setminus K}(w)=\log|w|-\log\operatorname{cap}(K)+o(1).
\]
Comparing constants gives \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\).  The same
exterior conformal map identifies harmonic measure from infinity on
\(E_r\) with the pushforward of normalized arclength on \(\TT\).  By
Frostman's theorem this harmonic measure is the equilibrium measure,
so \(\nu_{E_r}=J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/(2\pi))\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Boundary finite-energy equivalence under \(J_r\)]\label{lem:joukowsky-boundary-energy-equivalence}
Let \(r>1\).  For all \(z,t\in\TT\),
\[
(r-r^{-1})|z-t|
\le |J_r(z)-J_r(t)|
\le (r+r^{-1})|z-t|.
\]
Consequently a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\TT\) has finite circle logarithmic energy if and only if \(J_{r\ast}\mu\) has finite logarithmic energy on \(E_r\).  More precisely,
\[
I_{\TT}(\mu)+\log(r-r^{-1})
\le I(J_{r\ast}\mu)
\le I_{\TT}(\mu)+\log(r+r^{-1}),
\]
with the evident interpretation that both sides are \(-\infty\) in the non-finite-energy case.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The exact factorization on \(\TT^2\) is
\[
J_r(z)-J_r(t)=(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right).
\]
Since \(|z^{-1}t^{-1}|=1\), the reverse and direct triangle inequalities give
\[
r-r^{-1}\le |r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}|
\le r+r^{-1}.
\]
Taking logarithms gives
\[
\log|z-t|+\log(r-r^{-1})
\le \log|J_r(z)-J_r(t)|
\le \log|z-t|+\log(r+r^{-1})
\]
as lower-truncated extended kernels.  Integrating the truncated kernels and passing to the limit proves the two energy inequalities and hence the equivalence of finite energy.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Joukowsky boundary skeleton obstruction]\label{thm:joukowsky-boundary-skeleton-obstruction}
Let \(r>1\), let \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), and let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product.  Among the two algebraic collision relations in the degree-two Joukowsky pullback, only the diagonal relation survives on the boundary after composition with \(B\):
\[
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:J_r(B(z))=J_r(B(t))\}
=
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:B(z)=B(t)\}.
\]
Consequently, for every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) and \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\), the lower-truncated energy of the covered ellipse depends only on \(\eta\):
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)
=
\iint_{\TT^2}
\log\left|(u-v)\left(r-r^{-1}u^{-1}v^{-1}\right)\right|
\,\dd\eta(u)\dd\eta(v).
\]
The off-diagonal Joukowsky partner relation contributes no boundary singularity and no additional finite-cover invariant.  Therefore, if two boundary measures \(\mu_1,\mu_2\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) satisfy \(B_\ast\mu_1=B_\ast\mu_2\), then
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu_1)=I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu_2),
\]
with all energies interpreted by lower truncation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The degree-two collision theorem gives the complete collision set for \(J_r\) as the union of the diagonal relation \(u=v\) and the partner relation \(uv=r^{-2}\).  Since \(B(\TT)\subset\TT\), a covered boundary collision satisfies
\[
J_r(B(z))=J_r(B(t))
\]
only if either \(B(z)=B(t)\) or \(B(z)B(t)=r^{-2}\).  The second alternative is impossible on \(\TT^2\), because \(|B(z)B(t)|=1\) while \(r^{-2}<1\).  This proves the displayed collision skeleton.

For \(u,v\in\TT\), Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity} gives
\[
\log|J_r(u)-J_r(v)|=
\log\left|(u-v)\left(r-r^{-1}u^{-1}v^{-1}\right)\right|,
\]
where the second factor is continuous and nonzero on \(\TT^2\).  Applying the lower-truncated pushforward identity to the map \((z,t)\mapsto(B(z),B(t))\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)
&=\iint_{\TT^2}\log|J_r(B(z))-J_r(B(t))|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t)\\
&=\iint_{\TT^2}\log|J_r(u)-J_r(v)|\,\dd\eta(u)\dd\eta(v)\\
&=\iint_{\TT^2}
\log\left|(u-v)\left(r-r^{-1}u^{-1}v^{-1}\right)\right|
\,\dd\eta(u)\dd\eta(v),
\end{aligned}
\]
again with all logarithmic integrals interpreted by lower truncation.  Thus the energy is a function only of \(B_\ast\mu\), and equal pushforwards give equal energies.

Thus the covered problem has no kernel invariant beyond the pushed-forward boundary measure.  The later sharp equilibrium theorem will identify which pushed-forward measure maximizes this already descended problem.
\end{proof}

The next theorem is deliberately only a descent theorem.  It records the
homeomorphism, lift uniqueness, boundary kernel, and finite-cover
factorization that follow from the degree-two Joukowsky collision
skeleton.  The sharp variational value and equality class are proved
later, after the Fourier diagonalization, in Corollary
\ref{cor:sharp-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers}.  This keeps the
topological descent interface independent of the equilibrium theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Exact boundary descent for Joukowsky covers]\label{thm:exact-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers}
Let \(r>1\), put \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), and interpret logarithmic energies by lower truncation.  This is a boundary-kernel descent statement only: it asserts no capacity value, no maximizing measure, and no equality class.  Those variational conclusions enter later through Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} and Corollary \ref{cor:sharp-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers}.  Then the boundary parametrization \(J_r:\TT\to E_r\) is a homeomorphism, and every \(\sigma\in\mathcal P(E_r)\) has a unique source measure \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) satisfying \(J_{r\ast}\mu=\sigma\).  For this source measure,
\[
I(\sigma)=\iint_{\TT^2}\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t).
\]
If \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product, then the covered problem descends exactly through the single measure \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\): the complete covered collision relation on \(\TT^2\) is
\[
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:J_r(B(z))=J_r(B(t))\}
=
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:B(z)=B(t)\}.
\]
For every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\), with \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\), one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)
&=\iint_{\TT^2}\log|J_r(B(z))-J_r(B(t))|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t)\\
&=\iint_{\TT^2}\log\left|(B(z)-B(t))
\left(r-r^{-1}B(z)^{-1}B(t)^{-1}\right)\right|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t)\\
&=I(J_{r\ast}\eta),
\end{aligned}
\]
and the first two integrals are finite exactly when \(I(J_{r\ast}\eta)>-\infty\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity} shows that \(J_r\) is injective on \(\TT\).  Since \(J_r:\TT\to E_r\) is a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, it is a homeomorphism.  Therefore every probability measure \(\sigma\) on \(E_r\) has the unique lift \(\mu=(J_r^{-1})_\ast\sigma\), and the lower-truncated pushforward identity gives the displayed energy formula.

Now let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product.  Since \(B(\TT)\subset\TT\), injectivity of \(J_r\) on \(\TT\) gives
\[
J_r(B(z))=J_r(B(t))
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B(z)=B(t),
\]
which proves the covered collision classification.  Substituting \(B(z)\) and \(B(t)\) into the boundary kernel identity gives the displayed covered-kernel formula; the factor
\[
r-r^{-1}B(z)^{-1}B(t)^{-1}
\]
is continuous and nonzero on \(\TT^2\), because \(|B(z)B(t)|=1\) whereas \(r^{-2}<1\).  Hence the lower-truncated singular set is exactly \(B(z)=B(t)\).

Functoriality of pushforward gives
\[
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu).
\]
Applying the lower-truncated logarithmic energy functional to both sides proves the energy identity and the equivalence of finiteness.  Thus all covered-boundary kernel calculations factor through the single pushed-forward source measure \(B_\ast\mu\), before any extremal theorem is invoked.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lower-truncated Fourier evaluation on the circle]\label{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation}
Let \(\mu\) be a probability measure on \(\TT\) with finite logarithmic energy.  Then
\[
\iint_{\TT\times\TT}\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=-
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k},
\]
where the integral is the lower-truncated logarithmic integral.  If
\(q\in\CC\) and \(|q|<1\), then the continuous kernel
\(\log|1-qe^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|\) satisfies
\[
\iint\log|1-qe^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=-
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Re}(q^k\widehat\mu(k)^2)}{k}.
\]
The finite-energy hypothesis is part of the assertion: for example,
\(\mu=\delta_1\) is excluded, since its circle logarithmic energy is
\(-\infty\) and the singular-kernel Fourier series is divergent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(0<\rho<1\), the Abel-regularized identity
\[
\log|1-\rho e^{i(\theta-\phi)}|
=-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^k\cos k(\theta-\phi)}{k}
\]
is uniformly convergent on compact subannuli and may be integrated term by term against \(\mu\times\mu\).  It gives
\[
\iint\log|1-\rho e^{i(\theta-\phi)}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=-
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^k|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}.
\]
We now pass from the Abel kernels to the lower-truncated boundary kernel without using any pointwise monotonicity in \(\rho\).  Put
\[
\kappa(\alpha)=\log|1-e^{i\alpha}|,
\qquad
\kappa_\rho(\alpha)=\log|1-\rho e^{i\alpha}|,
\]
and let \(\lambda\) be the image of \(\mu\times\mu\) under
\((\theta,\phi)\mapsto\theta-\phi\).  The finite-energy hypothesis is exactly
\(\int\kappa\,\dd\lambda>-\infty\), with the integral understood by lower truncation, because
\(|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|=|1-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}|\).  We justify the Abel passage directly.  The kernel \(\kappa\) is bounded above by \(\log 2\), and the finite-energy hypothesis gives \(\kappa^-\in L^1(\lambda)\); in particular \(\lambda\{0\}=0\).  For \(\alpha\ne0\), \(\kappa_\rho(\alpha)\to\kappa(\alpha)\) as \(\rho\uparrow1\).  Moreover, for \(\rho\ge1/2\),
\[
|1-\rho e^{i\alpha}|^2=(1-\rho)^2+\rho|1-e^{i\alpha}|^2,
\]
and hence
\[
\kappa(\alpha)+\frac12\log\rho\le \kappa_\rho(\alpha)\le \log2
\]
for \(\lambda\)-almost every \(\alpha\).  Thus \(\kappa_\rho^+\le\log2\) and \(\kappa_\rho^-\le \kappa^-+\frac12\log2\) for \(\rho\ge1/2\).  Dominated convergence, with the right-hand side interpreted as the lower-truncated logarithmic integral, gives
\[
\lim_{\rho\uparrow1}\int\kappa_\rho(\alpha)\,\dd\lambda(\alpha)
=\int\kappa(\alpha)\,\dd\lambda(\alpha),
\]
and hence the left side of the Abel identity tends to
\[
\iint\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi).
\]
On the right, \(|\widehat\mu(k)|^2/k\ge0\), so monotone convergence for the nonnegative partial Abel sums gives
\[
\lim_{\rho\uparrow1}
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^k|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}
=
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}
\]
in \([0,\infty]\).  This is the only passage from the singular Abel
series to the boundary Fourier series; it uses monotone convergence for
the nonnegative sums, while dominated convergence was used only for the
lower-truncated kernel integral.  The finite lower-truncated energy just
obtained forces this series to be finite, and the first identity follows.

For complex \(|q|<1\), the principal power series for
\(\log(1-qe^{-i(\theta+\phi)})\) is uniformly and absolutely convergent
on \(\TT^2\).  Taking real parts gives
\[
\log|1-qe^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|
=-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\operatorname{Re}\!\left(q^k e^{-ik(\theta+\phi)}\right)}{k},
\]
again uniformly and absolutely.  Termwise integration gives the second
identity because
\[
\iint e^{-ik(\theta+\phi)}\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=\widehat\mu(k)^2.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Joukowsky boundary kernel diagonalization]\label{lem:joukowsky-boundary-kernel-diagonalization}
Let \(r>1\), let \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\), and let
\(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) have finite circle logarithmic energy.  Then
\[
I(J_{r\ast}\mu)
=
\log r-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2+r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\mu(k)^2)}{k}.
\]
The identity is a finite-energy identity only: it is obtained by applying
the lower-truncated circle Fourier formula to the diagonal singularity and
ordinary uniform Fourier integration to the remaining continuous factor.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(z=e^{i\theta}\) and \(w=e^{i\phi}\), the degree-two boundary
mechanism is the factorization
\[
J_r(z)-J_r(w)
=(z-w)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}w^{-1}\right).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\log|J_r(e^{i\theta})-J_r(e^{i\phi})|
=\log r+\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|
 +\log|1-r^{-2}e^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|.
\]
The first term after \(\log r\) is the only singular term.  By Lemma
\ref{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation},
\[
\iint\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}.
\]
The second factor is continuous because \(r^{-2}<1\), and its Fourier
series is uniformly and absolutely convergent:
\[
\log|1-r^{-2}e^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|
=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\frac{r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(e^{-ik(\theta+\phi)})}{k}.
\]
Termwise integration gives
\[
\iint\log|1-r^{-2}e^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|
\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\frac{r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\mu(k)^2)}{k}.
\]
Combining the singular diagonal contribution with the continuous
reciprocal-scale contribution proves the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem A: finite-energy Joukowsky Fourier identity]\label{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity}
Let \(r>1\), let \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\), and let
\[
E_r=J_r(\TT),\qquad \mu_r=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right).
\]
For a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\TT\), write
\[
\widehat\mu(k)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-ik\theta}\,\dd\mu(\theta),\qquad k\in\ZZ,
\]
and let
\[
I(\sigma):=\iint\log|x-y|\,\dd\sigma(x)\dd\sigma(y)
\]
denote logarithmic energy as the lower-truncated extended logarithmic integral.
If \(I_{\TT}(\mu)>-\infty\), equivalently \(J_{r\ast}\mu\) has finite logarithmic energy on \(E_r\), then
\[
\boxed{\;
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu)
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2+r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\mu(k)^2)}{k}.
\;}
\]
This displayed equality is a finite-energy identity, not an assertion
obtained by termwise integration for arbitrary probability measures.
Consequently,
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-r^{-2k})|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}
\le \log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu)
\le
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1+r^{-2k})|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}.
\]
The finite-energy restriction is essential.  If \(\mu=\delta_1\), then
\(I(J_{r\ast}\delta_1)=-\infty\), while \(\widehat\mu(k)=1\) for all
\(k\ge1\) and the displayed Fourier series diverges.
For each \(k\ge1\) the constants \(1+r^{-2k}\) and \(1-r^{-2k}\) are sharp on the \(k\)-th Fourier mode: for \(|\varepsilon|\le1/2\),
\[
\dd\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}=(1+2\varepsilon\cos k\theta)\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi},
\qquad
\dd\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}=(1+2\varepsilon\sin k\theta)\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}
\]
satisfy
\[
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon})=\frac{(1+r^{-2k})\varepsilon^2}{k},
\qquad
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon})=\frac{(1-r^{-2k})\varepsilon^2}{k}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exact finite-energy diagonalization is Lemma
\ref{lem:joukowsky-boundary-kernel-diagonalization}.  By Lemma
\ref{lem:joukowsky-boundary-energy-equivalence}, the same hypothesis is
equivalently the condition that the pushed-forward boundary measure
\(J_{r\ast}\mu\) have finite energy on \(E_r\).  The identity is not used
for arbitrary measures because the singular circle Fourier series need not
represent a finite energy outside that class.

Writing \(\widehat\mu(k)=x_k+i y_k\), the \(k\)-th summand numerator is
\[
|\widehat\mu(k)|^2+r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\mu(k)^2)
=(1+r^{-2k})x_k^2+(1-r^{-2k})y_k^2,
\]
which proves the two modewise bounds.  Equality in the left or right coefficient for a fixed mode is attained by purely imaginary or purely real \(\widehat\mu(k)\), respectively.  The densities \(\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}\) and \(\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}\) are nonnegative probability measures for \(|\varepsilon|\le1/2\), have no nonzero Fourier coefficients except in modes \(\pm k\), and have \(\widehat\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}(k)=\varepsilon\) and \(\widehat\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}(k)=-i\varepsilon\).  The displayed extremal identities follow.

Since every term in the deficit is nonnegative and \(1-r^{-2k}>0\), one has \(I(J_{r\ast}\mu)\le\log r\) for finite-energy source measures, with equality only when \(\widehat\mu(k)=0\) for all \(k\ne0\), that is, when \(\mu\) is Haar measure on \(\TT\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Abel passage and Frostman extremality are separate]\label{lem:abel-passage-frostman-separation}
Let \(r>1\), let \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\), and put
\(E_r=J_r(\TT)\).  For \(0<\rho<1\), set
\[
K_{\rho,r}(\theta,\phi)
:=\log r+\log|1-\rho e^{i(\theta-\phi)}|
  +\log|1-r^{-2}e^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|.
\]
Then the following two assertions have different hypotheses.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item If \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) has finite circle logarithmic energy, then
  \[
  \lim_{\rho\uparrow1}
  \iint K_{\rho,r}(\theta,\phi)\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
  =
  I(J_{r\ast}\mu),
  \]
  with the limit equal to the lower-truncated logarithmic energy on \(E_r\).  Equivalently the Abel-regularized identity passes to
  \[
  I(J_{r\ast}\mu)
  =
  \log r-\sum_{k\ge1}
  \frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2+r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\mu(k)^2)}{k}.
  \]
  No such Fourier identity is asserted outside the finite-energy class.

  \item For every \(\eta\in\mathcal P(\TT)\), with no finite-energy assumption,
  \[
  I(J_{r\ast}\eta)\le \log r,
  \]
  and equality holds if and only if \(\eta\) is Haar measure on \(\TT\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(\textup{(a)}\), the boundary factorization gives, for
\(z=e^{i\theta}\) and \(w=e^{i\phi}\),
\[
\log|J_r(z)-J_r(w)|
=\log r+\log|1-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}|
 +\log|1-r^{-2}e^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|.
\]
The first singular term is exactly the Abel passage proved under the
finite-energy hypothesis in Lemma
\ref{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation}.  The second term is a
continuous kernel, since \(r^{-2}<1\), and its Fourier series is uniformly
and absolutely convergent on \(\TT^2\).  Thus its integral is obtained by
ordinary termwise integration, while the singular term is obtained only as
the lower-truncated Abel limit supplied by that lemma.  Combining the two
terms gives the displayed identity, and the proof uses finite energy
precisely to identify the Abel limit with the lower-truncated singular
circle energy.

For \(\textup{(b)}\), first compute the capacity normalization by the
exterior map itself.  Since
\[
J_r'(z)=r-r^{-1}z^{-2}
\]
has its critical points \(z=\pm r^{-1}\) inside \(\{|z|<1\}\), the branch
of \(J_r\) on \(\{|z|>1\}\) maps the exterior disk conformally onto
\(\CC\setminus E_r\).  Its inverse branch \(z_+(w)\), characterized by
\(|z_+(w)|>1\), is obtained from
\[
r z^2-wz+r^{-1}=0
\]
and has the expansion
\[
z_+(w)=\frac{w}{r}+O(w^{-1}),\qquad w\to\infty .
\]
Pulling back the Green function of the exterior unit disk therefore gives
\[
g_{E_r}(w)=\log|z_+(w)|
=\log|w|-\log r+o(1),\qquad w\to\infty .
\]
With the standard normalization
\[
g_K(w)=\log|w|-\log\operatorname{cap}(K)+o(1)
\]
for a compact nonpolar set \(K\), the constant term is \(-\log r\), hence
\(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\).  Harmonic measure from infinity is
\[
\mu_r=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right).
\]
Frostman's variational principle for the compact nonpolar set \(E_r\)
therefore gives, for every \(\nu\in\mathcal P(E_r)\),
\[
I(\nu)\le\log\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=\log r,
\]
with equality precisely for the equilibrium measure \(\nu=\mu_r\).  Apply
this to \(\nu=J_{r\ast}\eta\).  Since \(J_r:\TT\to E_r\) is a homeomorphism
by Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity}, the equality
condition \(J_{r\ast}\eta=\mu_r\) is equivalent to
\(\eta=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).  This proof of the all-measure extremal
statement does not use the Abel--Fourier identity from \(\textup{(a)}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem B: all-measure Joukowsky equilibrium bound]\label{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability}\label{thm:all-measure-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}
Let \(r>1\), let \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\), put
\(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), and interpret logarithmic energies by lower
truncation.  For every \(\eta\in\mathcal P(\TT)\),
\[
I(J_{r\ast}\eta)\le \log r,
\]
with equality if and only if \(\eta\) is Haar measure.  Equivalently,
\(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\) and
\[
\mu_r=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right)
\]
is the unique equilibrium measure of \(E_r\).
This theorem is the all-measure variational bound only; it does not
extend the Fourier identity of Theorem
\ref{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity} beyond finite-energy
measures.  Singular measures such as \(\delta_1\) are included here only
through the inequality \(-\infty=I(J_{r\ast}\delta_1)\le\log r\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The all-measure assertion is Lemma
\ref{lem:abel-passage-frostman-separation}\textup{(b)}.  That lemma also
records separately that the Abel--Fourier passage belongs only to the
finite-energy statement in Lemma
\ref{lem:abel-passage-frostman-separation}\textup{(a)} and Theorem
\ref{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp boundary descent for Joukowsky covers]\label{cor:sharp-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers}
Let \(r>1\), put \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), and interpret logarithmic energies by lower truncation.  The exact descent theorem and the sharp equilibrium theorem combine as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item The boundary variational problem has the sharp extended-energy form
  \[
  \sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)} I(J_{r\ast}\mu)=\log r,
  \qquad
  I(J_{r\ast}\mu)=\log r
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
  \]

  \item If \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product and \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\), then
  \[
  I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=I(J_{r\ast}\eta)
  \]
  for every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\).  Consequently
  \[
  \sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)}I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=\log r,
  \qquad
  I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=\log r
  \Longleftrightarrow
  B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
Thus the exact boundary descent is a kernel and pushforward statement, while the sharp value \(\log r\) and the Haar-lift equality class enter only after Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The sharp Joukowsky equilibrium theorem gives
\[
I(J_{r\ast}\eta)\le\log r
\]
for every probability measure \(\eta\) on \(\TT\), with equality exactly when \(\eta=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).  Taking \(\eta=\mu\) proves \textup{(a)}.  Since \(J_r:\TT\to E_r\) is a homeomorphism by Theorem \ref{thm:exact-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers}, this is also the unique equilibrium statement on \(E_r\).

For a finite Blaschke product \(B\), Theorem \ref{thm:exact-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers} gives the energy identity
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=I(J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu)).
\]
Applying the sharp equilibrium bound to \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\) gives the covered supremum and the equality condition \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).  Haar lifts exist for every finite Blaschke product by Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula}, so the supremum is attained.
Equivalently, Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-haar-lift-fiber} identifies the whole maximizer set as the Haar-lift fiber through \(B\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Calibrated two-test identity for the Joukowsky scale]\label{cor:two-mode-joukowsky-scale-rigidity}
Fix an integer \(k\ge1\) and a real number \(\varepsilon\) with \(0<|\varepsilon|\le1/2\).  For \(r>1\), let
\[
\dd\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}=(1+2\varepsilon\cos k\theta)\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi},
\qquad
\dd\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}=(1+2\varepsilon\sin k\theta)\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Then, within this fixed probe family and not as a general inverse invariant, the calibrated ordered pair of boundary pushforward energies
\[
\mathcal A_k(r,\varepsilon):=
\left(
I(J_{r\ast}\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}),
I(J_{r\ast}\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon})
\right)
\]
determines the Joukowsky scale \(r\) uniquely within this prescribed two-test family.  More precisely, if
\[
E_c=I(J_{r\ast}\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}),
\qquad
E_s=I(J_{r\ast}\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}),
\]
then
\[
E_s-E_c=\frac{2\varepsilon^2}{k}r^{-2k},
\qquad
\log r=\frac{E_c+E_s}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{k},
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{\;
r=\left(\frac{2\varepsilon^2}{k(E_s-E_c)}\right)^{1/(2k)}
=\exp\!\left(\frac{E_c+E_s}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{k}\right).
\;}
\]
Consequently, for \(r,s>1\), equality
\[
\mathcal A_k(r,\varepsilon)=\mathcal A_k(s,\varepsilon)
\]
forces \(r=s\).  The same conclusion holds after a finite Blaschke cover \(B\) whenever the pushed-forward probe measures \(B_\ast\nu^c\) and \(B_\ast\nu^s\) are \(\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}\) and \(\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}\), respectively; in that case the two covered energies
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\nu^c),
\qquad
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\nu^s)
\]
  recover the same \(r\).  This corollary is not an inverse theorem for arbitrary energy observations: two unspecified energies, even if taken from measures on the same ellipse, need not determine \(r\) without the prescribed probe family and its normalization.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The measures \(\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}\) and \(\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}\) have bounded densities on \(\TT\), hence finite logarithmic energy.  The sharp mode computation in Theorem \ref{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity} therefore applies and gives
\[
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon})=\frac{(1+r^{-2k})\varepsilon^2}{k},
\qquad
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon})=\frac{(1-r^{-2k})\varepsilon^2}{k}.
\]
With \(E_c\) and \(E_s\) as in the statement, subtracting the first energy identity from the second gives
\[
E_s-E_c=
\left(\log r-\frac{(1-r^{-2k})\varepsilon^2}{k}\right)
-
\left(\log r-\frac{(1+r^{-2k})\varepsilon^2}{k}\right)
=\frac{2\varepsilon^2}{k}r^{-2k}.
\]
This quantity is positive because \(\varepsilon\ne0\) and \(r>1\), so the first displayed reconstruction formula for \(r\) follows.  Adding the two identities gives
\[
E_c+E_s=2\log r-\frac{2\varepsilon^2}{k},
\]
which is the logarithmic reconstruction formula.  Either formula is injective on \((1,\infty)\), so equality of the ordered pairs for two scales \(r,s>1\) implies \(r=s\).

For the covered assertion, the descent identity in Theorem \ref{thm:exact-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers} gives
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\nu)=I(J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\nu))
\]
for every probability measure \(\nu\) on \(\TT\).  Applying this first to \(\nu=\nu^c\) and then to \(\nu=\nu^s\) reduces the two covered energies to the two already treated probe energies for \(\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}\) and \(\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}\).  The same reconstruction formulas therefore recover \(r\) after the finite Blaschke lift.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Probe calibration is necessary]\label{prop:two-mode-calibration-necessary}
Fix \(k\ge1\).  The scale recovery in Corollary
\ref{cor:two-mode-joukowsky-scale-rigidity} is a statement about the
prescribed ordered cosine--sine probe family and not about arbitrary
boundary energy observations.  More precisely, for any two scales
\(1<r<s\) there are finite-energy probability measures
\(\mu_r,\nu_r,\mu_s,\nu_s\) on \(\TT\), all with smooth positive
density, such that
\[
I(J_{r\ast}\mu_r)=I(J_{s\ast}\mu_s),
\qquad
I(J_{r\ast}\nu_r)=I(J_{s\ast}\nu_s).
\]
Thus an unordered or uncalibrated pair of finite-energy observations
does not determine the scale \(r\); the recovery formulas in Corollary
\ref{cor:two-mode-joukowsky-scale-rigidity} use the fixed probes and
the known normalization \((k,\varepsilon)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix a scale \(\rho>1\).  Let \(h_N(\theta)\dd\theta/(2\pi)\) be any
smooth positive approximate identity on \(\TT\), normalized to have
total mass one, and put
\[
\dd\lambda_{\rho,N,a}
=\bigl((1-a)+a h_N(\theta)\bigr)\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi},
\qquad 0\le a\le1.
\]
For fixed \(N\), the density is smooth and positive, and
the energy \(a\mapsto I(J_{\rho\ast}\lambda_{\rho,N,a})\) is continuous
on \([0,1]\), because the pushed-forward density is smooth on the
analytic Jordan curve \(E_\rho\) and the logarithmic kernel is locally
integrable against such densities.  At \(a=0\) the measure is Haar, so
Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} gives the value
\(\log\rho\).

As \(N\to\infty\), the self-interaction of \(h_N(\theta)\dd\theta\)
concentrates on the diagonal.  Since \(J_\rho:\TT\to E_\rho\) is a
homeomorphism and the local boundary kernel is
\(\log|\theta-\phi|+O(1)\), these smooth measures have
\[
I(J_{\rho\ast}(h_N\,\dd\theta/(2\pi)))\longrightarrow -\infty.
\]
Consequently, for every \(E<\log\rho\) there are \(N\) and \(a\in[0,1]\) with
\[
I(J_{\rho\ast}\lambda_{\rho,N,a})=E.
\]

Choose two distinct real numbers \(E,E'<\log r\).  Since \(r<s\), both
also lie below \(\log s\).  Applying the preceding paragraph at the two
scales \(\rho=r\) and \(\rho=s\) gives smooth positive source measures
whose pushed-forward Joukowsky energies are \(E\), and then again whose
energies are \(E'\).  Naming these four measures
\(\mu_r,\mu_s,\nu_r,\nu_s\) proves the displayed equalities.  The
measures depend on the scale and on the target energy; this is exactly
the freedom excluded by the calibrated probe family in Corollary
\ref{cor:two-mode-joukowsky-scale-rigidity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[All-measure Joukowsky equilibrium bound]\label{prop:extended-energy-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}
Let \(r>1\) and let \(\mu\) be an arbitrary Borel probability measure on \(\TT\).  With logarithmic energies interpreted by lower truncation,
\[
I(J_{r\ast}\mu)\le \log r.
\]
Equality holds if and only if \(\mu\) is Haar measure on \(\TT\).  Equivalently, for every probability measure \(\eta\) on \(\TT\), finite source energy is not needed for the sharp equilibrium bound
\[
I(J_{r\ast}\eta)\le \log r,
\]
and the unique maximizer is \(\eta=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).
This proposition records only the all-measure variational inequality.  The
Fourier deficit formula remains restricted to the finite-energy class in
Theorem \ref{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is Theorem \ref{thm:all-measure-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound},
repeated here for later reference in the finite-cover argument.  The
final formulation is the same assertion with \(\eta\) in place of
\(\mu\).
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Boundary covering formula for finite Blaschke products]\label{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula}
Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\ge1\).  Then \(B\) has no critical point on \(\TT\), the boundary map \(B:\TT\to\TT\) is an orientation-preserving degree-\(d\) covering, and for every continuous function \(f\) on \(\TT\),
\[
\int_{0}^{2\pi} f(B(e^{i\theta}))\,|B'(e^{i\theta})|\,\dd\theta
=d\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{it})\,\dd t.
\]
Consequently the probability measure
\[
\dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})=\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\,\dd\theta
\]
satisfies \(B_\ast\mu_B=\dd t/(2\pi)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
B(z)=\zeta\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{z-a_j}{1-\overline{a_j}z},
\qquad |\zeta|=1,
\qquad a_j\in\DD.
\]
The following boundary computation is the standard finite-Blaschke boundary argument and Poisson-kernel derivative formula \citep[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970}.  For \(z=e^{i\theta}\), differentiating a continuous boundary argument \(B(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\beta(\theta)}\) on any local arc gives
\[
\beta'(\theta)=\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{e^{i\theta}B'(e^{i\theta})}{B(e^{i\theta})}\right)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1-|a_j|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-a_j|^2}>0.
\]
Thus \(B'(e^{i\theta})\ne0\) on \(\TT\), and \(|B'(e^{i\theta})|=\beta'(\theta)\).  The argument increase over one full turn is \(\beta(2\pi)-\beta(0)=2\pi d\), so \(B:\TT\to\TT\) is an orientation-preserving covering of degree \(d\).  Partition \([0,2\pi]\) into the \(d\) inverse branches on which \(\beta\) maps the branch interval bijectively onto an interval of length \(2\pi\).  On each branch the substitution \(t=\beta(\theta)\) gives one copy of \(\int_0^{2\pi}f(e^{it})\,\dd t\).  Summing the \(d\) branches proves the change-of-variables formula, and division by \(2\pi d\) gives the asserted pushforward identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite Blaschke Haar-lift fiber]\label{lem:finite-blaschke-haar-lift-fiber}
Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\ge1\), and let
\[
m_{\TT}:=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}
\]
denote normalized Haar measure on \(\TT\).  A \emph{Haar lift through
\(B\)} means any probability measure \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) satisfying
\[
B_\ast\mu=m_{\TT}.
\]
Thus ``Haar lift'' names the whole pushforward fiber over \(m_{\TT}\), not
a distinguished source measure.  For every \(r>1\), the maximizers of
\[
\mu\longmapsto I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)
\]
over \(\mathcal P(\TT)\), with logarithmic energy interpreted by lower
truncation, are precisely the Haar lifts through \(B\).

The absolutely continuous measure
\[
\boxed{\;
\dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})
=\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\,\dd\theta
\;}
\]
is the canonical absolutely continuous Haar lift.  It is only one member of
the Haar-lift fiber; when \(d>1\), the source lift is not unique in general.
More precisely, the whole fiber is parametrized as follows.  A probability
measure \(\mu\) is a Haar lift through \(B\) if and only if there is a
measurable probability kernel
\[
u\longmapsto \kappa_u
\qquad (u\in\TT)
\]
such that \(\kappa_u\) is supported on the finite fiber \(B^{-1}\{u\}\) for
\(m_{\TT}\)-almost every \(u\), and
\[
\int_{\TT} f(z)\,\dd\mu(z)
=\int_{\TT}\left(\int_{B^{-1}\{u\}} f(z)\,\dd\kappa_u(z)\right)\dd m_{\TT}(u)
\]
for every continuous \(f\) on \(\TT\).  Since each fiber has \(d\) points,
this is equivalently a measurable choice of probability weights on the
points of \(B^{-1}\{u\}\).  The canonical lift \(\mu_B\) corresponds to the
uniform conditional weights
\[
\kappa_u=\frac1d\sum_{z\in B^{-1}\{u\}}\delta_z
\qquad\text{for }m_{\TT}\text{-almost every }u.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For any \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\), put \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\).  Then
\[
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}\eta.
\]
The all-measure Joukowsky equilibrium bound, Proposition
\ref{prop:extended-energy-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}, gives
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=I(J_{r\ast}\eta)\le \log r,
\]
with equality if and only if \(\eta=m_{\TT}\).  This is exactly the
condition \(B_\ast\mu=m_{\TT}\), proving the maximizer characterization.
The boundary covering formula in Lemma
\ref{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula} gives
\[
B_\ast\mu_B=m_{\TT},
\]
so the displayed absolutely continuous measure is a Haar lift.  It is
canonical because it is obtained from the covering Jacobian \(|B'|/d\).
It remains only to justify the fiber parametrization.  If
\(B_\ast\mu=m_{\TT}\), standard disintegration on the compact metric spaces
\(\TT\) and \(\TT\) gives a regular conditional probability
\(u\mapsto\kappa_u\) for \(\mu\) over the map \(B\), with
\(\kappa_u(B^{-1}\{u\})=1\) for \(m_{\TT}\)-almost every \(u\), and hence the
displayed integral formula.  Conversely, any measurable kernel supported on
the fibers and integrated against \(m_{\TT}\) defines a probability measure
\(\mu\) satisfying \(B_\ast\mu=m_{\TT}\), because for every continuous
\(g\) on \(\TT\),
\[
\int_{\TT}g(B(z))\,\dd\mu(z)
=\int_{\TT}g(u)\,\dd m_{\TT}(u).
\]
Finally, applying the covering formula to inverse branches gives, for every
continuous \(f\),
\[
\int_{\TT}f(z)\,\dd\mu_B(z)
=\int_{\TT}\frac1d\sum_{z\in B^{-1}\{u\}}f(z)\,\dd m_{\TT}(u),
\]
so \(\mu_B\) is exactly the uniform-kernel member of the disintegrated
Haar-lift fiber.  Non-uniform kernels, including measurable selections when
\(d>1\), give further, generally singular, Haar lifts.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite Blaschke--Joukowsky Fourier identity and equilibrium bound]\label{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover}
Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\ge1\), let \(r>1\), and set
\[
R_{B,r}(e^{i\theta})=J_r(B(e^{i\theta})).
\]
\emph{Finite-energy Fourier identity.}  For a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\TT\), put \(\eta:=B_\ast\mu\).  If \(\eta\) has finite logarithmic energy on \(\TT\), equivalently \(J_{r\ast}\eta=(R_{B,r})_\ast\mu\) has finite logarithmic energy on \(E_r\), then
\[
\log r-I((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu)
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\frac{|\widehat\eta(k)|^2+r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\eta(k)^2)}{k}.
\]
This hypothesis is on the pushed-forward circle measure \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\), not on the original source measure \(\mu\).  Outside this finite-energy class the displayed Fourier identity is not asserted.  For instance, if \(\eta=\delta_1\) (equivalently, \(\mu\) is any probability measure supported on the finite set \(B^{-1}\{1\}\)), then
\[
I((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu)=I(J_{r\ast}\delta_1)=-\infty,
\]
and \(\widehat\eta(k)=1\) for all \(k\ge1\), so the Fourier series
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+r^{-2k}}{k}
\]
diverges.

\emph{All-measure equilibrium bound.}  For every probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\TT\), with \(\eta:=B_\ast\mu\), and with no finite-energy assumption on either \(\mu\) or \(\eta\), one has the extended-energy bound
\[
I((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu)
=I(J_{r\ast}\eta)
\le \log r,
\]
and equality holds if and only if \(\eta\) is Haar measure.  Thus the energy deficit and the sharp equilibrium bound factor only through the pushed-forward measure \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\); finite energy is assumed only when invoking the displayed Fourier identity for \(\eta\).  For this fixed finite Blaschke product \(B\), the equilibrium lifts are exactly the probability measures \(\mu\) satisfying
\[
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Equivalently, they are exactly the Haar lifts through \(B\) in the sense of
Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-haar-lift-fiber}.  Among them is the
canonical absolutely continuous member
\[
\boxed{\;
\dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})=\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\,\dd\theta.
\;}
\]
This canonical member is not a uniqueness assertion about the source lift.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity \((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu)=J_{r\ast}\eta\) reduces the all-measure maximal-energy statement directly to Proposition \ref{prop:extended-energy-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}.  When \(\eta\) has finite logarithmic energy, Theorem \ref{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity} gives the displayed Fourier deficit formula.  The same proposition shows that equality in the maximal-energy problem occurs exactly when \(\eta\) is Haar measure.  Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-haar-lift-fiber} gives the terminology and the canonical absolutely continuous member of this full lift fiber.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Parametrization of the Haar-lift fiber]\label{rem:haar-lift-fiber-parametrization}
Let \(m\) be normalized Haar measure on \(\TT\).  Since
\(B:\TT\to\TT\) is a degree-\(d\) covering, every
\(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) satisfying \(B_\ast\mu=m\) has a finite-fiber
disintegration
\[
\mu(E)=\int_{\TT}\kappa_u\bigl(E\cap B^{-1}\{u\}\bigr)\,\dd m(u),
\qquad E\subset\TT\ \text{Borel},
\]
where \(u\mapsto\kappa_u\) is a Borel probability kernel supported on the
finite fiber \(B^{-1}\{u\}\).  Conversely, every such kernel defines a
Haar lift through \(B\).  The canonical absolutely continuous lift is the
uniform-fiber member
\[
\kappa_u=\frac1d\sum_{B(z)=u}\delta_z,
\]
with fiber points counted once; the covering has no boundary ramification,
so each boundary fiber has exactly \(d\) points.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Canonical Blaschke lift source energy]\label{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy}
Let
\[
B(z)=\zeta\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{z-a_j}{1-\overline{a_j}z},
\qquad |\zeta|=1,
\qquad a_j\in\DD,
\]
and let \(\mu_B\) be the canonical lift from Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover}.  Then its source-circle logarithmic energy is
\[
\boxed{\;
I_{\TT}(\mu_B):=\iint_{\TT\times\TT}\log|z-t|\,\dd\mu_B(z)\dd\mu_B(t)
=\frac1{d^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\log|1-\overline{a_i}\,a_j|.
\;}
\]
Here and below the product in the logarithm is the explicitly conjugated factor
\(\overline{a_i}\,a_j\): the conjugation applies to \(a_i\) and not to \(a_j\).
For \(d=1\), this gives the normalization check
\[
I_{\TT}(\mu_{B_a})=\log|1-\overline a\,a|=\log(1-|a|^2).
\]
In particular \(I_{\TT}(\mu_B)\le0\), with equality if and only if \(a_1=\cdots=a_d=0\), equivalently \(B(z)=\zeta z^d\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the standard finite-Blaschke boundary derivative formula, used already in Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula} and classically recorded in \citet[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970}, the boundary derivative is the sum of the Poisson kernels of its zeros:
\[
\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{d}
=\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1-|a_j|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-a_j|^2}.
\]
With the convention \(\widehat\sigma(k)=\int_{\TT} z^{-k}\,\dd\sigma(z)\), equivalently \(z=e^{i\theta}\) in angular coordinates, the Poisson kernel has the classical Fourier expansion \citep[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970}
\[
\frac{1-|a|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-a|^2}=
1+2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{k\ge1}\overline a^{\,k}e^{ik\theta},
\]
so its \(k\)-th coefficient is \(\overline a^{\,k}\).  This fixes the conjugation convention used in the product formula: pairing the \(k\)-th coefficient with its complex conjugate gives
\[
\left(\frac1d\sum_i\overline{a_i}^{\,k}\right)
\overline{\left(\frac1d\sum_j\overline{a_j}^{\,k}\right)}
=\frac1{d^2}\sum_{i,j}(\overline{a_i}\,a_j)^k.
\]
The normalization is important: the density of \(\mu_B\) is \(|B'|/(2\pi d)\), so the displayed Poisson sum is divided by \(d\), and the circle logarithmic kernel is the unnormalized kernel \(\log|z-t|\) rather than \(-\log|z-t|\).  Consequently the sign below is the same as in Lemma \ref{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation}: there is no constant term and mode \(k\) contributes \(-|\widehat\sigma(k)|^2/k\).
Thus, for \(k\ge1\), the Fourier coefficients of \(\mu_B\) are
\[
\widehat\mu_B(k)=\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^{d}\overline{a_j}^{\,k}.
\]
The displayed boundary density is smooth on \(\TT\), hence \(\mu_B\) has finite circle logarithmic energy and Lemma \ref{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation} applies.  Using the circle-kernel expansion from that lemma,
\[
\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|
=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Re}(e^{ik(\theta-\phi)})}{k},
\]
we get
\[
I_{\TT}(\mu_B)=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat\mu_B(k)|^2}{k}
=-\frac1{d^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\overline{a_i}\,a_j)^k}{k}.
\]
Since \(|\overline{a_i}\,a_j|<1\), the inner series is \(-\log(1-\overline{a_i}\,a_j)\).  Taking real parts gives
\[
I_{\TT}(\mu_B)=\frac1{d^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\log|1-\overline{a_i}\,a_j|.
\]
The Fourier form also gives \(I_{\TT}(\mu_B)=-\sum_{k\ge1}|\widehat\mu_B(k)|^2/k\le0\).  Equality holds if and only if all positive Fourier coefficients vanish, equivalently \(\sum_j\overline{a_j}^{\ k}=0\) for every \(k\ge1\).  Newton's identities then force all elementary symmetric functions of \(a_1,\dots,a_d\) to vanish, so \(a_1=\cdots=a_d=0\).  Conversely, if all zeros are zero, then \(B(z)=\zeta z^d\), \(\mu_B\) is Haar measure, and the energy is \(0\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Collected Joukowsky Boundary Facts.}\label{par:joukowsky-capacity-package-dependencies}
For \(r>1\), the preceding results give the following
Joukowsky-boundary facts.  They are the capacity-theoretic input for the
fixed-scale transport statements later in the paper.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item The degree-two pullback has complete collision skeleton
  \[
  J_r(z)=J_r(t)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  z=t\quad\hbox{or}\quad zt=r^{-2},
  \]
  on \(\CC^\times\).  On \(\TT^2\), only the diagonal remains.

  \item The ellipse \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\) has
  \[
  \operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r,
  \qquad
  \nu_{E_r}=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right),
  \]
  and \(\nu_{E_r}\) is the unique equilibrium measure.

  \item For every finite-energy probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\TT\),
  \[
  \log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu)
  =\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
  \frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2+r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\mu(k)^2)}{k},
  \]
  with the sharp mode constants \(1-r^{-2k}\) and \(1+r^{-2k}\).  Outside the finite-energy class this Fourier identity is not asserted; the all-measure assertion is only the variational bound \(I(J_{r\ast}\eta)\le\log r\), with equality exactly for Haar measure.

  \item If \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product of positive degree, then the covered equilibrium problem depends only on \(B_\ast\mu\):
  \[
  I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=I(J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu)),
  \]
  and its maximizers are exactly the full Haar-lift fiber
  \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).  The canonical absolutely continuous
  member of this fiber has density \(|B'|/(2\pi\deg B)\) and source energy
  given by Proposition \ref{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy}.
\end{enumerate}
The auxiliary rational pullback results collected in Appendix~\ref{app:auxiliary-rational-pullbacks} supply only the lower-truncated kernel and collision terminology in \textup{(a)}.  Part \textup{(a)} is Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation} and Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity}; the capacity, unique equilibrium identification, and unrestricted extremal inequality in \textup{(b)} are Theorem \ref{thm:all-measure-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}, isolated again in Proposition \ref{prop:extended-energy-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}; part \textup{(c)} is Theorem \ref{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity}, with the finite-energy equivalence supplied by Lemma \ref{lem:joukowsky-boundary-energy-equivalence}; and part \textup{(d)} is Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} together with Proposition \ref{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy}.  Thus the capacity scale, equilibrium lift, and boundary deficit used later are all the degree-two Joukowsky objects displayed above.

\begin{corollary}[Collected Joukowsky boundary record]\label{thm:main-joukowsky-boundary-scale}
For each \(r>1\), let \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\) on
\(\CC^\times\), and let \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\).  The following assertions
are exactly the boundary conclusions recorded above:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item the algebraic collision skeleton of
  \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\), with boundary reduction to the diagonal on
  \(\TT^2\);
  \item the equilibrium value \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\), the unique
  equilibrium measure \(J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/(2\pi))\), and the
  lower-truncated all-measure extremal bound for arbitrary Borel
  probability measures;
  \item the finite-energy Fourier diagonalization, only for finite-energy
  source measures, with sharp weights
  \(1-r^{-2k}\) and \(1+r^{-2k}\);
  \item for a fixed finite Blaschke product \(B\), the finite-Blaschke
  descent through \(B_\ast\mu\), the
  characterization \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\) of all covered
  equilibrium lifts, and the canonical lift source-energy formula.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Part \textup{(a)} is Theorem
\ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation} together with
Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity}.  Part \textup{(b)}
is Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability}, Proposition
\ref{prop:extended-energy-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}, and Lemma
\ref{lem:joukowsky-boundary-energy-equivalence}.  Part \textup{(c)} is
Theorem \ref{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity}, whose
diagonal real and imaginary mode weights are \(1+r^{-2k}\) and
\(1-r^{-2k}\).  Part \textup{(d)} is Theorem
\ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} together with Proposition
\ref{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy}.
\end{proof}
