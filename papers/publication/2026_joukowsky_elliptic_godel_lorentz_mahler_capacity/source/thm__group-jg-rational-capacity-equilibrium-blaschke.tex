\subsection{Degree-Two Joukowsky Boundary Theory}\label{subsec:main-joukowsky-boundary-scale}

The boundary theory begins with a lower-truncated rational pullback
kernel interface and then specializes to the degree-two map
\(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\).  In that specialization the collision
relation becomes \(z=t\) or \(zt=r^{-2}\), which is disjoint from
\(\TT^2\) except on the diagonal.  This elementary skeleton is the
common source of the capacity formula, the sharp Fourier equilibrium
deficit, and the finite-Blaschke lift theorem.

\paragraph{Fixed-Scale Joukowsky Consequences.}\label{par:fixed-scale-joukowsky-package-scope}
At a fixed scale \(r>1\), the analytic conclusions eventually assembled
in Corollary \ref{thm:main-joukowsky-boundary-scale} provide the capacity
value, the ellipse, the pushed-forward equilibrium measure, the Fourier
deficit, and the finite-Blaschke Haar-lift condition.  The transport
polynomial \(Q_r(P)\), the one-scale annular shell theorem, and the
Rouch\'e selector are added later in the fixed-scale synthesis section,
after their algebraic hypotheses have been proved.

\subsection{Auxiliary lower-truncated pullback kernel}\label{subsec:degree-two-joukowsky-specialization}

The general rational-map input for this section is only the standard
lower-truncated pullback identity and the pushforward variational
principle, collected in Appendix~\ref{app:auxiliary-rational-pullbacks}.
No closed formula for \(\operatorname{cap}\) of general rational images,
no Fourier diagonalization, and no rational inverse theorem is claimed
here.
\begin{proposition}[Auxiliary lower-truncated pullback interface]\label{prop:main-rational-pullback-interface}
Let \(R=A/C\) be nonconstant, with coprime \(A,C\in\CC[z]\), and let
\(K\subset\CC\) be compact, nonpolar, and pole-free for \(R\).  Put
\[
\Psi_R(z,t):=\frac{A(z)C(t)-A(t)C(z)}{z-t},
\]
with the polynomial extension to \(z=t\).  Then for every
\(\mu\in\mathcal P(K)\),
\[
I(R_\ast\mu)=
\iint_{K^2}\log\left|
\frac{(z-t)\Psi_R(z,t)}{C(z)C(t)}
\right|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t),
\]
interpreted by lower truncation.  Writing \(R(z)-R(t)\) through this
divided difference only names the diagonal, branch, and equal-value
collision loci.  The rest of the paper uses this interface only after
the specialization \(R=J_r\).
The accompanying capacity equality is only the classical variational
principle after pushforward,
\[
\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))
=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(K)}I(R_\ast\mu).
\]
Its equality cases are exactly the lifts of the equilibrium measure of
\(R(K)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is Proposition~\ref{prop:general-rational-image-capacity-pullback}
from Appendix~\ref{app:auxiliary-rational-pullbacks}.
\end{proof}

For the map used in the paper these facts reduce immediately to one
explicit statement: for
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}=\frac{rz^2+r^{-1}}{z},\qquad r>1,
\]
the collision equation is
\[
J_r(z)=J_r(t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (z-t)(rzt-r^{-1})=0.
\]
Thus the only off-diagonal partner is the involution
\(t=r^{-2}/z\), and this graph misses \(\TT^2\).  The theorem below is
the form used in the sequel; all nonpolarity, selection, and general
rational-pushforward details have been moved out of the main line.
\begin{theorem}[Degree-two Joukowsky boundary reduction]\label{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation}
Let \(r>1\), let
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}=\frac{rz^2+r^{-1}}{z}
\]
  and let \(K\subset\CC\setminus\{0\}\) be compact and nonpolar.  Equivalently, \(J_r=A/C\) with coprime polynomials \(A(z)=rz^2+r^{-1}\) and \(C(z)=z\), so \(K\) avoids the unique pole.  With the standing lower-truncated convention of Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}, \(J_r(K)\) is compact and nonpolar and
\[
\log\operatorname{cap}(J_r(K))
=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(K)}\iint_{K\times K}\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|\,
\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t),
\]
where the integral is the lower-truncated extended value of the single pullback kernel \(\log|J_r(z)-J_r(t)|\); equivalently the displayed supremum is \(\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(K)}I((J_r)_\ast\mu)\).  The complete collision set on \(K\times K\) is
\[
\{(z,t):z=t\}\ \cup\ \{(z,t):zt=r^{-2}\}.
\]
Thus every collision is either the ordinary logarithmic diagonal or the single involutive partner \(t=r^{-2}/z\).  Each vertical collision section contains at most the two points \(z\) and \(r^{-2}/z\), with coalescence only at the critical fixed points \(z=\pm r^{-1}\).  The maximizers are exactly the lifts of the equilibrium measure of \(J_r(K)\):
\[
\mathcal E_{J_r}(\mu)=\log\operatorname{cap}(J_r(K))
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(J_r)_\ast\mu=\nu_{J_r(K)}.
\]
In particular the off-diagonal graph is disjoint from \(\TT\times\TT\).  Hence the boundary map \(J_r:\TT\to E_r:=J_r(\TT)\) is a homeomorphism, every \(\sigma\in\mathcal P(E_r)\) has a unique source measure \(\eta\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) with \(J_{r\ast}\eta=\sigma\), and
\[
I(\sigma)=
\iint_{\TT^2}
\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|
\,\dd\eta(z)\dd\eta(t).
\]
Thus the elliptic-boundary equilibrium problem in Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} has only the ordinary diagonal collision singularity on the source circle.

Moreover, this theorem is the capacity bridge used in the sequel.  Proposition \ref{prop:general-rational-image-capacity-pullback} enters only as infrastructure for the lower-truncated rational kernel: after the specialization \(A(z)=rz^2+r^{-1}\), \(C(z)=z\), all nontrivial singular information descends to the two explicit relations \(z=t\) and \(zt=r^{-2}\).  In particular the source-circle boundary problem, the sharp Fourier deficit, and the finite Blaschke-cover equilibrium theorem depend on no further rational-map ambiguity beyond this degree-two skeleton.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(A(z)=rz^2+r^{-1}\) and \(C(z)=z\), the difference quotient in Proposition \ref{prop:general-rational-image-capacity-pullback} is
\[
\Psi_{J_r}(z,t)=\frac{(rz^2+r^{-1})t-(rt^2+r^{-1})z}{z-t}
=rzt-r^{-1}.
\]
Substitution into the pullback kernel gives
\[
\log|z-t|+
\log|rzt-r^{-1}|-
\log|z|-
\log|t|
=
\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|.
\]
The off-diagonal collision relation is \(rzt-r^{-1}=0\), equivalently \(zt=r^{-2}\).  If \(|z|=|t|=1\), this relation cannot hold for \(r>1\).  The maximizer statement is the specialization of Proposition \ref{prop:general-rational-image-capacity-pullback} to \(R=J_r\).  The section bound follows from the displayed factorization: for fixed \(z\), a collision point \(t\) must be either \(z\) or \(r^{-2}/z\), and these coincide exactly when \(z^2=r^{-2}\), that is, when \(z=\pm r^{-1}\).

It remains to record the boundary descent, since this is the degree-two form used later.  If \(z,t\in\TT\), then the relation \(J_r(z)=J_r(t)\) forces either \(z=t\) or \(zt=r^{-2}\).  The second alternative is impossible on \(\TT^2\), so \(J_r\) is injective on \(\TT\).  It is continuous from the compact space \(\TT\) onto the Hausdorff space \(E_r\), hence is a homeomorphism.  Therefore every \(\sigma\in\mathcal P(E_r)\) has the unique source \(\eta=(J_r^{-1})_\ast\sigma\).  Substituting \(K=\TT\) in the kernel identity above and pushing the lower-truncated logarithmic integral through this homeomorphism gives the displayed boundary energy formula.  Thus the final assertion follows by specializing the general singularity description in Proposition \ref{prop:general-rational-image-capacity-pullback} to the unit circle.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Degree-two theorem controls the later equilibrium chain]\label{cor:primary-joukowsky-capacity-chain}
Let \(r>1\), let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product, and let \(R_{B,r}=J_r\circ B\).  For every pair of Borel probability measures \(\mu_1,\mu_2\) on \(\TT\), if
\[
B_\ast\mu_1=B_\ast\mu_2,
\]
then
\[
I((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu_1)=I((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu_2)
\]
as lower-truncated extended energies.  Hence the covered Joukowsky equilibrium problem factors through the single boundary measure \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\) and through the degree-two kernel
\[
\log\left|(u-v)\left(r-r^{-1}u^{-1}v^{-1}\right)\right|,
\qquad u,v\in\TT.
\]
Consequently the finite Blaschke-cover theorem, the sharp Joukowsky equilibrium stability theorem, and the later fixed-scale classification have no independent rational-pullback input: their capacity normalization is exactly Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation} together with the boundary descent in Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation} gives the complete boundary kernel for \(J_r\) on \(\TT\): the off-diagonal partner relation \(uv=r^{-2}\) is disjoint from \(\TT^2\), and the boundary energy of any pushforward \(J_{r\ast}\eta\) is the lower-truncated integral of the displayed kernel against \(\eta\times\eta\).  Since \((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu)\), equality of \(B_\ast\mu_1\) and \(B_\ast\mu_2\) gives equality of the two energies by substituting the same measure \(\eta\) into this kernel formula.  The final sentence is just the same factorization read through the hypotheses of the subsequent results: Theorem \ref{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity} diagonalizes this boundary kernel on \(\TT\), Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} applies it after the finite cover \(B\), and the fixed-scale classification uses the resulting scale \(\log r\).  No part of this argument invokes a general rational map after the degree-two Joukowsky specialization has been made.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Degree-two boundary collision reduction]\label{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity}
Let \(r>1\).  On the source circle \(\TT\), the degree-two Joukowsky map has no branch-graph collision except the ordinary diagonal:
\[
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:J_r(z)=J_r(t)\}=\{(z,z):z\in\TT\}.
\]
Consequently the boundary kernel on \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\) is exactly
\[
\log|J_r(z)-J_r(t)|=
\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|,
\qquad z,t\in\TT,
\]
where the second factor is continuous and nonzero on \(\TT^2\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation}, the full collision set for \(J_r\) is the union of the diagonal \(z=t\) and the involutive partner relation \(zt=r^{-2}\).  On \(\TT^2\) the second relation is impossible, because \(|zt|=1\) while \(r^{-2}<1\).  Hence the only boundary collision for \(J_r\) is the ordinary diagonal.  The displayed identity is the same theorem specialized to \(K=\TT\), and the factor \(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\) cannot vanish on \(\TT^2\), since vanishing would again imply \(|zt|=r^{-2}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Capacity normalization of \(E_r\)]\label{lem:joukowsky-ellipse-capacity-normalization}
Let \(r>1\), put \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), and let \(z_+(w)\) denote the branch
of the inverse of \(J_r\) satisfying \(|z_+(w)|>1\) near infinity.  Then
\[
z_+(w)=\frac{w}{r}+O(w^{-1}),\qquad w\to\infty,
\]
and consequently
\[
\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r,\qquad
\nu_{E_r}=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The inverse relation \(w=J_r(z)\) is
\[
r z^2-wz+r^{-1}=0.
\]
The root with modulus \(>1\) for large \(w\) is
\[
z_+(w)=\frac{w+\sqrt{w^2-4}}{2r},
\]
where the square root is chosen so that \(\sqrt{w^2-4}=w+O(w^{-1})\)
at infinity.  Hence \(z_+(w)=w/r+O(w^{-1})\).

On \(\{|z|>1\}\), the derivative \(J_r'(z)=r-r^{-1}z^{-2}\) has no zero,
since its critical points \(z=\pm r^{-1}\) lie in the unit disk.  The
collision relation \(J_r(z)=J_r(t)\) implies \(z=t\) or \(zt=r^{-2}\);
the second alternative is impossible when \(|z|,|t|>1\).  Thus the
exterior branch is univalent and maps \(\{|z|>1\}\) conformally onto
\(\CC\setminus E_r\), with inverse \(z_+\).  Pulling back the Green
function of the exterior unit disk gives
\[
g_{\CC\setminus E_r}(w)=\log|z_+(w)|
=\log|w|-\log r+o(1),\qquad w\to\infty.
\]
For a compact nonpolar set \(K\), the Green function with pole at infinity
has the normalization
\[
g_{\CC\setminus K}(w)=\log|w|-\log\operatorname{cap}(K)+o(1).
\]
Comparing constants gives \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\).  The same
exterior conformal map identifies harmonic measure from infinity on
\(E_r\) with the pushforward of normalized arclength on \(\TT\).  By
Frostman's theorem this harmonic measure is the equilibrium measure,
so \(\nu_{E_r}=J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/(2\pi))\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Boundary finite-energy equivalence under \(J_r\)]\label{lem:joukowsky-boundary-energy-equivalence}
Let \(r>1\).  For all \(z,t\in\TT\),
\[
(r-r^{-1})|z-t|
\le |J_r(z)-J_r(t)|
\le (r+r^{-1})|z-t|.
\]
Consequently a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\TT\) has finite circle logarithmic energy if and only if \(J_{r\ast}\mu\) has finite logarithmic energy on \(E_r\).  More precisely,
\[
I_{\TT}(\mu)+\log(r-r^{-1})
\le I(J_{r\ast}\mu)
\le I_{\TT}(\mu)+\log(r+r^{-1}),
\]
with the evident interpretation that both sides are \(-\infty\) in the non-finite-energy case.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The exact factorization on \(\TT^2\) is
\[
J_r(z)-J_r(t)=(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right).
\]
Since \(|z^{-1}t^{-1}|=1\), the reverse and direct triangle inequalities give
\[
r-r^{-1}\le |r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}|
\le r+r^{-1}.
\]
Taking logarithms gives
\[
\log|z-t|+\log(r-r^{-1})
\le \log|J_r(z)-J_r(t)|
\le \log|z-t|+\log(r+r^{-1})
\]
as lower-truncated extended kernels.  Integrating the truncated kernels and passing to the limit proves the two energy inequalities and hence the equivalence of finite energy.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Joukowsky boundary skeleton obstruction]\label{thm:joukowsky-boundary-skeleton-obstruction}
Let \(r>1\), let \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), and let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product.  Among the two algebraic collision relations in the degree-two Joukowsky pullback, only the diagonal relation survives on the boundary after composition with \(B\):
\[
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:J_r(B(z))=J_r(B(t))\}
=
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:B(z)=B(t)\}.
\]
Consequently, for every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) and \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\), the lower-truncated energy of the covered ellipse depends only on \(\eta\):
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)
=
\iint_{\TT^2}
\log\left|(u-v)\left(r-r^{-1}u^{-1}v^{-1}\right)\right|
\,\dd\eta(u)\dd\eta(v).
\]
The off-diagonal Joukowsky partner relation contributes no boundary singularity and no additional finite-cover invariant.  Therefore, if two boundary measures \(\mu_1,\mu_2\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) satisfy \(B_\ast\mu_1=B_\ast\mu_2\), then
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu_1)=I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu_2),
\]
with all energies interpreted by lower truncation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The degree-two collision theorem gives the complete collision set for \(J_r\) as the union of the diagonal relation \(u=v\) and the partner relation \(uv=r^{-2}\).  Since \(B(\TT)\subset\TT\), a covered boundary collision satisfies
\[
J_r(B(z))=J_r(B(t))
\]
only if either \(B(z)=B(t)\) or \(B(z)B(t)=r^{-2}\).  The second alternative is impossible on \(\TT^2\), because \(|B(z)B(t)|=1\) while \(r^{-2}<1\).  This proves the displayed collision skeleton.

For \(u,v\in\TT\), Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity} gives
\[
\log|J_r(u)-J_r(v)|=
\log\left|(u-v)\left(r-r^{-1}u^{-1}v^{-1}\right)\right|,
\]
where the second factor is continuous and nonzero on \(\TT^2\).  Applying the lower-truncated pushforward identity to the map \((z,t)\mapsto(B(z),B(t))\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)
&=\iint_{\TT^2}\log|J_r(B(z))-J_r(B(t))|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t)\\
&=\iint_{\TT^2}\log|J_r(u)-J_r(v)|\,\dd\eta(u)\dd\eta(v)\\
&=\iint_{\TT^2}
\log\left|(u-v)\left(r-r^{-1}u^{-1}v^{-1}\right)\right|
\,\dd\eta(u)\dd\eta(v),
\end{aligned}
\]
again with all logarithmic integrals interpreted by lower truncation.  Thus the energy is a function only of \(B_\ast\mu\), and equal pushforwards give equal energies.

Thus the covered problem has no kernel invariant beyond the pushed-forward boundary measure.  The later sharp equilibrium theorem will identify which pushed-forward measure maximizes this already descended problem.
\end{proof}

The next theorem is deliberately only a descent theorem.  It records the
homeomorphism, lift uniqueness, boundary kernel, and finite-cover
factorization that follow from the degree-two Joukowsky collision
skeleton.  The sharp variational value and equality class are proved
later, after the Fourier diagonalization, in Corollary
\ref{cor:sharp-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers}.  This keeps the
topological descent interface independent of the equilibrium theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Exact boundary descent for Joukowsky covers]\label{thm:exact-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers}
Let \(r>1\), put \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), and use the lower-truncated convention of Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}.  This is a boundary-kernel descent statement only; the variational conclusions enter later through Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} and Corollary \ref{cor:sharp-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers}.  Then the boundary parametrization \(J_r:\TT\to E_r\) is a homeomorphism, and every \(\sigma\in\mathcal P(E_r)\) has a unique source measure \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) satisfying \(J_{r\ast}\mu=\sigma\).  For this source measure,
\[
I(\sigma)=\iint_{\TT^2}\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t).
\]
If \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product, then the covered problem descends exactly through the single measure \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\): the complete covered collision relation on \(\TT^2\) is
\[
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:J_r(B(z))=J_r(B(t))\}
=
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:B(z)=B(t)\}.
\]
For every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\), with \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\), one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)
&=\iint_{\TT^2}\log|J_r(B(z))-J_r(B(t))|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t)\\
&=\iint_{\TT^2}\log\left|(B(z)-B(t))
\left(r-r^{-1}B(z)^{-1}B(t)^{-1}\right)\right|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t)\\
&=I(J_{r\ast}\eta),
\end{aligned}
\]
and the first two integrals are finite exactly when \(I(J_{r\ast}\eta)>-\infty\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity} shows that \(J_r\) is injective on \(\TT\).  Since \(J_r:\TT\to E_r\) is a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, it is a homeomorphism.  Therefore every probability measure \(\sigma\) on \(E_r\) has the unique lift \(\mu=(J_r^{-1})_\ast\sigma\), and the lower-truncated pushforward identity gives the displayed energy formula.

Now let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product.  Since \(B(\TT)\subset\TT\), injectivity of \(J_r\) on \(\TT\) gives
\[
J_r(B(z))=J_r(B(t))
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B(z)=B(t),
\]
which proves the covered collision classification.  Substituting \(B(z)\) and \(B(t)\) into the boundary kernel identity gives the displayed covered-kernel formula; the factor
\[
r-r^{-1}B(z)^{-1}B(t)^{-1}
\]
is continuous and nonzero on \(\TT^2\), because \(|B(z)B(t)|=1\) whereas \(r^{-2}<1\).  Hence the lower-truncated singular set is exactly \(B(z)=B(t)\).

Functoriality of pushforward gives
\[
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu).
\]
Applying the lower-truncated logarithmic energy functional to both sides proves the energy identity and the equivalence of finiteness.  Thus all covered-boundary kernel calculations factor through the single pushed-forward source measure \(B_\ast\mu\), before any extremal theorem is invoked.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lower-truncated Fourier evaluation on the circle]\label{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation}
Let \(\mu\) be a probability measure on \(\TT\) with finite logarithmic energy.  Then
\[
\iint_{\TT\times\TT}\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=-
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k},
\]
where the integral is the lower-truncated logarithmic integral.  If
\(q\in\CC\) and \(|q|<1\), then the continuous kernel
\(\log|1-qe^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|\) satisfies
\[
\iint\log|1-qe^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=-
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Re}(q^k\widehat\mu(k)^2)}{k}.
\]
The finite-energy hypothesis is part of the assertion: for example,
\(\mu=\delta_1\) is excluded, since its circle logarithmic energy is
\(-\infty\) and the singular-kernel Fourier series is divergent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(0<\rho<1\), the Abel-regularized identity
\[
\log|1-\rho e^{i(\theta-\phi)}|
=-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^k\cos k(\theta-\phi)}{k}
\]
is uniformly convergent on compact subannuli and may be integrated term by term against \(\mu\times\mu\).  It gives
\[
\iint\log|1-\rho e^{i(\theta-\phi)}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=-
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^k|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}.
\]
We now pass from the Abel kernels to the lower-truncated boundary kernel without using any pointwise monotonicity in \(\rho\).  Put
\[
\kappa(\alpha)=\log|1-e^{i\alpha}|,
\qquad
\kappa_\rho(\alpha)=\log|1-\rho e^{i\alpha}|,
\]
and let \(\lambda\) be the image of \(\mu\times\mu\) under
\((\theta,\phi)\mapsto\theta-\phi\).  The finite-energy hypothesis is exactly
\(\int\kappa\,\dd\lambda>-\infty\), with the integral understood by lower truncation, because
\(|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|=|1-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}|\).  We justify the Abel passage directly.  The kernel \(\kappa\) is bounded above by \(\log 2\), and the finite-energy hypothesis gives \(\kappa^-\in L^1(\lambda)\); in particular \(\lambda\{0\}=0\).  For \(\alpha\ne0\), \(\kappa_\rho(\alpha)\to\kappa(\alpha)\) as \(\rho\uparrow1\).  Moreover, for \(\rho\ge1/2\),
\[
|1-\rho e^{i\alpha}|^2=(1-\rho)^2+\rho|1-e^{i\alpha}|^2,
\]
and hence
\[
\kappa(\alpha)+\frac12\log\rho\le \kappa_\rho(\alpha)\le \log2
\]
for \(\lambda\)-almost every \(\alpha\).  Thus \(\kappa_\rho^+\le\log2\) and \(\kappa_\rho^-\le \kappa^-+\frac12\log2\) for \(\rho\ge1/2\).  Dominated convergence, with the right-hand side interpreted as the lower-truncated logarithmic integral, gives
\[
\lim_{\rho\uparrow1}\int\kappa_\rho(\alpha)\,\dd\lambda(\alpha)
=\int\kappa(\alpha)\,\dd\lambda(\alpha),
\]
and hence the left side of the Abel identity tends to
\[
\iint\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi).
\]
On the right, \(|\widehat\mu(k)|^2/k\ge0\), so monotone convergence for the nonnegative partial Abel sums gives
\[
\lim_{\rho\uparrow1}
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^k|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}
=
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}
\]
in \([0,\infty]\).  This is the only passage from the singular Abel
series to the boundary Fourier series; it uses monotone convergence for
the nonnegative sums, while dominated convergence was used only for the
lower-truncated kernel integral.  The finite lower-truncated energy just
obtained forces this series to be finite, and the first identity follows.

For complex \(|q|<1\), the principal power series for
\(\log(1-qe^{-i(\theta+\phi)})\) is uniformly and absolutely convergent
on \(\TT^2\).  Taking real parts gives
\[
\log|1-qe^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|
=-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\operatorname{Re}\!\left(q^k e^{-ik(\theta+\phi)}\right)}{k},
\]
again uniformly and absolutely.  Termwise integration gives the second
identity because
\[
\iint e^{-ik(\theta+\phi)}\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=\widehat\mu(k)^2.
\]
\end{proof}
