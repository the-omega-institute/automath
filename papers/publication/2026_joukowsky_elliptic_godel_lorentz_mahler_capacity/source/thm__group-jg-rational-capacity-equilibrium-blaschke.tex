We use the following standard potential-theoretic tools throughout this section.  For compact nonpolar plane sets, logarithmic capacity is the variational supremum of the lower-truncated energy \(I\), and the supremum is attained by a unique equilibrium measure; see the compact-set equilibrium discussion in \citet[Chap.~5, pp.~105--107]{Ransford1995}, or equivalently the classical Frostman equilibrium theorem in Tsuji's terminology \citep[Chap.~III, Secs.~15--17]{Tsuji1959}.  The remaining set-theoretic inputs needed for the rational pullback formula are isolated in the next three lemmas: nonpolarity of rational images, equality of lower-truncated pullback and pushforward energies, and a compact-fiber Borel selector.  The main article-level application is the degree-two Joukowsky theorem below; the general rational pullback theorem is included only to organize the singular-kernel bookkeeping, so that the subsequent degree-two theorem can be read as the load-bearing capacity result of the paper rather than as a disguised appeal to an unspecified classical pullback formula.

\begin{lemma}[Nonpolarity is preserved by nonconstant rational images]\label{lem:nonpolar-rational-image}
Let \(K\subset\CC\) be compact and nonpolar, and let \(R\) be a nonconstant rational map with no pole on \(K\).  Then \(R(K)\) is compact and nonpolar.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Compactness follows from continuity of \(R\) on \(K\).  We next give the standard pullback argument in full.  Suppose, for a contradiction, that \(R(K)\) is polar.  By the subharmonic characterization of polar sets, in the form complementary to Cartan's theorem \citep[Thm.~3.5.1, p.~65]{Ransford1995}, there is a subharmonic function \(u\not\equiv -\infty\) on a neighbourhood \(V\) of \(R(K)\) such that \(u=-\infty\) on \(R(K)\).  Since \(R\) has no pole on \(K\) and is continuous there, choose an open neighbourhood \(U\) of \(K\), with no component on which \(R\) is constant after discarding components disjoint from \(K\) if necessary, such that \(R\) is holomorphic on \(U\) and \(R(U)\subset V\).  The composition \(u\circ R\) is subharmonic on \(U\) unless it is identically \(-\infty\) on a connected component.  On no component meeting \(K\) can it be identically \(-\infty\): otherwise the open mapping theorem would force \(u\equiv -\infty\) on a nonempty open subset of the domain of \(u\), hence everywhere on the relevant component, contrary to the choice of \(u\).  Thus \(u\circ R\) is a genuine subharmonic function on a neighbourhood of \(K\) and equals \(-\infty\) on \(K\).  This makes \(K\) polar, contradicting the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Extended pullback energy equals pushforward energy]\label{lem:extended-pullback-energy-equals-pushforward-energy}
Let \(X\) be a compact metric space, let \(F:X\to\CC\) be continuous, and let \(\mu\in\mathcal P(X)\).  Define
\[
L_F(x,y):=
\begin{cases}
\log|F(x)-F(y)|,&F(x)\ne F(y),\\
-\infty,&F(x)=F(y),
\end{cases}
\]
and let \(\nu=F_\ast\mu\).  If both logarithmic integrals are interpreted by lower truncation, namely as the limit of the kernels \(\max\{\log|\cdot|,-M\}\) as \(M\to\infty\), then
\[
\iint_{X\times X}L_F(x,y)\,\dd\mu(x)\dd\mu(y)
=
\iint_{F(X)\times F(X)}\log|u-v|\,\dd\nu(u)\dd\nu(v)
\]
as an identity in \([ -\infty,\infty)\).  In particular the left-hand side is greater than \(-\infty\) if and only if the pushforward logarithmic energy is greater than \(-\infty\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(M>0\), put \(h_M(u,v)=\max\{\log|u-v|,-M\}\).  This is a bounded Borel function on the compact set \(F(X)\times F(X)\), with the value \(-M\) on the diagonal.  Since \((F\times F)_\ast(\mu\times\mu)=\nu\times\nu\),
\[
\iint_{X\times X} h_M(F(x),F(y))\,\dd\mu(x)\dd\mu(y)
=
\iint_{F(X)\times F(X)}h_M(u,v)\,\dd\nu(u)\dd\nu(v).
\]
As \(M\to\infty\), the kernels \(h_M\) decrease pointwise to the extended logarithmic kernel \(\log|u-v|\), with value \(-\infty\) on the diagonal.  The image \(F(X)\) is compact, so these kernels are bounded above uniformly.  Applying monotone convergence to a fixed upper bound minus the two sides gives equality of the lower-truncated limits.  The final equivalence is the same identity read in the extended real line.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Compact-fiber Borel selection]\label{lem:compact-fiber-borel-selection}
Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be compact metric spaces, equipped with their standard Borel structures, and let \(G\subset X\times Y\) be closed with every vertical section
\[
G_x:=\{y\in Y:(x,y)\in G\}
\]
nonempty.  Then there is a standard-Borel measurable map \(s:X\to Y\) such that \((x,s(x))\in G\) for every \(x\in X\).  Consequently, for every Borel probability measure \(\nu\) on \(X\), the pushforward \(s_\ast\nu\) is a Borel probability measure on \(Y\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emergencystretch=1em
This is the compact-valued measurable-selection theorem, equivalently
the closed-section uniformization theorem for standard Borel spaces
\citep[Theorem~18.13]{Kechris1995}.  For completeness we recall why the
hypotheses match that theorem.  Compact metric spaces are Polish and
carry their standard Borel structures.  Since \(G\) is closed in
\(X\times Y\), its graph is Borel, and for every closed set \(F\subset Y\)
the set
\[
\{x\in X:G_x\cap F\ne\varnothing\}
\]
is the projection of the compact set \(G\cap(X\times F)\), hence compact
and therefore Borel.  Thus \(x\mapsto G_x\) is a Borel multifunction with
nonempty compact values.  The measurable-selection theorem gives a Borel
selector \(s:X\to Y\) with \(s(x)\in G_x\) for every \(x\in X\), which is
precisely \((x,s(x))\in G\).  Since \(s\) is Borel between standard Borel spaces, the image measure \(s_\ast\nu\) is defined on the Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra of \(Y\) and is a probability measure whenever \(\nu\) is one.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bounded-term separation for lower-truncated kernels]\label{lem:lower-truncated-bounded-term-separation}
Let \(X\) be a compact metric space, let \(L:X\times X\to[-\infty,M]\) be an upper-bounded Borel kernel, and let \(b:X\times X\to\RR\) be bounded and Borel.  For \(N>0\) put
\[
L_N:=\max\{L,-N\},
\qquad
(L+b)_N:=\max\{L+b,-N\}.
\]
Then for every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(X)\),
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\iint (L+b)_N\,\dd\mu\dd\mu
=
\lim_{N\to\infty}\iint L_N\,\dd\mu\dd\mu
+
\iint b\,\dd\mu\dd\mu
\]
as an identity in \([-\infty,\infty)\).  In particular, a symbolic decomposition of a logarithmic pullback kernel into a singular part plus bounded pole terms is legitimate only after lower truncation has been applied to the single extended kernel, and it preserves the distinction between finite and \(-\infty\) energy.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(C=\|b\|_\infty\).  For \(N>C\),
\[
L_{N+C}-C\le (L+b)_N\le L_{N-C}+C.
\]
If the lower-truncated integral of \(L\) is \(-\infty\), the right-hand side tends to \(-\infty\) after integration, so the lower-truncated integral of \(L+b\) is also \(-\infty\).  Suppose therefore that the lower-truncated integral of \(L\) is finite.  Since \(L\) is upper bounded, this implies that the negative part of \(L\) is integrable and that \(L> -\infty\) for \(\mu\times\mu\)-almost every point.  The truncation map \(T_N(s)=\max\{s,-N\}\) is one-Lipschitz, hence
\[
\left|(L+b)_N-L_N\right|=\left|T_N(L+b)-T_N(L)\right|\le C.
\]
For almost every point, \(T_N(L+b)-T_N(L)\to b\).  Dominated convergence gives
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\iint\bigl((L+b)_N-L_N\bigr)\,\dd\mu\dd\mu
=\iint b\,\dd\mu\dd\mu,
\]
which is the asserted identity in the finite case.  Since \(b\) is bounded, the same argument also proves that adding \(b\) cannot turn a finite lower-truncated integral into \(-\infty\), or remove a \(-\infty\) singular-energy contribution.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Divided-difference kernel for rational pullbacks]\label{lem:rational-divided-difference-kernel}
Let \(R=A/B\) be a rational map with coprime \(A,B\in\CC[z]\), and set
\[
\Phi_R(z,t):=A(z)B(t)-A(t)B(z).
\]
Then \(\Phi_R\) is divisible by \(z-t\) in \(\CC[z,t]\).  The quotient
\[
\Psi_R(z,t):=\frac{\Phi_R(z,t)}{z-t}
\]
is therefore a polynomial in the two variables and has diagonal value
\[
\Psi_R(z,z)=A'(z)B(z)-A(z)B'(z).
\]
If \(K\subset\CC\) is compact and \(B\ne0\) on \(K\), then, as lower-truncated extended kernels on \(K\times K\),
\[
\log|R(z)-R(t)|
=
\log|z-t|+\log|\Psi_R(z,t)|-
\log|B(z)|-\log|B(t)|,
\]
where the right-hand side is notation for the single extended kernel obtained from the rational identity, not an ordinary sum of independently defined logarithms on the collision or branch set.  Thus both sides take the value \(-\infty\) exactly on \(R(z)=R(t)\), and the finite continuous pole terms \(-\log|B(z)|-\log|B(t)|\) may be separated before integration only by the rule of Lemma \ref{lem:lower-truncated-bounded-term-separation}.  Consequently, for every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(K)\), the lower-truncated integral of the displayed pullback kernel is finite if and only if \(R_\ast\mu\) has finite logarithmic energy.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The polynomial \(\Phi_R\) vanishes identically on the diagonal \(z=t\).  Dividing \(\Phi_R\) by \(z-t\), viewed as a monic polynomial in \(z\) with coefficients in \(\CC[t]\), leaves a remainder depending only on \(t\); evaluating at \(z=t\) shows that this remainder is zero.  Hence \(\Psi_R\in\CC[z,t]\).  Taking the limit as \(t\to z\), equivalently differentiating \(\Phi_R(z,t)\) in the second variable and accounting for the denominator \(z-t\), gives
\[
\Psi_R(z,z)=A'(z)B(z)-A(z)B'(z).
\]

For \(z\ne t\) with \(B(z)B(t)\ne0\),
\[
R(z)-R(t)=\frac{A(z)B(t)-A(t)B(z)}{B(z)B(t)}
=\frac{(z-t)\Psi_R(z,t)}{B(z)B(t)}.
\]
Taking logarithmic absolute values gives the asserted identity away from the collision set.  On the collision set the factor \((z-t)\Psi_R(z,t)\) vanishes and the logarithmic kernel is \(-\infty\); the two terms involving \(B\) remain bounded and continuous on \(K\), because \(B\) has no zeros there.  Thus the equality holds for the single lower-truncated extended kernel, and Lemma \ref{lem:lower-truncated-bounded-term-separation} is exactly the permitted separation of the bounded pole terms from the singular collision terms.  Applying Lemma \ref{lem:extended-pullback-energy-equals-pushforward-energy} to the continuous map \(R:K\to R(K)\) proves the final equivalence of finite energies.
\end{proof}


\begin{theorem}[Rational-image capacity pullback]\label{thm:rational-image-capacity-pullback}
Let \(R=A/B\) be a nonconstant rational map, with coprime polynomials \(A,B\in\CC[z]\), and let \(K\subset\CC\) be compact and nonpolar with \(B\ne0\) on \(K\).  All compact sets in this statement, in particular \(K\) and \(R(K)\), are viewed as compact metric spaces with their standard Borel structures.  Put
\[
\Psi_R(z,t):=\frac{A(z)B(t)-A(t)B(z)}{z-t}\qquad (z\ne t),
\]
and extend this polynomial to the diagonal by
\[
\Psi_R(z,z)=A'(z)B(z)-A(z)B'(z).
\]
For a Borel probability measure \(\mu\) on \(K\), define the pullback energy by
\[
\mathcal E_R(\mu):=\iint_{K\times K}
\Bigl(\log|z-t|+\log|\Psi_R(z,t)|-\log|B(z)|-\log|B(t)|\Bigr)
\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t),
\]
where the displayed parenthesis is not a termwise definition of separate singular integrals.  It denotes the single extended kernel \(\log|R(z)-R(t)|\) supplied by Lemma \ref{lem:rational-divided-difference-kernel}, and the integral is the monotone limit of that one kernel truncated below.  In particular, the singular factor \(\log|\Psi_R|\) is never integrated by itself before truncation: near branch or equal-value collisions it is part of the single logarithm of \((z-t)\Psi_R(z,t)/(B(z)B(t))\), and only the bounded continuous pole terms \(-\log|B(z)|-\log|B(t)|\) may be separated after the lower-truncated limit, by Lemma \ref{lem:lower-truncated-bounded-term-separation}.  A measure is called an admissible finite-energy lift if \(R_\ast\mu\) has finite logarithmic energy on \(R(K)\); by Lemma \ref{lem:extended-pullback-energy-equals-pushforward-energy}, this is equivalent to the displayed integral being greater than \(-\infty\).  Then
\[
\boxed{\;
\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))
=\sup_{\mu}\mathcal E_R(\mu),
\;}
\]
where the supremum ranges over all admissible finite-energy lifts \(\mu\) on \(K\).  Moreover the maximizers are exactly the admissible lifts whose pushforward is the equilibrium measure \(\nu_{R(K)}\) of \(R(K)\):
\[
\mathcal E_R(\mu)=\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
R_\ast\mu=\nu_{R(K)}.
\]
Every admissible lift satisfying this pushforward condition has finite pullback energy by Lemma \ref{lem:rational-divided-difference-kernel}, since \(\nu_{R(K)}\) has finite logarithmic energy.
In particular \(R(K)\) is compact and nonpolar, its equilibrium measure exists, and at least one maximizing lift exists.

The singular sets in the formula have the following explicit interpretation.  The factor \(z-t\) records the ordinary diagonal collision.  The diagonal zeros \(\Psi_R(z,z)=0\) are precisely the finite critical points of \(R\) in the source plane, and their images are the corresponding finite branch values.  The off-diagonal equation \(\Psi_R(z,t)=0\), with \(z\ne t\), is the equal-value collision relation \(R(z)=R(t)\) between two distinct source points.  Equivalently, in local coordinates \(u=z-z_0\), \(v=t-z_0\), an ordinary noncritical diagonal has \(R(z)-R(t)\) equal to \(u-v\) times a holomorphic unit, a critical point of local degree \(e\ge2\) has \(R(z)-R(t)=(u-v)H(u,v)\), where \(H\) has leading homogeneous part a nonzero scalar multiple of \(u^{e-1}+u^{e-2}v+\cdots+v^{e-1}\), and an off-diagonal collision at \((z_0,t_0)\), \(z_0\ne t_0\), is exactly a zero of the remaining factor \(\Psi_R\).  Each of these sets contributes the value \(-\infty\) to the pullback kernel, and an admissible finite-energy lift gives them zero \(\mu\times\mu\)-mass.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The image \(R(K)\) is compact and nonpolar by Lemma \ref{lem:nonpolar-rational-image}.  Hence its equilibrium measure \(\nu_{R(K)}\) exists and satisfies
\[
\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))
=\max_{\nu\in\mathcal P(R(K))}\iint\log|u-v|\,\dd\nu(u)\dd\nu(v),
\]
with the logarithmic integral understood by lower truncation.  For each \(\mu\in\mathcal P(K)\), Lemma \ref{lem:rational-divided-difference-kernel} and Lemma \ref{lem:extended-pullback-energy-equals-pushforward-energy} give
\[
\mathcal E_R(\mu)=I(R_\ast\mu).
\]
Therefore \(\mathcal E_R(\mu)\le\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))\) for every admissible finite-energy lift \(\mu\), with equality only when \(R_\ast\mu=\nu_{R(K)}\), because the equilibrium measure of a compact nonpolar set is unique.

It remains only to see that the upper bound is attained by a lift on \(K\).  The sets \(K\) and \(R(K)\) are compact subsets of \(\CC\), hence compact metric and standard Borel.  Apply Lemma \ref{lem:compact-fiber-borel-selection} to the closed set
\[
G:=\{(u,z)\in R(K)\times K:R(z)=u\}.
\]
The fibers are nonempty compact subsets of \(K\) by definition of \(R(K)\) and continuity of \(R\), so there is a standard-Borel selector \(s:R(K)\to K\) with \(R(s(u))=u\).  Put \(\mu=s_\ast\nu_{R(K)}\).  By the final assertion of Lemma \ref{lem:compact-fiber-borel-selection}, this selected lift is a Borel probability measure on \(K\).  Moreover \(R_\ast\mu=(R\circ s)_\ast\nu_{R(K)}=\nu_{R(K)}\).  Lemma \ref{lem:rational-divided-difference-kernel} therefore makes the selected lift admissible with finite pullback energy, and \(\mathcal E_R(\mu)=\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))\).  The same lemma applies to every lift whose pushforward is \(\nu_{R(K)}\), so this proves the variational formula and the stated characterization of all maximizers.

The branch and collision interpretation follows from the algebraic identity
\[
R(z)-R(t)=\frac{(z-t)\Psi_R(z,t)}{B(z)B(t)}
\]
on \(K\times K\), from \(B\ne0\) on \(K\), and from
\[
R'(z)=\frac{\Psi_R(z,z)}{B(z)^2}.
\]
Thus \(z=t\) gives the ordinary diagonal collision, diagonal zeros of \(\Psi_R\) are precisely finite critical points, and off-diagonal zeros of \(\Psi_R\) are precisely distinct equal-value collisions.  The local normal forms in the final sentence are the three alternatives of Lemma \ref{lem:rational-local-collision-classification}.  Since finite-energy of \(R_\ast\mu\) is equivalent to \(\mathcal E_R(\mu)>-\infty\), any admissible lift assigns zero \(\mu\times\mu\)-mass to every subset on which the displayed pullback kernel is \(-\infty\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite collision skeleton of the rational pullback]\label{cor:finite-collision-skeleton-rational-pullback}
In the setting of Theorem \ref{thm:rational-image-capacity-pullback}, let
\[
d:=\max\{\deg A,\deg B\},
\]
the degree of the induced nonconstant map on \(\mathbb P^1\).  For every \(z\in K\), the fiber
\[
\{t\in K:R(t)=R(z)\}
\]
is finite of cardinal at most \(d\).  More precisely, if \(e_z\) is the local degree of \(R\) at \(z\), then \(t=z\) occurs in the equation
\[
A(z)B(t)-A(t)B(z)=0
\]
with multiplicity \(e_z\), and the off-diagonal collision points in \(K\) have total multiplicity at most \(d-e_z\).  Thus the singular locus of the pullback kernel on \(K\times K\) is the finite-degree algebraic collision skeleton
\[
\{(z,t)\in K^2:z=t\}\cup\{(z,t)\in K^2:\Psi_R(z,t)=0\},
\]
whose vertical sections are finite.  Every admissible finite-energy lift in Theorem \ref{thm:rational-image-capacity-pullback} assigns this skeleton zero \(\mu\times\mu\)-mass.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(z\in K\).  Since \(B(z)\ne0\), the equation \(R(t)=R(z)\) on points with \(B(t)\ne0\) is equivalent to
\[
F_z(t):=A(z)B(t)-A(t)B(z)=0.
\]
The polynomial \(F_z\) has degree at most \(d\).  It is not identically zero, for otherwise the rational function \(A/B\) would agree with the constant value \(A(z)/B(z)\) away from the finitely many zeros of \(B\), and hence would be constant, contrary to the hypothesis.  Therefore its zero set is finite and has total multiplicity at most \(d\).

Near \(z\), the denominator is nonzero and
\[
F_z(t)=B(z)B(t)\bigl(R(z)-R(t)\bigr).
\]
If \(e_z\) is the local degree of \(R\) at \(z\), then
\[
R(t)-R(z)=(t-z)^{e_z}U(t),\qquad U(z)\ne0,
\]
so \(t=z\) is a zero of \(F_z\) of multiplicity \(e_z\).  The remaining roots, counted with multiplicity, account for all off-diagonal equal-value collisions and have total multiplicity at most \(d-e_z\).  Dividing by \(z-t\) gives the stated description in terms of \(\Psi_R\), with the diagonal retaining precisely the ramification multiplicity beyond the ordinary logarithmic collision.  The final zero-mass assertion is the last assertion of Theorem \ref{thm:rational-image-capacity-pullback} applied to this explicitly finite-degree skeleton.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local collision classification for the rational pullback kernel]\label{lem:rational-local-collision-classification}
Let \(R=A/B\) be as in Theorem \ref{thm:rational-image-capacity-pullback}, and let \(z_0,t_0\in\CC\) with \(B(z_0)B(t_0)\ne0\).  The singular part of the local pullback kernel near \((z_0,t_0)\) is exhausted by the following three cases.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item If \(z_0=t_0\) and \(R'(z_0)\ne0\), then in a local coordinate \(u=z-z_0\), \(v=t-z_0\),
\[
R(z)-R(t)=(u-v)U(u,v)
\]
with \(U(0,0)\ne0\).  Hence the only singular term is the ordinary logarithmic collision \(\log|z-t|\).
\item If \(z_0=t_0\) and \(R\) has local degree \(e\ge2\) at \(z_0\), then
\[
R(z)-R(t)=(u-v)H(u,v)
\]
where \(H\) has leading homogeneous part a nonzero scalar multiple of \(u^{e-1}+u^{e-2}v+\cdots+v^{e-1}\).  Thus \(z_0\) is a finite critical point, \(R(z_0)\) is the corresponding finite branch value, and the diagonal value \(\Psi_R(z_0,z_0)=0\) records exactly the nontrivial ramification factor beyond the ordinary collision.
\item If \(z_0\ne t_0\), then \(R(z_0)=R(t_0)\) if and only if \(\Psi_R(z_0,t_0)=0\).  In this case the singularity is an off-diagonal equal-value collision and is not a ramification statement unless one of the two source points is separately critical.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because \(B(z_0)\ne0\), the rational map is holomorphic near \(z_0\).  If \(z_0=t_0\), choose a local coordinate \(u=z-z_0\) and write
\[
R(z_0+u)=R(z_0)+u^e U_0(u)
\]
where \(e\ge1\) is the local degree and \(U_0(0)\ne0\).  For two nearby points \(z=z_0+u\) and \(t=z_0+v\),
\[
R(z)-R(t)=u^eU_0(u)-v^eU_0(v).
\]
This holomorphic function vanishes on the diagonal \(u=v\), hence is divisible by \(u-v\).  When \(e=1\), the quotient has value \(U_0(0)\ne0\), giving (i).  When \(e\ge2\), the quotient \(H(u,v):=(R(z_0+u)-R(z_0+v))/(u-v)\) has initial homogeneous part
\[
U_0(0)\bigl(u^{e-1}+u^{e-2}v+\cdots+v^{e-1}\bigr),
\]
which is nonzero as a polynomial and records the ramification beyond the diagonal factor.  The identity
\[
R'(z)=\frac{A'(z)B(z)-A(z)B'(z)}{B(z)^2}=\frac{\Psi_R(z,z)}{B(z)^2}
\]
shows that the diagonal zeros of \(\Psi_R\) are exactly the finite critical points, and their images under \(R\) are the finite branch values.  Finally, if \(z_0\ne t_0\), then \(z-t\) and \(B(z)B(t)\) are nonzero near \((z_0,t_0)\), so
\[
R(z)-R(t)=\frac{(z-t)\Psi_R(z,t)}{B(z)B(t)}
\]
vanishes at \((z_0,t_0)\) exactly when \(\Psi_R(z_0,t_0)=0\).  This proves the off-diagonal alternative and completes the classification.
\end{proof}

We next state the reader-facing degree-two form used in the rest of the paper.  The preceding rational theorem is kept as proof infrastructure: it explains why no other singularities can appear, while the following theorem gives the actual Joukowsky capacity problem and its complete collision skeleton.

\begin{theorem}[Main degree-two Joukowsky capacity pullback and boundary descent]\label{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation}
Let \(r>1\), let
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}=\frac{rz^2+r^{-1}}{z}
\]
and let \(K\subset\CC\setminus\{0\}\) be compact and nonpolar.  Equivalently, \(J_r=A/B\) with coprime polynomials \(A(z)=rz^2+r^{-1}\) and \(B(z)=z\), so \(K\) avoids the unique pole.  All energies below are lower-truncated extended logarithmic energies, and the displayed pullback integral is the single truncated kernel \(\log|J_r(z)-J_r(t)|\), not an independent integration of its singular factors.  Then \(J_r(K)\) is compact and nonpolar and
\[
\log\operatorname{cap}(J_r(K))
=\sup_{\mu}\iint_{K\times K}\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|\,
\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t),
\]
where the supremum is over all admissible finite-energy lifts, equivalently all Borel probability measures \(\mu\) on \(K\) for which \((J_r)_\ast\mu\) has finite logarithmic energy on \(J_r(K)\).  The complete collision set on \(K\times K\) is
\[
\{(z,t):z=t\}\ \cup\ \{(z,t):zt=r^{-2}\}.
\]
Thus every collision is either the ordinary logarithmic diagonal or the single involutive partner \(t=r^{-2}/z\).  Each vertical collision section contains at most the two points \(z\) and \(r^{-2}/z\), with coalescence only at the critical fixed points \(z=\pm r^{-1}\).  The maximizers are exactly the admissible lifts of the equilibrium measure of \(J_r(K)\):
\[
\mathcal E_{J_r}(\mu)=\log\operatorname{cap}(J_r(K))
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(J_r)_\ast\mu=\nu_{J_r(K)}.
\]
In particular the off-diagonal graph is disjoint from \(\TT\times\TT\).  Hence the boundary map \(J_r:\TT\to E_r:=J_r(\TT)\) is a homeomorphism, every \(\sigma\in\mathcal P(E_r)\) has a unique source measure \(\eta\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) with \(J_{r\ast}\eta=\sigma\), and
\[
I(\sigma)=
\iint_{\TT^2}
\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|
\,\dd\eta(z)\dd\eta(t).
\]
Thus the elliptic-boundary equilibrium problem in Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} has only the ordinary diagonal collision singularity on the source circle.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(A(z)=rz^2+r^{-1}\) and \(B(z)=z\), the difference quotient in Theorem \ref{thm:rational-image-capacity-pullback} is
\[
\Psi_{J_r}(z,t)=\frac{(rz^2+r^{-1})t-(rt^2+r^{-1})z}{z-t}
=rzt-r^{-1}.
\]
Substitution into the pullback kernel gives
\[
\log|z-t|+
\log|rzt-r^{-1}|-
\log|z|-
\log|t|
=
\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|.
\]
The off-diagonal collision relation is \(rzt-r^{-1}=0\), equivalently \(zt=r^{-2}\).  If \(|z|=|t|=1\), this relation cannot hold for \(r>1\).  The maximizer statement is the specialization of Theorem \ref{thm:rational-image-capacity-pullback} to \(R=J_r\).  The section bound follows from the displayed factorization: for fixed \(z\), a collision point \(t\) must be either \(z\) or \(r^{-2}/z\), and these coincide exactly when \(z^2=r^{-2}\), that is, when \(z=\pm r^{-1}\).

It remains to record the boundary descent, since this is the degree-two form used later.  If \(z,t\in\TT\), then the relation \(J_r(z)=J_r(t)\) forces either \(z=t\) or \(zt=r^{-2}\).  The second alternative is impossible on \(\TT^2\), so \(J_r\) is injective on \(\TT\).  It is continuous from the compact space \(\TT\) onto the Hausdorff space \(E_r\), hence is a homeomorphism.  Therefore every \(\sigma\in\mathcal P(E_r)\) has the unique source \(\eta=(J_r^{-1})_\ast\sigma\).  Substituting \(K=\TT\) in the kernel identity above and pushing the lower-truncated logarithmic integral through this homeomorphism gives the displayed boundary energy formula.  Thus the final assertion follows by specializing the general singularity description in Theorem \ref{thm:rational-image-capacity-pullback} to the unit circle.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Degree-two boundary collision reduction]\label{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity}
Let \(r>1\).  On the source circle \(\TT\), the degree-two Joukowsky map has no branch-graph collision except the ordinary diagonal:
\[
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:J_r(z)=J_r(t)\}=\{(z,z):z\in\TT\}.
\]
Consequently the boundary kernel on \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\) is exactly
\[
\log|J_r(z)-J_r(t)|=
\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|,
\qquad z,t\in\TT,
\]
where the second factor is continuous and nonzero on \(\TT^2\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation}, the full collision set for \(J_r\) is the union of the diagonal \(z=t\) and the involutive partner relation \(zt=r^{-2}\).  On \(\TT^2\) the second relation is impossible, because \(|zt|=1\) while \(r^{-2}<1\).  Hence the only boundary collision for \(J_r\) is the ordinary diagonal.  The displayed identity is the same theorem specialized to \(K=\TT\), and the factor \(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\) cannot vanish on \(\TT^2\), since vanishing would again imply \(|zt|=r^{-2}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Joukowsky boundary skeleton obstruction]\label{thm:joukowsky-boundary-skeleton-obstruction}
Let \(r>1\), let \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), and let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product.  Among the two algebraic collision relations in the degree-two Joukowsky pullback, only the diagonal relation survives on the boundary after composition with \(B\):
\[
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:J_r(B(z))=J_r(B(t))\}
=
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:B(z)=B(t)\}.
\]
Consequently, for every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) and \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\), the lower-truncated energy of the covered ellipse depends only on \(\eta\):
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)
=
\iint_{\TT^2}
\log\left|(u-v)\left(r-r^{-1}u^{-1}v^{-1}\right)\right|
\,\dd\eta(u)\dd\eta(v).
\]
The off-diagonal Joukowsky partner relation contributes no boundary singularity and no additional finite-cover invariant.  Therefore, if two boundary measures \(\mu_1,\mu_2\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) satisfy \(B_\ast\mu_1=B_\ast\mu_2\), then
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu_1)=I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu_2),
\]
and every maximizer for the covered boundary problem is detected exactly by the single condition \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The degree-two collision theorem gives the complete collision set for \(J_r\) as the union of the diagonal relation \(u=v\) and the partner relation \(uv=r^{-2}\).  Since \(B(\TT)\subset\TT\), a covered boundary collision satisfies
\[
J_r(B(z))=J_r(B(t))
\]
only if either \(B(z)=B(t)\) or \(B(z)B(t)=r^{-2}\).  The second alternative is impossible on \(\TT^2\), because \(|B(z)B(t)|=1\) while \(r^{-2}<1\).  This proves the displayed collision skeleton.

For \(u,v\in\TT\), Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity} gives
\[
\log|J_r(u)-J_r(v)|=
\log\left|(u-v)\left(r-r^{-1}u^{-1}v^{-1}\right)\right|,
\]
where the second factor is continuous and nonzero on \(\TT^2\).  Applying the lower-truncated pushforward identity to the map \((z,t)\mapsto(B(z),B(t))\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)
&=\iint_{\TT^2}\log|J_r(B(z))-J_r(B(t))|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t)\\
&=\iint_{\TT^2}\log|J_r(u)-J_r(v)|\,\dd\eta(u)\dd\eta(v)\\
&=\iint_{\TT^2}
\log\left|(u-v)\left(r-r^{-1}u^{-1}v^{-1}\right)\right|
\,\dd\eta(u)\dd\eta(v),
\end{aligned}
\]
again with all logarithmic integrals interpreted by lower truncation.  Thus the energy is a function only of \(B_\ast\mu\), and equal pushforwards give equal energies.

Finally, Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} identifies the unique maximizing source measure for \(J_r:\TT\to E_r\) as Haar measure and the maximum as \(\log r\).  Applying this sharp boundary statement to the measure \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\) gives equality in the covered problem exactly when \(\eta=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).  Hence the covered problem has no invariant beyond the pushed-forward boundary measure, and the surviving maximizers are precisely the Haar lifts through \(B\).
\end{proof}

The next theorem is stated here because its collision and descent identities are used to organize the finite-cover discussion.  Its sharp variational assertion explicitly depends on the Fourier diagonalization in Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} below; that later theorem uses only the degree-two kernel identity above and not the present descent theorem, so the reference is forward but not circular.

\begin{theorem}[Exact boundary descent for Joukowsky covers]\label{thm:exact-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers}
Let \(r>1\), put \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), and interpret logarithmic energies by lower truncation.  Then the boundary parametrization \(J_r:\TT\to E_r\) is a homeomorphism, and every \(\sigma\in\mathcal P(E_r)\) has a unique source measure \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) satisfying \(J_{r\ast}\mu=\sigma\).  For this source measure,
\[
I(\sigma)=\iint_{\TT^2}\log\left|(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)\right|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t).
\]
Moreover the boundary variational problem has the sharp extended-energy form
\[
\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)} I(J_{r\ast}\mu)=\log r,
\qquad
I(J_{r\ast}\mu)=\log r
\Longleftrightarrow
\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
If \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product, then the covered problem descends exactly through the single measure \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\): the complete covered collision relation on \(\TT^2\) is
\[
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:J_r(B(z))=J_r(B(t))\}
=
\{(z,t)\in\TT^2:B(z)=B(t)\}.
\]
For every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\), with \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\), one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)
&=\iint_{\TT^2}\log|J_r(B(z))-J_r(B(t))|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t)\\
&=\iint_{\TT^2}\log\left|(B(z)-B(t))
\left(r-r^{-1}B(z)^{-1}B(t)^{-1}\right)\right|\,\dd\mu(z)\dd\mu(t)\\
&=I(J_{r\ast}\eta),
\end{aligned}
\]
and the first two integrals are finite exactly when \(I(J_{r\ast}\eta)>-\infty\).  Consequently
\[
\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)}I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=\log r,
\qquad
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=\log r
\Longleftrightarrow
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity} shows that \(J_r\) is injective on \(\TT\).  Since \(J_r:\TT\to E_r\) is a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space, it is a homeomorphism.  Therefore every probability measure \(\sigma\) on \(E_r\) has the unique lift \(\mu=(J_r^{-1})_\ast\sigma\), and the lower-truncated pushforward identity gives the displayed energy formula.

The equilibrium statement follows from Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} below; that theorem uses only the degree-two kernel identity and not the present descent conclusion.  It gives \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\) and identifies \(J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/(2\pi))\) as the unique equilibrium measure of \(E_r\).  Hence equality in the capacity variational problem for a source measure \(\mu\) is equivalent to
\[
J_{r\ast}\mu=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right),
\]
and injectivity gives \(\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).

Now let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product.  Since \(B(\TT)\subset\TT\), injectivity of \(J_r\) on \(\TT\) gives
\[
J_r(B(z))=J_r(B(t))
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B(z)=B(t),
\]
which proves the covered collision classification.  Substituting \(B(z)\) and \(B(t)\) into the boundary kernel identity gives the displayed covered-kernel formula; the factor
\[
r-r^{-1}B(z)^{-1}B(t)^{-1}
\]
is continuous and nonzero on \(\TT^2\), because \(|B(z)B(t)|=1\) whereas \(r^{-2}<1\).  Hence the lower-truncated singular set is exactly \(B(z)=B(t)\).

Functoriality of pushforward gives
\[
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu).
\]
Applying the lower-truncated logarithmic energy functional to both sides proves the energy identity and the equivalence of finiteness.  The sharp supremum and equality criterion now follow from the already proved boundary variational statement applied to \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\).  Conversely, Haar lifts exist for every finite Blaschke product by Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula}, so the supremum is attained.  Thus all covered-boundary capacity calculations factor through the single pushed-forward source measure \(B_\ast\mu\), and the only residual maximizers are Haar lifts through the chosen finite cover.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Lower-truncated Fourier evaluation on the circle]\label{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation}
Let \(\mu\) be a probability measure on \(\TT\) with finite logarithmic energy.  Then
\[
\iint_{\TT\times\TT}\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=-
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k},
\]
where the integral is the lower-truncated logarithmic integral.  If \(q\in\RR\) and \(|q|<1\), then the continuous kernel \(\log|1-qe^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|\) satisfies
\[
\iint\log|1-qe^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=-
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^k\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\mu(k)^2)}{k}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(0<\rho<1\), the Abel-regularized identity
\[
\log|1-\rho e^{i(\theta-\phi)}|
=-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^k\cos k(\theta-\phi)}{k}
\]
is uniformly convergent on compact subannuli and may be integrated term by term against \(\mu\times\mu\).  It gives
\[
\iint\log|1-\rho e^{i(\theta-\phi)}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi)
=-
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^k|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}.
\]
We now pass from the Abel kernels to the lower-truncated boundary kernel without using any pointwise monotonicity in \(\rho\).  Put
\[
\kappa(\alpha)=\log|1-e^{i\alpha}|,
\qquad
\kappa_\rho(\alpha)=\log|1-\rho e^{i\alpha}|,
\]
and let \(\lambda\) be the image of \(\mu\times\mu\) under
\((\theta,\phi)\mapsto\theta-\phi\).  The finite-energy hypothesis is exactly
\(\int\kappa\,\dd\lambda>-\infty\), with the integral understood by lower truncation, because
\(|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|=|1-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}|\).  We justify the Abel passage directly.  The kernel \(\kappa\) is bounded above by \(\log 2\), and the finite-energy hypothesis gives \(\kappa^-\in L^1(\lambda)\); in particular \(\lambda\{0\}=0\).  For \(\alpha\ne0\), \(\kappa_\rho(\alpha)\to\kappa(\alpha)\) as \(\rho\uparrow1\).  Moreover, for \(\rho\ge1/2\),
\[
|1-\rho e^{i\alpha}|^2=(1-\rho)^2+\rho|1-e^{i\alpha}|^2,
\]
and hence
\[
\kappa(\alpha)+\frac12\log\rho\le \kappa_\rho(\alpha)\le \log2
\]
for \(\lambda\)-almost every \(\alpha\).  Thus \(\kappa_\rho^+\le\log2\) and \(\kappa_\rho^-\le \kappa^-+\frac12\log2\) for \(\rho\ge1/2\).  Dominated convergence, with the right-hand side interpreted as the lower-truncated logarithmic integral, gives
\[
\lim_{\rho\uparrow1}\int\kappa_\rho(\alpha)\,\dd\lambda(\alpha)
=\int\kappa(\alpha)\,\dd\lambda(\alpha),
\]
Hence the left side of the Abel identity tends to
\[
\iint\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|\,\dd\mu(\theta)\dd\mu(\phi).
\]
On the right, \(|\widehat\mu(k)|^2/k\ge0\), so monotone convergence for the nonnegative partial Abel sums gives
\[
\lim_{\rho\uparrow1}
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\rho^k|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}
=
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}
\]
in \([0,\infty]\).  The finite lower-truncated energy just obtained forces this series to be finite, and the first identity follows.  For real \(|q|<1\), the expansion of \(\log(1-qe^{-i(\theta+\phi)})\) is uniformly and absolutely convergent on \(\TT^2\), and termwise integration gives the second identity because \(\int e^{-ik\theta}\,\dd\mu(\theta)=\widehat\mu(k)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp Joukowsky equilibrium stability]\label{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability}
Let \(r>1\), let \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\), and let
\[
E_r=J_r(\TT),\qquad \mu_r=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right).
\]
For a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\TT\), write
\[
\widehat\mu(k)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-ik\theta}\,\dd\mu(\theta),\qquad k\in\ZZ,
\]
and let
\[
I(\sigma):=\iint\log|x-y|\,\dd\sigma(x)\dd\sigma(y)
\]
denote logarithmic energy as the lower-truncated extended logarithmic integral.  If \(\mu\) has finite logarithmic energy on \(\TT\), then
\[
\boxed{\;
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu)
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2+r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\mu(k)^2)}{k}.
\;}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-r^{-2k})|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}
\le \log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu)
\le
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1+r^{-2k})|\widehat\mu(k)|^2}{k}.
\]
The preceding boxed identity and the two-sided Fourier bounds are asserted only for finite-energy source measures.  Separately, the unrestricted capacity variational principle on the compact set \(E_r\) gives the following all-measure extremal statement: for every Borel probability measure \(\eta\) on \(\TT\), with no source-energy hypothesis,
\[
I(J_{r\ast}\eta)\le \log r,
\]
and equality holds if and only if \(\eta=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).  Equivalently, \(\mu_r\) is the unique equilibrium measure of \(E_r\), and \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\).  For each \(k\ge1\) the constants \(1+r^{-2k}\) and \(1-r^{-2k}\) are sharp on the \(k\)-th Fourier mode: for \(|\varepsilon|\le1/2\),
\[
\dd\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}=(1+2\varepsilon\cos k\theta)\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi},
\qquad
\dd\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}=(1+2\varepsilon\sin k\theta)\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}
\]
satisfy
\[
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon})=\frac{(1+r^{-2k})\varepsilon^2}{k},
\qquad
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon})=\frac{(1-r^{-2k})\varepsilon^2}{k}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(z=e^{i\theta}\) and \(w=e^{i\phi}\),
\[
J_r(z)-J_r(w)=(z-w)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}w^{-1}\right),
\]
and hence
\[
\log|J_r(e^{i\theta})-J_r(e^{i\phi})|
=\log r+\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|
 +\log|1-r^{-2}e^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|.
\]
The standard Fourier expansions, interpreted in the lower-truncated sense justified by Lemma \ref{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation},
\[
\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|
=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Re}(e^{ik(\theta-\phi)})}{k},
\quad
\log|1-qe^{-i(\theta+\phi)}|
=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^k\operatorname{Re}(e^{-ik(\theta+\phi)})}{k}
\]
with \(q=r^{-2}\), give
\[
I(J_{r\ast}\mu)
=\log r-
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2+r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\mu(k)^2)}{k}.
\]
This proves the exact diagonalization for finite-energy source measures only; the argument above is not used for arbitrary measures because the circle Fourier series need not represent a finite energy outside that class.

Writing \(\widehat\mu(k)=x_k+i y_k\), the \(k\)-th summand numerator is
\[
|\widehat\mu(k)|^2+r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\mu(k)^2)
=(1+r^{-2k})x_k^2+(1-r^{-2k})y_k^2,
\]
which proves the two modewise bounds.  Equality in the left or right coefficient for a fixed mode is attained by purely imaginary or purely real \(\widehat\mu(k)\), respectively.  The densities \(\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}\) and \(\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}\) are nonnegative probability measures for \(|\varepsilon|\le1/2\), have no nonzero Fourier coefficients except in modes \(\pm k\), and have \(\widehat\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}(k)=\varepsilon\) and \(\widehat\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}(k)=-i\varepsilon\).  The displayed extremal identities follow.

Since every term in the deficit is nonnegative and \(1-r^{-2k}>0\), one has \(I(J_{r\ast}\mu)\le\log r\) for finite-energy source measures, with equality only when \(\widehat\mu(k)=0\) for all \(k\ne0\), that is, when \(\mu\) is Haar measure on \(\TT\).

It remains to identify the global equilibrium and the capacity of \(E_r\).  This is the standard exterior conformal normalization of logarithmic capacity, but we spell out the normalization to fix the factor \(r\).  Put \(\zeta=rz\).  Then
\[
J_r(z)=\zeta+\zeta^{-1},
\]
and the exterior of \(E_r\) is the conformal image of \(\{|\zeta|>r\}\) under \(\zeta\mapsto\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\).  Its inverse branch satisfies \(\zeta(w)=w+O(1/w)\) at infinity, so the Green function of \(\CC\setminus E_r\) with pole at infinity is
\[
g_{E_r}(w)=\log|\zeta(w)|-\log r.
\]
The expansion at infinity is \(g_{E_r}(w)=\log|w|-\log r+o(1)\).  With the usual convention that \(g_K(w)=\log|w|-\log\operatorname{cap}(K)+o(1)\) for a compact nonpolar set \(K\), this gives \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\).  Harmonic measure from infinity is the pushforward of normalized arclength on \(|\zeta|=r\), equivalently \(\mu_r=J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/(2\pi))\).  By uniqueness of the equilibrium measure, \(\mu_r\) is the unique equilibrium measure of \(E_r\).  The logarithmic-capacity variational principle gives, for every Borel probability measure \(\nu\) on \(E_r\),
\[
I(\nu)\le\log\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=\log r,
\]
with equality only when \(\nu=\mu_r\).  Applying this to \(\nu=J_{r\ast}\eta\) proves the asserted all-measure bound.  Since the degree-two boundary map \(J_r:\TT\to E_r\) is a homeomorphism by Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity}, the equality condition \(J_{r\ast}\eta=\mu_r\) is equivalent to \(\eta=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Calibrated two-probe scale formula for the Joukowsky scale]\label{thm:two-mode-joukowsky-scale-rigidity}
Fix an integer \(k\ge1\) and a real number \(\varepsilon\) with \(0<|\varepsilon|\le1/2\).  For \(r>1\), let
\[
\dd\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}=(1+2\varepsilon\cos k\theta)\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi},
\qquad
\dd\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}=(1+2\varepsilon\sin k\theta)\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Then the calibrated ordered pair of boundary pushforward energies
\[
\mathcal A_k(r,\varepsilon):=
\left(
I(J_{r\ast}\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}),
I(J_{r\ast}\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon})
\right)
\]
determines the Joukowsky scale \(r\) uniquely within this prescribed two-probe family.  More precisely, if
\[
E_c=I(J_{r\ast}\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}),
\qquad
E_s=I(J_{r\ast}\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}),
\]
then
\[
E_s-E_c=\frac{2\varepsilon^2}{k}r^{-2k},
\qquad
\log r=\frac{E_c+E_s}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{k},
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{\;
r=\left(\frac{2\varepsilon^2}{k(E_s-E_c)}\right)^{1/(2k)}
=\exp\!\left(\frac{E_c+E_s}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{k}\right).
\;}
\]
Consequently, for \(r,s>1\), equality
\[
\mathcal A_k(r,\varepsilon)=\mathcal A_k(s,\varepsilon)
\]
forces \(r=s\).  The same conclusion holds after a finite Blaschke cover \(B\) whenever the pushed-forward probe measures \(B_\ast\nu^c\) and \(B_\ast\nu^s\) are \(\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}\) and \(\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}\), respectively; in that case the two covered energies
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\nu^c),
\qquad
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\nu^s)
\]
recover the same \(r\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The measures \(\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}\) and \(\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}\) have bounded densities on \(\TT\), hence finite logarithmic energy.  The sharp mode computation in Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} therefore applies and gives
\[
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon})=\frac{(1+r^{-2k})\varepsilon^2}{k},
\qquad
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon})=\frac{(1-r^{-2k})\varepsilon^2}{k}.
\]
With \(E_c\) and \(E_s\) as in the statement, subtracting the first energy identity from the second gives
\[
E_s-E_c=
\left(\log r-\frac{(1-r^{-2k})\varepsilon^2}{k}\right)
-
\left(\log r-\frac{(1+r^{-2k})\varepsilon^2}{k}\right)
=\frac{2\varepsilon^2}{k}r^{-2k}.
\]
This quantity is positive because \(\varepsilon\ne0\) and \(r>1\), so the first displayed reconstruction formula for \(r\) follows.  Adding the two identities gives
\[
E_c+E_s=2\log r-\frac{2\varepsilon^2}{k},
\]
which is the logarithmic reconstruction formula.  Either formula is injective on \((1,\infty)\), so equality of the ordered pairs for two scales \(r,s>1\) implies \(r=s\).

For the covered assertion, the descent identity in Theorem \ref{thm:exact-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers} gives
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\nu)=I(J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\nu))
\]
for every probability measure \(\nu\) on \(\TT\).  Applying this first to \(\nu=\nu^c\) and then to \(\nu=\nu^s\) reduces the two covered energies to the two already treated probe energies for \(\mu^c_{k,\varepsilon}\) and \(\mu^s_{k,\varepsilon}\).  The same reconstruction formulas therefore recover \(r\) after the finite Blaschke lift.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Extended-energy Joukowsky equilibrium bound]\label{prop:extended-energy-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}
Let \(r>1\) and let \(\mu\) be an arbitrary Borel probability measure on \(\TT\).  With logarithmic energies interpreted by lower truncation,
\[
I(J_{r\ast}\mu)\le \log r.
\]
Equality holds if and only if \(\mu\) is Haar measure on \(\TT\).  Equivalently, for every probability measure \(\eta\) on \(\TT\), finite source energy is not needed for the sharp equilibrium bound
\[
I(J_{r\ast}\eta)\le \log r,
\]
and the unique maximizer is \(\eta=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is the all-measure extremal part of Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability}, repeated here for later reference in the finite-cover argument.  The final formulation is the same assertion with \(\eta\) in place of \(\mu\).
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Boundary covering formula for finite Blaschke products]\label{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula}
Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\ge1\).  Then \(B\) has no critical point on \(\TT\), the boundary map \(B:\TT\to\TT\) is an orientation-preserving degree-\(d\) covering, and for every continuous function \(f\) on \(\TT\),
\[
\int_{0}^{2\pi} f(B(e^{i\theta}))\,|B'(e^{i\theta})|\,\dd\theta
=d\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{it})\,\dd t.
\]
Consequently the probability measure
\[
\dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})=\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\,\dd\theta
\]
satisfies \(B_\ast\mu_B=\dd t/(2\pi)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
B(z)=\zeta\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{z-a_j}{1-\overline{a_j}z},
\qquad |\zeta|=1,
\qquad a_j\in\DD.
\]
The following boundary computation is the standard finite-Blaschke boundary argument and Poisson-kernel derivative formula \citep[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970}.  For \(z=e^{i\theta}\), differentiating a continuous boundary argument \(B(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\beta(\theta)}\) on any local arc gives
\[
\beta'(\theta)=\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{e^{i\theta}B'(e^{i\theta})}{B(e^{i\theta})}\right)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1-|a_j|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-a_j|^2}>0.
\]
Thus \(B'(e^{i\theta})\ne0\) on \(\TT\), and \(|B'(e^{i\theta})|=\beta'(\theta)\).  The argument increase over one full turn is \(\beta(2\pi)-\beta(0)=2\pi d\), so \(B:\TT\to\TT\) is an orientation-preserving covering of degree \(d\).  Partition \([0,2\pi]\) into the \(d\) inverse branches on which \(\beta\) maps the branch interval bijectively onto an interval of length \(2\pi\).  On each branch the substitution \(t=\beta(\theta)\) gives one copy of \(\int_0^{2\pi}f(e^{it})\,\dd t\).  Summing the \(d\) branches proves the change-of-variables formula, and division by \(2\pi d\) gives the asserted pushforward identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite Blaschke--Joukowsky covers]\label{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover}
Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\ge1\), let \(r>1\), and set
\[
R_{B,r}(e^{i\theta})=J_r(B(e^{i\theta})).
\]
For every probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\TT\), with \(\eta:=B_\ast\mu\), and with no finite-energy assumption on \(\mu\), one has the extended-energy bound
\[
I((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu)
=I(J_{r\ast}\eta)
\le \log r,
\]
and equality holds if and only if \(\eta\) is Haar measure.  If \(\eta\) has finite logarithmic energy, then the sharper Fourier identity holds:
\[
\log r-I((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu)
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\frac{|\widehat\eta(k)|^2+r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\eta(k)^2)}{k}.
\]
Thus the energy deficit and the sharp equilibrium bound factor only through the pushed-forward measure \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\); source finite energy is not part of the equilibrium-lift class and is assumed only when invoking the displayed Fourier identity for \(\eta\).  The equilibrium lifts are exactly the probability measures \(\mu\) satisfying
\[
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Among them is the canonical absolutely continuous lift
\[
\boxed{\;
\dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})=\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\,\dd\theta.
\;}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity \((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu)=J_{r\ast}\eta\) reduces the all-measure maximal-energy statement directly to Proposition \ref{prop:extended-energy-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}.  When \(\eta\) has finite logarithmic energy, Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} gives the displayed Fourier deficit formula.  The same proposition shows that equality in the maximal-energy problem occurs exactly when \(\eta\) is Haar measure.  This proves the characterization of all equilibrium lifts without any finite source-energy assumption on \(\mu\).

It remains to verify the canonical density.  Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula} applies to every continuous test function on \(\TT\) and gives \(B_\ast\mu_B\) equal to Haar measure.  Thus \(\mu_B\) is an equilibrium lift.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Canonical Blaschke lift source energy]\label{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy}
Let
\[
B(z)=\zeta\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{z-a_j}{1-\overline{a_j}z},
\qquad |\zeta|=1,
\qquad a_j\in\DD,
\]
and let \(\mu_B\) be the canonical lift from Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover}.  Then its source-circle logarithmic energy is
\[
\boxed{\;
I_{\TT}(\mu_B):=\iint_{\TT\times\TT}\log|z-t|\,\dd\mu_B(z)\dd\mu_B(t)
=\frac1{d^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\log|1-\overline{a_i}a_j|.
\;}
\]
In particular \(I_{\TT}(\mu_B)\le0\), with equality if and only if \(a_1=\cdots=a_d=0\), equivalently \(B(z)=\zeta z^d\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the standard finite-Blaschke boundary derivative formula, used already in Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula} and classically recorded in \citet[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970}, the boundary derivative is the sum of the Poisson kernels of its zeros:
\[
\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{d}
=\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1-|a_j|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-a_j|^2}.
\]
With the convention \(\widehat\sigma(k)=\int e^{-ik\theta}\,\dd\sigma(\theta)\), the Poisson kernel has the classical Fourier expansion \citep[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970}
\[
\frac{1-|a|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-a|^2}=
1+2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{k\ge1}\overline a^{\,k}e^{ik\theta},
\]
so its \(k\)-th coefficient is \(\overline a^{\,k}\).  The normalization is important: the density of \(\mu_B\) is \(|B'|/(2\pi d)\), so the displayed Poisson sum is divided by \(d\), and the circle logarithmic kernel is the unnormalized kernel \(\log|z-t|\) rather than \(-\log|z-t|\).  Consequently the sign below is the same as in Lemma \ref{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation}: there is no constant term and mode \(k\) contributes \(-|\widehat\sigma(k)|^2/k\).
Thus, for \(k\ge1\), the Fourier coefficients of \(\mu_B\) are
\[
\widehat\mu_B(k)=\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^{d}\overline{a_j}^{\ k}.
\]
The displayed boundary density is smooth on \(\TT\), hence \(\mu_B\) has finite circle logarithmic energy and Lemma \ref{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation} applies.  Using the circle-kernel expansion from that lemma,
\[
\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|
=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Re}(e^{ik(\theta-\phi)})}{k},
\]
we get
\[
I_{\TT}(\mu_B)=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat\mu_B(k)|^2}{k}
=-\frac1{d^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\overline{a_i}a_j)^k}{k}.
\]
Since \(|\overline{a_i}a_j|<1\), the inner series is \(-\log(1-\overline{a_i}a_j)\).  Taking real parts gives
\[
I_{\TT}(\mu_B)=\frac1{d^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\log|1-\overline{a_i}a_j|.
\]
The Fourier form also gives \(I_{\TT}(\mu_B)=-\sum_{k\ge1}|\widehat\mu_B(k)|^2/k\le0\).  Equality holds if and only if all positive Fourier coefficients vanish, equivalently \(\sum_j\overline{a_j}^{\ k}=0\) for every \(k\ge1\).  Newton's identities then force all elementary symmetric functions of \(a_1,\dots,a_d\) to vanish, so \(a_1=\cdots=a_d=0\).  Conversely, if all zeros are zero, then \(B(z)=\zeta z^d\), \(\mu_B\) is Haar measure, and the energy is \(0\).
\end{proof}
