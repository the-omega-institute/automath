\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
The classical Joukowsky map is a basic conformal mechanism by which a
circle gives an ellipse, and it gives a particularly transparent test
case for the interaction of logarithmic capacity, boundary energy, and
algebraic elimination \citep{Pommerenke1992,Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,SaffTotik1997,StahlTotik1992,GKZ1994}.  We work throughout with
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},\qquad r\ge1,
\]
and write \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), with the elliptic regime understood to be
\(r>1\).  The elementary factorization
\[
J_r(z)-J_r(t)=(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)
\]
separates the boundary diagonal from the reciprocal collision locus
\(zt=r^{-2}\).  For \(r>1\), this second locus misses \(\TT^2\), so the boundary
energy on \(E_r\) descends to the circle logarithmic kernel plus one
bounded Fourier-explicit factor.  The same reciprocal scale later
appears in the polynomial identity
\[
r^nz^nQ_r(J_r(z))=P(z)P^\vee(r^2z),
\]
where it separates the pulled-back divisor into the two shells
\(\TT\) and \(r^{-2}\TT\).  The paper is organized around this common
scale in capacity, Fourier stability, finite Blaschke covers, Mahler
transport, exact complete coefficient-vector inverse theory, and annular
Rouch\'e stability.
At \(r=1\), the reciprocal locus enters \(\TT^2\) and \(E_1\) collapses
to \([-2,2]\).  The resulting endpoint theorem classifies the enlarged
equilibrium fiber and computes the sharp first-order functional that
selects Haar measure when the ellipse reopens.

The main body is organized as a focused synthesis rather than as an
inventory of independent theorems.  Its principal assertions are the
following five packages; the auxiliary rational-pullback facts, repeated
normalization reminders, and endpoint bookkeeping records are placed in
the appendix.
Throughout the inverse module, inverse assertions have the fixed data
meaning of Definition \ref{def:transport-coefficient-observations}:
exact algebraic recovery from complete ordered coefficient vectors of
transported polynomials, or from shell data only after those complete
vectors have already been obtained.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Joukowsky boundary package.}
  The degree-two specialization of the lower-truncated rational pullback
  kernel gives the collision skeleton \(z=t\) or \(zt=r^{-2}\).  On
  \(\TT^2\) this yields \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\), the equilibrium
  measure \(J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/2\pi)\), the all-measure-only
  variational inequality, and the separate finite-energy-only Fourier
  deficit with sharp mode constants.  At \(r=1\), the same kernel
  factorization gives the complete reflection-symmetry equality fiber
  and its sharp opening selection law.
  \item \emph{Finite Blaschke covers.}  For \(J_r\circ B\), the boundary
  energy depends on a source measure only through \(B_\ast\mu\).  The
  equality class is the full Haar-lift fiber
  \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).  The canonical absolutely continuous
  member of that fiber has source energy given by an explicit product
  over the Blaschke zeros.
  \item \emph{Transport and exact inverse package.}  The transport
  polynomial \(Q_r\) carries Jensen--Mahler means, resultants, and
  discriminants in the fixed normalization.  The exact inverse
  statements are confined to complete coefficient-vector data: one scale
  gives the \(z\mapsto r^{-2}/z\) resonance fiber, two scales give the
  finite perfect-matching graph, and \(n+1\) scales give the
  Laurent-interpolation coefficient-vector proof.  The \(n+1\)-scale
  count is a sharp interpolation budget for that proof, not a sharp
  source-recovery lower bound for every algebraically constrained source
  class.
  \item \emph{Local contour-selected annular divisor stability.}  Under the unit-circle
  source-root separation, an explicit Rouch\'e envelope on contours
  between \(|z|=r^{-2}\) and \(|z|=1\) determines a unique
  contour-selected annular factor, local to the already fixed model
  \((r,P)\) and those contours.  This is a zero-count stability result,
  not a global inverse-reconstruction stability theorem; exact
  unit-circle realizability is still certified separately.  The endpoint record combines the
  capacity coordinate, the Fourier scale weights, the source-factor
  inverse, and the Blaschke equality class to recover
  \[
  \bigl(\log r,E_r,J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/2\pi),Q_r(P),P\bigr)
  \]
  with precisely the finite-Blaschke Haar-lift fiber remaining.  This is
  a compatibility record, not an additional theorem source.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Principal dependency map}
Only five packages are used as principal inputs in the main line; the
appendix records the general rational-map interface and the formal
endpoint bookkeeping.  The one-direction dependency graph is:
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\boxed{\text{Joukowsky boundary package}}\\
\text{\scriptsize collision skeleton, capacity normalization,
Fourier identity, all-measure bound, collapsed-endpoint selection}\\[0.35em]
\Downarrow\\[-0.1em]
\boxed{\text{finite-Blaschke lift package}}\\
\text{\scriptsize dependence through \(B_\ast\mu\), Haar-lift equality
class, canonical lift energy}\\[0.35em]
\Downarrow\\[-0.1em]
\boxed{\text{transport package}}\\
\text{\scriptsize pullback factorization, Mahler identities,
resultant and discriminant transport}\\[0.35em]
\Downarrow\\[-0.1em]
\boxed{\text{exact coefficient-vector inverse package}}\\
\text{\scriptsize one-scale resonance fiber, two-scale matching graph,
\(n+1\)-scale interpolation budget}\\[0.35em]
\Downarrow\\[-0.1em]
\boxed{\text{local contour-selected annular divisor selector}}\\
\text{\scriptsize zero-count stability after exact model identification
and fixed contours}\\[0.35em]
\Downarrow\\[-0.1em]
\boxed{\text{appendix endpoint record}}\\
\text{\scriptsize formal realizability certificate and residual
finite-Blaschke Haar-lift fiber.}
\end{array}
\]
The first box uses classical logarithmic-potential theory: lower-truncated
energies, the pushforward variational principle for capacity, and the
divided-difference expression for a rational pullback kernel.  The
paper-specific step begins with the specialization
\[
J_r(z)=J_r(t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z=t
\ \text{or}\ zt=r^{-2},
\]
because on \(\TT^2\) the second collision graph disappears while the same
reciprocal scale \(r^{-2}\) remains visible in the algebraic pullback
identity.  The Fourier diagonalization of the resulting boundary kernel is
the first new fixed-scale analytic recombination, but it is used only in
the finite-energy class; the unrestricted measure statement is only the
separate variational bound.  The finite-Blaschke theorem uses only the
classical boundary-covering property of finite Blaschke products plus these
separated Joukowsky conclusions, and its new content is the
Haar-lift equality class and canonical-lift energy in the present
normalization.  The transport identity uses elementary algebra, resultants,
and Jensen--Mahler input; the paper-specific inverse results are the
exact complete coefficient-vector shell selection, matching graph, and
multiscale Laurent interpolation tied to the same \(r^{-2}\)-shell.  The Rouch\'e step uses
the classical zero-counting theorem only locally, after the model and
separating contours have already been fixed.  The final synthesis is
therefore not a parallel theorem list: it is the compatibility of these
classical inputs and paper-specific fixed-scale recombinations along the
single chain displayed above.

\subsection*{Related work and novelty boundary}
The classical inputs are the variational definition of logarithmic
capacity and equilibrium measure, harmonic measure from infinity,
Jensen--Mahler identities, Poisson-kernel Fourier coefficients,
finite Blaschke product boundary theory
\citep[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970} and \citep{Colwell1985}, standard resultants
and discriminants \citep[Chap.~12]{GKZ1994} and \citep{CoxLittleOShea2005}, and
Rouch\'e's theorem in its usual contour-counting form
\citep[pp.~152--153]{Ahlfors1979}.  The Joukowsky ellipse itself is a
standard conformal object in this setting
\citep{Landkof1972,Pommerenke1992,Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,SaffTotik1997,StahlTotik1992}.
Classical Faber-polynomial theory begins with Faber's polynomial
expansions and the later Faber-series literature
\citep{Faber1903,Curtiss1971,Suetin1998}.  For ellipses and rational exterior
maps, \(J_r\) enters the Faber/Chebyshev relation and its rational-map
generalizations \citep{Liesen2001}; for Joukowski airfoils, recent
work studies zero distributions of Faber polynomials and electrostatic
skeletons \citep{LevenbergWielonsky2020}.  These
Faber works are adjacent to the present paper because they use the
same exterior map or nearby Joukowsky geometries, but they do not
address the lower-truncated boundary energy, finite-Blaschke
Haar-lift equality class, or exact transport inverse chain studied
below.

The finite Blaschke facts used here are also classical at the level of
boundary covering and Poisson-kernel derivative formulas; modern
accounts and surveys place these maps in a broader complex-analytic,
operator-theoretic, and interpolation context
\citep{Colwell1985,GarciaMashreghiRoss2017,GarciaMashreghiRoss2018}.
Recent adjacent work on rational inner functions develops Clark
measures, Dirichlet-type norms, stable-polynomial numerators, product
domain boundary behavior, and several-variable analogues of finite
Blaschke products
\citep{FelderLe2022,BickelCimaSola2023,Bergqvist2023,AndersonBergqvistBickelCimaSola2024,Jacobsson2024ClarkProduct,Knese2024RationalInnerSurvey,BickelKnesePascoeSola2025}.
In particular, recent Clark-measure work for product functions,
compositions of inner functions, and multiplicative embeddings on
polydiscs \citep{Jacobsson2024ClarkProduct} is the closest current
measure-theoretic context for comparing finite-cover fibers under
products or compositions.
Those papers are close in boundary function theory and finite-cover
language, but they address rational-inner structure rather than the
one-variable Joukowsky lower-truncated energy factorization used here.  Our
finite-cover contribution is not a new theory of Blaschke products. It
is the specialization of the Joukowsky boundary energy after the
single pushforward \(B_\ast\mu\), with equality exactly for the full
Haar-lift fiber \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\), and with the canonical
absolutely continuous member's energy written in the normalization used by
the rest of the paper.

Capacity computation is a separate adjacent literature.  Numerical
methods based on lemniscatic-domain conformal maps, log-lightning
approximations, and charge simulation now compute logarithmic
capacities for broad classes of compact sets
\citep{LiesenSeteNasser2017,BaddooTrefethen2021,LiesenNasserSete2023}.
Recent extremal and shape-optimization questions for logarithmic or
Riesz capacities study how capacity changes under linear images and
under capacity-ratio optimization \citep{Laugesen2022,ClarkLaugesen2025}.
Those works are computational and general-purpose; here the capacity
formula \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\) is the classical Joukowsky
normalization, used as one coordinate in an exact compatibility chain.
Similarly, Mahler measure enters through Jensen's formula and the
standard one- and several-variable Mahler literature
\citep{Mahler1962,Smyth1981,Boyd1981,EverestWard1999,BrunaultZudilin2020,McKeeSmyth2021}.
Recent work relating Mahler measures of exact polynomials to
special \(L\)-values of \(K3\) surfaces \citep{Trieu2024K3Mahler}
is adjacent current context for Mahler-measure methods, but it concerns
motivic and \(K3\)-surface structures rather than the one-variable
Joukowsky boundary skeleton or the fixed-scale transport inverse used
here.  The stable-polynomial context is background for unit-circle zero
conditions and transport
\citep{RahmanSchmeisser2002,BorceaBranden2009,LeeYang1952I,LeeYang1952II}.
The elimination-theoretic use of resultants and discriminants is likewise
standard: the paper fixes the Sylvester normalization and then applies
one-variable specialization identities, rather than developing the
general multivariate resultant formalism
\citep{Jouanolou1991,GKZ1994,CoxLittleOShea2005}.

The one-scale inverse fiber also sits next to the classical and modern
phase-retrieval and spectral-factorization ambiguity literature.  In
one-dimensional Fourier phase retrieval, equality of the autocorrelation
or Fourier magnitude determines a polynomial or entire function only up
to choices among zero pairs reflected by the reciprocal-conjugate
operation, together with the usual translation, modulation, and
conjugate-reflection symmetries.  This zero-flipping viewpoint goes back
to the one-dimensional classifications of Akutowicz, Walther, and
Hofstetter and was developed further in ambiguity classifications for
Fourier-magnitude reconstruction
\citep{Akutowicz1956,Walther1963,Hofstetter1964,BruckSodin1979,BeinertPlonka2015,BeinertPlonka2018,Edidin2019}.
Stability and ill-conditioning issues for phase retrieval, including the
effect of flipping zeros across the Fourier-reflection partner, are also
well studied \citep{GrohsKoppensteinerRathmair2020,JamingKellayPerez2022}.
Spectral factorization has the same structural ambiguity: a nonnegative
trigonometric polynomial or finite autocorrelation fixes reciprocal
zero pairs, while stable or minimum-phase conventions choose one member
from each pair \citep{OppenheimSchafer2010}.

The involution \(z\mapsto r^{-2}/z\) in Lemma
\ref{lem:one-scale-orbit-fiber-matching} is therefore not presented as
a new abstract zero-flipping phenomenon.  The difference is the exact
normalization of the flip.  Classical Fourier phase retrieval reflects a
zero through the unit circle, with conjugation in the usual
finite-signal normalization, because the known object is an
autocorrelation or boundary modulus.  Here the partner of \(z\) is the
unconjugated scaled reciprocal \(r^{-2}/z\), forced by the algebraic
identity
\(r^nz^nQ_r(J_r(z))=P(z)P^\vee(r^2z)\) for the fixed Joukowsky map
\(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\).  Thus the ambiguity is not a modulus-only
or noisy phase-retrieval stability statement; it is an exact divisor
choice inside the two-shell transport polynomial, with a product
resonance condition in that normalization.  The paper-specific increment
is this Joukowsky-transport normalization and the two-scale matching
graph that compares differently scaled reciprocal choices.

The endpoint theorem uses only circle equilibrium uniqueness, reflection
symmetrization, and Parseval's identity; its increment is the complete
collapsed equality fiber and the sharp reopening functional
\(\frac12\|h\|_2^2\).  The theorem-by-theorem boundary for the elliptic
\(r>1\) package is recorded in
Table~\ref{tab:theorem-package-novelty-boundary}.  The level column is
deliberately conservative: \textbf{LOW} means a standard theorem is being
specialized or normalized for this paper's notation, while
\textbf{MEDIUM} means that the paper adds a fixed-\(J_r\) recombination,
matching rule, or stability statement after classical inputs have already
been invoked.  No row claims a general replacement for potential theory,
Faber-polynomial asymptotics, finite-Blaschke theory, capacity algorithms,
Mahler-measure theory, phase retrieval, spectral factorization, or
inverse-polynomial theory.

All inverse assertions below use the exact algebraic data specified in
their hypotheses.  In particular, the \(n+1\)-scale multiscale theorem
contains a sharpness statement only for Laurent coefficient interpolation:
it supplies a sharp interpolation budget for the coefficient-vector
proof, not a source-recovery lower bound for every algebraically
constrained source class.

Section \ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation and proves the algebraic
pullback and discriminant factorizations.  Section
\ref{sec:rational-capacity} contains the degree-two Joukowsky boundary
theorem, with the general lower-truncated rational pullback-kernel
interface deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:auxiliary-rational-pullbacks},
the sharp equilibrium theorem, and finite Blaschke covers.  Section
\ref{sec:mahler-transport} proves the transported Jensen--Mahler
identities.  Section \ref{sec:discriminant} treats discriminants,
resultants, one-scale shell recognition from an exact complete
coefficient vector, the two-scale matching inverse from exact complete
coefficient vectors, the multiscale exact complete-coefficient-vector
inverse theorem, local annular Rouch\'e zero-count selection, and
the fixed-scale synthesis.  Sections
\ref{sec:examples} and \ref{sec:discussion} record normalizations and
summarize the complex-variable consequences.

The paper proves a fixed-scale theorem package.  The only capacity input
beyond standard logarithmic potential theory is the degree-two Joukowsky
boundary factorization
\[
J_r(z)-J_r(t)=(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right).
\]
Since the partner locus \(zt=r^{-2}\) misses \(\TT^2\), the boundary
energy reduces to the circle logarithmic kernel plus a nonsingular
Fourier-explicit term.  The finite Blaschke, Mahler transport, inverse,
and Rouch\'e statements all use this same \(r\).  The inverse statements
keep the data model of Definition
\ref{def:transport-coefficient-observations}.
