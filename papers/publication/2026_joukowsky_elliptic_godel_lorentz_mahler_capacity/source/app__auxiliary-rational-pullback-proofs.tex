\section{Auxiliary Rational-Pullback Proofs}\label{app:auxiliary-rational-pullbacks}

This appendix contains the routine proof infrastructure for the auxiliary rational-pullback interface in Section~\ref{subsec:auxiliary-rational-pullbacks}.  These arguments are used only to justify the lower-truncated kernel notation and the diagonal, branch, and equal-value collision bookkeeping before the degree-two Joukowsky specialization.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:nonpolar-rational-image}]
Compactness follows from continuity of \(R\) on \(K\).  Suppose, for a contradiction, that \(R(K)\) is polar.  By the subharmonic characterization of polar sets, in the form complementary to Cartan's theorem \citep[Thm.~3.5.1, p.~65]{Ransford1995}, there is a subharmonic function \(u\not\equiv -\infty\) on a neighbourhood \(V\) of \(R(K)\) such that \(u=-\infty\) on \(R(K)\).  Since \(R\) has no pole on \(K\) and is continuous there, choose an open neighbourhood \(U\) of \(K\), with no component on which \(R\) is constant after discarding components disjoint from \(K\) if necessary, such that \(R\) is holomorphic on \(U\) and \(R(U)\subset V\).  The composition \(u\circ R\) is subharmonic on \(U\) unless it is identically \(-\infty\) on a connected component.  On no component meeting \(K\) can it be identically \(-\infty\): otherwise the open mapping theorem would force \(u\equiv -\infty\) on a nonempty open subset of the domain of \(u\), hence everywhere on the relevant component, contrary to the choice of \(u\).  Thus \(u\circ R\) is a genuine subharmonic function on a neighbourhood of \(K\) and equals \(-\infty\) on \(K\).  This makes \(K\) polar, contradicting the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:extended-pullback-energy-equals-pushforward-energy}]
For \(M>0\), put \(h_M(u,v)=\max\{\log|u-v|,-M\}\).  This is a bounded Borel function on the compact set \(F(X)\times F(X)\), with the value \(-M\) on the diagonal.  Since \((F\times F)_\ast(\mu\times\mu)=\nu\times\nu\),
\[
\iint_{X\times X} h_M(F(x),F(y))\,\dd\mu(x)\dd\mu(y)
=
\iint_{F(X)\times F(X)}h_M(u,v)\,\dd\nu(u)\dd\nu(v).
\]
As \(M\to\infty\), the kernels \(h_M\) decrease pointwise to the extended logarithmic kernel \(\log|u-v|\), with value \(-\infty\) on the diagonal.  The image \(F(X)\) is compact, so these kernels are bounded above uniformly.  Applying monotone convergence to a fixed upper bound minus the two sides gives equality of the lower-truncated limits.  The final equivalence is the same identity read in the extended real line.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:compact-fiber-borel-selection}]
This is the compact-valued measurable-selection theorem, equivalently the closed-section uniformization theorem for standard Borel spaces \citep[Theorem~18.13]{Kechris1995}.  For completeness we recall why the hypotheses match that theorem.  Compact metric spaces are Polish and carry their standard Borel structures.  Since \(G\) is closed in \(X\times Y\), its graph is Borel, and for every closed set \(F\subset Y\) the set
\[
\{x\in X:G_x\cap F\ne\varnothing\}
\]
is the projection of the compact set \(G\cap(X\times F)\), hence compact and therefore Borel.  Thus \(x\mapsto G_x\) is a Borel multifunction with nonempty compact values.  The measurable-selection theorem gives a Borel selector \(s:X\to Y\) with \(s(x)\in G_x\) for every \(x\in X\), which is precisely \((x,s(x))\in G\).  Since \(s\) is Borel between standard Borel spaces, the image measure \(s_\ast\nu\) is defined on the Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra of \(Y\) and is a probability measure whenever \(\nu\) is one.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:lower-truncated-bounded-term-separation}]
Let \(C=\|b\|_\infty\).  For \(N>C\),
\[
L_{N+C}-C\le (L+b)_N\le L_{N-C}+C.
\]
If the lower-truncated integral of \(L\) is \(-\infty\), the right-hand side tends to \(-\infty\) after integration, so the lower-truncated integral of \(L+b\) is also \(-\infty\).  Suppose therefore that the lower-truncated integral of \(L\) is finite.  Since \(L\) is upper bounded, this implies that the negative part of \(L\) is integrable and that \(L> -\infty\) for \(\mu\times\mu\)-almost every point.  The truncation map \(T_N(s)=\max\{s,-N\}\) is one-Lipschitz, hence
\[
\left|(L+b)_N-L_N\right|=\left|T_N(L+b)-T_N(L)\right|\le C.
\]
For almost every point, \(T_N(L+b)-T_N(L)\to b\).  Dominated convergence gives
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\iint\bigl((L+b)_N-L_N\bigr)\,\dd\mu\dd\mu
=\iint b\,\dd\mu\dd\mu,
\]
which is the asserted identity in the finite case.  Since \(b\) is bounded, the same argument also proves that adding \(b\) cannot turn a finite lower-truncated integral into \(-\infty\), or remove a \(-\infty\) singular-energy contribution.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rational-divided-difference-kernel}]
The polynomial \(\Phi_R\) vanishes identically on the diagonal \(z=t\).  Dividing \(\Phi_R\) by \(z-t\), viewed as a monic polynomial in \(z\) with coefficients in \(\CC[t]\), leaves a remainder depending only on \(t\); evaluating at \(z=t\) shows that this remainder is zero.  Hence \(\Psi_R\in\CC[z,t]\).  Taking the limit as \(t\to z\), equivalently differentiating \(\Phi_R(z,t)\) in the second variable and accounting for the denominator \(z-t\), gives
\[
\Psi_R(z,z)=A'(z)B(z)-A(z)B'(z).
\]
For \(z\ne t\) with \(B(z)B(t)\ne0\),
\[
R(z)-R(t)=\frac{A(z)B(t)-A(t)B(z)}{B(z)B(t)}
=\frac{(z-t)\Psi_R(z,t)}{B(z)B(t)}.
\]
Taking logarithmic absolute values gives the asserted identity away from the collision set.  On the collision set the factor \((z-t)\Psi_R(z,t)\) vanishes and the logarithmic kernel is \(-\infty\); the two terms involving \(B\) remain bounded and continuous on \(K\), because \(B\) has no zeros there.  Thus the equality holds for the single lower-truncated extended kernel, and Lemma \ref{lem:lower-truncated-bounded-term-separation} is exactly the permitted separation of the bounded pole terms from the singular collision terms.  Applying Lemma \ref{lem:extended-pullback-energy-equals-pushforward-energy} to the continuous map \(R:K\to R(K)\) proves the final equivalence of finite energies.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:general-rational-image-capacity-pullback}]
The image \(R(K)\) is compact and nonpolar by Lemma \ref{lem:nonpolar-rational-image}.  Hence its equilibrium measure \(\nu_{R(K)}\) exists and satisfies
\[
\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))
=\max_{\nu\in\mathcal P(R(K))}\iint\log|u-v|\,\dd\nu(u)\dd\nu(v),
\]
with the logarithmic integral understood by lower truncation.  For each \(\mu\in\mathcal P(K)\), Lemmas \ref{lem:rational-divided-difference-kernel} and \ref{lem:extended-pullback-energy-equals-pushforward-energy} give \(\mathcal E_R(\mu)=I(R_\ast\mu)\).  Therefore \(\mathcal E_R(\mu)\le\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))\) for every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(K)\), with equality only when \(R_\ast\mu=\nu_{R(K)}\).

Conversely, the closed set
\[
\{(u,z)\in R(K)\times K:R(z)=u\}
\]
has nonempty compact vertical sections over \(R(K)\).  Lemma \ref{lem:compact-fiber-borel-selection} gives a standard-Borel selector \(s:R(K)\to K\) with \(R(s(u))=u\).  Put \(\mu=s_\ast\nu_{R(K)}\).  Then \(R_\ast\mu=(R\circ s)_\ast\nu_{R(K)}=\nu_{R(K)}\), so Lemma \ref{lem:rational-divided-difference-kernel} makes the selected lift admissible with finite pullback energy and \(\mathcal E_R(\mu)=\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))\).  The same lemma applies to every lift whose pushforward is \(\nu_{R(K)}\), proving the variational formula and the characterization of all maximizers.

The branch and collision interpretation follows from the algebraic identity
\[
R(z)-R(t)=\frac{(z-t)\Psi_R(z,t)}{B(z)B(t)}
\]
on \(K\times K\), from \(B\ne0\) on \(K\), and from
\[
R'(z)=\frac{\Psi_R(z,z)}{B(z)^2}.
\]
Thus \(z=t\) gives the ordinary diagonal collision, diagonal zeros of \(\Psi_R\) are precisely finite critical points, and off-diagonal zeros of \(\Psi_R\) are precisely distinct equal-value collisions.  The local normal forms in the final sentence are the three alternatives of Lemma \ref{lem:rational-local-collision-classification}.  Since finite-energy of \(R_\ast\mu\) is equivalent to \(\mathcal E_R(\mu)>-\infty\), any admissible lift assigns zero \(\mu\times\mu\)-mass to every subset on which the displayed pullback kernel is \(-\infty\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-collision-skeleton-rational-pullback}]
Fix \(z\in K\).  Since \(B(z)\ne0\), the equation \(R(t)=R(z)\) on points with \(B(t)\ne0\) is equivalent to
\[
F_z(t):=A(z)B(t)-A(t)B(z)=0.
\]
The polynomial \(F_z\) has degree at most \(d\).  It is not identically zero, for otherwise \(A/B\) would agree with the constant value \(A(z)/B(z)\) away from the finitely many zeros of \(B\), and hence would be constant, contrary to the hypothesis.  Therefore its zero set is finite and has total multiplicity at most \(d\).

Near \(z\), the denominator is nonzero and
\[
F_z(t)=B(z)B(t)\bigl(R(z)-R(t)\bigr).
\]
If \(e_z\) is the local degree of \(R\) at \(z\), then
\[
R(t)-R(z)=(t-z)^{e_z}U(t),\qquad U(z)\ne0,
\]
so \(t=z\) is a zero of \(F_z\) of multiplicity \(e_z\).  The remaining roots, counted with multiplicity, account for all off-diagonal equal-value collisions and have total multiplicity at most \(d-e_z\).  Dividing by \(z-t\) gives the stated description in terms of \(\Psi_R\), with the diagonal retaining precisely the ramification multiplicity beyond the ordinary logarithmic collision.  The final zero-mass assertion is the last assertion of Proposition \ref{prop:general-rational-image-capacity-pullback} applied to this finite-degree skeleton.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rational-local-collision-classification}]
Because \(B(z_0)\ne0\), the rational map is holomorphic near \(z_0\).  If \(z_0=t_0\), choose a local coordinate \(u=z-z_0\) and write
\[
R(z_0+u)=R(z_0)+u^e U_0(u)
\]
where \(e\ge1\) is the local degree and \(U_0(0)\ne0\).  For two nearby points \(z=z_0+u\) and \(t=z_0+v\),
\[
R(z)-R(t)=u^eU_0(u)-v^eU_0(v).
\]
This holomorphic function vanishes on the diagonal \(u=v\), hence is divisible by \(u-v\).  When \(e=1\), the quotient has value \(U_0(0)\ne0\), giving the noncritical case.  When \(e\ge2\), the quotient \(H(u,v):=(R(z_0+u)-R(z_0+v))/(u-v)\) has initial homogeneous part
\[
U_0(0)\bigl(u^{e-1}+u^{e-2}v+\cdots+v^{e-1}\bigr),
\]
which is nonzero as a polynomial and records the ramification beyond the diagonal factor.  The identity
\[
R'(z)=\frac{A'(z)B(z)-A(z)B'(z)}{B(z)^2}=\frac{\Psi_R(z,z)}{B(z)^2}
\]
shows that the diagonal zeros of \(\Psi_R\) are exactly the finite critical points, and their images under \(R\) are the finite branch values.  Finally, if \(z_0\ne t_0\), then \(z-t\) and \(B(z)B(t)\) are nonzero near \((z_0,t_0)\), so
\[
R(z)-R(t)=\frac{(z-t)\Psi_R(z,t)}{B(z)B(t)}
\]
vanishes at \((z_0,t_0)\) exactly when \(\Psi_R(z_0,t_0)=0\).  This proves the off-diagonal alternative and completes the classification.
\end{proof}
