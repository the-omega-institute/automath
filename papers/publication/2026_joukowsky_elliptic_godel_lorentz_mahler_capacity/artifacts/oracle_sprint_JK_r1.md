(1) Is there a paper inside this one?
Yes. There is one short paper inside it. The 90-page manuscript itself is not that paper.
As assembled, this is much closer to a collection of exercises and normalization checks around one exceptionally tractable map than to a monograph-style contribution. “Exercises” is not meant to imply incorrectness or triviality. It means that most of the manuscript takes classical tools—Frostman extremality, circle Fourier expansions, finite Blaschke covering, Jensen’s formula, resultants, reciprocal zero flipping, Laurent interpolation, and Rouché counting—and works out their exact Joukowsky specialization. The novelty table is substantially honest about this: the Fourier formula, Blaschke lift statement, two-scale matching, and local Rouché selector are all presented as fixed-map recombinations of classical inputs, while the capacity, transport, and interpolation rows are explicitly LOW. 
It is not a monograph-style treatment whose principal value is coverage. A useful monograph chapter would organize and compare a significant body of external theory. This manuscript instead exhausts one model from many directions. Its appendices are described by the authors themselves as “routine rational-map infrastructure,” a “collected … record,” and “bookkeeping,” rather than additional mathematics.    That makes the document a research note surrounded by a defensive dossier, not a reference work that needs ninety pages.
The result inside it: Theorem 3.21
The one theorem I would extract is Theorem 3.21, “Collapsed-ellipse equilibrium fiber and opening selection.”
Strictly speaking, this theorem is not assigned a LOW or MEDIUM row in Table 1: the manuscript says that Table 1 concerns the elliptic r>1 package.  That omission is itself the authors’ most consequential misvaluation. None of the results explicitly rated MEDIUM in the table deserves promotion much beyond MEDIUM. Theorem 3.21 does.
What makes 3.21 different is not merely that another formula is exact. It identifies a genuine change in the structure of the extremal problem:


for r>1, Haar measure is the unique source maximizer;


at r=1, the equality set becomes an infinite-dimensional affine fiber determined by Sη=mT​;


that fiber is described completely by reflection-antisymmetric L∞ perturbations dη=(1+h)dmT​;


reopening with r=es produces an exact first-order selection functional, with limit
21​∥h∥L2(mT​)2​;


the finite-Blaschke version identifies exactly which members of the collapsed fiber remain maximizers for all r>1. 


That is a coherent bifurcation theorem: loss of injectivity, enlargement of the equality class, and quantitative selection when injectivity is restored. The other MEDIUM packages provide exact formulas or classifications within a fixed nondegenerate model. Theorem 3.21 explains what qualitatively changes at the singular boundary of the model.
The proof is elementary once the right statement has been found, but that is not an objection. The exact identity
I(J1∗​η)=2IT​(Sη)
turns equilibrium uniqueness on the circle into a complete equality classification, and the reopening law follows from a nontrivial interaction between the r>1 Fourier weights and Parseval.  The conceptual package is stronger than the amount of machinery needed to prove it.
I would rate it MEDIUM-HIGH for a focused specialist paper, not HIGH in the sense required by a strong general complex-analysis journal. The authors were wrong not because they discovered a major new theory, but because they gave their one genuinely tellable theorem essentially the same rhetorical status as the surrounding transport identities and normalization lemmas.
(2) Is the value coverage?
No. Coverage is not the publishable value here.
The manuscript covers one Joukowsky model exhaustively, but it does not survey a sufficiently broad theory. It does not compare alternative approaches to degenerating equilibrium problems, organize a literature on singular limits of conformal images, or provide a reusable framework covering a substantial class of rational maps. Indeed, it repeatedly limits its general rational-map material to an auxiliary interface and insists that the actual conclusions arise only after the degree-two Joukowsky specialization. 
So I would not reformat this as a survey, lecture-note series, or expository article. A survey cannot be created merely by changing the label on a long research manuscript. It needs breadth, comparative judgment, historical organization, and a reason for readers working beyond this one map to consult it. This manuscript has depth of bookkeeping, not breadth of coverage.
A chapter in a very narrowly themed proceedings volume might physically accommodate it, but that would be placement by invitation rather than a convincing publication strategy. It would not cure the disproportion between the amount of text and the mathematical payload.
(3) What should happen to it?
Extract a specific short paper centered on Theorem 3.21.
The paper should be approximately 15–18 pages, including references. A viable structure is:


a two-page introduction stating the unique-maximizer regime for r>1, the collapsed equality fiber at r=1, and the reopening-selection question;


the minimum Joukowsky boundary factorization and lower-truncated-energy conventions;


the r>1 Fourier deficit only in the form needed for the limit;


Theorem 3.21, divided into the collapsed identity, equality classification, Fourier description, and opening-selection law;


the finite-Blaschke endpoint corollary;


one example illustrating the extreme value 21​ and one paragraph explaining why the phenomenon is a singular-limit effect rather than merely the noninjectivity of J1​.


The theorem already occupies only a few pages in the present manuscript, including its proof.  The additional length should be spent on motivation, prior-art positioning, and explaining the bifurcation—not on reproducing the rational-pullback appendix, transport polynomial theory, resultant identities, multiscale interpolation, or endpoint bookkeeping.
My rough acceptance probability for that extracted paper is about 50% at a properly chosen specialist journal in complex analysis or potential theory. I would put it below 15% at the kind of strong journal that rejected the ninety-page version. The result is elegant and exact, but it remains a theorem about a very special degenerating family, proved largely by Fourier decomposition and classical equilibrium uniqueness.
I am not recommending the next option up—splitting the manuscript into two or more research papers—because there is no convincing second paper. The most plausible second candidate would be the algebraic inverse package around Theorem 5.9. But that theorem assumes exact complete coefficient vectors and exact factorization into root divisors, and then classifies compatible sources by a perfect-matching graph obtained from solving the two-scale linear equations.  It is neat and correct-looking, but the authors’ MEDIUM rating is fair. It does not have enough conceptual or technical distance from reciprocal zero flipping and exact root matching to carry an independent substantial article.
Nor does Theorem 5.23 provide a second paper: the manuscript correctly restricts it to a contour-selected zero count after the exact model and contours have already been fixed, and expressly denies both unit-circle realizability and global inverse stability.  That is a useful lemma for an application, not a separate research contribution.
So the decision is:
Extract Theorem 3.21 into a 15–18 page paper and withdraw the 90-page submission. Do not polish the present manuscript further. Its central publication problem is not exposition; it is that eighty or more pages are serving results that should become background, remarks, or omitted calculations around one short theorem.
