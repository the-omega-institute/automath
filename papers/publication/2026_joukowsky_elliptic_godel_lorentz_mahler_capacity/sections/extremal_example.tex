\section{The sharp endpoint and the singular limit}\label{sec:example}

The upper endpoint in \eqref{eq:selection-range} is attained by a
measure that chooses one representative from almost every reflected
pair.  This makes both the geometry of the collapsed fiber and the
normalization \(1/2\) transparent.

\begin{example}[An extremal semicircle source]\label{ex:semicircle-source}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sign-density}
 h(e^{i\theta})=\operatorname{sgn}(\sin\theta),
 \qquad
 \dd\eta=(1+h)\dd m_{\TT}.
\end{equation}
Thus \(\eta\) has density \(2\) on the open upper semicircle and density
\(0\) on the open lower semicircle.  Since
\(h(e^{-i\theta})=-h(e^{i\theta})\), Theorem
\ref{thm:collapsed-selection}\textup{(b)} shows that
\(\mathsf S\eta=m_{\TT}\).  Therefore
\[
 I(J_{1\ast}\eta)=0,
 \qquad
 J_{1\ast}\eta=J_{1\ast}m_{\TT}.
\]
Although the source uses only one semicircle, it still produces the
full arcsine equilibrium measure on the segment because
\(J_1(e^{i\theta})=J_1(e^{-i\theta})\).

The square of \(h\) is one almost everywhere, so the opening functional
immediately gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sign-extremal-value}
 \lim_{s\downarrow0}
 \frac{s-I(J_{e^s\ast}\eta)}{2s}
 =\frac12\int_{\TT}|h|^2\dd m_{\TT}
 =\frac12.
\end{equation}
This is the largest possible value.

The same value can be checked mode by mode.  In \(L^2(\TT)\),
\[
 \operatorname{sgn}(\sin\theta)
 =\frac4\pi\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{\sin((2j+1)\theta)}{2j+1}.
\]
With our Fourier convention,
\[
 \widehat\eta(2j+1)
 =-\frac{2i}{\pi(2j+1)},
 \qquad
 \widehat\eta(2j)=0\quad(j\geq1).
\]
Hence the exact selection identity becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sign-exact-selection}
 \frac{s-I(J_{e^s\ast}\eta)}{2s}
 =\frac4{\pi^2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{\varphi(2(2j+1)s)}{(2j+1)^2}.
\end{equation}
Letting \(s\downarrow0\) and using
\(\sum_{j\geq0}(2j+1)^{-2}=\pi^2/8\) again gives \(1/2\).
\end{example}

The effect is stronger than the statement that \(J_1\) is not
injective.  Noninjectivity explains why a source measure can move mass
inside each reflected pair without changing its collapsed image.  It
does not by itself determine how a nearby injective family compares
those sources.  That information comes from the singular change in the
kernel.  For the antisymmetric modes that span the equality fiber, the
noncollapsed deficit has weights
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vanishing-weights}
 \frac{1-r^{-2k}}{k}
 =\frac{1-e^{-2ks}}{k}
 =2s+O_k(s^2).
\end{equation}
All these weights vanish at the collapsed scale, but after division by
(2s) they converge to the same unit weight.  Their sum is exactly the
\(L^2\) mass of the hidden antisymmetric component.  Thus the selection
law records how the energy changes while injectivity is restored; it is
not a property of the degenerate quotient map alone.

The conclusion is a source-space bifurcation.  In the open regime,
Frostman uniqueness on \(E_r\) and injectivity of \(J_r|_{\TT}\) give a
single source maximizer.  At the endpoint, image equilibrium remains
unique but the source equality class expands to an
infinite-dimensional affine fiber.  Reopening the ellipse contracts
that fiber back to Haar measure and supplies the exact first-order cost
of every other collapsed maximizer.
