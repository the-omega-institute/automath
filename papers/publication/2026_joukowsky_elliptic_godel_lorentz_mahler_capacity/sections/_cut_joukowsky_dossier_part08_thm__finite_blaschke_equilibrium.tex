\begin{lemma}[Boundary covering formula for finite Blaschke products]\label{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula}
Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\ge1\).  Then \(B\) has no critical point on \(\TT\), the boundary map \(B:\TT\to\TT\) is an orientation-preserving degree-\(d\) covering, and for every continuous function \(f\) on \(\TT\),
\[
\int_{0}^{2\pi} f(B(e^{i\theta}))\,|B'(e^{i\theta})|\,\dd\theta
=d\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{it})\,\dd t.
\]
Consequently the probability measure
\[
\dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})=\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\,\dd\theta
\]
satisfies \(B_\ast\mu_B=\dd t/(2\pi)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
B(z)=\zeta\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{z-a_j}{1-\overline{a_j}z},
\qquad |\zeta|=1,
\qquad a_j\in\DD.
\]
The following boundary computation is the standard finite-Blaschke boundary argument and Poisson-kernel derivative formula \citep[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970}.  For \(z=e^{i\theta}\), differentiating a continuous boundary argument \(B(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\beta(\theta)}\) on any local arc gives
\[
\beta'(\theta)=\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{e^{i\theta}B'(e^{i\theta})}{B(e^{i\theta})}\right)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1-|a_j|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-a_j|^2}>0.
\]
Thus \(B'(e^{i\theta})\ne0\) on \(\TT\), and \(|B'(e^{i\theta})|=\beta'(\theta)\).  The argument increase over one full turn is \(\beta(2\pi)-\beta(0)=2\pi d\), so \(B:\TT\to\TT\) is an orientation-preserving covering of degree \(d\).  Partition \([0,2\pi]\) into the \(d\) inverse branches on which \(\beta\) maps the branch interval bijectively onto an interval of length \(2\pi\).  On each branch the substitution \(t=\beta(\theta)\) gives one copy of \(\int_0^{2\pi}f(e^{it})\,\dd t\).  Summing the \(d\) branches proves the change-of-variables formula, and division by \(2\pi d\) gives the asserted pushforward identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite Blaschke Haar-lift fiber]\label{lem:finite-blaschke-haar-lift-fiber}
Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\ge1\), and let
\[
m_{\TT}:=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}
\]
denote normalized Haar measure on \(\TT\).  A \emph{Haar lift through
\(B\)} means any probability measure \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) satisfying
\[
B_\ast\mu=m_{\TT}.
\]
Thus ``Haar lift'' names the whole pushforward fiber over \(m_{\TT}\), not
a distinguished source measure.  The whole fiber has the following
disintegration description.  A probability measure \(\mu\) is a Haar lift
through \(B\) if and only if there is a measurable probability kernel
\[
u\longmapsto \kappa_u
\qquad (u\in\TT)
\]
such that \(\kappa_u\) is supported on the finite fiber \(B^{-1}\{u\}\) for
\(m_{\TT}\)-almost every \(u\), and
\[
\int_{\TT} f(z)\,\dd\mu(z)
=\int_{\TT}\left(\int_{B^{-1}\{u\}} f(z)\,\dd\kappa_u(z)\right)\dd m_{\TT}(u)
\]
for every continuous \(f\) on \(\TT\).  Since each boundary fiber has
\(d\) points, this is equivalently a measurable choice of probability
weights on the points of \(B^{-1}\{u\}\).  This disintegrated pushforward
fiber is the full Haar-lift fiber.

For every \(r>1\), the maximizers of
\[
\mu\longmapsto I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)
\]
over \(\mathcal P(\TT)\), with logarithmic energy interpreted by lower
truncation, are precisely this full Haar-lift fiber.

The absolutely continuous measure
\[
\boxed{\;
\dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})
=\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\,\dd\theta
\;}
\]
is the canonical absolutely continuous member of the Haar-lift fiber.  It
is the explicit member corresponding in the preceding disintegration to the
uniform conditional weights
\[
\kappa_u=\frac1d\sum_{z\in B^{-1}\{u\}}\delta_z
\qquad\text{for }m_{\TT}\text{-almost every }u.
\]
It is only one member of the Haar-lift fiber; when \(d>1\), the maximizing
source lift is not unique in general.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For any \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\), put \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\).  Then
\[
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}\eta.
\]
The all-measure Joukowsky equilibrium bound, Proposition
\ref{prop:extended-energy-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}, gives
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=I(J_{r\ast}\eta)\le \log r,
\]
with equality if and only if \(\eta=m_{\TT}\).  This is exactly the
condition \(B_\ast\mu=m_{\TT}\), proving that the maximizers are the full
Haar-lift fiber.
The boundary covering formula in Lemma
\ref{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula} gives
\[
B_\ast\mu_B=m_{\TT},
\]
so the displayed absolutely continuous measure is one Haar lift.  It is
canonical because it is obtained from the covering Jacobian \(|B'|/d\).
It remains to justify the fiber parametrization and identify this canonical
member inside it.  If
\(B_\ast\mu=m_{\TT}\), standard disintegration on the compact metric spaces
\(\TT\) and \(\TT\) gives a regular conditional probability
\(u\mapsto\kappa_u\) for \(\mu\) over the map \(B\), with
\(\kappa_u(B^{-1}\{u\})=1\) for \(m_{\TT}\)-almost every \(u\), and hence the
displayed integral formula.  Conversely, any measurable kernel supported on
the fibers and integrated against \(m_{\TT}\) defines a probability measure
\(\mu\) satisfying \(B_\ast\mu=m_{\TT}\), because for every continuous
\(g\) on \(\TT\),
\[
\int_{\TT}g(B(z))\,\dd\mu(z)
=\int_{\TT}g(u)\,\dd m_{\TT}(u).
\]
Finally, applying the covering formula to inverse branches gives, for every
continuous \(f\),
\[
\int_{\TT}f(z)\,\dd\mu_B(z)
=\int_{\TT}\frac1d\sum_{z\in B^{-1}\{u\}}f(z)\,\dd m_{\TT}(u),
\]
so \(\mu_B\) is exactly the uniform-kernel member of the disintegrated
Haar-lift fiber.  Non-uniform kernels, including measurable selections when
\(d>1\), give further, generally singular, Haar lifts; all of them are
maximizers because the maximizer condition is the pushforward condition
\(B_\ast\mu=m_{\TT}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite Blaschke--Joukowsky Fourier identity and equilibrium bound]\label{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover}
Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\ge1\), let \(r>1\), and set
\[
R_{B,r}(e^{i\theta})=J_r(B(e^{i\theta})).
\]
\emph{Finite-energy Fourier identity.}  For a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\TT\), put \(\eta:=B_\ast\mu\).  If \(\eta\) has finite logarithmic energy on \(\TT\), equivalently \(J_{r\ast}\eta=(R_{B,r})_\ast\mu\) has finite logarithmic energy on \(E_r\), then
\[
\log r-I((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu)
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
\frac{|\widehat\eta(k)|^2+r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\eta(k)^2)}{k}.
\]
This finite-energy hypothesis is on the pushed-forward circle measure
\(\eta=B_\ast\mu\), as prescribed by the standing conventions; outside
that class the displayed Fourier identity is not asserted.

\emph{All-measure equilibrium bound.}  For every probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\TT\), with \(\eta:=B_\ast\mu\), one has the extended-energy bound
\[
I((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu)
=I(J_{r\ast}\eta)
\le \log r,
\]
and equality holds if and only if \(\eta\) is Haar measure.  Thus the energy deficit and the sharp equilibrium bound factor only through the pushed-forward measure \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\), with the finite-energy/all-measure separation exactly as in Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}.  For this fixed finite Blaschke product \(B\), the equilibrium lifts are exactly the probability measures \(\mu\) satisfying
\[
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Equivalently, they are exactly the Haar lifts through \(B\) in the sense of
Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-haar-lift-fiber}.  Among them is the
canonical absolutely continuous member
\[
\boxed{\;
\dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})=\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\,\dd\theta.
\;}
\]
This canonical member is not a uniqueness assertion about the source lift.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity \((R_{B,r})_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu)=J_{r\ast}\eta\) reduces the all-measure maximal-energy statement directly to Proposition \ref{prop:extended-energy-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}.  When \(\eta\) has finite logarithmic energy, Theorem \ref{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity} gives the displayed Fourier deficit formula.  The same proposition shows that equality in the maximal-energy problem occurs exactly when \(\eta\) is Haar measure.  Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-haar-lift-fiber} gives the terminology and the canonical absolutely continuous member of this full lift fiber.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Parametrization of the Haar-lift fiber]\label{rem:haar-lift-fiber-parametrization}
Let \(m\) be normalized Haar measure on \(\TT\).  Since
\(B:\TT\to\TT\) is a degree-\(d\) covering, every
\(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) satisfying \(B_\ast\mu=m\) has a finite-fiber
disintegration
\[
\mu(E)=\int_{\TT}\kappa_u\bigl(E\cap B^{-1}\{u\}\bigr)\,\dd m(u),
\qquad E\subset\TT\ \text{Borel},
\]
where \(u\mapsto\kappa_u\) is a Borel probability kernel supported on the
finite fiber \(B^{-1}\{u\}\).  Conversely, every such kernel defines a
Haar lift through \(B\).  The canonical absolutely continuous lift is the
uniform-fiber member
\[
\kappa_u=\frac1d\sum_{B(z)=u}\delta_z,
\]
with fiber points counted once; the covering has no boundary ramification,
so each boundary fiber has exactly \(d\) points.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Canonical Blaschke lift source energy]\label{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy}
Let
\[
B(z)=\zeta\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{z-a_j}{1-\overline{a_j}z},
\qquad |\zeta|=1,
\qquad a_j\in\DD,
\]
and let \(\mu_B\) be the canonical lift from Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover}.  Then its source-circle logarithmic energy is
\[
\boxed{\;
I_{\TT}(\mu_B):=\iint_{\TT\times\TT}\log|z-t|\,\dd\mu_B(z)\dd\mu_B(t)
=\frac1{d^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\log|1-\overline{a_i}\,a_j|.
\;}
\]
Here and below the product in the logarithm is the explicitly conjugated factor
\(\overline{a_i}\,a_j\): the conjugation applies to \(a_i\) and not to \(a_j\).
For \(d=1\), this gives the normalization check
\[
I_{\TT}(\mu_{B_a})=\log|1-\overline a\,a|=\log(1-|a|^2).
\]
In particular \(I_{\TT}(\mu_B)\le0\), with equality if and only if \(a_1=\cdots=a_d=0\), equivalently \(B(z)=\zeta z^d\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the standard finite-Blaschke boundary derivative formula, used already in Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula} and classically recorded in \citet[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970}, the boundary derivative is the sum of the Poisson kernels of its zeros:
\[
\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{d}
=\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1-|a_j|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-a_j|^2}.
\]
With the convention \(\widehat\sigma(k)=\int_{\TT} z^{-k}\,\dd\sigma(z)\), equivalently \(z=e^{i\theta}\) in angular coordinates, the Poisson kernel has the classical Fourier expansion \citep[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970}
\[
\frac{1-|a|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-a|^2}=
1+2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{k\ge1}\overline a^{\,k}e^{ik\theta},
\]
so its \(k\)-th coefficient is \(\overline a^{\,k}\).  This fixes the conjugation convention used in the product formula: pairing the \(k\)-th coefficient with its complex conjugate gives
\[
\left(\frac1d\sum_i\overline{a_i}^{\,k}\right)
\overline{\left(\frac1d\sum_j\overline{a_j}^{\,k}\right)}
=\frac1{d^2}\sum_{i,j}(\overline{a_i}\,a_j)^k.
\]
The normalization is important: the density of \(\mu_B\) is \(|B'|/(2\pi d)\), so the displayed Poisson sum is divided by \(d\), and the circle logarithmic kernel is the unnormalized kernel \(\log|z-t|\) rather than \(-\log|z-t|\).  Consequently the sign below is the same as in Lemma \ref{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation}: there is no constant term and mode \(k\) contributes \(-|\widehat\sigma(k)|^2/k\).
Thus, for \(k\ge1\), the Fourier coefficients of \(\mu_B\) are
\[
\widehat\mu_B(k)=\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^{d}\overline{a_j}^{\,k}.
\]
The displayed boundary density is smooth on \(\TT\), hence \(\mu_B\) has finite circle logarithmic energy and Lemma \ref{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation} applies.  Using the circle-kernel expansion from that lemma,
\[
\log|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|
=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Re}(e^{ik(\theta-\phi)})}{k},
\]
we get
\[
I_{\TT}(\mu_B)=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat\mu_B(k)|^2}{k}
=-\frac1{d^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\overline{a_i}\,a_j)^k}{k}.
\]
Since \(|\overline{a_i}\,a_j|<1\), the inner series is \(-\log(1-\overline{a_i}\,a_j)\).  Taking real parts gives
\[
I_{\TT}(\mu_B)=\frac1{d^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\log|1-\overline{a_i}\,a_j|.
\]
The Fourier form also gives \(I_{\TT}(\mu_B)=-\sum_{k\ge1}|\widehat\mu_B(k)|^2/k\le0\).  Equality holds if and only if all positive Fourier coefficients vanish, equivalently \(\sum_j\overline{a_j}^{\ k}=0\) for every \(k\ge1\).  Newton's identities then force all elementary symmetric functions of \(a_1,\dots,a_d\) to vanish, so \(a_1=\cdots=a_d=0\).  Conversely, if all zeros are zero, then \(B(z)=\zeta z^d\), \(\mu_B\) is Haar measure, and the energy is \(0\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Collected Joukowsky Boundary Facts.}\label{par:joukowsky-capacity-package-dependencies}
For \(r>1\), the preceding results give the following
Joukowsky-boundary facts.  They are the capacity-theoretic input for the
fixed-scale transport statements later in the paper.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item The degree-two pullback has complete collision skeleton
  \[
  J_r(z)=J_r(t)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  z=t\quad\hbox{or}\quad zt=r^{-2},
  \]
  on \(\CC^\times\).  On \(\TT^2\), only the diagonal remains.

  \item The ellipse \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\) has
  \[
  \operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r,
  \qquad
  \nu_{E_r}=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right),
  \]
  and \(\nu_{E_r}\) is the unique equilibrium measure.

  \item For every finite-energy probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\TT\),
  \[
  \log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu)
  =\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
  \frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2+r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\mu(k)^2)}{k},
  \]
  with the sharp mode constants \(1-r^{-2k}\) and \(1+r^{-2k}\).  The finite-energy/all-measure separation is the one fixed in Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}: the unrestricted assertion is only the variational bound \(I(J_{r\ast}\eta)\le\log r\), with equality exactly for Haar measure.

  \item If \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product of positive degree, then the covered equilibrium problem depends only on \(B_\ast\mu\):
  \[
  I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=I(J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu)),
  \]
  and its maximizers are exactly the full Haar-lift fiber
  \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).  The canonical absolutely continuous
  member of this fiber has density \(|B'|/(2\pi\deg B)\) and source energy
  given by Proposition \ref{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy}.
\end{enumerate}
The auxiliary rational pullback results collected in Appendix~\ref{app:auxiliary-rational-pullbacks} supply only the lower-truncated kernel and collision terminology in \textup{(a)}.  Part \textup{(a)} is Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation} and Corollary \ref{cor:degree-two-boundary-operativity}; the capacity, unique equilibrium identification, and unrestricted extremal inequality in \textup{(b)} are Theorem \ref{thm:all-measure-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}, isolated again in Proposition \ref{prop:extended-energy-joukowsky-equilibrium-bound}; part \textup{(c)} is Theorem \ref{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity}, with the finite-energy equivalence supplied by Lemma \ref{lem:joukowsky-boundary-energy-equivalence}; and part \textup{(d)} is Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} together with Proposition \ref{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy}.  Thus the capacity scale, equilibrium lift, and boundary deficit used later are all the degree-two Joukowsky objects displayed above.
