\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Joukowsky family
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-joukowsky}
 J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},\qquad r\geq1,
\end{equation}
maps the unit circle \(\TT\) onto an ellipse \(E_r\).  For \(r>1\),
the restriction \(J_r:\TT\to E_r\) is one-to-one.  The ellipse has
logarithmic capacity \(r\), its equilibrium measure is the image of
normalized Haar measure \(m_{\TT}\), and the source variational problem
therefore has the unique maximizer \(m_{\TT}\).  These facts are a
particularly explicit instance of the classical Frostman extremal
principle for logarithmic energy; we use the sign convention in which
the maximal energy of a compact set \(K\) is
\(\log\operatorname{cap}(K)\) \citep{Ransford1995,SaffTotik1997}.
In particular, the capacity and equilibrium measure in the open regime
are classical data, not new conclusions of this paper.

The endpoint \(r=1\) is qualitatively different.  The minor axis of
\(E_r\) vanishes, the ellipse becomes the segment \([-2,2]\), and
\[
 J_1(e^{i\theta})=2\cos\theta
\]
identifies \(e^{i\theta}\) with \(e^{-i\theta}\).  Consequently the
unique equilibrium measure on the image no longer has a unique source.
That observation alone, however, leaves two questions unanswered.
First, which probability measures on \(\TT\) push forward to the
equilibrium measure on the segment?  Second, once the ellipse is
reopened, how does the nondegenerate problem distinguish among these
collapsed maximizers?

\subsection*{What is known at the level of the image}

There are two standard ways to place the limit \(E_r\to[-2,2]\) in
potential theory.  First, equilibrium measure on a planar continuum is
harmonic measure at infinity for the unbounded complementary domain.
For simply connected domains, Carath\'eodory convergence is equivalent
to weak convergence of harmonic measures
\citep{BinderRojasYampolsky2019}.  Thus the usual domain-convergence
theory accounts for convergence of the image equilibrium measures to
the arcsine law.  Second, convergence of Green functions and of
logarithmic capacity under kernel or Hausdorff convergence has been
studied in considerably greater generality
\citep{KalmykovKovalev2019,KalmykovKovalev2022}.  The hypotheses in
such theorems are substantive: logarithmic capacity need not be
continuous for arbitrary Hausdorff-convergent sequences.  For the
present ellipses these issues are absent because the exterior map is
explicit and \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\).

This convergence theory concerns measures on the varying image sets.
It does not distinguish different measures on \(\TT\) that acquire the
same image when a boundary parametrization ceases to be injective.  In
our example the image measures have the unambiguous limit
\[
 J_{r\ast}m_{\TT}\ \longrightarrow\ J_{1\ast}m_{\TT},
\]
whereas the source variational problem changes from one maximizer to
an infinite-dimensional equality class.  Weak convergence of harmonic
measure therefore describes the zeroth-order image limit but does not
see the source fiber or assign a cost to its members.

The underlying conformal picture also belongs to a classical
literature on variable regions and boundary behavior.  Quantitative
variation of conformal maps goes back at least to
\citet{Warschawski1950}, while kernel convergence and boundary limits
are part of the standard theory summarized by
\citet{Pommerenke1992}.  Those results compare normalized conformal
maps or their image domains.  Here the exterior conformal description
remains regular in the limit, but the chosen boundary source map
\(J_r|_{\TT}\) changes its multiplicity at \(r=1\).  That loss of
boundary injectivity is the singular event relevant to the present
variational problem.

There is also Joukowsky-specific work nearby.  In a different problem,
\citet{LevenbergWielonsky2020} determine weak limits of zero-counting
measures for Faber polynomials of Joukowski airfoils and relate those
limits, by balayage, to equilibrium measure and electrostatic
skeletons.  This demonstrates that singular Joukowsky geometry can
produce nontrivial limiting measures.  Its varying objects are Faber
zeros for a fixed airfoil, however, rather than probability measures
on a boundary source circle, and it does not study the collapse of an
ellipse to a segment or a reopening penalty.

The classical conclusions available before the present result may
therefore be summarized as follows.  The equilibrium measure on each
image is unique; its expression as harmonic measure is transported by
the conformal boundary map; the ellipse capacities and their limit are
explicit; and at the endpoint the image measure is the arcsine law.
Noninjectivity immediately suggests that the arcsine law has more than
one source lift.  What these facts do not give is the complete source
equality class, its affine parametrization, or the first nonvanishing
energy term that compares all of its members under reopening.

\subsection*{The bifurcation statement}

The point of this paper is that the answers form one bifurcation
statement.  Let \(\iota(z)=\overline z\) and write
\[
 \mathsf S\eta=\frac12(\eta+\iota_\ast\eta)
\]
for reflection symmetrization.  At the collapsed scale the exact energy
identity is
\[
 I(J_{1\ast}\eta)=2I_{\TT}(\mathsf S\eta).
\]
Circle equilibrium uniqueness then shows that equality in the segment
problem is equivalent to \(\mathsf S\eta=m_{\TT}\).  This equality
class is not merely described as a pushforward fiber: it is the explicit
infinite-dimensional affine family
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-fiber}
 \dd\eta=(1+h)\dd m_{\TT},\qquad
 h(\overline z)=-h(z),\qquad |h|\leq1\quad\text{a.e.}
\end{equation}
The symmetric component is fixed, while every admissible
antisymmetric component is invisible at \(r=1\).

To study selection, we use logarithmic scale and set \(r=e^s\).  For
every measure in \eqref{eq:intro-fiber}, the reopened energy satisfies
the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-selection-exact}
 \frac{s-I(J_{e^s\ast}\eta)}{2s}
 =\sum_{k\geq1}
 \frac{1-e^{-2ks}}{2ks}\,|\widehat\eta(k)|^2.
\end{equation}
The multiplier tends monotonically to one as \(s\downarrow0\), so
Parseval's identity gives the limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-selection-limit}
 \lim_{s\downarrow0}
 \frac{s-I(J_{e^s\ast}\eta)}{2s}
 =\frac12\int_{\TT}|h|^2\dd m_{\TT}.
\end{equation}
The limiting functional vanishes only at Haar measure and has sharp
maximum \(1/2\).  In this sense the nondegenerate family selects a
single point from the collapsed affine fiber.  The theorem records loss
of injectivity, enlargement of the equality class, and quantitative
selection when injectivity is restored.

The word ``selection'' is used here in this precise source-space
sense.  We do not assert a general compactness or
\(\Gamma\)-convergence theorem.  On the collapsed fiber, every
reflection-antisymmetric Fourier mode is invisible to the endpoint
energy.  Reopening gives its \(k\)-th mode the weight
\((1-e^{-2ks})/k\); division by \(2s\) makes all of these weights tend
to one.  The limiting quadratic form is consequently the
\(L^2(m_{\TT})\) norm of the hidden component.  This simultaneous
lifting of an infinite-dimensional null space is the additional datum
beyond convergence of capacities and equilibrium measures.  We are
not aware of an earlier result that records both a complete source
fiber and an exact first opening functional for a boundary
parametrization degenerating in this way.

\subsection*{Classical inputs and novelty boundary}

This is a deliberately elementary argument once the correct endpoint
statement is isolated.  The inputs used in the proof are Frostman
extremality, the Fourier expansion of the logarithmic kernel on the
circle, Parseval's identity, and the boundary covering formula for a
finite Blaschke product.  The latter belongs to the standard theory of
finite Blaschke products \citep{GarciaMashreghiRoss2018}.  Our result is
not a new general theory of degenerating conformal maps or inner
functions.  It is an exact theorem for the singular boundary of the
degree-two Joukowsky family.  The special factorization of that family
makes it possible to describe the whole equality fiber and its opening
penalty without an asymptotic remainder.

It is also useful to mark what is not used.  Jensen's formula,
resultants, reciprocal zero flipping, Laurent interpolation, and
Rouch\'e counting are classical tools that arise naturally in
polynomial-transport and inverse questions around the same Joukowsky
map.  Jensen's and Rouch\'e's theorems are standard one-variable
complex analysis \citep{Conway1973}; resultants are standard
elimination-theoretic infrastructure \citep{CoxLittleOShea1998}; and
reciprocal zero flipping has a long history in one-dimensional phase
reconstruction \citep{Akutowicz1956}.  Laurent interpolation in the
finite-dimensional transport setting is likewise linear-algebraic.
None of those five tools enters the endpoint theorem proved here.
Exact root matching and contour-selected zero counts likewise play no
role.  Keeping them out is mathematically important: the present
contribution is the change in the extremal problem at the singular
scale, not a collection of exact formulas for a fixed nondegenerate
model.

Accordingly, the claim is not that a difficult new analytic apparatus
is required.  Frostman extremality supplies uniqueness on each image,
the circle Fourier expansion diagonalizes the two Joukowsky factors,
and the finite Blaschke formula only transports Haar measure through a
classical covering.  The contribution is the statement assembled from
those inputs: loss of injectivity at \(r=1\), the resulting
infinite-dimensional affine equality fiber, and quantitative selection
of Haar measure when injectivity is restored.

The finite-Blaschke statement has the same focus.  If \(B\) is a finite
Blaschke product and \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\), then the covered energy depends
only on \(\eta\).  At \(r=1\), maximization requires only
\(\mathsf S\eta=m_{\TT}\); for every \(r>1\), it requires
\(\eta=m_{\TT}\).  Thus the corollary identifies exactly which members
of the enlarged endpoint fiber remain maximizers throughout the open
elliptic regime.  It uses the classical covering property of \(B\), not
new structure theory for Blaschke products.
