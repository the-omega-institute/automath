\section{Finite-Blaschke endpoint}\label{sec:blaschke-endpoint}

Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\geq1\), written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blaschke-product}
 B(z)=\zeta\prod_{j=1}^{d}
 \frac{z-a_j}{1-\overline{a_j}z},
 \qquad |\zeta|=1,\quad a_j\in\DD.
\end{equation}
The only fact about \(B\) needed here is its classical boundary covering
formula.  We include it to make the normalization of the endpoint
corollary explicit.

\begin{lemma}[Boundary covering formula]\label{lem:blaschke-covering}
The restriction \(B:\TT\to\TT\) is an orientation-preserving covering
of degree \(d\), with no critical point on \(\TT\).  For every continuous
\(f:\TT\to\CC\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blaschke-change-variables}
 \int_0^{2\pi}f(B(e^{i\theta}))|B'(e^{i\theta})|\dd\theta
 =d\int_0^{2\pi}f(e^{it})\dd t.
\end{equation}
Consequently the probability measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-haar-lift}
 \dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})
 =\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\dd\theta
\end{equation}
satisfies \(B_\ast\mu_B=m_{\TT}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a continuous local boundary argument
\(B(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\beta(\theta)}\).  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:blaschke-product} on \(\TT\) gives the Poisson-kernel formula
\[
 \beta'(\theta)
 =\sum_{j=1}^{d}
 \frac{1-|a_j|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-a_j|^2}>0.
\]
Thus (B') has no boundary zero,
\(|B'(e^{i\theta})|=\beta'(\theta)\), and the boundary argument
increases by \(2\pi d\) over one turn.  The map is therefore an
orientation-preserving degree-\(d\) covering.  Partitioning the circle
into its \(d\) inverse branches and substituting
\(t=\beta(\theta)\) on each branch proves
\eqref{eq:blaschke-change-variables}.  Formula
\eqref{eq:canonical-haar-lift} follows by normalization.
\end{proof}

A probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\TT\) satisfying
\(B_\ast\mu=m_{\TT}\) is called a Haar lift through \(B\).  There are
usually many such measures when \(d>1\): one may choose measurable
probability weights on each finite fiber \(B^{-1}\{u\}\) and integrate
those conditional measures against \(m_{\TT}(u)\).  The measure
\(\mu_B\) in \eqref{eq:canonical-haar-lift} is the canonical
absolutely continuous member obtained from uniform fiber weights.  The
covering and this lift-fiber interpretation are standard in finite
Blaschke theory \citep{GarciaMashreghiRoss2018}.

\begin{corollary}[Collapsed and persistent finite-cover maximizers]
\label{cor:finite-blaschke-endpoint}
Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of positive degree, let
\(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\), and set \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\).

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*]
\item At the collapsed scale,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covered-collapsed-bound}
 I((J_1\circ B)_\ast\mu)\leq0,
\end{equation}
and equality holds if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covered-collapsed-fiber}
 \mathsf S(B_\ast\mu)=m_{\TT}.
\end{equation}

\item If equality holds in \eqref{eq:covered-collapsed-bound} and
\(\dd\eta=(1+h)\dd m_{\TT}\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covered-opening-law}
 \lim_{s\downarrow0}
 \frac{s-I((J_{e^s}\circ B)_\ast\mu)}{2s}
 =\frac12\|h\|_{L^2(m_{\TT})}^2.
\end{equation}

\item A collapsed maximizer \(\mu\) remains a maximizer for every
\(r>1\) if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:persistent-haar-lift}
 B_\ast\mu=m_{\TT}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, among the measures \(\eta\) in the collapsed affine fiber,
the only pushed-forward source that persists as an elliptic maximizer is
\(m_{\TT}\); its full preimage under \(B_\ast\) is the Haar-lift fiber.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Functoriality of pushforward gives, for every \(r\geq1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covered-pushforward-factor}
 (J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu)=J_{r\ast}\eta.
\end{equation}
Part~\textup{(a)} is therefore Theorem
\ref{thm:collapsed-selection}\textup{(a)} applied to \(\eta\), and
part~\textup{(b)} is its opening-selection law.

For \(r>1\), Proposition~\ref{prop:unique-elliptic-maximizer} and
\eqref{eq:covered-pushforward-factor} give
\[
 I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)\leq\log r,
\]
with equality exactly when \(B_\ast\mu=m_{\TT}\).  Such measures exist
by Lemma~\ref{lem:blaschke-covering}; indeed \(\mu_B\) is one.  The
condition is independent of \(r\), so it characterizes the measures
that maximize at every noncollapsed scale.  This proves part
\textup{(c)}.
\end{proof}

The corollary contains no additional endpoint penalty hidden at the
level of the covering map.  All selection occurs after the single
pushforward \(\eta=B_\ast\mu\).  The nonuniqueness that remains for
\(d>1\) is exactly the classical freedom to distribute conditional mass
inside the finite fibers of \(B\).
