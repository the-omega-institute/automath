\begin{abstract}
We study Joukowsky maps as a complex-variable transport mechanism for logarithmic capacity, equilibrium energy, Mahler measure, and algebraic inverse reconstruction.  For a degree-two rational map \(R=A/B\), a compact nonpolar source set avoiding the poles, and the divided kernel \(\Psi_R(z,t)=(A(z)B(t)-A(t)B(z))/(z-t)\), we prove an exact variational formula for \(\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))\) over finite-energy lifts on \(K\), including the branch and collision singularities.  For \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\), we diagonalize the logarithmic energy of \(J_{r\ast}\mu\) by Fourier coefficients, obtain the sharp mode constants \(1\pm r^{-2k}\), and identify the unique equilibrium measure.  Finite Blaschke covers \(J_r\circ B\) inherit the same deficit through \(B_\ast\mu\); their canonical equilibrium lift has density \(|B'|/(2\pi\deg B)\) and an explicit zero-product source-energy formula.  Finally, the transport polynomial \(Q_r\) gives exact discriminant, Mahler, multiscale interpolation, one-scale resonance, Lee--Yang injectivity, and quantitative annular Rouch\'e reconstruction theorems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
The classical Joukowsky map provides one of the simplest conformal mechanisms by which circles generate ellipses and, more generally, confocal families in the plane. It therefore sits naturally at the intersection of conformal mapping, algebraic elimination, and logarithmic capacity theory \citep{Pommerenke1992,Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,GKZ1994}. The purpose of the present paper is to make this boundary object carry a complete potential-theoretic and Mahler-transport calculus: rational pullbacks give exact capacity formulae, Fourier diagonalization gives sharp equilibrium stability on the ellipse, finite Blaschke covers describe all equilibrium lifts, and polynomial elimination gives inverse reconstruction from transported data.  Arithmetic registers, hyperbolic matrices, and symbolic models appear only after this complex-variable spine has been fixed and only when they express invariants of the same boundary.

The dependence on classical material is deliberately separated from the new assertions.  We use the standard variational definition of logarithmic capacity, Jensen--Mahler identities, Fekete equilibrium facts, and elementary finite Blaschke product boundary theory as tools.  The new contribution is the transport package tying them together: the degree-two rational pullback kernel with explicit branch/collision singularities, the sharp Joukowsky Fourier deficit with its extremizers, the Blaschke-cover lift classification and canonical lift energy, and the algebraic inverse package combining multiscale Laurent interpolation, one-scale resonance fibers, unit-circle injectivity, and Rouch\'e-stable annular recovery.

Our first object is the \(m\)-fold elliptic projection
\[
J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})=e^{im\theta}+c\,e^{-im\theta},
\qquad c\in(0,1)\cap\QQ,\ \ m\in\NN,
\]
whose image is an ellipse traversed \(m\) times. The logarithmic speed defect of this normalized parametrized covering has an exceptionally sparse Fourier expansion, and that sparsity converts arithmetic information about \(c\) into linear asymptotics of \(p\)-adic valuations. The Fourier coefficients used here are therefore data of the chosen covering coordinate, not naive invariants of the unparametrized boundary curve. This places the problem in the general circle of ideas surrounding local valuations and Fourier rigidity in arithmetic analysis \citep{Gouvea1997,Koblitz1984,Serre1973}. We show that the full normalized Fourier family determines \(m\) and all prime valuations of \(c\), while finite congruence tests, at any fixed covering multiplicity and modulus, necessarily miss sufficiently deep \(p\)-adic perturbations.

Only after these boundary and transport data have been fixed do we introduce finitely supported multiplicative prime data. Passing to logarithmic coordinates turns the Joukowsky capacity scale into a concrete rapidity parameter in \(\mathrm{SO}^+(1,1)\), with the norm of a register determining the same confocal ellipse. This gives an exact capacity-calibrated bridge between multiplicative arithmetic, condition numbers, Einstein addition, and ellipse geometry \citep{Ungar2008}. The resulting primorial minimization principles are used in this article only in that calibrated form: among decomposable square-free codebooks with pairwise coprime images, the first \(n\) primes uniquely minimize the Joukowsky capacity product, the ellipse-area product, and the auxiliary normalized ellipsoid-volume character that keeps prime coordinates separated before taking determinants.  For a fixed ambient prime set, the corresponding minimizer is the set of the \(n\) smallest primes available in that ambient set, and it is the usual primorial codebook only when the ambient set contains the first \(n\) primes.

The third theme is potential-theoretic. For a polynomial \(P\) with nonzero constant term, the two-scale Joukowsky transform \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\) generates a transport polynomial \(Q_r\) whose pullback along the circle factors exactly as a product of \(P\) and its reciprocal polynomial. This identity leads to transported Jensen formulas on the ellipse, a shell-counting interpretation of the logarithmic-mean slope, a closed Bessel representation for the exponential moment generating function of the pushed-forward Haar measure, and a Lee--Yang Mahler law when the zeros of \(P\) lie on the unit circle \citep{LeeYang1952I,LeeYang1952II,Mahler1962,Smyth1981,Boyd1981,EverestWard1999,Watson1944,NISTDLMF}. At the level of elimination theory, the same factorization yields explicit discriminant formulas and an exact one-scale resultant reconstruction procedure under noiseless full-polynomial data \citep{GKZ1994}.


Thus the common complex-variable object throughout the paper is the boundary of a Joukowsky ellipse.  The Fourier and \(p\)-adic part studies this boundary through the normalized speed data of the covering \(z\mapsto z^m+c z^{-m}\); the multiplicative-data part studies the same confocal family through its capacity scale, area, and condition number; and the Mahler/resultant part studies how a polynomial divisor on the unit circle is transported to that boundary by \(J_r\).  The arithmetic quantities are therefore not separate decorations: they are exact consequences of the same ellipse boundary through explicitly stated Fourier-cover, capacity/Mahler, and elimination-theoretic data models.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem map for the Joukowsky transport spine]\label{thm:main-theorem-map}
The paper proves the following complex-variable transport package for the Joukowsky boundary.  Each clause is a pointer to the precise theorem below where its hypotheses, normalization, and proof are given.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item \textbf{Rational capacity.}  The degree-two rational image theorem, Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-rational-image-capacity}, gives the exact variational formula for \(\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))\) by the divided pullback kernel, including branch and collision singularities.
  \item \textbf{Sharp ellipse equilibrium.}  The Joukowsky stability theorem, Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability}, diagonalizes the logarithmic-energy deficit on \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), proves the sharp mode constants, identifies the unique equilibrium measure, and yields \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\).
  \item \textbf{Blaschke-cover lifts.}  The finite-cover theorem and canonical-lift computation, Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} and Proposition \ref{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy}, show that \(J_r\circ B\) factors the deficit through \(B_\ast\mu\), classify all equilibrium lifts, and compute the canonical source energy from the zeros of \(B\).
  \item \textbf{Mahler and inverse transport.}  The pullback, discriminant, Jensen--Mahler, fiber, Lee--Yang, and annular stability results are Theorems \ref{thm:pullback-factorization}, \ref{thm:discriminant-factorization}, \ref{thm:elliptic-two-scale-stokes-mean}, \ref{thm:transported-jensen-shell-count}, \ref{thm:leyang-mahler-transport}, \ref{thm:algebraic-mahler-transport-inverse-package}, \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction}, and \ref{thm:quantitative-annular-rouche-stability}, together with Corollaries \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection} and \ref{cor:coefficient-annular-rouche-stability}.
\end{enumerate}
The Fourier-cover and capacity-scaled prime-register sections are retained as calibrated applications of this boundary package, rather than as independent formal parts of the complex-variable core.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Clause \textup{(i)} is exactly Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-rational-image-capacity}.  Clause \textup{(ii)} is Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability}.  Clause \textup{(iii)} is Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} plus Proposition \ref{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy}.  Clause \textup{(iv)} follows by combining the cited algebraic transport identities, Mahler mean formulae, exact fiber theorem, Lee--Yang reconstruction theorem, and Rouch\'e stability theorem.  Thus the formal core is the potential-theoretic and Mahler/resultant theorem chain below, while the arithmetic sections use the same Joukowsky boundary scale only after that chain has been fixed.
\end{proof}

The capacity-scaled prime-register and primorial results in Section \ref{sec:godel-lorentz} are applications of the same Joukowsky capacity scale, not independent first-rank clauses of Theorem \ref{thm:main-theorem-map}.  They record how multiplicative codebooks are represented once the confocal scale has already been fixed by the complex-variable boundary theory.

\begin{theorem}[Compatibility of the Joukowsky boundary data]\label{thm:joukowsky-boundary-compatibility}
For \(L\ge 1\), put
\[
\Gamma_L:=\bigl\{\,L^{1/2}z+L^{-1/2}z^{-1}:|z|=1\,\bigr\}.
\]
The main data models of Theorem \ref{thm:main-theorem-map}, together with the capacity-scaled prime-register applications of Section \ref{sec:godel-lorentz}, are compatible with this single confocal boundary family in the following precise sense.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item If \(c\in(0,1)\) and \(m\in\NN\), then
  \[
  J_{c,m}(\TT)=c^{1/2}\Gamma_{c^{-1}}.
  \]
  The covering multiplicity \(m\) changes only the parametrization of this boundary, while the Fourier support and \(p\)-adic slopes in the Fourier-cover application section recover the same scale parameter through \(L=c^{-1}\).

  \item If \(s\in\mathsf P\) is a prime register, then the capacity-scaled ellipse used in Section \ref{sec:godel-lorentz} is
  \[
  E_J(s)=\Gamma_{N(s)}.
  \]
  Consequently its logarithmic capacity, area, Lorentz rapidity, and condition number are functions of the boundary scale \(L=N(s)\).

  \item If \(r>1\), then the transport ellipse in Theorem \ref{thm:main-theorem-map}\textup{(ii)--(iv)} is
  \[
  E_r=J_r(\TT)=\Gamma_{r^2}.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
Thus the spectral, Mahler, and elimination probes in the main theorem, and the capacity--Lorentz application in Section \ref{sec:godel-lorentz}, are not separate geometric inputs: after the explicit changes of scale
\[
L=c^{-1},\qquad L=N(s),\qquad L=r^2,
\]
they are probes of the same one-parameter Joukowsky boundary \(\Gamma_L\), up to the harmless homothety \(c^{1/2}\) in the elliptic-covering normalization.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(z=e^{i\phi}\),
\[
\Gamma_L=\bigl\{(L^{1/2}+L^{-1/2})\cos\phi+i(L^{1/2}-L^{-1/2})\sin\phi:\phi\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\bigr\},
\]
so \(\Gamma_L\) is exactly the confocal Joukowsky ellipse with focal distance \(2\).  For the elliptic covering, take \(L=c^{-1}\) and set \(z=e^{im\theta}\).  Then
\[
c^{1/2}\bigl(L^{1/2}z+L^{-1/2}z^{-1}\bigr)
=c^{1/2}\bigl(c^{-1/2}e^{im\theta}+c^{1/2}e^{-im\theta}\bigr)
=e^{im\theta}+c e^{-im\theta},
\]
which proves \(J_{c,m}(\TT)=c^{1/2}\Gamma_{c^{-1}}\).  Because \(\theta\mapsto e^{im\theta}\) is an \(m\)-fold covering of \(\TT\), the integer \(m\) affects only how the same ellipse is traversed; the scale is still determined by \(c\).

For a prime register \(s\), the definition in Theorem \ref{thm:capacity-scaled-prime-representation} is
\[
E_J(s)=\bigl\{N(s)^{1/2}z+N(s)^{-1/2}z^{-1}:|z|=1\bigr\},
\]
which is \(\Gamma_{N(s)}\).  The cited theorem and Corollaries \ref{cor:capacity-condition-number-identity}, \ref{cor:capacity-modulus-koenigs}, and \ref{cor:ellipse-area-ellipsoid-gap} express the condition number, rapidity, capacity, and area in terms of \(N(s)\), hence in terms of \(L\).

Finally, by definition,
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}=(r^2)^{1/2}z+(r^2)^{-1/2}z^{-1},
\]
so \(J_r(\TT)=\Gamma_{r^2}\).  The three asserted changes of scale therefore put all boundaries in the single family \(\Gamma_L\), and the only additional operation in the first model is the explicitly displayed scalar homothety.
\end{proof}

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:preliminaries}
We write \(\mathbb P\) for the set of prime numbers. For \(x\in\QQ^\times\) and \(p\in\mathbb P\), \(\nu_p(x)\) denotes the normalized \(p\)-adic valuation, characterized by \(\nu_p(p)=1\) and additivity under multiplication; we also set \(\nu_p(0)=+\infty\). The prime-register monoid is
\[
\mathsf P:=\bigl\{\,r:\mathbb P\to\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}\;:\;r(p)=0\text{ for all but finitely many }p\,\bigr\},
\]
endowed with pointwise addition. Its associated norm is
\[
N(r):=\prod_{p\in\mathbb P} p^{r(p)}\in\NN_{>0}.
\]
We further write \(p_n\#:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_i\) for the \(n\)th primorial, \(B_d\subset\RR^d\) for the Euclidean unit ball, and \(\NN_{\mathrm{sf},>1}\) for the square-free integers greater than \(1\).

The terminology is as follows.  The Joukowsky maps, confocal ellipses, logarithmic capacity, Mahler measure, resultants, discriminants, Bessel functions, Lorentz rapidity, and symbolic dynamics of the doubling map are standard objects, with references cited where they enter the proofs.  A \emph{prime register} is only the finitely supported exponent vector in \(\mathsf P\), and a \emph{codebook} is the pairwise-coprime square-free injection in Definition \ref{def:coprime-squarefree-codebook}.  A \emph{transport polynomial} is the polynomial \(Q_r\) in Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}; a \emph{shell} is one of the exact zero circles \(|z|=1\) or \(|z|=r^{-2}\) appearing in the resultant reconstruction theorem.  Reconstruction always means exact recovery from the complete data explicitly stated in the relevant theorem.  Thus the preliminaries below are the analytic core of the paper: they normalize transport, prove coefficient descent, fix the pullback factorization, and then extract the discriminant factorization used by the later inverse and capacity statements.

\begin{definition}[Pairwise-coprime square-free codebook]\label{def:coprime-squarefree-codebook}
Let \(X\) be a finite set. An injection
\[
\iota:X\hookrightarrow\NN_{\mathrm{sf},>1}
\]
is called \emph{pairwise-coprime square-free} if its image consists of pairwise coprime square-free integers, equivalently
\[
\gcd(\iota(x),\iota(y))=1
\qquad(x\ne y).
\]
Its total logarithmic cost is
\[
C(\iota):=\sum_{x\in X}\log\iota(x)
=\log\!\Bigl(\prod_{x\in X}\iota(x)\Bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

The first boundary model is the normalized covering of a Joukowsky ellipse. For \(c\in(0,1)\cap\QQ\) and \(m\in\NN\), the elliptic covering is
\[
J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})=e^{im\theta}+c\,e^{-im\theta}\qquad (\theta\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ),
\]
and its image ellipse is
\[
E_c=\bigl\{(1+c)\cos t+i(1-c)\sin t:\ t\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\bigr\}.
\]
For \(r>0\), we also use the two-scale Joukowsky map
\[
J_r(z):=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},
\qquad
E_r:=J_r(\TT),
\qquad
\TT:=\{e^{i\theta}:\theta\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\}.
\]
If \(f\in L^1(\TT)\), its Fourier coefficients are
\[
\widehat f(k):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} f(\theta)e^{-ik\theta}\,\dd\theta
\qquad (k\in\ZZ).
\]

The second boundary model is the algebraic transport through \(J_r\). Let \(K\) be a field.  All roots of polynomials over \(K\) are taken in a fixed algebraic closure \(\overline K\), always with multiplicity.  No separability hypothesis is imposed unless it is stated explicitly: repeated roots are allowed, and in that case the relevant discriminant is zero.  For a degree-\(n\) polynomial \(F(x)=b_n\prod_{j=1}^n(x-\alpha_j)\) with \(b_n\ne0\), we use the resultant normalization
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_x(F):=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}b_n^{-1}\operatorname{Res}_x(F,F')
=b_n^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)^2,
\]
where the displayed root formula is interpreted in \(\overline K\); its value lies in \(K\).  The resultant is the standard Sylvester resultant in the displayed order, satisfying
\[
\operatorname{Res}_x\bigl(A(x),c(\beta-x)\bigr)=c^{\deg A}A(\beta)
\]
for every polynomial \(A\) and scalar \(c\).  Equivalently, \(\operatorname{Res}_x(A(x),c(x-\beta))=(-c)^{\deg A}A(\beta)\).  In particular, because \(r z^2-wz+r^{-1}=z\bigl(J_r(z)-w\bigr)\) is linear in \(w\), this convention gives \(\operatorname{Res}_w(Q_r(w),rz^2-wz+r^{-1})=z^nQ_r(J_r(z))\), not \((-z)^nQ_r(J_r(z))\).  If \(K\subseteq\CC\), the same symbols may also be evaluated at complex points.  If \(K\) is arbitrary, identities involving \(J_r(z)\) are identities in the Laurent polynomial ring over \(K(r)\) after adjoining the displayed variable \(z^{\pm1}\).  The following convention fixes the normalization used everywhere below.

\begin{definition}[Reciprocal and transport normalization]\label{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}
Let
\[
P(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k u^k=a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(u-z_j)\in K[u],
\qquad
n\ge 1,
\qquad
 a_n a_0\ne0,
\]
with roots taken in a fixed algebraic closure of \(K\).  Its reciprocal polynomial is the root-normalized reciprocal
\[
P^{\vee}(u):=a_n^{-1}u^nP(1/u)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-u z_j),
\]
so the leading coefficient of \(P^\vee\) is \(a_0/a_n\) and the constant coefficient is \(1\).  The unnormalized reciprocal is \(u^nP(1/u)=a_nP^\vee(u)\).  For an indeterminate \(r\), or for \(r\in K^\times\), the associated Joukowsky transport polynomial is
\[
Q_r(w):=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(w-r z_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)\in K(r)[w].
\]
Thus \(Q_r\) is normalized with leading coefficient \(a_0\).  If a monic transport polynomial is required, we write
\[
\widetilde Q_r(w):=a_0^{-1}Q_r(w).
\]
The phrase ``monic Lee--Yang input'' always refers to \(P\) being monic in the variable \(u\); the induced \(Q_r\) has leading coefficient \(a_0=(-1)^n\prod_j z_j\), while \(\widetilde Q_r\) is the corresponding monic transport polynomial with the same zero divisor.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient descent for the transport polynomial]\label{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}
In Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, the expression
\[
Q_r(w)=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(w-r z_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)
\]
is independent of the ordering of the roots and belongs to \(K(r)[w]\).  The same conclusion holds after specializing \(r\in K^\times\), in which case \(Q_r\in K[w]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to prove that each coefficient of \(Q_r\) lies in the indicated coefficient field.  The coefficient of \(w^{n-\ell}\) is \((-1)^\ell a_0\) times the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(\ell\) in the quantities \(r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\).  Expanding this elementary symmetric polynomial gives a finite \(\ZZ[r,r^{-1}]\)-linear combination of monomials
\[
z_1^{e_1}\cdots z_n^{e_n},
\qquad e_j\in\{-1,0,1\},
\]
summed over subsets in a way that is invariant under every permutation of the root labels.  Since \(a_0\ne0\), all roots are nonzero, and multiplication by a sufficiently large power of \(z_1\cdots z_n\) converts each such symmetric Laurent polynomial into an ordinary symmetric polynomial in \(z_1,
\dots,z_n\).  By the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials, it is a polynomial expression in the elementary symmetric functions of the roots and in \((z_1\cdots z_n)^{-1}\), with coefficients in \(\ZZ[r,r^{-1}]\).

The elementary symmetric functions of the \(z_j\) are \((-1)^k a_{n-k}/a_n\), and \((z_1\cdots z_n)^{-1}=(-1)^n a_n/a_0\).  Hence every coefficient of \(Q_r\) belongs to \(K(r)\).  The construction used only symmetric expressions in the multiset of roots, so it is independent of the chosen ordering.  If \(r\in K^\times\) is specialized, the same formulas have coefficients in \(K\), proving \(Q_r\in K[w]\).
\end{proof}

The normalizations just fixed form the algebraic spine of the article: after Section \ref{sec:elliptic-gates} recovers the boundary parameter from Fourier data, the same normalization controls Mahler means, zero shells, and one-scale resultants. We use the zero set notation \(\mathrm{Zero}(F)\) for a polynomial \(F\), and the standard resultant/discriminant notation \(\operatorname{Res}\) and \(\operatorname{Disc}\) \citep{GKZ1994}. For \(\rho>0\), we define the logarithmic circular mean
\[
\mathcal M_\rho(P):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|P(\rho e^{i\theta})|\,\dd\theta,
\]
so that \(\mathcal M_1(P)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of \(P\) \citep{Mahler1962,EverestWard1999}.

The next two identities are the algebraic input for the Mahler, discriminant, and one-scale reconstruction statements in Sections \ref{sec:mahler-transport} and \ref{sec:discriminant}.

\begin{theorem}[Pullback factorization]\label{thm:pullback-factorization}
Let \(P\), \(P^\vee\), and \(Q_r\) be as in Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, and let \(r\) be an indeterminate or an element of \(K^\times\). Then \(Q_r(w)\in K(r)[w]\), and in \(K(r)[z,z^{-1}]\) one has
\[
\boxed{\;
r^n z^n\,Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)=P(z)\,P^\vee(r^2 z).
\;}
\]
If \(K\subseteq\CC\) and \(r\in K^\times\), the same identity may equivalently be evaluated at every \(z\in\CC^\times\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient assertion is Lemma \ref{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}. For each \(j\),
\[
J_r(z)-\bigl(r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\bigr)
=r(z-z_j)+r^{-1}(z^{-1}-z_j^{-1})
=\frac{(z-z_j)(r^2 z z_j-1)}{r z z_j}.
\]
Multiplying over \(j\) yields
\[
Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)
=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(z-z_j)(r^2 z z_j-1)}{r z z_j}
=r^{-n}z^{-n}\Bigl(a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
Since \(a_0=(-1)^n a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n} z_j\), we have \(a_0\prod_j z_j^{-1}=(-1)^n a_n\). Therefore
\[
Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)
=r^{-n}z^{-n}\,a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\cdot (-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
The first factor is \(P(z)\), while
\[
P^\vee(r^2 z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-r^2 z z_j)=(-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
Combining the two identities gives the stated factorization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Discriminant factorization]\label{thm:discriminant-factorization}
In the setting of Theorem \ref{thm:pullback-factorization},
\[
\boxed{\;
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)
=\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)\,
r^{-n(n-1)}
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2 z_i z_j-1)^2.
\;}
\]
The identity is taken in \(\overline K(r)\), and both sides are in fact elements of \(K(r)\).  It remains valid without assuming that \(P\) is separable; if roots collide, the corresponding discriminants vanish.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(w_j:=r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)=a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(w_i-w_j)^2.
\]
Using
\[
w_i-w_j
=r(z_i-z_j)+r^{-1}(z_i^{-1}-z_j^{-1})
=\frac{(z_i-z_j)(r^2 z_i z_j-1)}{r z_i z_j},
\]
we obtain
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)
=a_0^{2n-2}r^{-n(n-1)}
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i-z_j)^2
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2 z_i z_j-1)^2
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}.
\]
Now
\[
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}
=\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{n} z_i\Bigr)^{-2(n-1)},
\]
and \(a_0=(-1)^n a_n\prod_i z_i\) implies
\[
a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}=a_n^{2n-2}.
\]
Since
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)=a_n^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i-z_j)^2,
\]
the desired identity follows.
\end{proof}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Several themes emerging from the preceding sections deserve emphasis. First, the Joukowsky ellipse is not merely a convenient parametrized curve: it is a conformal boundary object whose logarithmic capacity, annular modulus, and Mahler averages are all controlled by the same scale parameter. In this sense, the transport identity of Theorem \ref{thm:pullback-factorization} should be viewed as a potential-theoretic bridge between circle data and ellipse data, parallel to the classical role of conformal maps in logarithmic potential theory \citep{Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,Pommerenke1992}. The discriminant and resultant formulas show that the same bridge also preserves delicate elimination-theoretic information \citep{GKZ1994}.

Second, the \(p\)-adic rigidity statements suggest a broader arithmetic theory. For rational \(c\), the slopes of \(\nu_p(\widehat u_{c,m}(2mn))\) recover the full local data of \(c\), while Theorem \ref{thm:elliptic-gate-padics-obstruction} shows that any fixed finite congruence window leaves a systematic non-identifiability window. Extending this full-spectrum analysis from rational parameters to algebraic parameters is a natural continuation, but it is separate from the exact reconstruction results proved here \citep{Koblitz1984,Serre1973}.

Third, the capacity-scaled prime-register dictionary gives a geometric interpretation of multiplicative coding: additive rapidities in \(\mathrm{SO}^+(1,1)\) correspond to multiplicative norms, while the normalized ellipse parameter obeys Einstein addition. This block is kept in the present article because its invariants are exactly the Joukowsky capacity, area, and condition-number invariants of the same confocal family. Possible higher-rank analogues, where one replaces \(\mathrm{SO}^+(1,1)\) by more general diagonal or Cartan subgroups, are separate from the one-boundary theorem chain proved here. A parallel question is whether the primorial extremizers obtained here remain optimal under weaker combinatorial constraints than pairwise coprimality.

Finally, the inverse results emphasize which data are exact and where stability is available.  The multiscale and one-scale reconstructions use full coefficient data and exact known scales; the Lee--Yang uniqueness statement additionally uses the unit-circle certification of the source zeros.  Perturbative recovery is asserted only in the annular Rouch\'e regime of Theorem \ref{thm:quantitative-annular-rouche-stability}, where the admissible error is bounded on separating contours.  Partial observation, unknown scale, and multivariable transport remain outside the present theorem chain.
