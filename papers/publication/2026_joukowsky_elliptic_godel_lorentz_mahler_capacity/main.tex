\begin{abstract}
We study Joukowsky maps as a complex-variable transport mechanism for logarithmic capacity, equilibrium energy, Mahler measure, and algebraic inverse reconstruction.  For a finite-degree rational map \(R=A/B\), a compact nonpolar source set avoiding the poles, and the divided kernel \(\Psi_R(z,t)=(A(z)B(t)-A(t)B(z))/(z-t)\), we prove an exact variational formula for \(\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))\) over finite-energy lifts on \(K\), including the branch and collision singularities; its degree-two specialization gives the Joukowsky capacity spine used later.  For \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\), we diagonalize the logarithmic energy of \(J_{r\ast}\mu\) by Fourier coefficients, obtain the sharp mode constants \(1\pm r^{-2k}\), and identify the unique equilibrium measure.  Finite Blaschke covers \(J_r\circ B\) inherit the same deficit through \(B_\ast\mu\); their canonical equilibrium lift has density \(|B'|/(2\pi\deg B)\) and an explicit zero-product source-energy formula.  Finally, the transport polynomial \(Q_r\) gives exact discriminant, Mahler, multiscale interpolation, one-scale resonance, Lee--Yang injectivity, and quantitative annular Rouch\'e reconstruction theorems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
The classical Joukowsky map provides one of the simplest conformal mechanisms by which circles generate ellipses and, more generally, confocal families in the plane. It therefore sits naturally at the intersection of conformal mapping, algebraic elimination, and logarithmic capacity theory \citep{Pommerenke1992,Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,GKZ1994}. The purpose of the present paper is to make this boundary object carry a complete potential-theoretic and Mahler-transport calculus: rational pullbacks give exact capacity formulae, Fourier diagonalization gives sharp equilibrium stability on the ellipse, finite Blaschke covers describe all equilibrium lifts, and polynomial elimination gives inverse reconstruction from transported data.  Arithmetic registers, hyperbolic matrices, and symbolic models are kept out of the main theorem chain; when mentioned in the appendices, they serve only as calibrated coordinates for the same boundary scale.

The dependence on classical material is deliberately separated from the new assertions.  We use the standard variational definition of logarithmic capacity, Jensen--Mahler identities, Fekete equilibrium facts, and elementary finite Blaschke product boundary theory as tools.  The new contribution is the transport package tying them together: a finite-degree rational pullback kernel used as the support theorem for the degree-two Joukowsky specialization, the degree-two collision skeleton \(zt=r^{-2}\), the sharp Joukowsky Fourier deficit with its extremizers, the Blaschke-cover lift classification and canonical lift energy, and the algebraic inverse package combining multiscale Laurent interpolation, one-scale resonance fibers, unit-circle injectivity, and Rouch\'e-stable annular recovery.

The main analytic object is the two-scale Joukowsky transform
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},\qquad r>1,
\]
and its boundary ellipse \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\).  The capacity section is organized so that the broad rational statement is a tool, not a second research program: for a finite-degree rational map \(R=A/B\), the divided difference \(\Psi_R\) gives an exact pullback formula for \(\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))\), including ordinary collisions, branch points, and off-diagonal branch-graph collisions; the paper immediately specializes that formula to the degree-two map \(J_r\).  In this specialization the off-diagonal branch graph is the single involution \(t=r^{-2}/z\), it misses \(\TT\times\TT\), the equilibrium deficit on \(E_r\) diagonalizes into Fourier modes with sharp constants, and finite Blaschke covers preserve the deficit precisely through the pushed-forward source measure.

The Mahler-transport part uses the same scale.  For a polynomial \(P\) with nonzero constant term, the transform \(J_r\) generates a transport polynomial \(Q_r\) whose pullback along the circle factors exactly as a product of \(P\) and its reciprocal polynomial. This identity leads to transported Jensen formulas on the ellipse, a shell-counting interpretation of the logarithmic-mean slope, and a Lee--Yang Mahler law when the zeros of \(P\) lie on the unit circle \citep{LeeYang1952I,LeeYang1952II,Mahler1962,Smyth1981,Boyd1981,EverestWard1999}. At the level of elimination theory, the same factorization yields explicit discriminant formulas, multiscale interpolation, one-scale resonance fibers, exact Lee--Yang reconstruction, and quantitative annular Rouch\'e stability \citep{GKZ1994}.

Thus the common complex-variable object throughout the main body is the boundary of a Joukowsky ellipse.  The appendices record two calibrated arithmetic applications only after the potential-theoretic and Mahler inverse spine has been established: the normalized speed spectrum of the covering \(z\mapsto z^m+c z^{-m}\), including its \(p\)-adic slopes, and the capacity-scaled prime-register dictionary attaching multiplicative data to the same confocal scale.  These applications use the same boundary normalization; stronger Fourier, \(p\)-adic, Lorentz-rigidity, codebook-optimization, and primorial-extremality theories are deliberately left outside this article.

\paragraph{Main theorem package.}
The paper proves the following complex-variable transport package for the Joukowsky boundary.  The primary contributions are the capacity kernel, the sharp Fourier deficit, the finite Blaschke lift theorem, the inverse resonance classification, and the quantitative Rouch\'e recovery criterion.  The endpoint synthesis statements and the final scale-rigidity/fiber statements at the end of Section \ref{sec:discriminant} assemble these contributions rather than replacing them as the source of novelty.

Section \ref{sec:discriminant} derives discriminant and resultant corollaries, inverse resonance classification, one-scale reconstruction, quantitative annular Rouch\'e stability, the endpoint synthesis proposition used by the scale-rigidity theorem, and the exact fiber classification. Section \ref{sec:discussion} closes the main complex-variable theorem chain, while Appendices \ref{sec:elliptic-gates} and \ref{sec:godel-lorentz} contain short calibrated arithmetic applications.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:preliminaries}
The terminology is as follows.  The Joukowsky maps, confocal ellipses, logarithmic capacity, Mahler measure, resultants, and discriminants are standard objects, with references cited where they enter the proofs.  A \emph{transport polynomial} is the polynomial \(Q_r\) in Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}; a \emph{shell} is one of the exact zero circles \(|z|=1\) or \(|z|=r^{-2}\) appearing in the resultant reconstruction theorem.  Reconstruction always means exact recovery from the complete data explicitly stated in the relevant theorem.  Thus the preliminaries below are the analytic core of the paper: they normalize logarithmic energy and Joukowsky transport, prove coefficient descent, fix the pullback factorization, and then extract the discriminant factorization used by the later inverse and capacity statements.  The prime-register and codebook notation used only in the calibrated applications is introduced locally in Appendix \ref{sec:godel-lorentz}.

All logarithmic energies in the paper use one global convention.  If \(X\subset\CC\) is compact and \(\sigma\) is a Borel probability measure on \(X\), put
\[
I_X^{(M)}(\sigma):=\iint_{X\times X}\max\{\log|z-t|,-M\}\,\dd\sigma(z)\dd\sigma(t)
\qquad(M>0)
\]
and define
\[
I_X(\sigma):=\lim_{M\to\infty}I_X^{(M)}(\sigma)\in[-\infty,\infty).
\]
The kernels decrease as \(M\) increases, so this lower-truncated limit is well-defined; a measure is called \emph{finite-energy} when \(I_X(\sigma)>-\infty\).  When the compact set is clear from context we write simply \(I(\sigma)\).  For a rational map \(R\) with no pole on \(X\), a finite-energy lift means a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(X\) for which \(I_{R(X)}(R_\ast\mu)>-\infty\); the pullback kernels below are always interpreted by this same lower-truncated convention, including diagonal, branch, and off-diagonal collision singularities.  Circular logarithmic means are the corresponding one-variable Lebesgue integrals, equivalently these lower-truncated limits; the logarithmic singularity at a zero of a nonzero polynomial is locally integrable, so Jensen's formula applies with this convention.  On the unit circle we also write
\[
I_{\TT}(\sigma):=\iint_{\TT\times\TT}\log|z-t|\,\dd\sigma(z)\dd\sigma(t)
\]
for the same lower-truncated energy.  If \(\widehat\sigma(k)=\int_{\TT}z^{-k}\,\dd\sigma(z)\), then for finite-energy circle measures the classical Fourier identity is
\[
I_{\TT}(\sigma)=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat\sigma(k)|^2}{k},
\]
with the series understood as a finite real number exactly in the finite-energy case.  Abel regularization of the circle kernel is used below only as a proof device for this same lower-truncated limit under the finite-energy hypothesis.  Ordinary logarithmic means such as \(\int_{\TT}\log|F(z)|\,\dd\sigma(z)\) are always the corresponding lower-truncated limits unless the integrand is continuous on the support.

The first boundary model is the normalized covering of a Joukowsky ellipse. For \(c\in(0,1)\cap\QQ\) and \(m\in\NN\), the elliptic covering is
\[
J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})=e^{im\theta}+c\,e^{-im\theta}\qquad (\theta\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ),
\]
and its image ellipse is
\[
E_c=\bigl\{(1+c)\cos t+i(1-c)\sin t:\ t\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\bigr\}.
\]
For \(r>0\), we also use the two-scale Joukowsky map
\[
J_r(z):=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},
\qquad
E_r:=J_r(\TT),
\qquad
\TT:=\{e^{i\theta}:\theta\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\}.
\]
If \(f\in L^1(\TT)\), its Fourier coefficients are
\[
\widehat f(k):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} f(\theta)e^{-ik\theta}\,\dd\theta
\qquad (k\in\ZZ).
\]

The second boundary model is the algebraic transport through \(J_r\). Throughout the algebraic transport statements, \(K\) denotes a field of characteristic zero; whenever absolute values, circles, ellipses, or logarithmic integrals are used, the notation is specialized to a subfield of \(\CC\), and in the analytic Mahler section to \(\CC\) itself.  All roots of polynomials over \(K\) are taken in a fixed algebraic closure \(\overline K\), always with multiplicity.  Repeated roots are allowed, and in that case the relevant discriminant is zero.  For a degree-\(n\) polynomial \(F(x)=b_n\prod_{j=1}^n(x-\alpha_j)\) with \(b_n\ne0\), we use the resultant normalization
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_x(F):=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}b_n^{-1}\operatorname{Res}_x(F,F')
=b_n^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)^2,
\]
where the displayed root formula is interpreted in \(\overline K\); its value lies in \(K\).  The resultant is the standard Sylvester resultant in the displayed order; equivalently, for every polynomial \(A(w)\) and every affine linear polynomial \(a(z)w+b(z)\) in the variable \(w\),
\[
\operatorname{Res}_w\bigl(A(w),a(z)w+b(z)\bigr)=(-a(z))^{\deg A}A\!\left(-\frac{b(z)}{a(z)}\right).
\]
In particular it satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Res}_x\bigl(A(x),c(\beta-x)\bigr)=c^{\deg A}A(\beta)
\]
for every polynomial \(A\) and scalar \(c\).  Equivalently, \(\operatorname{Res}_x(A(x),c(x-\beta))=(-c)^{\deg A}A(\beta)\).  In particular, because \(r z^2-wz+r^{-1}=z\bigl(J_r(z)-w\bigr)\) is linear in \(w\), this convention gives \(\operatorname{Res}_w(Q_r(w),rz^2-wz+r^{-1})=z^nQ_r(J_r(z))\), not \((-z)^nQ_r(J_r(z))\).  If \(K\subseteq\CC\), the same symbols may also be evaluated at complex points.  If \(K\) is arbitrary, identities involving \(J_r(z)\) are identities in the Laurent polynomial ring over \(K(r)\) after adjoining the displayed variable \(z^{\pm1}\).  The following convention fixes the normalization used everywhere below.

\begin{definition}[Reciprocal and transport normalization]\label{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}
Let
\[
P(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k u^k=a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(u-z_j)\in K[u],
\qquad
n\ge 1,
\qquad
 a_n a_0\ne0,
\]
with roots taken in a fixed algebraic closure of \(K\).  Its reciprocal polynomial is the root-normalized reciprocal
\[
P^{\vee}(u):=a_n^{-1}u^nP(1/u)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-u z_j),
\]
so the leading coefficient of \(P^\vee\) is \(a_0/a_n\) and the constant coefficient is \(1\).  The unnormalized reciprocal is \(u^nP(1/u)=a_nP^\vee(u)\).  For an indeterminate \(r\), or for \(r\in K^\times\), the associated Joukowsky transport polynomial is
\[
Q_r(w):=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(w-r z_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)\in K(r)[w].
\]
Thus \(Q_r\) is normalized with leading coefficient \(a_0\).  If a monic transport polynomial is required, we write
\[
\widetilde Q_r(w):=a_0^{-1}Q_r(w).
\]
The phrase ``monic Lee--Yang input'' always refers to \(P\) being monic in the variable \(u\); the induced \(Q_r\) has leading coefficient \(a_0=(-1)^n\prod_j z_j\), while \(\widetilde Q_r\) is the corresponding monic transport polynomial with the same zero divisor.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient descent for the transport polynomial]\label{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}
In Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, the expression
\[
Q_r(w)=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(w-r z_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)
\]
is independent of the ordering of the roots and belongs to \(K(r)[w]\).  The same conclusion holds after specializing \(r\in K^\times\), in which case \(Q_r\in K[w]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to prove that each coefficient of \(Q_r\) lies in the indicated coefficient field.  The coefficient of \(w^{n-\ell}\) is \((-1)^\ell a_0\) times the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(\ell\) in the quantities \(r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\).  Expanding this elementary symmetric polynomial gives a finite \(\ZZ[r,r^{-1}]\)-linear combination of monomials
\[
z_1^{e_1}\cdots z_n^{e_n},
\qquad e_j\in\{-1,0,1\},
\]
summed over subsets in a way that is invariant under every permutation of the root labels.  Since \(a_0\ne0\), all roots are nonzero, and multiplication by a sufficiently large power of \(z_1\cdots z_n\) converts each such symmetric Laurent polynomial into an ordinary symmetric polynomial in \(z_1,
\dots,z_n\).  By the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials, it is a polynomial expression in the elementary symmetric functions of the roots and in \((z_1\cdots z_n)^{-1}\), with coefficients in \(\ZZ[r,r^{-1}]\).

The elementary symmetric functions of the \(z_j\) are \((-1)^k a_{n-k}/a_n\), and \((z_1\cdots z_n)^{-1}=(-1)^n a_n/a_0\).  Hence every coefficient of \(Q_r\) belongs to \(K(r)\).  The construction used only symmetric expressions in the multiset of roots, so it is independent of the chosen ordering.  If \(r\in K^\times\) is specialized, the same formulas have coefficients in \(K\), proving \(Q_r\in K[w]\).
\end{proof}

The normalizations just fixed form the algebraic spine of the article: the same normalization controls Mahler means, zero shells, and one-scale resultants, and it is compatible with the optional Fourier-cover parameter recovery recorded in Appendix \ref{sec:elliptic-gates}. We use the zero set notation \(\mathrm{Zero}(F)\) for a polynomial \(F\), and the standard resultant/discriminant notation \(\operatorname{Res}\) and \(\operatorname{Disc}\) \citep{GKZ1994}. For \(\rho>0\), we define the logarithmic circular mean
\[
\mathcal M_\rho(P):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|P(\rho e^{i\theta})|\,\dd\theta,
\]
so that \(\mathcal M_1(P)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of \(P\) \citep{Mahler1962,EverestWard1999}.

The next two identities are the algebraic input for the Mahler, discriminant, and one-scale reconstruction statements in Sections \ref{sec:mahler-transport} and \ref{sec:discriminant}.

\begin{theorem}[Pullback factorization]\label{thm:pullback-factorization}
Let \(P\), \(P^\vee\), and \(Q_r\) be as in Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, and let \(r\) be an indeterminate or an element of \(K^\times\). Then \(Q_r(w)\in K(r)[w]\), and in \(K(r)[z,z^{-1}]\) one has
\[
\boxed{\;
r^n z^n\,Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)=P(z)\,P^\vee(r^2 z).
\;}
\]
If \(K\subseteq\CC\) and \(r\in K^\times\), the same identity may equivalently be evaluated at every \(z\in\CC^\times\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient assertion is Lemma \ref{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}. For each \(j\),
\[
J_r(z)-\bigl(r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\bigr)
=r(z-z_j)+r^{-1}(z^{-1}-z_j^{-1})
=\frac{(z-z_j)(r^2 z z_j-1)}{r z z_j}.
\]
Multiplying over \(j\) yields
\[
Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)
=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(z-z_j)(r^2 z z_j-1)}{r z z_j}
=r^{-n}z^{-n}\Bigl(a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
Since \(a_0=(-1)^n a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n} z_j\), we have \(a_0\prod_j z_j^{-1}=(-1)^n a_n\). Therefore
\[
Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)
=r^{-n}z^{-n}\,a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\cdot (-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
The first factor is \(P(z)\), while
\[
P^\vee(r^2 z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-r^2 z z_j)=(-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
Combining the two identities gives the stated factorization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Discriminant factorization]\label{thm:discriminant-factorization}
In the setting of Theorem \ref{thm:pullback-factorization},
\[
\boxed{\;
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)
=\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)\,
r^{-n(n-1)}
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2 z_i z_j-1)^2.
\;}
\]
The identity is taken in \(\overline K(r)\), and both sides are in fact elements of \(K(r)\).  It remains valid without assuming that \(P\) is separable; if roots collide, the corresponding discriminants vanish.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(w_j:=r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)=a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(w_i-w_j)^2.
\]
Using
\[
w_i-w_j
=r(z_i-z_j)+r^{-1}(z_i^{-1}-z_j^{-1})
=\frac{(z_i-z_j)(r^2 z_i z_j-1)}{r z_i z_j},
\]
we obtain
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)
=a_0^{2n-2}r^{-n(n-1)}
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i-z_j)^2
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2 z_i z_j-1)^2
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}.
\]
Now
\[
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}
=\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{n} z_i\Bigr)^{-2(n-1)},
\]
and \(a_0=(-1)^n a_n\prod_i z_i\) implies
\[
a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}=a_n^{2n-2}.
\]
Since
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)=a_n^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i-z_j)^2,
\]
the desired identity follows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Endpoint synthesis consequences}\label{subsec:endpoint-synthesis}

The capacity/Fourier/Blaschke and Mahler-inverse results now supply the core theorem package.  The remaining statements in this section are placed here as synthesis consequences: they package what the preceding theorems already prove about the endpoint data and then record the exact residual fiber.  They are not additional headline inputs to the analytic spine.  The first proposition extracts the two endpoint facts used in the closing recognition argument: the covered-equilibrium consequence of the finite Blaschke--Joukowsky theorem and the one-scale Lee--Yang annular reconstruction consequence.  No arithmetic-register input enters this closure step.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint inputs for boundary-scale rigidity]\label{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis}
Fix \(r>1\) and let \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\ge1\), and write
  \[
  I(\sigma):=\iint\log|x-y|\,\dd\sigma(x)\dd\sigma(y)
  \]
  with lower truncation when necessary.  Then
  \[
  \sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)} I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=\log r.
  \]
  For \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\), equality in
  \[
  I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)\le \log r
  \]
  holds if and only if
  \[
  B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
  \]
  Thus all equilibrium lifts of the covered ellipse are exactly the Haar lifts through \(B\), and the canonical absolutely continuous lift is
  \[
  \dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})=\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\,\dd\theta.
  \]

  \item Let \(P\in\CC[z]\) be a monic polynomial of positive degree whose zeros all lie on \(\TT\), and let \(Q_r\) be its transport polynomial in the normalization of Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}.  Then the full one-scale polynomial \(Q_r\) determines \(P\) uniquely.  Equivalently, for every \(\rho\) with \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\), \(P\) is the unique monic factor of
  \[
  S_{r,Q_r}(z):=z^{\deg P}Q_r(J_r(z))
  \]
  whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\); the complementary factor has all zeros in \(\{|z|<\rho\}\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the analytic coordinate recovers the boundary scale through \(\sup I=\log r\), while the algebraic coordinate is recognized at that recovered scale by the intrinsic Lee--Yang shell test of Theorem \ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \textup{(a)}, Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} gives, for every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\),
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=I(J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu))\le\log r,
\]
with equality exactly when \(B_\ast\mu\) is Haar measure.  This proves both the supremum and the equality criterion.  The displayed canonical lift is the canonical measure constructed in the same finite-cover theorem, and Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula} gives that its pushforward by \(B\) is Haar measure.

For \textup{(b)}, apply Theorem \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction} to the fixed scale \(r>1\).  It gives injectivity of \(P\mapsto Q_r\) on the monic Lee--Yang class and constructive recovery from \((r,Q_r)\).  Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection} identifies this constructive recovery with the stated annular factor selection for every \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\).  The final sentence combines \textup{(a)} with Theorem \ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Realizable boundary-scale data]\label{def:realizable-boundary-scale-data}
Let \(\mathcal L\) denote the class of monic polynomials of positive degree whose zeros, counted with multiplicity, lie on \(\TT\).  A \emph{realizable boundary-scale datum} is a pair
\[
\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]
\]
for which there are a number \(r>1\), a finite Blaschke product \(B\) of positive degree, and a polynomial \(P\in\mathcal L\) such that \(Q=Q_r(P)\) is the transport polynomial of \(P\) in the normalization of Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, and
\[
e=\mathcal E(B,r):=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)} I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu),
\]
with logarithmic energy interpreted by lower truncation as in Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis}.  The set of all such pairs is denoted by \(\mathfrak B\).  A triple \((B,r,P)\) satisfying these conditions is called a \emph{realization} of \(\mathfrak b\), and the associated ellipse is \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\).
\end{definition}

The following endpoint theorem is deliberately placed after the main capacity/Fourier/Blaschke and Mahler-inverse theorems.  It is a final synthesis, not the primary novelty: it records the intrinsic test for when a pair consisting of a capacity scale and a transported polynomial can have come from the Joukowsky boundary spine.  Positivity of the energy coordinate fixes the scale, and the pulled-back polynomial must split into the two reciprocal spectral shells dictated by that same scale.  Once this shell test holds, the covered-equilibrium number and the Lee--Yang transport polynomial determine the ellipse and the source polynomial; the only coordinate not seen by the endpoint datum is the harmless choice of finite Blaschke cover and Haar lift.  In particular, all later references to this theorem use it only after the degree-two capacity skeleton, sharp equilibrium theorem, finite Blaschke lift theorem, and one-scale Lee--Yang reconstruction theorem have supplied their inputs.

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint recognition and boundary-scale rigidity]\label{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}
Let \(\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]\), with \(\deg_w Q=n_{\mathfrak b}\ge1\).  Put
\[
r_{\mathfrak b}:=\exp(e),
\]
and, when \(e>0\), define
\[
S_{\mathfrak b}(z):=z^{n_{\mathfrak b}}Q\!\left(J_{r_{\mathfrak b}}(z)\right).
\]
Then \(\mathfrak b\) is a realizable boundary-scale datum in the sense of Definition \ref{def:realizable-boundary-scale-data} if and only if the following intrinsic shell condition holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item \(e>0\);
\item for some \(\rho\) with \(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}<\rho<1\), the polynomial \(S_{\mathfrak b}\) has exactly \(n_{\mathfrak b}\) zeros in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\), counted with multiplicity; if \(P_{\mathfrak b}\) is the monic factor determined by those outer zeros, then all zeros of \(P_{\mathfrak b}\) lie on \(\TT\) and
\[
S_{\mathfrak b}(z)=r_{\mathfrak b}^{n_{\mathfrak b}}P_{\mathfrak b}(z)\,z^{n_{\mathfrak b}}P_{\mathfrak b}\!\left(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}z^{-1}\right).
\]
\end{enumerate}
When these equivalent conditions hold, every realization \((B,r,P)\) of \(\mathfrak b\) has
\[
r=r_{\mathfrak b},
\qquad
E_r=E_{r_{\mathfrak b}},
\qquad
\deg P=n_{\mathfrak b}.
\]
Moreover the source polynomial is recovered from the two coordinates \((e,Q)\) alone: for every \(\rho\) with \(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}<\rho<1\), \(P=P_{\mathfrak b}\) is the unique monic factor of
\[
z^{n_{\mathfrak b}}Q\!\left(J_{r_{\mathfrak b}}(z)\right)
\]
whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\), and the complementary factor has all zeros in \(\{|z|<\rho\}\).

Thus the recognition datum has the following canonical-core form.  The assignment
\[
\mathfrak b\longmapsto (r_{\mathfrak b},P_{\mathfrak b},\mu_{r_{\mathfrak b}}),
\qquad
\mu_{r_{\mathfrak b}}:=J_{r_{\mathfrak b}\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right),
\]
is intrinsic on the realizable locus, and every realization maps to the same core triple.  Conversely, any finite Blaschke cover \(B\) and any probability measure \(\mu\) with \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\) realize exactly this same core triple after applying \(J_{r_{\mathfrak b}}\).  Hence the endpoint datum has no residual analytic or algebraic ambiguity beyond the finite-cover Haar-lift coordinate.

Equivalently, if \((B,r,P)\) and \((C,s,\widetilde P)\) are two realizations, with transport polynomials \(Q_r\) and \(\widetilde Q_s\), and if
\[
\mathcal E(B,r)=\mathcal E(C,s)
\]
and
\[
Q_r=\widetilde Q_s
\]
as polynomials in \(w\), then \(\deg P=\deg\widetilde P\), \(r=s\), \(E_r=E_s\), and \(P=\widetilde P\).  Moreover, if \(\mu,\nu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) satisfy
\[
B_\ast\mu=C_\ast\nu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi},
\]
then their covered equilibrium images agree and are the unique equilibrium measure on the recovered ellipse:
\[
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=(J_s\circ C)_\ast\nu=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First assume that \(\mathfrak b\) is realizable, and choose a realization \((B,r,P)\).  By Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis},
\[
e=\mathcal E(B,r)=\log r,
\]
so \(e>0\) and \(r=r_{\mathfrak b}\).  Since \(Q=Q_r(P)\) with \(P\in\mathcal L\), Theorem \ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition} gives exactly the shell condition \textup{(b)} and identifies the outer factor as \(P\).  Thus every realizable datum satisfies the intrinsic criterion.

Conversely suppose that the intrinsic shell condition holds.  Theorem \ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition}, applied to \((e,Q)\), gives a monic Lee--Yang polynomial \(P_{\mathfrak b}\in\mathcal L\) such that \(Q=Q_{r_{\mathfrak b}}(P_{\mathfrak b})\).  Taking the degree-one Blaschke product \(B(z)=z\), Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} gives \(\mathcal E(B,r_{\mathfrak b})=\log r_{\mathfrak b}=e\).  Hence \((B,r_{\mathfrak b},P_{\mathfrak b})\) is a realization in the sense of Definition \ref{def:realizable-boundary-scale-data}.  This proves intrinsic recognizability of \(\mathfrak B\).

Choose a realization \((B,r,P)\) of \(\mathfrak b\).  By Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover},
\[
e=\mathcal E(B,r)=\log r.
\]
Since \(r>1\), this gives \(e>0\) and \(r=\exp(e)=r_{\mathfrak b}\).  The transport normalization has nonzero leading coefficient, so Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization} gives
\[
n_{\mathfrak b}=\deg_w Q=\deg P.
\]
With this recovered scale and degree fixed, Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection} applies to \(Q\) and gives, for every \(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}<\rho<1\), the unique monic outer factor of \(z^{n_{\mathfrak b}}Q(J_{r_{\mathfrak b}}(z))\) as exactly \(P\); the remaining factor has all zeros in the inner shell.  Thus the inverse formula for \(P\) depends only on \((e,Q)\), not on the realizing Blaschke product.

Now suppose \((B,r,P)\) and \((C,s,\widetilde P)\) are two realizations satisfying the displayed equalities.  The preceding paragraph applied to the common datum gives \(r=s=\exp(\mathcal E(B,r))\), \(E_r=E_s\), and \(\deg P=\deg\widetilde P\).  At the common scale, the identity \(Q_r=\widetilde Q_s\) is an equality of one-scale Lee--Yang transports.  Theorem \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction} then gives \(P=\widetilde P\), in agreement with the explicit annular factor formula above.

Finally, functoriality of pushforward and the equality \(s=r\) give
\[
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu)=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right)
=J_{s\ast}(C_\ast\nu)=(J_s\circ C)_\ast\nu,
\]
which is the asserted common equilibrium measure.
Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} identifies this measure as the unique equilibrium measure on \(E_r\), so the common covered image is unique after the scale is recovered.

The canonical-core statement is the same argument read without naming a second realization.  The preceding paragraphs show that every realization has the same recovered scale and source polynomial, namely \((r_{\mathfrak b},P_{\mathfrak b})\).  Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} gives the covered-equilibrium equality criterion: a lift measure realizes equality exactly when it is a Haar lift through its finite Blaschke cover.  For such a lift,
\[
(J_{r_{\mathfrak b}}\circ B)_\ast\mu
=J_{r_{\mathfrak b}\ast}(B_\ast\mu)
=J_{r_{\mathfrak b}\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right)
=\mu_{r_{\mathfrak b}},
\]
and every finite Blaschke cover with a Haar lift gives this same image.  Therefore the only coordinate not detected by \((e,Q)\) is exactly the finite-cover Haar-lift coordinate.

This proves the data equivalence and completes the endpoint synthesis.
\end{proof}

The preceding endpoint recognition theorem immediately yields an obstruction form that is often the most useful way to read the classification: every failed datum fails at one of only two gates, the positive capacity scale or the reciprocal shell factorization at that scale.  This is the promised bad-example skeleton for the endpoint: there are no further hidden boundary or cover obstructions after the main theorem chain has supplied capacity equilibrium, Blaschke lifts, and Lee--Yang reconstruction.

\begin{proposition}[Two-gate obstruction for endpoint data]\label{prop:boundary-scale-two-gate-obstruction}
Let \(\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]\) with \(\deg Q=n\ge1\).  Then exactly one of the following alternatives holds.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item \(e\le0\).  In this case \(\mathfrak b\) is not realizable as a boundary-scale datum.
  \item \(e>0\), but for \(r=\exp(e)\) the polynomial \(z^nQ(J_r(z))\) has no factorization
  \[
  z^nQ(J_r(z))=r^nP(z)\,z^nP(r^{-2}z^{-1})
  \]
  with \(P\) monic of degree \(n\) and all zeros of \(P\) on \(\TT\).  In this case \(\mathfrak b\) is not realizable.
  \item There is a unique monic \(P\in\mathcal L\) for which the displayed factorization holds with \(r=\exp(e)\).  In this case \(\mathfrak b\) is realizable, all its realizations have the same scale \(r\), the same ellipse \(E_r\), and the same source polynomial \(P\), and every remaining choice is only a finite Blaschke cover together with a Haar-lift measure for that cover.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The alternatives are exhaustive by the trichotomy \(e\le0\), \(e>0\) with no reciprocal shell factorization, and \(e>0\) with such a factorization.  In the first case Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity} rules out realizability, because every realization satisfies \(e=\log r>0\) by Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis}.  In the second case the same theorem rules out realizability because its intrinsic shell condition is exactly the displayed reciprocal factorization, with the outer factor required to lie in \(\mathcal L\).

In the third case Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity} gives realizability and identifies the monic outer factor in any annulus \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\) as the source polynomial.  The uniqueness follows because the zeros of the two factors occupy disjoint shells, \(\TT\) and \(r^{-2}\TT\), so the monic outer factor is uniquely determined by the zeros in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\).  The same theorem fixes the scale and ellipse in every realization.  Finally, Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} and Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} say that equality in the covered equilibrium problem is equivalent to pushing the chosen source measure to Haar measure under the finite Blaschke product.  Therefore, after \((r,P)\) has been recovered, the only remaining freedom is precisely the finite cover and the choice of Haar-lift measure.
\end{proof}

The final classification is an application of the endpoint rigidity theorem to the residual covering coordinate.  It is the closing consequence of the core theorem package rather than a competing main theorem.  It records that the full capacity--Mahler datum has no hidden analytic or algebraic boundary ambiguity after the capacity, Blaschke-cover, Lee--Yang reconstruction, and two-gate obstruction results have supplied their inputs.  The data map loses precisely the finite-cover Haar-lift coordinate and nothing else.

\begin{corollary}[Residual Blaschke-lift fiber for the capacity--Mahler datum]\label{cor:capacity-mahler-fiber-classification}
Fix an integer \(n\ge1\), and let \(\mathfrak X_n\) be the class of all quadruples \((B,r,P,\mu)\) in which \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product of positive degree, \(r>1\), \(P\) is a monic degree-\(n\) polynomial with all zeros on \(\TT\), \(Q_r(P)\) is its Lee--Yang transport polynomial, and \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\) satisfies
\[
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Define an equivalence relation on \(\mathfrak X_n\) by
\[
(B,r,P,\mu)\sim(C,s,\widetilde P,\nu)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r=s\ \text{ and }\ P=\widetilde P.
\]
Attach to such a quadruple the three objects
\[
\mathfrak D(B,r,P,\mu):=\bigl(\mathcal E(B,r),\,Q_r(P),\,(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu\bigr),
\]
where \(\mathcal E(B,r)\) is the covered-boundary energy supremum from Definition \ref{def:realizable-boundary-scale-data}.  Put \(\mu_r:=J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/(2\pi))\).  Then \(\mathfrak D\) is constant on \(\sim\)-classes and induces a bijection
\[
\overline{\mathfrak D}:\mathfrak X_n/\!\sim\ \longrightarrow
\left\{\bigl(\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r\bigr): r>1,
\ P\in\mathcal L,
\ \deg P=n\right\}.
\]
Equivalently, if two admissible quadruples \((B,r,P,\mu)\) and \((C,s,\widetilde P,\nu)\) have the same datum \(\mathfrak D\), then the following conclusions hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item \emph{Scale and one-scale resonance rigidity.}  The first two components of \(\mathfrak D\), through Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity} and Theorem \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction}, force
  \[
  r=s,
  \qquad
  E_r=E_s,
  \qquad
  P=\widetilde P.
  \]
  Thus the one-scale resonance fiber of the transport polynomial contains no second Lee--Yang source at the recovered scale.

  \item \emph{Blaschke lift fiber.}  The lift hypotheses and the equality \(r=s\), together with Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis}, force
  \[
  (J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=(J_s\circ C)_\ast\nu=\mu_r.
  \]
  Hence the only remaining freedom is the finite Blaschke cover and the choice of a probability measure in its Haar-lift fiber.

  \item \emph{Lee--Yang annular selection.}  For every \(\rho\) with \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\), Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection} identifies \(P\) as the unique monic degree-\(n\) factor of
  \[
  z^nQ_r(J_r(z))
  \]
  whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\); the complementary factor has all zeros in \(\{|z|<\rho\}\).
\end{enumerate}
Conversely, the complete fiber over a point \((\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r)\) is exactly
\[
\left\{(B,r,P,\mu)\in\mathfrak X_n:
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right\},
\]
and every element of this fiber gives the same datum
\[
\bigl(\log r,\,Q_r(P),\,\mu_r\bigr).
\]
In particular there is no additional obstruction, resonance, or boundary normalization hidden in the covering coordinate.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
First fix \((B,r,P,\mu)\in\mathfrak X_n\).  Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} gives
\[
\mathcal E(B,r)=\log r,
\]
and functoriality of pushforward gives
\[
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu)=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right)=\mu_r.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathfrak D(B,r,P,\mu)=\bigl(\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r\bigr),
\]
which depends only on \((r,P)\).  Thus \(\mathfrak D\) is constant on \(\sim\)-classes, and the displayed formula defines a surjection from \(\mathfrak X_n/\!\sim\) onto the stated set.

Let two admissible quadruples have the same datum.  Equality of the first two components says
\[
\mathcal E(B,r)=\mathcal E(C,s),
\qquad
Q_r(P)=Q_s(\widetilde P).
\]
Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity} applies directly to the realizable boundary-scale datum \((\mathcal E(B,r),Q_r(P))=(\mathcal E(C,s),Q_s(\widetilde P))\).  Its proof uses Theorem \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction} after the equality of capacities has fixed the scale, and gives \(r=s\), \(E_r=E_s\), and \(P=\widetilde P\).  This proves the scale and one-scale resonance clause.

From the lift hypotheses \(B_\ast\mu=C_\ast\nu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\) and the equality \(r=s\), functoriality of pushforward gives the common image identity
\[
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=(J_s\circ C)_\ast\nu=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right)=\mu_r.
\]
Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} supplies the converse equality criterion for the covered-boundary equilibrium problem, so the only possible variation inside this part of one fiber lies in the chosen finite Blaschke cover and the chosen measure on its Haar-lift fiber.  This proves the Blaschke lift clause.

With the recovered scale \(r\) fixed, Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection} applies to \(Q_r\) and gives, for every \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\), the unique monic outer factor of \(z^nQ_r(J_r(z))\) as exactly \(P\); the remaining factor has all zeros in the inner shell.  This proves the annular selection clause.

Conversely, fix \(r\), \(P\), and its transport polynomial \(Q_r(P)\).  If \(B\) is any finite Blaschke product and \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\), the first paragraph gives exactly \((\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r)\).  The preceding injectivity argument shows that no quadruple with a different scale or a different Lee--Yang source can lie in this fiber.  Therefore the displayed fiber is complete, and \(\overline{\mathfrak D}\) is injective as well as surjective.
\end{proof}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Several themes emerging from the core sections deserve emphasis. First, the Joukowsky ellipse is not merely a convenient parametrized curve: it is a conformal boundary object whose logarithmic capacity, annular modulus, and Mahler averages are all controlled by the same scale parameter. In this sense, the transport identity of Theorem \ref{thm:pullback-factorization} and the degree-two capacity formula of Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-skeleton} should be viewed as potential-theoretic bridges between circle data and ellipse data, parallel to the classical role of conformal maps in logarithmic potential theory \citep{Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,Pommerenke1992}. The discriminant and resultant formulas show that the same bridge also preserves delicate elimination-theoretic information \citep{GKZ1994}.

Second, the endpoint synthesis statements, the boundary-scale rigidity theorem, and the exact fiber classification corollary are closure devices for the main transport package.  The novelty lies earlier in the capacity pullback kernel with its branch/collision terms, the degree-two boundary operativity theorem, the sharp Fourier deficit on \(E_r\), the finite Blaschke lift theorem, the inverse resonance classification, the no-resonance Lee--Yang fiber collapse, and the explicit Rouch\'e envelope for annular recovery.  Those results imply that the covered-equilibrium supremum is \(\log r\), the transport polynomial is injective at fixed Lee--Yang scale, and the annular factor selection is stable.  Thus the potential-theoretic normalization and the algebraic inverse normalization determine the same ellipse, rather than two unrelated parameters; the remaining fiber is exactly the finite-cover Haar-lift ambiguity classified in Corollary \ref{cor:capacity-mahler-fiber-classification}.

The two appendices record calibrated arithmetic applications of this spine.  Appendix \ref{sec:appendix-scale-dictionary} begins with a formal secondary-calibration theorem proving that these coordinates factor through the already established capacity scale and cannot add a new endpoint gate to Corollary \ref{cor:capacity-mahler-fiber-classification}.  Appendix \ref{sec:elliptic-gates} studies the normalized speed spectrum of an elliptic covering and its \(p\)-adic slopes as data of a chosen covering coordinate.  Appendix \ref{sec:godel-lorentz} translates finitely supported prime-register data into the same Joukowsky capacity scale.  These applications are deliberately separated from the main theorem chain; finite-window spectral obstructions, outer-function rigidity, Lorentz representation rigidity, primorial extremality, higher-rank Cartan models, and codebook optimization belong to subsequent work.

\appsection{Common boundary scale dictionary}{sec:appendix-scale-dictionary}

The following theorem is a scale dictionary and a no-backflow statement for the optional application appendices.  It is not used to prove the analytic and algebraic spine above; that spine is supplied by Theorems \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-skeleton}, \ref{thm:degree-two-boundary-operativity}, \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability}, \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover}, \ref{thm:algebraic-mahler-transport-inverse-package}, \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction}, and \ref{thm:quantitative-annular-rouche-stability}.  The endpoint statements, Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}, Proposition \ref{prop:boundary-scale-two-gate-obstruction}, and Corollary \ref{cor:capacity-mahler-fiber-classification}, are closing consequences of that spine.

\begin{theorem}[Secondary calibration factorization]\label{thm:secondary-calibration-factorization}
For \(L\ge 1\), put
\[
\Gamma_L:=\bigl\{\,L^{1/2}z+L^{-1/2}z^{-1}:|z|=1\,\bigr\}.
\]
The boundary normalizations used in the theorem package above can be placed on this single confocal family by the following elementary changes of scale.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item If \(c\in(0,1)\) and \(m\in\NN\), then
  \[
  J_{c,m}(\TT)=c^{1/2}\Gamma_{c^{-1}}.
  \]
  The covering multiplicity \(m\) changes only the parametrization of this boundary, while the Fourier support and \(p\)-adic slopes in the Fourier-cover application section recover the same scale parameter through \(L=c^{-1}\).

  \item If \(r>1\), then the transport ellipse in the sharp-equilibrium, Blaschke-cover, and Mahler-transport theorems is
  \[
  E_r=J_r(\TT)=\Gamma_{r^2}.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
In this common notation the capacity normalization is not merely formal: for every \(L>1\),
\[
\operatorname{cap}(\Gamma_L)=L^{1/2},
\]
and hence
\[
\operatorname{cap}(J_{c,m}(\TT))=1,
\qquad
\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r.
\]
Thus the spectral, Mahler, and elimination probes in the main theorem share the same elementary boundary notation: after the explicit changes of scale
\[
L=c^{-1},\qquad L=r^2,
\]
they are written as probes of the same one-parameter Joukowsky boundary \(\Gamma_L\), up to the harmless homothety \(c^{1/2}\) in the elliptic-covering normalization.  This theorem is only a dictionary of notation and scale together with the formal factorization statement below; it neither substitutes for nor strengthens the precise capacity, equilibrium, inverse-transport, or fiber-classification theorems proved in the main sections.  The optional prime-register application repeats this dictionary locally in Appendix \ref{sec:godel-lorentz}.
Moreover this dictionary is formally secondary in the following sense.  Fix \(n\ge1\), let \(\mathfrak X_n\), \(\sim\), and \(\mathfrak D\) be the core objects of Corollary \ref{cor:capacity-mahler-fiber-classification}, and let \(\mathfrak X_n^{\rm sec}\) be obtained from \(\mathfrak X_n\) by adjoining any finite list of auxiliary coordinates whose boundary contribution is one of the displayed forms \(J_{c,m}(\TT)\) or \(\Gamma_L\).  Let
\[
\pi:\mathfrak X_n^{\rm sec}\longrightarrow\mathfrak X_n
\]
be the forgetting map and set \(\mathfrak D^{\rm sec}:=\mathfrak D\circ\pi\).  Then \(\mathfrak D^{\rm sec}\) is constant on the equivalence relation pulled back from \(\sim\) and induces exactly the same bijection as \(\overline{\mathfrak D}\).  In particular, adjoining the elliptic-cover Fourier coordinate, the \(p\)-adic slope readout, or the capacity-scaled prime-register coordinate cannot change the recovered scale \(r\), the Lee--Yang source polynomial \(P\), the Haar-lift fiber, or the two-gate obstruction of Proposition \ref{prop:boundary-scale-two-gate-obstruction}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(z=e^{i\phi}\),
\[
\Gamma_L=\bigl\{(L^{1/2}+L^{-1/2})\cos\phi+i(L^{1/2}-L^{-1/2})\sin\phi:\phi\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\bigr\},
\]
so \(\Gamma_L\) is exactly the confocal Joukowsky ellipse with focal distance \(2\).  For the elliptic covering, take \(L=c^{-1}\) and set \(z=e^{im\theta}\).  Then
\[
c^{1/2}\bigl(L^{1/2}z+L^{-1/2}z^{-1}\bigr)
=c^{1/2}\bigl(c^{-1/2}e^{im\theta}+c^{1/2}e^{-im\theta}\bigr)
=e^{im\theta}+c e^{-im\theta},
\]
which proves \(J_{c,m}(\TT)=c^{1/2}\Gamma_{c^{-1}}\).  Because \(\theta\mapsto e^{im\theta}\) is an \(m\)-fold covering of \(\TT\), the integer \(m\) affects only how the same ellipse is traversed; the scale is still determined by \(c\).  This proves the asserted scale factorization for the elliptic-covering coordinates.

Finally, by definition,
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}=(r^2)^{1/2}z+(r^2)^{-1/2}z^{-1},
\]
so \(J_r(\TT)=\Gamma_{r^2}\).  The two asserted changes of scale therefore put the relevant boundaries in the single family \(\Gamma_L\), and the only additional operation in the first model is the explicitly displayed scalar homothety.

It remains to verify the capacity normalization.  If \(L>1\), then \(\Gamma_L=E_{L^{1/2}}\), and Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} gives \(\operatorname{cap}(\Gamma_L)=L^{1/2}\).  Logarithmic capacity scales under homotheties by \(\operatorname{cap}(aK)=|a|\operatorname{cap}(K)\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{cap}(J_{c,m}(\TT))=
\operatorname{cap}(c^{1/2}\Gamma_{c^{-1}})=c^{1/2}c^{-1/2}=1,
\]
while \(E_r=\Gamma_{r^2}\) gives the other displayed capacity identity.  These identities are the promised nonredundant compatibility assertion: the boundary parameter \(L\) and the Mahler transport scale \(r\) induce the same logarithmic-capacity scale after the explicitly stated normalizations.

It remains only to prove the no-backflow assertion.  By definition \(\mathfrak D^{\rm sec}=\mathfrak D\circ\pi\), so two secondary objects have the same secondary datum if and only if their core projections have the same core datum.  The equivalence relation on \(\mathfrak X_n^{\rm sec}\) is the pullback of \(\sim\), hence Corollary \ref{cor:capacity-mahler-fiber-classification} gives the induced bijection
\[
\mathfrak X_n^{\rm sec}/\pi^{-1}(\!\sim\!)\ \cong
\left\{\bigl(\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r\bigr): r>1,
\ P\in\mathcal L,
\ \deg P=n\right\}.
\]
The theorem and propositions in the two application appendices attach only the auxiliary scale coordinates just computed: the Fourier-cover parameter contributes \(c\) and a covering multiplicity lying in the forgotten fiber, and the prime-register parameter contributes the same \(L=N(s)\) capacity scale through \(\Gamma_L\).  Since the core datum after forgetting is unchanged, the recovered \(r\), the recovered Lee--Yang polynomial \(P\), and the Haar-lift fiber are exactly those supplied by Corollary \ref{cor:capacity-mahler-fiber-classification}.  Finally, realizability and nonrealizability of endpoint data are decided after projection by Proposition \ref{prop:boundary-scale-two-gate-obstruction}; therefore no auxiliary coordinate can create a third gate or remove either of the two existing gates.
\end{proof}

\appsection{Calibrated elliptic projections and \texorpdfstring{$p$}{p}-adic slope readout}{sec:elliptic-gates}

This appendix records only the calibrated Fourier calculation for the one-parameter family \(J_{c,m}\).  The observable belongs to the normalized covering coordinate; it is not an invariant of the unparametrized ellipse under arbitrary reparametrization.  No finite-window obstruction, outer-function rigidity, or full parametrized-gate reconstruction theorem is used in the main paper.

In this appendix, \(\mathbb P\) denotes the set of prime numbers.  For \(x\in\QQ^\times\) and \(p\in\mathbb P\), \(\nu_p(x)\) is the normalized \(p\)-adic valuation, characterized by \(\nu_p(p)=1\) and additivity under multiplication.

\begin{proposition}[Normalized speed spectrum of an elliptic covering]\label{prop:short-elliptic-speed-spectrum}
Let \(0<c<1\), let \(m\in\NN\), and put
\[
J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})=e^{im\theta}+c e^{-im\theta}.
\]
Define the logarithmic speed defect by
\[
u_{c,m}(\theta):=\log\left|\frac{\dd}{\dd\theta}J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})\right|-
\log m.
\]
Then
\[
u_{c,m}(\theta)=\frac12\log\bigl(1-2c\cos(2m\theta)+c^2\bigr)
=-\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c^n}{n}\cos(2mn\theta),
\]
and its Fourier coefficients are
\[
\widehat u_{c,m}(0)=0,
\qquad
\widehat u_{c,m}(\pm 2mn)=-\frac{c^n}{2n}\quad(n\ge1),
\qquad
\widehat u_{c,m}(k)=0\quad(k\notin2m\ZZ).
\]
If \(c\in\QQ\), then for every prime \(p\) and every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\nu_p\!\bigl(\widehat u_{c,m}(2mn)\bigr)=n\nu_p(c)-\nu_p(2n).
\]
Consequently the normalized Fourier family determines the support lattice \(2m\ZZ\), the covering multiplicity \(m\), and the ellipse scale \(c=-2\widehat u_{c,m}(2m)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating gives
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd\theta}J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})=im\bigl(e^{im\theta}-c e^{-im\theta}\bigr),
\]
so
\[
u_{c,m}(\theta)=\log|e^{im\theta}-c e^{-im\theta}|=\frac12\log|1-ce^{-2im\theta}|^2.
\]
Since \(|c|<1\), the logarithmic series is uniformly convergent on the circle:
\[
\log(1-ce^{-2im\theta})=-\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c^n}{n}e^{-2imn\theta}.
\]
Adding its complex conjugate and dividing by two gives the displayed cosine expansion and therefore the Fourier coefficients.  The valuation formula follows from additivity of \(\nu_p\) and from \(\nu_p(-1)=0\).  The nonzero support is exactly \(2m\ZZ\setminus\{0\}\), so it determines \(m\); the first positive coefficient then gives \(c=-2\widehat u_{c,m}(2m)\).
\end{proof}

\appsection{Calibrated capacity-scaled multiplicative data}{sec:godel-lorentz}

This appendix records a second calibrated application: finitely supported prime registers can be used as capacity parameters for the same Joukowsky ellipse family.  The Lorentz matrix is only a coordinate dictionary for this scale.  No representation-rigidity, Cayley-addition, primorial-extremality, ellipsoid-volume, or higher-rank arithmetic-register theorem is asserted here.

Continue to write \(\mathbb P\) for the set of prime numbers and \(\nu_p\) for the normalized \(p\)-adic valuation; set \(\nu_p(0)=+\infty\).  The prime-register monoid is
\[
\mathsf P:=\bigl\{\,s:\mathbb P\to\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}\;:\;s(p)=0\text{ for all but finitely many }p\,\bigr\},
\]
with pointwise addition and norm
\[
N(s):=\prod_{p\in\mathbb P}p^{s(p)}\in\NN_{>0}.
\]
If \(a>1\) is a square-free integer, its exponent register is the element \(s_a\in\mathsf P\) defined by \(s_a(p)=\nu_p(a)\).  Codebook optimization and primorial extremality for such registers are independent combinatorial questions and are not used below.

\begin{proposition}[Capacity-scaled prime-register dictionary]\label{prop:short-capacity-prime-register-dictionary}
For a prime register \(s\in\mathsf P\), put \(L=N(s)\),
\[
E_J(s):=\bigl\{L^{1/2}z+L^{-1/2}z^{-1}:|z|=1\bigr\},
\]
and
\[
\Lambda(s):=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{L^{1/2}+L^{-1/2}}{2}&\dfrac{L^{1/2}-L^{-1/2}}{2}\\[2pt]
\dfrac{L^{1/2}-L^{-1/2}}{2}&\dfrac{L^{1/2}+L^{-1/2}}{2}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(E_J(s)=\Gamma_{N(s)}\), \(\Lambda(s)\in\mathrm{SO}^+(1,1)\), and
\[
\Lambda(s)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cosh\Delta(s)&\sinh\Delta(s)\\
\sinh\Delta(s)&\cosh\Delta(s)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Delta(s)=\frac12\log N(s).
\]
Moreover \(\Lambda(s_1+s_2)=\Lambda(s_1)\Lambda(s_2)\),
\[
\rho(\Lambda(s))=N(s)^{1/2},
\qquad
\kappa_2(\Lambda(s))=N(s),
\qquad
\operatorname{cap}(E_J(s))=N(s)^{1/2},
\]
and, for \(N(s)>1\),
\[
\operatorname{Area}(E_J(s))=
\pi\bigl(N(s)-N(s)^{-1}\bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identity \(E_J(s)=\Gamma_{N(s)}\) is the definition.  Put \(\Delta(s)=\frac12\log N(s)\).  Then \(L^{1/2}=e^{\Delta(s)}\) and \(L^{-1/2}=e^{-\Delta(s)}\), so the displayed matrix is the standard Lorentz boost with entries \(\cosh\Delta(s)\) and \(\sinh\Delta(s)\).  Since \(N(s_1+s_2)=N(s_1)N(s_2)\), rapidities add, giving the homomorphism law.  The eigenvalues are \(e^{\pm\Delta(s)}\), hence the spectral radius is \(N(s)^{1/2}\) and the Euclidean condition number is \(e^{2\Delta(s)}=N(s)\).  The ellipse \(E_J(s)\) has semiaxes \(L^{1/2}+L^{-1/2}\) and \(L^{1/2}-L^{-1/2}\), so its area is \(\pi(L-L^{-1})\).  Its exterior conformal map has leading coefficient \(L^{1/2}\), giving \(\operatorname{cap}(E_J(s))=L^{1/2}\); equivalently this is Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} with \(r=L^{1/2}\).
\end{proof}
