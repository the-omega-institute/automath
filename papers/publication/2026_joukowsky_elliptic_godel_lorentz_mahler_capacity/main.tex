\begin{abstract}
\hbadness=10000
\emergencystretch=2em
We develop an exact potential-theoretic and algebraic calculus for the
Joukowsky ellipse \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), where
\(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\) and \(r>1\).  Every inverse theorem uses the
exact algebraic coefficient-vector model later fixed in Definition
\ref{def:transport-coefficient-observations}; any shell data enter only
after those vectors are known.  Analytically, the standard
pushforward variational principle for logarithmic capacity is used only
through the lower-truncated pullback kernel for a rational map
\(R=A/B\); after the specialization
\[
J_r(z)=J_r(t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z=t
\ \text{or}\ zt=r^{-2},
\]
the second collision locus is disjoint from \(\TT^2\).  The all-measure
variational consequence is
\(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\), with equilibrium measure
\(J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/2\pi)\) and sharp bound
\(I(J_{r\ast}\eta)\le\log r\) for every probability measure \(\eta\) on
\(\TT\).  Separately, for finite-energy source measures only, one has the
Fourier identity
\[
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu)
=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|^2+
r^{-2k}\operatorname{Re}(\widehat\mu(k)^2)}{k}.
\]
The all-measure bound has equality only for Haar measure, and the finite
Blaschke-cover version identifies the equilibrium lifts as the full
Haar-lift fiber \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\) while giving an explicit
zero-product formula for the canonical lift's source energy.

Algebraically, a source polynomial \(P\) with nonzero constant term has
a transported polynomial \(Q_r\) characterized by
\[
r^nz^nQ_r(J_r(z))=P(z)P^\vee(r^2z).
\]
For unit-circle sources this creates the two separated shells
\(\TT\) and \(r^{-2}\TT\), and the same identity yields the stated
Mahler, resultant, and discriminant transport formulas.

The inverse theorems are exactly the complete-vector algebraic
statements just described.  Their inputs are the ordered coefficient
vectors of the transported polynomials, or the explicitly stated shell
data after such vectors are known, in the sense of Definition
\ref{def:transport-coefficient-observations}.  Under this input model we classify the
one-scale fibers by the involution \(z\mapsto r^{-2}/z\), reduce the
two-scale common inverse problem to a finite perfect-matching graph with
a generic nonresonant injectivity corollary, and use \(n+1\)-scale
Laurent interpolation in the coefficient-vector proof without
genericity; the \(n+1\) count is a sharp interpolation budget for the
coefficient-vector proof, not a sharp source-recovery lower bound for
every algebraically constrained source class.

The final synthesis combines the analytic and algebraic layers only
after the Joukowsky specialization above.  A quantitative annular
Rouch\'e theorem selects the unique contour-selected annular factor once
the model \((r,P)\) and separating contours are fixed, and from the same
exact coefficient-vector and shell data one recovers the fixed-scale
object \((\log r,E_r,J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/2\pi),Q_r(P),P)\); the
remaining measure-theoretic ambiguity is precisely the finite-Blaschke
Haar-lift fiber.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
The classical Joukowsky map is a basic conformal mechanism by which a
circle gives an ellipse, and it gives a particularly transparent test
case for the interaction of logarithmic capacity, boundary energy, and
algebraic elimination \citep{Pommerenke1992,Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,SaffTotik1997,StahlTotik1992,GKZ1994}.  We work throughout with
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},\qquad r>1,
\]
and write \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\).  The elementary factorization
\[
J_r(z)-J_r(t)=(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right)
\]
separates the boundary diagonal from the reciprocal collision locus
\(zt=r^{-2}\).  Since this second locus misses \(\TT^2\), the boundary
energy on \(E_r\) descends to the circle logarithmic kernel plus one
bounded Fourier-explicit factor.  The same reciprocal scale later
appears in the polynomial identity
\[
r^nz^nQ_r(J_r(z))=P(z)P^\vee(r^2z),
\]
where it separates the pulled-back divisor into the two shells
\(\TT\) and \(r^{-2}\TT\).  The paper is organized around this common
scale in capacity, Fourier stability, finite Blaschke covers, Mahler
transport, inverse reconstruction, and annular Rouch\'e stability.

The results divide into four mathematical modules.
Throughout the inverse module, the word ``reconstruction'' has the
fixed meaning of Definition
\ref{def:transport-coefficient-observations}: exact algebraic recovery
from complete ordered coefficient vectors of transported polynomials, or
from shell data only after those complete vectors have already been
obtained.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Lower-truncated pullback kernels and Joukowsky capacity.}
  We use the rational-map pullback-kernel interface, recorded in
  Appendix~\ref{app:auxiliary-rational-pullbacks}, for maps \(R=A/B\)
  on compact nonpolar sets avoiding poles, using
  \[
  \Psi_R(z,t)=\frac{A(z)B(t)-A(t)B(z)}{z-t}.
  \]
  This appendix material is a bookkeeping interface for the single
  lower-truncated kernel \(\log|R(z)-R(t)|\), together with the standard
  pushforward variational formula for capacity.  Its Joukowsky boundary
  specialization gives \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\), identifies the
  equilibrium measure, and leads to the main analytic theorem: the
  sharp Fourier deficit with optimal constants.
  \item \emph{Finite Blaschke covers.}  For \(J_r\circ B\), the boundary
  energy depends on a source measure only through \(B_\ast\mu\).  The
  equality class is the full Haar-lift fiber
  \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).  The canonical absolutely continuous
  member of that fiber has source energy given by an explicit product
  over the Blaschke zeros.
  \item \emph{Mahler transport and inverse reconstruction.}  The
  transport polynomial \(Q_r\) carries Jensen--Mahler means,
  resultants, and discriminants.  At one scale the full fiber is
  described by replacing selected roots \(z\) by \(r^{-2}/z\) subject
  to a finite product resonance.  At two distinct scales, the common
  fiber is the set of perfect matchings in an explicit candidate graph
  built from the two transported root multisets; outside the resonances
  \(z_i z_j=r^{-2}\), \(z_i z_j=s^{-2}\), and
  \(z_j=(r/s)^2z_i\), this matching is unique for squarefree sources.
  Exact complete coefficient vectors at \(n+1\) distinct scales still give
  the uniform Laurent-interpolation coefficient-vector proof without
  genericity; this \(n+1\)-scale count is a sharp interpolation budget
  for that coefficient-vector proof, not a sharp source-recovery lower
  bound for every algebraically constrained source class.
  \item \emph{Annular stability and synthesis.}  Under the unit-circle
  source-root separation, an explicit Rouch\'e envelope on contours
  between \(|z|=r^{-2}\) and \(|z|=1\) determines a unique
  contour-selected annular factor, local to the already fixed model
  \((r,P)\) and those contours.  The final recognition results combine the capacity
  coordinate, the Fourier scale weights, the source-factor inverse, and
  the Blaschke equality class to recover
  \[
  \bigl(\log r,E_r,J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/2\pi),Q_r(P),P\bigr)
  \]
  with precisely the finite-Blaschke Haar-lift fiber remaining.
\end{enumerate}

\[
J_r(z)=J_r(t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z=t
\ \text{or}\ zt=r^{-2},
\]
because on \(\TT^2\) the second collision graph disappears while the same
reciprocal scale \(r^{-2}\) remains visible in the algebraic pullback

the finite-energy class; the unrestricted measure statement is only the
separate variational bound.  The finite-Blaschke theorem uses only the
classical boundary-covering property of finite Blaschke products plus these

Haar-lift equality class and canonical-lift energy in the present

the classical zero-counting theorem only locally, after the model and
separating contours have already been fixed.  The final synthesis is
therefore not a parallel theorem list: it is the compatibility of these
classical inputs and paper-specific fixed-scale recombinations along the
single chain displayed above.

\subsection*{Related work and novelty boundary}
The classical inputs are the variational definition of logarithmic
capacity and equilibrium measure, harmonic measure from infinity,
Jensen--Mahler identities, Poisson-kernel Fourier coefficients,
finite Blaschke product boundary theory
\citep[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970} and \citep{Colwell1985}, standard resultants
and discriminants \citep[Chap.~12]{GKZ1994} and \citep{CoxLittleOShea2005}, and
Rouch\'e's theorem in its usual contour-counting form
\citep[pp.~152--153]{Ahlfors1979}.  The Joukowsky ellipse itself is a
standard conformal object in this setting
\citep{Landkof1972,Pommerenke1992,Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,SaffTotik1997,StahlTotik1992}.
Classical Faber-polynomial theory begins with Faber's polynomial
expansions and the later Faber-series literature
\citep{Faber1903,Curtiss1971,Suetin1998}.  For ellipses and rational exterior
maps, \(J_r\) enters the Faber/Chebyshev relation and its rational-map
generalizations \citep{Liesen2001}; for Joukowski airfoils, recent
work studies zero distributions of Faber polynomials and electrostatic
skeletons \citep{LevenbergWielonsky2020}.  These
Faber works are adjacent to the present paper because they use the
same exterior map or nearby Joukowsky geometries, but they do not
address the lower-truncated boundary energy, finite-Blaschke
Haar-lift equality class, or exact transport inverse chain studied
below.

The finite Blaschke facts used here are also classical at the level of
boundary covering and Poisson-kernel derivative formulas; modern
accounts and surveys place these maps in a broader complex-analytic,
operator-theoretic, and interpolation context
\citep{Colwell1985,GarciaMashreghiRoss2017,GarciaMashreghiRoss2018}.
Recent adjacent work on rational inner functions develops Clark
measures, Dirichlet-type norms, stable-polynomial numerators, product
domain boundary behavior, and several-variable analogues of finite
Blaschke products
\citep{FelderLe2022,BickelCimaSola2023,Bergqvist2023,AndersonBergqvistBickelCimaSola2024,Jacobsson2024ClarkProduct,Knese2024RationalInnerSurvey,BickelKnesePascoeSola2025}.
In particular, recent Clark-measure work for product functions,
compositions of inner functions, and multiplicative embeddings on
polydiscs \citep{Jacobsson2024ClarkProduct} is the closest current
measure-theoretic context for comparing finite-cover fibers under
products or compositions.
Those papers are close in boundary function theory and finite-cover
language, but they address rational-inner structure rather than the
one-variable Joukowsky lower-truncated energy factorization used here.  Our
finite-cover contribution is not a new theory of Blaschke products. It
is the specialization of the Joukowsky boundary energy after the
single pushforward \(B_\ast\mu\), with equality exactly for the full
Haar-lift fiber \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\), and with the canonical
absolutely continuous member's energy written in the normalization used by
the rest of the paper.

Capacity computation is a separate adjacent literature.  Numerical
methods based on lemniscatic-domain conformal maps, log-lightning
approximations, and charge simulation now compute logarithmic
capacities for broad classes of compact sets
\citep{LiesenSeteNasser2017,BaddooTrefethen2021,LiesenNasserSete2023}.
Recent extremal and shape-optimization questions for logarithmic or
Riesz capacities study how capacity changes under linear images and
under capacity-ratio optimization \citep{Laugesen2022,ClarkLaugesen2025}.
Those works are computational and general-purpose; here the capacity
formula \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\) is the classical Joukowsky
normalization, used as one coordinate in an exact compatibility chain.
Similarly, Mahler measure enters through Jensen's formula and the
standard one- and several-variable Mahler literature
\citep{Mahler1962,Smyth1981,Boyd1981,EverestWard1999,BrunaultZudilin2020,McKeeSmyth2021}.
Recent work relating Mahler measures of exact polynomials to
special \(L\)-values of \(K3\) surfaces \citep{Trieu2024K3Mahler}
is adjacent current context for Mahler-measure methods, but it concerns
motivic and \(K3\)-surface structures rather than the one-variable
Joukowsky boundary skeleton or the fixed-scale transport inverse used
here.  The stable-polynomial context is background for unit-circle zero
conditions and transport
\citep{RahmanSchmeisser2002,BorceaBranden2009,LeeYang1952I,LeeYang1952II}.
The elimination-theoretic use of resultants and discriminants is likewise
standard: the paper fixes the Sylvester normalization and then applies
one-variable specialization identities, rather than developing the
general multivariate resultant formalism
\citep{Jouanolou1991,GKZ1994,CoxLittleOShea2005}.

The one-scale inverse fiber also sits next to the classical
phase-retrieval and spectral-factorization ambiguity literature.  In
one-dimensional Fourier phase retrieval, equality of the autocorrelation
or Fourier magnitude determines a polynomial or entire function only up
to choices among zero pairs reflected by the reciprocal-conjugate
operation, together with the usual translation, modulation, and
conjugate-reflection symmetries; this zero-flipping viewpoint goes back
to the one-dimensional classifications of Akutowicz, Walther, and
Hofstetter and appears in later signal-reconstruction accounts
\citep{Akutowicz1956,Walther1963,Hofstetter1964,Hayes1982}.  Spectral
factorization has the same structural ambiguity: a nonnegative
trigonometric polynomial or finite autocorrelation fixes reciprocal
zero pairs, while stable or minimum-phase conventions choose one member
from each pair \citep{OppenheimSchafer2010}.  The involution
\(z\mapsto r^{-2}/z\) in Lemma
\ref{lem:one-scale-orbit-fiber-matching} is therefore not presented as
a new abstract zero-flipping phenomenon.  The increment here is the
Joukowsky-transport normalization
\(r^nz^nQ_r(J_r(z))=P(z)P^\vee(r^2z)\), the exact product-resonance
condition in that normalization, and the two-scale matching graph that
matches two differently scaled reciprocal choices.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:preliminaries}

\paragraph{Lower-Truncated Energy.}
All logarithmic energies in the paper use one global convention.  If \(X\subset\CC\) is compact and \(\sigma\) is a Borel probability measure on \(X\), put
\[
I_X^{(M)}(\sigma):=\iint_{X\times X}\max\{\log|z-t|,-M\}\,\dd\sigma(z)\dd\sigma(t)
\qquad(M>0)
\]
and define
\[
I_X(\sigma):=\lim_{M\to\infty}I_X^{(M)}(\sigma)\in[-\infty,\infty).
\]
The kernels decrease as \(M\) increases, so this lower-truncated limit is well-defined; a measure is called \emph{finite-energy} when \(I_X(\sigma)>-\infty\).  When the compact set is clear from context we write simply \(I(\sigma)\).  More generally, if \(L\) is an upper-bounded Borel kernel on a compact metric space, its integral is always the monotone lower-truncated value \(\lim_{M\to\infty}\iint\max\{L,-M\}\).  If \(b\) is bounded and Borel, then bounded terms are separated only after this single-kernel truncation:
\[
\iint(L+b)\,\dd\mu\dd\mu=\iint L\,\dd\mu\dd\mu+\iint b\,\dd\mu\dd\mu,
\]
with the first integral on each side understood by lower truncation.  This convention forbids termwise integration of singular logarithmic factors before the complete pullback kernel has been truncated.  For a rational map \(R\) with no pole on \(X\), an \emph{admissible finite-energy lift} means a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(X\) for which \(I_{R(X)}(R_\ast\mu)>-\infty\), equivalently for which the lower-truncated pullback energy is finite.  This is not a condition on the ordinary logarithmic energy of \(\mu\) on the source set.  The pullback kernels below are always interpreted by this same lower-truncated convention, including diagonal, branch, and off-diagonal collision singularities.  Circular logarithmic means are the corresponding one-variable Lebesgue integrals, equivalently these lower-truncated limits; the logarithmic singularity at a zero of a nonzero polynomial is locally integrable, so Jensen's formula applies with this convention.  On the unit circle we also write
\[
I_{\TT}(\sigma):=\iint_{\TT\times\TT}\log|z-t|\,\dd\sigma(z)\dd\sigma(t)
\]
for the same lower-truncated energy.  If \(\widehat\sigma(k)=\int_{\TT}z^{-k}\,\dd\sigma(z)\), then the circle Fourier identity proved by Abel regularization in Lemma \ref{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation}, and consistent with the classical Hardy-space kernel expansion \citep[Chap.~1]{Duren1970}, is
\[
I_{\TT}(\sigma)=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat\sigma(k)|^2}{k},
\]
with the series understood as a finite real number exactly in the finite-energy case.  Abel regularization of the circle kernel is used below only as a proof device for this same lower-truncated limit under the finite-energy hypothesis.  Ordinary logarithmic means such as \(\int_{\TT}\log|F(z)|\,\dd\sigma(z)\) are always the corresponding lower-truncated limits unless the integrand is continuous on the support.

\paragraph{Boundary and Coefficient Notation.}
For \(r>0\), we use the two-scale Joukowsky map
\[
J_r(z):=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},
\qquad
E_r:=J_r(\TT),
\qquad
\TT:=\{e^{i\theta}:\theta\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\}.
\]
If \(f\in L^1(\TT)\), its Fourier coefficients are
\[
\widehat f(k):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} f(\theta)e^{-ik\theta}\,\dd\theta
\qquad (k\in\ZZ).
\]

The second boundary model is the algebraic transport through \(J_r\). Throughout the algebraic transport statements, \(K\) denotes a field of characteristic zero; whenever absolute values, circles, ellipses, or logarithmic integrals are used, the notation is specialized to a subfield of \(\CC\), and in the analytic Mahler section to \(\CC\) itself.  All roots of polynomials over \(K\) are taken in a fixed algebraic closure \(\overline K\), always with multiplicity.  Repeated roots are allowed, and in that case the relevant discriminant is zero.  For a degree-\(n\) polynomial \(F(x)=b_n\prod_{j=1}^n(x-\alpha_j)\) with \(b_n\ne0\), we use the resultant normalization
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_x(F):=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}b_n^{-1}\operatorname{Res}_x(F,F')
=b_n^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)^2,
\]
where the displayed root formula is interpreted in \(\overline K\); its value lies in \(K\).  The resultant is the standard Sylvester resultant in the displayed order; equivalently, for every polynomial \(A(w)\) and every affine linear polynomial \(a(z)w+b(z)\) in the variable \(w\),
\[
\operatorname{Res}_w\bigl(A(w),a(z)w+b(z)\bigr)=(-a(z))^{\deg A}A\!\left(-\frac{b(z)}{a(z)}\right).
\]
In particular it satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Res}_x\bigl(A(x),c(\beta-x)\bigr)=c^{\deg A}A(\beta)
\]
for every polynomial \(A\) and scalar \(c\).  Equivalently,
\[
\operatorname{Res}_x(A(x),c(x-\beta))=(-c)^{\deg A}A(\beta).
\]
In particular, because \(r z^2-wz+r^{-1}=z\bigl(J_r(z)-w\bigr)\) is linear in \(w\), this convention gives
\[
\operatorname{Res}_w(Q_r(w),rz^2-wz+r^{-1})=z^nQ_r(J_r(z)),
\]
not \((-z)^nQ_r(J_r(z))\).  If \(K\subseteq\CC\), the same symbols may also be evaluated at complex points.  If \(K\) is arbitrary, identities involving \(J_r(z)\) are identities in the Laurent polynomial ring over \(K(r)\) after adjoining the displayed variable \(z^{\pm1}\).  The following convention fixes the normalization used everywhere below.

\begin{definition}[Reciprocal and transport normalization]\label{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}
Let
\[
P(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k u^k=a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(u-z_j)\in K[u],
\qquad
n\ge 1,
\qquad
 a_n a_0\ne0,
\]
with roots taken in a fixed algebraic closure of \(K\).  We use the scalar conventions of Table~\ref{tab:normalization-ledger}: the monic transport associated with the zero divisor of \(P\) is
\[
\widetilde Q_r(w):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(w-r z_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\Bigr).
\]
All scalar normalizations of the transported zero divisor are converted through this monic polynomial.  Its scaled version used in the resultant identities is
\[
Q_r(w):=a_0\widetilde Q_r(w).
\]
Thus \(Q_r\) has leading coefficient \(a_0\), while \(\widetilde Q_r\) is monic.  If \(P\) is monic, then
\[
a_0=(-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^n z_j.
\]
The reciprocal polynomial is the root-normalized reciprocal
\[
P^{\vee}(u):=a_n^{-1}u^nP(1/u)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-u z_j),
\]
Thus ``root-normalized'' means normalized to have constant coefficient \(1\).  The remaining leading-coefficient, monic-source, and projective-comparison conventions are those in Table~\ref{tab:normalization-ledger}.  For an indeterminate \(r\), or for \(r\in K^\times\), the expressions above lie in \(K(r)[w]\) by Lemma \ref{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Exact complete coefficient-vector transport observations]\label{def:transport-coefficient-observations}
Let
\[
Q_r(w)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_k(r)w^{n-k},
\qquad A_0(r)=a_0\ne0,
\]
be a degree-\(n\) Joukowsky transport polynomial in the normalization
of Table~\ref{tab:normalization-ledger}.  Every
inverse theorem in the paper uses complete ordered coefficient-vector
observations at each scale; this is an exact algebraic data model.  The
allowed normalization conventions are the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Full-normalized coefficient data.}
The observed object at \(r_i\) is the ordered coefficient vector
\((A_0(r_i),\dots,A_n(r_i))\) of \(Q_{r_i}\).  The common leading
coefficient is visible at every scale: \(A_0(r_i)=a_0\).  Reconstruction
interpolates \(A_k(r)\) and divides the top Laurent coefficient by
\(a_0\).
\item \emph{Projective monic-normalized coefficient data.}
The observed object at \(r_i\) is a nonzero scalar multiple
\(\lambda_i(A_0(r_i),\dots,A_n(r_i))\), where \(\lambda_i\ne0\) may
depend on \(i\).  Dividing by the leading entry \(\lambda_i a_0\ne0\)
gives the monic transport \(\widetilde Q_{r_i}=a_0^{-1}Q_{r_i}\); one
then interpolates \(\widetilde A_k(r)\), recovers \(e_k\) from its top
Laurent coefficient, and recovers \(a_0=[r^n]\widetilde A_n(r)\).
\item \emph{Complete-vector requirement.}
The inverse theorems use only the complete coefficient vectors just
described.  Point samples, noisy samples, moment data, and truncated
coefficient windows are not inverse data unless they have first been
converted exactly to one of the complete ordered coefficient-vector
forms in \textup{(i)} or \textup{(ii)}.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the projective normalization is well defined exactly because the
observed object is the complete ordered coefficient vector of a
transported degree-\(n\) zero divisor, whose leading entry is nonzero.
The normalization algorithm used later is Lemma
\ref{lem:full-monic-multiscale-normalization}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient descent for the transport polynomial]\label{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}
In Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, the expression
\[
Q_r(w)=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(w-r z_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)
\]
is independent of the ordering of the roots and belongs to \(K(r)[w]\).  The same conclusion holds after specializing \(r\in K^\times\), in which case \(Q_r\in K[w]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to prove that each coefficient of \(Q_r\) lies in the indicated coefficient field.  The coefficient of \(w^{n-\ell}\) is \((-1)^\ell a_0\) times the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(\ell\) in the quantities \(r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\).  Expanding this elementary symmetric polynomial gives a finite \(\ZZ[r,r^{-1}]\)-linear combination of monomials
\[
z_1^{e_1}\cdots z_n^{e_n},
\qquad e_j\in\{-1,0,1\},
\]
summed over subsets in a way that is invariant under every permutation of the root labels.  Since \(a_0\ne0\), all roots are nonzero, and multiplication by a sufficiently large power of \(z_1\cdots z_n\) converts each such symmetric Laurent polynomial into an ordinary symmetric polynomial in \(z_1,
\dots,z_n\).  By the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials, it is a polynomial expression in the elementary symmetric functions of the roots and in \((z_1\cdots z_n)^{-1}\), with coefficients in \(\ZZ[r,r^{-1}]\).

The elementary symmetric functions of the \(z_j\) are \((-1)^k a_{n-k}/a_n\), and \((z_1\cdots z_n)^{-1}=(-1)^n a_n/a_0\).  Hence every coefficient of \(Q_r\) belongs to \(K(r)\).  The construction used only symmetric expressions in the multiset of roots, so it is independent of the chosen ordering.  If \(r\in K^\times\) is specialized, the same formulas have coefficients in \(K\), proving \(Q_r\in K[w]\).
\end{proof}

The normalizations just fixed are used throughout the algebraic part of the article: the same normalization controls logarithmic means, zero shells, and one-scale resultants. We use the zero set notation \(\mathrm{Zero}(F)\) for a polynomial \(F\), and the standard resultant/discriminant notation \(\operatorname{Res}\) and \(\operatorname{Disc}\) \citep{GKZ1994}. For \(\rho>0\), we define the logarithmic circular mean
\[
\mathcal M_\rho(P):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|P(\rho e^{i\theta})|\,\dd\theta,
\]
so that \(\mathcal M_1(P)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of \(P\) \citep{Mahler1962,EverestWard1999}.

The next two identities are the algebraic input for the Mahler, discriminant, and one-scale reconstruction statements in Sections \ref{sec:mahler-transport} and \ref{sec:discriminant}.

\begin{theorem}[Pullback factorization]\label{thm:pullback-factorization}
Let \(P(z)=a_n\prod_{j=1}^n(z-z_j)\) with \(a_0a_n\ne0\), and let \(P^\vee\) and \(Q_r\) be normalized as in Table~\ref{tab:normalization-ledger}.  These ledger normalizations are part of the statement: \(P^\vee\) is root-normalized and \(Q_r\) is the scaled transport, not the monic transport \(\widetilde Q_r\).  Let \(r\) be an indeterminate or an element of \(K^\times\). Then \(Q_r(w)\in K(r)[w]\), and in \(K(r)[z,z^{-1}]\) one has
\[
\boxed{\;
r^n z^n\,Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)=P(z)\,P^\vee(r^2 z).
\;}
\]
If \(K\subseteq\CC\) and \(r\in K^\times\), the same identity may equivalently be evaluated at every \(z\in\CC^\times\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient assertion is Lemma \ref{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}. For each \(j\),
\[
J_r(z)-\bigl(r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\bigr)
=r(z-z_j)+r^{-1}(z^{-1}-z_j^{-1})
=\frac{(z-z_j)(r^2 z z_j-1)}{r z z_j}.
\]
Multiplying over \(j\) yields
\[
Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)
=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(z-z_j)(r^2 z z_j-1)}{r z z_j}
=r^{-n}z^{-n}\Bigl(a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
Since \(a_0=(-1)^n a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n} z_j\), we have \(a_0\prod_j z_j^{-1}=(-1)^n a_n\). Therefore
\[
Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)
=r^{-n}z^{-n}\,a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\cdot (-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
The first factor is \(P(z)\), while
\[
P^\vee(r^2 z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-r^2 z z_j)=(-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
Combining the two identities gives the stated factorization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Discriminant factorization]\label{thm:discriminant-factorization}
In the setting of Theorem \ref{thm:pullback-factorization},
\[
\boxed{\;
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)
=\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)\,
r^{-n(n-1)}
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2 z_i z_j-1)^2.
\;}
\]
The identity is taken in \(\overline K(r)\), and both sides are in fact elements of \(K(r)\).  It remains valid without assuming that \(P\) is separable; if roots collide, the corresponding discriminants vanish.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(w_j:=r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)=a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(w_i-w_j)^2.
\]
Using
\[
w_i-w_j
=r(z_i-z_j)+r^{-1}(z_i^{-1}-z_j^{-1})
=\frac{(z_i-z_j)(r^2 z_i z_j-1)}{r z_i z_j},
\]
we obtain
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)
=a_0^{2n-2}r^{-n(n-1)}
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i-z_j)^2
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2 z_i z_j-1)^2
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}.
\]
Now
\[
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}
=\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{n} z_i\Bigr)^{-2(n-1)},
\]
and \(a_0=(-1)^n a_n\prod_i z_i\) implies
\[
a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}=a_n^{2n-2}.
\]
Since
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)=a_n^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i-z_j)^2,
\]
with the same discriminant convention as in \(\operatorname{Disc}_w(Q_r)=a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{i<j}(w_i-w_j)^2\), no extra sign appears: the only sign from \(a_0=(-1)^na_n\prod_i z_i\) is raised to the even power \(2n-2\), and the transport contribution is exactly the displayed factor \(r^{-n(n-1)}\).
The desired identity follows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fixed-Scale Synthesis}\label{subsec:endpoint-synthesis}

The capacity, Fourier, Blaschke, and polynomial-transport inverse results
now determine the fixed Joukowsky scale from two complementary exact data
coordinates.  Corollary \ref{cor:joukowsky-package-synthesis} records the
boundary and transport ingredients at a fixed scale, using only results
already proved in Sections \ref{sec:rational-capacity}--\ref{sec:discriminant}.
The final record below is the single endpoint synthesis: the energy
coordinate fixes \(\log r\), the exact complete coefficient vector of the
transport fixes the unit-circle source at that scale, the local Rouch\'e
theorem gives only a contour-selected annular divisor after the model has
already been fixed, and the finite-cover ambiguity remains exactly the
Haar-lift fiber.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-scale Joukowsky capacity--transport package]\label{cor:joukowsky-package-synthesis}
Let \(r>1\), let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of positive degree,
and let \(P\in\mathcal L\) be monic of degree \(n\).  Put
\[
Q=Q_r(P),\qquad
S_{r,Q}(z)=z^nQ(J_r(z)),
\qquad
\mu_r:=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right).
\]
Then the boundary side and the transport side have the same fixed scale
\(r\) in the following precise sense:
\[
\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)}I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=\log r,
\qquad
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=\mu_r
\quad\text{for every Haar lift }B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Moreover the pulled-back transport polynomial satisfies
\[
r^nS_{r,Q}(z)=P(z)P^\vee(r^2z)
=r^{2n}P(z)\,z^nP(r^{-2}z^{-1}),
\]
equivalently
\[
S_{r,Q}(z)=r^nP(z)\,z^nP(r^{-2}z^{-1}).
\]
Here \(P^\vee(u)=u^nP(1/u)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-u z_j)\) for
\(P(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\); this is the monic specialization in
Table~\ref{tab:normalization-ledger}, not an additional scalar
convention.  If \(a_0=P(0)=(-1)^n\prod_j z_j\), then Lemma
\ref{lem:unit-circle-reciprocal-shell-scalar} gives
\[
z^nP(r^{-2}z^{-1})
=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-r^{-2}z_j^{-1}),
\]
so the reflected-shell product carries this unimodular scalar in the
compact reciprocal-polynomial factor.

Consequently the zero divisor of \(S_{r,Q}\) is the disjoint union of the
unit-circle divisor of \(P\) and its reflected shell on \(r^{-2}\TT\).  For
every \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\), \(P\) is the unique monic degree-\(n\) factor of
\(S_{r,Q}\) whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\).  After a model \((r,P)\)
and contours \(r^{-2}<\rho_-<\rho_+<1\) have been fixed, the Rouch\'e
envelope \(\Delta(r,P;\rho_-,\rho_+)\) of Theorem
\ref{thm:quantitative-annular-rouche-stability} gives the corresponding
local contour-selected annular factor for nearby transported polynomials;
exact realizability still requires the separate closed algebraic
unit-circle and reciprocal-shell conditions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The boundary statements are Theorem
\ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover}: the covered equilibrium
supremum is \(\log r\), and the maximizers are exactly the Haar lifts
\(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).  For such a lift,
\[
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu
=J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu)
=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right)
=\mu_r.
\]

The transport identity is Theorem \ref{thm:pullback-factorization}; under
the unit-circle zero hypothesis \(P\in\mathcal L\), Corollary
\ref{cor:leyang-transport-identity} rewrites it as the reciprocal-shell
factorization.  Explicitly, if \(P(z)=\prod_j(z-z_j)\), then
\[
P^\vee(r^2z)=\prod_j(1-r^2zz_j)
=r^{2n}z^nP(r^{-2}z^{-1}),
\]
and \(r^nS_{r,Q}=r^nz^nQ(J_r(z))=P(z)P^\vee(r^2z)\) becomes
\[
S_{r,Q}(z)=r^nP(z)\,z^nP(r^{-2}z^{-1}).
\]
Equivalently, with \(a_0=P(0)=(-1)^n\prod_jz_j\),
\[
S_{r,Q}(z)
=a_0r^n\prod_j(z-z_j)\prod_j(z-r^{-2}z_j^{-1}),
\]
by Lemma \ref{lem:unit-circle-reciprocal-shell-scalar}.  This is the
expanded zero-shell product; the scalar is not cancelled by the compact
formula.
The shell-selection assertion is Corollary
\ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection}, and the local perturbative
selector is Theorem \ref{thm:quantitative-annular-rouche-stability}.  The
last sentence is the exact unit-circle recovery condition from Theorem
\ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition} after that annular factor has
been selected.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Realizable fixed-scale data]\label{def:realizable-boundary-scale-data}
With \(\mathcal L\) as fixed in the preliminaries, a \emph{realizable fixed-scale datum} is a pair
\[
\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]
\]
for which there are a number \(r>1\), a finite Blaschke product \(B\) of positive degree, and a polynomial \(P\in\mathcal L\) such that \(Q=Q_r(P)\) is the transport polynomial of \(P\) in the normalization of Definition~\ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, and
\[
e=\mathcal E(B,r):=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)} I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu),
\]
with logarithmic energy interpreted by the lower-truncated convention of Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}.  The set of all such pairs is denoted by \(\mathfrak B\).  A triple \((B,r,P)\) satisfying these conditions is called a \emph{realization} of \(\mathfrak b\), and the associated ellipse is \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{conclusion}[Final fixed-scale synthesis record]\label{thm:fixed-scale-final-synthesis}\label{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis}\label{prop:scale-recognition-factorization}\label{cor:boundary-scale-rigidity}\label{prop:boundary-scale-certificate-map}\label{cor:stable-boundary-scale-recognition}\label{prop:boundary-scale-recognition-obstruction}\label{cor:capacity-mahler-fiber-classification}
The following consequences are exactly the endpoint consequences of the
Joukowsky boundary record, the exact complete-vector unit-circle inverse,
and the local annular Rouch\'e theorem.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Endpoint factor consequences.}
Fix \(r>1\), let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\ge1\),
and let \(P\in\mathcal L\).  Then
\[
\mathcal E(B,r)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)}
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=\log r,
\]
and equality in \(I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)\le\log r\) holds exactly when
\[
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Equivalently, the maximizers are the full Haar-lift fiber through \(B\).
The canonical absolutely continuous member of that fiber is
\[
\dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})=\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\,\dd\theta.
\]
At the same fixed scale, the exact complete coefficient vector of
\(Q_r\) determines \(P\) uniquely; equivalently, for every
\(r^{-2}<\rho<1\), \(P\) is the unique monic factor of
\[
S_{r,Q_r}(z):=z^{\deg P}Q_r(J_r(z))
\]
whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\).

\item \emph{Factorization and injectivity.}
Let \(\mathcal R\) be the class of triples \((B,r,P)\) as above and define
\begin{align*}
\Sigma(B,r,P)&:=(\mathcal E(B,r),Q_r(P)),\\
\Pi(B,r,P)&:=(r,P),\\
\mathcal S(r,P)&:=(\log r,Q_r(P)).
\end{align*}
Then \(\Sigma=\mathcal S\circ\Pi\), and \(\mathcal S\) is injective on
\((1,\infty)\times\mathcal L\).  Thus equality
\(\Sigma(B,r,P)=\Sigma(C,s,\widetilde P)\) forces \(r=s\) and
\(P=\widetilde P\), but it does not reconstruct the finite Blaschke
cover; after \((r,P)\) is fixed, the residual maximizing measures are
precisely the Haar-lift fibers \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).

\item \emph{Exact realizability and certificate.}
For \(\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]\) with
\(\deg_wQ=n_{\mathfrak b}\ge1\), put \(r_{\mathfrak b}=\exp(e)\) and,
when \(e>0\),
\[
S_{\mathfrak b}(z):=z^{n_{\mathfrak b}}Q(J_{r_{\mathfrak b}}(z)).
\]
Then \(\mathfrak b\) is realizable if and only if \(e>0\) and, for some
equivalently every separating radius \(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}<\rho<1\),
the outer monic factor \(P_{\mathfrak b}\) of \(S_{\mathfrak b}\) has all
zeros on \(\TT\) and satisfies
\[
S_{\mathfrak b}(z)=
r_{\mathfrak b}^{n_{\mathfrak b}}P_{\mathfrak b}(z)\,
z^{n_{\mathfrak b}}P_{\mathfrak b}(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}z^{-1}).
\]
Here the reciprocal-shell scalar is retained inside
\(z^{n_{\mathfrak b}}P_{\mathfrak b}(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}z^{-1})\); if
one expands this factor into roots, Lemma
\ref{lem:unit-circle-reciprocal-shell-scalar} supplies the unimodular
constant.
Equivalently, the certificate set consisting of pairs \((r,P)\) with
\[
r>1,\qquad e=\log r,\qquad P\in\mathcal L,\qquad
z^{\deg Q}Q(J_r(z))=r^{\deg Q}P(z)z^{\deg Q}P(r^{-2}z^{-1})
\]
is empty or the singleton \((r_{\mathfrak b},P_{\mathfrak b})\).  When it
is nonempty, every realization has scale \(r_{\mathfrak b}\), ellipse
\(E_{r_{\mathfrak b}}\), and source \(P_{\mathfrak b}\).

\item \emph{Local annular Rouch\'e selector.}
Let \(\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\mathfrak B\) be realizable of degree \(n\), and
let \(r=\exp(e)>1\) and \(P\in\mathcal L\) be the recovered scale and
source.  Put
\[
S(z):=z^nQ(J_r(z))=z^nQ_r(J_r(z)).
\]
Choose radii
\[
r^{-2}<\rho_-<\rho_+<1,
\]
let \(\Gamma_-=\{|z|=\rho_-\}\), \(\Gamma_+=\{|z|=\rho_+\}\), and \(\Gamma_0=\{|z|=\rho_+^{-1}\}\), and define
\[
\Delta_\mathfrak b:=\frac12\min\left\{
\inf_{z\in\Gamma_-}|S(z)|,
\inf_{z\in\Gamma_+}|S(z)|,
\inf_{z\in\Gamma_0}|S(z)|
\right\}.
\]
Writing \(P(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\), the exact shell factorization is
\[
S(z)
=a_0r^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)
\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-r^{-2}z_j^{-1})
=r^nP(z)\,z^nP(r^{-2}z^{-1}),
\]
where \(a_0=P(0)=(-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_j\).  Thus the compact
reciprocal-polynomial formula retains the unimodular scalar \(a_0\) in
\(z^nP(r^{-2}z^{-1})\).  Since \(|a_0|=1\) for unit-circle roots,
\[
|S(z)|=r^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}|z-z_j|
\prod_{j=1}^{n}|z-r^{-2}z_j^{-1}|,
\qquad |z_j|=1.
\]
Lemma \ref{lem:unit-circle-reciprocal-shell-scalar} also gives
\[
\left|z^nP(r^{-2}z^{-1})\right|
=\prod_{j=1}^{n}|z-r^{-2}z_j^{-1}|,
\]
for the reciprocal-shell factor.  Consequently this contour envelope has
the usable lower bound
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_\mathfrak b
&\ge
\frac{r^n}{2}\min\Bigl\{
(1-\rho_-)^n(\rho_- - r^{-2})^n,\\
&\hspace{5.7em}
(1-\rho_+)^n(\rho_+ - r^{-2})^n,\,
(\rho_+^{-1}-1)^n(\rho_+^{-1}-r^{-2})^n
\Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(\widehat Q\in\CC[w]\) has \(\deg\widehat Q\le n\) and
\[
\sup_{z\in\Gamma_-\cup\Gamma_+\cup\Gamma_0}
\left|z^n\widehat Q(J_r(z))-S(z)\right|<\Delta_\mathfrak b,
\]
then \(\widehat S(z):=z^n\widehat Q(J_r(z))\) is a degree-\(2n\)
polynomial with exactly \(n\) zeros in the annulus
\(\rho_+<|z|<\rho_+^{-1}\).  The monic degree-\(n\) polynomial formed
from these zeros is denoted
\[
P_{\mathrm{ann}}^{\rho_-,\rho_+}(\widehat Q;r,P).
\]
Every unit-circle source \(\widetilde P\in\mathcal L\) satisfying
\(\widehat Q=Q_r(\widetilde P)\) equals this contour-selected factor.
Consequently \((e,\widehat Q)\) is realizable at the fixed capacity scale
if and only if this factor has all zeros on \(\TT\) and satisfies the
exact reciprocal identity
\[
\widehat S(z)=
r^nP_{\mathrm{ann}}^{\rho_-,\rho_+}(\widehat Q;r,P)(z)\,
z^nP_{\mathrm{ann}}^{\rho_-,\rho_+}(\widehat Q;r,P)(r^{-2}z^{-1}).
\]
Write
\[
Q(w)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}b_\ell w^\ell,
\qquad
\widehat Q(w)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\widehat b_\ell w^\ell,
\]
allowing \(\widehat b_n=0\), and set the explicit coefficient-to-contour amplification factor
\[
B_n(r;\rho_-,\rho_+):=
\max_{z\in\Gamma_-\cup\Gamma_+\cup\Gamma_0}
|z|^n\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}|J_r(z)|^\ell.
\]
The coefficient budget
\[
\max_{0\le\ell\le n}|\widehat b_\ell-b_\ell|<
\frac{\Delta_\mathfrak b}{B_n(r;\rho_-,\rho_+)}
\]
implies the usable perturbation bound
\[
\sup_{\Gamma_-\cup\Gamma_+\cup\Gamma_0}
\left|z^n\widehat Q(J_r(z))-S(z)\right|<\Delta_\mathfrak b
\]
and hence the displayed contour hypothesis.  Moreover, if the zero
divisor of \(P\) is \(\sum_{a\in Z(P)}m_a[a]\), if \(U_a\) are pairwise
disjoint Jordan domains with \(a\in U_a\), whose closures lie in
\(\rho_+<|z|<\rho_+^{-1}\) and contain no other zero of \(P\), and if
\[
\sup_{z\in\bigcup_{a\in Z(P)}\partial U_a}
\left|z^n\widehat Q(J_r(z))-S(z)\right|
<
\frac12\min_{a\in Z(P)}\inf_{z\in\partial U_a}|S(z)|,
\]
then \(P_{\mathrm{ann}}^{\rho_-,\rho_+}(\widehat Q;r,P)\) has exactly \(m_a\) zeros in \(U_a\) for each \(a\).  In particular, if each \(U_a\subset\{|z-a|<\eta\}\) and
\[
P(z)=z^n+p_1z^{n-1}+\cdots+p_n,
\qquad
P_{\mathrm{ann}}^{\rho_-,\rho_+}(\widehat Q;r,P)(z)=z^n+\widehat p_1z^{n-1}+\cdots+\widehat p_n,
\]
then
\[
|\widehat p_k-p_k|\le \binom{n}{k}k\eta(1+\eta)^{k-1},
\qquad 1\le k\le n.
\]

\item \emph{Obstruction trichotomy.}
For every \(\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]\), with \(\deg Q\ge1\),
exactly one of the following alternatives holds: \(e\le0\), in which case
\(\mathfrak b\) is not realizable; \(e>0\) but the reciprocal-shell
factorization in part \textup{(c)} fails, in which case \(\mathfrak b\) is
not realizable; or there is a unique monic \(P\in\mathcal L\) satisfying
that factorization with \(r=\exp(e)\), in which case \(\mathfrak b\) is
realizable and all realizations have this scale, ellipse, and source.

\item \emph{Residual fiber.}
Fix \(n\ge1\), and let \(\mathfrak X_n\) be the class of all quadruples
\((B,r,P,\mu)\) in which \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product of positive
degree, \(r>1\), \(P\in\mathcal L\) has degree \(n\), \(Q_r(P)\) is its
transport polynomial, and
\[
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Define
\[
(B,r,P,\mu)\sim(C,s,\widetilde P,\nu)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r=s\ \text{ and }\ P=\widetilde P.
\]
Attach to a quadruple the datum
\[
\mathfrak D(B,r,P,\mu):=\bigl(\mathcal E(B,r),\,Q_r(P),\,(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu\bigr),
\]
where \(\mathcal E(B,r)\) is the covered-boundary energy supremum from Definition \ref{def:realizable-boundary-scale-data}, and put
\[
\mu_r:=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right).
\]
Then \(\mathfrak D\) is constant on \(\sim\)-classes and induces a
bijection
\[
\overline{\mathfrak D}:\mathfrak X_n/\!\sim\ \longrightarrow
\left\{\bigl(\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r\bigr): r>1,
\ P\in\mathcal L,
\ \deg P=n\right\}.
\]
Equivalently, if two admissible quadruples \((B,r,P,\mu)\) and \((C,s,\widetilde P,\nu)\) have the same datum \(\mathfrak D\), then
\[
r=s,
\qquad
E_r=E_s,
\qquad
P=\widetilde P,
\qquad
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=(J_s\circ C)_\ast\nu=\mu_r.
\]
For every \(\rho\) with \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\), the recovered polynomial \(P\) is also the unique monic degree-\(n\) factor of \(z^nQ_r(J_r(z))\) whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\).  Conversely, the complete fiber over \((\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r)\) is exactly
\[
\left\{(B,r,P,\mu): B\text{ a finite Blaschke product of positive degree and }
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right\}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{conclusion}

\begin{proof}
Part \textup{(a)} is the combination of Theorem
\ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover}, Theorem
\ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction}, Corollary
\ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection}, and the Haar-lift
parametrization in Lemmas
\ref{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula} and
\ref{lem:finite-blaschke-haar-lift-fiber}.  Part \textup{(b)} is then the
factorization \(\Sigma=\mathcal S\circ\Pi\) together with the same
fixed-scale injectivity theorem.

Part \textup{(c)} is the realizability criterion of Theorem
\ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition} after the scale is identified
from \(\mathcal E(B,r)=\log r\); the converse is realized by the
degree-one Blaschke product \(B(z)=z\).  Part \textup{(d)} is precisely
Theorem \ref{thm:quantitative-annular-rouche-stability} applied to the
recovered model \((r,P)\), with Lemma
\ref{lem:unit-circle-reciprocal-shell-scalar} giving the displayed
product form of \(|S|\).  The coefficient budget is the defining estimate
for \(B_n(r;\rho_-,\rho_+)\).

Part \textup{(e)} is the trichotomy obtained by applying part
\textup{(c)} to the cases \(e\le0\), \(e>0\) without the reciprocal-shell
certificate, and \(e>0\) with that certificate.  Part \textup{(f)} follows
from
\[
\mathfrak D(B,r,P,\mu)
=\bigl(\log r,Q_r(P),J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/(2\pi))\bigr)
\]
for every Haar lift \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\), and from
fixed-scale injectivity of \(P\mapsto Q_r(P)\) on \(\mathcal L\).
\end{proof}

\section{Examples and Normalizations}\label{sec:examples}

The following elementary cases fix the normalizations used in the preceding sections.

\begin{example}[The ellipse and its scale]\label{ex:joukowsky-ellipse-scale}
For \(z=e^{i\theta}\),
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}=(r+r^{-1})\cos\theta+i(r-r^{-1})\sin\theta.
\]
Thus \(E_r\) is the ellipse with semiaxes \(r+r^{-1}\) and \(r-r^{-1}\).  The exterior conformal normalization in Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} gives \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\), and the equilibrium measure is the image of Haar measure under this parametrization.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[One Fourier mode]\label{ex:one-fourier-mode-deficit}
Let \(m\ge1\) and, for small real \(\varepsilon\), let
\[
\dd\mu_\varepsilon(e^{i\theta})=(1+2\varepsilon\cos m\theta)\,\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Then \(\widehat\mu_\varepsilon(m)=\varepsilon\) and all other positive Fourier coefficients vanish.  The deficit formula gives
\[
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu_\varepsilon)=\frac{1+r^{-2m}}{m}\varepsilon^2.
\]
If instead \(\dd\nu_\varepsilon=(1+2\varepsilon\sin m\theta)\dd\theta/(2\pi)\), then \(\widehat\nu_\varepsilon(m)=-i\varepsilon\) and
\[
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\nu_\varepsilon)=\frac{1-r^{-2m}}{m}\varepsilon^2.
\]
This realizes the two sharp mode weights in Theorem \ref{thm:finite-energy-joukowsky-fourier-identity}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Degree-one transport]\label{ex:degree-one-transport}
Let \(P(z)=z-\alpha\), \(\alpha\ne0\).  Then \(a_0=-\alpha\), and Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization} gives
\[
Q_r(w)=-\alpha\bigl(w-r\alpha-r^{-1}\alpha^{-1}\bigr).
\]
If \(|\alpha|=1\), then
\[
zQ_r(J_r(z))=r(z-\alpha)(r^{-2}-\alpha z)
=-r\alpha\,(z-\alpha)(z-r^{-2}\alpha^{-1}),
\]
so the pulled-back doubled polynomial has one zero on \(\TT\) and its reflected partner on \(r^{-2}\TT\).  Any radius \(\rho\) with \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\) selects the outer factor \(z-\alpha\).
This is the degree-one instance of Lemma
\ref{lem:unit-circle-reciprocal-shell-scalar}: the apparent scalar
\(-\alpha\) is the reciprocal-shell scalar \(a_0\), and it disappears
only after taking absolute values in the Rouch\'e estimates.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[\(n=2\) one-scale resonance]\label{ex:n2-one-scale-resonance}
The one-scale fiber classification is nontrivial away from the unit-circle source class.  Fix \(r>1\), and set
\[
P(z)=(z-1)(z+r^{-2}),\qquad
\widetilde P(z)=(z-r^{-2})(z+1).
\]
The roots of \(P\) are \(z_1=1\) and \(z_2=-r^{-2}\).  For the full reflected choice \(S=\{1,2\}\),
\[
z_1^2z_2^2=r^{-4}=r^{-2|S|},
\]
so the subset-resonance equation in Lemma \ref{lem:one-scale-orbit-fiber-matching} is satisfied.  Concretely,
\[
T_r(1)=T_r(r^{-2})=r+r^{-1},\qquad
T_r(-r^{-2})=T_r(-1)=-(r+r^{-1}),
\]
and both source-root products are \(-r^{-2}\).  Hence the complete transported polynomials agree:
\[
Q_r(P)(w)=Q_r(\widetilde P)(w)
=-r^{-2}\bigl(w^2-(r+r^{-1})^2\bigr).
\]
Thus a complete one-scale coefficient vector can have a genuine two-point source ambiguity; the unit-circle collapse in Theorem \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction} is not a statement about arbitrary off-circle divisors.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Repeated source root in the one-scale fiber]\label{ex:repeated-root-one-scale-fiber}
Repeated roots are governed by orbit multiplicities rather than by a subset of distinct indices.  Let
\[
a=\ii r^{-1},\qquad \tau_r(a)=r^{-2}/a=-\ii r^{-1},
\]
and compare
\[
P(z)=(z-a)^2,\qquad \widetilde P(z)=(z-\tau_r(a))^2.
\]
Both are monic of degree \(2\), and their source-root products are equal:
\[
a^2=\tau_r(a)^2=-r^{-2}.
\]
Moreover
\[
T_r(a)=r(\ii r^{-1})+r^{-1}(\ii r^{-1})^{-1}
=\ii-\ii=0,
\qquad
T_r(\tau_r(a))=0.
\]
Therefore the two repeated-root sources have the same full transport,
\[
Q_r(P)(w)=Q_r(\widetilde P)(w)=-r^{-2}w^2.
\]
This is the multiplicity version of the resonance condition: the two-point \(\tau_r\)-orbit has total multiplicity \(2\), and moving both copies from \(a\) to \(\tau_r(a)\) preserves the leading coefficient, while moving only one copy would change the source-root product.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A two-scale candidate graph]\label{ex:two-scale-candidate-graph}
The two-scale graph makes the residual matching problem finite and explicit.  Take \(r=2\), \(s=3\), and
\[
P(z)=(z-1)(z+\tfrac14).
\]
At the first scale,
\[
T_2(1)=\frac52,\qquad T_2(-\tfrac14)=-\frac52,
\]
so Example \ref{ex:n2-one-scale-resonance} shows that the same \(Q_2\) is also produced by \((z-\tfrac14)(z+1)\).  At the second scale, however,
\[
T_3(1)=\frac{10}{3},\qquad
T_3(-\tfrac14)=-\frac{25}{12}.
\]
The graph \(\Gamma_{2,3}(Q_2(P),Q_3(P))\) has left vertices
\[
\alpha_+=\frac52,\qquad \alpha_-=-\frac52
\]
and right vertices
\[
\beta_1=\frac{10}{3},\qquad \beta_2=-\frac{25}{12}.
\]
Since
\[
\Delta_{2,3}=\frac23-\frac32=-\frac56,
\]
the edge test in Definition \ref{def:two-scale-candidate-graph} gives
\[
x(\alpha_+,\beta_1)=1,\quad y(\alpha_+,\beta_1)=1,
\]
and
\[
x(\alpha_-,\beta_2)=-\frac14,\quad y(\alpha_-,\beta_2)=-4.
\]
These two pairs have \(xy=1\) and hence give edges labelled by the original source roots \(1\) and \(-1/4\).  The off-diagonal tests fail:
\[
x(\alpha_+,\beta_2)y(\alpha_+,\beta_2)=-\frac{63}{2},
\qquad
x(\alpha_-,\beta_1)y(\alpha_-,\beta_1)=-51.
\]
Thus the candidate graph has a unique perfect matching and the two-scale inverse recovers the source divisor \([1]+[-1/4]\), even though the first scale alone had a resonant competitor.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A single Blaschke zero]\label{ex:single-blaschke-zero}
For
\[
B_a(z)=\zeta\frac{z-a}{1-\overline a z},\qquad |a|<1,
\]
the canonical absolutely continuous Haar lift has density
\[
\dd\mu_{B_a}(e^{i\theta})=\frac{1-|a|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-a|^2}\,\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Its positive Fourier coefficients are \(\widehat\mu_{B_a}(k)=\overline a^{\,k}\), hence Proposition \ref{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy} gives
\[
I_{\TT}(\mu_{B_a})=\log|1-\overline a\,a|=\log(1-|a|^2).
\]
For the monomial cover \(B(z)=\zeta z^d\), all Blaschke zeros are at the origin, the lift is Haar measure, and the source-circle energy is \(0\).
\end{example}


\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Several themes emerging from the core sections deserve emphasis. First, the Joukowsky ellipse is not merely a convenient parametrized curve: it is a conformal boundary object whose logarithmic capacity, annular modulus, Fourier deficit, finite-cover equilibrium lifts, and transported Mahler averages are all controlled by the same scale parameter.  This is the content summarized in Corollary \ref{cor:joukowsky-package-synthesis}.
