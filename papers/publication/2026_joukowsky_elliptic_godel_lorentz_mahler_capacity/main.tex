\begin{abstract}
We study two families of Joukowsky-type maps that send the unit circle to confocal ellipses and extract from them arithmetic, algebraic, and potential-theoretic invariants. First, for the elliptic projection gates \(J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})=e^{im\theta}+c e^{-im\theta}\), we prove a sharp Fourier support theorem for the logarithmic speed of the normalized parametrized covering, identify its \(p\)-adic slope invariants, and show that this full parametrized speed spectrum uniquely reconstructs the rational parameter \(c\) and the covering multiplicity \(m\). Second, we algebraize prime-register data by a homomorphism into \(\mathrm{SO}^+(1,1)\), obtaining Lorentz-type composition laws, condition-number identities, and primorial extremality statements for associated ellipses and ellipsoids. Third, for the two-scale transport polynomial generated by \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\), we establish pullback and discriminant factorizations, derive transported Jensen/Mahler and Lee--Yang free-energy identities, and prove exact noiseless holographic reconstruction from a single transport scale.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
The classical Joukowsky map provides one of the simplest conformal mechanisms by which circles generate ellipses and, more generally, confocal families in the plane. It therefore sits naturally at the intersection of conformal mapping, algebraic elimination, and logarithmic capacity theory \citep{Pommerenke1992,Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,GKZ1994}. The purpose of the present paper is to show that once the Joukowsky correspondence is equipped with arithmetic parameters, it also supports a rigid \(p\)-adic spectral calculus and a Lorentz-type algebraization of prime data.

Our first object is the \(m\)-fold elliptic projection gate
\[
J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})=e^{im\theta}+c\,e^{-im\theta},
\qquad c\in(0,1)\cap\QQ,\ \ m\in\NN,
\]
whose image is an ellipse traversed \(m\) times. The logarithmic speed defect of this normalized parametrized covering has an exceptionally sparse Fourier expansion, and that sparsity converts arithmetic information about \(c\) into linear asymptotics of \(p\)-adic valuations. The Fourier coefficients used here are therefore data of the chosen covering coordinate, not naive invariants of the unparametrized boundary curve. This places the problem in the general circle of ideas surrounding local valuations and spectral rigidity in arithmetic analysis \citep{Gouvea1997,Koblitz1984,Serre1973}. We show that the full normalized Fourier family determines \(m\) and all prime valuations of \(c\), while finite congruence audits, at any fixed covering multiplicity and modulus, necessarily miss sufficiently deep \(p\)-adic perturbations.

Our second theme concerns the multiplicative encoding of prime data by finitely supported prime registers. After passing to logarithmic coordinates, the Joukowsky ellipse family becomes a concrete model for rapidity addition in \(\mathrm{SO}^+(1,1)\), with the norm of a register playing the role of a Lorentz scale parameter. This produces an exact bridge between G\"odel-type multiplicative coding, condition numbers, Einstein addition, logarithmic capacity, and confocal ellipse geometry \citep{Ungar2008}. The resulting formalism also yields primorial minimization principles anchored in the same complex-variable scale: among decomposable codebooks with pairwise coprime images, the smallest total cost is attained by the first \(n\) primes, and the same extremizer uniquely minimizes the associated normalized ellipsoid-volume character, the Joukowsky capacity product, and the ellipse-area product. When all registers are embedded into one fixed ambient prime set, this is equivalently a fixed-dimensional Euclidean volume minimization.

The third theme is potential-theoretic. For a polynomial \(P\) with nonzero constant term, the two-scale Joukowsky transform \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\) generates a transport polynomial \(Q_r\) whose pullback along the circle factors exactly as a product of \(P\) and its reciprocal polynomial. This identity leads to transported Jensen formulas on the ellipse, a shell-counting interpretation of the free-energy slope, a closed Bessel representation for the exponential moment generating function of the pushed-forward Haar measure, and a Lee--Yang free-energy law when the zeros of \(P\) lie on the unit circle \citep{LeeYang1952I,LeeYang1952II,Mahler1962,Smyth1981,Boyd1981,EverestWard1999}. At the level of elimination theory, the same factorization yields explicit discriminant formulas and an exact one-scale holographic reconstruction procedure under noiseless full-polynomial data \citep{GKZ1994}.


Thus the common complex-variable object throughout the paper is the boundary of a Joukowsky ellipse.  The Fourier and \(p\)-adic part studies this boundary through the normalized speed data of the covering \(z\mapsto z^m+c z^{-m}\); the G\"odel--Lorentz part studies the same confocal family through its scale, capacity, area, and condition number; and the Mahler/resultant part studies how a polynomial divisor on the unit circle is transported to that boundary by \(J_r\).  The arithmetic quantities are therefore not separate decorations: they are tied to the same ellipse boundary through explicitly stated spectral-cover, capacity/Mahler, and elimination-theoretic data models.

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem: hypothesis-separated Joukowsky invariants]\label{thm:main-theorem-map}
The confocal Joukowsky ellipse carries the following arithmetic, Lorentz, and elimination-theoretic invariants, each under the exact data model specified in its clause.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item \textbf{Full-spectrum elliptic gates.}
  Let \(c\in(0,1)\cap\QQ\) and \(m\in\NN\), and assume that the complete Fourier coefficient family of the logarithmic speed defect is known in the normalized covering coordinate \(\theta\) of Definition \ref{def:group-jg-elliptic-gate-logspeed-spectrum}.  For
  \[
  u_{c,m}(\theta)=\log\abs{1-c e^{-2im\theta}}
  \]
  one has \(\widehat u_{c,m}(0)=0\) and
  \[
  \widehat u_{c,m}(\pm 2mn)=-\frac{c^n}{2n}\qquad(n\ge1),
  \]
  with all other Fourier coefficients equal to zero.  Hence the full normalized parametrized logarithmic speed spectrum reconstructs
  \[
  m=\frac12\gcd\{\,|k|:\widehat u_{c,m}(k)\ne0\,\},
  \qquad
  c=-2\widehat u_{c,m}(2m),
  \]
  and its odd-prime valuation slopes are \(\nu_p(\widehat u_{c,m}(2mn))=n\nu_p(c)-\nu_p(n)\).  This is a parametrized-cover inverse statement; for arbitrary circle reparametrizations one must first return to the normalized gauge specified below.  Conversely, the finite-audit obstruction stated below concerns only fixed finite congruence windows and is not used as a substitute for full-spectrum reconstruction.

  \item \textbf{Prime registers and coprime codebooks.}
  For every finitely supported prime register \(r\in\mathsf P\), with norm \(N(r)\), the matrix \(\Lambda(r)\in\mathrm{SO}^+(1,1)\) defined from the Joukowsky scale \(N(r)^{1/2}\) is a monoid homomorphism: register addition becomes rapidity addition, and the same scale determines the capacity, area, and condition number of the corresponding confocal ellipse. Moreover, if \(X\) is a finite set with \(|X|=n\ge1\) and \(\iota:X\hookrightarrow\NN_{\mathrm{sf},>1}\) is a G\"odel-decomposable codebook, meaning that the integers \(\iota(x)\) are square-free and pairwise coprime, then the total cost \(C(\iota)=\sum_x\log\iota(x)\) is minimized precisely by \(\iota(X)=\{p_1,\dots,p_n\}\). The same primorial codebook is the unique minimizer for the normalized ellipsoid-volume character, for the Joukowsky capacity product, and for the ellipse-area product; if a single finite ambient prime set is fixed in advance, the corresponding Euclidean ellipsoid-volume product has the same minimizer.

  \item \textbf{Exact polynomial transport.}
  Let \(K\) be a field, let \(P(u)=\sum_{k=0}^n a_k u^k\in K[u]\) with \(n\ge1\) and \(a_0a_n\ne0\), and let \(r\) be either an indeterminate or an element of \(K^\times\).  With roots \(z_j\) taken with multiplicity in an algebraic closure, the reciprocal and transport polynomials
  \[
  P^\vee(u)=a_n^{-1}u^nP(1/u),
  \qquad
  Q_r(w)=a_0\prod_{j=1}^n(w-rz_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1})
  \]
  satisfy \(Q_r\in K(r)[w]\) and the algebraic pullback identity
  \[
  r^n z^nQ_r(J_r(z))=P(z)P^\vee(r^2z).
  \]
  Their discriminants satisfy
  \[
  \operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)=\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)
  r^{-n(n-1)}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2z_iz_j-1)^2,
  \]
  with both sides interpreted in the algebraic closure and hence in \(K(r)\).  If in addition \(K=\CC\) and \(r>1\), then the pushed-forward Haar mean on \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\) satisfies the two-scale identity
  \[
  \int_{E_r}\log|Q_r(w)|\,\dd\mu_r(w)
  =\mathcal M_1(P)+\mathcal M_{r^{-2}}(P)+n\log r-\log|a_n|.
  \]

  \item \textbf{Noiseless one-scale Lee--Yang reconstruction.}
  Fix the exact real scale \(r>1\).  Suppose the full coefficient list of \(Q_r\in\CC[w]\) is given without noise and is known to arise from a monic polynomial \(P\in\CC[z]\) whose zeros, counted with multiplicity, all lie on \(\TT\).  Then \(n=\deg_w Q_r\) is recovered from the data.  The exact resultant
  \[
  S_r(z)=\operatorname{Res}_w(Q_r(w),rz^2-wz+r^{-1})
  \]
  has its zero divisor split into the two shells \(|z|=1\) and \(|z|=r^{-2}\), and the shell \(|z|=1\), counted with multiplicity, reconstructs \(P\) uniquely.  Thus the single-scale transport map is injective on the exact monic Lee--Yang class; no finite register, partial coefficient list, unknown scale, or noisy sampling model is asserted here.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part \textup{(i)} is Theorem \ref{thm:group-jg-elliptic-gate-prime-spectrum-rigidity}, with the parametrized-cover gauge made explicit in Lemma \ref{lem:group-jg-elliptic-gate-parametrized-gauge}.  The accompanying outer-function rigidity, minimal-register, classification, and finite-audit obstruction statements are Theorem \ref{thm:group-jg-outer-function-rigidity-joukowsky-ellipse}, Proposition \ref{prop:group-jg-elliptic-gate-minimal-register}, Corollary \ref{cor:group-jg-elliptic-gate-prime-spectrum-classification}, and Theorem \ref{thm:group-jg-elliptic-gate-padics-blindspot}; the finite obstruction is used only in the restricted finite-window model named there.  Part \textup{(ii)} is the content of Theorems \ref{thm:group-jg-godel-lorentz-algebraization}, \ref{thm:group-jg-godel-lorentz-dictionary-rigidity}, \ref{thm:group-jg-joukowsky-area-preserving-cayley}, and \ref{thm:group-jg-godel-ellipsoid-volume}, together with the primorial extremality theorem \ref{prop:group-jg-min-total-godel-cost-primorial}; the latter is exactly the pairwise-coprime square-free codebook model stated above.  Part \textup{(iii)} is proved in Lemma \ref{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}, Theorems \ref{thm:group-jg-pullback-factorization} and \ref{thm:group-jg-discriminant-factorization}, and the two-scale mean identity Theorem \ref{thm:group-jg-elliptic-two-scale-stokes-mean}.  The further Mahler and free-energy consequences are Theorems \ref{thm:group-jg-transported-jensen-shell-count} and \ref{thm:group-jg-leyang-mahler-free-energy}.  Part \textup{(iv)} is Definition \ref{def:group-jg-noiseless-leyang-reconstruction-data}, Proposition \ref{prop:group-jg-exact-reconstruction-interface}, Theorem \ref{thm:group-jg-leyang-single-scale-reconstruction}, and Corollary \ref{cor:group-jg-leyang-annular-shell-projection}; these results require the exact scale \(r\), the full polynomial \(Q_r\), monicity, and the Lee--Yang unit-circle zero condition.  These cited results have complete proofs below and use the same boundary map \(z\mapsto rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\), so the asserted identities, rigidity statements, and reconstruction claim form a single theorem chain.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Joukowsky boundary compatibility]\label{prop:joukowsky-boundary-compatibility}
For \(L\ge 1\), put
\[
\Gamma_L:=\bigl\{\,L^{1/2}z+L^{-1/2}z^{-1}:|z|=1\,\bigr\}.
\]
The three data models in Theorem \ref{thm:main-theorem-map} are compatible with this single confocal boundary family in the following precise sense.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item If \(c\in(0,1)\) and \(m\in\NN\), then
  \[
  J_{c,m}(\TT)=c^{1/2}\Gamma_{c^{-1}}.
  \]
  The covering multiplicity \(m\) changes only the parametrization of this boundary, while the Fourier support and \(p\)-adic slopes in Theorem \ref{thm:main-theorem-map}\textup{(i)} recover the same scale parameter through \(L=c^{-1}\).

  \item If \(s\in\mathsf P\) is a prime register, then the G\"odel--Lorentz ellipse used in Theorem \ref{thm:main-theorem-map}\textup{(ii)} is
  \[
  E_J(s)=\Gamma_{N(s)}.
  \]
  Consequently its logarithmic capacity, area, Lorentz rapidity, and condition number are functions of the boundary scale \(L=N(s)\).

  \item If \(r>1\), then the transport ellipse in Theorem \ref{thm:main-theorem-map}\textup{(iii)--(iv)} is
  \[
  E_r=J_r(\TT)=\Gamma_{r^2}.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
Thus the spectral, capacity--Lorentz, Mahler, and elimination probes in the main theorem are not separate geometric inputs: after the explicit changes of scale
\[
L=c^{-1},\qquad L=N(s),\qquad L=r^2,
\]
they are probes of the same one-parameter Joukowsky boundary \(\Gamma_L\), up to the harmless homothety \(c^{1/2}\) in the elliptic-gate normalization.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(z=e^{i\phi}\),
\[
\Gamma_L=\bigl\{(L^{1/2}+L^{-1/2})\cos\phi+i(L^{1/2}-L^{-1/2})\sin\phi:\phi\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\bigr\},
\]
so \(\Gamma_L\) is exactly the confocal Joukowsky ellipse with focal distance \(2\).  For the elliptic gate, take \(L=c^{-1}\) and set \(z=e^{im\theta}\).  Then
\[
c^{1/2}\bigl(L^{1/2}z+L^{-1/2}z^{-1}\bigr)
=c^{1/2}\bigl(c^{-1/2}e^{im\theta}+c^{1/2}e^{-im\theta}\bigr)
=e^{im\theta}+c e^{-im\theta},
\]
which proves \(J_{c,m}(\TT)=c^{1/2}\Gamma_{c^{-1}}\).  Because \(\theta\mapsto e^{im\theta}\) is an \(m\)-fold covering of \(\TT\), the integer \(m\) affects only how the same ellipse is traversed; the scale is still determined by \(c\).

For a prime register \(s\), the definition in Theorem \ref{thm:group-jg-godel-lorentz-algebraization} is
\[
E_J(s)=\bigl\{N(s)^{1/2}z+N(s)^{-1/2}z^{-1}:|z|=1\bigr\},
\]
which is \(\Gamma_{N(s)}\).  The cited theorem and Corollaries \ref{cor:group-jg-godel-condition-number-identity}, \ref{cor:group-jg-capacity-modulus-koenigs}, and \ref{cor:group-jg-ellipse-area-ellipsoid-gap} express the condition number, rapidity, capacity, and area in terms of \(N(s)\), hence in terms of \(L\).

Finally, by definition,
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}=(r^2)^{1/2}z+(r^2)^{-1/2}z^{-1},
\]
so \(J_r(\TT)=\Gamma_{r^2}\).  The three asserted changes of scale therefore put all boundaries in the single family \(\Gamma_L\), and the only additional operation in the first model is the explicitly displayed scalar homothety.
\end{proof}

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:preliminaries}
We write \(\mathbb P\) for the set of prime numbers. For \(x\in\QQ^\times\) and \(p\in\mathbb P\), \(\nu_p(x)\) denotes the normalized \(p\)-adic valuation, characterized by \(\nu_p(p)=1\) and additivity under multiplication; we also set \(\nu_p(0)=+\infty\). The prime-register monoid is
\[
\mathsf P:=\bigl\{\,r:\mathbb P\to\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}\;:\;r(p)=0\text{ for all but finitely many }p\,\bigr\},
\]
endowed with pointwise addition. Its associated norm is
\[
N(r):=\prod_{p\in\mathbb P} p^{r(p)}\in\NN_{>0}.
\]
We further write \(p_n\#:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_i\) for the \(n\)th primorial, \(I:=[0,1]\), \(B_d\subset\RR^d\) for the Euclidean unit ball, and \(\NN_{\mathrm{sf},>1}\) for the square-free integers greater than \(1\).

\begin{definition}[G\"odel-decomposable codebook]\label{def:godel-decomposable-codebook}
Let \(X\) be a finite set. An injection
\[
\iota:X\hookrightarrow\NN_{\mathrm{sf},>1}
\]
is called \emph{G\"odel-decomposable} if its image consists of pairwise coprime square-free integers, equivalently
\[
\gcd(\iota(x),\iota(y))=1
\qquad(x\ne y).
\]
Its total G\"odel cost is
\[
C(\iota):=\sum_{x\in X}\log\iota(x)
=\log\!\Bigl(\prod_{x\in X}\iota(x)\Bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

For \(c\in(0,1)\cap\QQ\) and \(m\in\NN\), the elliptic projection gate is
\[
J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})=e^{im\theta}+c\,e^{-im\theta}\qquad (\theta\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ),
\]
and its image ellipse is
\[
E_c=\bigl\{(1+c)\cos t+i(1-c)\sin t:\ t\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\bigr\}.
\]
For \(r>0\), we also use the two-scale Joukowsky map
\[
J_r(z):=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},
\qquad
E_r:=J_r(\TT),
\qquad
\TT:=\{e^{i\theta}:\theta\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\}.
\]
If \(f\in L^1(\TT)\), its Fourier coefficients are
\[
\widehat f(k):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} f(\theta)e^{-ik\theta}\,\dd\theta
\qquad (k\in\ZZ).
\]

Let \(K\) be a field.  All roots of polynomials over \(K\) are taken in a fixed algebraic closure \(\overline K\), always with multiplicity.  No separability hypothesis is imposed unless it is stated explicitly: repeated roots are allowed, and in that case the relevant discriminant is zero.  For a degree-\(n\) polynomial \(F(x)=b_n\prod_{j=1}^n(x-\alpha_j)\) with \(b_n\ne0\), we use the resultant normalization
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_x(F):=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}b_n^{-1}\operatorname{Res}_x(F,F')
=b_n^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)^2,
\]
where the displayed root formula is interpreted in \(\overline K\); its value lies in \(K\).  If \(K\subseteq\CC\), the same symbols may also be evaluated at complex points.  If \(K\) is arbitrary, identities involving \(J_r(z)\) are identities in the Laurent polynomial ring over \(K(r)\) after adjoining the displayed variable \(z^{\pm1}\).  The following convention fixes the normalization used everywhere below.

\begin{definition}[Reciprocal and transport normalization]\label{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}
Let
\[
P(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k u^k=a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(u-z_j)\in K[u],
\qquad
n\ge 1,
\qquad
 a_n a_0\ne0,
\]
with roots taken in a fixed algebraic closure of \(K\).  Its reciprocal polynomial is the root-normalized reciprocal
\[
P^{\vee}(u):=a_n^{-1}u^nP(1/u)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-u z_j),
\]
so the leading coefficient of \(P^\vee\) is \(a_0/a_n\) and the constant coefficient is \(1\).  The unnormalized reciprocal is \(u^nP(1/u)=a_nP^\vee(u)\).  For an indeterminate \(r\), or for \(r\in K^\times\), the associated Joukowsky transport polynomial is
\[
Q_r(w):=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(w-r z_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)\in K(r)[w].
\]
Thus \(Q_r\) is normalized with leading coefficient \(a_0\).  If a monic transport polynomial is required, we write
\[
\widetilde Q_r(w):=a_0^{-1}Q_r(w).
\]
The phrase ``monic Lee--Yang input'' always refers to \(P\) being monic in the variable \(u\); the induced \(Q_r\) has leading coefficient \(a_0=(-1)^n\prod_j z_j\), while \(\widetilde Q_r\) is the corresponding monic transport polynomial with the same zero divisor.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient descent for the transport polynomial]\label{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}
In Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, the expression
\[
Q_r(w)=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(w-r z_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)
\]
is independent of the ordering of the roots and belongs to \(K(r)[w]\).  The same conclusion holds after specializing \(r\in K^\times\), in which case \(Q_r\in K[w]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to prove that each coefficient of \(Q_r\) lies in the indicated coefficient field.  The coefficient of \(w^{n-\ell}\) is \((-1)^\ell a_0\) times the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(\ell\) in the quantities \(r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\).  Expanding this elementary symmetric polynomial gives a finite \(\ZZ[r,r^{-1}]\)-linear combination of monomials
\[
z_1^{e_1}\cdots z_n^{e_n},
\qquad e_j\in\{-1,0,1\},
\]
summed over subsets in a way that is invariant under every permutation of the root labels.  Since \(a_0\ne0\), all roots are nonzero, and multiplication by a sufficiently large power of \(z_1\cdots z_n\) converts each such symmetric Laurent polynomial into an ordinary symmetric polynomial in \(z_1,
\dots,z_n\).  By the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials, it is a polynomial expression in the elementary symmetric functions of the roots and in \((z_1\cdots z_n)^{-1}\), with coefficients in \(\ZZ[r,r^{-1}]\).

The elementary symmetric functions of the \(z_j\) are \((-1)^k a_{n-k}/a_n\), and \((z_1\cdots z_n)^{-1}=(-1)^n a_n/a_0\).  Hence every coefficient of \(Q_r\) belongs to \(K(r)\).  The construction used only symmetric expressions in the multiset of roots, so it is independent of the chosen ordering.  If \(r\in K^\times\) is specialized, the same formulas have coefficients in \(K\), proving \(Q_r\in K[w]\).
\end{proof}

We use the zero set notation \(\mathrm{Zero}(F)\) for a polynomial \(F\), and the standard resultant/discriminant notation \(\operatorname{Res}\) and \(\operatorname{Disc}\) \citep{GKZ1994}. For \(\rho>0\), we define the logarithmic circular mean
\[
\mathcal M_\rho(P):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|P(\rho e^{i\theta})|\,\dd\theta,
\]
so that \(\mathcal M_1(P)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of \(P\) \citep{Mahler1962,EverestWard1999}.

The next two identities are the algebraic input for the Mahler, discriminant, and holographic statements in Sections \ref{sec:mahler-free-energy} and \ref{sec:discriminant}.

\begin{theorem}[Pullback factorization]\label{thm:group-jg-pullback-factorization}
Let \(P\), \(P^\vee\), and \(Q_r\) be as in Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, and let \(r\) be an indeterminate or an element of \(K^\times\). Then \(Q_r(w)\in K(r)[w]\), and in \(K(r)[z,z^{-1}]\) one has
\[
\boxed{\;
r^n z^n\,Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)=P(z)\,P^\vee(r^2 z).
\;}
\]
If \(K\subseteq\CC\) and \(r\in K^\times\), the same identity may equivalently be evaluated at every \(z\in\CC^\times\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient assertion is Lemma \ref{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}. For each \(j\),
\[
J_r(z)-\bigl(r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\bigr)
=r(z-z_j)+r^{-1}(z^{-1}-z_j^{-1})
=\frac{(z-z_j)(r^2 z z_j-1)}{r z z_j}.
\]
Multiplying over \(j\) yields
\[
Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)
=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(z-z_j)(r^2 z z_j-1)}{r z z_j}
=r^{-n}z^{-n}\Bigl(a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
Since \(a_0=(-1)^n a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n} z_j\), we have \(a_0\prod_j z_j^{-1}=(-1)^n a_n\). Therefore
\[
Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)
=r^{-n}z^{-n}\,a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\cdot (-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
The first factor is \(P(z)\), while
\[
P^\vee(r^2 z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-r^2 z z_j)=(-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
Combining the two identities gives the stated factorization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Discriminant factorization]\label{thm:group-jg-discriminant-factorization}
In the setting of Theorem \ref{thm:group-jg-pullback-factorization},
\[
\boxed{\;
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)
=\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)\,
r^{-n(n-1)}
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2 z_i z_j-1)^2.
\;}
\]
The identity is taken in \(\overline K(r)\), and both sides are in fact elements of \(K(r)\).  It remains valid without assuming that \(P\) is separable; if roots collide, the corresponding discriminants vanish.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(w_j:=r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)=a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(w_i-w_j)^2.
\]
Using
\[
w_i-w_j
=r(z_i-z_j)+r^{-1}(z_i^{-1}-z_j^{-1})
=\frac{(z_i-z_j)(r^2 z_i z_j-1)}{r z_i z_j},
\]
we obtain
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)
=a_0^{2n-2}r^{-n(n-1)}
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i-z_j)^2
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2 z_i z_j-1)^2
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}.
\]
Now
\[
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}
=\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{n} z_i\Bigr)^{-2(n-1)},
\]
and \(a_0=(-1)^n a_n\prod_i z_i\) implies
\[
a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}=a_n^{2n-2}.
\]
Since
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)=a_n^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i-z_j)^2,
\]
the desired identity follows.
\end{proof}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Several themes emerging from the preceding sections deserve emphasis. First, the Joukowsky ellipse is not merely a convenient parametrized curve: it is a conformal boundary object whose logarithmic capacity, annular modulus, and Mahler averages are all controlled by the same scale parameter. In this sense, the transport identity of Theorem \ref{thm:group-jg-pullback-factorization} should be viewed as a potential-theoretic bridge between circle data and ellipse data, parallel to the classical role of conformal maps in logarithmic potential theory \citep{Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,Pommerenke1992}. The discriminant and resultant formulas show that the same bridge also preserves delicate elimination-theoretic information \citep{GKZ1994}.

Second, the \(p\)-adic rigidity statements suggest a broader arithmetic theory. For rational \(c\), the slopes of \(\nu_p(\widehat u_{c,m}(2mn))\) recover the full local data of \(c\), while Theorem \ref{thm:group-jg-elliptic-gate-padics-blindspot} shows that any finite congruence audit leaves a systematic uncertainty window. It would be natural to extend this analysis from rational parameters to algebraic numbers, to study whether the resulting local slope packages satisfy compatibility relations across extensions, and to compare the finite-audit blindspot with asymptotic phenomena from Iwasawa-theoretic growth or \(p\)-adic dynamical interpolation \citep{Koblitz1984,Serre1973}.

Third, the G\"odel--Lorentz dictionary gives a geometric interpretation of multiplicative coding: additive rapidities in \(\mathrm{SO}^+(1,1)\) correspond to multiplicative norms, while the normalized ellipse parameter obeys Einstein addition. This perspective may be useful beyond the present setting, for example in higher-rank analogues where one replaces \(\mathrm{SO}^+(1,1)\) by more general diagonal or Cartan subgroups and asks for analogous capacity/volume formulas. A parallel question is whether the primorial extremizers obtained here remain optimal under weaker combinatorial constraints than pairwise coprimality.

Finally, the Lee--Yang and holographic results leave several concrete open problems. One would like to extend the transported shell-counting identity to multivariable polynomials and higher-genus or higher-dimensional transport geometries; the single-scale reconstruction theorem proved here is exact and noiseless, so stability under perturbation or partial observation is a separate question; and the full resultant/discriminant infrastructure underlying the present arguments remains to be mechanized. These problems lie at the interface of complex analysis, arithmetic geometry, and formalized algebra, and they seem well suited for further development.
