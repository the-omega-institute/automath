\begin{abstract}
\emergencystretch=2em
We develop a logarithmic-potential and Mahler-transport calculus for
Joukowsky ellipses.  A nonconstant rational pullback theorem gives an
exact capacity formula with branch and collision singularities handled
by a lower-truncated extended kernel.  Its degree-two specialization is
the Joukowsky collision relation used throughout the paper.  For
\(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\), the equilibrium deficit on \(J_r(\TT)\)
diagonalizes in Fourier modes with sharp constants \(1\pm r^{-2k}\),
yielding the unique equilibrium measure and
\(\operatorname{cap}(J_r(\TT))=r\).  Finite Blaschke covers preserve this
deficit through the pushed-forward source measure and give a complete
equilibrium-lift description.  The transported polynomial \(Q_r\)
supplies discriminant and Mahler identities, multiscale inverse
reconstruction, one-scale resonance fibers, Lee--Yang shell injectivity,
and quantitative annular Rouch\'e stability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
The classical Joukowsky map provides one of the simplest conformal mechanisms by which circles generate ellipses and, more generally, confocal families in the plane. It therefore sits naturally at the intersection of conformal mapping, algebraic elimination, and logarithmic capacity theory \citep{Pommerenke1992,Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,GKZ1994}. The purpose of the present paper is to develop a potential-theoretic and Mahler-transport calculus for this boundary map: rational pullbacks give exact capacity formulae, Fourier diagonalization gives sharp equilibrium stability on the ellipse, finite Blaschke covers describe equilibrium lifts, and polynomial elimination gives inverse reconstruction from transported data.

The classical inputs are the variational definition of logarithmic capacity and equilibrium measure, Jensen--Mahler identities, finite Blaschke product boundary theory \citep[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970}, Poisson-kernel Fourier coefficients, standard facts on resultants and discriminants \citep[Chap.~12]{GKZ1994}, and Rouch\'e's theorem in its usual contour-counting form \citep[pp.~152--153]{Ahlfors1979}.  The new contribution is the way these tools combine under Joukowsky transport: a nonconstant rational pullback kernel with finite collision skeleton specializes to the degree-two collision relation \(zt=r^{-2}\), the resulting ellipse energy has a sharp Fourier deficit with extremizers, Blaschke covers admit a complete lift classification with a computable canonical source energy, and the algebraic side gives multiscale Laurent interpolation, one-scale resonance fibers, unit-circle injectivity, and Rouch\'e-stable annular recovery.

The main analytic object is the two-scale Joukowsky transform
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},\qquad r>1,
\]
and its boundary ellipse \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\).  The capacity section begins with a nonconstant rational-image theorem: for \(R=A/B\), the divided difference \(\Psi_R\) gives an exact pullback formula for \(\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))\), including ordinary collisions, branch points, and off-diagonal equal-value collisions; the finite degree of \(R:\\mathbb P^1\to\\mathbb P^1\) enters through the finite collision skeleton.  Specializing to \(J_r\), the off-diagonal collision relation is the single involution \(t=r^{-2}/z\); it misses \(\TT\times\TT\), the equilibrium deficit on \(E_r\) diagonalizes into Fourier modes with sharp constants, and finite Blaschke covers preserve the deficit precisely through the pushed-forward source measure.

The Mahler-transport part uses the same scale.  For a polynomial \(P\) with nonzero constant term, the transform \(J_r\) generates a transport polynomial \(Q_r\) whose pullback along the circle factors exactly as a product of \(P\) and its reciprocal polynomial. This identity leads to transported Jensen formulas on the ellipse, a shell-counting interpretation of the logarithmic-mean slope, and a Lee--Yang Mahler law when the zeros of \(P\) lie on the unit circle \citep{LeeYang1952I,LeeYang1952II,Mahler1962,Smyth1981,Boyd1981,EverestWard1999}. At the level of elimination theory, the same factorization yields explicit discriminant formulas, multiscale interpolation, one-scale resonance fibers, exact Lee--Yang reconstruction, and quantitative annular Rouch\'e stability \citep{GKZ1994}.

Thus the common complex-variable object throughout the main body is the boundary of a Joukowsky ellipse.  The appendices follow from this chain: Appendix \ref{sec:appendix-scale-dictionary} records the common boundary-scale dictionary, Appendix \ref{sec:elliptic-gates} gives the normalized speed spectrum of the covering \(z\mapsto z^m+c z^{-m}\), and Appendix \ref{sec:godel-lorentz} gives a capacity scaling example for finitely supported multiplicative parameters.

\paragraph{Main theorem package.}
The proof has a short dependency spine.  Classical inputs are used only as tools: Frostman--capacity variational theory, lower-truncated logarithmic energy, measurable selection, finite Blaschke boundary formulas, Jensen--Mahler identities, and standard resultant/discriminant algebra.  The new chain is as follows: the rational pullback kernel supplies the Joukowsky boundary model; the sharp Fourier theorem solves its equilibrium problem; finite Blaschke covers transport that solution through inner covers; Mahler transport attaches polynomial data to the same scale; and the inverse, endpoint, and residual-fiber statements are downstream consequences of this chain.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item \textbf{Rational capacity.}  The nonconstant rational-image theorem, Theorem \ref{thm:rational-image-capacity-pullback}, gives the extremal pullback and lift-classification principle, while Corollary \ref{cor:finite-collision-skeleton-rational-pullback} records the finite-degree collision skeleton.  Its degree-two Joukowsky specialization, Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation}, classifies the collisions as the diagonal together with \(zt=r^{-2}\), and Corollary \ref{thm:degree-two-boundary-operativity} proves that the off-diagonal graph is absent from \(\TT\times\TT\).  The exact descent theorem, Theorem \ref{thm:exact-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers}, is the bridge used later: it identifies the ellipse problem with the reduced circle variational problem and reduces covered-boundary energies to pushed-forward circle data.
  \item \textbf{Sharp ellipse equilibrium.}  The Joukowsky stability theorem, Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability}, diagonalizes the logarithmic-energy deficit on \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), proves the sharp mode constants, identifies the unique equilibrium measure, and yields \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\).
  \item \textbf{Blaschke-cover lifts.}  The finite-cover theorem and canonical-lift computation, Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} and Proposition \ref{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy}, show that \(J_r\circ B\) factors the deficit through \(B_\ast\mu\), classify all equilibrium lifts, and compute the canonical source energy from the zeros of \(B\).
  \item \textbf{Mahler and inverse transport.}  The pullback, discriminant, Jensen--Mahler, fiber, Lee--Yang, and annular stability results are Theorems \ref{thm:pullback-factorization}, \ref{thm:discriminant-factorization}, \ref{thm:elliptic-two-scale-stokes-mean}, \ref{thm:transported-jensen-shell-count}, \ref{thm:leyang-mahler-transport}, \ref{thm:leyang-elliptic-shell-separation}, \ref{thm:algebraic-mahler-transport-inverse-package}, \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction}, and \ref{thm:quantitative-annular-rouche-stability}, together with Corollaries \ref{cor:no-resonance-leyang-fiber-collapse}, \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection}, and \ref{cor:coefficient-annular-rouche-stability}.  These statements use the same scale fixed by the capacity and Fourier theorems.
\end{enumerate}
The three appendices are not additional theorem branches.  They only identify the common confocal scale and give two calibrated examples after the complex-variable results have been proved.

The paper is organized as follows. Section \ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation and records the pullback and discriminant factorizations used later. Section \ref{sec:rational-capacity} proves the nonconstant rational-image capacity theorem, records its finite collision skeleton, specializes it to the degree-two Joukowsky collision relation, proves the sharp Joukowsky equilibrium theorem, and gives the finite Blaschke-cover lift classification. Section \ref{sec:mahler-transport} establishes the Mahler identities, the shell-counting obstruction to off-circle false positives, and supporting ellipse normalizations. Section \ref{sec:discriminant} derives discriminant and resultant corollaries, inverse resonance classification, one-scale reconstruction, quantitative annular Rouch\'e stability, and the resulting scale and fiber classification. Section \ref{sec:discussion} summarizes the complex-variable consequences. Appendix \ref{sec:appendix-scale-dictionary} gives the common boundary-scale dictionary; Appendices \ref{sec:elliptic-gates} and \ref{sec:godel-lorentz} give the two short calibrated examples.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:preliminaries}
The terminology is as follows.  The Joukowsky maps, confocal ellipses, logarithmic capacity, Mahler measure, resultants, and discriminants are standard objects, with references cited where they enter the proofs.  A \emph{transport polynomial} is the polynomial \(Q_r\) in Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}; a \emph{shell} is one of the exact zero circles \(|z|=1\) or \(|z|=r^{-2}\) appearing in the resultant reconstruction theorem.  Reconstruction always means exact recovery from the complete data explicitly stated in the relevant theorem.  Thus the preliminaries below are the analytic core of the paper: they normalize logarithmic energy and Joukowsky transport, prove coefficient descent, fix the pullback factorization, and then extract the discriminant factorization used by the later inverse and capacity statements.  The multiplicative-coordinate notation used only in the calibrated application is introduced locally in Appendix \ref{sec:godel-lorentz}.

All logarithmic energies in the paper use one global convention.  If \(X\subset\CC\) is compact and \(\sigma\) is a Borel probability measure on \(X\), put
\[
I_X^{(M)}(\sigma):=\iint_{X\times X}\max\{\log|z-t|,-M\}\,\dd\sigma(z)\dd\sigma(t)
\qquad(M>0)
\]
and define
\[
I_X(\sigma):=\lim_{M\to\infty}I_X^{(M)}(\sigma)\in[-\infty,\infty).
\]
The kernels decrease as \(M\) increases, so this lower-truncated limit is well-defined; a measure is called \emph{finite-energy} when \(I_X(\sigma)>-\infty\).  When the compact set is clear from context we write simply \(I(\sigma)\).  For a rational map \(R\) with no pole on \(X\), an \emph{admissible finite-energy lift} means a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(X\) for which \(I_{R(X)}(R_\ast\mu)>-\infty\), equivalently for which the lower-truncated pullback energy is finite.  This is not a condition on the ordinary logarithmic energy of \(\mu\) on the source set.  The pullback kernels below are always interpreted by this same lower-truncated convention, including diagonal, branch, and off-diagonal collision singularities.  Circular logarithmic means are the corresponding one-variable Lebesgue integrals, equivalently these lower-truncated limits; the logarithmic singularity at a zero of a nonzero polynomial is locally integrable, so Jensen's formula applies with this convention.  On the unit circle we also write
\[
I_{\TT}(\sigma):=\iint_{\TT\times\TT}\log|z-t|\,\dd\sigma(z)\dd\sigma(t)
\]
for the same lower-truncated energy.  If \(\widehat\sigma(k)=\int_{\TT}z^{-k}\,\dd\sigma(z)\), then for finite-energy circle measures the classical Fourier identity \citep[Chap.~1]{Duren1970} is
\[
I_{\TT}(\sigma)=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat\sigma(k)|^2}{k},
\]
with the series understood as a finite real number exactly in the finite-energy case.  Abel regularization of the circle kernel is used below only as a proof device for this same lower-truncated limit under the finite-energy hypothesis.  Ordinary logarithmic means such as \(\int_{\TT}\log|F(z)|\,\dd\sigma(z)\) are always the corresponding lower-truncated limits unless the integrand is continuous on the support.

The first boundary model is the normalized covering of a Joukowsky ellipse. For \(c\in(0,1)\cap\QQ\) and \(m\in\NN\), the elliptic covering is
\[
J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})=e^{im\theta}+c\,e^{-im\theta}\qquad (\theta\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ),
\]
and its image ellipse is
\[
E_c=\bigl\{(1+c)\cos t+i(1-c)\sin t:\ t\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\bigr\}.
\]
For \(r>0\), we also use the two-scale Joukowsky map
\[
J_r(z):=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},
\qquad
E_r:=J_r(\TT),
\qquad
\TT:=\{e^{i\theta}:\theta\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\}.
\]
If \(f\in L^1(\TT)\), its Fourier coefficients are
\[
\widehat f(k):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} f(\theta)e^{-ik\theta}\,\dd\theta
\qquad (k\in\ZZ).
\]

The second boundary model is the algebraic transport through \(J_r\). Throughout the algebraic transport statements, \(K\) denotes a field of characteristic zero; whenever absolute values, circles, ellipses, or logarithmic integrals are used, the notation is specialized to a subfield of \(\CC\), and in the analytic Mahler section to \(\CC\) itself.  All roots of polynomials over \(K\) are taken in a fixed algebraic closure \(\overline K\), always with multiplicity.  Repeated roots are allowed, and in that case the relevant discriminant is zero.  For a degree-\(n\) polynomial \(F(x)=b_n\prod_{j=1}^n(x-\alpha_j)\) with \(b_n\ne0\), we use the resultant normalization
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_x(F):=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}b_n^{-1}\operatorname{Res}_x(F,F')
=b_n^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)^2,
\]
where the displayed root formula is interpreted in \(\overline K\); its value lies in \(K\).  The resultant is the standard Sylvester resultant in the displayed order; equivalently, for every polynomial \(A(w)\) and every affine linear polynomial \(a(z)w+b(z)\) in the variable \(w\),
\[
\operatorname{Res}_w\bigl(A(w),a(z)w+b(z)\bigr)=(-a(z))^{\deg A}A\!\left(-\frac{b(z)}{a(z)}\right).
\]
In particular it satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Res}_x\bigl(A(x),c(\beta-x)\bigr)=c^{\deg A}A(\beta)
\]
for every polynomial \(A\) and scalar \(c\).  Equivalently,
\[
\operatorname{Res}_x(A(x),c(x-\beta))=(-c)^{\deg A}A(\beta).
\]
In particular, because \(r z^2-wz+r^{-1}=z\bigl(J_r(z)-w\bigr)\) is linear in \(w\), this convention gives
\[
\operatorname{Res}_w(Q_r(w),rz^2-wz+r^{-1})=z^nQ_r(J_r(z)),
\]
not \((-z)^nQ_r(J_r(z))\).  If \(K\subseteq\CC\), the same symbols may also be evaluated at complex points.  If \(K\) is arbitrary, identities involving \(J_r(z)\) are identities in the Laurent polynomial ring over \(K(r)\) after adjoining the displayed variable \(z^{\pm1}\).  The following convention fixes the normalization used everywhere below.

\begin{definition}[Reciprocal and transport normalization]\label{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}
Let
\[
P(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k u^k=a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(u-z_j)\in K[u],
\qquad
n\ge 1,
\qquad
 a_n a_0\ne0,
\]
with roots taken in a fixed algebraic closure of \(K\).  Its reciprocal polynomial is the root-normalized reciprocal
\[
P^{\vee}(u):=a_n^{-1}u^nP(1/u)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-u z_j),
\]
so the leading coefficient of \(P^\vee\) is \(a_0/a_n\) and the constant coefficient is \(1\).  The unnormalized reciprocal is \(u^nP(1/u)=a_nP^\vee(u)\).  For an indeterminate \(r\), or for \(r\in K^\times\), the associated Joukowsky transport polynomial is
\[
Q_r(w):=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(w-r z_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)\in K(r)[w].
\]
Thus \(Q_r\) is normalized with leading coefficient \(a_0\).  If a monic transport polynomial is required, we write
\[
\widetilde Q_r(w):=a_0^{-1}Q_r(w).
\]
The phrase ``monic Lee--Yang input'' always refers to \(P\) being monic in the variable \(u\); the induced \(Q_r\) has leading coefficient \(a_0=(-1)^n\prod_j z_j\), while \(\widetilde Q_r\) is the corresponding monic transport polynomial with the same zero divisor.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient descent for the transport polynomial]\label{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}
In Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, the expression
\[
Q_r(w)=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(w-r z_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)
\]
is independent of the ordering of the roots and belongs to \(K(r)[w]\).  The same conclusion holds after specializing \(r\in K^\times\), in which case \(Q_r\in K[w]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to prove that each coefficient of \(Q_r\) lies in the indicated coefficient field.  The coefficient of \(w^{n-\ell}\) is \((-1)^\ell a_0\) times the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(\ell\) in the quantities \(r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\).  Expanding this elementary symmetric polynomial gives a finite \(\ZZ[r,r^{-1}]\)-linear combination of monomials
\[
z_1^{e_1}\cdots z_n^{e_n},
\qquad e_j\in\{-1,0,1\},
\]
summed over subsets in a way that is invariant under every permutation of the root labels.  Since \(a_0\ne0\), all roots are nonzero, and multiplication by a sufficiently large power of \(z_1\cdots z_n\) converts each such symmetric Laurent polynomial into an ordinary symmetric polynomial in \(z_1,
\dots,z_n\).  By the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials, it is a polynomial expression in the elementary symmetric functions of the roots and in \((z_1\cdots z_n)^{-1}\), with coefficients in \(\ZZ[r,r^{-1}]\).

The elementary symmetric functions of the \(z_j\) are \((-1)^k a_{n-k}/a_n\), and \((z_1\cdots z_n)^{-1}=(-1)^n a_n/a_0\).  Hence every coefficient of \(Q_r\) belongs to \(K(r)\).  The construction used only symmetric expressions in the multiset of roots, so it is independent of the chosen ordering.  If \(r\in K^\times\) is specialized, the same formulas have coefficients in \(K\), proving \(Q_r\in K[w]\).
\end{proof}

The normalizations just fixed are used throughout the algebraic part of the article: the same normalization controls Mahler means, zero shells, and one-scale resultants, and it is compatible with the Fourier-cover parameter recovery recorded in Appendix \ref{sec:elliptic-gates}. We use the zero set notation \(\mathrm{Zero}(F)\) for a polynomial \(F\), and the standard resultant/discriminant notation \(\operatorname{Res}\) and \(\operatorname{Disc}\) \citep{GKZ1994}. For \(\rho>0\), we define the logarithmic circular mean
\[
\mathcal M_\rho(P):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|P(\rho e^{i\theta})|\,\dd\theta,
\]
so that \(\mathcal M_1(P)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of \(P\) \citep{Mahler1962,EverestWard1999}.

The next two identities are the algebraic input for the Mahler, discriminant, and one-scale reconstruction statements in Sections \ref{sec:mahler-transport} and \ref{sec:discriminant}.

\begin{theorem}[Pullback factorization]\label{thm:pullback-factorization}
Let \(P\), \(P^\vee\), and \(Q_r\) be as in Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, and let \(r\) be an indeterminate or an element of \(K^\times\). Then \(Q_r(w)\in K(r)[w]\), and in \(K(r)[z,z^{-1}]\) one has
\[
\boxed{\;
r^n z^n\,Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)=P(z)\,P^\vee(r^2 z).
\;}
\]
If \(K\subseteq\CC\) and \(r\in K^\times\), the same identity may equivalently be evaluated at every \(z\in\CC^\times\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient assertion is Lemma \ref{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}. For each \(j\),
\[
J_r(z)-\bigl(r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\bigr)
=r(z-z_j)+r^{-1}(z^{-1}-z_j^{-1})
=\frac{(z-z_j)(r^2 z z_j-1)}{r z z_j}.
\]
Multiplying over \(j\) yields
\[
Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)
=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(z-z_j)(r^2 z z_j-1)}{r z z_j}
=r^{-n}z^{-n}\Bigl(a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
Since \(a_0=(-1)^n a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n} z_j\), we have \(a_0\prod_j z_j^{-1}=(-1)^n a_n\). Therefore
\[
Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)
=r^{-n}z^{-n}\,a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\cdot (-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
The first factor is \(P(z)\), while
\[
P^\vee(r^2 z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-r^2 z z_j)=(-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
Combining the two identities gives the stated factorization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Discriminant factorization]\label{thm:discriminant-factorization}
In the setting of Theorem \ref{thm:pullback-factorization},
\[
\boxed{\;
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)
=\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)\,
r^{-n(n-1)}
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2 z_i z_j-1)^2.
\;}
\]
The identity is taken in \(\overline K(r)\), and both sides are in fact elements of \(K(r)\).  It remains valid without assuming that \(P\) is separable; if roots collide, the corresponding discriminants vanish.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(w_j:=r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)=a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(w_i-w_j)^2.
\]
Using
\[
w_i-w_j
=r(z_i-z_j)+r^{-1}(z_i^{-1}-z_j^{-1})
=\frac{(z_i-z_j)(r^2 z_i z_j-1)}{r z_i z_j},
\]
we obtain
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)
=a_0^{2n-2}r^{-n(n-1)}
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i-z_j)^2
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2 z_i z_j-1)^2
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}.
\]
Now
\[
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}
=\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{n} z_i\Bigr)^{-2(n-1)},
\]
and \(a_0=(-1)^n a_n\prod_i z_i\) implies
\[
a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}=a_n^{2n-2}.
\]
Since
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)=a_n^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i-z_j)^2,
\]
the desired identity follows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Scale recognition consequences}\label{subsec:endpoint-synthesis}

The capacity, Fourier, Blaschke, and Mahler-inverse results now determine the boundary scale from two complementary kinds of data.  The remaining statements in this section record the resulting recognition criterion and the exact residual fiber.  The next proposition is only a synthesis input for the rigidity theorem: it packages the equilibrium-lift consequence of the exact boundary descent and finite Blaschke--Joukowsky theorems together with the one-scale Lee--Yang annular reconstruction consequence.

\begin{proposition}[Boundary-scale recognition inputs]\label{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis}
Fix \(r>1\) and let \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\ge1\), and write
  \[
  I(\sigma):=\iint\log|x-y|\,\dd\sigma(x)\dd\sigma(y)
  \]
  with lower truncation when necessary.  Then
  \[
  \sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)} I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=\log r.
  \]
  For \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\), equality in
  \[
  I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)\le \log r
  \]
  holds if and only if
  \[
  B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
  \]
  Thus all equilibrium lifts of the covered ellipse are exactly the Haar lifts through \(B\), and the canonical absolutely continuous lift is
  \[
  \dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})=\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\,\dd\theta.
  \]

  \item Let \(P\in\CC[z]\) be a monic polynomial of positive degree whose zeros all lie on \(\TT\), and let \(Q_r\) be its transport polynomial in the normalization of Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}.  Then the full one-scale polynomial \(Q_r\) determines \(P\) uniquely.  Equivalently, for every \(\rho\) with \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\), \(P\) is the unique monic factor of
  \[
  S_{r,Q_r}(z):=z^{\deg P}Q_r(J_r(z))
  \]
  whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\); the complementary factor has all zeros in \(\{|z|<\rho\}\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the analytic coordinate recovers the boundary scale through \(\sup I=\log r\), while the algebraic coordinate is recognized at that recovered scale by the intrinsic Lee--Yang shell test of Theorem \ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \textup{(a)}, Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} gives, for every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\),
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=I(J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu))\le\log r,
\]
with equality exactly when \(B_\ast\mu\) is Haar measure.  This proves both the supremum and the equality criterion.  The displayed canonical lift is the canonical measure constructed in the same finite-cover theorem, and Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula} gives that its pushforward by \(B\) is Haar measure.

For \textup{(b)}, apply Theorem \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction} to the fixed scale \(r>1\).  It gives injectivity of \(P\mapsto Q_r\) on the monic Lee--Yang class and constructive recovery from \((r,Q_r)\).  Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection} identifies this constructive recovery with the stated annular factor selection for every \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\).  The final sentence combines \textup{(a)} with Theorem \ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Realizable boundary-scale data]\label{def:realizable-boundary-scale-data}
Let \(\mathcal L\) denote the class of monic polynomials of positive degree whose zeros, counted with multiplicity, lie on \(\TT\).  A \emph{realizable boundary-scale datum} is a pair
\[
\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]
\]
for which there are a number \(r>1\), a finite Blaschke product \(B\) of positive degree, and a polynomial \(P\in\mathcal L\) such that \(Q=Q_r(P)\) is the transport polynomial of \(P\) in the normalization of Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, and
\[
e=\mathcal E(B,r):=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)} I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu),
\]
with logarithmic energy interpreted by lower truncation as in Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis}.  The set of all such pairs is denoted by \(\mathfrak B\).  A triple \((B,r,P)\) satisfying these conditions is called a \emph{realization} of \(\mathfrak b\), and the associated ellipse is \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\).
\end{definition}

The next theorem is a compressed synthesis consequence, not a second data-realization theory.  The central complex-variable package has already supplied the rational capacity kernel, the sharp Fourier deficit, the Blaschke lift theorem, and the inverse-resonance/annular recovery theorems.  The theorem states exactly what their completed capacity--Mahler chain recognizes: positivity of the energy coordinate fixes the scale, the pulled-back polynomial must split into the two reciprocal shells dictated by that scale, and the only forgotten coordinate is the finite Blaschke cover together with its Haar-lift fiber.

\begin{theorem}[Boundary-scale recognition and residual fiber]\label{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}\label{prop:boundary-scale-recognition-obstruction}\label{cor:capacity-mahler-fiber-classification}
The following three assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Alph*)}]
\item \emph{Scale and polynomial recognition.}  Let \(\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]\), with \(\deg_w Q=n_{\mathfrak b}\ge1\).  Put
\[
r_{\mathfrak b}:=\exp(e),
\]
and, when \(e>0\), define
\[
S_{\mathfrak b}(z):=z^{n_{\mathfrak b}}Q\!\left(J_{r_{\mathfrak b}}(z)\right).
\]
Then \(\mathfrak b\) is a realizable boundary-scale datum in the sense of Definition \ref{def:realizable-boundary-scale-data} if and only if the following intrinsic shell condition holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item \(e>0\);
\item for some \(\rho\) with \(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}<\rho<1\), the polynomial \(S_{\mathfrak b}\) has exactly \(n_{\mathfrak b}\) zeros in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\), counted with multiplicity; if \(P_{\mathfrak b}\) is the monic factor determined by those outer zeros, then all zeros of \(P_{\mathfrak b}\) lie on \(\TT\) and
\[
S_{\mathfrak b}(z)=r_{\mathfrak b}^{n_{\mathfrak b}}P_{\mathfrak b}(z)\,z^{n_{\mathfrak b}}P_{\mathfrak b}\!\left(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}z^{-1}\right).
\]
\end{enumerate}
When these equivalent conditions hold, every realization \((B,r,P)\) of \(\mathfrak b\) has
\[
r=r_{\mathfrak b},
\qquad
E_r=E_{r_{\mathfrak b}},
\qquad
P=P_{\mathfrak b}.
\]
Moreover, for every \(\rho\) with \(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}<\rho<1\), \(P_{\mathfrak b}\) is the unique monic factor of \(S_{\mathfrak b}\) whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\), and the complementary factor has all zeros in \(\{|z|<\rho\}\).

\item \emph{Two-test obstruction.}  For every \(\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]\), with \(\deg Q\ge1\), exactly one of the following alternatives holds.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item \(e\le0\).  In this case \(\mathfrak b\) is not realizable as a boundary-scale datum.
  \item \(e>0\), but for \(r=\exp(e)\) the polynomial \(z^{\deg Q}Q(J_r(z))\) has no factorization
  \[
  z^{\deg Q}Q(J_r(z))=r^{\deg Q}P(z)\,z^{\deg Q}P(r^{-2}z^{-1})
  \]
  with \(P\in\mathcal L\).  In this case \(\mathfrak b\) is not realizable.
  \item There is a unique monic \(P\in\mathcal L\) for which the displayed factorization holds with \(r=\exp(e)\).  In this case \(\mathfrak b\) is realizable, all its realizations have the same scale \(r\), the same ellipse \(E_r\), and the same source polynomial \(P\).
\end{enumerate}
Thus the pair \((e,Q)\) never reconstructs the finite Blaschke product; once the scale and source polynomial have been recovered, the remaining choices are precisely finite Blaschke covers together with probability measures lifting Haar measure under those covers.

\item \emph{Residual Blaschke-lift fiber.}  Fix \(n\ge1\), and let \(\mathfrak X_n\) be the class of all quadruples \((B,r,P,\mu)\) in which \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product of positive degree, \(r>1\), \(P\in\mathcal L\) has degree \(n\), \(Q_r(P)\) is its Lee--Yang transport polynomial, and
\[
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Define
\[
(B,r,P,\mu)\sim(C,s,\widetilde P,\nu)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r=s\ \text{ and }\ P=\widetilde P.
\]
Attach to a quadruple the datum
\[
\mathfrak D(B,r,P,\mu):=\bigl(\mathcal E(B,r),\,Q_r(P),\,(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu\bigr),
\]
where \(\mathcal E(B,r)\) is the covered-boundary energy supremum from Definition \ref{def:realizable-boundary-scale-data}, and put
\[
\mu_r:=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right).
\]
Then \(\mathfrak D\) is constant on \(\sim\)-classes and induces a bijection
\[
\overline{\mathfrak D}:\mathfrak X_n/\!\sim\ \longrightarrow
\left\{\bigl(\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r\bigr): r>1,
\ P\in\mathcal L,
\ \deg P=n\right\}.
\]
Equivalently, if two admissible quadruples \((B,r,P,\mu)\) and \((C,s,\widetilde P,\nu)\) have the same datum \(\mathfrak D\), then
\[
r=s,
\qquad
E_r=E_s,
\qquad
P=\widetilde P,
\qquad
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=(J_s\circ C)_\ast\nu=\mu_r.
\]
For every \(\rho\) with \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\), the recovered polynomial \(P\) is also the unique monic degree-\(n\) factor of \(z^nQ_r(P)(J_r(z))\) whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\).  Conversely, the complete fiber over \((\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r)\) is exactly
\[
\left\{(B,r,P,\mu): B\text{ a finite Blaschke product of positive degree and }
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right\}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \textup{(A)}, first assume that \(\mathfrak b\) is realizable and choose a realization \((B,r,P)\).  Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} gives
\[
e=\mathcal E(B,r)=\log r,
\]
so \(e>0\) and \(r=r_{\mathfrak b}\).  Since \(Q=Q_r(P)\) with \(P\in\mathcal L\), Theorem \ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition} gives the displayed shell condition and identifies the outer factor as \(P\).  Conversely, suppose that the shell condition holds.  The same intrinsic recognition theorem gives a monic Lee--Yang polynomial \(P_{\mathfrak b}\in\mathcal L\) such that \(Q=Q_{r_{\mathfrak b}}(P_{\mathfrak b})\).  Taking the degree-one Blaschke product \(B(z)=z\), Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} gives \(\mathcal E(B,r_{\mathfrak b})=\log r_{\mathfrak b}=e\).  Hence \((B,r_{\mathfrak b},P_{\mathfrak b})\) is a realization.  The uniqueness of the recovered source polynomial and the annular selection statement are exactly Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection}, applied after the scale has been fixed by the energy coordinate.

For \textup{(B)}, the three alternatives are the trichotomy \(e\le0\), \(e>0\) with no reciprocal shell factorization by a polynomial in \(\mathcal L\), and \(e>0\) with such a factorization.  Part \textup{(A)} rules out realizability in the first two cases and gives realizability in the third.  In the third case uniqueness follows because the two factors occupy disjoint shells, \(\TT\) and \(r^{-2}\TT\); hence the monic outer factor is uniquely determined by the zeros in any annulus \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\).  Part \textup{(A)} also fixes the scale and ellipse in every realization, while Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} and Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} identify the residual maximizing measures as exactly the Haar lifts through the chosen finite cover.

For \textup{(C)}, if \((B,r,P,\mu)\in\mathfrak X_n\), Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} gives
\[
\mathcal E(B,r)=\log r,
\qquad
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right)=\mu_r.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathfrak D(B,r,P,\mu)=\bigl(\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r\bigr),
\]
which depends only on \((r,P)\).  This proves that \(\mathfrak D\) is constant on \(\sim\)-classes and that the displayed map is surjective.  If two quadruples have the same datum, equality of the first components gives \(\log r=\log s\), hence \(r=s\).  Equality of the second components then becomes \(Q_r(P)=Q_r(\widetilde P)\), and Theorem \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction} gives \(P=\widetilde P\).  The equality of the covered equilibrium images is the pushforward identity above.  With the recovered scale fixed, Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection} gives the stated annular factor selection.  Finally, the same displayed formula for \(\mathfrak D\) shows that every finite Blaschke cover and every Haar-lift measure over that cover lies in the fiber over \((\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r)\), and the injectivity just proved shows that no different scale or source polynomial can lie in the fiber.
\end{proof}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Several themes emerging from the core sections deserve emphasis. First, the Joukowsky ellipse is not merely a convenient parametrized curve: it is a conformal boundary object whose logarithmic capacity, annular modulus, and Mahler averages are all controlled by the same scale parameter. In this sense, the transport identity of Theorem \ref{thm:pullback-factorization} and the degree-two capacity formula of Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation} should be viewed as potential-theoretic bridges between circle data and ellipse data, parallel to the classical role of conformal maps in logarithmic potential theory \citep{Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,Pommerenke1992}. The discriminant and resultant formulas show that the same bridge also preserves delicate elimination-theoretic information \citep{GKZ1994}.

Second, the boundary-scale recognition and residual-fiber theorem is a deliberately secondary classification built from the main analytic and algebraic package.  The capacity pullback kernel with its branch and collision terms, the exact degree-two boundary descent, the sharp Fourier deficit on \(E_r\), the finite Blaschke lift theorem, the inverse resonance classification, the no-resonance Lee--Yang fiber collapse, and the explicit Rouch\'e envelope for annular recovery imply that the Blaschke-cover equilibrium supremum is \(\log r\), the transport polynomial is injective at fixed Lee--Yang scale, and the annular factor selection is stable.  Thus the potential-theoretic normalization and the algebraic inverse normalization determine the same ellipse; the remaining fiber is exactly the finite-cover Haar-lift ambiguity classified in Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}.

The appendices record calibrated applications of this boundary scale.  Appendix \ref{sec:appendix-scale-dictionary} begins with a formal boundary-scale theorem proving that these coordinates factor through the already established capacity scale and cannot add a new realization alternative to Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}.  Appendix \ref{sec:elliptic-gates} studies the normalized speed spectrum of an elliptic covering; its valuation line is only a coefficient readout for rational scales in a fixed covering coordinate.  Appendix \ref{sec:godel-lorentz} translates finitely supported multiplicative parameters into the same Joukowsky capacity scale.  These applications are included only to calibrate the confocal scale used in the main complex-variable theorems.

\section{Common boundary scale dictionary}\label{sec:appendix-scale-dictionary}

The following theorem records how the two appendix coordinates factor through the boundary scale determined by Theorems \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation}, \ref{thm:exact-boundary-descent-joukowsky-covers}, \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability}, \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover}, \ref{thm:algebraic-mahler-transport-inverse-package}, \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction}, and \ref{thm:quantitative-annular-rouche-stability}.  The same scale is then used in the three-part recognition theorem, Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}.

\begin{theorem}[Boundary-scale factorization for auxiliary coordinates]\label{thm:auxiliary-boundary-scale-factorization}
For \(L\ge 1\), put
\[
\Gamma_L:=\bigl\{\,L^{1/2}z+L^{-1/2}z^{-1}:|z|=1\,\bigr\}.
\]
The boundary normalizations used in the theorem package above can be placed on this single confocal family by the following elementary changes of scale.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item If \(c\in(0,1)\) and \(m\in\NN\), then
  \[
  J_{c,m}(\TT)=c^{1/2}\Gamma_{c^{-1}}.
  \]
  The covering multiplicity \(m\) changes only the parametrization of this boundary, while the Fourier support and valuation formula in the Fourier-cover application section recover the same scale parameter through \(L=c^{-1}\).

  \item If \(r>1\), then the transport ellipse in the sharp-equilibrium, Blaschke-cover, and Mahler-transport theorems is
  \[
  E_r=J_r(\TT)=\Gamma_{r^2}.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
In this common notation the capacity normalization is not merely formal: for every \(L>1\),
\[
\operatorname{cap}(\Gamma_L)=L^{1/2},
\]
and hence
\[
\operatorname{cap}(J_{c,m}(\TT))=1,
\qquad
\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r.
\]
Thus the spectral, Mahler, and elimination probes in the main theorem share the same elementary boundary notation: after the explicit changes of scale
\[
L=c^{-1},\qquad L=r^2,
\]
they are written as probes of the same one-parameter Joukowsky boundary \(\Gamma_L\), up to the harmless homothety \(c^{1/2}\) in the elliptic-covering normalization.  This gives a common notation for the scale comparisons; the multiplicative-parameter application repeats the same dictionary locally in Appendix \ref{sec:godel-lorentz}.
Moreover this dictionary is auxiliary in the following precise sense.  Fix \(n\ge1\), let \(\mathfrak X_n\), \(\sim\), and \(\mathfrak D\) be the core objects of Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}, and let \(\mathfrak X_n^{\rm aux}\) be obtained from \(\mathfrak X_n\) by adjoining any finite list of auxiliary coordinates whose boundary contribution is one of the displayed forms \(J_{c,m}(\TT)\) or \(\Gamma_L\).  Let
\[
\pi:\mathfrak X_n^{\rm aux}\longrightarrow\mathfrak X_n
\]
be the forgetting map and set \(\mathfrak D^{\rm aux}:=\mathfrak D\circ\pi\).  Then \(\mathfrak D^{\rm aux}\) is constant on the equivalence relation pulled back from \(\sim\) and induces exactly the same bijection as \(\overline{\mathfrak D}\).  In particular, adjoining the elliptic-cover Fourier coordinate, the valuation readout, or the capacity-scaled multiplicative coordinate cannot change the recovered scale \(r\), the Lee--Yang source polynomial \(P\), the Haar-lift fiber, or the two recognition tests in Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(z=e^{i\phi}\),
\[
\Gamma_L=\bigl\{(L^{1/2}+L^{-1/2})\cos\phi+i(L^{1/2}-L^{-1/2})\sin\phi:\phi\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\bigr\},
\]
so \(\Gamma_L\) is exactly the confocal Joukowsky ellipse with focal distance \(2\).  For the elliptic covering, take \(L=c^{-1}\) and set \(z=e^{im\theta}\).  Then
\[
c^{1/2}\bigl(L^{1/2}z+L^{-1/2}z^{-1}\bigr)
=c^{1/2}\bigl(c^{-1/2}e^{im\theta}+c^{1/2}e^{-im\theta}\bigr)
=e^{im\theta}+c e^{-im\theta},
\]
which proves \(J_{c,m}(\TT)=c^{1/2}\Gamma_{c^{-1}}\).  Because \(\theta\mapsto e^{im\theta}\) is an \(m\)-fold covering of \(\TT\), the integer \(m\) affects only how the same ellipse is traversed; the scale is still determined by \(c\).  This proves the asserted scale factorization for the elliptic-covering coordinates.

Finally, by definition,
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}=(r^2)^{1/2}z+(r^2)^{-1/2}z^{-1},
\]
so \(J_r(\TT)=\Gamma_{r^2}\).  The two asserted changes of scale therefore put the relevant boundaries in the single family \(\Gamma_L\), and the only additional operation in the first model is the explicitly displayed scalar homothety.

It remains to verify the capacity normalization.  If \(L>1\), then \(\Gamma_L=E_{L^{1/2}}\), and Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} gives \(\operatorname{cap}(\Gamma_L)=L^{1/2}\).  Logarithmic capacity scales under homotheties by \(\operatorname{cap}(aK)=|a|\operatorname{cap}(K)\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{cap}(J_{c,m}(\TT))=
\operatorname{cap}(c^{1/2}\Gamma_{c^{-1}})=c^{1/2}c^{-1/2}=1,
\]
while \(E_r=\Gamma_{r^2}\) gives the other displayed capacity identity.  These identities are the promised nonredundant compatibility assertion: the boundary parameter \(L\) and the Mahler transport scale \(r\) induce the same logarithmic-capacity scale after the explicitly stated normalizations.

It remains only to prove the factorization assertion.  By definition \(\mathfrak D^{\rm aux}=\mathfrak D\circ\pi\), so two auxiliary objects have the same auxiliary datum if and only if their core projections have the same core datum.  The equivalence relation on \(\mathfrak X_n^{\rm aux}\) is the pullback of \(\sim\), hence Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity} gives the induced bijection
\[
\mathfrak X_n^{\rm aux}/\pi^{-1}(\!\sim\!)\ \cong
\left\{\bigl(\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r\bigr): r>1,
\ P\in\mathcal L,
\ \deg P=n\right\}.
\]
The auxiliary theorem attaches only the scale coordinates just computed: the Fourier-cover parameter contributes \(c\) and a covering multiplicity lying in the forgotten fiber, and the multiplicative parameter contributes the same \(L=N(s)\) capacity scale through \(\Gamma_L\).  Since the core datum after forgetting is unchanged, the recovered \(r\), the recovered Lee--Yang polynomial \(P\), and the Haar-lift fiber are exactly those supplied by Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}.  Finally, realizability and nonrealizability are decided after projection by Theorem \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}; therefore no auxiliary coordinate changes either of the two existing tests.
\end{proof}

\section{Elliptic covering speed spectrum}\label{sec:elliptic-gates}

This appendix records the calibrated Fourier calculation for the one-parameter family \(J_{c,m}\).  When \(c\) is rational, the valuation identity below is only a readout of the rational Fourier coefficients after the Joukowsky scale has already been fixed; it is not an arithmetic rigidity theorem and has no role in the main complex-variable theorem chain.  The observable belongs to the normalized covering coordinate and is not an invariant of the unparametrized ellipse under arbitrary reparametrization.  No further reconstruction or rigidity theorem is used in the main paper.

In this appendix, \(\mathbb P\) denotes the set of prime numbers.  For \(x\in\QQ^\times\) and \(p\in\mathbb P\), \(\nu_p(x)\) is the normalized \(p\)-adic valuation, characterized by \(\nu_p(p)=1\) and additivity under multiplication.

\begin{proposition}[Normalized speed spectrum of an elliptic covering]\label{prop:short-elliptic-speed-spectrum}
Let \(0<c<1\), let \(m\in\NN\), and put
\[
J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})=e^{im\theta}+c e^{-im\theta}.
\]
Define the logarithmic speed defect by
\[
u_{c,m}(\theta):=\log\left|\frac{\dd}{\dd\theta}J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})\right|-
\log m.
\]
Then
\[
u_{c,m}(\theta)=\frac12\log\bigl(1-2c\cos(2m\theta)+c^2\bigr)
=-\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c^n}{n}\cos(2mn\theta),
\]
and its Fourier coefficients are
\[
\widehat u_{c,m}(0)=0,
\qquad
\widehat u_{c,m}(\pm 2mn)=-\frac{c^n}{2n}\quad(n\ge1),
\qquad
\widehat u_{c,m}(k)=0\quad(k\notin2m\ZZ).
\]
If \(c\in\QQ\), then for every prime \(p\) and every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\nu_p\!\bigl(\widehat u_{c,m}(2mn)\bigr)=n\nu_p(c)-\nu_p(2n).
\]
Consequently the normalized Fourier family determines the support lattice \(2m\ZZ\), the covering multiplicity \(m\), and the ellipse scale \(c=-2\widehat u_{c,m}(2m)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating gives
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd\theta}J_{c,m}(e^{i\theta})=im\bigl(e^{im\theta}-c e^{-im\theta}\bigr),
\]
so
\[
u_{c,m}(\theta)=\log|e^{im\theta}-c e^{-im\theta}|=\frac12\log|1-ce^{-2im\theta}|^2.
\]
Since \(|c|<1\), the logarithmic series is uniformly convergent on the circle:
\[
\log(1-ce^{-2im\theta})=-\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c^n}{n}e^{-2imn\theta}.
\]
Adding its complex conjugate and dividing by two gives the displayed cosine expansion and therefore the Fourier coefficients.  The valuation formula follows from additivity of \(\nu_p\) and from \(\nu_p(-1)=0\).  The nonzero support is exactly \(2m\ZZ\setminus\{0\}\), so it determines \(m\); the first positive coefficient then gives \(c=-2\widehat u_{c,m}(2m)\).
\end{proof}

\section{Capacity-scaled multiplicative parameters}\label{sec:godel-lorentz}

This appendix records one calibrated application: finitely supported multiplicative parameters can be used as capacity parameters for the same Joukowsky ellipse family.  The displayed Lorentz matrix is only a coordinate chart for this scale and the proposition below is a derived capacity-scale dictionary.  No conclusion beyond these elementary scale identities is asserted here.

Continue to write \(\mathbb P\) for the set of prime numbers and \(\nu_p\) for the normalized \(p\)-adic valuation; set \(\nu_p(0)=+\infty\).  The finitely supported parameter monoid is
\[
\mathsf P:=\bigl\{\,s:\mathbb P\to\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}\;:\;s(p)=0\text{ for all but finitely many }p\,\bigr\},
\]
with pointwise addition and norm
\[
N(s):=\prod_{p\in\mathbb P}p^{s(p)}\in\NN_{>0}.
\]
If \(a>1\) is a square-free integer, its exponent vector is the element \(s_a\in\mathsf P\) defined by \(s_a(p)=\nu_p(a)\).  No optimization property of such vectors is used below.

\begin{proposition}[Capacity-scaled multiplicative dictionary]\label{prop:capacity-scaled-multiplicative-dictionary}
For \(s\in\mathsf P\), put \(L=N(s)\),
\[
E_J(s):=\bigl\{L^{1/2}z+L^{-1/2}z^{-1}:|z|=1\bigr\},
\]
and
\[
\Lambda(s):=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{L^{1/2}+L^{-1/2}}{2}&\dfrac{L^{1/2}-L^{-1/2}}{2}\\[2pt]
\dfrac{L^{1/2}-L^{-1/2}}{2}&\dfrac{L^{1/2}+L^{-1/2}}{2}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(E_J(s)=\Gamma_{N(s)}\), \(\Lambda(s)\in\mathrm{SO}^+(1,1)\), and
\[
\Lambda(s)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cosh\Delta(s)&\sinh\Delta(s)\\
\sinh\Delta(s)&\cosh\Delta(s)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Delta(s)=\frac12\log N(s).
\]
Moreover \(\Lambda(s_1+s_2)=\Lambda(s_1)\Lambda(s_2)\),
\[
\rho(\Lambda(s))=N(s)^{1/2},
\qquad
\kappa_2(\Lambda(s))=N(s),
\qquad
\operatorname{cap}(E_J(s))=N(s)^{1/2},
\]
and, for \(N(s)>1\),
\[
\operatorname{Area}(E_J(s))=
\pi\bigl(N(s)-N(s)^{-1}\bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identity \(E_J(s)=\Gamma_{N(s)}\) is the definition.  Put \(\Delta(s)=\frac12\log N(s)\).  Then \(L^{1/2}=e^{\Delta(s)}\) and \(L^{-1/2}=e^{-\Delta(s)}\), so the displayed matrix is the standard Lorentz boost with entries \(\cosh\Delta(s)\) and \(\sinh\Delta(s)\).  Since \(N(s_1+s_2)=N(s_1)N(s_2)\), rapidities add, giving the homomorphism law.  The eigenvalues are \(e^{\pm\Delta(s)}\), hence the spectral radius is \(N(s)^{1/2}\) and the Euclidean condition number is \(e^{2\Delta(s)}=N(s)\).  The ellipse \(E_J(s)\) has semiaxes \(L^{1/2}+L^{-1/2}\) and \(L^{1/2}-L^{-1/2}\), so its area is \(\pi(L-L^{-1})\).  Its exterior conformal map has leading coefficient \(L^{1/2}\), giving \(\operatorname{cap}(E_J(s))=L^{1/2}\); equivalently this is Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} with \(r=L^{1/2}\).
\end{proof}
