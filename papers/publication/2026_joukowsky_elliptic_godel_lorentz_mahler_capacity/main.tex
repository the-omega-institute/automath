\begin{abstract}
\hbadness=10000
\emergencystretch=2em
We give a normalized synthesis and exact compatibility chain for the Joukowsky ellipse
\(E_r=J_r(\TT)\), where \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\) and \(r>1\).  The
load-bearing capacity theorem is degree two: the complete collision
skeleton of \(J_r(z)-J_r(t)\) is the diagonal together with
\(zt=r^{-2}\), and the second component is disjoint from \(\TT^2\).  From
this boundary descent we obtain, in one normalization,
\(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\), the equilibrium measure
\(J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/2\pi)\), the exact finite-energy Fourier deficit
with sharp constants \(1\pm r^{-2k}\), and the finite-Blaschke
Haar-lift classification; these four assertions are used as one
primary capacity block, not as separate restatements of standard facts.
The same scale then drives the algebraic
side: the pullback identity
\(r^nz^nQ_r(J_r(z))=P(z)P^\vee(r^2z)\) puts the transported divisor on
the two separated shells \(\TT\) and \(r^{-2}\TT\), gives Mahler and
discriminant identities, and supplies exact one-scale source-factor
recovery from exact complete coefficient vectors only, together with a quantitative Rouch\'e annular-divisor stability theorem.
Thus the new object of the paper is the fixed-scale compatibility chain in
which the capacity coordinate, Fourier deficit, Blaschke equality class,
transport polynomial, inverse source factor, and annular stability all
use the same Joukowsky parameter and the same reciprocal shell
\(r^{-2}\TT\).  A preliminary rational-map subsection is used
only to define the lower-truncated pullback kernel and to name branch
and equal-value collision loci; it is not a separate rational-capacity
theory.  The resulting synthesis determines the fixed-scale object
\((\log r,E_r,J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/2\pi),Q_r(P),P)\); the only residual
freedom is the finite-Blaschke Haar-lift fiber.  The final recognition
and residual-fiber statements are therefore corollaries of the
capacity--Fourier--Blaschke--Mahler--inverse--Rouch\'e chain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
The classical Joukowsky map provides one of the simplest conformal mechanisms by which circles generate ellipses and, more generally, confocal families in the plane. It therefore sits naturally at the intersection of conformal mapping, algebraic elimination, and logarithmic capacity theory \citep{Pommerenke1992,Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,SaffTotik1997,StahlTotik1992,GKZ1994}. The purpose of the present paper is not to introduce a new potential-theoretic formalism for rational maps, but to isolate the exact Joukowsky boundary scale at which several standard tools become a single computable chain.  We work throughout around
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},\qquad r>1,
\]
and the ellipse \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\).  The analytic spine is the Joukowsky boundary-capacity theorem: the exact collision factorization of \(J_r(z)-J_r(t)\), the resulting capacity and equilibrium measure of \(E_r\), the sharp Fourier expansion of the equilibrium deficit, and the finite-Blaschke lift classification.  Its algebraic continuation uses the same parameter \(r\) and the same annular separation between \(\TT\) and \(r^{-2}\TT\): the polynomial transported by \(J_r\) carries Mahler means, resultants, exact inverse reconstruction, and annular-divisor stability.

The classical inputs are the variational definition of logarithmic capacity and equilibrium measure, harmonic measure from infinity, Jensen--Mahler identities, finite Blaschke product boundary theory \citep[Sec.~1.2]{Duren1970}, Poisson-kernel Fourier coefficients, standard facts on resultants and discriminants \citep[Chap.~12]{GKZ1994}, and Rouch\'e's theorem in its usual contour-counting form \citep[pp.~152--153]{Ahlfors1979}.  These inputs are deliberately not presented as new machinery; they are the background from logarithmic potential theory, polynomial-zero geometry, and stable-polynomial/Lee--Yang theory \citep{SaffTotik1997,StahlTotik1992,RahmanSchmeisser2002,BorceaBranden2009,LeeYang1952I,LeeYang1952II}.  The contribution here is narrower: a normalized synthesis and exact compatibility chain in which the same number \(r\) is simultaneously the exterior conformal coefficient of \(E_r\), the square-root reciprocal collision scale, the sharp Fourier weight parameter, the Mahler transport scale, the inverse annular separator, and the local stability scale for Rouch\'e divisor selection.

For clarity, the conclusions are separated as follows.  The capacity variational principle, Jensen's formula, Blaschke boundary covering facts, Poisson-kernel Fourier coefficients, and Rouch\'e zero counts are classical.  The capacity value, equilibrium measure, Fourier deficit, finite-Blaschke lift classification, Mahler identity, and annular shell factorization are explicit recombinations of those inputs after substituting the degree-two Joukowsky factorization.  The genuinely paper-specific content is the normalized compatibility chain: the same Joukowsky scale is shown to pass through the capacity, Fourier, Blaschke, Mahler, exact complete-vector inverse, and Rouch\'e annular-divisor layers without a change of convention.  Thus the paper does not claim a new capacity theory, a new Jensen formula, a new finite-Blaschke theory, or a broad noisy inverse theorem.  The final recognition statements are synthesis corollaries of the analytic theorem, the inverse theorem from exact complete coefficient vectors, and the local annular stability theorem.  The following roadmap is the intended reading of the results.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Capacity layer.}  The load-bearing result is the degree-two Joukowsky boundary theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:main-joukowsky-boundary-scale}.  It rests on the complete factorization
  \[
  J_r(z)-J_r(t)=(z-t)\left(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\right),
  \]
  so the collision skeleton is exactly the diagonal together with \(zt=r^{-2}\).  Since \(zt=r^{-2}\) is disjoint from \(\TT^2\), the boundary energy reduces to the circle logarithmic kernel plus an explicit nonsingular factor.  This is the source of \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\), \(J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/2\pi)\), the sharp Fourier deficit, and the finite-Blaschke Haar-lift classification.
  \item \emph{Transport layer.}  Once the same scale \(r\) is fixed, the polynomial identity
  \[
  r^nz^nQ_r(J_r(z))=P(z)P^\vee(r^2z)
  \]
  places the transported zero divisor on the two separated shells \(\TT\) and \(r^{-2}\TT\).  The Jensen--Mahler, resultant, and discriminant identities are consequences of this identity, not independent capacity statements.
  \item \emph{Inverse and stability layer.}  The one-scale reconstruction, resonance exclusions, and Rouch\'e annular-divisor theorem use the two-shell separation from the transport layer.  They recover the outer unit-circle source factor after the capacity layer has already fixed \(r\).  They do not reconstruct a finite Blaschke product.
  \item \emph{Synthesis layer.}  The final recognition and residual-fiber statements combine the preceding three layers.  They record scale compatibility: the capacity coordinate fixes \(r\), the sharp Fourier weights give an independent two-probe check of the same \(r\), the shell theorem recovers the source divisor at that same \(r\), and the Blaschke equality class is exactly the remaining measure-theoretic fiber.  Thus the fixed-scale tuple
  \[
  \bigl(\log r,E_r,J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/2\pi),Q_r(P),P\bigr)
  \]
  is determined, the same annular selector is stable under the explicit Rouch\'e coefficient envelope, and only the finite-Blaschke Haar-lift fiber remains.  This synthesis is recorded once in Corollary~\ref{thm:joukowsky-package-synthesis}; the final recognition corollaries use no additional inverse principle.
\end{enumerate}

The scope boundary is equally important.  The general rational-map material in Section~\ref{sec:rational-capacity} is auxiliary: for \(R=A/B\), the divided difference \(\Psi_R\) packages the ordinary diagonal, branch points, and off-diagonal equal-value collisions into one lower-truncated extended kernel.  It records the tautological variational identity
\[
\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))=
\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(K)} I(R_\ast\mu),
\]
with equality exactly for lifts of the equilibrium measure of \(R(K)\).  No independent rational-capacity theorem is claimed from this interface.  Its role is bookkeeping for lower-truncated kernels before the degree-two specialization.  In particular, it does not assert a closed formula for \(\operatorname{cap}(R(K))\), a Fourier diagonalization, a Blaschke lift classification, or a rational-map inverse theorem.  The substantive capacity theorem begins only with the degree-two Joukowsky map: for \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\), the complete collision skeleton is
\[
z=t\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad zt=r^{-2}.
\]
On \(\TT^2\) the second relation is impossible, and the boundary problem therefore reduces to the ordinary circle logarithmic singularity plus the explicit nonvanishing factor \(r-r^{-1}z^{-1}t^{-1}\).  From that one boundary identity, together with the standard exterior conformal normalization of capacity, the paper derives \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\), the equilibrium measure \(J_{r\ast}(\dd\theta/2\pi)\), the sharp Fourier deficit with constants \(1\pm r^{-2k}\), and the finite-Blaschke equilibrium-lift classification.  We regard precisely this chain as the main capacity theorem of the article; it is stated first in Section~\ref{sec:rational-capacity} as Theorem~\ref{thm:main-joukowsky-boundary-scale}, before the auxiliary rational pullback interface.  Only after this analytic package is fixed do we pass to the algebraic transport package: Mahler transport, discriminants, multiscale Laurent interpolation from exact complete coefficient vectors, one-scale resonance fibers, fixed-scale unit-circle zero-divisor injectivity, and Rouch\'e-stable annular divisor recovery.  The final fixed-scale factor-recovery statements are consequences of these theorems; they combine the capacity scale with the exact unit-circle annular factor theorem and do not replace the capacity, Fourier, Blaschke, inverse, or Rouch\'e theorems as the main results.

Concretely, the main analytic object is the two-scale Joukowsky transform
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},\qquad r>1,
\]
and its boundary ellipse \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\).  The capacity section states this Joukowsky theorem before the proof infrastructure.  The later auxiliary rational subsection is used only as bookkeeping: for \(R=A/B\), the divided difference \(\Psi_R\) gives the single lower-truncated pullback kernel for \(\log\operatorname{cap}(R(K))\), before any bounded-term separation; ordinary diagonal collisions, branch points, and off-diagonal equal-value collisions are all singularities of that one kernel.  The degree-two specialization is the operative theorem: the only off-diagonal Joukowsky collision relation is \(t=r^{-2}/z\); it misses \(\TT\times\TT\), the equilibrium deficit on \(E_r\) diagonalizes into Fourier modes with sharp constants, and finite Blaschke covers preserve the deficit precisely through the pushed-forward source measure.

The polynomial-transport part uses the same scale.  For a polynomial \(P\) with nonzero constant term, the transform \(J_r\) generates a polynomial \(Q_r\) whose pullback along the circle factors exactly as a product of \(P\) and its reciprocal polynomial. This identity leads to transported Jensen formulas on the ellipse, a zero-shell count for the logarithmic-mean slope, and a Jensen identity under the classical unit-circle zero condition \citep{LeeYang1952I,LeeYang1952II,Mahler1962,Smyth1981,Boyd1981,EverestWard1999}. At the level of elimination theory, the same factorization yields explicit discriminant formulas, multiscale interpolation from exact complete coefficient vectors, one-scale resonance fibers, exact unit-circle divisor reconstruction, and quantitative annular-divisor Rouch\'e stability \citep{GKZ1994,RahmanSchmeisser2002}.

Thus the common complex-variable object throughout the main body is the boundary of a Joukowsky ellipse.  The discussion closes the paper by summarizing how the capacity, Blaschke-lift, polynomial-transport, inverse, and annular-divisor results all refer back to this same ellipse scale.

The paper is organized as follows. Section \ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation and records the pullback and discriminant factorizations used later. Section \ref{sec:rational-capacity} states the main Joukowsky boundary-scale theorem first, then proves it by separating the auxiliary rational lower-truncated kernel interface from the degree-two capacity, Fourier-deficit, and finite-Blaschke lift arguments. Section \ref{sec:mahler-transport} establishes the transported Jensen identities and shell-counting consequences. Section \ref{sec:discriminant} derives discriminant and resultant corollaries, inverse resonance classification, one-scale reconstruction, quantitative annular-divisor Rouch\'e stability, and the resulting fixed-scale fiber classification. Section \ref{sec:discussion} summarizes the complex-variable consequences.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:preliminaries}
We use the following conventions throughout.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Standard objects.}  Joukowsky maps, confocal ellipses, logarithmic capacity, Mahler measure, resultants, and discriminants are used in their classical senses, with references cited where they enter the proofs.
  \item \textbf{Transport notation.}  The \emph{transport polynomial} is \(Q_r\) from Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}.  A \emph{shell} is one of the exact zero circles \(|z|=1\) or \(|z|=r^{-2}\) in the resultant reconstruction theorem.
  \item \textbf{Exact algebraic data.}  Reconstruction means exact recovery from the complete data explicitly stated in the relevant theorem; in the inverse theorem this means full coefficient vectors or projective monic-normalized coefficient vectors, not point samples, noisy samples, moment data, or truncated lists.\end{itemize}
The preliminaries normalize logarithmic energy and Joukowsky transport, prove coefficient descent, fix the pullback factorization, and extract the discriminant factorization used by the later inverse and capacity statements.

For quick reference, the symbols used most often below are as follows.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\): the two-scale Joukowsky map, with \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\) and \(r>1\) in the analytic sections.
  \item \(I_X(\sigma)\): lower-truncated logarithmic energy on \(X\), never a termwise integral of separated singular factors.
  \item \(P^\vee(u)\): the root-normalized reciprocal polynomial of \(P\) from Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}.
  \item \(Q_r(w)\): the transport polynomial attached to \(P\) and \(J_r\) in Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}.
  \item \(\mathcal L\): monic polynomials of positive degree whose zeros, with multiplicity, lie on \(\TT\).
  \item \emph{Shell}: one of the zero circles \(\TT\) and \(r^{-2}\TT\) arising from \(z^nQ_r(J_r(z))\).
\end{itemize}
All inverse statements use the exact data specified in their hypotheses.  In particular, the algebraic Mahler inverse theorem concerns complete ordered coefficient vectors, or projective monic-normalized coefficient vectors, and no theorem below asserts recovery from samples, moments, or a finite coefficient window.

\paragraph{Lower-truncated energy.}
All logarithmic energies in the paper use one global convention.  If \(X\subset\CC\) is compact and \(\sigma\) is a Borel probability measure on \(X\), put
\[
I_X^{(M)}(\sigma):=\iint_{X\times X}\max\{\log|z-t|,-M\}\,\dd\sigma(z)\dd\sigma(t)
\qquad(M>0)
\]
and define
\[
I_X(\sigma):=\lim_{M\to\infty}I_X^{(M)}(\sigma)\in[-\infty,\infty).
\]
The kernels decrease as \(M\) increases, so this lower-truncated limit is well-defined; a measure is called \emph{finite-energy} when \(I_X(\sigma)>-\infty\).  When the compact set is clear from context we write simply \(I(\sigma)\).  More generally, if \(L\) is an upper-bounded Borel kernel on a compact metric space, its integral is always the monotone lower-truncated value \(\lim_{M\to\infty}\iint\max\{L,-M\}\).  If \(b\) is bounded and Borel, then bounded terms are separated only after this single-kernel truncation:
\[
\iint(L+b)\,\dd\mu\dd\mu=\iint L\,\dd\mu\dd\mu+\iint b\,\dd\mu\dd\mu,
\]
with the first integral on each side understood by lower truncation.  This convention forbids termwise integration of singular logarithmic factors before the complete pullback kernel has been truncated.  For a rational map \(R\) with no pole on \(X\), an \emph{admissible finite-energy lift} means a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(X\) for which \(I_{R(X)}(R_\ast\mu)>-\infty\), equivalently for which the lower-truncated pullback energy is finite.  This is not a condition on the ordinary logarithmic energy of \(\mu\) on the source set.  The pullback kernels below are always interpreted by this same lower-truncated convention, including diagonal, branch, and off-diagonal collision singularities.  Circular logarithmic means are the corresponding one-variable Lebesgue integrals, equivalently these lower-truncated limits; the logarithmic singularity at a zero of a nonzero polynomial is locally integrable, so Jensen's formula applies with this convention.  On the unit circle we also write
\[
I_{\TT}(\sigma):=\iint_{\TT\times\TT}\log|z-t|\,\dd\sigma(z)\dd\sigma(t)
\]
for the same lower-truncated energy.  If \(\widehat\sigma(k)=\int_{\TT}z^{-k}\,\dd\sigma(z)\), then the circle Fourier identity proved by Abel regularization in Lemma \ref{lem:circle-log-kernel-fourier-truncation}, and consistent with the classical Hardy-space kernel expansion \citep[Chap.~1]{Duren1970}, is
\[
I_{\TT}(\sigma)=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat\sigma(k)|^2}{k},
\]
with the series understood as a finite real number exactly in the finite-energy case.  Abel regularization of the circle kernel is used below only as a proof device for this same lower-truncated limit under the finite-energy hypothesis.  Ordinary logarithmic means such as \(\int_{\TT}\log|F(z)|\,\dd\sigma(z)\) are always the corresponding lower-truncated limits unless the integrand is continuous on the support.

\paragraph{Boundary and coefficient notation.}
For \(r>0\), we use the two-scale Joukowsky map
\[
J_r(z):=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1},
\qquad
E_r:=J_r(\TT),
\qquad
\TT:=\{e^{i\theta}:\theta\in\RR/2\pi\ZZ\}.
\]
If \(f\in L^1(\TT)\), its Fourier coefficients are
\[
\widehat f(k):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi} f(\theta)e^{-ik\theta}\,\dd\theta
\qquad (k\in\ZZ).
\]

The second boundary model is the algebraic transport through \(J_r\). Throughout the algebraic transport statements, \(K\) denotes a field of characteristic zero; whenever absolute values, circles, ellipses, or logarithmic integrals are used, the notation is specialized to a subfield of \(\CC\), and in the analytic Mahler section to \(\CC\) itself.  All roots of polynomials over \(K\) are taken in a fixed algebraic closure \(\overline K\), always with multiplicity.  Repeated roots are allowed, and in that case the relevant discriminant is zero.  For a degree-\(n\) polynomial \(F(x)=b_n\prod_{j=1}^n(x-\alpha_j)\) with \(b_n\ne0\), we use the resultant normalization
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_x(F):=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}b_n^{-1}\operatorname{Res}_x(F,F')
=b_n^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)^2,
\]
where the displayed root formula is interpreted in \(\overline K\); its value lies in \(K\).  The resultant is the standard Sylvester resultant in the displayed order; equivalently, for every polynomial \(A(w)\) and every affine linear polynomial \(a(z)w+b(z)\) in the variable \(w\),
\[
\operatorname{Res}_w\bigl(A(w),a(z)w+b(z)\bigr)=(-a(z))^{\deg A}A\!\left(-\frac{b(z)}{a(z)}\right).
\]
In particular it satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Res}_x\bigl(A(x),c(\beta-x)\bigr)=c^{\deg A}A(\beta)
\]
for every polynomial \(A\) and scalar \(c\).  Equivalently,
\[
\operatorname{Res}_x(A(x),c(x-\beta))=(-c)^{\deg A}A(\beta).
\]
In particular, because \(r z^2-wz+r^{-1}=z\bigl(J_r(z)-w\bigr)\) is linear in \(w\), this convention gives
\[
\operatorname{Res}_w(Q_r(w),rz^2-wz+r^{-1})=z^nQ_r(J_r(z)),
\]
not \((-z)^nQ_r(J_r(z))\).  If \(K\subseteq\CC\), the same symbols may also be evaluated at complex points.  If \(K\) is arbitrary, identities involving \(J_r(z)\) are identities in the Laurent polynomial ring over \(K(r)\) after adjoining the displayed variable \(z^{\pm1}\).  The following convention fixes the normalization used everywhere below.

\begin{definition}[Reciprocal and transport normalization]\label{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}
Let
\[
P(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k u^k=a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(u-z_j)\in K[u],
\qquad
n\ge 1,
\qquad
 a_n a_0\ne0,
\]
with roots taken in a fixed algebraic closure of \(K\).  The monic transport associated with the zero divisor of \(P\) is
\[
\widetilde Q_r(w):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(w-r z_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\Bigr).
\]
All scalar normalizations of the transported zero divisor are converted through this monic polynomial.  Its scaled version used in the resultant identities is
\[
Q_r(w):=a_0\widetilde Q_r(w).
\]
Thus \(Q_r\) has leading coefficient \(a_0\), while \(\widetilde Q_r\) is monic.  If \(P\) is monic, then
\[
a_0=(-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^n z_j.
\]
The reciprocal polynomial is the root-normalized reciprocal
\[
P^{\vee}(u):=a_n^{-1}u^nP(1/u)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-u z_j),
\]
so the leading coefficient of \(P^\vee\) is \(a_0/a_n\) and the constant coefficient is \(1\).  The unnormalized reciprocal is \(u^nP(1/u)=a_nP^\vee(u)\).  For an indeterminate \(r\), or for \(r\in K^\times\), the expressions above lie in \(K(r)[w]\) by Lemma \ref{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}.  The phrase ``monic unit-circle input'' always refers to \(P\) being monic in the variable \(u\); the induced \(Q_r\) need not be monic, and every projective or scalar comparison below is made only after passing to \(\widetilde Q_r\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient descent for the transport polynomial]\label{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}
In Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, the expression
\[
Q_r(w)=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(w-r z_j-r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)
\]
is independent of the ordering of the roots and belongs to \(K(r)[w]\).  The same conclusion holds after specializing \(r\in K^\times\), in which case \(Q_r\in K[w]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to prove that each coefficient of \(Q_r\) lies in the indicated coefficient field.  The coefficient of \(w^{n-\ell}\) is \((-1)^\ell a_0\) times the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(\ell\) in the quantities \(r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\).  Expanding this elementary symmetric polynomial gives a finite \(\ZZ[r,r^{-1}]\)-linear combination of monomials
\[
z_1^{e_1}\cdots z_n^{e_n},
\qquad e_j\in\{-1,0,1\},
\]
summed over subsets in a way that is invariant under every permutation of the root labels.  Since \(a_0\ne0\), all roots are nonzero, and multiplication by a sufficiently large power of \(z_1\cdots z_n\) converts each such symmetric Laurent polynomial into an ordinary symmetric polynomial in \(z_1,
\dots,z_n\).  By the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials, it is a polynomial expression in the elementary symmetric functions of the roots and in \((z_1\cdots z_n)^{-1}\), with coefficients in \(\ZZ[r,r^{-1}]\).

The elementary symmetric functions of the \(z_j\) are \((-1)^k a_{n-k}/a_n\), and \((z_1\cdots z_n)^{-1}=(-1)^n a_n/a_0\).  Hence every coefficient of \(Q_r\) belongs to \(K(r)\).  The construction used only symmetric expressions in the multiset of roots, so it is independent of the chosen ordering.  If \(r\in K^\times\) is specialized, the same formulas have coefficients in \(K\), proving \(Q_r\in K[w]\).
\end{proof}

The normalizations just fixed are used throughout the algebraic part of the article: the same normalization controls logarithmic means, zero shells, and one-scale resultants. We use the zero set notation \(\mathrm{Zero}(F)\) for a polynomial \(F\), and the standard resultant/discriminant notation \(\operatorname{Res}\) and \(\operatorname{Disc}\) \citep{GKZ1994}. For \(\rho>0\), we define the logarithmic circular mean
\[
\mathcal M_\rho(P):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|P(\rho e^{i\theta})|\,\dd\theta,
\]
so that \(\mathcal M_1(P)\) is the logarithmic Mahler measure of \(P\) \citep{Mahler1962,EverestWard1999}.

The next two identities are the algebraic input for the Mahler, discriminant, and one-scale reconstruction statements in Sections \ref{sec:mahler-transport} and \ref{sec:discriminant}.

\begin{theorem}[Pullback factorization]\label{thm:pullback-factorization}
Let \(P\), \(P^\vee\), and \(Q_r\) be as in Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, and let \(r\) be an indeterminate or an element of \(K^\times\). Then \(Q_r(w)\in K(r)[w]\), and in \(K(r)[z,z^{-1}]\) one has
\[
\boxed{\;
r^n z^n\,Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)=P(z)\,P^\vee(r^2 z).
\;}
\]
If \(K\subseteq\CC\) and \(r\in K^\times\), the same identity may equivalently be evaluated at every \(z\in\CC^\times\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient assertion is Lemma \ref{lem:transport-coefficient-descent}. For each \(j\),
\[
J_r(z)-\bigl(r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\bigr)
=r(z-z_j)+r^{-1}(z^{-1}-z_j^{-1})
=\frac{(z-z_j)(r^2 z z_j-1)}{r z z_j}.
\]
Multiplying over \(j\) yields
\[
Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)
=a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(z-z_j)(r^2 z z_j-1)}{r z z_j}
=r^{-n}z^{-n}\Bigl(a_0\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_j^{-1}\Bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
Since \(a_0=(-1)^n a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n} z_j\), we have \(a_0\prod_j z_j^{-1}=(-1)^n a_n\). Therefore
\[
Q_r\!\bigl(J_r(z)\bigr)
=r^{-n}z^{-n}\,a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_j)\cdot (-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
The first factor is \(P(z)\), while
\[
P^\vee(r^2 z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-r^2 z z_j)=(-1)^n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(r^2 z z_j-1).
\]
Combining the two identities gives the stated factorization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Discriminant factorization]\label{thm:discriminant-factorization}
In the setting of Theorem \ref{thm:pullback-factorization},
\[
\boxed{\;
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)
=\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)\,
r^{-n(n-1)}
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2 z_i z_j-1)^2.
\;}
\]
The identity is taken in \(\overline K(r)\), and both sides are in fact elements of \(K(r)\).  It remains valid without assuming that \(P\) is separable; if roots collide, the corresponding discriminants vanish.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(w_j:=r z_j+r^{-1}z_j^{-1}\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)=a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(w_i-w_j)^2.
\]
Using
\[
w_i-w_j
=r(z_i-z_j)+r^{-1}(z_i^{-1}-z_j^{-1})
=\frac{(z_i-z_j)(r^2 z_i z_j-1)}{r z_i z_j},
\]
we obtain
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{w}(Q_r)
=a_0^{2n-2}r^{-n(n-1)}
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i-z_j)^2
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(r^2 z_i z_j-1)^2
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}.
\]
Now
\[
\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}
=\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{n} z_i\Bigr)^{-2(n-1)},
\]
and \(a_0=(-1)^n a_n\prod_i z_i\) implies
\[
a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i z_j)^{-2}=a_n^{2n-2}.
\]
Since
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(P)=a_n^{2n-2}\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}(z_i-z_j)^2,
\]
with the same discriminant convention as in \(\operatorname{Disc}_w(Q_r)=a_0^{2n-2}\prod_{i<j}(w_i-w_j)^2\), no extra sign appears: the only sign from \(a_0=(-1)^na_n\prod_i z_i\) is raised to the even power \(2n-2\), and the transport contribution is exactly the displayed factor \(r^{-n(n-1)}\).
The desired identity follows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fixed-scale factor recovery consequences}\label{subsec:endpoint-synthesis}

The capacity, Fourier, Blaschke, and polynomial-transport inverse results now determine the fixed Joukowsky scale from two complementary kinds of exact data.  The synthesis theorem, Theorem \ref{thm:joukowsky-package-synthesis}, is the package closure point: capacity and finite-Blaschke theory supply \(\log r\), \(E_r\), the equilibrium image, and the Haar-lift class, while the one-scale unit-circle inverse theorem supplies the annular source factor \(P\) at that already fixed scale.  The calibrated two-test identity of Corollary \ref{cor:two-mode-joukowsky-scale-rigidity} shows that two prescribed Fourier probe energies recover the Joukowsky scale inside that fixed probe family.  The remaining statements are corollary-level consequences of this closed chain.  They recover a fixed scale and a fixed source factor; they do not reconstruct a Blaschke cover, and they are not a second inverse theory for arbitrary boundary observations.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-scale factor recovery consequences]\label{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis}
Fix \(r>1\) and let \(J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product of degree \(d\ge1\), and write
  \[
  I(\sigma):=\iint\log|x-y|\,\dd\sigma(x)\dd\sigma(y)
  \]
  with lower truncation when necessary.  Then
  \[
  \sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)} I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=\log r.
  \]
  For \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\), equality in
  \[
  I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)\le \log r
  \]
  holds if and only if
  \[
  B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
  \]
  Thus all equilibrium lifts of the covered ellipse are exactly the Haar lifts through \(B\), and the canonical absolutely continuous lift is
  \[
  \dd\mu_B(e^{i\theta})=\frac{|B'(e^{i\theta})|}{2\pi d}\,\dd\theta.
  \]

  \item Let \(P\in\CC[z]\) be a monic polynomial of positive degree whose zeros all lie on \(\TT\), and let \(Q_r\) be its transport polynomial in the normalization of Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}.  Then the full one-scale polynomial \(Q_r\) determines \(P\) uniquely.  Equivalently, for every \(\rho\) with \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\), \(P\) is the unique monic factor of
  \[
  S_{r,Q_r}(z):=z^{\deg P}Q_r(J_r(z))
  \]
  whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\); the complementary factor has all zeros in \(\{|z|<\rho\}\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the analytic coordinate recovers the fixed Joukowsky scale through \(\sup I=\log r\), consistently with the calibrated two-test consequence in Corollary \ref{cor:two-mode-joukowsky-scale-rigidity}, while the algebraic coordinate recovers the source factor at that scale by the exact unit-circle annular factor test of Theorem \ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \textup{(a)}, the Joukowsky boundary skeleton obstruction, Theorem \ref{thm:joukowsky-boundary-skeleton-obstruction}, first shows that the covered boundary energy depends on \(\mu\) only through \(B_\ast\mu\); equivalently Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} gives, for every \(\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)\),
\[
I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu)=I(J_{r\ast}(B_\ast\mu))\le\log r,
\]
with equality exactly when \(B_\ast\mu\) is Haar measure.  This proves both the supremum and the equality criterion.  The displayed canonical lift is the canonical measure constructed in the same finite-cover theorem, and Lemma \ref{lem:finite-blaschke-boundary-covering-formula} gives that its pushforward by \(B\) is Haar measure.

For \textup{(b)}, apply Theorem \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction} to the fixed scale \(r>1\).  It gives injectivity of \(P\mapsto Q_r\) on the monic unit-circle class and constructive recovery from \((r,Q_r)\).  Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection} identifies this constructive recovery with the stated annular factor selection for every \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\).  The final sentence combines \textup{(a)} with Theorem \ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Realizable fixed-scale data]\label{def:realizable-boundary-scale-data}
With \(\mathcal L\) as fixed in the preliminaries, a \emph{realizable fixed-scale datum} is a pair
\[
\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]
\]
for which there are a number \(r>1\), a finite Blaschke product \(B\) of positive degree, and a polynomial \(P\in\mathcal L\) such that \(Q=Q_r(P)\) is the transport polynomial of \(P\) in the normalization of Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization}, and
\[
e=\mathcal E(B,r):=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal P(\TT)} I((J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu),
\]
with logarithmic energy interpreted by lower truncation as in Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis}.  The set of all such pairs is denoted by \(\mathfrak B\).  A triple \((B,r,P)\) satisfying these conditions is called a \emph{realization} of \(\mathfrak b\), and the associated ellipse is \(E_r=J_r(\TT)\).  This definition packages exact data from earlier theorems; it does not assert perturbative persistence of unit-circle zeros.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Factorization of fixed-scale recovery]\label{prop:scale-recognition-factorization}
Let \(\mathcal R\) be the class of triples \((B,r,P)\), where \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product of positive degree, \(r>1\), and \(P\in\mathcal L\).  Define
\begin{align*}
\Sigma(B,r,P)&:=\bigl(\mathcal E(B,r),Q_r(P)\bigr),\qquad
\Pi(B,r,P):=(r,P),\\
\mathcal S(r,P)&:=(\log r,Q_r(P)).
\end{align*}
Then
\[
\Sigma=\mathcal S\circ\Pi.
\]
Moreover \(\mathcal S\) is injective on \((1,\infty)\times\mathcal L\).  Hence these fixed-scale recovery consequences have exactly the following dependence on the main theorem chain: the first coordinate is fixed by the capacity, Fourier, and Blaschke-lift chain, while the second coordinate is fixed at that recovered scale by the unit-circle inverse theorem.  In particular, if
\[
\Sigma(B,r,P)=\Sigma(C,s,\widetilde P),
\]
then \(r=s\) and \(P=\widetilde P\); the finite Blaschke product is not reconstructed by \(\Sigma\), and after \((r,P)\) is fixed the residual maximizing measures are precisely the probability measures satisfying
\[
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} gives
\[
\mathcal E(B,r)=\log r
\]
for every finite Blaschke product \(B\) of positive degree and every \(r>1\).  It also identifies equality in the covered energy problem with the condition \(B_\ast\mu=\dd\theta/(2\pi)\).  Therefore \(\Sigma(B,r,P)=(\log r,Q_r(P))=\mathcal S(\Pi(B,r,P))\), proving the factorization and the asserted residual lift condition once \((r,P)\) is known.

It remains to prove injectivity of \(\mathcal S\).  If \(\mathcal S(r,P)=\mathcal S(s,\widetilde P)\), then \(\log r=\log s\), hence \(r=s\).  At this fixed scale the equality \(Q_r(P)=Q_r(\widetilde P)\) holds, and Theorem \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction} gives \(P=\widetilde P\) on the monic unit-circle class \(\mathcal L\).  Thus equality of fixed-scale data recovers the scale and the source polynomial.  The Blaschke cover and the choice of an equilibrium lift remain exactly the ambiguity classified by the finite Blaschke lift theorem, so the synthesis results below record the precise output of the capacity and inverse chain.
\end{proof}

The next result is the fixed-scale recognition output of the completed capacity--transport chain.  The central complex-variable results have supplied the auxiliary lower-truncated pullback interface, the degree-two Joukowsky capacity theorem, the sharp Fourier deficit, the Blaschke lift theorem, the calibrated two-test identity, and the inverse-resonance/annular recovery theorems.  The result records exactly what this chain recovers: positivity of the energy coordinate fixes the scale, and the pulled-back polynomial must split into the two reciprocal zero shells dictated by that scale.  The following annular-divisor result is derived from the quantitative annular Rouch\'e theorem and gives a fixed-scale coefficient criterion: within an explicit coefficient ball, a canonical annular source exists and the remaining exact realizability obstruction is the closed algebraic unit-circle reciprocal-shell test.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-scale factor recovery criterion]\label{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}
Let \(\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]\), with \(\deg_w Q=n_{\mathfrak b}\ge1\).  Put
\[
r_{\mathfrak b}:=\exp(e),
\]
and, when \(e>0\), define
\[
S_{\mathfrak b}(z):=z^{n_{\mathfrak b}}Q\!\left(J_{r_{\mathfrak b}}(z)\right).
\]
Then \(\mathfrak b\) is a realizable fixed-scale datum in the sense of Definition \ref{def:realizable-boundary-scale-data} if and only if the following exact shell condition holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item \(e>0\);
\item for some \(\rho\) with \(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}<\rho<1\), the polynomial \(S_{\mathfrak b}\) has exactly \(n_{\mathfrak b}\) zeros in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\), counted with multiplicity; if \(P_{\mathfrak b}\) is the monic factor determined by those outer zeros, then all zeros of \(P_{\mathfrak b}\) lie on \(\TT\) and
\[
S_{\mathfrak b}(z)=r_{\mathfrak b}^{n_{\mathfrak b}}P_{\mathfrak b}(z)\,z^{n_{\mathfrak b}}P_{\mathfrak b}\!\left(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}z^{-1}\right).
\]
\end{enumerate}
When these equivalent conditions hold, every realization \((B,r,P)\) of \(\mathfrak b\) has
\[
r=r_{\mathfrak b},
\qquad
E_r=E_{r_{\mathfrak b}},
\qquad
P=P_{\mathfrak b}.
\]
Moreover, for every \(\rho\) with \(r_{\mathfrak b}^{-2}<\rho<1\), \(P_{\mathfrak b}\) is the unique monic factor of \(S_{\mathfrak b}\) whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\), and the complementary factor has all zeros in \(\{|z|<\rho\}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
First assume that \(\mathfrak b\) is realizable and choose a realization \((B,r,P)\).  Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} gives
\[
e=\mathcal E(B,r)=\log r,
\]
so \(e>0\) and \(r=r_{\mathfrak b}\).  Since \(Q=Q_r(P)\) with \(P\in\mathcal L\), Theorem \ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition} gives the displayed shell condition and identifies the outer factor as \(P\).  Conversely, suppose that the shell condition holds.  The same exact unit-circle factor-recovery theorem gives a monic polynomial \(P_{\mathfrak b}\in\mathcal L\) such that \(Q=Q_{r_{\mathfrak b}}(P_{\mathfrak b})\).  Taking the degree-one Blaschke product \(B(z)=z\), Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} gives \(\mathcal E(B,r_{\mathfrak b})=\log r_{\mathfrak b}=e\).  Hence \((B,r_{\mathfrak b},P_{\mathfrak b})\) is a realization.  The uniqueness of the recovered source polynomial and the annular selection statement are exactly Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection}, applied after the scale has been fixed by the energy coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Annular certificate map for fixed-scale perturbations]\label{prop:boundary-scale-certificate-map}
Let \(\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]\) with \(\deg Q=n\ge1\).  A certificate for \(\mathfrak b\) is a pair \((r,P)\) such that
\[
r>1,
\qquad e=\log r,
\qquad P\in\mathcal L,
\qquad
z^nQ(J_r(z))=r^nP(z)z^nP(r^{-2}z^{-1}).
\]
The certificate set of \(\mathfrak b\) is either empty or a singleton.  It is nonempty exactly when \(\mathfrak b\) is realizable, and in that case the unique certificate is
\[
(r_{\mathfrak b},P_{\mathfrak b})
\]
from Corollary \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}.  Moreover the following certificate test is intrinsic: first require \(e>0\) and set \(r=\exp(e)\); then choose any \(\rho\) with \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\) and take the monic factor \(P_\rho\) of \(z^nQ(J_r(z))\) whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\), if such a factor exists.  The datum is realizable if and only if this factor exists, is independent of the chosen separating radius, has all zeros on \(\TT\), and satisfies the displayed reciprocal identity.  Thus Corollary \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity} is a certification criterion assembled from the capacity scale and the exact unit-circle shell theorem, not an independent reconstruction of arbitrary boundary data.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If a certificate \((r,P)\) exists, then \(e=\log r>0\), so \(r=\exp(e)=r_{\mathfrak b}\) is forced.  The reciprocal identity is precisely the intrinsic shell identity in Corollary \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}, with outer factor \(P\); since the two shell radii are \(1\) and \(r^{-2}\), Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection} gives uniqueness of the monic outer factor for every separating radius \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\).  Hence two certificates must have the same scale and the same polynomial.

The same theorem proves existence of a certificate from any realization: if \((B,r,P)\) realizes \(\mathfrak b\), then Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} gives \(e=\log r\), and Theorem \ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition} gives the displayed reciprocal factorization for \(Q=Q_r(P)\).  Conversely, if a certificate exists, then the reciprocal identity and the exact unit-circle factor-recovery theorem imply \(Q=Q_r(P)\); choosing the degree-one Blaschke product \(B(z)=z\) and using Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} gives \(\mathcal E(B,r)=\log r=e\), so \((B,r,P)\) realizes \(\mathfrak b\).  The radius-independent test is just this equivalence read through the annular outer-factor selection of Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection}; the final sentence records the dependence of the criterion on those earlier exact theorems.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Fixed-scale annular-divisor Rouch\'e criterion]\label{thm:stable-boundary-scale-recognition}
Let \(\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\mathfrak B\) be a realizable fixed-scale datum of degree \(n\), and let \(r=\exp(e)>1\) and \(P\in\mathcal L\) be the scale and source polynomial recovered in Corollary \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}.  Put
\[
S(z):=z^nQ(J_r(z))=z^nQ_r(P)(J_r(z)).
\]
Choose radii
\[
r^{-2}<\rho_-<\rho_+<1,
\]
let \(\Gamma_-=\{|z|=\rho_-\}\), \(\Gamma_+=\{|z|=\rho_+\}\), and \(\Gamma_0=\{|z|=\rho_+^{-1}\}\), and define
\[
\Delta_\mathfrak b:=\frac12\min\left\{
\inf_{z\in\Gamma_-}|S(z)|,
\inf_{z\in\Gamma_+}|S(z)|,
\inf_{z\in\Gamma_0}|S(z)|
\right\}.
\]
Write
\[
Q(w)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}b_\ell w^\ell,
\qquad
\widehat Q(w)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\widehat b_\ell w^\ell,
\]
allowing \(\widehat b_n=0\), and set the explicit coefficient-to-contour amplification factor
\[
\mathfrak M_\mathfrak b:=
\max_{z\in\Gamma_-\cup\Gamma_+\cup\Gamma_0}
|z|^n\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}|J_r(z)|^\ell.
\]
This theorem is a synthesis statement, not a second Rouch\'e principle: its perturbative input is exactly the core annular-divisor stability theorem \ref{thm:quantitative-annular-rouche-stability}, and its additional work is to combine that annular factor with the already fixed capacity coordinate \(e=\log r\) and the exact unit-circle reciprocal-shell test.  If \(\widehat Q\in\CC[w]\) has \(\deg\widehat Q\le n\) and
\[
\sup_{z\in\Gamma_-\cup\Gamma_+\cup\Gamma_0}
\left|z^n\widehat Q(J_r(z))-S(z)\right|<\Delta_\mathfrak b,
\]
then \(\widehat S(z):=z^n\widehat Q(J_r(z))\) is a polynomial of degree \(2n\), has no zeros on \(\Gamma_-\cup\Gamma_+\cup\Gamma_0\), and has exactly \(n\) zeros, counted with multiplicity, in the annulus
\[
\rho_+<|z|<\rho_+^{-1}.
\]
Let \(\widehat P_{\rm ann}\) be the monic degree-\(n\) polynomial with precisely these annular zeros.  Then every monic unit-circle polynomial \(\widetilde P\in\mathcal L\) satisfying \(\widehat Q=Q_r(\widetilde P)\) is equal to \(\widehat P_{\rm ann}\).  Consequently the perturbed datum \((e,\widehat Q)\) is realizable if and only if all zeros of \(\widehat P_{\rm ann}\) lie on \(\TT\) and the exact reciprocal identity
\[
\widehat S(z)=r^n\widehat P_{\rm ann}(z)\,z^n\widehat P_{\rm ann}\!\left(r^{-2}z^{-1}\right)
\]
holds.  If this condition holds, all realizations of \((e,\widehat Q)\) have scale \(r\) and source polynomial \(\widehat P_{\rm ann}\); if it fails, then \((e,\widehat Q)\) is not realizable at the capacity scale \(e\).

Moreover, the candidate source is locally stable.  If the zero divisor of \(P\) is \(\sum_{a\in Z(P)}m_a[a]\), if \(U_a\) are pairwise disjoint Jordan domains with \(a\in U_a\), whose closures lie in \(\rho_+<|z|<\rho_+^{-1}\) and contain no other zero of \(P\), and if
\[
\sup_{z\in\bigcup_{a\in Z(P)}\partial U_a}
\left|z^n\widehat Q(J_r(z))-S(z)\right|
<
\frac12\min_{a\in Z(P)}\inf_{z\in\partial U_a}|S(z)|,
\]
then \(\widehat P_{\rm ann}\) has exactly \(m_a\) zeros in \(U_a\) for each \(a\).  In particular, if each \(U_a\subset\{|z-a|<\eta\}\) and
\[
P(z)=z^n+p_1z^{n-1}+\cdots+p_n,
\qquad
\widehat P_{\rm ann}(z)=z^n+\widehat p_1z^{n-1}+\cdots+\widehat p_n,
\]
then
\[
|\widehat p_k-p_k|\le \binom{n}{k}k\eta(1+\eta)^{k-1},
\qquad 1\le k\le n.
\]
Finally, the preceding global annular-factor, uniqueness, and fixed-scale realizability conclusions hold under the purely coefficient-level budget
\[
\max_{0\le\ell\le n}|\widehat b_\ell-b_\ell|
<
\frac{\Delta_\mathfrak b}{\mathfrak M_\mathfrak b}.
\]
Equivalently, this coefficient ball has a well-defined canonical annular source map \(\widehat Q\mapsto\widehat P_{\rm ann}\); inside that ball, realizability at the fixed capacity coordinate \(e\) is exactly the closed algebraic unit-circle test consisting of the unit-circle zero condition for \(\widehat P_{\rm ann}\) and the displayed reciprocal identity.  The annular divisor is stable under the stated perturbation; exact unit-circle realizability is still the separate closed algebraic condition just stated.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Because \(\mathfrak b\) is realizable, Corollary \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity} gives \(Q=Q_r(P)\) and fixes the scale \(r=\exp(e)\).  The zeros of \(S\) are the two exact shells \(|z|=1\) and \(|z|=r^{-2}\), by Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-double-resultant}.  Hence none of the three contours \(\Gamma_-\), \(\Gamma_+\), and \(\Gamma_0\) meets the zero set of \(S\), so \(\Delta_\mathfrak b>0\).  At this point the analytic part is completely delegated to the core Rouch\'e theorem; the present proof only transports its annular divisor into the fixed-scale certificate.

The displayed perturbation hypothesis is precisely the Rouch\'e envelope hypothesis in Theorem \ref{thm:quantitative-annular-rouche-stability}, with \(\widetilde Q=\widehat Q\).  That theorem gives the degree \(2n\) conclusion, absence of zeros on the three contours, the count of exactly \(n\) zeros in \(\rho_+<|z|<\rho_+^{-1}\), and the existence and uniqueness of the canonical annular factor \(\widehat P_{\rm ann}\).

Suppose now that \(\widetilde P\in\mathcal L\) and \(\widehat Q=Q_r(\widetilde P)\).  Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection} applied to \((r,\widehat Q)\) says that, for every separating radius between \(r^{-2}\) and \(1\), the monic factor selected from the outer shell is exactly \(\widetilde P\).  Since the unit-circle zeros lie on \(|z|=1\), they are precisely the zeros of \(\widehat S\) in the annulus \(\rho_+<|z|<\rho_+^{-1}\); hence \(\widetilde P=\widehat P_{\rm ann}\).  This proves uniqueness of any possible unit-circle source at the fixed scale.

If \((e,\widehat Q)\) is realizable, Corollary \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity} and the preceding paragraph force its source to be \(\widehat P_{\rm ann}\).  The same corollary then gives that the annular factor has all zeros on \(\TT\) and satisfies the displayed reciprocal identity.  Conversely, if these two displayed conditions hold, the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:intrinsic-leyang-shell-recognition} applies verbatim: the identity for \(\widehat S(z)=z^n\widehat Q(J_r(z))\) and the corresponding transport identity for \(Q_r(\widehat P_{\rm ann})\) agree for all \(z\ne0\), and the image \(J_r(\CC^\times)\) is infinite, so \(\widehat Q=Q_r(\widehat P_{\rm ann})\).  Taking \(B(z)=z\) gives \(\mathcal E(B,r)=\log r=e\) by Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis}, hence \((e,\widehat Q)\) is realizable.  The final assertion about all realizations again follows from Corollary \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity}; if either the circle condition or the reciprocal identity fails, the same equivalence proves nonrealizability at the fixed capacity scale.

The local stability and coefficient estimate are exactly the local part of Theorem \ref{thm:quantitative-annular-rouche-stability} applied with the same unperturbed pair \((r,P)\), its transport \(Q=Q_r(P)\), and perturbation \(\widehat Q\).

It remains only to verify the advertised coefficient budget.  On the three contours,
\[
\left|z^n\widehat Q(J_r(z))-S(z)\right|
=
\left|z^n\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}(\widehat b_\ell-b_\ell)J_r(z)^\ell\right|
\le
\max_{0\le\ell\le n}|\widehat b_\ell-b_\ell|\,
\mathfrak M_\mathfrak b.
\]
Thus the coefficient inequality implies the Rouch\'e envelope hypothesis already used above.  The preceding paragraphs therefore give a canonical annular source for every polynomial in the coefficient ball and prove that the only remaining obstruction to realizability at the fixed capacity scale is precisely the unit-circle zero condition and the exact reciprocal identity.
\end{proof}

The obstruction test is therefore a formal corollary of the fixed-scale factor recovery result and the Joukowsky transport identities: the paper has two exact checks, the positive capacity scale and the unit-circle reciprocal-shell condition.

\begin{proposition}[Two-test fixed-scale obstruction]\label{prop:boundary-scale-recognition-obstruction}
For every \(\mathfrak b=(e,Q)\in\RR\times\CC[w]\), with \(\deg Q\ge1\), exactly one of the following alternatives holds.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item \(e\le0\).  In this case \(\mathfrak b\) is not realizable as a fixed-scale datum.
  \item \(e>0\), but for \(r=\exp(e)\) the polynomial \(z^{\deg Q}Q(J_r(z))\) has no factorization
  \[
  z^{\deg Q}Q(J_r(z))=r^{\deg Q}P(z)\,z^{\deg Q}P(r^{-2}z^{-1})
  \]
  with \(P\in\mathcal L\).  In this case \(\mathfrak b\) is not realizable.
  \item There is a unique monic \(P\in\mathcal L\) for which the displayed factorization holds with \(r=\exp(e)\).  In this case \(\mathfrak b\) is realizable, all its realizations have the same scale \(r\), the same ellipse \(E_r\), and the same source polynomial \(P\).
\end{enumerate}
Thus the pair \((e,Q)\) never reconstructs the finite Blaschke product; once the scale and source polynomial have been recovered, the remaining choices are precisely finite Blaschke covers together with probability measures lifting Haar measure under those covers.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The three alternatives are the trichotomy \(e\le0\), \(e>0\) with no reciprocal shell factorization by a polynomial in \(\mathcal L\), and \(e>0\) with such a factorization.  Corollary \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity} rules out realizability in the first two cases and gives realizability in the third.  In the third case uniqueness follows because the two factors occupy disjoint shells, \(\TT\) and \(r^{-2}\TT\); hence the monic outer factor is uniquely determined by the zeros in any annulus \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\).  Corollary \ref{thm:boundary-scale-rigidity} also fixes the scale and ellipse in every realization, while Theorem \ref{thm:finite-blaschke-joukowsky-cover} and Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} identify the residual maximizing measures as exactly the Haar lifts through the chosen finite cover.
\end{proof}

The exact residual fiber is the finite-cover ambiguity left after the preceding fixed-scale corollary and proposition have fixed the Joukowsky scale and the unit-circle source polynomial; it is not an additional inverse-recognition theory beyond those dependencies.

\begin{corollary}[Dependent capacity--transport residual fiber]\label{cor:capacity-mahler-fiber-classification}
Fix \(n\ge1\), and let \(\mathfrak X_n\) be the class of all quadruples \((B,r,P,\mu)\) in which \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product of positive degree, \(r>1\), \(P\in\mathcal L\) has degree \(n\), \(Q_r(P)\) is its Joukowsky transport polynomial, and
\[
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Define
\[
(B,r,P,\mu)\sim(C,s,\widetilde P,\nu)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r=s\ \text{ and }\ P=\widetilde P.
\]
Attach to a quadruple the datum
\[
\mathfrak D(B,r,P,\mu):=\bigl(\mathcal E(B,r),\,Q_r(P),\,(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu\bigr),
\]
where \(\mathcal E(B,r)\) is the covered-boundary energy supremum from Definition \ref{def:realizable-boundary-scale-data}, and put
\[
\mu_r:=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right).
\]
Then \(\mathfrak D\) is constant on \(\sim\)-classes and induces a bijection
\[
\overline{\mathfrak D}:\mathfrak X_n/\!\sim\ \longrightarrow
\left\{\bigl(\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r\bigr): r>1,
\ P\in\mathcal L,
\ \deg P=n\right\}.
\]
Equivalently, if two admissible quadruples \((B,r,P,\mu)\) and \((C,s,\widetilde P,\nu)\) have the same datum \(\mathfrak D\), then
\[
r=s,
\qquad
E_r=E_s,
\qquad
P=\widetilde P,
\qquad
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=(J_s\circ C)_\ast\nu=\mu_r.
\]
For every \(\rho\) with \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\), the recovered polynomial \(P\) is also the unique monic degree-\(n\) factor of \(z^nQ_r(P)(J_r(z))\) whose zeros lie in \(\{|z|>\rho\}\).  Conversely, the complete fiber over \((\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r)\) is exactly
\[
\left\{(B,r,P,\mu): B\text{ a finite Blaschke product of positive degree and }
B_\ast\mu=\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right\}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If \((B,r,P,\mu)\in\mathfrak X_n\), Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-input-synthesis} gives
\[
\mathcal E(B,r)=\log r,
\qquad
(J_r\circ B)_\ast\mu=J_{r\ast}\!\left(\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}\right)=\mu_r.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathfrak D(B,r,P,\mu)=\bigl(\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r\bigr),
\]
which depends only on \((r,P)\).  This proves that \(\mathfrak D\) is constant on \(\sim\)-classes and that the displayed map is surjective.  If two quadruples have the same datum, equality of the first components gives \(\log r=\log s\), hence \(r=s\).  Equality of the second components then becomes \(Q_r(P)=Q_r(\widetilde P)\), and Theorem \ref{thm:leyang-single-scale-reconstruction} gives \(P=\widetilde P\).  The equality of the covered equilibrium images is the pushforward identity above.  With the recovered scale fixed, Corollary \ref{cor:leyang-annular-shell-projection} gives the stated annular factor selection.  Finally, the same displayed formula for \(\mathfrak D\) shows that every finite Blaschke cover and every Haar-lift measure over that cover lies in the fiber over \((\log r,Q_r(P),\mu_r)\), and the injectivity just proved shows that no different scale or source polynomial can lie in the fiber.
\end{proof}

\section{Examples and normalizations}\label{sec:examples}

The following elementary cases fix the normalizations used in the preceding sections.

\begin{example}[The ellipse and its scale]\label{ex:joukowsky-ellipse-scale}
For \(z=e^{i\theta}\),
\[
J_r(z)=rz+r^{-1}z^{-1}=(r+r^{-1})\cos\theta+i(r-r^{-1})\sin\theta.
\]
Thus \(E_r\) is the ellipse with semiaxes \(r+r^{-1}\) and \(r-r^{-1}\).  The exterior conformal normalization in Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} gives \(\operatorname{cap}(E_r)=r\), and the equilibrium measure is the image of Haar measure under this parametrization.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[One Fourier mode]\label{ex:one-fourier-mode-deficit}
Let \(m\ge1\) and, for small real \(\varepsilon\), let
\[
\dd\mu_\varepsilon(e^{i\theta})=(1+2\varepsilon\cos m\theta)\,\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Then \(\widehat\mu_\varepsilon(m)=\varepsilon\) and all other positive Fourier coefficients vanish.  The deficit formula gives
\[
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\mu_\varepsilon)=\frac{1+r^{-2m}}{m}\varepsilon^2.
\]
If instead \(\dd\nu_\varepsilon=(1+2\varepsilon\sin m\theta)\dd\theta/(2\pi)\), then \(\widehat\nu_\varepsilon(m)=-i\varepsilon\) and
\[
\log r-I(J_{r\ast}\nu_\varepsilon)=\frac{1-r^{-2m}}{m}\varepsilon^2.
\]
This realizes the two sharp mode weights in Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Degree-one transport]\label{ex:degree-one-transport}
Let \(P(z)=z-\alpha\), \(\alpha\ne0\).  Then \(a_0=-\alpha\), and Definition \ref{def:reciprocal-transport-normalization} gives
\[
Q_r(w)=-\alpha\bigl(w-r\alpha-r^{-1}\alpha^{-1}\bigr).
\]
If \(|\alpha|=1\), then
\[
zQ_r(J_r(z))=r(z-\alpha)(z-r^{-2}\alpha^{-1}),
\]
so the pulled-back doubled polynomial has one zero on \(\TT\) and its reflected partner on \(r^{-2}\TT\).  Any radius \(\rho\) with \(r^{-2}<\rho<1\) selects the outer factor \(z-\alpha\).
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A single Blaschke zero]\label{ex:single-blaschke-zero}
For
\[
B_a(z)=\zeta\frac{z-a}{1-\overline a z},\qquad |a|<1,
\]
the canonical Haar lift has density
\[
\dd\mu_{B_a}(e^{i\theta})=\frac{1-|a|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-a|^2}\,\frac{\dd\theta}{2\pi}.
\]
Its positive Fourier coefficients are \(\widehat\mu_{B_a}(k)=\overline a^{\,k}\), hence Proposition \ref{prop:canonical-blaschke-lift-source-energy} gives
\[
I_{\TT}(\mu_{B_a})=\log(1-|a|^2).
\]
For the monomial cover \(B(z)=\zeta z^d\), all Blaschke zeros are at the origin, the lift is Haar measure, and the source-circle energy is \(0\).
\end{example}


\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Several themes emerging from the core sections deserve emphasis. First, the Joukowsky ellipse is not merely a convenient parametrized curve: it is a conformal boundary object whose logarithmic capacity, annular modulus, Fourier deficit, finite-cover equilibrium lifts, and transported Mahler averages are all controlled by the same scale parameter.  This is the content of the theorem package summarized in Theorem \ref{thm:joukowsky-package-synthesis}.  In this sense, the transport identity of Theorem \ref{thm:pullback-factorization} and the degree-two capacity formula of Theorem \ref{thm:degree-two-joukowsky-capacity-collision-relation} should be viewed as potential-theoretic bridges between circle data and ellipse data, parallel to the classical role of conformal maps in logarithmic potential theory \citep{Tsuji1959,Ransford1995,Pommerenke1992}. The discriminant and resultant formulas show that the same bridge also preserves delicate elimination-theoretic information \citep{GKZ1994}.

Joukowsky and ellipse parametrizations also appear in adjacent literatures on computational conformal mapping and approximation on Bernstein ellipses \citep{Henrici1974,Trefethen2013}, Schwarz-function and Laplacian-growth methods for algebraic domains \citep{GustafssonVasiliev2006,Eynard2016}, and contour-based matrix or graph functional calculus where ellipses and conformal contours control analytic approximation \citep{HaleHighamTrefethen2008}.  These works are adjacent because they use the same conformal geometry or ellipse coordinates.  They do not contain the fixed-scale package isolated here: the exact lower-truncated capacity descent, sharp Fourier deficit with weights \(1\pm r^{-2k}\), finite-Blaschke Haar-lift fiber, exact complete-vector transport inverse, and local annular Rouch\'e selector all tied to the same reciprocal shell \(r^{-2}\TT\).

Second, the fixed-scale factor recovery result and residual-fiber corollary are deliberately secondary consequences of the main analytic and algebraic results.  The capacity pullback kernel with its branch and collision terms, the exact degree-two boundary descent, the sharp Fourier deficit on \(E_r\), the finite Blaschke lift theorem, the inverse resonance classification, the no-resonance unit-circle fiber collapse, and the explicit Rouch\'e envelope for annular-divisor recovery imply that the Blaschke-cover equilibrium supremum is \(\log r\), the transport polynomial is injective at fixed unit-circle zero scale, and the annular divisor selection is stable.  Thus the potential-theoretic normalization and the algebraic inverse normalization determine the same ellipse; the remaining fiber is exactly the finite-cover Haar-lift ambiguity classified in Corollary \ref{cor:capacity-mahler-fiber-classification}.  This stability statement is only annular-divisor stability; exact unit-circle zeros and exact reciprocal factorization remain separate closed algebraic conditions.

As a final notation check, if
\[
\Gamma_L:=\bigl\{\,L^{1/2}z+L^{-1/2}z^{-1}:|z|=1\,\bigr\},
\]
then the transport ellipse in the main body satisfies \(E_r=\Gamma_{r^2}\), and Theorem \ref{thm:sharp-joukowsky-equilibrium-stability} gives \(\operatorname{cap}(\Gamma_L)=L^{1/2}\) for \(L>1\).  A parametrized covering such as \(e^{im\theta}+c e^{-im\theta}\) only changes this boundary coordinate; it is not used in any proof of the capacity, Blaschke, transport, inverse, or annular-divisor results.
