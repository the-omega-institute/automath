\begin{abstract}
We study the canonical normalization map behind Zeckendorf addition: a finite
word over $\{0,1,2\}$ is replaced by the unique admissible Zeckendorf word
with the same Fibonacci value.  We first present this map as the canonical
normalization induced by a section of the Fibonacci congruence on
finite-support digit-count sequences
and give a terminating local rewrite presentation whose terminal irreducible
word is independent of the rewrite strategy.

The main results give exact least-significant-digit-first (LSDF)
noncausality invariants for this canonical map.  For every output coordinate
$j$, the LSDF anticipation horizon is infinite, even after restricting the
domain to genuine digitwise sums of two admissible Zeckendorf inputs:
$A_{\{0,1,2\}^{(\mathbb N)}}(j)=A_{\operatorname{Add}_2}(j)=\infty$.  The
map has no continuous extension at the natural alternating one-sided limit
tails.  For LSDF finite words, the online commitment deficit is maximal:
$B(n)=n$; equivalently, the prefix-destruction index---the largest number of
initial normalized-output digits that may be invalidated by extending an
input---satisfies $\Delta(n)=n$.  The same maximal prefix erasure holds on the genuine addition
image, $\Delta_{\operatorname{Add}_2}(n)=n$, and the input-scale reduced
residual complexity satisfies $R_{\mathrm{in}}(n)\ge n$.
Non-subsequentiality---that is, impossibility of realization by a
deterministic one-pass finite transducer with bounded terminal output---is
then a corollary of these sharper quantitative obstructions.

These statements are specific to the canonical greedy Zeckendorf normalizer
in the LSDF one-sided model and are not claims of a new general normalization
theory for Fibonacci or confluent numeration systems.  We contrast them with
the classical most-significant-digit-first Berstel adder.  The existence and
ten-state presentation of that adder are classical.  We clarify its
output-delay quotient: the ten displayed complete-output residuals are
distinct as a raw letter-to-letter kernel, but standard subsequential
output-delay normalization gives a six-state quotient.  We then study the
operation of iterating the Berstel recoding until the greedy Zeckendorf normal
form is reached.  Binary cleanup has exact worst-case depth
$\lfloor L/2\rfloor$ on trimmed binary words of length $L$, and on genuine
addition inputs the sharp depth is
$\max_{w\in\operatorname{Add}_2^{\mathrm{MSD}}(n)}\tau(w)=\lceil n/2\rceil$;
the maximum is already attained at significant length, without leading-zero
padding.  A standard local-propagation obstruction is recorded separately in an
appendix.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background and Main Results}

Zeckendorf addition can be viewed as a normalization problem.  If $c,d$ are
admissible Zeckendorf words, then their digitwise sum $c+d$ is a finite word
over $\{0,1,2\}$, and the canonical addition task is to replace this
noncanonical word by the unique admissible word with the same Fibonacci value.
After the notation is introduced in Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}, the
central map is
\[
\Fold_\infty:\D\to \Xinf,\qquad \Fold_\infty(a)=Z(\Val(a)).
\]
Here $\D$ is the monoid of finite-support sequences of nonnegative digit
counts, $\Xinf$ is the set of finite-support binary sequences with no adjacent
$1$s, and $Z(N)$ denotes the Zeckendorf representative of $N$.

Finite-state arithmetic in Fibonacci and more general linear numeration
systems is classical.  Frougny developed finite-automaton and online-addition
methods for Fibonacci and Pisot numeration systems
\cite{Frougny1991FibonacciFiniteAutomata,Frougny1992NumbersAutomata,Frougny1999OnlineAddition}.
Sakarovitch's work on Fibonacci multiplication and normalization exhibits the
need to combine one-sided subsequential transformations
\cite{Sakarovitch1987EasyMultiplications}.  Frougny's confluent linear
systems place the Fibonacci case in a broader class
\cite{Frougny1992ConfluentLinearNumeration}.  Berstel's adder is the
classical finite transducer for Fibonacci addition
\cite{Berstel1986FibonacciWords,ITA_2001__35_6_491_0}; mechanical
finite-automaton verification of related Fibonacci transducers appears in
\cite{MousaviSchaefferShallit2016FibonacciAutomatic}.  Recent work continues
to treat Fibonacci-based numeration from an automata-theoretic viewpoint; see
\cite{AhlbachUsatineFrougnyPippenger2013,LabbeLepsova2023FibonacciTwosComplement}.
For Ostrowski-type systems, see also
\cite{HieronymiTerry2018OstrowskiAddition,BaranwalSchaefferShallit2021OstrowskiAutomatic}.
The present paper is deliberately narrower: it gives a self-contained account
of the canonical Zeckendorf addition normalizer and records explicit witness
families for the least-significant-digit-first obstruction.  It does not claim
a new general normalization theory beyond the Fibonacci setting.

We use the standard reading-direction terminology.  In the
\emph{least-significant-digit-first} (LSDF) convention, a word or sequence is
read from low Fibonacci weights to high Fibonacci weights.  In the
\emph{most-significant-digit-first} (MSDF) convention, a finite word is read
from high weights to low weights.  The distinction matters because
subsequential realizability depends on the reading direction and on whether
one asks for a canonical Zeckendorf output or only for a value-preserving
binary recoding.

Informally, a deterministic subsequential transducer is a deterministic finite
automaton with word outputs on transitions and a terminal output word.  A
one-pass online delay bound means that each output position is determined after
reading only a fixed number of later input positions.  Formal definitions for
the LSDF finite-support setting are given in Section~\ref{sec:streaming}; the
classical MSDF online convention is different.

Our first contribution is expository and structural.  We write
$\Fold_\infty$ as the normalization induced by the canonical section of the
Fibonacci congruence on the free commutative monoid of scale counts, and we give a terminating local
rewrite system whose irreducible configurations are exactly the admissible
Zeckendorf words.  In this language, canonical Zeckendorf addition factors as
\[
\text{digitwise sum of two admissible words}
\quad\hbox{followed by}\quad
\Fold_\infty .
\]

Our second contribution is a package of exact LSDF noncausality invariants for
the canonical normalizer.  For each output coordinate and each proposed
lookahead bound, we construct two finite-support inputs which agree on the
required LSDF prefix but whose normalized outputs differ at that coordinate.
This gives the exact anticipation profile
\[
A_{\{0,1,2\}^{(\NN)}}(j)=A_{\AddTwo}(j)=\infty
\qquad (j\ge 1),
\]
where $\AddTwo$ is the set of genuine digitwise sums of two admissible
Zeckendorf inputs.  The same witnesses show that no continuous extension
exists at the alternating one-sided limit tails.

For LSDF finite words we then measure not just finite-state realizability, but
how much output an online normalizer can safely commit.  With
$\mathcal N:\{0,1,2\}^\ast\to\{0,1\}^\ast$ denoting the LSDF finite-word
normalizer, we define the forced committed prefix $C(u)$, the commitment
deficit $B(n)$, and the prefix-destruction index $\Delta(n)$, and prove
\[
B(n)=\Delta(n)=n\qquad (n\ge 1).
\]
Thus an LSDF online normalizer may sometimes be forced to keep its entire
current normalized output uncommitted.  We further prove that the same maximal
prefix erasure holds inside the genuine addition image,
\[
\Delta_{\AddTwo}(n)=n\qquad (n\ge 1),
\]
and that the input-scale reduced residual complexity grows at least linearly,
$R_{\mathrm{in}}(n)\ge n$.  The familiar conclusion that $\mathcal N$ is not
deterministic subsequential is then derived as a corollary.  These are concrete witness
refinements for the canonical addition normalizer.  They should be read
alongside the classical directional decompositions of Fibonacci normalization
\cite{Frougny1992NumbersAutomata,Sakarovitch1987EasyMultiplications}.

We then recall the classical ten-state Berstel adder in the MSDF setting.  We
do not claim that the existence of the ten-state presentation is new.  The
purpose of Sections~\ref{sec:kernel} and \ref{sec:minimality} is to clarify
the exact state model: the ten displayed states are distinct as raw
complete-output residuals of the classical letter-to-letter kernel, but after
standard output-delay normalization the induced subsequential function admits
a six-state quotient.  This removes the possible ambiguity between the
classical ten-state table and unrestricted subsequential minimization.

The Berstel adder maps a ternary digitwise sum to a value-equivalent binary
word, but that binary word need not be greedy.  This leads to a natural
iteration problem.  Let $\Berstel$ denote the trimmed Berstel recoding in the
MSDF convention, and let $\tau(w)$ be the least number of Berstel iterations
needed to reach the greedy Zeckendorf representative of the same value.  We
first prove the exact binary cleanup bound
\[
\max_{\substack{u\in\{0,1\}^L\\ u=\trimMSD(u)}}\tau(u)
=
\left\lfloor\frac L2\right\rfloor .
\]
On genuine addition inputs of length $n$ we then prove the sharp bound
\[
\max \tau(w)=\left\lceil\frac n2\right\rceil .
\]
The maximum is already attained by trimmed inputs, so it is not a leading-zero
padding artifact.  Thus the non-greedy part of the Berstel output is not only
present; its worst-case cleanup depth is exactly linear with slope $1/2$.

For reference, Appendix~\ref{sec:complexity} records the local-propagation
witness used here for the nonredundant canonical map.  This should be read
against the existing literature on parallel addition in nonstandard
numeration systems
\cite{FrougnyPelantovaSvobodova2013MinimalDigitSets}; related nonlocality
phenomena are already present in that literature.

The paper is organized as follows.  Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} introduces
the quotient description of Zeckendorf normalization.  Section~\ref{sec:overlay}
gives the rewrite system and identifies addition with digitwise summation plus
normalization.  Section~\ref{sec:streaming} proves the exact LSDF
anticipation profile, the nonextension result at alternating tails, the
maximal commitment and prefix-erasure theorems, the genuine-addition
strengthening, the residual-growth lower bound, and the subsequential
impossibility corollary.  Sections~\ref{sec:kernel} and
\ref{sec:minimality} turn to the classical Berstel adder and its six-state
output-delay quotient.  Section~\ref{sec:berstel-iteration} proves the sharp
iteration-depth bound.  Appendix~\ref{sec:complexity} records the local
parallel witness.

\section{Canonical Normalization in Zeckendorf Numeration}\label{sec:preliminaries}

\subsection{Admissible Words and the Value Map}

\paragraph{Fibonacci weights.}
Throughout the paper we use the convention
\[
F_1=F_2=1,\qquad F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n\quad (n\ge 1).
\]
The weight at position $k$ is $F_{k+1}$.

\paragraph{Finite-support digit-count sequences.}
If an alphabet $\mathcal A$ has a distinguished zero symbol, write
\[
\mathcal A^{(\NN)}
:=
\{x\in\mathcal A^{\NN}:\supp(x):=\{k:x_k\ne0\}\text{ is finite}\}.
\]
The distinguished zero is also used for zero padding outside the displayed
support.  In particular, the all-zero sequence is the zero element of every
finite-support sequence space used below.

Let
\[
\D
:=
\left\{
a=(a_1,a_2,\ldots) : a_k\in\NN,\ \supp(a)\text{ finite}
\right\},
\]
with pointwise addition.  We write $e_k$ for the $k$th standard basis vector.
Thus $e_k$ is the sequence in $\D$ with digit $1$ at position $k$ and digit
$0$ elsewhere.

\begin{definition}[Fibonacci congruence]\label{def:fib-congruence}
Let $\equiv$ be the least commutative-monoid congruence on $\D$ generated by
\[
e_2\equiv 2e_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
e_{k+2}\equiv e_{k+1}+e_k \quad (k\ge 1).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Value map on digit-count sequences]\label{def:val-on-D}
For $a\in\D$ we define
\[
\Val(a):=\sum_{k\ge 1} a_k F_{k+1}.
\]
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Admissible representatives.}
Let
\[
\Xinf
:=
\left\{
c\in\{0,1\}^{(\NN)} : c_kc_{k+1}=0\text{ for all }k
\right\}
\subseteq\D.
\]
By Zeckendorf's theorem, every $N\in\NN$ admits a unique representative
$Z(N)\in \Xinf$ such that $\Val(Z(N))=N$ \cite{Zeckendorf1972}.

\begin{proposition}[Value invariance of Fibonacci congruence]\label{prop:val-invariant}
If $a\equiv b$ in $\D$, then $\Val(a)=\Val(b)$.  Consequently the value map
descends to a well-defined monoid homomorphism
\[
\overline{\Val}:\D/\!\equiv \to \NN.
\]
\leanverified{paper_zeckendorf_val_invariant_seeds}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to check the defining relations.  For the recurrence relation,
\[
\Val(e_{k+2})=F_{k+3}=F_{k+2}+F_{k+1}=\Val(e_{k+1}+e_k).
\]
For the base relation,
\[
\Val(e_2)=F_3=2=2F_2=\Val(2e_1).
\]
The claim follows because $\equiv$ is generated from these relations by
additivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The quotient monoid is $\NN$]\label{thm:monoid-quotient-is-N}
The homomorphism $\overline{\Val}:\D/\!\equiv \to \NN$ is an isomorphism of
commutative monoids.  Moreover every congruence class contains a unique
representative in $\Xinf$.
\leanverified{paper_zeckendorf_monoid_quotient_is_N_seeds}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Surjectivity is immediate from Zeckendorf's theorem: for every $N\in\NN$,
$Z(N)\in \Xinf$ satisfies $\Val(Z(N))=N$.  For injectivity, write
$\varepsilon=[e_1]\in\D/\!\equiv$.  By induction on the recurrence relation,
\[
[e_k]=F_{k+1}\varepsilon \qquad (k\ge 1).
\]
Hence every class $[a]\in\D/\!\equiv$ satisfies
\[
[a]
=
\sum_{k\ge 1} a_k[e_k]
=
\left(\sum_{k\ge 1} a_kF_{k+1}\right)\varepsilon
=
\Val(a)\varepsilon.
\]
So the congruence class is uniquely determined by its value.  The uniqueness
of the representative in $\Xinf$ is then exactly the uniqueness of the
Zeckendorf expansion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Canonical Representatives and the Fold Section}

\begin{corollary}[Normalization induced by the canonical section]\label{cor:fold-as-section}
Let $q:\D\to\D/\!\equiv$ be the quotient map and define
\[
 s:\D/\!\equiv\to\D,
 \qquad
 s([a]):=Z(\overline{\Val}([a])).
\]
Then $s$ is well-defined, its image is $\Xinf$, and $q\circ s$ is the identity
on $\D/\!\equiv$.  Moreover
\[
\Fold_\infty=s\circ q:\D\to\Xinf,
\qquad
\Fold_\infty(a)=Z(\Val(a)).
\]
Thus $s$ is the canonical section selected by the unique admissible
representative in each congruence class.
\leanverified{paper_zeckendorf_fold_as_section_seeds}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:monoid-quotient-is-N}, the value
$\overline{\Val}([a])$ determines the class $[a]$, so $s$ is well-defined and
$Z(\overline{\Val}([a]))$ belongs to the same class.  Hence
$q(s([a]))=[a]$.  For $a\in\D$,
$s(q(a))=Z(\overline{\Val}([a]))=Z(\Val(a))=\Fold_\infty(a)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Digitwise Sums and Normalization}\label{sec:overlay}

\subsection{Value-Preserving Local Digit Rewrites}

Although $\Fold_\infty$ is defined quotient-theoretically, its action can be
described directly by local digit rewrites on $\D$.  For
$a=(a_1,a_2,\ldots)\in\D$, consider the following directed moves:
\begin{align*}
\text{(R1)}\qquad
a&\to a-e_k-e_{k+1}+e_{k+2}
&&\text{if } a_k\ge 1,\ a_{k+1}\ge 1,\\
\text{(R2)}\qquad
a&\to a-2e_1+e_2
&&\text{if } a_1\ge 2,\\
\text{(R3)}\qquad
a&\to a-2e_2+e_1+e_3
&&\text{if } a_2\ge 2,\\
\text{(R4)}\qquad
a&\to a-2e_k+e_{k-2}+e_{k+1}
&&\text{if } k\ge 3,\ a_k\ge 2.
\end{align*}
Each rule preserves value, since it is a directed form of one of the
identities
\[
F_{k+1}+F_{k+2}=F_{k+3},\qquad
2F_2=F_3,\qquad
2F_3=F_2+F_4,\qquad
2F_{k+1}=F_{k-1}+F_{k+2}\ (k\ge 3).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Termination and unique normal form]\label{prop:rewrite-termination}
Starting from any $a\in\D$, every rewrite sequence generated by
\textup{(R1)}--\textup{(R4)} terminates.  The irreducible configurations are
exactly the admissible words $\Xinf$, and the terminal irreducible word is
$\Fold_\infty(a)$.
\leanverified{paper_zeckendorf_rewrite_termination_seeds}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Define
\[
A(a):=\sum_{k\ge 1} a_k,\qquad
B(a):=\sum_{k\ge 1} k\,a_k,\qquad
C(a):=a_2,
\]
and compare triples $\mu(a):=(A(a),B(a),C(a))$ lexicographically.
Rule \textup{(R1)} and rule \textup{(R2)} decrease $A$ by $1$.
Rule \textup{(R4)} preserves $A$ and decreases $B$ by $1$.
Rule \textup{(R3)} preserves both $A$ and $B$, but decreases $C$ by $2$.
Hence every rewrite step strictly decreases $\mu(a)$, so infinite rewrite
sequences are impossible.

If $a$ is irreducible, then no adjacent pair of positive digits can occur, or
else \textup{(R1)} would apply; likewise no digit can be $\ge 2$, or else one of
\textup{(R2)}--\textup{(R4)} would apply.  Thus every irreducible configuration is a
finitely supported $\{0,1\}$-word with no adjacent $1$s, i.e. an element of
$\Xinf$.  Conversely, no rule applies to an element of $\Xinf$, so the
irreducibles are exactly $\Xinf$.

Every rewrite step preserves $\Val$, hence the terminal irreducible word has
the same value as $a$.  By Corollary~\ref{cor:fold-as-section}, there is a
unique admissible word with this value, namely $\Fold_\infty(a)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Confluence of the rewrite system]\label{cor:rewrite-confluence}
The rewrite relation generated by \textup{(R1)}--\textup{(R4)} is confluent.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $a\to^\ast b$ and $a\to^\ast c$.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-termination}, every continuation from $b$ and
from $c$ terminates at the unique normal form $\Fold_\infty(a)$.  Hence $b$
and $c$ are joinable, which is confluence.
\end{proof}

For digitwise sums of two admissible inputs the initial alphabet is only
$\{0,1,2\}$, but it is convenient to formulate the rewrite system on the whole
monoid $\D$.  This avoids case distinctions and keeps the normalization target
equal to the normalization induced by the section in
Corollary~\ref{cor:fold-as-section}.

\subsection{Canonical Addition}

For $c,d\in \Xinf$ we define the digitwise sum
\[
(c+d)_k:=c_k+d_k \in \{0,1,2\}.
\]
Since $c$ and $d$ have finite support, $c+d$ can be viewed as an element of
$\D$.

\begin{definition}[Canonical Zeckendorf addition]\label{def:stable-add}
For $c,d\in\Xinf$, define
\[
\ZAdd(c,d):=Z(\Val(c)+\Val(d)).
\]
\end{definition}

The previous subsection shows that $\ZAdd$ can be computed without leaving the
digit system.

\begin{theorem}[Addition is digitwise summation followed by normalization]\label{thm:zeckendorf-add-fst}
For all $c,d\in \Xinf$ one has
\[
\ZAdd(c,d)=\Fold_\infty(c+d).
\]
Equivalently, canonical Zeckendorf addition is the normalization of the
noncanonical digitwise sum.
\leanverified{paper_zeckendorf_add_fst}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By definition of $\Fold_\infty$,
\[
\Fold_\infty(c+d)=Z(\Val(c+d)).
\]
Since $\Val$ is additive on finitely supported digit strings,
\[
\Val(c+d)=\Val(c)+\Val(d).
\]
Therefore
\[
\Fold_\infty(c+d)=Z(\Val(c)+\Val(d))=\ZAdd(c,d).
\]
\end{proof}

Thus the addition problem reduces to the study of the canonical map
$\Fold_\infty$ on inputs over $\{0,1,2\}$.  The remainder of the paper
compares its LSDF sequential behavior with the classical MSDF Berstel adder.

Once the canonical section has been fixed, the arithmetic problem reduces to
realizing $\Fold_\infty$ by a finite sequential device with bounded delay
\cite{Frougny1992NumbersAutomata,Frougny1999OnlineAddition,
AhlbachUsatineFrougnyPippenger2013}.

\begin{example}[A short normalization trace]\label{rem:worked-normalization-example}
Using the LSDF Fibonacci-scale convention, let
\[
c=(1,0,1,0,0),\qquad d=(1,0,0,1,0)\in \Xinf.
\]
Then
\[
c+d=(2,0,1,1,0),
\]
so the digitwise sum contains both a digit $2$ and the forbidden pattern $11$.
Its value is
\[
\Val(c+d)=2F_2+F_4+F_5=2+3+5=10=F_3+F_6.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Fold_\infty(c+d)=Z(10)=(0,1,0,0,1)=\ZAdd(c,d).
\]
This is the smallest type of example in which normalization must resolve the
two standard obstructions simultaneously.
\end{example}

\section{Unbounded Anticipation and Non-Subsequentiality}\label{sec:streaming}

\subsection{Anticipation Profiles}

This section studies the LSDF one-sided model.  The input is a
finite-support sequence $x=(x_1,x_2,\ldots)$, where coordinate $k$ carries the
digit at Fibonacci weight $F_{k+1}$.  Reading the input LSDF means reading
$x_1,x_2,\ldots$ in increasing order of $k$.

Let
\[
\AddTwo:=\{c+d:c,d\in\Xinf\}\subseteq \{0,1,2\}^{(\NN)}
\]
be the set of genuine digitwise sums of two admissible Zeckendorf
representations.  For a class
$\mathcal C\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^{(\NN)}$ and a coordinate $j\ge 1$, define the
\emph{LSDF anticipation horizon}
\[
A_{\mathcal C}(j)
:=
\inf\left\{
h\ge j:
\begin{array}{l}
a_i=b_i\ (1\le i\le h),\ a,b\in\mathcal C\\
\Longrightarrow\quad
\Fold_\infty(a)_j=\Fold_\infty(b)_j
\end{array}
\right\},
\]
with $A_{\mathcal C}(j)=\infty$ if no such $h$ exists.  Thus
$A_{\mathcal C}(j)$ is the finite input horizon which would determine the
$j$th normalized output digit on the class $\mathcal C$, if such a horizon
exists.

\begin{definition}[One-pass online delay]\label{def:online-delay}
Let $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ be finite alphabets with distinguished zero
symbols.  A map
\[
T:\mathcal A^{(\NN)}\to \mathcal B^{(\NN)}
\]
has \emph{one-pass online delay} $\delta\in\NN$ if, for every $j\ge 1$, the
output digit $(T(x))_j$ depends only on the input prefix
$x_1,\dots,x_{j+\delta}$.  Equivalently, after reading the first $n$ input
digits, a sequential device with delay $\delta$ has already determined the
first $\max\{n-\delta,0\}$ output digits.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:delay-convention}
Definition~\ref{def:online-delay} is the LSDF notion adapted to
$\Fold_\infty$ on finite-support sequences.  It is not the MSDF anticipation
parameter used in the classical online-addition literature
\cite{Frougny1999OnlineAddition}.  The next theorem says more than the failure
of a global bounded delay: every individual output coordinate has infinite
LSDF anticipation, even on genuine addition inputs.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Exact infinite anticipation profile]\label{thm:coordinatewise-unbounded-anticipation}
For every output coordinate $j\ge 1$,
\[
A_{\{0,1,2\}^{(\NN)}}(j)=A_{\AddTwo}(j)=\infty.
\]
Equivalently, for every $j\ge 1$ and every $\delta\ge 0$, there exist
genuine addition inputs $x,y\in\AddTwo$ such that
\[
x_i=y_i \qquad (1\le i\le j+\delta),
\]
but
\[
(\Fold_\infty(x))_j\neq (\Fold_\infty(y))_j.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $j\ge 1$ and $\delta\ge 0$.

If $j=1$, choose $m\ge 1$ with $2m\ge \delta+1$ and define
\[
x:=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1} e_{2t+1},
\qquad
y:=x+2e_{2m+1}.
\]
The two inputs agree on the first $\delta+1$ positions.  Since $x$ is already
admissible, $\Fold_\infty(x)=x$, so $(\Fold_\infty(x))_1=1$.  Moreover
\[
\Val(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{m}F_{2t}=F_{2m+1}-1,
\]
hence
\[
\Val(y)=F_{2m+1}-1+2F_{2m+2}=F_{2m+4}-1=\sum_{t=1}^{m+1}F_{2t+1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Fold_\infty(y)=\sum_{t=1}^{m+1} e_{2t},
\]
and so $(\Fold_\infty(y))_1=0$.
Both inputs lie in $\AddTwo$: $x=x+0$ with $x,0\in\Xinf$, while
\[
y=
\left(\sum_{t=0}^{m}e_{2t+1}\right)+e_{2m+1},
\]
and both summands are admissible.

Assume now that $j\ge 2$.  Choose $m\ge 1$ with $2m-1\ge \delta$ and set
\[
x:=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1} e_{j+2t},
\qquad
y:=x+2e_{j+2m}.
\]
The two inputs agree on the first $j+2m-1$ positions, hence on the first
$j+\delta$ positions.  Again $x$ is admissible, so $\Fold_\infty(x)=x$ and
$(\Fold_\infty(x))_j=1$.

For the second input, one computes
\[
\Val(y)
=
\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}F_{j+2t+1}+2F_{j+2m+1}
=
F_{j-1}+\sum_{t=0}^{m}F_{j+2t+2}.
\]
The last identity may be checked directly by induction on $m$ from the
Fibonacci recurrence.
Hence the admissible word
\[
z:=Z(F_{j-1})+\sum_{t=0}^{m} e_{j+1+2t}
\]
has the same value as $y$.  By uniqueness of admissible representatives,
$\Fold_\infty(y)=z$.  Since all nonzero digits of the second summand lie
strictly to the right of position $j$, and $Z(F_{j-1})$ is supported in
positions $<j$, one has $z_j=0$.  Thus $(\Fold_\infty(y))_j=0$.
Again $x=x+0$ lies in $\AddTwo$, and
\[
y=
\left(\sum_{t=0}^{m}e_{j+2t}\right)+e_{j+2m}
\]
is a sum of two admissible words.  Therefore the same witnesses prove the
claim on $\AddTwo$, and the claim on all ternary finite-support inputs follows
because $\AddTwo\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^{(\NN)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No finite one-pass delay]\label{thm:no-bounded-delay}
The canonical normalization map $\Fold_\infty$ admits no finite one-pass
online delay.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $\Fold_\infty$ had one-pass online delay $\delta$, then the $j$th output
digit would be determined by the first $j+\delta$ input digits for every
$j\ge 1$.  This contradicts
Theorem~\ref{thm:coordinatewise-unbounded-anticipation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No continuous extension at alternating tails]\label{thm:no-continuous-extension}
Embed $\{0,1,2\}^{(\NN)}$ into the one-sided product space
$\{0,1,2\}^{\NN}$ with the product topology.  For every $j\ge 1$, let
\[
z^{(j)}:=\sum_{t\ge 0}e_{j+2t}
\]
be the infinite alternating tail beginning at $j$.  The $j$th coordinate of
$\Fold_\infty$ has no continuous extension to any domain containing
$z^{(j)}$ and the finite-support inputs.  More precisely, there are
finite-support sequences $a_m,b_m\to z^{(j)}$ such that
\[
\Fold_\infty(a_m)_j=1,\qquad \Fold_\infty(b_m)_j=0
\]
for all $m$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take
\[
a_m:=\sum_{t=0}^{m}e_{j+2t},
\qquad
b_m:=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}e_{j+2t}+2e_{j+2m}.
\]
Both sequences converge coordinatewise to $z^{(j)}$.  The computation in the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:coordinatewise-unbounded-anticipation}, with the
obvious shift of the parameter for $a_m$, gives
\[
\Fold_\infty(a_m)_j=1,\qquad \Fold_\infty(b_m)_j=0.
\]
Thus any coordinatewise continuous extension would assign two different
values to the same limiting input $z^{(j)}$ at coordinate $j$, which is
impossible.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite-Word Commitment and Prefix Erasure}

To compare the one-sided canonical map with standard transducer models, we
encode finitely supported inputs as LSDF finite words.  For
$u=u_1\cdots u_n\in\{0,1,2\}^\ast$, write
\[
\encLSDF(u):=\sum_{i=1}^n u_i e_i\in \D.
\]
If $a\in\{0,1\}^{(\NN)}$ is finitely supported, let $\operatorname{trim}(a)$
denote the finite word obtained by deleting trailing zeros; by convention,
$\operatorname{trim}(0)=\varepsilon$ for the all-zero sequence.  We then define
\[
\mathcal N(u):=\operatorname{trim}(\Fold_\infty(\encLSDF(u)))
\qquad (u\in\{0,1,2\}^\ast).
\]
After reading a finite input prefix $u$, the part of the normalized output
which can be emitted irrevocably is the common prefix of all possible future
normalizations.  Define
\[
C(u):=\lcp\{\mathcal N(uw):w\in\{0,1,2\}^\ast\}.
\]
Equivalently, $C(u)$ is the longest word which is a prefix of
$\mathcal N(uw)$ for every continuation $w$.  Since the empty continuation is
allowed, $C(u)$ is a prefix of $\mathcal N(u)$.

For $n\ge 1$, define the \emph{online commitment deficit}
\[
B(n):=
\max\Bigl\{
|\mathcal N(u)|-|C(u)|:
|\mathcal N(u)|=n
\Bigr\}.
\]
Thus $B(n)$ is the largest amount of currently normalized output of length
$n$ which an LSDF online algorithm may still be unable to commit.  We also
use the finite-shock version
\[
\Delta(n):=
\max\Bigl\{
n-\bigl|\lcp(\mathcal N(u),\mathcal N(v))\bigr|:
u\text{ is a proper prefix of }v,\ |\mathcal N(u)|=n
\Bigr\}.
\]
This is the \emph{prefix-destruction index}: it measures how much common
output prefix can be destroyed by passing from a prefix $u$ to a proper
extension $v$ when $|\mathcal N(u)|=n$.

\begin{theorem}[Maximal LSDF commitment deficit]\label{thm:exact-prefix-destruction}
For every $n\ge 1$,
\[
B(n)=\Delta(n)=n.
\]
Equivalently, for every output length $n$ there is an input prefix $u$ with
$|\mathcal N(u)|=n$ for which no nonempty prefix of $\mathcal N(u)$ is safe to
emit before the future input is known.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since both $C(u)$ and $\lcp(\mathcal N(u),\mathcal N(v))$ have nonnegative
length, one always has $B(n)\le n$ and $\Delta(n)\le n$.

It remains to exhibit, for each $n$, a pair in which $u$ is a proper prefix
of $v$, $|\mathcal N(u)|=n$, and
$\lcp(\mathcal N(u),\mathcal N(v))=\varepsilon$.  This proves $\Delta(n)=n$; it also
forces $C(u)=\varepsilon$, because $C(u)$ must be a common prefix of
$\mathcal N(u)$ and $\mathcal N(v)$, and hence proves $B(n)=n$.

If $n=2m-1$ is odd, use
\[
u_m=(10)^{m-1}1,
\qquad
v_m=u_m02.
\]
Then
\[
\encLSDF(u_m)=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1} e_{2t+1},
\qquad
\encLSDF(v_m)=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1} e_{2t+1}+2e_{2m+1}.
\]
These are the $j=1$ witnesses from the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:coordinatewise-unbounded-anticipation}.  Therefore
\[
\mathcal N(u_m)=u_m,
\qquad
\mathcal N(v_m)=0(10)^m1.
\]
The two normalized outputs have no common first symbol, and
$|\mathcal N(u_m)|=2m-1=n$.

If $n=2m$ is even, set
\[
a_m:=(10)^{m-1}2,
\qquad
b_m:=a_m1.
\]
Then $a_m$ is a proper prefix of $b_m$.  Moreover
\[
\Val(a_m)=\sum_{t=1}^{m-1}F_{2t}+2F_{2m}
=(F_{2m-1}-1)+2F_{2m}=F_{2m+2}-1
=\sum_{t=1}^{m}F_{2t+1},
\]
so
\[
\mathcal N(a_m)=(01)^m.
\]
Likewise,
\[
\Val(b_m)=\Val(a_m)+F_{2m+1}=F_{2m+3}-1
=\sum_{t=1}^{m+1}F_{2t},
\]
and therefore
\[
\mathcal N(b_m)=1(01)^m.
\]
Again the normalized outputs have no common first symbol, and
$|\mathcal N(a_m)|=2m=n$.
\end{proof}

The previous theorem uses all ternary inputs.  The same maximal prefix erasure
occurs even after restricting to genuine digitwise sums of admissible
Zeckendorf inputs.  Let
\[
\AddTwo^\ast:=\{u\in\{0,1,2\}^\ast:\encLSDF(u)\in\AddTwo\}.
\]
For $n\ge 1$, define
\[
\Delta_{\AddTwo}(n):=
\max\Bigl\{
n-\bigl|\lcp(\mathcal N(u),\mathcal N(v))\bigr|:
u\text{ is a proper prefix of }v,\ u,v\in\AddTwo^\ast,\ |\mathcal N(u)|=n
\Bigr\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Normal forms of all-ones inputs]\label{lem:all-ones-normal-forms}
For $r\ge 1$, let $1^r$ denote the LSDF word of length $r$ all of whose
digits are $1$.  Then
\[
\mathcal N(1)=1,
\]
and, for $s\ge 1$,
\[
\mathcal N(1^{2s})=\operatorname{trim}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s} e_{2i+1}\right),
\qquad
\mathcal N(1^{2s+1})=
\operatorname{trim}\left(e_1+\sum_{i=2}^{s+1}e_{2i}\right).
\]
In particular, for every $r\ge 2$ one has
$|\mathcal N(1^r)|=r+1$, and the first digit of $\mathcal N(1^r)$ is $0$
for even $r$ and $1$ for odd $r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The value of $1^r$ is
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{r}F_{k+1}=F_{r+3}-2.
\]
For $r=1$ this is $F_2$, so $\mathcal N(1)=1$.
For $r=2s$ with $s\ge 1$, the displayed candidate is admissible and has value
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{s}F_{2i+2}=F_{2s+3}-2.
\]
For $r=2s+1$ with $s\ge 1$, the displayed candidate is admissible and has
value
\[
F_2+\sum_{i=2}^{s+1}F_{2i+1}=F_{2s+4}-2.
\]
The two Fibonacci-sum identities follow by induction from the recurrence.
Uniqueness of Zeckendorf representatives then gives the claimed normal forms.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Maximal prefix erasure on genuine addition inputs]\label{thm:addtwo-prefix-destruction}
For every $n\ge 1$,
\[
\Delta_{\AddTwo}(n)=n.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound $\Delta_{\AddTwo}(n)\le n$ is immediate.  We prove the matching
lower bound by explicit prefix pairs in $\AddTwo^\ast$.

For $n=1$, take $u=1$ and $v=11$.  Both are in $\AddTwo^\ast$:
$1=1+0$, while $11=10+01$ as a digitwise sum of two admissible words.  Also
\[
\mathcal N(1)=1,\qquad \mathcal N(11)=001,
\]
so the common prefix is empty.

For $n=2$, take $u=2$ and $v=2002$.  Here $2=1+1$ and
$2002=1001+1001$, so both words are in $\AddTwo^\ast$.  Moreover
\[
\mathcal N(2)=01,\qquad \mathcal N(2002)=10101,
\]
and again the common prefix is empty.

Now assume $n\ge 3$.  Put
\[
u=1^{n-1},\qquad v=1^n.
\]
Each all-ones word $1^r$ lies in $\AddTwo^\ast$, since it is the digitwise sum
of the two admissible alternating words with support on the odd and even
positions, respectively.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:all-ones-normal-forms},
$|\mathcal N(u)|=n$, and the first digit of $\mathcal N(v)$ is the opposite
of the first digit of $\mathcal N(u)$.  Hence
\[
\lcp(\mathcal N(u),\mathcal N(v))=\varepsilon.
\]
Thus the upper bound is attained for every $n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reduced Residual Growth and Subsequentiality}

Since $C(u)$ is a prefix of every $\mathcal N(uw)$, define the reduced
residual at $u$ by the identity
\[
\mathcal N(uw)=C(u)\rho_u(w)
\qquad (w\in\{0,1,2\}^\ast).
\]
For $n\ge 1$, let the input-scale reduced-residual complexity be
\[
R_{\mathrm{in}}(n):=\#\{\rho_u:\ |u|\le n\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Linear reduced-residual growth]\label{thm:reduced-residual-growth}
For every $n\ge 1$,
\[
R_{\mathrm{in}}(n)\ge n.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the explicit prefixes from the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-prefix-destruction}.  For each $1\le k\le n$, choose
$u_k=(10)^{m-1}1$ when $k=2m-1$ and $u_k=(10)^{m-1}2$ when $k=2m$.
Then $|u_k|=k$ in the odd case and $|u_k|=k-1$ in the even case, while
$|\mathcal N(u_k)|=k$ and $C(u_k)=\varepsilon$ in both cases.  Therefore
\[
\rho_{u_k}(\varepsilon)=\mathcal N(u_k).
\]
These residual functions are pairwise distinct, because their values at
$\varepsilon$ have different lengths.  Hence at least $n$ distinct reduced
residuals occur among prefixes of input length at most $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:addtwo-prefix-destruction}, the prefixes $u_k$ in the
previous proof may be chosen from $\AddTwo^\ast$.  Thus the same linear
lower-bound witnesses occur inside the LSDF genuine addition image, even though
the residuals are still evaluated as functions on all ternary continuations.
\end{remark}

Here a \emph{deterministic subsequential transducer} is a deterministic finite
automaton with an output word attached to each transition and a terminal output
word attached to each final state; the value of the realized function is the
concatenation of the transition outputs along the unique run and the terminal
output of the final state.  This is the standard sequential-transducer model.

\begin{lemma}[Bounded terminal discrepancy for subsequential transducers]\label{lem:subseq-terminal-bound}
Let $T:\Sigma^\ast\to\Gamma^\ast$ be realized by a deterministic
subsequential transducer with terminal output map $\omega$.  Set
\[
L:=\max_{q\in Q} |\omega(q)|,
\]
where $Q$ is the finite state set.
If $u$ is a prefix of $v$, then $T(u)$ and $T(v)$ have a common prefix of
length at least $|T(u)|-L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $v=uw$.  Let $\alpha$ be the output produced while reading $u$, and let
$q$ be the state reached after $u$.  Then
\[
T(u)=\alpha\,\omega(q).
\]
If the run is continued along $w$, the output has the form
\[
T(v)=\alpha\,\beta\,\omega(q')
\]
for some word $\beta$ and some terminal state $q'$.  Thus $T(u)$ and $T(v)$
share the prefix $\alpha$, whose length is
\[
|\alpha|=|T(u)|-|\omega(q)|\ge |T(u)|-L.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The one-sided canonical normalizer is not subsequential]\label{thm:canonical-not-subsequential}
The finite-word normalization map
\[
\mathcal N:\{0,1,2\}^\ast\to\{0,1\}^\ast
\]
is not realizable by any deterministic subsequential transducer reading the
input in LSDF order.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If such a transducer existed, let $L$ be the bound from
Lemma~\ref{lem:subseq-terminal-bound}.  Choose $n>L$ and take a prefix pair
in which $u$ is a proper prefix of $v$, $|\mathcal N(u)|=n$, and
$\lcp(\mathcal N(u),\mathcal N(v))=\varepsilon$, whose existence is guaranteed by
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-prefix-destruction}.  The lemma would imply that
$\mathcal N(u)$ and $\mathcal N(v)$ have a common prefix of length at least
$n-L>0$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:frougny-separation}
The point of Corollary~\ref{thm:canonical-not-subsequential} is not to claim a
first non-sequentiality phenomenon in Fibonacci numeration.  Rather, the
preceding results give sharpened invariants for the specific canonical LSDF
normalization map: the obstruction is coordinatewise, the online commitment
deficit is exactly maximal, the reduced residual complexity grows at least
linearly, and the same prefix-erasure phenomenon already appears on genuine
digitwise sums of admissible Zeckendorf inputs.
\par
Corollary~\ref{thm:canonical-not-subsequential} shows that the one-sided map
$\Fold_\infty$ is not the same object as the classical online adders of
Frougny and Berstel.  In Frougny's MSDF model,
addition in base $\varphi$ is computable with delay $3$, and the corresponding
Fibonacci finite-word recoding uses a terminal output; the output is not the
greedy expansion \cite[Prop.~13 and Cor.~4]{Frougny1999OnlineAddition}.  The next two sections
revisit that classical transducer on its own terms.
\end{remark}

\section{The Classical Berstel Adder}\label{sec:kernel}

\subsection{A Deterministic Subsequential Transducer}

We now switch to the classical finite-word MSDF setting of
\cite{Frougny1999OnlineAddition,LabbeLepsova2023FibonacciTwosComplement}.
The relevant device is the ten-state Berstel adder.  Its construction and
ten-state presentation are classical.  We use the deterministic
subsequential convention in which every transition produces one output letter
and a terminal output word is appended at the end of the run.  Mousavi,
Schaeffer, and Shallit use the state set $\{0,\ldots,16\}$ for a $17$-state
three-tape DFA recognizing padded triples $(x,y,z)_F$ satisfying $x+y=z$;
state $0$ is the unique dead state, and the remaining $16$ states form a
relation acceptor, not an alternative state realization of the single-valued
Berstel recoding $\mathcal K$
\cite{MousaviSchaefferShallit2016FibonacciAutomatic}.  A direct constructive
proof of $\mathcal K$ is given by Labb\'e and Lep\v{s}ov\'a
\cite{LabbeLepsova2023FibonacciTwosComplement}.

Its state set is
\[
Q=
\{000,001,002,010,100,101,0\bar{1}2,1\bar{1}2,01\bar{1},11\bar{1}\}.
\]
The transitions are
\[
\begin{aligned}
000&\xrightarrow{d/0}00d,\qquad 100\xrightarrow{d/1}00d\quad(d=0,1,2),\\
001&\xrightarrow{0/0}010,\ \xrightarrow{1/0}100,\ \xrightarrow{2/0}101,\\
002&\xrightarrow{0/0}11\bar{1},\ \xrightarrow{1/1}000,\ \xrightarrow{2/1}001,\\
010&\xrightarrow{0/0}100,\ \xrightarrow{1/0}101,\ \xrightarrow{2/1}0\bar{1}2,\\
101&\xrightarrow{0/1}010,\ \xrightarrow{1/1}100,\ \xrightarrow{2/1}101,\\
0\bar{1}2&\xrightarrow{0/0}01\bar{1},\ \xrightarrow{1/0}010,\ \xrightarrow{2/0}100,\\
1\bar{1}2&\xrightarrow{0/1}01\bar{1},\ \xrightarrow{1/1}010,\ \xrightarrow{2/1}100,\\
01\bar{1}&\xrightarrow{0/0}001,\ \xrightarrow{1/0}002,\ \xrightarrow{2/0}1\bar{1}2,\\
11\bar{1}&\xrightarrow{0/1}001,\ \xrightarrow{1/1}002,\ \xrightarrow{2/1}1\bar{1}2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The terminal output map is
\[
\omega(000)=000,\ \omega(001)=001,\ \omega(002)=010,\ \omega(010)=010,
\]
\[
\omega(100)=100,\ \omega(101)=101,\ \omega(0\bar{1}2)=000,\ \omega(1\bar{1}2)=100,
\]
\[
\omega(01\bar{1})=001,\qquad \omega(11\bar{1})=101.
\]

Let
\[
\mathcal K=(Q,\delta,\eta,\omega)
\]
denote the deterministic subsequential transducer defined above, with initial
state $000$.  For a state $q\in Q$, let
\[
F_q:\{0,1,2\}^\ast\to\{0,1\}^\ast
\]
be the function obtained by starting in $q$, following the transition labels,
and appending the terminal word $\omega$ of the last state.
For a finite word $w=d_1\cdots d_n$ read most significant digit first, we
write
\[
\Val_{\mathrm{MSD}}(w):=\sum_{i=1}^{n} d_i F_{n-i+2}.
\]
When discussing residual functions and shortest separating words, we keep the
complete output word, including leading zeros, because those zeros belong to
the subsequential output.  They are immaterial only at the level of the
represented Fibonacci value.

All ten displayed states are reachable from the initial state.  One may use
\[
\varepsilon,\ 1,\ 2,\ 10,\ 11,\ 12,\ 20,\ 102,\ 202,\ 1020
\]
to reach respectively
\[
000,\ 001,\ 002,\ 010,\ 100,\ 101,\ 11\bar{1},\ 0\bar{1}2,\ 1\bar{1}2,\ 01\bar{1}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Value preservation of the Berstel recoding]\label{prop:classical-berstel-correctness}
The transducer $\mathcal K$ is the classical Berstel adder in the
most-significant-digit-first Fibonacci setting.  It computes a value-preserving
binary recoding of words over $\{0,1,2\}$: for every input word $w$, the
complete output $\mathcal K(w)$ is binary and satisfies
\[
\Val_{\mathrm{MSD}}(\mathcal K(w))=\Val_{\mathrm{MSD}}(w).
\]
In general this output is not the greedy Zeckendorf expansion.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This classical result is restated only to fix the conventions used below; see
\cite[Cor.~4]{Frougny1999OnlineAddition} and
\cite[Thm.~3.1]{LabbeLepsova2023FibonacciTwosComplement}.  Those references
show that the machine realizes a value-preserving recoding from ternary
Fibonacci input words to binary Fibonacci output words in the MSDF convention.
Frougny notes before Proposition~13 that the output need not be greedy, and
Corollary~4 supplies the terminal output for Fibonacci numeration.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A short non-greedy output witness]\label{ex:berstel-nongreedy}
Starting from the initial state $000$, the genuine addition input
$202=101+101$ produces the
complete output word $001100$.  After suppressing the two leading zeros, the
represented binary word is $1100$, which still contains the forbidden factor
$11$.  Hence the classical Berstel output is not the greedy Zeckendorf
expansion of the same value.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
Corollary~\ref{thm:no-bounded-delay} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:classical-berstel-correctness} concern different
sequential functions.  The former concerns the canonical one-sided map
$\Fold_\infty$, whereas the latter concerns the classical Berstel recoding in
the MSDF model.  Example~\ref{ex:berstel-nongreedy}
exhibits the difference
explicitly: the Berstel machine returns a non-greedy value-preserving recoding,
while the canonical map of Section~\ref{sec:streaming} is defined to return the greedy
representative.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The Berstel adder and normalization]\label{rem:berstel-contains-normalizer}
The Berstel adder contains normalization in the following operational sense:
it maps a ternary digitwise sum to a binary Fibonacci representation of the
same value.  The output need not be admissible, so a further normalization
step may be required to obtain the greedy Zeckendorf representation.  This is
why the Berstel adder realizes the noncanonical addition recoding $\gamma$
rather than the canonical normalizer $\mathcal N$ studied in
Section~\ref{sec:streaming}.
\end{remark}

\section{Output-Delay Quotient of the Berstel Kernel}\label{sec:minimality}

\subsection{Complete-Output Residuals}

The ten-state table above is the classical Berstel presentation.  In this
subsection we record exactly what is true for that displayed complete-output
kernel, and then normalize output delays.  This distinction matters:
unrestricted subsequential transducers allow common output prefixes of a
state-residual to be pushed to incoming transitions or to an initial output.
Consequently, pairwise inequality of the raw functions $F_q$ is a statement
about the displayed kernel, not by itself a proof of unrestricted
subsequential state minimality.

Define an equivalence relation on the displayed states by
\[
q\equiv_{\mathrm{raw}} q' \iff F_q=F_{q'}.
\]
This relation keeps the complete output word from each state, including the
terminal output and all leading zeros.

\begin{lemma}[Separation by terminal output]\label{lem:terminal-separation}
If $\omega(q)\neq \omega(q')$, then $q\not\equiv_{\mathrm{raw}} q'$.
Consequently the only
pairs not already separated by the empty input are
\[
(000,0\bar{1}2),\quad
(001,01\bar{1}),\quad
(002,010),\quad
(100,1\bar{1}2),\quad
(101,11\bar{1}).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $\omega(q)\neq \omega(q')$, then
\[
F_q(\varepsilon)=\omega(q)\neq \omega(q')=F_{q'}(\varepsilon),
\]
so $q\not\equiv_{\mathrm{raw}} q'$.  Reading the terminal output list above,
one sees that the only equal-terminal pairs are exactly the five pairs
displayed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The remaining pairs are separated by the input $0$]\label{lem:zero-separation}
The five pairs listed in Lemma~\ref{lem:terminal-separation} are all
distinct under $\equiv_{\mathrm{raw}}$.  More precisely,
\[
F_{000}(0)=0000 \neq 0001 = F_{0\bar{1}2}(0),
\]
\[
F_{001}(0)=0010 \neq 0001 = F_{01\bar{1}}(0),
\]
\[
F_{002}(0)=0101 \neq 0100 = F_{010}(0),
\]
\[
F_{100}(0)=1000 \neq 1001 = F_{1\bar{1}2}(0),
\]
\[
F_{101}(0)=1010 \neq 1001 = F_{11\bar{1}}(0).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each identity is obtained by following one transition on the input $0$ and
then appending the terminal output of the arrival state.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Six-State Output-Delay Quotient}

\begin{proposition}[The ten raw Berstel residuals are distinct]\label{prop:berstel-raw-residuals}
All ten states in the displayed Berstel table are reachable and pairwise
distinct under $\equiv_{\mathrm{raw}}$.  Moreover every pair of distinct raw
residuals is separated by a suffix of length at most $1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:terminal-separation}, every pair with distinct terminal
output is already separated by the empty input.  Lemma~\ref{lem:zero-separation}
handles the only five remaining pairs.  Hence all ten states are pairwise
distinct as complete-output residual functions, and the longest separating
suffix needed in this raw sense has length $1$:
\[
\boxed{|Q/\!\equiv_{\mathrm{raw}}|=10,\qquad
\max_{q\neq q'}|w_{q,q'}|\le 1,}
\]
where $w_{q,q'}$ denotes a shortest separating word for the pair $(q,q')$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Six-state output-delay quotient]\label{prop:berstel-six-state-quotient}
After removing the forced common output prefix from each raw residual, the
Berstel function is realized by a six-state subsequential quotient.  More
precisely, put
\[
p(q):=\lcp\{F_q(w):w\in\{0,1,2\}^\ast\},
\qquad
G_q(w):=p(q)^{-1}F_q(w).
\]
Here $p(q)^{-1}F_q(w)$ denotes the unique suffix $G_q(w)$ determined by
$F_q(w)=p(q)G_q(w)$; no group inverse is intended.
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
&p(000)=p(001)=p(0\bar{1}2)=p(01\bar{1})=0,\\
&p(100)=p(101)=p(1\bar{1}2)=p(11\bar{1})=1,\\
&p(002)=p(010)=\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\]
The reduced residuals form the six classes
\[
\{000,100\},\quad
\{001,101\},\quad
\{002\},\quad
\{010\},\quad
\{0\bar{1}2,1\bar{1}2\},\quad
\{01\bar{1},11\bar{1}\}.
\]
Writing these classes as $A,B,C,D,E,F$, respectively, the quotient
transducer is
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
&0&1&2&\omega\\
\hline
A &0/A&0/B&\varepsilon/C&00\\
B &\varepsilon/D&1/A&1/B&01\\
C &01/F&10/A&10/B&010\\
D &01/A&01/B&10/E&010\\
E &0/F&\varepsilon/D&1/A&00\\
F &0/B&\varepsilon/C&1/E&01 .
\end{array}
\]
With initial state $A$ and initial output $0$, this six-state quotient
realizes the same complete-output function as $\mathcal K$.  If one uses a
formalism without initial output, the initial output can be replaced by a
single dummy initial state.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each state $q$ and input digit $d$, write
\[
q\xrightarrow{d/o} q'
\]
for the transition of the ten-state table.  The reduced transition output is
obtained by factoring
\[
o\,p(q')=p(q)\,\alpha,
\]
and using $\alpha$ as the quotient output on $d$.  The displayed values of
$p(q)$ are read directly from the transition and terminal-output table: the
four states $000,001,0\bar{1}2,01\bar{1}$ have all complete outputs beginning
with $0$, the four states $100,101,1\bar{1}2,11\bar{1}$ have all complete
outputs beginning with $1$, and the states $002,010$ have no forced first
letter.
The prefixes are exact: the following suffixes produce two complete outputs
from the indicated state with different next letters after the displayed
prefix,
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
q&u&v\\
\hline
000&\varepsilon&21\\
001&0&1\\
0\bar{1}2&\varepsilon&2\\
01\bar{1}&0&2\\
100&\varepsilon&21\\
101&\varepsilon&2\\
1\bar{1}2&\varepsilon&2\\
11\bar{1}&\varepsilon&2\\
002&\varepsilon&1\\
010&\varepsilon&2 .
\end{array}
\]
For example, $F_{000}(\varepsilon)=000$ while $F_{000}(21)=01000$, so the lcp
from state $000$ is exactly $0$; the other rows are the same short suffix
check.

Substituting the ten-state transition table into the factorization above gives
exactly the six rows displayed.  For example, from $A$ represented by $000$,
the input $2$ gives
\[
000\xrightarrow{2/0}002,\qquad 0\,p(002)=0=p(000)\varepsilon,
\]
so the quotient transition is $A\xrightarrow{2/\varepsilon}C$.  From the other
representative $100$ of $A$ one has
\[
100\xrightarrow{2/1}002,\qquad 1\,p(002)=1=p(100)\varepsilon,
\]
which gives the same reduced transition.  The other entries are identical
checks.

The terminal outputs are reduced in the same way, by factoring
\[
\omega(q)=p(q)\,\omega_{\mathrm{red}}([q]).
\]
Thus, for instance, $\omega(000)=000=0\cdot 00$ and
$\omega(100)=100=1\cdot 00$, so class $A$ has terminal output $00$.
The remaining classes give the terminal outputs displayed in the table.
Finally, since the initial ten-state residual is $F_{000}=0G_A$, the quotient
realizes the same complete-output function with initial output $0$ and initial
state $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Minimality of the reduced quotient]\label{prop:berstel-six-state-minimal}
The six reduced residuals $A,B,C,D,E,F$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:berstel-six-state-quotient} are pairwise distinct.
Consequently the output-delay quotient displayed there is the minimal
subsequential residual quotient of the Berstel function, with the convention
that an initial output word is allowed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The normalized-residual construction and minimization theorem for sequential
string-to-string transducers are standard; see
\cite{Mohri1994MinimizationSequentialTransducers}.  For completeness, the
lower bound needed here can be seen directly.  If two input prefixes reach the
same state in an equivalent subsequential realization, then their continuation
outputs differ only by the output words accumulated before that common state.
After deleting the forced common prefix from each continuation function, the
two prefixes therefore induce the same normalized residual.  Consequently,
distinct reachable normalized residuals must be represented by distinct
states.  It remains to verify the separation of the six normalized residuals
displayed in Proposition~\ref{prop:berstel-six-state-quotient}.

The terminal outputs of the six reduced states are
\[
\omega(A)=\omega(E)=00,\qquad
\omega(B)=\omega(F)=01,\qquad
\omega(C)=\omega(D)=010.
\]
Thus any pair not among $(A,E)$, $(B,F)$, and $(C,D)$ is separated by the
empty suffix.  The remaining three pairs are separated by the one-letter
suffix $0$:
\[
G_A(0)=000 \neq 001=G_E(0),
\]
\[
G_B(0)=010 \neq 001=G_F(0),
\]
and
\[
G_C(0)=0101 \neq 0100=G_D(0).
\]
Hence the six reduced residual functions are pairwise distinct.  All six are
reachable because their constituent states are reachable in the ten-state
kernel.  Thus every equivalent subsequential realization has at least six
states, while Proposition~\ref{prop:berstel-six-state-quotient} supplies a
six-state realization.  The quotient is therefore minimal.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Propositions~\ref{prop:berstel-six-state-quotient} and
\ref{prop:berstel-six-state-minimal} are the sense in which the
ten-state Berstel presentation should not be confused with an unrestricted
minimal subsequential presentation.  The ten states are the classical
letter-to-letter complete-output kernel; after standard output-delay
normalization, the induced subsequential function has the six reduced
residuals displayed above.
\end{remark}

\section{Iterating the Berstel Recoding}\label{sec:berstel-iteration}

The Berstel adder does not always output the greedy Zeckendorf representative,
but it does output a binary word of the same Fibonacci value.  It is therefore
natural to iterate the same recoding until the greedy representative is
reached.

For a finite MSDF word $w$, let $\trimMSD(w)$ be the word obtained by deleting
leading zeros, with $\trimMSD(\varepsilon)=0$ and every all-zero word
represented by the single word $0$.
For $w\in\{0,1,2\}^\ast$, let
\[
\Berstel(w):=\trimMSD(\mathcal K(\trimMSD(w))),
\]
where $\mathcal K$ is the complete-output Berstel transducer of
Section~\ref{sec:kernel}.  Let $Z_{\mathrm{MSD}}(w)$ denote the greedy
Zeckendorf word, written MSDF and with $Z_{\mathrm{MSD}}(0)=0$, such that
$\Val_{\mathrm{MSD}}(Z_{\mathrm{MSD}}(w))=\Val_{\mathrm{MSD}}(w)$.  Define the
\emph{Berstel depth}, with the convention $\min\varnothing=\infty$,
\[
\tau(w):=\min\{t\ge 0:\Berstel^t(\trimMSD(w))=Z_{\mathrm{MSD}}(w)\}.
\]
Thus leading-zero padding is ignored by definition; in particular
$\tau(0w)=\tau(w)$ whenever $0w$ and $w$ have the same MSDF value.
For length $n$, let
\[
\AddTwo^{\mathrm{MSD}}(n)
\]
be the set of length-$n$ digitwise sums of two admissible binary Zeckendorf
words in the MSDF convention, allowing leading zeros in the two summands.

\begin{lemma}[Genuine two-summand image]\label{lem:addtwo-msd-characterization}
For $w\in\{0,1,2\}^n$, the following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item $w\in\AddTwo^{\mathrm{MSD}}(n)$.
\item None of the factors $12,21,22$ occurs in $w$.
\item If $w_i=2$, then every existing neighboring digit is $0$: $w_{i-1}=0$
whenever $i>1$, and $w_{i+1}=0$ whenever $i<n$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $w=x+y$ for admissible binary summands $x,y$, then $w_i=2$ implies
$x_i=y_i=1$.  Since both summands have no adjacent $1$'s, the neighboring
positions, if present, must satisfy $x_{i\pm1}=y_{i\pm1}=0$.  Hence a $2$
cannot be adjacent to a nonzero digit, proving (i)$\Rightarrow$(ii),(iii).

Conversely, suppose every $2$ is isolated by zeros.  Put $x_i=y_i=1$ at every
position where $w_i=2$.  The remaining nonzero positions form maximal runs of
$1$'s, separated from each other and from the $2$'s by zeros or by the
boundary.  On each such run, assign the $1$'s alternately to $x$ and $y$.
Then neither summand has adjacent $1$'s, and $x+y=w$.  Thus
$w\in\AddTwo^{\mathrm{MSD}}(n)$.
\end{proof}

For $r\ge 0$, put
\[
P_r:=(10)^r11 .
\]
For a binary word $u$, define its \emph{cascade depth}
\[
D(u):=\max\bigl(\{r+1:P_r\text{ occurs as a factor of }\trimMSD(u)\}\cup\{0\}\bigr).
\]
Thus $D(u)=0$ exactly when $u$ has no factor $11$, i.e. when $u$ is already an
admissible Zeckendorf word.

\begin{lemma}[Binary cascade preimages]\label{lem:berstel-binary-preimage}
For every binary word $u$ and every $r\ge 0$, if $P_r$ occurs as a factor of
$\Berstel(u)$, then $P_{r+1}$ occurs as a factor of $u$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $\widetilde u:=\trimMSD(u)$.  Since
$\Berstel(u)=\Berstel(\widetilde u)$, we follow the transducer run on
$\widetilde u$; any factor found in $\widetilde u$ is also a factor of $u$.
Restrict the Berstel transducer to binary inputs.  From the initial state
$000$, only the five states
\[
a=000,\qquad b=001,\qquad c=010,\qquad d=100,\qquad e=101
\]
are reachable.  On these states the transition table is
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc|c}
q&0&1&\lambda(q)\\
\hline
a& a& b&0\\
b& c& d&0\\
c& d& e&0\\
d& a& b&1\\
e& c& d&1 ,
\end{array}
\]
where $\lambda(q)$ is the output emitted when leaving $q$.
If
\[
q_0,q_1,\ldots,q_L
\]
is the state path while reading $\widetilde u$, then the transition-output part of the
complete Berstel output is
\[
\lambda(q_0)\lambda(q_1)\cdots\lambda(q_{L-1}).
\]
The terminal outputs of the five binary states are
\[
000,\qquad 001,\qquad 010,\qquad 100,\qquad 101,
\]
none of which contains the factor $11$.  Hence any occurrence of
$P_r=(10)^r11$ in $\Berstel(u)$ also occurs in the complete output before
leading zeros are trimmed, since trimming only deletes an initial block of
zeros.  Its final $11$ cannot lie wholly inside the terminal output; it ends
no later than the first terminal letter.  The first terminal letter is again
$\lambda(q_L)$ for the five states above, so the occurrence corresponds to
consecutive state labels
\[
\lambda(q_i)\lambda(q_{i+1})\cdots \lambda(q_{i+2r+1})=(10)^r11
\]
for some state interval in the path.

In the five-state graph, the only adjacent state pair with output labels
$11$ is
\[
e\xrightarrow{1}d .
\]
Therefore the final two states in the displayed interval are $e,d$.  Moving
left, the predecessor of $e$ is uniquely
\[
c\xrightarrow{1}e,
\]
and the only predecessor of $c$ with output label $1$ is
\[
e\xrightarrow{0}c .
\]
Iterating this backward argument gives the unique state pattern
\[
e,c,e,c,\ldots,e,d
\]
with output labels
\[
1,0,1,0,\ldots,1,1.
\]
The input labels along this internal part are
\[
0,1,0,1,\ldots,1.
\]

It remains only to account for the letters which force the first $e$.  The
state $e=101$ is reached only through
\[
c\xrightarrow{1}e.
\]
The state $c=010$ is reached either through $b\xrightarrow{0}c$ or through
$e\xrightarrow{0}c$, and both $b$ and $e$ themselves are reached through an
input letter $1$.  Starting from the initial state $a=000$, the state $e$ is
not reachable in fewer than three input letters, and its unique length-three
input prefix is $101$.  Thus, in the full input run, the state $e$ at the left
end of the lifted occurrence is reached by a suffix
\[
101
\]
of the input prefix read so far.  Concatenating this suffix with the internal
input labels gives
\[
101(01)^r1=(10)^{r+1}11=P_{r+1},
\]
as a factor of $\widetilde u$, and therefore as a factor of $u$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Binary cascade-depth decrement]\label{lem:berstel-cascade-decrement}
For every binary word $u$,
\[
D(\Berstel(u))\le \max\{D(u)-1,0\}.
\]
Consequently $\tau(u)\le D(u)$ for every binary word $u$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $D(\Berstel(u))=0$, there is nothing to prove.  Otherwise put
$s=D(\Berstel(u))>0$.  Then $\Berstel(u)$ contains
\[
P_{s-1}=(10)^{s-1}11.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:berstel-binary-preimage}, the word $u$ contains
\[
P_s=(10)^s11.
\]
Thus $D(u)\ge s+1$, equivalently
\[
D(\Berstel(u))=s\le D(u)-1.
\]

Iterating the displayed inequality gives
$D(\Berstel^t(u))\le \max\{D(u)-t,0\}$.  When the cascade depth reaches $0$,
the binary word has no adjacent $1$'s and is therefore already the greedy
Zeckendorf representative of its value.  Hence $\tau(u)\le D(u)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Explicit binary cascade]\label{lem:berstel-binary-cascade}
For all $k,s\ge 0$,
\[
\Berstel\bigl((10)^k110^s\bigr)
=
\begin{cases}
1\,0^{s+2},&k=0,\\[1mm]
(10)^{k-1}11\,0^{s+2},&k\ge 1.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular,
\[
\tau\bigl((10)^k1100\bigr)=k+1.
\]
\[
\tau\bigl((10)^k110^s\bigr)=k+1\qquad(s\ge 0).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the five-state binary table from Lemma~\ref{lem:berstel-binary-preimage}.
For $k=0$, reading $11$ from the initial state gives
\[
000\xrightarrow{1/0}001\xrightarrow{1/0}100.
\]
From state $100$, the suffix $0^s$ and the terminal output contribute
$1\,0^{s+2}$ after leading zeros are trimmed.  Hence
\[
\Berstel(110^s)=1\,0^{s+2}.
\]

Now suppose $k\ge 1$.  Reading the first block $10$ gives
\[
000\xrightarrow{10/00}010.
\]
Moreover
\[
010\xrightarrow{10/01}010,
\]
so each further block $10$ emits $01$ and returns to $010$.  Finally,
\[
010\xrightarrow{11/01}100.
\]
Again the suffix $0^s$ and the terminal output from state $100$ contribute
$1\,0^{s+2}$.  The complete output is therefore
\[
00(01)^k1\,0^{s+2},
\]
which becomes $(10)^{k-1}11\,0^{s+2}$ after trimming leading zeros.

For arbitrary $s\ge 0$, each Berstel pass removes exactly one leading $10$
block from the cascade and adds two zeros on the right until the last pass
sends $110^s$ to $10^{s+2}$.  Therefore
$\tau((10)^k110^s)=k+1$, and the case $s=2$ is the displayed special case.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Worst-case binary cleanup depth]\label{thm:berstel-binary-worst-case}
For every $L\ge 1$,
\[
\max\{\tau(u):u\in\{0,1\}^L,\ u=\trimMSD(u)\}
=
\left\lfloor\frac L2\right\rfloor .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $u$ be a trimmed binary word of length $L$.  If $D(u)=d>0$, then $u$
contains $P_{d-1}=(10)^{d-1}11$ as a factor.  This factor has length $2d$, so
\[
d\le \left\lfloor\frac L2\right\rfloor.
\]
If $D(u)=0$, the same inequality is trivial.  Corollary~\ref{lem:berstel-cascade-decrement}
therefore gives
\[
\tau(u)\le D(u)\le \left\lfloor\frac L2\right\rfloor.
\]

For the lower bound, the case $L=1$ is attained by $u=1$, for which
$\tau(u)=0=\lfloor L/2\rfloor$.  Now write $L=2m$ or $L=2m+1$ with $m\ge 1$.
If $L=2m$, take
\[
u=(10)^{m-1}11.
\]
If $L=2m+1$, take
\[
u=(10)^{m-1}110.
\]
In both cases $u$ is trimmed and Lemma~\ref{lem:berstel-binary-cascade} gives
$\tau(u)=m=\lfloor L/2\rfloor$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[First-pass cascade bound on genuine additions]\label{lem:berstel-first-pass-cascade}
For every $n\ge 1$ and every $w\in\AddTwo^{\mathrm{MSD}}(n)$,
\[
D(\Berstel(w))\le
\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor
=
\left\lceil\frac n2\right\rceil-1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since both $\Berstel$ and $D$ are defined after deleting leading zeros, we may
replace $w$ by
\[
v:=\trimMSD(w).
\]
Let $m=|v|$.  Then $m\le n$, and $v$ still satisfies the condition in
Lemma~\ref{lem:addtwo-msd-characterization}.  It is enough to prove
\[
D(\Berstel(v))\le \left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

Let $\mathcal K(v)$ be the complete Berstel output before leading zeros are
trimmed.  We first show that $\mathcal K(v)$ begins with two zeros.  The first
transition from the initial state $000$ always emits $0$.  For the second
output letter, consider the first input digit.  If $v_1=0$ or $v_1=1$, then
after reading $v_1$ the state is $000$ or $001$, and every outgoing transition
from these states emits $0$.  If $v_1=2$, then
Lemma~\ref{lem:addtwo-msd-characterization} forces $v_2=0$ when $m\ge 2$, and
the transition
\[
002\xrightarrow{0/0}11\bar{1}
\]
also emits $0$.  If $m=1$, the second complete-output letter is the first
terminal letter of one of $000,001,002$, again equal to $0$.  Thus
$\mathcal K(v)$ begins with $00$.

The terminal outputs of the ten Berstel states are
\[
000,\quad 001,\quad 010,\quad 010,\quad 100,\quad 101,\quad
000,\quad 100,\quad 001,\quad 101.
\]
None contains the factor $11$.  Therefore, if $\Berstel(v)$ contains a factor
$P_r=(10)^r11$, this factor occurs in $\mathcal K(v)$ after the deleted initial
block of zeros, because $\Berstel(v)$ is obtained from $\mathcal K(v)$ only by
trimming leading zeros.  The final $11$ of this lifted occurrence cannot lie
wholly inside the terminal output.  It ends no later than the first
terminal-output letter.  Since the complete output has $m$ transition-output
letters followed by three terminal letters, the last symbol of this occurrence
has position at most $m+1$, using one-based indexing.

Suppose $D(\Berstel(v))=d>0$.  Then $\Berstel(v)$, and hence $\mathcal K(v)$,
contains
\[
P_{d-1}=(10)^{d-1}11,
\]
whose length is $2d$.  Since $\mathcal K(v)$ begins with $00$, this occurrence
starts at position at least $3$.  Since its final symbol is at position at
most $m+1$,
\[
3+2d-1\le m+1.
\]
Thus $2d\le m-1$, and hence
\[
d\le \left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor
\le \left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]
The case $D(\Berstel(w))=0$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Upper bound from cascade depth]\label{lem:berstel-depth-upper}
For every $n\ge 1$ and every $w\in\AddTwo^{\mathrm{MSD}}(n)$,
\[
\tau(w)\le \left\lceil\frac n2\right\rceil.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After one Berstel pass, $\Berstel(w)$ is binary and value-equivalent to $w$.
Therefore
\[
\tau(w)\le 1+\tau(\Berstel(w)).
\]
By Corollary~\ref{lem:berstel-cascade-decrement},
\[
\tau(\Berstel(w))\le D(\Berstel(w)).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:berstel-first-pass-cascade},
\[
D(\Berstel(w))\le \left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]
Hence
\[
\tau(w)
\le
1+\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor
=
\left\lceil\frac n2\right\rceil .
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Extremal genuine-addition witnesses]\label{lem:berstel-depth-witnesses}
For every $k\ge 0$, define
\[
W_k=
\begin{cases}
(200)^{2a}202,&k=3a,\\
(200)^{2a}20102,&k=3a+1,\\
(200)^{2a+1}2011,&k=3a+2.
\end{cases}
\]
Then $W_k\in\AddTwo^{\mathrm{MSD}}(2k+3)$ and
\[
\Berstel(W_k)=(10)^k1100.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:addtwo-msd-characterization}, membership in
$\AddTwo^{\mathrm{MSD}}$ is equivalent to every $2$ being isolated from
nonzero neighbors.  This is immediate from the three displayed forms of
$W_k$, so $W_k\in\AddTwo^{\mathrm{MSD}}(2k+3)$.

We write
\[
q\xrightarrow{x/y}q'
\]
to mean that, starting in state $q$, reading the block $x$ emits the
transition-output block $y$ and ends in state $q'$, before terminal output is
appended.  From the Berstel table,
\[
000\xrightarrow{200/001}001,\qquad
001\xrightarrow{200/010}100,\qquad
100\xrightarrow{200/101}001.
\]
Hence, by induction,
\[
000\xrightarrow{(200)^{2a}/\,00(10)^{3a-1}}100
\qquad (a\ge 1),
\]
and
\[
000\xrightarrow{(200)^{2a+1}/\,00(10)^{3a}1}001
\qquad (a\ge 0).
\]

The suffix computations are
\[
000\xrightarrow{202/001}1\bar{1}2,\qquad
100\xrightarrow{202/101}1\bar{1}2,
\]
\[
000\xrightarrow{20102/00101}1\bar{1}2,\qquad
100\xrightarrow{20102/10101}1\bar{1}2,
\]
and
\[
001\xrightarrow{2011/0101}100.
\]
The terminal outputs needed below are
\[
\omega(1\bar{1}2)=100,\qquad \omega(100)=100.
\]

If $k=3a$, then $W_k=(200)^{2a}202$.  For $a=0$,
\[
\mathcal K(202)=001100,
\]
so $\Berstel(202)=1100=(10)^0 1100$.  For $a\ge 1$, the block identities give
\[
\mathcal K(W_k)=00(10)^{3a-1}101100,
\]
and after trimming leading zeros,
\[
\Berstel(W_k)=(10)^{3a}1100.
\]

If $k=3a+1$, then $W_k=(200)^{2a}20102$.  For $a=0$,
\[
\mathcal K(20102)=00101100,
\]
so $\Berstel(20102)=101100=(10)^1 1100$.  For $a\ge 1$, the block identities
give
\[
\mathcal K(W_k)=00(10)^{3a-1}10101100,
\]
and after trimming,
\[
\Berstel(W_k)=(10)^{3a+1}1100.
\]

If $k=3a+2$, then $W_k=(200)^{2a+1}2011$.  The prefix identity gives
\[
000\xrightarrow{(200)^{2a+1}/\,00(10)^{3a}1}001,
\]
and the suffix identity gives
\[
001\xrightarrow{2011/0101}100,\qquad \omega(100)=100.
\]
Thus
\[
\mathcal K(W_k)=00(10)^{3a}10101100,
\]
and after trimming,
\[
\Berstel(W_k)=(10)^{3a+2}1100.
\]
This proves $\Berstel(W_k)=(10)^k1100$ in all cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp Berstel iteration depth on genuine additions]\label{thm:berstel-depth}
For every $n\ge 1$,
\[
\max\{\tau(w):w\in\AddTwo^{\mathrm{MSD}}(n)\}
=
\left\lceil\frac n2\right\rceil .
\]
The maximum is already attained by words $w$ with $w=\trimMSD(w)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound is Lemma~\ref{lem:berstel-depth-upper}.

For the lower bound, first consider odd lengths $n=2k+3$ with $k\ge 0$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:berstel-depth-witnesses},
\[
W_k\in\AddTwo^{\mathrm{MSD}}(2k+3)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\Berstel(W_k)=(10)^k1100.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:berstel-binary-cascade},
\[
\tau\bigl((10)^k1100\bigr)=k+1.
\]
Since $W_k$ contains the digit $2$, it is not already a greedy binary
Zeckendorf word.  Hence
\[
\tau(W_k)=1+\tau(\Berstel(W_k))=k+2
=
\left\lceil\frac{2k+3}{2}\right\rceil.
\]

For even lengths $n=2m$ with $m\ge 1$, use the trimmed binary cascade
\[
u_m=(10)^{m-1}11.
\]
This word is binary, hence it lies in $\AddTwo^{\mathrm{MSD}}(2m)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:addtwo-msd-characterization}.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:berstel-binary-cascade},
\[
\tau(u_m)=m
=
\left\lceil\frac{2m}{2}\right\rceil.
\]

The remaining case is $n=1$, where $w=2$ gives $\Berstel(2)=10$, so
$\tau(2)=1=\lceil 1/2\rceil$.
Combining the upper and lower bounds proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{A Local Parallel Witness}\label{sec:complexity}

The failure of bounded one-pass delay does not, by itself, rule out efficient
synchronous local normalization.  The following argument records the specific
Fibonacci witness used here for the standard local-propagation obstruction:
for the nonredundant canonical map, information from the low end of the input
can be forced to affect an arbitrarily high output coordinate.  This is a
finite-scale light-cone formulation of the known nonlocality phenomenon; see
\cite[Prop.~14]{Frougny1999OnlineAddition}.

\begin{theorem}[Local parallel lower bound for Zeckendorf normalization]\label{thm:zeckendorf-parallel-propagation-lower-bound}
Let $\A=\{0,1,2\}$, with distinguished zero, and view finitely supported
one-sided configurations $x\in\A^{(\NN)}$ as elements of $\D$, with the value
map from Definition~\ref{def:val-on-D}.
Let $Z(\Val(x))\in\{0,1\}^{(\NN)}$ denote the unique admissible Zeckendorf
representative with the same value.

Suppose $\Phi:\A^{(\NN)}\to\{0,1\}^{(\NN)}$ is a synchronous local map of
radius $r\ge1$, meaning that after extending every input by zeros outside $\NN$
there exists a local rule $f:\A^{2r+1}\to\{0,1\}$ such that
\[
(\Phi(x))_j=f(x_{j-r},\dots,x_{j+r})
\qquad (j\ge 1),
\]
and let $\Phi^t$ denote the $t$-fold iterate
$\Phi\circ\cdots\circ\Phi$, with $t$ copies of $\Phi$.  Fix $m\ge2$.  If for
some $t\ge 1$ one has
\[
\Phi^t(x)=Z(\Val(x))
\]
for every input $x$ with $\supp(x)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$, then
\[
t\ge \left\lceil \frac{m-1}{r}\right\rceil.
\]
In particular, no time-homogeneous fixed-radius synchronous normalization
obtained by iterating one local rule can run in a number of rounds independent
of the input scale.
\leanverified{paper_zeckendorf_parallel_propagation_lower_bound}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After $t$ iterations, the output coordinate at position $j$ depends only on
the input in the interval $[j-rt,j+rt]\cap\NN$.
Define
\[
x^{(m)}_k=
\begin{cases}
1,&1\le k\le m-1\text{ and }k\equiv m-1\pmod 2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
y^{(m)}:=x^{(m)}+e_1.
\]
The configuration $x^{(m)}$ is already admissible, hence
$Z(\Val(x^{(m)}))=x^{(m)}$ and its $m$th coordinate is $0$.

On the other hand,
\[
\Val(x^{(m)})
=
\sum_{\substack{1\le k\le m-1\\k\equiv m-1\pmod 2}}F_{k+1}
=
F_{m+1}-1,
\]
so
\[
\Val(y^{(m)})=\Val(x^{(m)})+F_2=F_{m+1}.
\]
Therefore $Z(\Val(y^{(m)}))$ is the admissible word with a single $1$ at
position $m$, and hence
\[
Z(\Val(x^{(m)}))_m=0,
\qquad
Z(\Val(y^{(m)}))_m=1.
\]

If $rt<m-1$, then the coordinate $(\Phi^t(\cdot))_m$ cannot depend on the input
at position $1$, so it must take the same value on $x^{(m)}$ and $y^{(m)}$.
This contradicts the required normalization outputs above.  Hence
$rt\ge m-1$, which is equivalent to the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:zeckendorf-parallel-propagation-lower-bound} concerns the
nonredundant input alphabet $\{0,1,2\}$ and the canonical Zeckendorf output
map $x\mapsto Z(\Val(x))$.  It does not contradict constant-time parallel
addition results for redundant digit sets, where carries can be absorbed by
extra local freedom.  We also do not claim a matching upper bound here:
the theorem only shows that every time-homogeneous fixed-radius synchronous
realization obtained by iterating one local rule requires a number of rounds
growing linearly with the input scale.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}

We have separated four layers of the Zeckendorf addition problem.  First, the
canonical normalization map $\Fold_\infty$ is induced by the section of the
Fibonacci congruence selected by admissible representatives, and it admits a direct
rewrite-theoretic description.  Second, in the LSDF one-sided model, this
canonical map is maximally noncausal in several exact senses: every output
coordinate has infinite anticipation horizon, the map has no continuous
extension at the alternating one-sided limit tails, the commitment deficit and
prefix-destruction index satisfy $B(n)=\Delta(n)=n$, the same maximal prefix
erasure holds on LSDF genuine addition inputs, and the input-scale reduced
residual complexity has the lower bound $R_{\mathrm{in}}(n)\ge n$.
Non-subsequentiality is a consequence of these sharper invariants.  Third,
the classical $10$-state Berstel adder
belongs to a different, MSDF subsequential setting.  Its ten-state
presentation is classical; the ten displayed complete-output residuals are
distinct as a raw kernel, while output-delay normalization gives a six-state
subsequential quotient.  Fourth, iterating the Berstel recoding to reach the
greedy Zeckendorf normal form has sharp worst-case depth
$\lceil n/2\rceil$ on length-$n$ genuine addition inputs.  Natural next
questions concern analogous explicit witness refinements for other confluent
or Pisot numeration systems and for redundant or signed digit alphabets.
