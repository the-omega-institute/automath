Theorem A — Complete canonical presentation and strategy-independent normalization
Let

$$D=\mathbb N^{(\mathbb N_{\ge 1})},\qquad
\operatorname{Val}(a)=\sum_{k\ge 1}a_kF_{k+1}.$$

Let  $\equiv$  be the congruence generated by

$$e_2=2e_1,\qquad e_{k+2}=e_{k+1}+e_k.$$

Let

$$X_\infty=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{(\mathbb N_{\ge1})}: x_kx_{k+1}=0\text{ for all }k\}.$$

Statement.
The quotient  $D/\equiv$  is canonically isomorphic to  $(\mathbb N,+)$  via  $\operatorname{Val}$ . Each congruence class contains a unique element of  $X_\infty$ , namely the Zeckendorf representative  $Z(\operatorname{Val}(a))$ . Moreover, the rewrite system

$$\begin{aligned}
R_1&: e_k+e_{k+1}\to e_{k+2},\\
R_2&: 2e_1\to e_2,\\
R_3&: 2e_2\to e_1+e_3,\\
R_4&: 2e_k\to e_{k-2}+e_{k+1}\quad(k\ge3)
\end{aligned}$$

is terminating, value-preserving, has irreducibles exactly  $X_\infty$ , and every maximal rewrite sequence from  $a$  terminates at

$$\operatorname{Fold}_\infty(a)=Z(\operatorname{Val}(a)).$$

Why this is stronger than the current framing.
This is not just “a normalizing algorithm.” It is a complete local presentation of the Zeckendorf quotient monoid, with the canonical section  $Z:\mathbb N\to D$  recovered as a strategy-independent normal form.
Status. Already essentially proved from ingredients 1–2. Confluence follows from termination plus uniqueness of terminal irreducibles.

Theorem B — Exact infinite LSDF anticipation profile
For a class  $\mathcal C\subseteq \{0,1,2\}^{(\mathbb N)}$ , define the coordinate anticipation modulus

$$A_{\mathcal C}(j)=\inf\{h\ge j:\ a_{\le h}=b_{\le h}\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Fold}_\infty(a)_j=\operatorname{Fold}_\infty(b)_j
\text{ for all }a,b\in\mathcal C\},$$

with  $A_{\mathcal C}(j)=\infty$  if no such  $h$  exists.
Let

$$\operatorname{Add}_2=X_\infty+X_\infty$$

be the set of genuine digitwise sums of two admissible Zeckendorf inputs.
Statement.
For every  $j\ge1$ ,

$$A_{\{0,1,2\}^{(\mathbb N)}}(j)=\infty$$

and, more strongly,

$$A_{\operatorname{Add}_2}(j)=\infty.$$

Equivalently, for every  $j\ge1$  and every delay  $\delta\ge0$ , there exist  $a,b\in\operatorname{Add}_2$  such that

$$a_i=b_i\quad(1\le i\le j+\delta)$$

but

$$\operatorname{Fold}_\infty(a)_j\ne \operatorname{Fold}_\infty(b)_j.$$

A particularly strong witness form is available. Choose  $m$  with  $j+2m>j+\delta$ , put

$$p=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}e_{j+2t},\qquad q=e_{j+2m}.$$

Then

$$a=p+q=\sum_{t=0}^{m}e_{j+2t}$$

is itself admissible, while

$$b=p+2q=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}e_{j+2t}+2e_{j+2m}$$

lies in  $\operatorname{Add}_2$  because

$$b=(p+q)+q,$$

and both  $p+q$  and  $q$  are admissible. The two inputs agree through  $j+\delta$ , but

$$\operatorname{Fold}_\infty(a)_j=1,\qquad
\operatorname{Fold}_\infty(b)_j=0.$$

Why this is strong.
This says every individual output digit has infinite right anticipation in LSDF order. There is no finite input horizon, however remote, that determines even a fixed low-order output coordinate. This is much sharper than non-subsequentiality.
Status. Already proved for arbitrary ternary inputs from ingredient 3. The strengthened  $\operatorname{Add}_2$  version follows directly from ingredient 4 plus the slightly modified pair  $a=p+q$ ,  $b=p+2q$ .

Theorem C — No continuous extension to the one-sided completion
Let the finite-support ternary sequences be embedded in the full one-sided product space  $\{0,1,2\}^{\mathbb N}$ . For each  $j$ , define the infinite alternating tail

$$z_j=\sum_{t\ge0}e_{j+2t}.$$

Statement.
The canonical normalizer  $\operatorname{Fold}_\infty$ , viewed as a map from finite-support ternary inputs to binary Zeckendorf outputs, has no coordinatewise continuous extension to any domain containing  $z_j$ . More precisely, there exist two sequences  $a_m,b_m\to z_j$  such that

$$\operatorname{Fold}_\infty(a_m)_j=1,\qquad
\operatorname{Fold}_\infty(b_m)_j=0$$

for all  $m$ .
Take

$$a_m=\sum_{t=0}^{m}e_{j+2t},
\qquad
b_m=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}e_{j+2t}+2e_{j+2m}.$$

Both tend to  $z_j$ , but their normalized  $j$ -th digits remain separated.
Why this matters.
This reframes the obstruction topologically: the LSDF canonical normalizer is not merely nonsequential; it is not continuously extendable at the natural alternating boundary points of the finite-support domain.
Status. Already follows from the coordinate witnesses, but it needs to be explicitly formulated.

Theorem D — Maximal online prefix erasure / exact commitment deficit
For a finite input word  $u$ , define

$$N(u)=\operatorname{trim}(\operatorname{Fold}_\infty(\operatorname{enc}_{\mathrm{LSDF}}(u))).$$

Let

$$C(u)=\operatorname{lcp}\{N(uw):w\in\{0,1,2\}^*\}$$

be the longest normalized output prefix that is forced after reading  $u$ , before seeing the future. Define the online commitment deficit

$$B(n)=\max\{|N(u)|-|C(u)|:\ |N(u)|=n\}.$$

Statement.

$$B(n)=n\qquad\text{for every }n\ge1.$$

Equivalently, for every output length  $n$ , there is an input prefix  $u$  with  $|N(u)|=n$  for which no nonempty prefix of  $N(u)$  is safe to emit.
The existing witnesses prove the sharper finite-shock form:

$$\Delta(n)=
\max\{n-\operatorname{lcp}(N(u),N(v)):
u\text{ proper prefix of }v,\ |N(u)|=n\}=n.$$

Odd case:

$$u_m=(10)^{m-1}1,\qquad v_m=u_m02,$$

with

$$N(u_m)=u_m,\qquad N(v_m)=0(10)^m1.$$

Even case:

$$a_m=(10)^{m-1}2,\qquad b_m=a_m1,$$

with

$$N(a_m)=(01)^m,\qquad N(b_m)=1(01)^m.$$

Corollary.
Every irrevocable LSDF online algorithm must sometimes keep its entire current normalized output uncommitted. A deterministic subsequential transducer has only bounded terminal discrepancy, so this exact commitment deficit immediately implies non-subsequentiality.
Why this is better than “not subsequential.”
It is an exact quantitative invariant:

$$\text{maximal possible uncommitted output of length }n = n.$$

The finite-state impossibility becomes a corollary, not the main theorem.
Status. Already proved from ingredients 6–7.

Theorem E — Genuine-addition prefix erasure, strengthened beyond the manuscript
The current addition-image obstruction is already strong for coordinate anticipation. It can likely be strengthened for finite prefixes as follows.
Let  $\Delta_{\operatorname{Add}_2}(n)$  be defined as  $\Delta(n)$ , but with both  $u$  and  $v$  required to lie in  $\operatorname{Add}_2$ .
Proposed statement.

$$\Delta_{\operatorname{Add}_2}(n)=n\qquad\text{for every }n\ge1.$$

Moreover, for every  $n\ne2$ , one can choose  $v=u1$ , a one-symbol extension.
For  $n\ge3$ , take

$$u=1^{\,n-1},\qquad v=1^{\,n}.$$

Both are genuine addition inputs because

$$1^r=(1010\cdots)+(0101\cdots),$$

with both summands admissible. The needed normal forms are

$$N(1^{2s})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}e_{2i+1},$$

and

$$N(1^{2s+1})=e_1+\sum_{i=2}^{s+1}e_{2i}.$$

Thus the first normalized digit alternates between  $0$  and  $1$  when one appends a single  $1$ . Also  $|N(1^r)|=r+1$  for  $r\ge2$ , so  $|N(u)|=n$  when  $u=1^{n-1}$ .
Special cases:

$$n=1:\quad u=1,\quad v=11,$$

and

$$n=2:\quad u=01,\quad v=0102,$$

with

$$N(01)=01,\qquad N(0102)=10101.$$

Proof obligation.
Add the short Fibonacci-sum lemma proving the displayed formulas for  $N(1^r)$ . This is modest: use

$$\sum_{k=1}^{r}F_{k+1}=F_{r+3}-2$$

and verify that the displayed right-hand sides are admissible Zeckendorf representatives of that value.
Status. Likely provable with modest additional work. This would be a major strengthening of the genuine-addition part.

Theorem F — Reduced residual complexity lower bound
Define the reduced residual at prefix  $u$  by

$$\rho_u(w)=C(u)^{-1}N(uw),$$

where  $C(u)$  is the forced committed prefix. Let

$$R(n)=\#\{\rho_u:\ |N(u)|\le n\}.$$

Statement.

$$R(n)\ge n.$$

The same lower bound should hold on the genuine addition domain once Theorem E is added.
Indeed, for each  $k\le n$ , choose a prefix  $u_k$  with  $|N(u_k)|=k$  and  $C(u_k)=\varepsilon$ . Then

$$\rho_{u_k}(\varepsilon)=N(u_k)$$

has length  $k$ , so the residuals are pairwise distinct.
Why this matters.
This is the Myhill–Nerode version of the online obstruction. It shows not only that no finite residual representation exists, but that reduced residual complexity grows at least linearly with output length.
Status. Follows from Theorem D. The genuine-addition version follows from Theorem E.

Theorem G — Berstel residual minimality and canonical/noncanonical separation
Let  $Q$  be Berstel’s ten-state set

$$000,001,002,010,100,101,0\bar12,1\bar12,01\bar1,11\bar1.$$

For each state  $q$ , let  $F_q(w)$  be the complete output produced from state  $q$  on suffix  $w$ , including terminal output.
Already supported statement.
All ten states are reachable, and the ten complete-output residual functions  $F_q$  are pairwise distinct. In fact, every pair is separated by a suffix of length at most  $1$ : pairs with different terminal output are separated by  $\varepsilon$ , and the five equal-terminal pairs are separated by  $0$ :

$$\begin{aligned}
F_{000}(0)&=0000\ne0001=F_{0\bar12}(0),\\
F_{001}(0)&=0010\ne0001=F_{01\bar1}(0),\\
F_{002}(0)&=0101\ne0100=F_{010}(0),\\
F_{100}(0)&=1000\ne1001=F_{1\bar12}(0),\\
F_{101}(0)&=1010\ne1001=F_{11\bar1}(0).
\end{aligned}$$

Therefore the displayed complete-output residual representation is minimal in the strict residual sense.
Stronger proposed statement.
After output pushing, the ten states remain inequivalent in the subsequential-minimization sense. Equivalently, the prefix-normalized residuals

$$\widetilde F_q(w)=P_q^{-1}F_q(w),\qquad
P_q=\operatorname{lcp}\{F_q(w):w\in\Sigma^*\},$$

are pairwise inequivalent.
Proof obligation.
Compute  $P_q$  for all ten states and repeat the residual separation after pushing. The current  $\varepsilon/0$  certificate proves strict residual inequivalence, but not automatically output-pushing minimality.
Canonical/noncanonical separation.
Once the genuine-addition non-subsequential theorem is stated, one can also prove:

There is no deterministic subsequential postprocessor  $P$  such that

$$P\circ \gamma=\operatorname{Fold}_\infty$$

on genuine addition inputs, where  $\gamma$  is Berstel’s noncanonical output map.

The proof is by closure of subsequential functions under composition: Berstel’s  $\gamma$  is subsequential; if such  $P$  existed, the canonical normalizer on genuine addition inputs would be subsequential, contradicting Theorem E plus the bounded-terminal-discrepancy lemma.
Status.
Strict residual minimality is already proved. Output-pushing minimality and the postprocessing separation are likely provable with modest additional formal work.
