You are refereeing the attached 55-page paper. I am not asking whether it is correct. I have already
established the defect and I want you to tell me what to do about it.

THE DEFECT. The paper advertises a cross-resolution compatibility theorem. That theorem does not
establish the compatibility it advertises: the hypotheses are satisfiable only in a degenerate range,
so the statement is vacuous rather than false. An earlier assessment of mine called it "false"; that
was wrong and I corrected it. Vacuous is the right word, and vacuous is worse for a reader than
false, because nothing in the proof looks broken.

Three questions, in order.

(1) CONFIRM OR REFUTE THE VACUITY, and say precisely where it enters. Which hypothesis is the one
that cannot be met nondegenerately, and what is the largest class of instances that does satisfy it?
If you find the theorem is in fact non-vacuous and I am wrong, say so and give one instance -- an
explicit pair of resolutions and a stable type -- that satisfies every hypothesis and has nontrivial
conclusion. One concrete instance settles it.

(2) WHAT IS ACTUALLY PROVED HERE? Set the headline aside and read the rest as if it were an
unlabelled collection of results. Is there a theorem in these 55 pages that a specialist would
consider worth publishing on its own? Name it by number. If your answer is that the surviving
material is definitions, worked examples and consistency checks, say that plainly -- I would rather
withdraw this manuscript than submit a vacuous headline with a halo of calculations.

(3) IF SOMETHING SURVIVES: what is the honest paper, at what length, and where? Name the venue and a
rough numeric probability, and say why you are not naming the next one up. If nothing survives, say
so and skip this question rather than inventing a target.

Do not summarize the paper back to me.
