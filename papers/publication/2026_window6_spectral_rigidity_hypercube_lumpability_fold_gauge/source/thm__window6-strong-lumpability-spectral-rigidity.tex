\begin{theorem}[Spectral rigidity of strong lumpability: eigenvalues of an equitable quotient must come from the discrete hypercube spectrum]\label{thm:window6-strong-lumpability-spectral-rigidity}
Let \(Q_m\) be the \(m\)-dimensional hypercube, with adjacency matrix \(A\) and simple random-walk operator \(T=\frac{1}{m}A\).
Given any surjection \(F:\{0,1\}^m\to X\), let \(M\) be the block-indicator matrix determined by the fibers of \(F\), whose columns are the indicator vectors of the fibers.
Assume that the partition is \emph{equitable}, namely that there exists a matrix \(B\) such that
\[
AM=MB.
\]
Then the spectrum of \(B\) is contained in the spectrum of \(A\).
Equivalently, the Walsh characters \(\chi_S(\omega)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}\omega_i}\) diagonalize \(A\), with eigenvalue \(m-2|S|\), so every eigenvalue of the induced quotient transition matrix \(P:=\frac{1}{m}B\) belongs to the set
\[
\Bigl\{1-\tfrac{2k}{m}: k=0,1,\dots,m\Bigr\}.
\]
Therefore, if a candidate transition kernel \(P\) obtained from a pushforward construction has an eigenvalue outside this discrete set, then the corresponding coarse-graining cannot arise from a first-order equitable quotient of \(Q_m\) without introducing additional hidden states.

\leanverified{spec\_inclusion\_of\_intertwine}
\leanverified{rescale\_eigenvalue}
\leanverified{paper\_window6\_strong\_lumpability\_spectral\_rigidity}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(Bv=\lambda v\).
Because the columns of \(M\) are the indicators of the pairwise disjoint nonempty fibers of \(F\), they are linearly independent; equivalently, \(M\) has trivial kernel.
Therefore every eigenvector \(v\neq 0\) of \(B\) satisfies \(Mv\neq 0\).
Now
\(
A(Mv)=M(Bv)=\lambda(Mv)
\),
so \(Mv\) is an eigenvector of \(A\) with eigenvalue \(\lambda\).
Hence \(\mathrm{spec}(B)\subseteq \mathrm{spec}(A)\).
For completeness we recall the Walsh calculation.  For \(S\subseteq[m]\), set \(\chi_S(\omega)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}\omega_i}\).  Flipping a coordinate in \(S\) changes the sign of \(\chi_S\), and flipping a coordinate outside \(S\) preserves it; hence
\[
A\chi_S=(m-|S|)\chi_S-|S|\chi_S=(m-2|S|)\chi_S.
\]
The \(2^m\) Walsh characters form an orthogonal basis of \(\CC^{\Omega_m}\), so the adjacency spectrum of \(Q_m\) is \(\{m-2k:0\le k\le m\}\), with multiplicities \(\binom mk\), as in the standard hypercube spectrum references \cite{biggs1993algebraic,godsil2001algebraic,macwilliams1977theory}.  Therefore
\[
\mathrm{spec}(P)=\frac{1}{m}\mathrm{spec}(B)\subseteq \Bigl\{1-\tfrac{2k}{m}\Bigr\}_{k=0}^m.
\]
The final statement is simply the contrapositive of this inclusion. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local residual certificate for hypercube fold nonintertwining]\label{thm:local-residual-certificate-hypercube-folds}
Let \(F:\Omega_m\twoheadrightarrow X\) be any surjection, let \(M\) be its block-indicator matrix, and let \(P_F=D_F^{-1}M^\top T_mM\) be the audited pushforward kernel.  For \(\omega\in\Omega_m\) and \(y\in X\), put
\[
c_\omega(y):=\#\{1\le i\le m:F(\omega+e_i)=y\}.
\]
Fix a fiber \(x\in X\), and suppose that there are \(\omega,\omega'\in F^{-1}(x)\) and \(y\in X\) such that
\[
c_\omega(y)\ne c_{\omega'}(y).
\]
Then no matrix \(P\) can satisfy \(T_mM=MP\), and hence the partition is not equitable.  More precisely, the audited residual
\[
R_F:=T_mM-MP_F
\]
has
\[
R_F(\omega,y)=\frac{1}{m}\left(c_\omega(y)-\frac{1}{d_F(x)}\sum_{\eta\in F^{-1}(x)}c_\eta(y)\right),
\qquad d_F(x)=|F^{-1}(x)|.
\]
In particular, if \(\delta:=|c_\omega(y)-c_{\omega'}(y)|\), then at least one of \(|R_F(\omega,y)|\) and \(|R_F(\omega',y)|\) is at least \(\delta/(2m)\).  Thus a single unequal target-fiber neighbor count is a finite algebraic-combinatorial certificate for nonintertwining.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The \((\omega,y)\)-entry of \(T_mM\) is
\[
(T_mM)(\omega,y)=\sum_{\eta\in F^{-1}(y)}T_m(\omega,\eta)=\frac{c_\omega(y)}{m}.
\]
If \(T_mM=MP\) for some matrix \(P\), then this entry would equal \(P(F(\omega),y)\).  For \(\omega,\omega'\in F^{-1}(x)\), the two entries would therefore both equal \(P(x,y)\), forcing \(c_\omega(y)=c_{\omega'}(y)\).  The displayed inequality contradicts this, so no intertwining exists.  By Lemma \ref{lem:equitable-strong-lumpability-audited-pushforward}, this is equivalent to failure of equitability.

It remains to compute the audited residual.  The \((x,y)\)-entry of \(P_F=D_F^{-1}M^\top T_mM\) is the average, over the source fiber \(F^{-1}(x)\), of the corresponding row entries of \(T_mM\):
\[
P_F(x,y)=\frac{1}{d_F(x)}\sum_{\eta\in F^{-1}(x)}\frac{c_\eta(y)}{m}
=\frac{1}{m d_F(x)}\sum_{\eta\in F^{-1}(x)}c_\eta(y).
\]
Since \((MP_F)(\omega,y)=P_F(F(\omega),y)=P_F(x,y)\), subtraction gives the stated formula for \(R_F(\omega,y)\).

Let
\[
\bar c_y:=d_F(x)^{-1}\sum_{\eta\in F^{-1}(x)}c_\eta(y).
\]
The two real numbers \(c_\omega(y)\) and \(c_{\omega'}(y)\) are separated by \(\delta\).  No point \(\bar c_y\) can lie within distance \(<\delta/2\) of both of them.  Therefore at least one of \(|c_\omega(y)-\bar c_y|\) and \(|c_{\omega'}(y)-\bar c_y|\) is at least \(\delta/2\), and division by \(m\) gives the residual bound. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-certificate dichotomy for hypercube fold quotients]\label{thm:finite-certificate-dichotomy-hypercube-folds}
Let \(F:\Omega_m\twoheadrightarrow X\) be any finite surjection, and let \(M\) be its block-indicator matrix.  For each \(x,y\in X\), form the finite list
\[
\mathcal C_{x,y}(F):=\bigl(c_\omega(y)\bigr)_{\omega\in F^{-1}(x)},
\qquad
c_\omega(y)=\#\{1\le i\le m:F(\omega+e_i)=y\}.
\]
Then exactly one of the following alternatives holds.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Every list \(\mathcal C_{x,y}(F)\) is constant.  In this case \(F\) is an equitable quotient of \(Q_m\), the quotient kernel is the audited pushforward
\[
P_F=D_F^{-1}M^\top T_mM,
\]
and \(\Spec(P_F)\subseteq\{1-2k/m:0\le k\le m\}\).
\item Some list \(\mathcal C_{x,y}(F)
\) is not constant.  In this case any two entries of that list with different values form a finite residual certificate: no kernel \(P\) satisfies \(T_mM=MP\), and if their difference has absolute value \(\delta\), then the audited residual \(R_F=T_mM-MP_F\) has one of the two corresponding entries of absolute value at least \(\delta/(2m)\).
\end{enumerate}
Thus hidden-state-free strong lumpability for a finite hypercube fold is decided by the family of integer target-fiber neighbor-count lists, and every failure has a two-vertex certificate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed pair \((x,y)\), the list \(\mathcal C_{x,y}(F)\) is constant precisely when all vertices in the source fiber \(F^{-1}(x)\) have the same number of neighbors in the target fiber \(F^{-1}(y)\).  Therefore all lists are constant precisely when the partition is equitable.  In that case Lemma \ref{lem:equitable-strong-lumpability-audited-pushforward} identifies the quotient kernel with the audited pushforward \(P_F=D_F^{-1}M^\top T_mM\), and Theorem \ref{thm:window6-strong-lumpability-spectral-rigidity} gives the stated spectral inclusion.

If some list is not constant, choose two entries \(c_\omega(y)\ne c_{\omega'}(y)\) in it.  Then \(\omega\) and \(\omega'\) lie in the same fiber over \(x\), and Theorem \ref{thm:local-residual-certificate-hypercube-folds} applies directly.  It rules out every intertwining \(T_mM=MP\) and gives the displayed residual lower bound with \(\delta=|c_\omega(y)-c_{\omega'}(y)|\).  The two alternatives are mutually exclusive and exhaustive because each finite list is either constant or not constant. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Novelty firewall for the Fold\texorpdfstring{$_6$}{6} nonlumpability theorem]\label{thm:fold6-novelty-firewall}
The window-\(6\) nonlumpability conclusion of Corollary \ref{cor:terminal-window6-nonlumpable-by-spectrum} is not asserted as a new theorem about equitable partitions in general.  Its proof has the following exact division of labor.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item The classical part is the finite-state lumpability criterion, the block-intertwining implication \(T_mM=MP\), and the Walsh spectral formula
\[
\Spec(T_m)=\{1-2k/m:0\le k\le m\}.
\]
\item The paper-specific part is the Fold\(_6\) certificate: the audited surjection \(\Fold_6^{\mathrm{bin}}\), the visible state set \(X_6\), the fiber multiplicities, the block-indicator table, the exact pushforward kernel \(P_6=D^{-1}M^\top T_6M\), the two-vertex nonintertwining witness, the cleared characteristic-polynomial factor, the rational Sturm isolating intervals, and the stabilizer identity used by the chiral and boundary consequences.
\item Therefore the new conclusion is the integrated certificate statement that this particular visible Fold\(_6\) pushforward fails hidden-state-free strong lumpability and nevertheless carries the audited chiral \(Q_4\) sector and boundary-gauge data used later in the paper.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part \textup{(i)} is exactly Theorem \ref{thm:window6-strong-lumpability-spectral-rigidity} together with the standard equitable-partition criterion recorded in Lemma \ref{lem:equitable-strong-lumpability-audited-pushforward}.  The proof of that theorem uses only the invariant subspace \(\im M\) and the Walsh diagonalization of the hypercube adjacency operator.

Part \textup{(ii)} is independent finite data.  Definition \ref{def:audited-binary-fold-certificate} defines the audited fold.  Proposition \ref{prop:foldbin6-canonical-fiber-table} gives the block-indicator encoding, Proposition \ref{prop:foldbin6-exact-pushforward-matrix} gives the directed edge counts and the exact matrix \(P_6\), Proposition \ref{prop:foldbin6-explicit-nonintertwining-witness} gives the two-vertex residual witness, Lemmas \ref{lem:foldbin6-characteristic-certificate} and \ref{lem:foldbin6-eigenvalue-interval-certificate} give the characteristic-polynomial and Sturm interval certificates, and Lemma \ref{lem:window6-geometric-stabilizer-audit} records the stabilizer identity used by the chiral compression.

Combining \textup{(i)} and \textup{(ii)}, the two certified eigenvalues of \(P_6\) lie outside \(\{1,2/3,1/3,0,-1/3,-2/3,-1\}\), and the residual witness independently rules out an intertwining on the same visible partition.  Corollary \ref{cor:terminal-window6-nonlumpable-by-spectrum} and Theorem \ref{thm:hidden-refinement-boundary} state exactly this visible hidden-state-free conclusion.  The chiral and boundary consequences use the same audited stabilizer and fiber data rather than an additional general lumpability theorem, proving \textup{(iii)}. \qedhere
\end{proof}
