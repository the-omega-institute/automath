\begin{theorem}[Spectral rigidity of strong lumpability: eigenvalues of an equitable quotient must come from the discrete hypercube spectrum]\label{thm:window6-strong-lumpability-spectral-rigidity}
Let \(Q_m\) be the \(m\)-dimensional hypercube, with adjacency matrix \(A\) and simple random-walk operator \(T=\frac{1}{m}A\).
Given any surjection \(F:\{0,1\}^m\to X\), let \(M\) be the block-indicator matrix determined by the fibers of \(F\), whose columns are the indicator vectors of the fibers.
Assume that the partition is \emph{equitable}, namely that there exists a matrix \(B\) such that
\[
AM=MB.
\]
Then the spectrum of \(B\) is contained in the spectrum of \(A\).
Equivalently, the Walsh characters \(\chi_S(\omega)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}\omega_i}\) diagonalize \(A\), with eigenvalue \(m-2|S|\), so every eigenvalue of the induced quotient transition matrix \(P:=\frac{1}{m}B\) belongs to the set
\[
\Bigl\{1-\tfrac{2k}{m}: k=0,1,\dots,m\Bigr\}.
\]
Therefore, if a candidate transition kernel \(P\) obtained from a pushforward construction has an eigenvalue outside this discrete set, then the corresponding coarse-graining cannot arise from a first-order equitable quotient of \(Q_m\) without introducing additional hidden states.

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(Bv=\lambda v\).
Because the columns of \(M\) are the indicators of the pairwise disjoint nonempty fibers of \(F\), they are linearly independent; equivalently, \(M\) has trivial kernel.
Therefore every eigenvector \(v\neq 0\) of \(B\) satisfies \(Mv\neq 0\).
Now
\(
A(Mv)=M(Bv)=\lambda(Mv)
\),
so \(Mv\) is an eigenvector of \(A\) with eigenvalue \(\lambda\).
Hence \(\mathrm{spec}(B)\subseteq \mathrm{spec}(A)\).
For completeness we recall the Walsh calculation.  For \(S\subseteq[m]\), set \(\chi_S(\omega)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}\omega_i}\).  Flipping a coordinate in \(S\) changes the sign of \(\chi_S\), and flipping a coordinate outside \(S\) preserves it; hence
\[
A\chi_S=(m-|S|)\chi_S-|S|\chi_S=(m-2|S|)\chi_S.
\]
The \(2^m\) Walsh characters form an orthogonal basis of \(\CC^{\Omega_m}\), so the adjacency spectrum of \(Q_m\) is \(\{m-2k:0\le k\le m\}\), with multiplicities \(\binom mk\), as in the standard hypercube spectrum references \cite{biggs1993algebraic,godsil2001algebraic,macwilliams1977theory}.  Therefore
\[
\mathrm{spec}(P)=\frac{1}{m}\mathrm{spec}(B)\subseteq \Bigl\{1-\tfrac{2k}{m}\Bigr\}_{k=0}^m.
\]
The final statement is simply the contrapositive of this inclusion. \qedhere
\end{proof}
