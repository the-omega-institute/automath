1. Significance
Desk-reject.
This is separate from correctness. On the description given, the paper establishes a sharp and unusually clean classification of one fixed partition of one 64-vertex graph. The affine-involution description, the 32+16 orbit structure, and the Q4​ antisymmetric sector make the answer more conceptual than a raw computer enumeration, but they do not remove the basic limitation: there is no varying parameter, no general theorem about cube folds, and apparently no previously recognized conjecture resolved.

2. Acceptance probability at EJC
20%.
That includes the chance that an editor is attracted by the exceptionally complete structure and sends it to a referee who values finite classification results. The more likely editorial reaction is that the 48-state theorem is elegant but too isolated.
3. Single highest-value change
Extend the six-cube result to an infinite family of Zeckendorf-prefix folds.
The added theorem should say, for a genuine unbounded sequence of dimensions or prefix lengths, that the coarsest equitable refinement is the orbit partition of an explicitly described affine symmetry, together with an exact or asymptotic state count and the corresponding spectral decomposition. The present Q6​ theorem would then become the first nontrivial case or a small exceptional case, rather than the entire phenomenon.
That would raise my EJC estimate by roughly 30 percentage points, from 20% to about 50%. Adding further certificates, tables, computations, or alternative proofs for the same Q6​ classification would not have a comparable effect.
4. Weakest load-bearing step
The hostile referee attacks the theorem or corollary asserting:

Every equitable hidden realization through which the 21-cell fold factors has at least 48 states, with equality unique up to relabeling.

The unique coarsest equitable set-partition refinement does imply that conclusion for deterministic hidden states whose observation map is literally a refinement of the visible partition. It does not automatically imply it for an arbitrary stochastic or linear hidden realization. In particular, showing that no intertwining exists at the original 21-state level does not by itself exclude realizations with 22,…,47 hidden states.
The paper therefore needs a precise bridge of one of the following forms:
every admissible hidden realization⟹an equitable partition refining the visible cells,
or an independent lower bound proving that every permitted intertwining representation has dimension at least 48.
Without the proof, I cannot tell whether that bridge is present. If “hidden realization” is defined deterministically, this is repairable, principally by making the category of realizations explicit and proving the reduction carefully. If the advertised theorem genuinely ranges over arbitrary stochastic hidden-state factorizations and the only evidence is the 21-state residual obstruction, the gap is fatal to the 48-state universality claim, though not to the underlying equitable-refinement classification.
There is also a numerical distinction that should remain explicit: the previously audited quantities were a sharp Chebyshev-type residual 1/6 and a separate pushforward norm 1/4. Calling the latter simply “the exact residual” would invite confusion unless the norm and optimization problem are specified.
