You are refereeing the attached 80-page paper. An earlier round of yours rejected it on two grounds:
several stated corollaries were false as written, and 80 pages support essentially one theorem
(a 48-state rigidity/repair result). Corollaries 1.4 and 1.5 have since been repaired --
1.5 now states the odd obstruction first, and 1.4's first clause has "fiberwise trivial" restored.

Do NOT trust your own earlier list of defects. Re-derive. Last time you were asked this way on a
different manuscript you found three further sites that your first pass had missed, including one in
the abstract, so please assume the same is true here.

Three questions.

(1) Are Corollaries 1.4 and 1.5 now true exactly as printed? Check the QUANTIFIERS and HYPOTHESES,
not the conclusions -- the recurring failure in this project is an advertised statement that asserts
a theorem's conclusion under only part of its hypotheses. Compare each corollary against the theorem
it is drawn from, clause by clause. Do the same for the abstract and for every numbered statement in
the introduction; report every site where the advertised claim is stronger than what is proved.

(2) The paper is 80 pages for one main theorem. Assume it must be cut to 15-20 pages for a
combinatorics journal. Tell me concretely what the 15-20 page paper IS: which theorem is the
headline, which sections survive, which become a remark, which are deleted outright, and -- most
importantly -- whether anything currently in the paper is a genuine second result that would be lost
and should instead become its own note. Be specific, cite section numbers.

(3) Given (1) and (2), name the strongest journal that would plausibly accept the CUT version, and
give a rough numeric probability. Do not name a journal merely because the topic matches; name it
because a referee there would find the headline theorem worth the pages.
