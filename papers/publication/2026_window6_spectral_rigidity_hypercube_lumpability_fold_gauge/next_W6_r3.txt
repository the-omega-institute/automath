You refereed this paper twice. Your standing objection is that it proves one exact six-dimensional
classification rather than a family theorem or a minimal-dimension obstruction, and you put it at
18-22 percent as submitted, 25-30 percent after the Section 3 repairs. Those repairs are done.

This round is one question: does the following change your assessment, and if so how should it be
framed? I am asking before writing it in, because I can establish the fact but cannot judge its
weight.

WHAT I COMPUTED. For each m I built the fold partition of Q_m and ran colour refinement to its
coarsest equitable refinement. My implementation reproduces the manuscript's own printed table at
m = 6 exactly - 21 cells over 64 vertices, the fibre of 000000 equal to {000000, 010101, 100010,
110111}, and the stated size vector - so it is the same object.

Results for 3 <= m <= 16:
 - m = 4, 5, 7, and every m from 10 to 16: the coarsest equitable refinement is the DISCRETE
   partition. The fold admits no nontrivial equitable hidden realization at all in those dimensions.
 - m = 3, 6, 8, 9: a nontrivial refinement exists, and in every one of those cases the structure is
   the same. Cells are singletons and pairs, and the discarded sector has dimension exactly 2^{m-2}.
   For those four I computed the full spectrum and the discarded sector carries exactly the
   adjacency spectrum of Q_{m-2}.

Two things I am NOT claiming. The first non-equitable dimension is 3, not 6, so there is no
minimal-dimension result here and the paper should not suggest one. And the sweep reaches m = 16, so
{3, 6, 8, 9} is what occurs below 17 rather than a proved classification.

QUESTIONS.
(1) Does this lift the paper out of "one exact six-dimensional classification"? Be blunt. A sporadic
    set of four dimensions with uniform structure may read as a genuine family phenomenon, or it may
    read as four unexplained coincidences, and I want your judgment rather than encouragement.
(2) If it helps, how should it enter - as a motivating remark, as a stated proposition with the
    computation as evidence, or only after someone proves which m qualify? Say what a referee at
    the Electronic Journal of Combinatorics would want.
(3) Is "which m admit a nontrivial equitable refinement of the fold partition" a tractable question,
    or is it the kind of sporadic arithmetic condition that resists a clean criterion? If you can see
    the mechanism selecting 3, 6, 8, 9 - or a reason to expect more beyond 16 - say so.
(4) Does your probability move? Give a number.
