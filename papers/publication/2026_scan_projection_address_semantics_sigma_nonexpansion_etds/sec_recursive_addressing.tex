\section{Finite cylinder holes by block recoding}
\label{sec:block-recoding}

The one-symbol description of the hole is a presentation choice rather
than a restriction to geometric intervals of one symbolic scale.  We
record precisely how the collision theorem transfers to a finite union
of cylinders.

Let \(\mathcal W\) be a finite collection of admissible words in \(Y\),
and let
\[
  H_{\mathcal W}:=\bigcup_{w\in\mathcal W}[w].
\]
Choose \(\ell\) at least as large as the longest word in
\(\mathcal W\).  After replacing a shorter word \(w\) by the finite
collection of admissible \(\ell\)-words extending it, we may assume that
all words in \(\mathcal W\) have length \(\ell\).

The \(\ell\)-block presentation \(Y^{(\ell)}\) has as symbols the
admissible \(\ell\)-words of \(Y\), with an edge when consecutive blocks
overlap in \(\ell-1\) symbols.  The coding map is
\[
  \Psi_\ell(y)_n:=y_n\cdots y_{n+\ell-1}.
\]
The hole \(H_{\mathcal W}\) becomes the one-symbol Markov hole whose
hole alphabet is \(\mathcal W\).

\begin{proposition}[Periodic collision law for finite cylinder holes]
\label{prop:cylinder-hole-recoding}
Let \(Y\), \(\nu_\varphi\), and \(H_{\mathcal W}\) be as above.  In
the \(\ell\)-block presentation, assume that the safe block graph is
irreducible of period \(d\) and branching.  For each \(m\), condition
the original process on avoiding \(H_{\mathcal W}\) at times
\(0,\ldots,m-1\), and observe its prefix of length \(m+\ell-1\).

Fix an integer \(k\geq2\), take \(N_m\) independent prefixes with this
common conditioned law, and count \(k\)-element subfamilies of equal
prefixes.  Then Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-collisions} applies with block
depth \(m\): its R\'enyi rate is the pressure expression for the
survivor block shift, and its critical Poisson prefactor is indexed by
\(m-1\bmod d\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first \(m\) block symbols determine, and are determined by, the
first \(m+\ell-1\) original symbols: overlap reconstructs the original
word uniquely.  Avoidance of the block-hole alphabet for these \(m\)
block symbols is exactly avoidance of \(H_{\mathcal W}\) at starting
positions \(0,\ldots,m-1\).  Thus \(\Psi_\ell\) is a bijection between
the two finite survivor-prefix sets, preserves cylinder probabilities,
and hence preserves every conditioned power sum and every equality
collision.

The image of a one-step Markov chain under the overlapping block code is
again a one-step Markov chain: the transition probability from one block
to the next is the transition probability of the newly appended symbol
given the terminal symbol of the first block.  Equivalently, the
one-step potential lifts to the block edge potential.  All hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-collisions} therefore hold in the block
presentation, and its conclusion pulls back through the bijection.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Depth convention]
The shift by \(\ell-1\) is not asymptotically important, but it is needed
for an exact finite-depth statement.  A collision of the first \(m\)
block symbols is a collision of the first \(m+\ell-1\) original symbols,
not merely of the first \(m\) original symbols.  Likewise, survival of
\(m\) block steps tests \(m\) possible starting locations for a forbidden
\(\ell\)-word.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the recoding]
The proposition extends the theorem from one-symbol Markov holes to
arbitrary finite unions of cylinders while retaining a one-step Markov
equilibrium law after recoding.  It does not turn a general H\"older
Gibbs measure into a finite-order Markov measure, and no such claim is
used here.
\end{remark}
