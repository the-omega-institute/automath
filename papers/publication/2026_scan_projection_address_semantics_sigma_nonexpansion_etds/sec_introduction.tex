\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Collision statistics of long symbolic words are controlled by R\'enyi
power sums.  In a primitive finite-state source, these power sums have a
single Perron asymptotic, and the usual dependency-graph argument turns
the strict separation of R\'enyi rates into a Poisson law for generalized
birthday coincidences.  An open symbolic system need not retain
primitivity after a hole is introduced.  Its survivor graph may remain
irreducible but acquire a period, and then the single Perron asymptotic
is replaced by a family of cyclic limits.

This paper determines what happens to collision laws in that periodic
regime.  The exponential birthday scale is unchanged, but its
multiplicative constant depends on the depth class.  This dependence is
not an artifact of the proof: in the explicit period-two system of
Section~\ref{sec:periodic-example}, the two pair-collision constants are
different.  A normalization that ignores the survivor period therefore
has two different Poisson subsequential limits and no full-sequence
limit.

The setting is deliberately finite-state but no longer primitive.  Let
\(Y\) be a topologically mixing one-step subshift of finite type, let
\(\nu_\varphi\) be the equilibrium Markov measure of a one-step
potential, and let \(H\) be a nonempty proper Markov hole.  We assume
that the graph induced by the safe symbols is irreducible of period
\(d\geq1\).  The case \(d=1\) recovers the primitive survivor law; the
new content concerns \(d>1\).  We exclude exactly one obstruction: a
deterministic safe cycle.  Equivalently, at least one safe state has two
safe successors.  This hypothesis is necessary for the strict R\'enyi
gap used by the collision theorem.

For a safe word \(a\) of length \(m\), write
\[
  \lambda_m^H(a)
  :=\nu_\varphi([a]\mid \tau_H\geq m),
\]
and define the R\'enyi power sum
\[
  S_s^H(m):=\sum_a\lambda_m^H(a)^s.
\]
If \(B_s\) is the entrywise \(s\)-th power of the killed transition
matrix and \(\rho_s=\rho(B_s)\), then
\[
  h_{s,H}:=-\log\frac{\rho_s}{\rho_1^s}
  =sP_{Y_H}(\varphi)-P_{Y_H}(s\varphi).
\]
The cyclic Perron theorem below produces positive constants
\(c_{s,0},\ldots,c_{s,d-1}\) such that, with \(n=m-1\),
\[
  S_s^H(m)
  =c_{s,r}e^{-nh_{s,H}}(1+O(\kappa_s^n)),
  \qquad n\equiv r\pmod d.
\]
All constants are explicit in the stationary initial law and the left
and right Perron vectors of \(B_s\).

\subsection*{Main collision theorem}

For fixed \(k\geq2\), let
\(X_m^{H,(1)},\ldots,X_m^{H,(N_m)}\) be independent survivor prefixes
with the common law \(\lambda_m^H\), and let \(W_{m,k}\) count the
\(k\)-element subfamilies whose members are all equal.  The independence,
common-law, and fixed-multiplicity assumptions are part of every
collision statement in this paper.

\begin{theorem}[Phase-resolved survivor collisions]
\label{thm:intro-collisions}
Assume that the safe graph is irreducible of period \(d\), has at least
two states, and is not a deterministic cycle.  Fix an integer
\(k\geq2\).  If
\[
  \binom{N_m}{k}S_k^H(m)\longrightarrow\tau\in(0,\infty),
\]
then
\[
  W_{m,k}\ \Rightarrow\ \operatorname{Poisson}(\tau).
\]
More explicitly, fix \(r\in\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z\) and let
\(m\to\infty\) through depths satisfying \(m-1\equiv r\pmod d\).  If
\[
  N_m e^{-(m-1)h_{k,H}/k}\longrightarrow\alpha\in(0,\infty),
\]
then
\[
  W_{m,k}\ \Rightarrow\
  \operatorname{Poisson}\!\left(\frac{c_{k,r}\alpha^k}{k!}\right).
\]
Below this scale the collision probability tends to zero, and above it
the collision probability tends to one.  A total-variation estimate is
given in Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-collisions}.
\end{theorem}

The proof has three ingredients.  First, powers of each \(B_s\) are
resolved along the cyclic classes of the safe graph.  Second, a strict
entrywise-power inequality gives
\[
  h_{t,H}>\frac{t}{s}h_{s,H}
  \qquad (1\leq s<t).
\]
Third, the dependency graph on intersecting \(k\)-subsets is inserted
into the Chen--Stein estimate of
\citet{ArratiaGoldsteinGordon1989}.  The overlap of two \(k\)-subsets
of size \(\ell\) contributes \(S_{2k-\ell}^H(m)\), and the strict
R\'enyi gap makes every such contribution exponentially negligible at
the critical scale.  Generalized birthday Poisson approximation is
classical; the point here is the periodic open-system asymptotic needed
to identify the correct family of critical means.

\subsection*{Finite observation of first-entry phases}

The same cyclic Perron resolution also changes the finite-observation
asymptotics of first-entry residue events.  This is a secondary
application, included because it makes the interaction between the hole
period \(d\) and an observation modulus \(q\) explicit.

Fix \(q\geq2\) and a nonempty proper subset
\(R\subsetneq\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\).  For every safe state \(i\), let
\(D_i\) be the set modulo \(q\) of attainable positive first-hit times
from \(i\).  We impose the all-state, all-phase condition
\[
  D_i\cap(R-r)\neq\varnothing,
  \qquad
  D_i\cap((\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\setminus R)-r)\neq\varnothing
  \tag{1.1}\label{eq:intro-nondegeneracy}
\]
for every safe \(i\) and every \(r\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\).  This
condition is stronger than what is needed for a leading asymptotic, but
it is exactly what yields a uniform finite-depth comparison with
survivor mass.

\begin{theorem}[Periodic first-entry ambiguity]
\label{thm:intro-bayes}
Under the preceding assumptions on \(Y\), \(\nu_\varphi\), and \(H\),
fix \(q\geq2\) and a nonempty proper
\(R\subsetneq\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\), and assume
\eqref{eq:intro-nondegeneracy} for every safe state and every phase.
Then the depth-\(m\) Bayes error \(\varepsilon_m\) of the event
\(\{\tau_H<\infty:\tau_H\bmod q\in R\}\) satisfies
\[
  \varepsilon_m
  =u^{\mathsf T}B_1^{m-1}b_{m-1\bmod q},
\]
where \(b_r\) is the terminal-state conditional Bayes ambiguity.
Moreover
\[
  \theta\,\nu_\varphi(\tau_H\geq m)
  \leq\varepsilon_m
  \leq\frac12\nu_\varphi(\tau_H\geq m)
\]
for some \(\theta>0\).  Along every class modulo
\(\operatorname{lcm}(d,q)\), the ratio of \(\varepsilon_m\) to survivor
mass converges to an explicit positive cyclic Perron amplitude.
\end{theorem}

Thus the periodic extension does not merely replace one Perron vector by
another.  It forces a change in the formulation of both principal
asymptotics: collision means are indexed by \(m-1\bmod d\), while
first-entry ambiguity amplitudes are indexed jointly by the survivor
phase modulo \(d\) and the observation phase modulo \(q\).

\subsection*{Relation to existing results}

Thermodynamic formalism for equilibrium states on shifts of finite type
is standard; we use the matrix form developed in
\citet{Ruelle1978,Bowen2008,Walters1975GMeasures}.  Escape through
symbolic holes and its transfer-operator description go back to
\citet{PianigianiYorke1979}; see also
\citet{ColletMartinezSchmitt1997,DemersYoung2006,KellerLiverani2009,
FergusonPollicott2012}.  Periodic Perron asymptotics are classical
nonnegative-matrix theory \citep{Seneta2006}.  We record the cyclic
constants because their dependence on the entrywise power \(s\) is what
survives normalization and enters the collision law.

Poisson limits for generalized birthday coincidences appear, for
example, in \citet{Henze1998}; related marked Poisson laws in symbolic
dynamics were proved by \citet{ChazottesCoelhoCollet2009}.  Our samples
are independent across the sample index, but their common distribution
changes with the survival depth.  The triangular array is therefore
controlled by the open-system R\'enyi spectrum.  The contribution here
is not a new Chen--Stein mechanism: it is the phase-resolved survivor
law, the explicit oscillating prefactor, and the proof that the standard
Poisson scheme remains valid after this nonprimitive extension.

\subsection*{Organization}

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes the open Markov model and proves
the cyclic Perron formula with its explicit constants.
Section~\ref{sec:double-budget} derives the phase-resolved R\'enyi
asymptotics, proves the strict spectral inequality, and establishes the
collision theorem with a total-variation bound.
Section~\ref{sec:open-system} gives the fully quantified first-entry
Bayes theorem and its joint \(d\)- and \(q\)-phase amplitudes.
Section~\ref{sec:periodic-example} computes a period-two example in
closed form and shows that the two critical Poisson means differ.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} records the scope and the deterministic-cycle
obstruction.
