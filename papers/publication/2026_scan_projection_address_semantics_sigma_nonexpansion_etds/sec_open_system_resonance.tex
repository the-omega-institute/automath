\section{A period-two survivor with two Poisson means}
\label{sec:periodic-example}

We compute a branching periodic example in closed form.  Besides
illustrating the constants in Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-renyi}, it shows
that the phase qualification in
Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-collisions} cannot be removed.

Let the alphabet be \(A=\{a,b,c,h\}\), let \(H=[h]\), and consider the
Markov transition matrix
\begin{equation}
  K=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0&1/3&1/3&1/3\\
    1/2&0&0&1/2\\
    1/4&0&0&3/4\\
    1/4&1/4&1/4&1/4
  \end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:example-K}
\end{equation}
with rows and columns ordered as \(a,b,c,h\).  The ambient graph is
primitive: the state \(h\) has a self-loop and communicates with every
state.  The stationary distribution is
\begin{equation}
  \pi=\frac1{89}(21,16,16,36).
  \label{eq:example-stationary}
\end{equation}
Indeed, direct multiplication verifies \(\pi K=\pi\).
The stationary Markov law determined by \((\pi,K)\) is fully supported
on this primitive graph and is the equilibrium measure of the one-step
potential \(\varphi(i,j)=\log K_{ij}\) on admissible edges: its transfer
matrix is \(K\), with Perron eigenvalue one and right Perron vector
\(\mathbf1\).  Thus the standing ambient hypotheses of
Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} hold.

The safe alphabet is \(\mathcal S=\{a,b,c\}\), and the killed family is
\begin{equation}
  B_s=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0&3^{-s}&3^{-s}\\
    2^{-s}&0&0\\
    4^{-s}&0&0
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:example-Bs}
\end{equation}
Its cyclic classes are
\[
  \mathcal C_0=\{a\},
  \qquad
  \mathcal C_1=\{b,c\}.
\]
Thus the survivor graph is irreducible of period two.  It is branching
because \(a\) has two safe successors.

Put
\[
  t_s:=3^{-s},\qquad r_s^*:=2^{-s},\qquad v_s:=4^{-s}.
\]
The Perron eigenvalue is
\begin{equation}
  \rho_s=\sqrt{t_s(r_s^*+v_s)}
  =\sqrt{6^{-s}+12^{-s}}.
  \label{eq:example-rhos}
\end{equation}
Compatible right and left Perron vectors, normalized to have scalar
product one, are
\begin{equation}
  r_s=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1\\ r_s^*/\rho_s\\ v_s/\rho_s
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \ell_s=\frac12
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1\\ t_s/\rho_s\\ t_s/\rho_s
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:example-perron-vectors}
\end{equation}
The initial weight on the two states \(b,c\) is the same.  Writing
\[
  x_s:=(21/89)^s,
  \qquad
  y_s:=(16/89)^s,
\]
formula \eqref{eq:cyclic-coefficient} gives
\begin{align}
  A_{s,0}(\mathbf1)
  &=x_s+2y_s,
  \label{eq:example-A0}\\
  A_{s,1}(\mathbf1)
  &=\frac{2x_st_s+y_s(r_s^*+v_s)}{\rho_s}.
  \label{eq:example-A1}
\end{align}
For completeness, the first identity follows by pairing starts and ends
within the same cyclic class:
\begin{align*}
 A_{s,0}(\mathbf1)
 &=2\left[
     x_s\left(\frac12\right)
     +y_s\frac{r_s^*+v_s}{\rho_s}
       \frac{t_s}{\rho_s}
   \right]\\
 &=x_s+2y_s,
\end{align*}
because \(\rho_s^2=t_s(r_s^*+v_s)\).  Pairing opposite cyclic classes
gives \eqref{eq:example-A1}.

\subsection{Pair-collision constants}

At \(s=1\),
\[
  \rho_1=\frac12,
  \qquad
  A_{1,0}(\mathbf1)=\frac{53}{89},
  \qquad
  A_{1,1}(\mathbf1)=\frac{52}{89}.
\]
At \(s=2\),
\[
  \rho_2=\frac{\sqrt5}{12},
  \qquad
  A_{2,0}(\mathbf1)=\frac{953}{7921},
  \qquad
  A_{2,1}(\mathbf1)=\frac{2136}{7921\sqrt5}.
\]
It follows from \eqref{eq:phase-renyi-constant} that
\begin{equation}
  c_{2,0}=\frac{953}{2809},
  \qquad
  c_{2,1}=\frac{267}{338\sqrt5}.
  \label{eq:example-collision-constants}
\end{equation}
These constants are unequal; numerically they are approximately
\(0.3393\) and \(0.3533\).  The pair R\'enyi rate is
\begin{equation}
  h_{2,H}
  =-\log\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1^2}
  =\log\frac3{\sqrt5}.
  \label{eq:example-h2}
\end{equation}

Let \(W_{m,2}\) count equal pairs among \(N_m\) independent survivor
prefixes with the common conditioned law \(\lambda_m^H\).  If
\[
  N_m\exp\left(-\frac{m-1}{2}\log\frac3{\sqrt5}\right)
  \longrightarrow\alpha\in(0,\infty),
\]
then Theorem~\ref{thm:periodic-collisions} gives
\begin{align}
  W_{m,2}
  &\Rightarrow
  \operatorname{Poisson}\!\left(
      \frac{953}{5618}\alpha^2
  \right)
  &&\text{when }m-1\equiv0\pmod2,
  \label{eq:example-even-limit}\\
  W_{m,2}
  &\Rightarrow
  \operatorname{Poisson}\!\left(
      \frac{267}{676\sqrt5}\alpha^2
  \right)
  &&\text{when }m-1\equiv1\pmod2.
  \label{eq:example-odd-limit}
\end{align}
The two means are different.  For example, choosing
\[
  N_m=\left\lfloor
    \alpha\exp\left(\frac{m-1}{2}\log\frac3{\sqrt5}\right)
  \right\rfloor
\]
produces both subsequential limits in
\eqref{eq:example-even-limit}--\eqref{eq:example-odd-limit}.  Hence
\(W_{m,2}\) has no full-sequence weak limit under this natural
phase-free normalization.

\subsection{Direct consistency check}

The phase effect can also be seen without spectral notation.  A safe
path alternates between \(\{a\}\) and \(\{b,c\}\).  After an even
number \(n=m-1\) of safe transitions, its terminal class equals its
initial class; after an odd number it is the opposite class.  The two
initial class masses in \eqref{eq:example-stationary} are
\[
  \pi(a)=\frac{21}{89},
  \qquad
  \pi(b)+\pi(c)=\frac{32}{89},
\]
and the two directions of the safe graph have different transition
weights.  There is therefore no symmetry forcing the even and odd
normalized power sums to coincide.  Formulas
\eqref{eq:example-A0}--\eqref{eq:example-A1} quantify exactly this
imbalance.

The example also checks the necessity of distinguishing period from
mixing in open dynamics.  The ambient chain \eqref{eq:example-K} is
primitive, but deleting the hole state leaves a period-two survivor.
Thus ambient mixing does not prevent the cyclic obstruction exhibited
by the collision process.
