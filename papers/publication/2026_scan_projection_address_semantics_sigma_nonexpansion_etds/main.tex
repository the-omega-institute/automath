\begin{abstract}
Let \(Y\) be a mixing one-step subshift of finite type carrying the
equilibrium Markov measure of a one-step potential, and let \(H\) be a
Markov hole whose safe graph is irreducible of period \(d\).  We study
independent prefixes with the common law obtained by conditioning on
survival to depth \(m\).  If the safe graph has at least two states and
is not a deterministic cycle, then for every fixed integer \(k\geq2\)
the number of \(k\)-fold
collisions has a Poisson limit at its pressure-determined birthday
scale.  The result is phase resolved: the R\'enyi power sum has an
explicit Perron prefactor depending on \(m-1\bmod d\), and consequently
the critical Poisson mean can oscillate between depth classes.  We give
an explicit total-variation error bound and a period-two example in
which the two limiting means are different.  Thus a phase-free
extension of the primitive-survivor collision law is in general false.
As a secondary finite-observation application, fix \(q\geq2\), a
nonempty proper set \(R\subsetneq\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\), and assume that
from every safe state and every current phase the attainable first-hit
times occur both in \(R\) minus that phase and in its complement.  Under
these hypotheses the Bayes error of the first-entry residue event has an
exact killed-matrix path sum and phase-resolved quasistationary
amplitudes along the classes modulo \(\operatorname{lcm}(d,q)\).
\end{abstract}
