\begin{abstract}
We study a variational inverse problem for primitives of constant
differential forms on axis-aligned boxes. Given a smooth $(k-1)$-form $\eta$
with $\dd\eta=\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k$, we ask how much of the
boundary trace of $\eta$ can be recovered from sharp, or nearly sharp,
saturation of the coefficient-$L^\infty$ norm in the interior. The analysis
starts from the midpoint-centered radial homotopy operator. On the class of
coordinate-monomial forms $f\,\dd x_I$ we use the sharp estimate
$\norm{K_k\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}\le (2k)^{-1}\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}$
and identify the precise incidence obstruction to extending this bound to
larger orientation classes. For a box $R=\prod_{j=1}^k[0,L_j]$ with positive
side lengths we compute the extremal value
$m(R)=(2\sum_{j=1}^kL_j^{-1})^{-1}$ for primitives of
$\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k$, construct an explicit affine minimizer
$\eta_R$, and establish a linear stability estimate which controls the
boundary trace by the interior norm gap
$\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}-m(R)$. In particular, every minimizer has the
canonical trace on each codimension-one face, while near-minimizers determine
that trace quantitatively. A slicing argument extends the readout mechanism to
constant coordinate-monomial forms on higher-dimensional boxes. On one cube,
the oriented cubical coboundary gives the corresponding discrete boundary
readout, and the Whitney construction gives the matching sharp decomposition
analogue.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Inverse problems are often formulated through a measurement operator: one
observes boundary or exterior data and seeks to recover an inaccessible
coefficient, source, or geometry. The present paper studies a different, but
still genuinely inverse, mechanism. The forward constraint is an interior
exactness relation, and the inverse information is encoded by sharp saturation
of an optimal coefficient-$L^\infty$ bound for a primitive. More precisely, we
ask whether a sharp interior bound determines the boundary trace of the
primitive. For constant differential forms on axis-aligned boxes the answer is
yes, and the determination is quantitative.

The question is motivated by local reconstruction problems in which a
primitive is controlled in an interior norm while the quantity exchanged
between neighboring cells or charts is its boundary trace. In such settings it
is natural to ask whether an optimal interior estimate already fixes the
boundary data. The box geometry considered here is simple enough for the sharp
constants to be computed explicitly, but rich enough to display a clear
inverse phenomenon: a small deficit from the extremal coefficient norm forces
a proportionally small boundary-trace error. In this sense, the paper provides
a local cubical model in which sharp interior estimates carry explicit inverse
information at the boundary.

Let
\[
I^k=[0,1]^k,
\qquad
\omega_0:=\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge \dd x_k,
\]
and consider
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intro-extremal-problem}
m_k:=
\inf\left\{
\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}:\ \eta\in\Omega^{k-1}_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^k),\
\dd\eta=\omega_0
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Here $\norm{\cdot}_{\coeff,\infty}$ denotes the supremum norm of the coordinate
coefficients. If the classical radial homotopy operator is centered at
$c=(1/2,\dots,1/2)$, one obtains the explicit primitive
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intro-explicit-minimizer}
\eta_\ast
=
\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^k (-1)^{j-1}\left(x_j-\frac12\right)
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge \dd x_k,
\end{equation}
and therefore $m_k\le 1/(2k)$. The main point of the paper is that this upper
bound is exact and that the corresponding equality and near-equality regimes
determine the boundary trace.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Main results.}
The analysis has three components. First, we recall from
\cite{MaZhangCubicalStokes2026} the class on which the midpoint-centered
homotopy operator carries the sharp coefficient bound
\[
\norm{K_k\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}\le \frac{1}{2k}\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}.
\]
This holds for coordinate-monomial forms $\omega=f\,\dd x_I$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:incidence-criterion} records the precise
combinatorial obstruction which prevents the same factor from extending to
larger orientation classes.

Second, we turn the sharp constant into an inverse statement. On every
axis-aligned box $R=\prod_{j=1}^k[0,L_j]$ with positive side lengths we compute
the exact extremal value
\[
m(R)=\frac{1}{2\sum_{j=1}^k L_j^{-1}}
\]
and construct an affine primitive $\eta_R$ attaining it. If $\eta$ is any
smooth primitive of $\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k$ on $R$, then
\[
\sum_{F\subset\partial R}
\norm{\Tr_F(\eta-\eta_R)}_{L^1(F;\coeff)}
\le
2P_1(R)\bigl(\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}-m(R)\bigr).
\]
Here the sum extends over the codimension-one faces of $R$, $\Tr_F$ denotes
the pullback to the face $F$, and $P_1(R)$ is the anisotropic $\ell_1$
perimeter. In particular, every minimizer has the canonical trace on each
face, while near-minimizers determine that trace with linear stability. On the
unit cube this gives the explicit boundary formula
\[
\iota_{j,\varepsilon}^\ast\eta
=
(-1)^{j-1}\frac{\varepsilon-\frac12}{k}\,
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge \dd x_k,
\qquad
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Third, we use the sliced readout mechanism for constant coordinate-monomial
data on higher-dimensional boxes. Under the weaker
hypothesis that $\dd\omega$ is coordinate-monomial, the homotopy identity also
gives the sharp decomposition
\[
\omega=\dd\eta+E,
\qquad
\norm{E}_{\coeff,\infty}\le \frac{1}{2(k+1)}\norm{\dd\omega}_{\coeff,\infty},
\]
again with sharp constant. We conclude with a one-cube cubical boundary
readout theorem and a Whitney analogue of the decomposition estimate, showing
that both structures persist at the level of cubical cochains.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Relation with earlier work.}
The homotopy formula used here is classical; see, for instance,
\cite[section~3]{BottTu1982} and
\cite{Whitney1957,Dodziuk1976,Dupont1976,DupontBook1978,Albert2020,
ArnoldFalkWinther2010,ArnoldAwanou2014}. For bounded right inverses on more
general domains we refer to
\cite{Bogovskii1979,IwaniecLutoborski1993,CostabelMcIntosh2010,
GuzmanSalgado2021,ChaumontFreletLichtVohralik2025}. On the anisotropic side,
related sharp constants for scalar Poincar\'e inequalities on convex domains
were studied in \cite{PayneWeinberger1960,AcostaDuran2004}. Our argument
belongs to the circle of ideas surrounding crystalline isoperimetry, Wulff
shapes, and calibration methods; see
\cite{Taylor1978,Fonseca1991,FonsecaMuller1991,DacorognaPfister1992,
FigalliZhang2022,DeMason2024,Strang1983,Nozawa1990,Grieser2006,Strang2010,
CasellesFaccioloMeinhardt2009,CasellesChambolleMollNovaga2008,Leonardi2015}.
The quotient $\Vol(E)/P_1(E)$ appearing below is the crystalline analogue of
the Cheeger ratio; compare
\cite{Cheeger1970,Leonardi2015,Parini2011}. For rigidity and uniqueness
phenomena in related Cheeger-type problems we also refer to
\cite{CasellesChambolleNovaga2007,CasellesChambolleNovaga2011}.
Our contribution is not a new homotopy formula or a new calibration
principle. Rather, we extract from these classical ingredients a sharp
coefficient-$L^\infty$ estimate on the restricted class $\omega=f\,\dd x_I$,
identify the combinatorial obstruction beyond that class, and show that the
resulting extremal problem yields quantitative boundary determination on
boxes. From the viewpoint of geometric discretization, the box theorem may be
read as a local statement on when a sharp interior certificate fixes the
boundary data.

From the inverse-problems viewpoint, the present problem is complementary to
the usual coefficient-recovery setting of Calder\'on type; compare
\cite{Calderon1980,Uhlmann2009}. Instead of recovering an interior coefficient
from a boundary measurement map, we recover boundary traces from sharp or
near-sharp interior norm saturation.

\subsection{Theorem-by-theorem structure}

Sections~2--4 give the continuous theorem chain used in the paper. The
coordinate-monomial definitions and the centered homotopy operator lead first
to the incidence obstruction in Proposition~\ref{prop:incidence-criterion} and
then to the sharp inverse estimate
Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal-continuous-inverse}. Proposition~\ref{thm:homotopy-decomposition}
and Corollary~\ref{cor:curl-reconstruction} record the corresponding
decomposition and vector-potential consequences. Section~4 proves the
anisotropic calibration bound, the quantitative boundary saturation identity,
the box self-Cheeger computation, the boundary readout theorem on boxes, and
the sliced active-coordinate extension. These continuous results are then used
as input for the one-cube cubical boundary readout theorem and the Whitney
decomposition results below.

In the discrete section, Proposition~\ref{prop:cubical-whitney} records
standard Whitney-form identities on one cell, now written in a cubical rather
than simplicial notation and consistent with the classical Whitney--Dupont
picture and later cubical FEEC constructions
\cite{Whitney1957,Dupont1976,ArnoldAwanou2014}. Theorem~\ref{thm:cubical-boundary-readout}
is the finite-dimensional counterpart of the unit-cube boundary readout
theorem: sharp saturation of the cell norm for a primitive of the top cochain
fixes every oriented boundary-face value. The paper-specific Whitney
definitions are Definition~\ref{def:whitney-decomposable} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:whitney-characterization}, which single out the Whitney
coordinate-monomial class on which the sharp estimate survives. With that class
in place, Proposition~\ref{thm:discrete-stokes-poincare} is the discrete
counterpart of the continuous sharp decomposition theorem, retaining the same
optimal factor $(2(k+1))^{-1}$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:hypercube-gradient} is
its one-cochain specialization with the optimal $1/4$ remainder bound.

\section{Preliminaries and the centered homotopy operator}

We begin by fixing notation and recalling the midpoint-centered radial
homotopy operator in the form needed below.

Fix $n\ge 1$ and write
\[
I^n:=[0,1]^n,
\qquad
c:=\left(\frac12,\dots,\frac12\right).
\]
For $1\le k\le n$, let $\Omega^k_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^n)$ denote the smooth
$k$-forms on a neighborhood of $I^n$, restricted to the cube. If
$R\subset\RR^n$ is any axis-aligned box, we use $\Omega^k_{\mathrm{sm}}(R)$ for
the analogous space of smooth $k$-forms on a neighborhood of $R$, restricted
to $R$. If $F$ is a codimension-one face of such a box and
$\iota_F:F\hookrightarrow R$ denotes the inclusion, we write
\[
\Tr_F(\eta):=\iota_F^\ast\eta
\]
for the face trace of a differential form $\eta$ on $R$. If
\[
\omega=\sum_{\abs{I}=k} a_I(x)\,\dd x_I,
\]
we set
\[
\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}:=
\max_{\abs{I}=k}\sup_{x\in I^n}\abs{a_I(x)}.
\]
All $L^\infty$ estimates below are taken with respect to this coordinate norm.
For a form evaluated at a fixed point $y$, we write
\[
\abs{\omega(y)}_{\coeff,\infty}:=
\max_{\abs{I}=k}\abs{a_I(y)}.
\]
If
\[
\beta=\sum_{\abs{J}=m} b_J(x)\,\dd x_J,
\]
we also write $\coeff_J(\beta):=b_J$ for the coefficient of $\dd x_J$.

\begin{remark}
Throughout, $\dd$ denotes the exterior derivative on smooth forms, whereas $d$
is reserved for the cubical coboundary used in Section~5.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Coordinate-monomial form]
\label{def:decomposable-form}
A smooth $k$-form $\omega\in\Omega^k_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^n)$ is
\emph{coordinate-monomial} if either $\omega=0$, or there are an ordered
coordinate set
\[
I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_k\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
\]
and a smooth coefficient $f$ on a neighborhood of $I^n$ such that
\[
\omega=f(x)\,\dd x_I
:=
f(x)\,\dd x_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge \dd x_{i_k}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
This requirement is stronger than decomposability in
$\Lambda^kT^\ast I^n$. For instance,
$(\dd x_1+\dd x_2)\wedge \dd x_3$ is decomposable in the exterior algebra, but
it is not coordinate-monomial.
\end{remark}

Let $X(x):=x-c$ be the radial vector field centered at $c$, and let
\[
H:[0,1]\times I^n\to I^n,
\qquad
H(t,x):=c+t(x-c).
\]
For each $t\in[0,1]$ we write $H_t(x):=H(t,x)$.

\begin{definition}[Radial homotopy operator]
\label{def:homotopy-operator}
For $1\le k\le n$ define
\[
K_k:\Omega^k_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^n)\longrightarrow
\Omega^{k-1}_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^n)
\]
by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:K-definition}
(K_k\omega)(x):=
\int_0^1 t^{k-1}\,
\bigl(\iota_{x-c}\omega\bigr)\bigl(c+t(x-c)\bigr)\,\dd t .
\end{equation}
Equivalently, if
\[
\omega=\sum_{\abs{I}=k}a_I(y)\,\dd y_I,
\qquad I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_k\},
\]
then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:K-coefficient-formula}
(K_k\omega)(x)=
\int_0^1 t^{k-1}
\sum_{\abs{I}=k}\sum_{r=1}^k
(-1)^{r-1}(x_{i_r}-c_{i_r})\,
a_I(c+t(x-c))\,\dd x_{I\setminus\{i_r\}}\,\dd t .
\end{equation}
We also set $K_{n+1}:=0$.
\end{definition}

This is the standard radial homotopy formula centered at the midpoint of the
cube; see \cite[Sec.~3]{BottTu1982} and
\cite{Dupont1976,DupontBook1978,Albert2020}. In particular, for
$\omega_0=\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge \dd x_k$ one obtains
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:explicit-minimizer}
K_k\omega_0
=
\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^k (-1)^{j-1}\left(x_j-\frac12\right)
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge \dd x_k.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Contraction estimate]
\label{lem:contraction-estimate}
Let
\[
\omega=f(y)\,\dd x_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_{i_k}
\]
be coordinate-monomial. Then for all $x,y\in I^n$,
\[
\abs{\bigl(\iota_{x-c}\omega\bigr)(y)}_{\coeff,\infty}
\le \frac12\,\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\norm{\iota_X\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}
\le \frac12\,\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Writing $I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_k\}$, contraction gives
\[
\bigl(\iota_{x-c}\omega\bigr)(y)
=
\sum_{r=1}^k
(-1)^{r-1}(x_{i_r}-c_{i_r})f(y)\,
\dd x_{I\setminus\{i_r\}} .
\]
Every coefficient therefore has absolute value at most
$\frac12\abs{f(y)}$, since $c_i=1/2$ and $0\le x_i\le 1$. Taking the maximum
over the output coefficients and then the supremum over $y$ proves the first
estimate. The second estimate is the special case $y=x$ followed by taking
the supremum over $x\in I^n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Incidence criterion]
\label{prop:incidence-criterion}
Let
\[
\omega=\sum_{\abs{I}=k} a_I(x)\,\dd x_I\in\Omega^k_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^n).
\]
For each ordered $(k-1)$-index $J$ define
\[
m_J(\omega):=
\#\{i\notin J:\ a_{J\cup\{i\}}\not\equiv 0\},
\qquad
m(\omega):=\max_{\abs{J}=k-1}m_J(\omega),
\]
where $J\cup\{i\}$ is always written in increasing order. Then
\[
\norm{\iota_X\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}
\le
\frac{m(\omega)}{2}\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}.
\]
In particular, let $\mathcal M$ be a family of $k$-indices and let
$\Omega^k_{\mathcal M}(I^n)$ denote the subspace of forms
\[
\omega=\sum_{I\in\mathcal M}a_I(x)\,\dd x_I .
\]
The universal bound
\[
\norm{\iota_X\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}
\le
\frac12\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}
\qquad
\text{for every }\omega\in\Omega^k_{\mathcal M}(I^n)
\]
holds if and only if, for each $(k-1)$-index $J$, at most one member of
$\mathcal M$ contains $J$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $J=\{j_1<\cdots<j_{k-1}\}$. In the coefficient of $\dd x_J$ in
$\iota_X\omega$ only those $k$-indices of the form $J\cup\{i\}$ can occur.
For such an index the contribution is
\[
\pm (x_i-\tfrac12)a_{J\cup\{i\}}(x),
\]
where the sign is determined only by the position of $i$ in
$J\cup\{i\}$. Therefore
\[
\abs{\coeff_J(\iota_X\omega)(x)}
\le
\sum_{\substack{i\notin J\\ a_{J\cup\{i\}}\not\equiv 0}}
\frac12\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}
\le
\frac{m_J(\omega)}{2}\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}.
\]
Taking the maximum over $J$ and the supremum over $x$ proves the estimate.

If every $(k-1)$-index $J$ is contained in at most one member of
$\mathcal M$, then $m(\omega)\le1$ for every
$\omega\in\Omega^k_{\mathcal M}(I^n)$, so the asserted universal
$1/2$ bound follows. Conversely, suppose that two distinct indices
$J\cup\{p\}$ and $J\cup\{q\}$ in $\mathcal M$ contain the same $J$.
Choose the constant coefficients on those two indices so that their
contributions to the $\dd x_J$ coefficient of $\iota_X\omega$ have the same
sign at a vertex with $x_p,x_q\in\{0,1\}$ and all other coefficients are zero.
Then $\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}=1$ and the corresponding coefficient of
$\iota_X\omega$ has absolute value $1$ at that vertex. Hence no universal
constant $1/2$ can hold on $\Omega^k_{\mathcal M}(I^n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:non-coord-monomial}
The restriction to coordinate-monomial forms is essential for the coefficient
norm. On $I^3$ consider
\[
\omega:=\dd x_1\wedge \dd x_3+\dd x_2\wedge \dd x_3.
\]
Then $\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}=1$, whereas
\[
\iota_X\omega
=
\bigl(x_1-\tfrac12+x_2-\tfrac12\bigr)\dd x_3
-\bigl(x_3-\tfrac12\bigr)(\dd x_1+\dd x_2),
\]
so $\norm{\iota_X\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}=1$. A direct computation gives
\[
K_2\omega
=
\frac{x_1+x_2-1}{2}\,\dd x_3
-\frac{x_3-\tfrac12}{2}\,(\dd x_1+\dd x_2),
\]
hence $\norm{K_2\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}=\frac12>\frac14$. Thus the estimate
quoted in Section~3 is genuinely tied to the coordinate-monomial setting.
\end{remark}

\section{Sharp coefficient bounds for coordinate-monomial data}

We now isolate the regime in which the centered homotopy operator carries the
factor $1/(2k)$ in the coefficient norm. For general forms, contraction by the
radial field may combine several active coefficients into a single output
coefficient. Proposition~\ref{prop:incidence-criterion} makes this obstruction
explicit. On the coordinate-monomial class no such interaction occurs, and the
optimal estimate follows directly from the homotopy formula.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp coefficient bound for the centered homotopy operator]
\label{thm:optimal-continuous-inverse}
Let $1\le k\le n$, and set $K_{n+1}:=0$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:homotopy-identity}
\dd K_k\omega+K_{k+1}\dd\omega=\omega
\end{equation}
for every $\omega\in\Omega^k_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^n)$. If, in addition,
$\omega$ is coordinate-monomial, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sharp-K-bound}
\norm{K_k\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}
\le
\frac{1}{2k}\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}.
\end{equation}
The constant $1/(2k)$ is sharp on the class of closed coordinate-monomial
$k$-forms. More precisely, if
$R:\Omega^k_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^n)\to\Omega^{k-1}_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^n)$ is linear
and satisfies $\dd R\omega=\omega$ for every closed coordinate-monomial
$k$-form $\omega$, then
\[
\sup\left\{
\frac{\norm{R\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}}{\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}}:
0\ne\omega\text{ closed coordinate-monomial}
\right\}
\ge
\frac{1}{2k}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity \eqref{eq:homotopy-identity} is Cartan's homotopy formula applied
to $H(t,x)=c+t(x-c)$. Since $H_0$ is the constant map and $H_1$ is the
identity,
\[
\omega=H_1^\ast\omega-H_0^\ast\omega
=\int_0^1\frac{\dd}{\dd t}H_t^\ast\omega\,\dd t
=\dd K_k\omega+K_{k+1}\dd\omega,
\]
with $H_0^\ast\omega=0$ for $k\ge1$.

If $\omega=f\,\dd x_I$ is coordinate-monomial, the contraction estimate gives
\[
\abs{(\iota_{x-c}\omega)(c+t(x-c))}_{\coeff,\infty}
\le \frac12\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}.
\]
Integrating against $t^{k-1}$ yields
\[
\abs{(K_k\omega)(x)}_{\coeff,\infty}
\le
\int_0^1 t^{k-1}\frac12\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}\,\dd t
=
\frac{1}{2k}\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty},
\]
and then \eqref{eq:sharp-K-bound} follows by taking the supremum over $x$.

For sharpness take $\omega_0=\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k$ on $I^k$
and regard it as a form on $I^n$ if $n>k$. Let $\eta$ be any smooth
$(k-1)$-form with $\dd\eta=\omega_0$, and put
$M:=\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}$. By Stokes' theorem,
\[
1=\int_{I^k}\omega_0=\int_{\partial I^k}\eta.
\]
With the coordinate face orientations from Definition~\ref{def:face-trace},
each of the $2k$ face integrals has absolute value at most $M$, because every
face has $(k-1)$-measure one and the pullback reads one coefficient of
$\eta$. Hence $1\le 2kM$, so $M\ge1/(2k)$. Since
$K_k\omega_0$ in \eqref{eq:explicit-minimizer} has coefficient norm
$1/(2k)$, the constant is attained.

Now let $R$ be any linear right inverse on closed coordinate-monomial forms.
Applying the preceding lower bound to $\eta=R\omega_0$ gives
\[
\norm{R\omega_0}_{\coeff,\infty}\ge\frac{1}{2k}
=\frac{1}{2k}\norm{\omega_0}_{\coeff,\infty},
\]
which proves the optimality of every such linear right inverse.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A sharp decomposition with controlled defect]
\label{thm:homotopy-decomposition}
Let $1\le k\le n-1$ and let
$\omega\in\Omega^k_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^n)$. If $\dd\omega$ is
coordinate-monomial and
\[
\eta:=K_k\omega,
\qquad
E:=K_{k+1}\dd\omega,
\]
then
\[
\omega=\dd\eta+E,
\qquad
E=K_{k+1}\dd\omega,
\]
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sharp-defect-bound}
\norm{E}_{\coeff,\infty}
\le
\frac{1}{2(k+1)}\norm{\dd\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}.
\end{equation}
The constant $1/(2(k+1))$ cannot be improved in any estimate of this form
that is required to hold uniformly for all $\omega$ with coordinate-monomial
exterior derivative.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The decomposition is exactly \eqref{eq:homotopy-identity} applied to the
$k$-form $\omega$:
\[
\omega=\dd K_k\omega+K_{k+1}\dd\omega=\dd\eta+E.
\]
Since $\dd\omega$ is coordinate-monomial, the estimate
\eqref{eq:sharp-K-bound} with degree $k+1$ gives
\eqref{eq:sharp-defect-bound}.

To see optimality, take
\[
\Omega_0:=\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_{k+1}
\]
on $I^{k+1}$ and choose any smooth $k$-form $\omega$ with
$\dd\omega=\Omega_0$, for instance $\omega=x_1\,\dd x_2\wedge\cdots
\wedge\dd x_{k+1}$. If a smaller uniform constant held for the defect term,
then it would give a primitive of $\Omega_0$ with coefficient norm strictly
less than $1/(2(k+1))$. This contradicts the Stokes lower bound in the
sharpness part of Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal-continuous-inverse}, applied in
degree $k+1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Potential reconstruction]
\label{cor:curl-reconstruction}
Let $n\ge2$, let $v=(v_1,\dots,v_n)$ be a smooth vector field on a
neighborhood of $I^n$, and set
\[
\omega:=\sum_{i=1}^n v_i\,\dd x_i .
\]
Assume that $\dd\omega$ is coordinate-monomial. Then
$U:=K_1\omega$ satisfies
\[
\norm{\omega-\dd U}_{\coeff,\infty}
\le
\frac14\norm{\dd\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}.
\]
Equivalently, under the usual identification of $1$-forms with vector fields,
\[
\norm{v-\nabla U}_{\coeff,\infty}
\le
\frac14\norm{\dd\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}.
\]
The constant $1/4$ is optimal among uniform estimates under the same
hypothesis.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{thm:homotopy-decomposition} with $k=1$. Then
\[
\omega=\dd K_1\omega+K_2\dd\omega,
\]
so $U=K_1\omega$ and $E=K_2\dd\omega$ give
\[
\omega-\dd U=E,
\qquad
\norm{E}_{\coeff,\infty}
\le
\frac14\norm{\dd\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}.
\]
For a $1$-form $\omega=\sum_i v_i\,\dd x_i$,
\[
\dd\omega=\sum_{i<j}(\partial_i v_j-\partial_j v_i)\,
\dd x_i\wedge\dd x_j,
\]
which is the coordinate curl-type two-form. Optimality is the $k=1$ case of
the sharpness statement in Proposition~\ref{thm:homotopy-decomposition}.
\end{proof}

\section{Extremal primitives and boundary readout on boxes}

We now turn to the inverse statement. The sharp lower bound in
\eqref{eq:intro-extremal-problem} is governed by the crystalline perimeter
\[
P_1(E):=\int_{\partial E}\abs{\nu_E}_1\,\dd\Haus^{n-1},
\qquad
\abs{\nu_E}_1:=\sum_{i=1}^n \abs{(\nu_E)_i}.
\]
For background on crystalline isoperimetry, the Wulff theorem, and calibrable
sets we refer to
\cite{Taylor1978,Fonseca1991,FonsecaMuller1991,DacorognaPfister1992,
FigalliZhang2022,DeMason2024} and
\cite{Strang1983,Nozawa1990,Grieser2006,Strang2010,
CasellesFaccioloMeinhardt2009,CasellesChambolleMollNovaga2008,
Leonardi2015,Parini2011}.

\begin{lemma}[Anisotropic calibration bound]
\label{lem:boundary-calibration}
Let $\Omega\subset\RR^n$ be a bounded Lipschitz domain and set
$\mathrm{vol}:=\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_n$. Define
\[
C_\infty(\Omega):=
\inf\left\{
\norm{V}_{L^\infty(\Omega;\ell^\infty)}:
V\in C^\infty(U;\RR^n),\ U\supset\overline\Omega\text{ open},\
\Div V=1\text{ on }\Omega
\right\}.
\]
Equivalently, $C_\infty(\Omega)$ is the infimum of
$\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}$ over smooth $(n-1)$-forms $\eta$ defined on a
neighborhood of $\Omega$ and satisfying $\dd\eta=\mathrm{vol}$ on $\Omega$.
Then every admissible vector field $V$ and every Lipschitz subdomain
$E\subset\Omega$ satisfy
\[
\Vol(E)\le \norm{V}_{L^\infty(\Omega;\ell^\infty)}\,P_1(E).
\]
Consequently,
\[
C_\infty(\Omega)
\ge
\sup\left\{\frac{\Vol(E)}{P_1(E)}:\ E\subset\Omega,\ E\text{ Lipschitz}\right\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The vector-field and form formulations are identified by
\[
\eta=\sum_{j=1}^n(-1)^{j-1}V_j\,
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_n.
\]
Then $\dd\eta=(\Div V)\,\mathrm{vol}$ and
$\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}=\norm{V}_{L^\infty(\Omega;\ell^\infty)}$.

If $V$ is admissible and $E\subset\Omega$ is Lipschitz, the divergence theorem
gives
\[
\Vol(E)=\int_E\Div V\,\dd x
=\int_{\partial E}V\cdot\nu_E\,\dd\Haus^{n-1}.
\]
Since $V\cdot\nu_E\le \norm{V}_{L^\infty(\Omega;\ell^\infty)}
\abs{\nu_E}_1$, we obtain
\[
\Vol(E)
\le
\norm{V}_{L^\infty(\Omega;\ell^\infty)}
\int_{\partial E}\abs{\nu_E}_1\,\dd\Haus^{n-1}
=
\norm{V}_{L^\infty(\Omega;\ell^\infty)}P_1(E).
\]
Taking the infimum over admissible $V$ and then the supremum over $E$ proves
the lower bound for $C_\infty(\Omega)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative boundary saturation on optimal domains]
\label{prop:quantitative-boundary-saturation}
Let $\Omega\subset\RR^n$ be a bounded Lipschitz domain and assume that
\[
C_\infty(\Omega)=\frac{\Vol(\Omega)}{P_1(\Omega)}.
\]
Let $V\in C^\infty(U;\RR^n)$ be admissible for $C_\infty(\Omega)$, meaning
$U\supset\overline\Omega$ is open and $\Div V=1$ on $\Omega$, and set
\[
M:=\norm{V}_{L^\infty(\Omega;\ell^\infty)}.
\]
Then the exact boundary deficit identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:boundary-deficit-identity}
\int_{\partial\Omega}
\bigl(M\abs{\nu_\Omega}_1-V\cdot\nu_\Omega\bigr)\,\dd\Haus^{n-1}
=
P_1(\Omega)\bigl(M-C_\infty(\Omega)\bigr)
\end{equation}
holds. In particular, if $M=C_\infty(\Omega)+\varepsilon$, then
\[
\norm{M\abs{\nu_\Omega}_1-V\cdot\nu_\Omega}_{L^1(\partial\Omega)}
=
\varepsilon\,P_1(\Omega).
\]
Every minimizer $V$ for $C_\infty(\Omega)$ therefore satisfies
\[
V\cdot\nu_\Omega
=C_\infty(\Omega)\abs{\nu_\Omega}_1
\quad\text{for }\Haus^{n-1}\text{-a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The integrand in \eqref{eq:boundary-deficit-identity} is nonnegative by
$V\cdot\nu_\Omega\le M\abs{\nu_\Omega}_1$. Hence its $L^1$ norm is its
integral. The divergence theorem and the optimal-domain hypothesis give
\[
\int_{\partial\Omega}V\cdot\nu_\Omega\,\dd\Haus^{n-1}
=\int_\Omega\Div V\,\dd x
=\Vol(\Omega)
=C_\infty(\Omega)P_1(\Omega).
\]
Also
\[
\int_{\partial\Omega}M\abs{\nu_\Omega}_1\,\dd\Haus^{n-1}
=M P_1(\Omega).
\]
Subtracting these two identities proves
\eqref{eq:boundary-deficit-identity}. If $M=C_\infty(\Omega)$, the
nonnegative integrand has integral zero and hence vanishes almost everywhere.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Axis-aligned boxes attain the crystalline ratio]
\label{lem:box-self-cheeger}
Let
\[
R:=\prod_{j=1}^k[0,L_j],
\qquad
m_R:=\frac{1}{2\sum_{j=1}^k L_j^{-1}},
\]
where all $L_j>0$. Then every Lipschitz subdomain $E\subset R$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:box-anisotropic-ineq}
2\left(\sum_{j=1}^k\frac1{L_j}\right)\Vol(E)\le P_1(E).
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\[
\sup\left\{\frac{\Vol(E)}{P_1(E)}:\ E\subset R,\ E\ {\rm Lipschitz}\right\}
=
\frac{\Vol(R)}{P_1(R)}
=m_R,
\]
and
\[
C_\infty(R)=m_R.
\]
Moreover, the affine vector field
\[
V_R(x):=
2m_R\sum_{j=1}^k
\frac{x_j-L_j/2}{L_j}\,\partial_{x_j}
\]
is admissible and satisfies
\[
\norm{V_R}_{L^\infty(R;\ell^\infty)}=m_R.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $j$. By slicing $E$ by lines parallel to the $x_j$-axis and applying the
one-dimensional inequality
\[
P(A; \RR)\ge \frac{2}{L_j}\abs{A}
\qquad
(A\subset[0,L_j]),
\]
we obtain
\[
\int_{\partial E}\abs{(\nu_E)_j}\,\dd\Haus^{k-1}
\ge
\frac{2}{L_j}\Vol(E).
\]
Summing over $j$ gives \eqref{eq:box-anisotropic-ineq}. Equality holds for
$E=R$, since
\[
P_1(R)=2\Vol(R)\sum_{j=1}^k L_j^{-1}.
\]
This proves the asserted value of the supremum.

The displayed affine field satisfies
\[
\Div V_R=
2m_R\sum_{j=1}^k L_j^{-1}=1,
\]
so it is admissible. Its $j$th component ranges between $-m_R$ and $m_R$ on
$R$, whence
$\norm{V_R}_{L^\infty(R;\ell^\infty)}=m_R$. Thus $C_\infty(R)\le m_R$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary-calibration} and the already computed supremum give
$C_\infty(R)\ge m_R$, so $C_\infty(R)=m_R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative boundary readout on axis-aligned boxes]
\label{thm:box-boundary-stability}
Let
\[
R:=\prod_{j=1}^k[0,L_j],
\qquad
\omega_R:=\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k,
\qquad
m_R:=\frac{1}{2\sum_{j=1}^kL_j^{-1}},
\]
and define
\[
\eta_R:=
2m_R\sum_{j=1}^k(-1)^{j-1}
\frac{x_j-L_j/2}{L_j}\,
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}
\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k .
\]
Then $\dd\eta_R=\omega_R$ and
$\norm{\eta_R}_{\coeff,\infty}=m_R$. Let
$\eta\in\Omega^{k-1}_{\mathrm{sm}}(R)$ satisfy $\dd\eta=\omega_R$, and set
$M:=\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}$. For
\[
F_{j,\lambda}:=\{x\in R:\ x_j=\lambda\},
\qquad
\lambda\in\{0,L_j\},
\]
write
\[
\iota_{j,\lambda}^\ast\eta
=
f_{j,\lambda}\,
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k
\]
with respect to the coordinate orientation on the face, and set
\[
s_{j,L_j}:=(-1)^{j-1},
\qquad
s_{j,0}:=-(-1)^{j-1}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:box-boundary-deficit}
\sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{\lambda\in\{0,L_j\}}
\int_{F_{j,\lambda}}\bigl(M-s_{j,\lambda}f_{j,\lambda}\bigr)
\,\dd\Haus^{k-1}
=
P_1(R)(M-m_R).
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:box-boundary-trace-stability}
\sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{\lambda\in\{0,L_j\}}
\norm{\iota_{j,\lambda}^\ast(\eta-\eta_R)}_{L^1(F_{j,\lambda};\coeff)}
\le
2P_1(R)(M-m_R).
\end{equation}
In particular, $M\ge m_R$, and every minimizer satisfies
\[
\iota_{j,\lambda}^\ast\eta
=
s_{j,\lambda}m_R\,
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k
\]
on every face.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The formula for $\eta_R$ is the form corresponding to the affine vector field
$V_R$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:box-self-cheeger} under the identification
\[
\eta=\sum_{j=1}^k(-1)^{j-1}V_j\,
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k.
\]
Thus $\dd\eta_R=(\Div V_R)\omega_R=\omega_R$ and
$\norm{\eta_R}_{\coeff,\infty}=\norm{V_R}_{L^\infty(R;\ell^\infty)}=m_R$.

Write the given $\eta$ in the same way, with associated vector field $V$.
Then $\Div V=1$ on $R$ and $M=\norm{V}_{L^\infty(R;\ell^\infty)}$.
On $F_{j,L_j}$, the outward normal is $e_j$ and
$V\cdot\nu=V_j=s_{j,L_j}f_{j,L_j}$; on $F_{j,0}$, the outward normal is
$-e_j$ and again $V\cdot\nu=s_{j,0}f_{j,0}$. Since
$\abs{\nu}_1=1$ on every face, Proposition~\ref{prop:quantitative-boundary-saturation}
applied to $\Omega=R$ gives \eqref{eq:box-boundary-deficit}.

For the affine minimizer one has
\[
\iota_{j,\lambda}^\ast\eta_R
=s_{j,\lambda}m_R\,
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k.
\]
Pointwise on each face,
\[
\abs{f_{j,\lambda}-s_{j,\lambda}m_R}
=\abs{s_{j,\lambda}f_{j,\lambda}-m_R}
\le
\bigl(M-s_{j,\lambda}f_{j,\lambda}\bigr)+(M-m_R),
\]
because $s_{j,\lambda}f_{j,\lambda}\le\abs{f_{j,\lambda}}\le M$ and
$M\ge m_R$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:box-self-cheeger}. Integrating over all faces and
using \eqref{eq:box-boundary-deficit} yields
\eqref{eq:box-boundary-trace-stability}. If $M=m_R$, the right-hand side
vanishes, giving the stated face trace of every minimizer.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Face trace]
\label{def:face-trace}
For $\eta\in\Omega^{k-1}_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^k)$ and
$1\le j\le k$, $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$, set
\[
F_{j,\varepsilon}:=\{x\in I^k:\ x_j=\varepsilon\},
\qquad
\iota_{j,\varepsilon}:F_{j,\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow I^k .
\]
The full codimension-one face trace is the ordered tuple
\[
\Tr_{\partial I^k}^{\mathrm{face}}(\eta)
:=
\bigl(\iota_{j,\varepsilon}^\ast\eta\bigr)_{
1\le j\le k,\ \varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}.
\]
On $F_{j,\varepsilon}$ we use the coordinate orientation
\[
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k.
\]
The outward-oriented boundary sign is
\[
s_{j,1}:=(-1)^{j-1},
\qquad
s_{j,0}:=-(-1)^{j-1},
\]
so that
\[
\partial I^k=
\sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}
s_{j,\varepsilon}F_{j,\varepsilon}
\]
with the above coordinate face orientations.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Boundary determination on the unit cube]
\label{thm:boundary-rigidity}
Let $\eta\in\Omega^{k-1}_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^k)$ satisfy
$\dd\eta=\omega_0:=\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k$ and
$\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}=1/(2k)$. Then, for every
$1\le j\le k$ and $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$,
\[
\iota_{j,\varepsilon}^\ast\eta
=
(-1)^{j-1}\frac{\varepsilon-\frac12}{k}\,
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k .
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:box-boundary-stability} with $R=I^k$. Then
$m_R=1/(2k)$. Since $\eta$ is a minimizer, the final assertion of the theorem
gives
\[
\iota_{j,\varepsilon}^\ast\eta
=s_{j,\varepsilon}\frac{1}{2k}\,
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k .
\]
The identity
$s_{j,\varepsilon}/(2k)=(-1)^{j-1}(\varepsilon-\frac12)/k$ is exactly the
displayed formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The centered radial primitive is extremal]
\label{cor:explicit-minimizer}
For $\omega_0=\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k$ on $I^k$, the centered
radial primitive $K_k\omega_0$ equals the affine minimizer $\eta_R$ from
Theorem~\ref{thm:box-boundary-stability} with $R=I^k$. Hence
\[
\dd K_k\omega_0=\omega_0,
\qquad
\norm{K_k\omega_0}_{\coeff,\infty}=\frac{1}{2k},
\]
and $K_k\omega_0$ has the canonical face trace in
Corollary~\ref{thm:boundary-rigidity}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $R=I^k$ one has $m_R=1/(2k)$, so the formula for $\eta_R$ becomes
\[
\eta_R
=
\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^k(-1)^{j-1}\left(x_j-\frac12\right)
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k,
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:explicit-minimizer}. The norm, exterior derivative,
and trace statements follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:box-boundary-stability} and
Corollary~\ref{thm:boundary-rigidity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sliced boundary readout for constant coordinate-monomial data]
\label{thm:sliced-active-readout}
Let
\[
Q:=\prod_{a=1}^n[0,L_a],
\qquad
I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_k\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\},
\qquad
\omega_I:=\dd x_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_{i_k},
\]
and set
\[
m_I:=\frac{1}{2\sum_{r=1}^kL_{i_r}^{-1}},
\qquad
Q_{I^c}:=\prod_{a\notin I}[0,L_a].
\]
Let $\eta\in\Omega^{k-1}_{\mathrm{sm}}(Q)$ satisfy $\dd\eta=\omega_I$, and
put $M:=\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}$. For
$1\le r\le k$ and $\lambda\in\{0,L_{i_r}\}$ define the active face
\[
G_{r,\lambda}:=\{x\in Q:\ x_{i_r}=\lambda\}
\]
and let $f_{r,\lambda}$ be the coefficient of
$\dd x_I\setminus\dd x_{i_r}$ in $\iota_{r,\lambda}^\ast\eta$, that is,
\[
\coeff_{I\setminus\{i_r\}}\bigl(\iota_{r,\lambda}^\ast\eta\bigr)
=f_{r,\lambda}.
\]
With
\[
s_{r,L_{i_r}}:=(-1)^{r-1},
\qquad
s_{r,0}:=-(-1)^{r-1},
\]
one has $M\ge m_I$ and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sliced-active-stability}
\sum_{r=1}^k\sum_{\lambda\in\{0,L_{i_r}\}}
\int_{G_{r,\lambda}}
\abs{f_{r,\lambda}-s_{r,\lambda}m_I}\,\dd\Haus^{n-1}
\le
2\,\Vol(Q_{I^c})\,P_1(Q_I)(M-m_I),
\end{equation}
where $Q_I:=\prod_{r=1}^k[0,L_{i_r}]$. In particular, if $M=m_I$, then the
active coefficient on every active face is fixed by
\[
f_{r,\lambda}=s_{r,\lambda}m_I
\qquad
\text{for all }r,\lambda.
\]
No assertion is made about face components involving inactive differentials.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix the inactive variables $z=(x_a)_{a\notin I}\in Q_{I^c}$ and let
\[
\sigma_z:Q_I\to Q
\]
be the active slice map. Since $\dd\eta=\omega_I$, the pulled-back
$(k-1)$-form $\sigma_z^\ast\eta$ satisfies
\[
\dd(\sigma_z^\ast\eta)=\dd x_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_{i_k}
\]
on the active box $Q_I$. Its coefficient norm is at most $M$. Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:box-boundary-stability} on this slice gives
\[
\sum_{r=1}^k\sum_{\lambda\in\{0,L_{i_r}\}}
\int_{G_{r,\lambda}\cap\sigma_z(Q_I)}
\abs{f_{r,\lambda}-s_{r,\lambda}m_I}\,\dd\Haus^{k-1}
\le
2P_1(Q_I)(M-m_I).
\]
The same slice theorem also gives $M\ge m_I$. Integrating the displayed
inequality over $z\in Q_{I^c}$ and using Fubini proves
\eqref{eq:sliced-active-stability}. Pullback to an active slice kills every
component containing an inactive differential, so the argument controls only
the coefficient of $\dd x_{I\setminus\{i_r\}}$ on the active faces.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
When $n>k$, Theorem~\ref{thm:sliced-active-readout} determines only the active
coefficient $\coeff_{I\setminus\{i_r\}}$ on the active faces
$G_{r,\varepsilon}$. Components involving inactive differentials are invisible
to the $k$-dimensional slice problem. The theorem therefore identifies the
maximal boundary information that is rigid under the present inverse data.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
For $k\ge 2$, Corollary~\ref{thm:boundary-rigidity} yields rigidity of the
boundary trace, but not uniqueness in the interior. If
$\psi$ is a smooth $(k-2)$-form compactly supported in the interior of $I^k$
and $\eta_\varepsilon:=\eta_\ast+\varepsilon\,\dd\psi$, then
$\dd\eta_\varepsilon=\omega_0$ for every $\varepsilon$,
$\Tr_{\partial I^k}(\eta_\varepsilon)=\Tr_{\partial I^k}(\eta_\ast)$, and
$\norm{\eta_\varepsilon}_{\coeff,\infty}=1/(2k)$ for all sufficiently small
$|\varepsilon|$. Indeed, $\dd\psi$ vanishes on a neighborhood of
$\partial I^k$, where $\eta_\ast$ attains the value $1/(2k)$ in the
coefficient norm, whereas $\abs{\eta_\ast}_{\coeff,\infty}<1/(2k)$ on the
compact support of $\dd\psi$.
\end{remark}

\section{A cubical Whitney counterpart}

We conclude with the cubical counterpart on a single cell. First, the oriented
coboundary on one top-dimensional cube gives the exact discrete analogue of
the boundary readout theorem on the unit cube. The Whitney construction is then
obtained by composing the continuous homotopy operator with the classical
Whitney map and the integration map. Together these give the one-cell discrete
versions of the boundary-determination and decomposition mechanisms; compare
\cite{Whitney1957,Dodziuk1976,Dupont1976,DupontBook1978,Albert2020,
ArnoldFalkWinther2010,ArnoldAwanou2014,KaczynskiMischaikowMrozek2004}.

Let $C^k(I^n;\RR)$ be the space of cubical $k$-cochains on the standard cell
decomposition of $I^n$, with coboundary $d$. For $\theta\in C^k(I^n;\RR)$ we
set
\[
\norm{\theta}_{\cell,\infty}:=
\max_{\sigma^{(k)}}\abs{\theta(\sigma)},
\]
where the maximum is taken over all oriented $k$-cells.

\begin{theorem}[Cubical boundary readout on one top cell]
\label{thm:cubical-boundary-readout}
Let $k\ge 1$, and let $\Theta\in C^k(I^k;\RR)$ be the top cochain with value
$1$ on the
oriented cube $I^k$. For $1\le j\le k$ and $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$, let
$F_{j,\varepsilon}:=\{x_j=\varepsilon\}$ be oriented by
$\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k$, and set
\[
s_{j,1}:=(-1)^{j-1},
\qquad
s_{j,0}:=-(-1)^{j-1}.
\]
Define the canonical boundary cochain $\eta_{\cell}\in C^{k-1}(I^k;\RR)$ by
\[
\eta_{\cell}(F_{j,\varepsilon})
=
\frac{s_{j,\varepsilon}}{2k}
\qquad
(1\le j\le k,\ \varepsilon\in\{0,1\}).
\]
Then $d\eta_{\cell}=\Theta$ and
$\norm{\eta_{\cell}}_{\cell,\infty}=1/(2k)$. Moreover, if
$\eta\in C^{k-1}(I^k;\RR)$ satisfies $d\eta=\Theta$ and
$M:=\norm{\eta}_{\cell,\infty}$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cubical-deficit-identity}
\sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}
\left(M-s_{j,\varepsilon}\eta(F_{j,\varepsilon})\right)
=
2k\left(M-\frac{1}{2k}\right),
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cubical-boundary-stability}
\sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}
\abs{\eta(F_{j,\varepsilon})-\eta_{\cell}(F_{j,\varepsilon})}
\le
4k\left(M-\frac{1}{2k}\right).
\end{equation}
In particular, $M\ge 1/(2k)$, the constant $1/(2k)$ is sharp, and every
minimizing cochain with $d\eta=\Theta$ has the canonical boundary values
\[
\eta(F_{j,\varepsilon})=\eta_{\cell}(F_{j,\varepsilon})
\qquad
\text{for all }j,\varepsilon.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The boundary of the oriented top cube is
\[
\partial I^k
=
\sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}
s_{j,\varepsilon}F_{j,\varepsilon}.
\]
Therefore
\[
d\eta_{\cell}(I^k)
=
\eta_{\cell}(\partial I^k)
=
\sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}
s_{j,\varepsilon}\frac{s_{j,\varepsilon}}{2k}
=1,
\]
so $d\eta_{\cell}=\Theta$. Its cell norm is plainly $1/(2k)$.

Now let $d\eta=\Theta$. Since $\eta(\partial I^k)=1$, we have
\[
\sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}
s_{j,\varepsilon}\eta(F_{j,\varepsilon})=1.
\]
Subtracting this identity from the sum of $2k$ copies of $M$ gives
\eqref{eq:cubical-deficit-identity}. Each summand on the left of
\eqref{eq:cubical-deficit-identity} is nonnegative because
$s_{j,\varepsilon}\eta(F_{j,\varepsilon})\le
\abs{\eta(F_{j,\varepsilon})}\le M$. Hence $M\ge 1/(2k)$.

For each face,
\[
\abs{\eta(F_{j,\varepsilon})-\eta_{\cell}(F_{j,\varepsilon})}
=
\abs{s_{j,\varepsilon}\eta(F_{j,\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{2k}}
\le
\left(M-s_{j,\varepsilon}\eta(F_{j,\varepsilon})\right)
+\left(M-\frac{1}{2k}\right).
\]
Summing over all $2k$ faces and using
\eqref{eq:cubical-deficit-identity} gives
\eqref{eq:cubical-boundary-stability}. If $M=1/(2k)$, then the right-hand side
of \eqref{eq:cubical-boundary-stability} is zero, which forces the stated
canonical boundary values. Since $\eta_{\cell}$ itself attains this norm, the
constant is sharp.
\end{proof}

For $I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_k\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}$ and
$\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{I^c}$, let
\[
\sigma_{I,\varepsilon}
:=
\{x\in I^n:\ x_j=\varepsilon_j \text{ for every } j\notin I\}
\]
be the oriented $k$-cell parallel to
$\dd x_I=\dd x_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge \dd x_{i_k}$, and let
$\delta_{I,\varepsilon}\in C^k(I^n;\RR)$ be the dual basis cochain. Define
\[
\phi_0(t):=1-t,
\qquad
\phi_1(t):=t,
\qquad
\lambda_{I,\varepsilon}(x):=
\prod_{j\notin I}\phi_{\varepsilon_j}(x_j).
\]
The cubical Whitney map is defined by
\[
W(\delta_{I,\varepsilon}):=
\lambda_{I,\varepsilon}(x)\,\dd x_I
\]
and extended linearly. For a smooth $k$-form $\alpha$ on a neighborhood of
$I^n$, the integration map is
\[
\mathcal I(\alpha)(\sigma):=
\int_\sigma \iota_\sigma^\ast\alpha.
\]
Since each $\lambda_{I,\varepsilon}$ is a multilinear polynomial on the whole
cube, $W\theta$ is a globally smooth differential form on $I^n$ for every
cochain $\theta$. Thus the continuous homotopy operator $K_j$ applies without
any regularity issue in the present one-cube setting. The next two
propositions collect the elementary identities needed for the discrete
argument.

\begin{proposition}[Whitney formulas and norm-one bounds]
\label{prop:cubical-whitney}
The cubical Whitney map and integration map satisfy
\[
\mathcal I W=\mathrm{id}_{C^\bullet(I^n;\RR)},
\qquad
\dd W\theta=W(d\theta)
\]
for every cubical cochain $\theta$ on the standard cell decomposition of
$I^n$. Moreover
\[
\norm{W\theta}_{\coeff,\infty}\le\norm{\theta}_{\cell,\infty},
\qquad
\norm{\mathcal I\alpha}_{\cell,\infty}\le\norm{\alpha}_{\coeff,\infty}
\]
for every cochain $\theta$ and every smooth form $\alpha$ on a neighborhood
of $I^n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to check basis cochains. If
$\delta_{I,\varepsilon}$ is dual to the oriented cell
$\sigma_{I,\varepsilon}$, then
\[
W(\delta_{I,\varepsilon})
=\lambda_{I,\varepsilon}\,\dd x_I,
\qquad
\lambda_{I,\varepsilon}=\prod_{a\notin I}\phi_{\varepsilon_a}(x_a).
\]
The restriction of this form to $\sigma_{I,\varepsilon}$ is $\dd x_I$, while
its restriction to any other parallel $I$-cell contains a factor
$\phi_{\varepsilon_a}(1-\varepsilon_a)=0$ for some inactive coordinate.
It restricts to zero on cells with a different tangent coordinate set. Hence
$\mathcal I W\delta_{I,\varepsilon}=\delta_{I,\varepsilon}$.

For the cochain map identity, differentiate
\[
\lambda_{I,\varepsilon}\,\dd x_I.
\]
Only inactive coordinates contribute:
\[
\dd(\lambda_{I,\varepsilon}\,\dd x_I)
=
\sum_{a\notin I}
(\partial_a\phi_{\varepsilon_a})
\prod_{b\notin I,\ b\ne a}\phi_{\varepsilon_b}\,
\dd x_a\wedge\dd x_I .
\]
After reordering $\dd x_a\wedge\dd x_I$ into increasing order, the sign is
exactly the cubical incidence sign of the two $(\abs I+1)$-cells obtained by
freeing the coordinate $a$. This is the standard oriented cubical coboundary,
so $\dd W=W d$ by linearity.

Finally, $0\le\lambda_{I,\varepsilon}\le1$ on $I^n$, which gives
$\norm{W\theta}_{\coeff,\infty}\le\norm{\theta}_{\cell,\infty}$ because for a
fixed coefficient $\dd x_I$ the functions $\lambda_{I,\varepsilon}$ form the
multilinear partition of unity over the inactive vertices. For integration,
each coordinate cell has volume one, and the integral of a coefficient over an
oriented cell is bounded by the coefficient supremum. Thus
$\norm{\mathcal I\alpha}_{\cell,\infty}\le\norm{\alpha}_{\coeff,\infty}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Whitney coordinate-monomial cochain]
\label{def:whitney-decomposable}
A cochain $\theta\in C^k(I^n;\RR)$ is \emph{Whitney coordinate-monomial} if
either $\theta=0$, or there is a single ordered coordinate set
$I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_k\}$ such that
\[
\theta=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{I^c}}
\theta_{I,\varepsilon}\,\delta_{I,\varepsilon}.
\]
Equivalently, all nonzero $k$-cell coefficients of $\theta$ are supported on
$k$-cells parallel to the same coordinate $k$-plane.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Characterization of the Whitney coordinate-monomial class]
\label{prop:whitney-characterization}
For $\theta\in C^k(I^n;\RR)$, the following are equivalent.
\[
\text{\rm(i) }\theta\text{ is Whitney coordinate-monomial.}
\]
\[
\text{\rm(ii) }W\theta\text{ is a coordinate-monomial smooth }k\text{-form.}
\]
More explicitly, if $\theta$ is supported on cells parallel to a fixed
$I$, then
\[
W\theta=
\left(
\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{I^c}}
\theta_{I,\varepsilon}\lambda_{I,\varepsilon}(x)
\right)\dd x_I.
\]
Conversely, if nonzero coefficients of $\theta$ occur for two different
coordinate sets $I\ne J$, then $W\theta$ has nonzero coefficients in both
$\dd x_I$ and $\dd x_J$ directions and is not coordinate-monomial.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $\theta$ is supported on a fixed coordinate set $I$, the displayed formula
follows directly from the definition of $W$, and the result is a single
coefficient multiplying $\dd x_I$. Hence $W\theta$ is coordinate-monomial.

Conversely, suppose that $\theta$ has a nonzero coefficient
$\theta_{I,\varepsilon}$ and a nonzero coefficient $\theta_{J,\varepsilon'}$
with $I\ne J$. The coefficient of $\dd x_I$ in $W\theta$ is a multilinear
polynomial in the inactive variables for $I$ whose Bernstein coefficient
$\theta_{I,\varepsilon}$ is nonzero. Such a polynomial cannot vanish
identically, since the functions
$\{\lambda_{I,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{I^c}}$ are linearly
independent. The same applies to the $\dd x_J$ coefficient. Thus $W\theta$
has at least two nonzero coordinate directions and cannot be
coordinate-monomial. This proves the equivalence.
\end{proof}
