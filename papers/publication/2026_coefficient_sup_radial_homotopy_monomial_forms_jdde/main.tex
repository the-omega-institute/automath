\begin{abstract}
We prove an anisotropic equality-and-stability theorem for
coefficient-$L^\infty$ primitives of the top form on a rectangular box.  For
$R=\prod_{j=1}^k[0,L_j]$ we compute the exact minimum
$m_R=(2\sum_{j=1}^kL_j^{-1})^{-1}$ among smooth $(k-1)$-forms $\eta$ satisfying
$\dd\eta=\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k$, exhibit the centered affine
minimizer $\eta_R$, and prove that every exact minimizer has the same signed
trace as $\eta_R$ on each coordinate face.  Quantitatively, the total $L^1$
face-trace error of any primitive is bounded linearly by
$\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}-m_R$, with the anisotropic perimeter as calibration
scale; the trace error also satisfies capped mean-zero saturation-deficit
conditions, and all smooth minimizers are exactly $\eta_R$ plus closed
zero-face-trace perturbations that remain in the same coefficient box.  The
finite-dimensional one-cell cubical analogue has an exact stability profile at
every norm deficit.  It is a finite maximum of explicit piecewise-linear
functions, its equality cases are attained, and its small-deficit slope on the
unit $k$-cube is $4k-2$.  Thus sharp cell-norm saturation for a primitive of
the top cochain fixes all oriented boundary-face values, while the entire
near-saturation regime is quantified optimally.  The midpoint radial homotopy estimate for
coordinate-monomial forms, its incidence obstruction, slicing, and the Whitney
transfer are used as supporting tools and local consequences, not as a general
homotopy-operator theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

This paper studies the sharp coefficient-$L^\infty$ primitive problem for the
constant top form on an axis-aligned rectangular box.  If
$R=\prod_{j=1}^k[0,L_j]$ and $\omega_R=\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k$, we
ask for the smallest possible coefficient norm of a smooth $(k-1)$-form
$\eta$ with $\dd\eta=\omega_R$, and then for the equality and near-equality
structure of the minimizers.  The answer is a box max-flow/min-cut statement in
anisotropic form: the sharp value is
$m_R=(2\sum_jL_j^{-1})^{-1}$, the centered affine primitive attains it, and
the same calibration forces the signed boundary trace of every minimizer.  If a
primitive is only nearly minimizing, its face trace remains linearly close in
$L^1$ to the canonical affine trace, with the norm gap
$\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}-m_R$ as the deficit.

This local equality/stability problem is motivated by discretizations in which
a cellwise primitive is controlled in an interior norm while the quantity
exchanged across interfaces is a boundary trace.  The box geometry is simple
enough for the sharp constant to be computed explicitly, but it is still rich
enough to exhibit trace rigidity, a quantitative stability estimate, and the
precise zero-trace kernel left after the calibration has fixed the face data.
Any inverse-problem interpretation is therefore secondary: the main object is
the anisotropic coefficient-$L^\infty$ equality case and its stability.

Let
\[
I^k=[0,1]^k,
\qquad
\omega_0:=\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge \dd x_k,
\]
and consider
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intro-extremal-problem}
m_k:=
\inf\left\{
\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}:\ \eta\in\Omega^{k-1}_{\mathrm{sm}}(I^k),\
\dd\eta=\omega_0
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Here $\norm{\cdot}_{\coeff,\infty}$ denotes the supremum norm of the coordinate
coefficients. If the classical radial homotopy operator is centered at
$c=(1/2,\dots,1/2)$, one obtains the explicit primitive
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intro-explicit-minimizer}
\eta_\ast
=
\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^k (-1)^{j-1}\left(x_j-\frac12\right)
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge \dd x_k,
\end{equation}
and therefore $m_k\le 1/(2k)$. The main point of the paper is that this upper
bound is exact and that the corresponding equality and near-equality regimes
determine the boundary trace.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Main results.}
The first-order theorem chain is the rectangular-box calibration and its
boundary consequences.  On every axis-aligned box
$R=\prod_{j=1}^k[0,L_j]$ with positive side lengths we compute the exact
extremal value
\[
m(R)=\frac{1}{2\sum_{j=1}^k L_j^{-1}}
\]
and construct an affine primitive $\eta_R$ attaining it. If $\eta$ is any
smooth primitive of $\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x_k$ on $R$, then
\[
\sum_{F\subset\partial R}
\norm{\Tr_F(\eta-\eta_R)}_{L^1(F;\coeff)}
\le
2P_1(R)\bigl(\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}-m(R)\bigr).
\]
Here the sum extends over the codimension-one faces of $R$, $\Tr_F$ denotes
the pullback to the face $F$, and $P_1(R)$ is the anisotropic $\ell_1$
perimeter. In particular, every minimizer has the canonical trace on each face,
while near-minimizers determine that trace with linear stability.

The same calibration gives the trace-kernel classification of exact
minimizers: after the affine trace is fixed, the only remaining freedom is a
closed zero-face-trace perturbation that stays inside the coefficient box.  For
nonzero deficit we prove the sharp necessary boundary-error conditions seen by
this calibration: the signed trace error is capped, has zero mean, factors
through a nonnegative saturation-deficit density, and has the resulting linear
variation budget.  We do not use these necessary conditions as a stand-alone
realization theorem for arbitrary prescribed boundary errors.

The midpoint-centered homotopy operator enters as a supporting computation.  We
identify the coordinate-monomial class on which it carries the sharp coefficient
bound
\[
\norm{K_k\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}\le \frac{1}{2k}\norm{\omega}_{\coeff,\infty}.
\]
This holds for coordinate-monomial forms $\omega=f\,\dd x_I$; we also record
the precise incidence theorem for $K_k$ which explains when the same factor
extends to a prescribed family of coordinate orientations.
On the unit cube the box theorem gives the explicit boundary formula
\[
\iota_{j,\varepsilon}^\ast\eta
=
(-1)^{j-1}\frac{\varepsilon-\frac12}{k}\,
\dd x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\dd x_j}\wedge\cdots\wedge \dd x_k,
\qquad
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}.
\]
We also use the sliced readout mechanism for constant coordinate-monomial
data on higher-dimensional boxes. The sliced statement is deliberately an
active-coefficient theorem: components involving inactive differentials are
not controlled, and a sharp invisibility result below shows that this loss is
not an artifact of the proof. Under the weaker
hypothesis that $\dd\omega$ is coordinate-monomial, the homotopy identity also
gives the sharp decomposition
\[
\omega=\dd\eta+E,
\qquad
\norm{E}_{\coeff,\infty}\le \frac{1}{2(k+1)}\norm{\dd\omega}_{\coeff,\infty},
\]
again with sharp constant. We conclude with a one-cell cubical boundary
readout theorem. In contrast to the continuous boundary measure, the cubical
boundary consists of finitely many weighted atoms. This permits an exact
stability profile, including all phase transitions and equality cases, rather
than only a linear upper bound. A Whitney analogue of the decomposition
estimate shows that the homotopy structure also persists at the level of
cubical cochains.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Relation with earlier work.}
The load-bearing comparison is with anisotropic perimeter, Cheeger-ratio,
max-flow/min-cut, and calibration ideas.  The quotient $\Vol(E)/P_1(E)$ below
is the crystalline analogue of the Cheeger ratio; compare
\cite{Cheeger1970,Leonardi2015,Parini2011}.  Related sharp constants for
scalar Poincar\'e inequalities on convex domains were studied in
\cite{PayneWeinberger1960,AcostaDuran2004}.  For crystalline isoperimetry,
Wulff shapes, calibrable sets, and rigidity phenomena in related Cheeger-type
problems we refer to
\cite{Taylor1978,Fonseca1991,FonsecaMuller1991,DacorognaPfister1992,
FigalliZhang2022,DeMason2024,Strang1983,Nozawa1990,Grieser2006,Strang2010,
CasellesFaccioloMeinhardt2009,CasellesChambolleMollNovaga2008,Leonardi2015,
CasellesChambolleNovaga2007,CasellesChambolleNovaga2011}.

The homotopy formula used here is classical; see, for instance,
\cite[section~3]{BottTu1982} and
\cite{Whitney1957,Dodziuk1976,Dupont1976,DupontBook1978,Albert2020,
ArnoldFalkWinther2010,ArnoldAwanou2014}. For bounded right inverses on more
general domains we refer to
\cite{Bogovskii1979,IwaniecLutoborski1993,CostabelMcIntosh2010,
GuzmanSalgado2021,ChaumontFreletLichtVohralik2025}. Endpoint primitive
estimates for differential forms are also connected to $L^1$-Poincar\'e and
$L^\infty$ primitive inequalities, including work of Baldi--Franchi--Pansu
\cite{BaldiFranchiPansu2019,BaldiFranchiPansu2020} and
Gol'dshtein--Dubrovskiy~\cite{GoldshteinDubrovskiy2009}. Our contribution is
not a new homotopy formula or a new calibration principle.  Rather, it is the
use of the rectangular-box calibration to obtain a sharp
coefficient-$L^\infty$ equality case, boundary trace rigidity, linear trace
stability, the minimizer kernel, and the corresponding one-cell cubical
statement.  From the viewpoint of geometric discretization, the box theorem may
be read as a local statement on when a sharp interior certificate fixes the
boundary data.

As a secondary interpretation, the result is complementary to the usual
coefficient-recovery setting of Calder\'on type; compare
\cite{Calderon1980,Uhlmann2009}. Inverse problems for differential forms have
been studied directly by Krupchyk--Lassas--Uhlmann
\cite{KrupchykLassasUhlmann2011}, which is the closer comparison point for the
present boundary-trace-from-interior-certificate problem. Instead of recovering
an interior coefficient from a boundary measurement map, we recover boundary
traces from sharp or near-sharp interior norm saturation.

\subsection{Theorem-by-theorem structure}

The primary contribution is the rectangular-box boundary trace
rigidity/stability chain in Section~4. The central result,
Theorem~\ref{thm:box-boundary-stability}, computes the sharp value
$m_R=(2\sum_jL_j^{-1})^{-1}$, exhibits the affine minimizer, proves the exact
signed face-trace deficit identity, and derives the linear $L^1$ trace-stability
estimate.  Theorem~\ref{thm:box-minimizer-structure} then identifies the
trace-kernel skeleton of all smooth minimizers and near-minimizers, while the
unit-cube corollaries state the corresponding rigidity and stability constants
in the equal-side case.  Theorem~\ref{thm:cubical-boundary-readout} is the
finite-dimensional one-cell analogue: sharp cochain norm saturation fixes every
oriented boundary face value. It also computes the exact weighted trace-error
profile for every norm deficit, constructs extremizers, and identifies the
improved sharp small-deficit constant.

Sections~2--3 are preparatory for that chain.  The coordinate-monomial
definitions and the centered homotopy operator provide the incidence
obstruction, the sharp supporting estimate, and the controlled-defect
decomposition needed to compare the continuous and one-cell settings; these
results are not presented as a general homotopy-operator theory.  The later
Whitney material is likewise consequential: it transfers the one-cell cubical
statement through the classical Whitney map and records only the local identities
needed for the one-cell counterpart.

In the discrete section, Proposition~\ref{prop:cubical-whitney} records
standard Whitney-form identities on one cell, now written in a cubical rather
than simplicial notation and consistent with the classical Whitney--Dupont
picture and later cubical, mimetic, and polytopal de Rham constructions
\cite{Whitney1957,Dupont1976,ArnoldAwanou2014,BuffaChristiansen2007,Gillette2016,DiPietroDroniou2020}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:cubical-boundary-readout}
is the finite-dimensional counterpart of the unit-cube boundary readout
theorem: sharp saturation of the cell norm for a primitive of the top cochain
fixes every oriented boundary-face value, and the atomic face structure yields
an exact stability profile beyond the equality case. The paper-specific Whitney
definitions are Definition~\ref{def:whitney-decomposable} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:whitney-characterization}, which single out the Whitney
coordinate-monomial class on which the sharp estimate survives. With that class
in place, Proposition~\ref{prop:discrete-stokes-poincare} is the discrete
counterpart of the continuous sharp decomposition theorem, retaining the same
optimal factor $(2(k+1))^{-1}$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:hypercube-gradient} is
its one-cochain specialization with the optimal $1/4$ remainder bound.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Motivation from dynamics and differential equations.}
The estimates below can be used as local cell estimates for evolution equations whose
unknowns are differential forms or cubical cochains and whose inter-cell
coupling is through face traces. Examples include semi-discrete exterior
calculus formulations of transport, diffusion, and balance laws in which a
cellwise primitive or potential is controlled by an interior norm while the
quantity exchanged with neighboring cells is the trace on a common face. In such
systems, an interior primitive bound can serve as a local stability certificate
for the boundary flux exchanged between cells: equality identifies the canonical
flux, and a small saturation gap bounds the trace error. Corollary~\ref{cor:local-cell-interface-stability}
makes this interpretation precise for every bounded face functional on a single
rectangular cell. Section~5 gives the one-cell cochain form of the same local
mechanism. No global well-posedness, convergence, or dynamics theorem is claimed
here; on a full cubical complex one also needs compatibility of the local
homotopies and interface unknowns across adjacent cells.

\section*{Conclusion}

We have isolated a sharp coefficient-$L^\infty$ equality and stability theorem
for primitives of the top form on rectangular boxes. The sharp case is governed
by the anisotropic perimeter calibration, which yields the boundary deficit
identity, boundary trace rigidity, and the corresponding trace-stability
estimate. The same structure identifies the minimizer kernel and, through the
coordinate-monomial homotopy and slicing tools, gives the sliced
active-coordinate consequence with inactive face components left invisible. It
also has a one-cell cubical counterpart through Whitney integration and the
cubical coboundary. On that finite boundary the atomic face weights determine
the exact stability profile at every deficit, including its phase transitions
and attained equality cases; in particular, the unit-cube small-deficit
constant is $4k-2$.
