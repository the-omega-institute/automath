\subsection{Optimality of the continuous trace-stability constant}

The factor in Corollary~\ref{cor:box-delta-trace-stability} is not a loss in
the passage from the exact signed deficit identity to total variation.  In
dimension at least two, divergence-free boundary layers can concentrate the
negative part of the trace error on sets whose relative face measure tends to
zero.  The required extension is nonlinear in the deficit scale and has no
uniform derivative bound.

\begin{theorem}[Microstructured divergence-free trace extension]
\label{thm:microstructured-trace-extension}
Let $k\ge2$, let $R=\prod_{j=1}^k[0,L_j]$, and let $V_R$ and $m_R$ be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:box-self-cheeger}.  For every $\gamma>0$ and every
$\delta_0>0$ there exist $\delta\in(0,\delta_0)$ and a vector field $W$,
smooth on a neighborhood of $R$, such that
\[
 \Div W=0,
 \qquad
 \norm{V_R+W}_{L^\infty(R;\ell^\infty)}=m_R+\delta,
\]
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:microstructured-trace-lower-bound}
 \int_{\partial R}\abs{W\cdot\nu_R}\,\dd\Haus^{k-1}
 \ge \bigl(2P_1(R)-\gamma\bigr)\delta .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $m=m_R$.  We first record the scalar boundary signal used in the
construction.  Let $I$ be a bounded open interval.  Given $\epsilon>0$, for
all sufficiently small $\delta>0$ there is a function
$q\in C_c^\infty(I)$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:boundary-signal-properties}
 \int_Iq=0,
 \qquad
 -(2m+\delta)\le q\le\delta,
 \qquad
 \max_I q=\delta,
 \qquad
 \int_I\abs q\ge(2-\epsilon)\delta\abs I.
\end{equation}
Moreover, if $Q(s):=\int_{-\infty}^s q(t)\,\dd t$, then
$\norm{Q}_{L^\infty}$ can be made arbitrarily small while preserving the
other properties.

To see this, on one period start from the two-level mean-zero signal which is
$\delta$ on a proportion $1-\theta$ and $-(2m+\delta)$ on a proportion
$\theta$, where
\[
 \theta=\frac{\delta}{2(m+\delta)}.
\]
Its mean absolute value is
\[
 2\delta(1-\theta)
 =\delta\frac{2m+\delta}{m+\delta},
\]
which tends to $2\delta$ as $\delta\downarrow0$.  Smooth the transitions
inside the two level intervals, retaining constant subintervals at both
levels, and make the signal vanish to infinite order at the period endpoints.
The transition intervals can be chosen to have arbitrarily small relative
length, and a change of the length of the negative interval restores zero
mean.  Repeating this smooth cell on successively shorter periods inside $I$
proves \eqref{eq:boundary-signal-properties}; the primitive resets to zero at
each cell endpoint, so its supremum tends to zero with the period.

We next construct a local solenoidal collar at one face $F$ of $R$.  Let
$\nu$ be its outward normal, let $r$ denote inward distance from $F$, and
choose one tangential coordinate $s$ with unit vector $\tau$; this is the only
point where $k\ge2$ is used.  Choose a rectangular set $G\Subset F$.  Writing
the other tangential variables as $z$, take
\[
 q_F(s,z)=a(z)q(s),
 \qquad
 Q_F(s,z)=a(z)Q(s),
\]
where $0\le a\le1$ is smooth and compactly supported in the $z$-projection
of $G$.  The choices can be made so that $q_F$ is supported in $G$, has zero
$s$-integral for every $z$, satisfies the two pointwise bounds in
\eqref{eq:boundary-signal-properties}, attains $\delta$, and obeys
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:face-signal-mass}
 \int_F\abs{q_F}\,\dd\Haus^{k-1}
 \ge (2-\epsilon)\delta\Haus^{k-1}(G).
\end{equation}
At the same time, $\norm{Q_F}_{L^\infty}$ is arbitrarily small.

If $F$ is perpendicular to the $j$th coordinate, then the outward component
of the affine field at inward distance $r$ is
\[
 V_R\cdot\nu=m-c_jr,
 \qquad c_j:=\frac{2m}{L_j}.
\]
Choose a small collar width $h>0$ and a smooth function $\chi$, defined on an
open interval containing $[0,h]$, such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:collar-cutoff-condition}
 0\le\chi\le1,
 \qquad
 \chi(0)=1,
 \qquad
 \chi=0\quad\text{near }h,
 \qquad
 (2m+\delta)(1-\chi(r))\ge c_jr
 \quad(0\le r\le h).
\end{equation}
Such a function is obtained by taking $e^{-Ar}$ near zero with
$A>c_j/(2m+\delta)$, decreasing it smoothly to zero, and then reducing $h$ if
necessary.  In the collar over $G$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:local-solenoidal-collar}
 W_F=q_F(s,z)\chi(r)\nu
       +Q_F(s,z)\chi'(r)\tau,
\end{equation}
and extend it by zero at the inner edge and the tangential edges of the
collar.  The infinite-order vanishing of the chosen factors makes this
extension smooth.  Since differentiation in the outward normal direction is
$-\partial_r$,
\[
 \Div W_F=-q_F\chi'+\partial_s(Q_F\chi')=0,
 \qquad
 W_F\cdot\nu=q_F\quad\text{on }F.
\]

The normal component of $V_R+W_F$ does not exceed $m+\delta$, because
$q_F\le\delta$.  Its lower bound follows from
\eqref{eq:collar-cutoff-condition} and $q_F\ge-(2m+\delta)$:
\[
 m-c_jr+q_F\chi
 \ge m-c_jr-(2m+\delta)\chi
 \ge-(m+\delta).
\]
The closure of $G$ has positive distance from every edge of $F$.  Hence the
tangential component of $V_R$ has a fixed positive amount of coefficient
slack on the collar.  After $\chi$ has been fixed, shorten the periods in the
scalar construction until
$\norm{Q_F\chi'}_{L^\infty}$ is smaller than this slack.  All remaining
coordinate components are unchanged.  It follows that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:local-collar-capacity}
 \norm{V_R+W_F}_{L^\infty(\operatorname{supp}W_F;\ell^\infty)}
 \le m+\delta.
\end{equation}

Choose one such set $G_F\Subset F$ on every coordinate face so that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:face-core-exhaustion}
 \sum_F\Haus^{k-1}(G_F)
 \ge P_1(R)-\frac{\gamma}{8}.
\end{equation}
The face cores may first be chosen and then all collar widths reduced so that
the $2k$ collar supports are pairwise disjoint.  Apply the preceding
construction on each face, with a common sufficiently small $\delta$, and set
$W=\sum_FW_F$.  Disjointness of the supports, the divergence identity, and
\eqref{eq:local-collar-capacity} give
\[
 \Div W=0,
 \qquad
 \norm{V_R+W}_{L^\infty(R;\ell^\infty)}\le m+\delta.
\]
Each face signal attains the value $\delta$ where its tangential cutoff equals
one.  Thus the outward normal component of $V_R+W$ equals $m+\delta$ there,
and the last norm inequality is an equality.

Finally choose the face cores sufficiently close to the full faces, then
choose $\epsilon$ and $\delta<\delta_0$ sufficiently small.  Summing
\eqref{eq:face-signal-mass} and using
\eqref{eq:face-core-exhaustion} gives
\[
 \int_{\partial R}\abs{W\cdot\nu_R}\,\dd\Haus^{k-1}
 \ge(2-\epsilon)\delta\sum_F\Haus^{k-1}(G_F)
 \ge(2P_1(R)-\gamma)\delta,
\]
as required.
\end{proof}

The theorem determines the optimal constant in the main continuous stability
estimate.  The one-dimensional case is different because there is no
tangential direction in which to close a solenoidal boundary layer.

\begin{theorem}[Exact continuous linear stability constant]
\label{thm:sharp-continuous-stability-constant}
For a rectangular box $R=\prod_{j=1}^k[0,L_j]$, let
\[
 \mathsf C_{\rm tr}(R):=
 \inf\left\{C\ge0:
 \begin{array}{l}
 \displaystyle
 \sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{\lambda\in\{0,L_j\}}
 \norm{\iota_{j,\lambda}^*(\eta-\eta_R)}_{L^1(F_{j,\lambda};\coeff)}
 \le C\bigl(\norm{\eta}_{\coeff,\infty}-m_R\bigr)\\[2mm]
 \text{for every }\eta\in\Omega^{k-1}_{\rm sm}(R)
 \text{ with }\dd\eta=\omega_R
 \end{array}
 \right\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact-continuous-linear-constant}
 \mathsf C_{\rm tr}(R)=
 \begin{cases}
 2, & k=1,\\
 2P_1(R), & k\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In particular, the constant $2P_1(R)$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:box-boundary-stability} is sharp in every dimension
$k\ge2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $k\ge2$, Theorem~\ref{thm:box-boundary-stability} gives
$\mathsf C_{\rm tr}(R)\le2P_1(R)$.  Given $\gamma>0$, take $W$ from
Theorem~\ref{thm:microstructured-trace-extension} and let $\eta$ be the form
corresponding to $V_R+W$ under \eqref{eq:form-vector-identification}.  Then
$\dd\eta=\omega_R$, its norm deficit is exactly $\delta$, and its total face
trace error is
\[
 \int_{\partial R}\abs{W\cdot\nu_R}\,\dd\Haus^{k-1}
 \ge(2P_1(R)-\gamma)\delta.
\]
Since $\gamma$ is arbitrary, the reverse inequality follows.

If $k=1$, write $R=[0,L]$.  Every primitive has the form
$\eta(x)=x-L/2+a$, while $\eta_R(x)=x-L/2$ and $m_R=L/2$.  Hence
\[
 \norm{\eta}_{L^\infty}-m_R=\abs a,
 \qquad
 \abs{\eta(0)-\eta_R(0)}+\abs{\eta(L)-\eta_R(L)}=2\abs a.
\]
This proves the first line of \eqref{eq:exact-continuous-linear-constant}.
\end{proof}

The construction does not contradict the endpoint obstruction for the
divergence equation.  Bourgain and Brezis proved bounded nonlinear solvability
of $\Div Y=f$ with $Y\in L^\infty\cap W^{1,k}$ for $f\in L^k$ and showed that
no bounded linear solution selection has the same endpoint estimate
\cite{BourgainBrezis2003,BourgainBrezis2007}; the surrounding limiting
Sobolev theory is organized by canceling and cocanceling operators
\cite{VanSchaftingen2013}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:microstructured-trace-extension}
is not a right inverse on a boundary data space.  Its boundary data, collar
widths, and oscillation periods are chosen together, and the period tends to
zero with the norm deficit.  It therefore supplies the sharp trace sequence
needed here without asserting an endpoint-bounded extension operator.
