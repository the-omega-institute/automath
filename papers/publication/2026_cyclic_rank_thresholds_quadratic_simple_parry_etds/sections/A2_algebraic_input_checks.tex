\section{Algebraic input checks}
\label{app:algebraic-inputs}

\subsection{A non-Pisot simple-Parry example}
\label{app:quartic-nonpisot}

Let $f(x)=x^4-2x^3-2$ and let $\beta$ be its positive root.  Eisenstein's
criterion at $2$ makes $f$ irreducible.  Since
\[
f(2)=-2,
\qquad
f(11/5)=\frac{81}{625},
\]
we have $2<\beta<11/5$, and the defining equation gives
\[
1=2\beta^{-1}+2\beta^{-4}.
\]
Every proper suffix of $2002\,0^\infty$ is lexicographically smaller than
the whole word: the first two start with zero and the third is
$2000^\infty$.  Parry's criterion therefore gives
$d_\beta(1)=2002\,0^\infty$.

Write the negative real root as $-u$.  The function
$g(u)=u^4+2u^3-2$ is strictly increasing for $u>0$, with $g(0)<0<g(1)$,
so $0<u<1$.  Since
\[
f'(x)=2x^2(2x-3),
\]
$f$ decreases on $(-\infty,3/2)$ and increases on $(3/2,\infty)$; hence it
has exactly one negative and one positive real root.  The remaining roots
are a nonreal pair $z,\overline z$.  Moreover,
\[
\beta^4g(2/\beta)=4(3+4\beta-\beta^3)>0,
\]
because $\beta<11/5$ and
$3+4\beta-\beta^3>11-(11/5)^3=44/125$.  Thus $u<2/\beta$.  The product of
the roots is $-2$, so
\[
|z|^2=\frac{2}{\beta u}>1.
\]
Irreducibility makes $z$ a conjugate of $\beta$, proving that $\beta$ is
not Pisot.

\subsection{Bassino's cubic data}
\label{app:bassino-data}

We prove Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic-unbounded-family-data}.  The polynomial
$M_n$ in \eqref{eq:cubic-unbounded-polynomial} satisfies
\[
M_n(n)=-n<0,
\qquad
M_n(n+1)=n^2-n-1>0.
\]
The identity
\[
M_n(x)=x^2(x-2)-n(x-1)^2
\]
shows that $M_n(x)<0$ for $x\le n$.  For $2<x\le n$, this follows from
\[
\frac{x^2(x-2)}{(x-1)^2}=x-\frac{x}{(x-1)^2}<x\le n;
\]
the remaining range is immediate.  If all three roots were real, the other
two would also exceed $n$, contradicting that their sum with $\beta_n$ is
$n+2$.  Thus $\beta_n\in(n,n+1)$ is the unique real root.  A reducible
monic cubic has an integral root, so $M_n$ is irreducible.  Its two nonreal
roots have common modulus
\[
\sqrt{\frac n{\beta_n}}<1
\]
by the product of the roots.  Hence $\beta_n$ is a nonintegral cubic Pisot
number.

Let
\[
P_{w_n}(x)=x^{2n+2}-\sum_{i=1}^{2n+2}(w_n)_ix^{2n+2-i}
\]
be the Parry polynomial of \eqref{eq:cubic-unbounded-parry-word}.  Direct
multiplication gives
\begin{equation}
P_{w_n}(x)=M_n(x)\frac{(x^n-1)(x^{n+1}-1)}{(x-1)^2}.
\label{eq:cubic-unbounded-parry-factorization}
\end{equation}
Thus $w_n$ represents one in base $\beta_n$.  The digit $n$ occurs only in
the first, $n$th, and last positions.  The suffix at the $n$th position
starts with $n10$, whereas $w_n$ starts with $n12$; the final suffix is
$n0^\infty$, and every other proper suffix starts below $n$.  Parry's
criterion gives $d_{\beta_n}(1)=w_n0^\infty$.

For $r\le n-1$, the initial digits are $t_1=n$ and $t_i=i-1$ for
$2\le i\le r$.  Formula~\eqref{eq:simple-parry-Q} becomes
\[
Q_r=1+nQ_{r-1}+\sum_{i=2}^r(i-1)Q_{r-i}.
\]
It gives
\[
Q_0=1,\qquad Q_1=n+1,\qquad Q_2=n^2+n+2
\]
and $Q_r>nQ_{r-1}$.  Subtracting the formulas at the preceding three
indices yields
\[
Q_r=(n+2)Q_{r-1}-2nQ_{r-2}+nQ_{r-3},
\qquad 3\le r\le n-1,
\]
which proves the remaining assertions of the lemma.
