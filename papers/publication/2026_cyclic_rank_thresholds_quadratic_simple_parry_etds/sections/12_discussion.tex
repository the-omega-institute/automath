\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The cyclic language-rank recoding has a complete exact picture in quadratic
Pisot systems.  Its least nontrivial overlap threshold is two except in two
extremal polynomial families, where it is three.  Chamber duality explains
the common aperture-two map, the critical systems have a two-fixed-point
normal form, and above threshold the natural overlap graph records exact
finite-block onset, the right Fischer cover, and Markov order in the fixed
output alphabet.

The simple-Parry difference quotient separates the graph mechanism from the
quadratic arithmetic.  It is an exact quotient of the classical pair graph,
not a heuristic reduction, because every bounded coordinate difference is
realized by two raw digits.  It supplies both the exact future-only
longest-path formula and the reachable-cycle injectivity decision.  The
aperture-two trichotomy holds beyond the Pisot class, while Bassino's family
shows that exact inverse depth is unbounded when the underlying cubic system
varies.

The companion article by the present authors, \emph{Linear overlap transients
for bounded zero representations in Pisot numeration: sharp inverse depth
for cyclic rank recodings}, addresses the orthogonal fixed-system question.
It proves an effective linear bound for overlap transients over arbitrary
bounded coefficient alphabets and a fixed cubic example attaining that
order.  The current paper supplies the exact family classifications and does
not use that theorem.

All conclusions concern cyclic reduction of finite-language ranks.  They do
not assert identities of numerical $\beta$-values, complexity bounds for
general normalization, or universal closing delays for arbitrary symbolic
factor codes.  This boundary is also why the supplement's entropy, pressure,
fiber, discrepancy, zeta, and geometric consequences remain consequences of
the recoding package rather than inputs to the threshold proofs.
