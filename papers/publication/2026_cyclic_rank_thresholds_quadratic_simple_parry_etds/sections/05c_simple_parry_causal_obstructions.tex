\section{Classical pair graphs and simple-Parry arithmetic quotients}
\label{sec:simple-parry-causal-obstructions}

The pair-graph decision used in this section is classical.  Decision
procedures for injectivity and surjectivity of one-dimensional local maps go
back to tessellation automata and cellular automata
\cite{AmorosoPatt1972,Richardson1972}.  Nasu's bundle graphs encode two input
paths with a common image, and Head formulates injectivity as unambiguity of a
labeled finite automaton \cite{Nasu1977,Head1989}.  In symbolic dynamics this
is the familiar fiber-product or pair-graph construction
\cite{LindMarcus1995,Nasu1995}.  A reachable off-diagonal directed cycle,
its periodic repetition, and the existence of some finite sliding-block
inverse in the injective case all belong to that tradition.  Quantitative
decoder windows likewise have an established theory
\cite{Ashley1988,Ashley1991,Ashley1996}.

What is specific here is the quotient of the full input-pair graph by the
coordinatewise difference: for cyclic language-rank recodings it closes on
the finite state set $\Delta_d^{m-1}$.  This arithmetic quotient gives the
exact memory-zero anticipation as a longest survival time, a concrete state
bound, and, for simple-Parry ranks, a unique-zero-predecessor property that
turns a general two-sided inverse into a future-only inverse.  The aperture
trichotomy and the exact quadratic and cubic depths are computations in this
quotient, not additional pair-graph principles.  Throughout, the congruences concern
integer language ranks, not equality of numerical $\beta$-values.

Recall that Pisot bases have eventually periodic greedy expansions of one
\cite{Schmidt1980}, whereas the finite expansion required below is the
simple-Parry hypothesis.  The collision results through
Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-parry-aperture-two} do not require the base to be
Pisot.  Parry admissibility and the associated Bertrand numeration are standard
\cite{Parry1960,CharlierCisterninoStipulanti2022}; the expansions of one for
cubic Pisot numbers were classified by Bassino \cite{Bassino2002}.  We record
the data needed
to distinguish these published inputs from the specific sliding-congruence
statement.

Let $\beta\notin\mathbb Z$ be a simple-Parry number with
\[
d_\beta(1)=t_1t_2\cdots t_p0^\infty,
\qquad t_1=d=\lfloor\beta\rfloor,
\qquad t_p>0,
\]
where $p\ge2$ and $0\le t_i\le d$.  The language used below is the canonical
Parry language determined by
$d_\beta^*(1)=(t_1\cdots t_{p-1}(t_p-1))^\infty$, not the noncanonical
Bertrand system generated directly by the finite word $d_\beta(1)$.
Bertrand--Mathis introduced the positional framework
\cite{BertrandMathis1989}; the canonical/noncanonical distinction in the
simple-Parry case is part of the correction and classification in
\cite{CharlierCisterninoStipulanti2022}.  Put
\[
P_\beta(z)=z^p-t_1z^{p-1}-\cdots-t_p,
\qquad
\mathcal A_d=\{0,1,\ldots,d\},
\qquad
\Delta_d=[-d,d]\cap\mathbb Z.
\]
The raw system is the full shift $\mathcal A_d^{\mathbb Z}$, so $\Delta_d$
is exactly its digit-difference alphabet.  Let $Q_n$ be the number of legal
length-$n$ words.  In the low-to-high convention used throughout this
article, the established Parry--Bertrand rank data are
\begin{align}
Q_0&=1,\nonumber\\
Q_n&=1+\sum_{i=1}^n t_iQ_{n-i} &&(1\le n<p),\label{eq:simple-parry-Q}\\
Q_n&=\sum_{i=1}^p t_iQ_{n-i} &&(n\ge p),\nonumber
\end{align}
and
\[
\Rank_{\beta,m}(w)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}w_jQ_j
\]
maps the legal length-$m$ words, in colexicographic order, bijectively onto
$\{0,\ldots,Q_m-1\}$.  We therefore define $\Fold_{\beta,m}$ and
$\Phi_{\beta,m}$ by the same rank reduction modulo $Q_m$ as in
Definition~\ref{def:quadratic-parry-fold}.

For $m\ge2$, define
\begin{equation}
\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,m)
=\min\{L\ge1:a_t=\psi(y_t,\ldots,y_{t+L-1})
\text{ for every }y=\Phi_{\beta,m}(a)\},
\label{eq:simple-parry-causal-length}
\end{equation}
with value $\infty$ if no such $L$ exists.  Thus
$\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,m)-1$ is the least anticipation of an inverse
sliding-block code constrained to have memory zero.  We do not use
``right-closing delay'' as a synonym: the latter belongs to general
factor-map theory, while \eqref{eq:simple-parry-causal-length} fixes a
one-sided convention and asks for its exact optimum.  Also set
\[
\mathfrak a(P_\beta,d)
=\min\{\deg H:0\ne H\in(P_\beta),\ H(0)\ne0,
\ |[z^j]H|\le d\text{ for all }j\}.
\]

\paragraph{Degeneration of the bounded-multiple order.}
\label{prop:simple-parry-bounded-order}
Under the preceding simple-Parry hypotheses,
\[
\mathfrak a(P_\beta,d)=p.
\]

\begin{proof}
The coefficient vector of $P_\beta$ consists of the leading coefficient
$1$ and the coefficients $-t_1,\ldots,-t_p$.  It lies in $\Delta_d$, and its
constant coefficient $-t_p$ is nonzero.  Hence
$\mathfrak a(P_\beta,d)\le p$.  Conversely, every nonzero member of the
principal ideal $(P_\beta)\subset\mathbb Z[z]$ has degree at least
$\deg P_\beta=p$.  This proves equality.
\end{proof}

For $L\ge1$, introduce the finite-block kernel
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N_{\beta,m,L}
=\left\{e\in\Delta_d^{m+L-1}:
e_0\ne0,\quad
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}Q_je_{t+j}\equiv0\pmod{Q_m}
\ (0\le t<L)\right\}.
\label{eq:bad-sliding-congruence-set}
\end{equation}
Here $T_{m,L}$ is the $L$-row banded Toeplitz matrix whose row at offset $t$
has coefficients $(Q_0,\ldots,Q_{m-1})$.  The last condition is
$T_{m,L}e\equiv0\pmod{Q_m}$.  Thus $\mathcal N_{\beta,m,L}$ is the bounded
finite-block kernel of this operator over $\mathbb Z/Q_m\mathbb Z$, with
first coordinate nonzero.

\subsection{The classical decision in the arithmetic difference quotient}

\begin{theorem}[Arithmetic quotient of the classical pair-graph decision]
\label{thm:simple-parry-causal-collision}
Apply the classical pair-graph construction to the cyclic rank recoding of a
nonintegral simple-Parry number.  For every $m\ge2$, its coordinatewise
difference quotient satisfies
\begin{equation}
\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,m)
=\min\{L\ge1:\mathcal N_{\beta,m,L}=\varnothing\},
\label{eq:exact-causal-obstruction}
\end{equation}
where the minimum is $\infty$ when all the obstruction sets are nonempty.

More explicitly, let $\Gamma_{\beta,m}$ be the directed graph with vertex set
$V=\Delta_d^{m-1}$.  There is an edge from
$v=(v_0,\ldots,v_{m-2})$ to $w=(w_0,\ldots,w_{m-2})$ precisely when
\[
w_i=v_{i+1}\quad(0\le i<m-2)
\]
and
\begin{equation}
\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}Q_jv_j+Q_{m-1}w_{m-2}
\equiv0\pmod{Q_m}.
\label{eq:collision-graph-edge}
\end{equation}
If $B=\{v\in V:v_0\ne0\}$, then
\begin{equation}
\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,m)
=1+\sup\{\text{number of edges in a directed path starting in }B\}.
\label{eq:collision-graph-longest-path}
\end{equation}
The supremum is infinite exactly when a directed cycle is reachable from
$B$.  Furthermore,
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\beta,m}\text{ is injective}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Gamma_{\beta,m}\text{ has no bi-infinite path other than the zero path}.
\label{eq:collision-graph-injectivity}
\end{equation}
If $\Phi_{\beta,m}$ is injective, then its future-only inverse length is
finite exactly when the zero vertex is not reachable from $B$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $u,v\in\mathcal A_d^{m+L-1}$ and put $e_i=u_i-v_i$.  Since the legal
rank is a bijection, the $t$th output symbols of the two blocks are equal if
and only if their raw ranks agree modulo $Q_m$, namely if and only if
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}Q_je_{t+j}\equiv0\pmod{Q_m}.
\]
Moreover, $u_0\ne v_0$ if and only if $e_0\ne0$.  Thus every pair of raw
blocks whose first symbols remain ambiguous after $L$ outputs determines an
element of $\mathcal N_{\beta,m,L}$.

Conversely, for any $e\in\Delta_d^{m+L-1}$ define coordinatewise
\[
u_i=\max\{e_i,0\},\qquad v_i=\max\{-e_i,0\}.
\]
Then $u_i,v_i\in\mathcal A_d$, $u_i-v_i=e_i$, and both finite blocks extend
arbitrarily to points of the full shift.  Hence every element of
$\mathcal N_{\beta,m,L}$ is realized by an actual output collision with
distinct first symbols.  If the obstruction set is empty, the first raw
symbol is constant on every fiber over an occurring length-$L$ output word;
the resulting function on this finite output language is the decoder $\psi$
in \eqref{eq:simple-parry-causal-length}.  This proves
\eqref{eq:exact-causal-obstruction}.

A length-$L$ path in $\Gamma_{\beta,m}$ beginning at
$(e_0,\ldots,e_{m-2})\in B$ is exactly a vector
$e\in\mathcal N_{\beta,m,L}$: each edge appends one coordinate and imposes
one of the $L$ congruences.  Formula
\eqref{eq:collision-graph-longest-path} follows.  Since the graph is finite,
arbitrarily long such paths exist exactly when a directed cycle is reachable
from $B$.

A bi-infinite difference sequence labels a bi-infinite path in the graph if
and only if all corresponding output ranks agree.  Every coordinate of that
difference sequence is realized by two raw digits by the positive--negative
part construction above.  Therefore a nonzero bi-infinite graph path is
equivalent to a pair of distinct points with the same image, proving
\eqref{eq:collision-graph-injectivity}.

Finally assume injectivity.  No directed cycle containing a nonzero vertex
can then occur, because its periodic repetition would be a nonzero
bi-infinite path.  The zero loop is consequently the only cycle reachable
from $B$ that is compatible with injectivity.  Thus paths from $B$ have
unbounded length exactly when they reach the zero vertex.  Combining this
with \eqref{eq:collision-graph-longest-path} proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Simple-Parry consequence of the classical decision]
\label{cor:simple-parry-causal-completeness}
Under the preceding hypotheses, for every $m\ge2$ the following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item $\Phi_{\beta,m}$ is injective;
  \item $\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,m)<\infty$;
  \item no directed cycle of $\Gamma_{\beta,m}$ is reachable from $B$.
\end{enumerate}
If these conditions hold, then
\begin{equation}
1\le \ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,m)
\le (2d+1)^{m-1}-1.
\label{eq:simple-parry-causal-state-bound}
\end{equation}
If they fail, there are distinct periodic raw points with the same image and
with common period at most $(2d+1)^{m-1}-1$.  Thus the classical finite
forward cycle search specializes to the arithmetic difference quotient
above.  Every failure has a purely periodic certificate with the displayed
explicit bound.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The recurrence \eqref{eq:simple-parry-Q} gives $Q_1=d+1$ and shows
inductively that $Q_n>Q_{n-1}$: before the terminal index there is the
additional summand $1$, while at and after that index the positive term
$t_pQ_{n-p}$ accompanies $dQ_{n-1}$.  In particular, $Q_m>d$.

Let $\mathbf0=(0,\ldots,0)\in\Delta_d^{m-1}$.  If an edge
$v\to\mathbf0$ exists, the overlap conditions give
$v_1=\cdots=v_{m-2}=0$, and the edge congruence reduces to
$v_0\equiv0\pmod{Q_m}$.  Since $|v_0|\le d<Q_m$, necessarily
$v=\mathbf0$.  Hence the zero vertex has the zero vertex as its unique
predecessor and is not reachable from $B$.

If $\Phi_{\beta,m}$ is injective, the last assertion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-parry-causal-collision} now gives a finite
future-only inverse.  The converse is immediate because a decoder recovers every raw
coordinate.  A directed cycle reachable from $B$ cannot contain
$\mathbf0$; repeating its first-coordinate labels gives a nonzero periodic
difference sequence and hence, by taking positive and negative parts, two
distinct periodic raw points with the same image.  Conversely, from any
nonzero bi-infinite collision path, shift to a vertex in $B$.  Its forward
path avoids $\mathbf0$ and therefore repeats among the
$(2d+1)^{m-1}-1$ nonzero vertices, producing a reachable directed cycle.
This proves all three equivalences and the periodic certificate, whose cycle
may be chosen simple.

In the injective case the forward subgraph reachable from $B$ is acyclic and
contains at most $(2d+1)^{m-1}-1$ vertices.  A directed path in it has at
most $(2d+1)^{m-1}-2$ edges.  Formula
\eqref{eq:collision-graph-longest-path} then gives
\eqref{eq:simple-parry-causal-state-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete aperture-two trichotomy]
\label{thm:simple-parry-aperture-two}
Set
\[
c_\beta=t_2+\ind_{\{p\ge3\}}.
\]
Then $1\le c_\beta\le d+1$ and
\[
Q_2=d(d+1)+c_\beta.
\]
Exactly one of the following alternatives holds.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item If $c_\beta=d+1$, then $\Fold_{\beta,2}$ is the identity on
  $\mathcal A_d^2$ and $\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,2)=1$.
  \item If $1\le c_\beta<d$, then $\Phi_{\beta,2}$ is injective and
  $\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,2)=2$.
  \item If $c_\beta=d$, then $\Phi_{\beta,2}$ is noninjective.  Its sole
  nonsingleton path-space fiber is
  \[
  \Phi_{\beta,2}(0^{\mathbb Z})
  =\Phi_{\beta,2}(d^{\mathbb Z})
  =((0,0))^{\mathbb Z},
  \]
  and $\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,2)=\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
For $p=2$ the three cases are determined by $c_\beta=t_2$; for
$p\ge3$ they are determined by $c_\beta=t_2+1$.  In particular, a binary
simple-Parry word of order at least three has an identity aperture-two fold
when $t_2=1$ and exactly the displayed constant branch pair when $t_2=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $q=d+1$ and $M=Q_2=dq+c_\beta$.  The formula for $Q_2$ follows directly
from \eqref{eq:simple-parry-Q}, with the boundary summand $1$ present exactly
when $2<p$.  Since $t_2\ge1$ when $p=2$, whereas $0\le t_2\le d$ when
$p\ge3$, the stated range for $c_\beta$ follows.

An edge of $\Gamma_{\beta,2}$ is a pair $(e,f)\in\Delta_d^2$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
e+qf\equiv0\pmod M.
\label{eq:aperture-two-edge-congruence}
\end{equation}
The left side has modulus at most
$d(1+q)=q^2-1<2M$, so it equals $-M$, $0$, or $M$.  If it is zero, then
$q>|e|$ forces $(e,f)=(0,0)$.  If it is $M$, the bound $e\le d$ forces
$f=d$, because $f\le d-1$ would give
$M-qf\ge q+c_\beta>d$; hence $(e,f)=(c_\beta,d)$, which is admissible
exactly when $c_\beta\le d$.  The case $-M$ is its negative.  Thus the full
edge set is
\[
\{(0,0)\}\quad(c_\beta=d+1),
\qquad
\{(0,0),\ \pm(c_\beta,d)\}\quad(c_\beta\le d).
\]

When $c_\beta=d+1$, one has $Q_2=q^2=|\mathcal A_d^2|$, so every raw
length-two word is legal and cyclic reduction does nothing.  This proves
(a).  If $c_\beta<d$, the two nonzero edges cannot be followed by another
edge, because their terminal vertices are $\pm d$, not $\pm c_\beta$.
There is a one-output collision but no two-output collision, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-parry-causal-collision} gives (b).  Finally, when
$c_\beta=d$, the two nonzero edges are the loops $(d,d)$ and $(-d,-d)$.
They give precisely the constant difference sequences $\pm d^{\mathbb Z}$.
The only raw realization of either is the pair
$\{0^{\mathbb Z},d^{\mathbb Z}\}$, and the common residue is zero in every
window.  No other nonzero bi-infinite path exists, proving (c) and the fiber
classification.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Pisot-free scope is strict]
\label{prop:nonpisot-simple-parry-scope}
The bounded-multiple calculation above,
Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-parry-causal-collision}, Corollary
\ref{cor:simple-parry-causal-completeness}, and Theorem
\ref{thm:simple-parry-aperture-two} hold for every nonintegral simple-Parry
number; no Pisot hypothesis is required.  This strictly enlarges their scope.
Indeed, let $\beta$ be the positive root of
\begin{equation}
x^4-2x^3-2=0.
\label{eq:nonpisot-simple-parry-witness}
\end{equation}
Then
\[
d_\beta(1)=2002\,0^\infty,
\]
$\beta$ is not Pisot, and the aperture-two cyclic rank fold is injective with
$\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,2)=2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The recurrence for $Q_n$, the consecutive colex-rank formula, and Parry
admissibility used above hold for every simple-Parry number
\cite{Parry1960,CharlierCisterninoStipulanti2022}.  The proofs of the four
listed results subsequently use only these data, the full raw alphabet
$\{0,\ldots,d\}$, integer congruences modulo $Q_m$, and finite directed
graphs.  No estimate on a Galois conjugate occurs.  This proves the claimed
hypothesis removal.

It remains to show that the enlargement is strict.  The polynomial in
\eqref{eq:nonpisot-simple-parry-witness} is Eisenstein at $2$, hence
irreducible.  Its positive root satisfies
\[
2<\beta<\frac{11}{5},
\]
because the polynomial takes the values $-2$ and $81/625$ at the two
endpoints.  Its defining equation gives
\[
1=2\beta^{-1}+2\beta^{-4}.
\]
The proper suffixes of $2002\,0^\infty$ are all lexicographically smaller
than $2002\,0^\infty$: the first two begin with $0$, while the third is
$2000^\infty<2002\,0^\infty$.  Parry's criterion therefore gives
$d_\beta(1)=2002\,0^\infty$.

Write the negative real root as $-u$.  The function
$g(u)=u^4+2u^3-2$ is strictly increasing for $u>0$, with $g(0)<0<g(1)$,
so $0<u<1$.  To count all real roots, put
$f(x)=x^4-2x^3-2$.  Its derivative is
\[
f'(x)=2x^2(2x-3).
\]
Thus $f$ is strictly decreasing on $(-\infty,3/2)$ apart from the stationary
flattening at $0$, which does not change monotonicity, and is strictly
increasing on $(3/2,\infty)$.  Since $f(x)\to+\infty$ as
$x\to-\infty$, $f(0)=-2$, and $f(x)\to+\infty$ as $x\to+\infty$, there is
exactly one negative real root and exactly one positive real root.  The two
remaining roots are therefore a nonreal conjugate pair $z,\overline z$.
Moreover,
\[
\beta^4 g(2/\beta)
=4(3+4\beta-\beta^3)>0,
\]
where the last inequality follows from
$\beta<11/5$ and
$3+4\beta-\beta^3>11-(11/5)^3=44/125$.
Thus $u<2/\beta$.  The product of the four roots is $-2$, whence
\[
|z|^2=\frac{2}{\beta u}>1.
\]
Irreducibility makes $z$ a Galois conjugate of $\beta$, so $\beta$ is not
Pisot.  Finally $d=2$, $p=4$, and
$c_\beta=t_2+1=1<d$; case~\textup{(b)} of
Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-parry-aperture-two} yields injectivity and
$\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,2)=2$.
\end{proof}
