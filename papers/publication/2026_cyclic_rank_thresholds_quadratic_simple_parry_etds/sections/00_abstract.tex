\begin{abstract}
We study sliding recodings obtained by reducing colexicographic language
ranks modulo the number of legal words and overlapping the resulting finite
windows.  For every quadratic Pisot $\beta$-language, the least nontrivial
injective aperture is three precisely for the roots of $x^2-ax-a$ and
$x^2-ax+1$, and is two otherwise.  At apertures $m\ge3$, the future-only
inverse length is three in the positive-conjugate chamber; it is two in the
negative chamber at aperture three, while for negative conjugate and
$m\ge4$ we prove the bounds
$2\le\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,m)\le m$.

For every nonintegral simple-Parry base, a coordinatewise-difference quotient
of the classical pair graph preserves all collisions.  Its longest directed
paths give the future-only inverse length, while reachable cycles decide
noninjectivity; the resulting aperture-two trichotomy needs no Pisot
hypothesis.  In Bassino's cubic simple-Parry Pisot family, the quotient gives
injective apertures $m_n=n-1$ with
$\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta_n,m_n)=n-1$, so future-only inverse depth is unbounded
across cubic systems.  The Fibonacci decoder, chamber duality, critical
Fischer covers, and Markov orders follow as concrete consequences of these
two collision mechanisms.
\end{abstract}
