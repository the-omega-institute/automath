\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Finite-window recodings can collapse individual input words and still become
invertible when their outputs are overlapped.  This paper determines where
that transition occurs, how much future output a memory-zero inverse needs,
and what local shift structure remains at and below threshold for quadratic
and simple-Parry beta-languages.

Let $X_m$ be the legal words of length $m$ in a greedy numeration language,
ordered colexicographically.  Positional rank identifies $X_m$ with
$\{0,\ldots,Q_m-1\}$, where $Q_m=|X_m|$.  We evaluate the same weighted rank
on the full digit cube, reduce it modulo $Q_m$, and return the legal word with
that residue.  Overlapping these cyclically recoded windows defines a sliding
factor $\Phi_{\beta,m}$ and its image $Y_{\beta,m}$.  Positional rank belongs
to Parry--Bertrand numeration theory
\cite{BertrandMathis1989,BruyereHansel1997,LecomteRigo2001,
CharlierCisterninoStipulanti2022}; reduction modulo the finite-language count
is the operation classified here.

The construction must not be confused with numerical
$\beta$-normalization.  The congruences compare integer language ranks, not
values in $\mathbb Z[\beta]$.  Nor is the rank-relabelled local rule a linear
cellular automaton: the input alphabet is a proper digit subset of
$\mathbb Z/Q_m\mathbb Z$, not a module on which the rule is a group
endomorphism.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The Fibonacci model.}
The first part develops the binary prototype completely.  The finite-window
rank is the Fibonacci value
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}F_{j+2}x_j\pmod {F_{m+2}},
\]
and legal words contain no adjacent ones.  Lemma~\ref{lem:m3-decoder} gives
an explicit decoder from three consecutive recoded windows.
Theorem~\ref{thm:block-invertibility} proves that aperture three is the exact
nontrivial threshold, while Proposition~\ref{prop:low-window-counterexamples}
gives the sharp finite-block failures below onset.  At aperture two,
Theorem~\ref{thm:m2-branch-locus} shows that the only nonsingleton fiber
identifies the two constant binary points.  This model isolates the mechanism
later seen at every extremal quadratic parameter.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The quadratic classification.}
Every quadratic Pisot number lies in one of the chambers
\[
x^2-ax-b,\quad a\ge b\ge1,
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
x^2-ax+b,\quad a\ge3,\ 1\le b\le a-2.
\]
After deriving the exact Parry words and rank recurrences, we prove a
nearest-multiple separation and a bounded sliding-congruence lemma.  They
lead to Theorem~\ref{thm:full-quadratic-pisot-threshold}:
\[
m_*(\beta)=3
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{minpoly}(\beta)\in
\{x^2-ax-a:a\ge1\}\cup\{x^2-ax+1:a\ge3\},
\]
and $m_*(\beta)=2$ otherwise.  Thus the exceptional negative chamber is the
metallic family, with its golden boundary member, and the exceptional
positive chamber is its critical dual.

The classification includes more than the first injective aperture.
Theorem~\ref{thm:quadratic-aperture-two-duality} gives the exact chamber
duality and first separation.  Theorem
\ref{thm:quadratic-optimal-causal-inverse} determines the positive-chamber
future-only optimum and bounds the negative chamber.  Theorem
\ref{thm:quadratic-critical-normal-form} identifies the two-fixed-point
critical quotient.  Finally, Theorem
\ref{thm:quadratic-exact-local-order} proves that finite-block injectivity
begins exactly at length $m$, the natural overlap presentation is the right
Fischer cover with $(\lfloor\beta\rfloor+1)^{m-1}$ states, and the image in
the fixed output alphabet is exactly $m$-step, not $(m-1)$-step.  Standard
Fischer--Krieger theory supplies the presentation framework
\cite{Fischer1975,Krieger1984,LindMarcus1995}; the exact onset and follower
separation are arithmetic conclusions of the cyclic rank map.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Simple-Parry quotients.}
For an arbitrary nonintegral simple-Parry number, no Pisot hypothesis is
needed for the graph reduction.  Theorem
\ref{thm:simple-parry-causal-collision} quotients the classical pair graph by
coordinatewise differences and proves that every bounded difference is
realized.  The causal inverse length is one plus an exact longest path, and a
reachable cycle is equivalent to noninjectivity.  Corollary
\ref{cor:simple-parry-causal-completeness} uses the unique predecessor of the
zero state to identify injectivity with finite future-only depth.
Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-parry-aperture-two} gives the complete aperture-two
trichotomy, while Proposition~\ref{prop:nonpisot-simple-parry-scope} records
that the scope is genuinely larger than the Pisot class.

Bassino's cubic family \cite{Bassino2002} then supplies a parameter-varying
unboundedness theorem.  Lemmas~\ref{lem:cubic-unbounded-family-data}--
\ref{lem:cubic-unbounded-terminal-paths} verify the Parry data, exclude the
second carry, and classify the exact terminal paths.  Theorem
\ref{thm:cubic-unbounded-causal-depth} obtains injective apertures
$m_n=n-1$ with
\[
\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta_n,m_n)=n-1.
\]
This varies the system and complements, rather than duplicates, the
fixed-system linear-order result.

That fixed-system theorem appears in the companion article by the present
authors, \emph{Linear overlap transients for bounded zero representations in
Pisot numeration: sharp inverse depth for cyclic rank recodings}.  It proves
an effective linear transient theorem for arbitrary bounded zero
representations and derives an aperture-linear inverse bound from an exact
collision quotient.  No theorem from that companion is used here.  The two
papers share only the cyclic rank definition and the caution separating it
from numerical normalization.

Sections~\ref{sec:stabilization}--\ref{sec:stab:branch-and-conjugacy} give
the Fibonacci model.  Section~\ref{sec:quadratic-pisot-extension} proves the
quadratic classification and local structure; the short metallic subsection
then records its constant-type specialization.  Section
\ref{sec:simple-parry-causal-obstructions} proves the simple-Parry quotient,
aperture-two trichotomy, and cubic unboundedness.  The supplementary material
contains entropy, Blackwell, pressure, fiber, discrepancy, bounded-multiple,
zeta, and geometric consequences; none enters the central proof chain.
