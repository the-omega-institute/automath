\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Finite-window recodings may collapse isolated input words and nevertheless
become invertible after their outputs are overlapped.  We determine this
transition for cyclic rank recodings of quadratic and simple-Parry
$\beta$-languages, together with the amount of future output needed by a
memory-zero inverse.

There are two scales in this question.  The aperture $m$ determines the raw
window on which the local recoding acts.  Once that aperture is fixed, the
future-only inverse length asks how many consecutive output symbols are
needed to recover the raw symbol at the initial time.  The first scale is a
threshold problem for overlapping finite windows; the second records the
directional depth of the inverse.  Keeping them separate is essential here:
the aperture threshold and future-only depth have different chamber
classifications.

For a general sliding block code, collisions are represented by paths in a
fiber product: two input paths are followed in parallel while their output
labels agree.  Global injectivity excludes nontrivial bi-infinite collision
paths.  A future-only decoder imposes a stronger directional condition.  It
requires every collision path whose two initial symbols differ to terminate
after a uniformly bounded number of forward steps.  This distinction is
particularly natural for greedy numeration languages, whose admissibility is
specified from the most significant end but whose sliding code is read along
the integer line.

Let $X_m$ be the legal length-$m$ words in a greedy numeration language,
ordered colexicographically, and put $Q_m=|X_m|$.  Positional rank identifies
$X_m$ with $\{0,\ldots,Q_m-1\}$.  We evaluate the same weighted rank on the
full digit cube, reduce it modulo $Q_m$, and return the legal word carrying
that residue.  Overlapping these recoded windows defines a sliding factor
$\Phi_{\beta,m}$ with image $Y_{\beta,m}$.  Positional ranks come from
Parry--Bertrand numeration
\cite{BertrandMathis1989,BruyereHansel1997,LecomteRigo2001,
CharlierCisterninoStipulanti2022}; the cyclic reduction and its overlap
structure are the objects studied here.  These are congruences of integer
language ranks, not equalities of numerical $\beta$-values.

The local rule is arithmetic, but injectivity is a collision problem.  In
the quadratic case the language-count recurrence separates bounded sliding
congruences directly.  In the simple-Parry case the same collision problem
closes on a finite quotient of the classical pair graph.  The two parts of
the paper are therefore different arithmetic realizations of one symbolic
mechanism: a nonzero difference path records two raw inputs with the same
overlapped output, and finite extinction of every such path gives a
future-only decoder.

Concretely, equality of two folded output windows is a congruence modulo the
language count $Q_m$.  Subtracting the two raw windows replaces the fiber
product by a bounded difference sequence.  Consecutive equal outputs then
give a banded system of sliding congruences with coefficients
$Q_0,\ldots,Q_{m-1}$.  In degree two, the recurrence for these coefficients
eliminates successive coordinates and reduces the problem to separation from
the nearest multiple of $Q_m$.  For a general simple-Parry recurrence, the
last $m-1$ differences form a finite state, and appending one coordinate is a
directed edge.  Recurrence separation and the difference graph are thus two
ways of organizing the same bounded collision equations.

\begin{introtheorem}[Quadratic threshold classification]
Let $\beta$ be a quadratic Pisot number, and use the greedy language and
cyclic Parry rank of Definition~\ref{def:quadratic-parry-fold}.  Then
\[
m_*(\beta)=3
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{minpoly}(\beta)\in
\{x^2-ax-a:a\ge1\}\cup\{x^2-ax+1:a\ge3\},
\]
and $m_*(\beta)=2$ otherwise.  For every $m\ge m_*(\beta)$ and $n\ge m$,
the length-$n$ block map is injective, $\Phi_{\beta,m}$ is a conjugacy onto
its SFT image, and its inverse has memory zero and anticipation at most
$m-1$.
\end{introtheorem}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:full-quadratic-pisot-threshold}.  The exceptional
families are exactly the two loci where the aperture-two map identifies the
constant zero and maximal-digit points.  Away from them, two consecutive
outputs admit an explicit decoder.  At and above threshold, the same
arithmetic gives finite-block onset, the right Fischer cover, and the Markov
order of the image.  Future-only depth is more delicate: Theorem
\ref{thm:quadratic-optimal-causal-inverse} gives the value three throughout
the positive-conjugate chamber and the value two throughout the
negative-conjugate chamber, for every $m\ge3$.

The hypotheses in this statement select the greedy Parry language and a
rigid arithmetic setting.  Quadratic Pisot numbers occupy two
parameter chambers according to the sign of the conjugate.  Their greedy
Parry words give recurrences $Q_{j+2}=aQ_{j+1}\pm bQ_j$, and the associated
colexicographic ranks fill the complete interval
$\{0,\ldots,Q_m-1\}$.  This interval property makes cyclic reduction
intrinsic to the language.  It also identifies the exceptional threshold
loci: at aperture two the wrap parameter reaches the maximal digit exactly
on the negative diagonal and the positive norm-one family.  The resulting
constant-point collision disappears at aperture three by the common
separation argument.

The dynamical conclusions use more than finite counting.  Once every
length-$m$ output block determines its overlapping raw block, consecutive
decodings glue to a sliding inverse and give a finite forbidden-word
description of the image.  The same onset length synchronizes the natural
overlap presentation, which identifies its right Fischer cover and Markov
order.  At a critical aperture-two parameter the only unresolved path is the
pair of constant points, producing a strictly sofic image with a single
double periodic fiber.  These features explain why the threshold theorem is
also a local-structure theorem for the image shift.

The Fibonacci system is the smallest exceptional parameter.  Its explicit
three-window decoder supplies a readable prototype for the general
recurrence argument, while its aperture-two branch consists only of the two
constant binary points.  The metallic family is recorded later as a direct
quadratic specialization rather than as an independent classification.

\begin{introtheorem}[Arithmetic difference quotient]
Let $\beta$ be a nonintegral simple-Parry number and $m\ge2$.  In the finite
graph $\Gamma_{\beta,m}$ on coordinate differences, the future-only inverse
length is one plus the longest directed path beginning with nonzero first
coordinate.  It is infinite exactly when such paths have unbounded length,
and $\Phi_{\beta,m}$ is injective exactly when the graph has no nonzero
bi-infinite path.  For simple-Parry ranks, injectivity is equivalent to
finite future-only inverse length.
\end{introtheorem}

The full statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-parry-causal-collision} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:simple-parry-causal-completeness}.  It is a quotient of
the standard fiber product by coordinatewise difference.  Every bounded
difference is realized by two raw digits, so the quotient loses no
collisions.  In particular it gives an exact depth formula rather than only
an injectivity test, and the unique predecessor of the zero state supplies
the future-only conclusion.  The aperture-two graph has at most three edge
types, leading to Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-parry-aperture-two}; none of these
arguments uses a Pisot estimate.

The quotient graph retains the directional information needed for inversion.
Its vertices are $(m-1)$-tuples of bounded coordinate differences, and an
edge appends the next difference subject to one rank congruence.  A path that
starts with nonzero first coordinate is precisely a pair of finite raw blocks
whose first symbols are still indistinguishable.  Its length measures the
delay before that ambiguity disappears.  Reachable cycles give periodic
collisions; in the acyclic case the longest path gives the decoder length.
For canonical simple-Parry ranks, the zero state has a unique predecessor,
so injectivity forces every transient beginning with unequal initial symbols
to terminate in finitely many steps.
This converts the classical injectivity decision into a future-only inverse
criterion with an explicit finite state bound.

At aperture two the recurrence has exposed only its first nonleading
coefficient.  The difference graph consequently has the zero edge and at
most one symmetric pair of nonzero edges.  According to their endpoints,
the fold is either a local bijection, has a two-output inverse, or has the
single constant branch pair.  This trichotomy illustrates what the quotient
adds to a pair-graph decision: it records both the existence and the exact
lifetime of a collision.

\begin{introtheorem}[Unbounded cubic depth]
For $n\ge4$, let $\beta_n\in(n,n+1)$ be the cubic simple-Parry Pisot root of
\[
x^3-(n+2)x^2+2nx-n,
\]
and put $m_n=n-1$.  Then $\Phi_{\beta_n,m_n}$ is injective and
\[
\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta_n,m_n)=n-1.
\]
Consequently future-only inverse lengths at injective apertures are
unbounded among nonintegral cubic simple-Parry Pisot systems.
\end{introtheorem}

This family separates algebraic degree from inverse depth.  The bases all
have degree three, but their relevant apertures and terminal collision paths
grow with $n$.  A fixed cubic recurrence therefore supports paths whose
extinction time is controlled by the arithmetic parameter.  The theorem
shows that a degree bound alone cannot provide a uniform future-only inverse
bound across systems.

The proof, given in Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-unbounded-causal-depth}, has two
ingredients.  A carry-exclusion estimate forces every collision path
after its first window into the zero-carry regime.  An induction then
classifies the two terminal nonzero paths and shows that one more output
eliminates both.  Bassino's expansion data identify the family; the
unbounded depth comes from the collision analysis.
