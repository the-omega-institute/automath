Verdict
Split. The residual objection is not merely the unavoidable price of scope. It is a correctable packaging defect: the manuscript contains a short, field-facing Pisot-transient paper inside a much longer, independently coherent paper about one cyclic language-rank recoding.
The present title advertises the map-specific threshold and inverse-depth narrative, even though the bounded-zero theorem is now the strongest externally recognizable result.  The manuscript itself confirms the structural separation: Section 2 proves the fixed-system theorem, sharpness and arbitrary-alphabet bounded-zero theorem, while Sections 3–7 develop Fibonacci, metallic, quadratic and simple-Parry classifications. 
My recommendation is therefore:

Extract the Section 2 theorem package as one paper; rebuild Sections 3–7 as a separate cyclic-rank classification paper. Submit the extracted paper to ETDS.

I would not submit the 67-page version as assembled, and I would not disguise the split by putting thirty-plus pages of independent theorems into “supplementary material.”

4. Does the restructuring cost mathematics?
No. In particular, it does not weaken the fixed-system sharpness of (A2)–(A3).
The proof of (A2)–(A3) is already self-contained within Section 2. It uses:


the recurrence for Qj​(θ);


the direct carry bound;


the explicit contraction threshold;


the suffix-sum recurrence;


the exact terminal equations;


the m=4,5 exhaustion.


It does not invoke the Fibonacci, metallic or quadratic threshold sections. The later material supplies comparisons, not logical support.
More strongly, the proof of Theorem 2.6 explicitly says that the words Em​, read merely as coefficient words, give the sharp lower-order paths independently of their realization as differences of raw cyclic-rank inputs.  Thus the same fixed cubic object witnesses sharpness on both sides of the paper:


for causal inverse depth in (A1);


for bounded-zero transient length in Theorem 2.6.


That dual role becomes more visible, not less visible, after extraction.
What is lost is only comparative context. Article A should retain about two pages explaining:


the distinction between fixed-system sharpness and the varying-system Bassino phenomenon;


that the fixed cubic is a canonical simple-Parry example but does not need the general simple-Parry theory;


that quadratic systems admit much smaller exact thresholds, without reproducing their classification.


A citation to Article B, or to its preprint if the papers are developed concurrently, is sufficient. No metallic or quadratic proof is needed for a referee to understand what (A2)–(A3) establish.
5. My decision as an author
I would split, and I would submit Article A to ETDS.
I would not submit the assembled 67-page manuscript to TAMS first. The present package makes a TAMS referee decide whether one field-facing theorem justifies fifty pages of additional theorems about a newly defined recoding. That is an avoidable test of patience and framing rather than a test of the strongest mathematics.
I would also not choose the compromise of a 40–44-page recast unless there were a strong nonmathematical reason to keep one publication. It improves the odds but leaves value on the table.
The extracted paper gives the bounded-zero theorem the status it has earned, makes (A1) an exact application rather than the paper’s definitional centre, and lets (A2)–(A3) close the article with fixed-system sharpness. The companion then becomes an honest exact-classification paper rather than a very large downstream section.
So the single recommendation is:

Split now; title the first paper around linear overlap transients for bounded zero representations; keep the complete fixed cubic sharpness proof in it; submit that paper to ETDS.
