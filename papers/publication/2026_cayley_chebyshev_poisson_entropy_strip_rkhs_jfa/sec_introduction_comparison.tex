\subsection*{Comparison by mechanism}
\label{subsec:intro-recent-jfa-comparison}

Classical moment decompositions expand a function or convolution solution
into masses and derivatives of a fundamental kernel; see
Duoandikoetxea--Zuazua \cite{DuoandikoetxeaZuazua1992} and the subsequent
large-time diffusion literature
\cite{EscobedoZuazua1991,IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2017,
IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2022RefinedFractional}.  In particular,
Ishige--Kawakami--Michihisa treat every \(0<\alpha<2\) and every weighted
moment order \(K\ge0\): for data in
\(L^1(\RR^d,(1+|x|)^K\dd x)\), their Theorem~1.1 subtracts the
moment-weighted derivatives of the fractional heat kernel and proves the
corresponding scaled \(L^q\) and weighted \(L^1\) remainder decay, including
the range \(K\ge\alpha\) where the stable semigroup does not preserve that
weighted space.  The refined follow-up also expands the inhomogeneous and
nonlinear fractional-diffusion solutions.  Those results supply the
density-level moment expansion.  They do not estimate the matched quotient
in the critical space \(L^{q_{\alpha,d}}(p_1^{(\alpha,d)}\dd y)\), transfer
it through the nonlinear KL functional, or identify the optimal uniform
moment exponent above.  Nor do they state the two-background, all-order
relative-entropy coefficient and its optimal exponent
\(p_{r,\alpha,d}\).  These are the increments in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-B}.

Relative \(\Phi\)-entropy and pure-jump semigroup calculus already provide
the formal two-solution identity
\eqref{eq:intro-general-two-solution-generator-identity}; see Chafa{\"\i}
\cite{Chafai2004PhiEntropies} and, for early nonlinear/nonlocal energy forms,
Bakry--Coulhon--Ledoux--Saloff-Coste
\cite{BakryCoulhonLedouxSaloffCoste1995}.  Its one-sided Bregman form for two
forward Kolmogorov solutions on finite or countable state spaces is explicit
in Hilder--Peletier--Sharma--Tse, who trace entropy contraction to Voigt
\cite{HilderPeletierSharmaTse2020EntropyDistance,
Voigt1981StochasticOperators}.  The Gaussian moving-reference precedent and
its integral representation are due to Hirata--Nemoto--Yoshida
\cite{HirataNemotoYoshida2012IntegralRepresentation}.  The contribution here
is the stable-continuum measure-data domain and endpoint theorem for this
classical two-solution entropy-production identity.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main-C}
is its finite-variance Cauchy specialization.

At formula level, the polarized Hardy--Stein identity
\cite{BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein} states, for its
symmetric Markov semigroup and \(p\ge2\),
\[
\int f g^{\langle p-1\rangle}\dd x
=\int_0^\infty\!\iint
\mathcal J_p(P_s\Phi(x),P_s\Phi(y))J(x,y)\dd x\dd y\dd s,
\qquad \Phi=(f,g),
\]
where, for \(w,z\in\RR^2\),
\[
\mathcal J_p(w,z)=z_1z_2^{\langle p-1\rangle}
-w_1w_2^{\langle p-1\rangle}-w_2^{\langle p-1\rangle}(z_1-w_1)
-(p-1)w_1|w_2|^{p-2}(z_2-w_2).
\]
This is a fixed power pairing.  It does not specialize directly to
\[
\int h_t\log(h_t/g_t)\dd x,
\]
nor does its \(p\ge2\) statement supply the logarithmic endpoint.  More
importantly for the present theorem,
it does not by specialization verify the compact-window bounds and noncompact
Green pairings for \(h_t/g_t\), or prove \(H(T)\to0\).  Those are precisely
the conclusions isolated in Theorem~\ref{thm:main-C}.  A broader comparison
by mechanism is given in Appendix~\ref{app:related-work-scope}.

\subsection*{Convention and organization}

The paper uses the extended nonnegative Kullback--Leibler functional of
\eqref{eq:extended-kl-definition}.

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation.
Section~\ref{sec:fractional-heat-relative-entropy} proves the field-facing
two-flow dissipation and endpoint theorem.  Sections~\ref{sec:cayley-gate}
and~\ref{sec:haar-pullback} then record the Cayley and Haar-pullback
normalizations, including the notation table used in the entropy arguments.
Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} proves the coefficient hierarchy, the
variance threshold, and the multidimensional quotient criteria.  It begins
with the detailed proof map, so the introduction need not carry the internal
hypothesis diagram.  Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy} proves the
compact-window Bregman domain bounds and the Cauchy/KL tail identity.  The
RKHS and lattice material is secondary.  The appendices contain the
related-work table, normalization details, auxiliary coefficient
certificates, and the proof of the regularly varying missing-moment boundary
layer.
