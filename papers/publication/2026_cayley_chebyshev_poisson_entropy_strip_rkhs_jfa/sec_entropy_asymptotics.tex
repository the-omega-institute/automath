\section{Poisson Smoothing and Entropy Asymptotics}%
\label{sec:entropy-asymptotics}

\paragraph{Local hypotheses.}
Throughout this section we write
$\Delta:=\gamma-\bar\gamma$ and
$\mu_n:=\int_{\RR}\Delta^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma)$ whenever it exists.  The
assumptions are local:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-second-order} uses the mean, variance,
and finite centred fourth moment through a direct Taylor expansion of
$R_\varepsilon$.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} uses a finite centred
$(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment, the density remainder, and Cayley parity.
Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-moment-ball-entropy-remainder} gives the uniform
version on centred $(2N+\eta)$-moment balls.
\item Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-cauchy-moment-inversion-by-phase-modes}
uses the same finite mode calculus at order four and a finite centred
fifth absolute moment to recover $(\sigma^2,\mu_3,\mu_4)$ from phase
Fourier coefficients.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-grid-poisson-moment-recovery} gives finite-grid,
finite-time recovery from exact phase samples.  It uses
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-grid-coefficient-recovery} to remove aliasing for
the truncated finite mode polynomial and the uniform order-five remainder to
quantify the residual large-time error.
\item Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} is proved directly from the
seventh-moment mode remainder.  Its coefficients use the formal mode algebra;
the $N=4$ finite-moment theorem and compact-support analytic theorem give the
same notation only under their stronger hypotheses.
The displayed coefficient arithmetic uses Lemma~\ref{lem:mode-parity-vanish}
and Appendix~\ref{app:entropy-integrals}.
\item Proposition~\ref{prop:eighth-order-finite-phase-verification} uses
the finite Fourier support of the Cayley--Chebyshev modes and the
finite-grid aliasing formula to compute the coefficient integrals
through order~$t^{-8}$ exactly on any phase grid $\mu_N$ with
$N\ge9$.
\item Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}
and~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}, together with
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-two-point-defect-rigidity}, use the same finite
centred seventh absolute moment hypothesis and the explicit coefficients from
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.
\item The fourth-moment identity \eqref{eq:defect-sixth-fourth-moment} and
coupling estimate \eqref{eq:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling} use only the
mean, variance, and finite centred fourth moment; they define the standalone
algebraic defect $\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)$.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-stability} is a coefficient-level
seventh-moment theorem for the sixth-order asymptotic defect, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-finite-time-defect-stability} gives the large-time
version using the order-nine remainder in Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.
Both use the mean, variance, and finite centred seventh absolute moment; only
then does Lemma~\ref{lem:seventh-moment-bridge} identify
$\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)$ with the genuine $t^{-6}$ entropy defect.
Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-finite-time-defect-stability} is the uniform version
on variance-fixed seventh-moment shells.
\end{itemize}
The appendix is used only to evaluate the displayed $t^{-6}$ and $t^{-8}$
coefficients in Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.  Below, the
fourth-moment identity is kept as the algebraic polynomial
$\Delta_6^{(4)}$ until the seventh-moment bridge gives it entropy-asymptotic
meaning.  Law-dependent and shell-uniform finite-time constants are separated
in the corresponding corollaries.

\paragraph{Section guide.}
This section first fixes the Cayley--Poisson normalization and the formal
coefficient algebra, then proves the finite-moment entropy asymptotics and the
seventh-moment eighth-order defect formulas, and finally derives
coefficient-level and finite-time stability.  The results are used in the
following order.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Normalization tier.}
Subsection~\ref{subsec:cayley-entropy} fixes the entropy identity,
the low-order Poisson comparison, and the Cayley--Chebyshev mode expansion.
These results depend only on the Poisson kernel identities and finite Taylor
expansions stated there.
\item \emph{Algebraic tier.}
Subsection~\ref{subsec:all-order-coefficient-calculus} is algebraic.
The universal formal polynomials $A_k^{\rm form}$ and the mode integrals
$I(n_1,\ldots,n_r)$ are defined before any law is evaluated or any
asymptotic limit is taken; their odd-order cancellation follows from the
parity of the modes.  Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}
then evaluates those polynomials at a law only when the required moments
exist.
\item \emph{Analytic tier.}
Subsection~\ref{subsec:finite-moment-entropy} is the finite-moment passage
step.  Under the finite centred moment hypotheses appearing in
Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-uniform-remainder} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder}, the evaluated algebraic
coefficient $A_k(\nu)$ is the actual coefficient of the large-time Poisson
relative entropy expansion.
\item \emph{Defect tier.}
Subsection~\ref{subsec:eighth-order-defects} is a specialization of
the preceding descent.  Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} supplies the
sixth- and eighth-order entropy jet under a finite centred seventh moment,
using the formal coefficient algebra only for notation and coefficient
identification.  Corollary~\ref{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization} factors that
jet through the centred moment jet of order six,
Proposition~\ref{prop:eighth-order-finite-phase-verification} checks the
required finite Fourier integrals on phase grids below the aliasing threshold,
and Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}
and~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification} use only those explicit
coefficients to prove the defect ladder and the two-point extremal
amplification statement.
\item \emph{Stability tier.}
Subsection~\ref{subsec:fourth-moment-stability} is downstream of the
defect formulas.  The symbols $C_6^{(4)}$ and $\Delta_6^{(4)}$ are only
fourth-moment algebraic polynomials until Lemma~\ref{lem:seventh-moment-bridge}
places them under the seventh-moment entropy expansion.  Only after that
bridge does Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-stability} become a stability
theorem for the genuine sixth-order entropy defect, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-finite-time-defect-stability} becomes its
finite-time consequence.  Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-finite-time-defect-stability}
is the corresponding controlled-class consequence, obtained by bounding the
same constants uniformly on a fixed variance and seventh-moment shell.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the coefficient algebra, finite-moment remainders, finite-grid
verification, defect identities, and stability consequences have distinct
hypotheses and are used in this order.  In particular, finite grids do not
replace the asymptotic passage, and the fourth-moment polynomial is not treated
as an entropy coefficient before the seventh-moment bridge.

\subsection{Entropy in Cayley coordinates}
\label{subsec:cayley-entropy}

The logarithmic Jacobian of the Cayley coordinate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:precision-potential}
\mathcal P(x):=-\log J(x),\qquad
J(x):=\upsilon'(x)=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})},
\end{equation}
equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:precision-potential-expanded}
\mathcal P(x)=\log\pi+\log(1+x^{2}).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Entropy identity in Cayley coordinates]%
\label{thm:cayley-entropy-identity}
Let $X$ be a real-valued random variable with density $f_X$ such that
$h_{\mathrm{diff}}(X)>-\infty$ and $\EE[\mathcal P(X)]<\infty$.  Set
$U:=\upsilon(X)\in\UU$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayley-entropy-identity}
h_{\mathrm{diff}}(X)
=h_{\mathrm{diff}}(U)+\EE[\mathcal P(X)].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The density of $U$ is
\[
f_U(u)=f_X(\tan(\pi u))\,\pi(1+\tan^2(\pi u)).
\]
The standard change-of-variables formula for differential entropy
\cite[Chapter~9]{CoverThomas2006} gives
\[
h_{\mathrm{diff}}(X)
=h_{\mathrm{diff}}(U)+\EE\!\left[\log\frac{1}{\abs{\upsilon'(X)}}\right]
=h_{\mathrm{diff}}(U)+\EE[\mathcal P(X)].
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Poisson smoothing}

For $t>0$ and $x\in\RR$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-kernel}
P_t(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{t}{x^{2}+t^{2}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Basic identities for the Poisson kernels]%
\label{prop:poisson-identities}
For $s,t>0$ one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-basic}
P_t(x)=\frac{1}{t}P_1\!\left(\frac{x}{t}\right),
\qquad
\widehat{P_t}(\xi)=e^{-t\abs{\xi}},
\qquad
P_t*P_s=P_{t+s}.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
\mu=P_1(x)\dd x,
\qquad
P_t*\mu=P_{t+1}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The scaling identity is immediate from the definition.  The Fourier
transform of~$P_1$ is $e^{-\abs{\xi}}$
\cite[Chapter~III]{Stein1971}; scaling yields
$\widehat{P_t}(\xi)=e^{-t\abs{\xi}}$.  The convolution law follows by
multiplying Fourier transforms.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Standard facts on the Poisson semigroup]%
\label{prop:poisson-semigroup-law}
For $1\le p\le\infty$ and $t>0$ define
$T_t f:=P_t*f$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\|T_t f\|_{L^p(\RR)}\le \|f\|_{L^p(\RR)}$ for every $f\in L^p$;
\item $T_{t+s}=T_tT_s$ for all $s,t>0$;
\item for $1\le p<\infty$, $T_t f\to f$ in $L^p(\RR)$ as $t\downarrow 0$;
\item on $L^2(\RR)$, $\widehat{T_t f}(\xi)=e^{-t\abs{\xi}}\widehat f(\xi)$
      and the generator is $-|D|$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\int_{\RR}P_t(x)\dd x=1$ and $P_t\ge 0$, Young's inequality gives
(i).  The semigroup law is Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-identities}.
Strong continuity for $1\le p<\infty$ follows because $\{P_t\}_{t>0}$ is
an approximate identity.  The $L^2$-multiplier statement is exactly
\eqref{eq:poisson-basic}; the generator follows from
\[
\frac{e^{-t\abs{\xi}}-1}{t}\longrightarrow -\abs{\xi}
\qquad(t\downarrow 0)
\]
in the Fourier representation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The preceding proposition is standard and is included only to fix the
normalization of the Poisson kernel and of the generator $-|D|$ used in
the sequel; see, for example, the usual Poisson-integral and semigroup
formulations in \cite[Chapter~III]{Stein1971} and
\cite[Chapter~17]{Grafakos2014}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Large-time comparison with a translated Poisson profile}

\begin{proposition}[Second-order approximation]%
\label{prop:poisson-second-order}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean
\[
\bar\gamma:=\int_{\RR}\gamma\dd\nu(\gamma),
\]
variance
\[
\sigma^{2}:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^{2}\dd\nu(\gamma),
\]
and finite centred fourth moment.  Define
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma).
\]
Let
\[
U(y):=\frac{3y^{2}-1}{(1+y^{2})^{2}}.
\]
Then there exists a constant $C_\nu>0$ such that for every $t\ge 1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-profile}
\sup_{x\in\RR}\left|
\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}
-1
-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{t^{2}}U\!\left(\frac{x-\bar\gamma}{t}\right)
\right|
\le \frac{C_\nu}{t^{3}}.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-l1-rate}
\|h_t-g_t\|_{L^{1}(\RR)}=O(t^{-2}),
\qquad
\|h_t-g_t\|_{L^\infty(\RR)}=O(t^{-3}).
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-kl-rate}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=O(t^{-4}).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Delta:=\gamma-\bar\gamma$ and $x=\bar\gamma+ty$.  Then
\[
\frac{P_t(x-\gamma)}{g_t(x)}
=\frac{1+y^{2}}{1+(y-\Delta/t)^{2}}.
\]
Set $\varepsilon:=\Delta/t$.  A direct algebraic computation gives the
exact identity
\begin{align*}
\frac{1+y^{2}}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^{2}}
&=1+\frac{2y}{1+y^{2}}\varepsilon
+\frac{3y^{2}-1}{(1+y^{2})^{2}}\varepsilon^{2} \\
&\quad
+\frac{4y(y^{2}-1)\varepsilon^{3}-(3y^{2}-1)\varepsilon^{4}}
{(1+y^{2})^{2}(1+(y-\varepsilon)^{2})}.
\end{align*}
Since
\[
\frac{4\abs{y}\abs{y^{2}-1}}{(1+y^{2})^{2}}\le 2,
\qquad
\frac{\abs{3y^{2}-1}}{(1+y^{2})^{2}}\le 1,
\]
the remainder is bounded by $2\abs{\varepsilon}^{3}+\abs{\varepsilon}^{4}$
uniformly in~$y$.  Taking expectation and using $\EE[\Delta]=0$ yields
\[
\frac{h_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}{g_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}
=1+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{t^{2}}U(y)+R_t(y),
\]
with
\[
\sup_{y\in\RR}\abs{R_t(y)}
\le \frac{2\EE[\abs{\Delta}^{3}]+\EE[\abs{\Delta}^{4}]}{t^{3}}
\]
for $t\ge 1$.  This proves \eqref{eq:poisson-profile}.

The function~$U$ is bounded, so
$\sup_x\abs{h_t(x)/g_t(x)-1}=O(t^{-2})$.  Since $\int_{\RR}g_t=1$,
\[
\|h_t-g_t\|_{L^1}
\le \sup_{x}\abs{\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}-1}\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\dd x
=O(t^{-2}).
\]
Also $\|g_t\|_{L^\infty}=1/(\pi t)$, so
\[
\|h_t-g_t\|_{L^\infty}
\le \|g_t\|_{L^\infty}\sup_{x}\abs{\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}-1}
=O(t^{-3}).
\]
Let
\[
\delta_t(x):=\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}-1.
\]
The bound \eqref{eq:poisson-profile} implies that
$\|\delta_t\|_{L^\infty}=O(t^{-2})$, so for large $t$ one has
$\|\delta_t\|_{L^\infty}\le 1/2$.  Since
$\int g_t\delta_t\dd x=\int(h_t-g_t)\dd x=0$ and
\[
(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\le 2s^{2}
\qquad(\abs{s}\le 1/2),
\]
it follows that
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\bigl((1+\delta_t(x))\log(1+\delta_t(x))-\delta_t(x)\bigr)\dd x
\le 2\|\delta_t\|_{L^\infty}^{2}.
\]
Hence \eqref{eq:poisson-kl-rate} holds.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cayley--Chebyshev mode calculus}

The first two results of this subsection are algebraic:
Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} compute the
Cauchy--Haar mode integrals and the formal polynomials $A_k^{\rm form}$.
Theorems~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder},
\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}, and
\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder} then identify the evaluated
polynomials with Poisson entropy coefficients under the finite centred
$(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment hypothesis, with fixed-law constants.  The
compact-support theorem below gives a separate analytic-interchange statement,
while Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} and the subsequent defect and
stability results use the resulting coefficient calculus at the first
nontrivial orders.

For $\varepsilon\in\RR$ and $y\in\RR$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayley-comparison-kernel}
R_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $y$, the Taylor expansion of $R_\varepsilon(y)$ at
$\varepsilon=0$ defines coefficients $u_n(y)$ through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayley-mode-series}
R_\varepsilon(y)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u_n(y)\varepsilon^n,
\qquad
|\varepsilon|<\sqrt{1+y^2}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Cayley--Chebyshev mode formula]%
\label{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode}
Let $U_n$ denote the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind.  If
$y=\tan\theta$ with $\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$, then for every $n\ge 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayley-chebyshev-mode}
u_n(\tan\theta)=\cos^n\theta\,U_n(\sin\theta).
\end{equation}
For $n\ge 1$ this is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayley-chebyshev-trig}
u_n(\tan\theta)
=\cos^{n-1}\theta\,
\sin\!\Bigl((n+1)\Bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\Bigr)\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayley-mode-parity}
u_n(-y)=(-1)^n u_n(y)
\qquad(n\ge 0).
\end{equation}
Moreover, for every integer $m\ge 1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayley-mode-even-fourier}
u_{2m}(\tan\theta)
=(-1)^m 2^{-(2m-1)}
\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\binom{2m-1}{j}\cos(2(j+1)\theta),
\end{equation}
and for every integer $m\ge 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayley-mode-odd-fourier}
u_{2m+1}(\tan\theta)
=(-1)^m 2^{-2m}
\sum_{j=0}^{2m}\binom{2m}{j}\sin(2(j+1)\theta).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $y=\tan\theta$ one has
\[
R_\varepsilon(\tan\theta)
=\frac{1}{1-2(\varepsilon\cos\theta)\sin\theta+(\varepsilon\cos\theta)^2}.
\]
The Chebyshev generating function
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}U_n(x)r^n=\frac{1}{1-2xr+r^2}
\]
\cite[Chapters~1 and~5]{MasonHandscomb2002}
therefore yields \eqref{eq:cayley-chebyshev-mode} with
$x=\sin\theta$ and $r=\varepsilon\cos\theta$.  Since
\[
U_n(\sin\theta)
=\frac{\sin((n+1)(\pi/2-\theta))}{\cos\theta},
\]
formula \eqref{eq:cayley-chebyshev-trig} follows for $n\ge 1$.  The
parity relation \eqref{eq:cayley-mode-parity} is immediate from
\eqref{eq:cayley-chebyshev-trig}.

For the finite Fourier expansions, write
\[
u_{2m}(\tan\theta)=(-1)^m\cos^{2m-1}\theta\,\cos((2m+1)\theta),
\]
\[
u_{2m+1}(\tan\theta)=(-1)^m\cos^{2m}\theta\,\sin((2m+2)\theta),
\]
and use
\[
1+e^{2\ii\theta}=2e^{\ii\theta}\cos\theta.
\]
Taking real and imaginary parts after the binomial expansion gives
\eqref{eq:cayley-mode-even-fourier} and
\eqref{eq:cayley-mode-odd-fourier}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{All-order coefficient calculus}%
\label{subsec:all-order-coefficient-calculus}

This subsection concerns the coefficient algebra.  The display
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient} defines a formal expression;
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation} evaluates it
only when the required moments exist; and the finite Fourier theorem later in
the subsection reduces the required mode integrals to exact Laurent or
root-of-unity calculations.  Finite-moment entropy remainders and analytic
interchange enter in the following subsection with their separate hypotheses.

The coefficient calculus is universal once the Cayley--Chebyshev modes have
been fixed.  For integers $r\ge2$ and $n_1,\dots,n_r\ge2$ define the
Cauchy-weighted mode integral
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode-integral-definition}
I(n_1,\dots,n_r):=I_{n_1,\dots,n_r}:=
\int_{\RR}u_{n_1}(y)\cdots u_{n_r}(y)\,\omega(\dd y),
\qquad
\omega(\dd y):=\frac{\dd y}{\pi(1+y^2)}.
\end{equation}
Everything in this block is finite algebra.  The modes $u_n$, their Laurent
polynomials $Q_n$, the integrals $I(n_1,\ldots,n_r)$, and the formal
coefficient polynomials $A_k^{\rm form}$ are defined before any asymptotic
truncation is taken and before any measure-level moments are evaluated.
The finite-moment density and entropy remainders enter only in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:finite-moment-entropy}, where this algebraic
polynomial is identified with the actual large-time entropy polynomial.
For $k\ge0$ let
$\mathcal P_k:=\QQ[\mathfrak m_2,\ldots,\mathfrak m_k]$, with the convention
$\mathcal P_0=\mathcal P_1=\QQ$.  In this formal polynomial algebra set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient}
A_k^{\rm form}:=
\sum_{r=2}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}
\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\substack{n_1,\dots,n_r\ge2\\ n_1+\cdots+n_r=k}}
\mathfrak m_{n_1}\cdots\mathfrak m_{n_r}\,I(n_1,\dots,n_r),
\end{equation}
with the convention that an empty sum is zero.  Thus
$A_0^{\rm form}=A_1^{\rm form}=A_2^{\rm form}=A_3^{\rm form}=0$.  If a
probability measure $\nu$ has mean $\bar\gamma$ and centred moments
$\mu_n=\int(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^n\dd\nu$ through order~$k$, we write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}
A_k(\nu):={\rm ev}_{\nu,k}(A_k^{\rm form}),
\qquad
{\rm ev}_{\nu,k}(\mathfrak m_n)=\mu_n,
\end{equation}
for the resulting finite-moment evaluation.
The phrase ``moments through order~$k$'' is only a uniform sufficient
convention: a particular polynomial $A_k^{\rm form}$ may be evaluated as soon
as the moments that actually occur in it exist.  In particular the printed
$A_8^{\rm form}$ below involves only $\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6$, which is why the
seventh-moment eighth-order bridge can use the notation $A_8(\nu)$ without
invoking the finite-$(8+\eta)$ theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Universal entropy coefficient package]%
\label{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients}
Let $k\ge0$.  In the completed formal Laurent algebra
$\mathcal P_k[[t^{-1}]]$, truncate every product after collecting total
$t$-degree at most~$k$.  If
\[
\delta^{\rm form}_t(y)=\sum_{n=2}^{k}\mathfrak m_n u_n(y)t^{-n},
\]
then the coefficient of $t^{-k}$ in the formal expression
\[
\int_{\RR}\bigl((1+\delta^{\rm form}_t)\log(1+\delta^{\rm form}_t)
-\delta^{\rm form}_t\bigr)\,\dd\omega
\]
is precisely $A_k^{\rm form}$ in
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient}.  Moreover, if $\nu$ is a
probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean $\bar\gamma$ and centred moments
$\mu_n=\int(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^n\dd\nu$ through order~$k$, then the evaluated
coefficient defined by \eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation} is
the explicit finite polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-coefficient-theorem-evaluated}
A_k(\nu)
=
\sum_{r=2}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}
\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\substack{n_1,\dots,n_r\ge2\\ n_1+\cdots+n_r=k}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r}\,I(n_1,\dots,n_r),
\end{equation}
and is equivalently the coefficient of $t^{-k}$ in
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-coefficient-theorem-generating}
\int_{\RR}
\left(
\left(1+\sum_{n=2}^{k}\mu_n\,u_n(y)t^{-n}\right)
\log\left(1+\sum_{n=2}^{k}\mu_n\,u_n(y)t^{-n}\right)
-\sum_{n=2}^{k}\mu_n\,u_n(y)t^{-n}
\right)\dd\omega,
\end{equation}
after formal truncation at total $t$-degree~$k$.  Finally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-odd-coefficients-vanish}
A_k^{\rm form}=0\qquad\text{whenever }k\text{ is odd}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $A_k(\nu)=0$ for odd~$k$ whenever the moments required to evaluate
$A_k(\nu)$ exist.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Taylor series
\[
(1+s)\log(1+s)-s
=\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}s^r
\]
has no constant or linear term.  In the formal substitution for
$\delta_t^{\rm form}$, a monomial in the $r$th power has the form
\[
\mathfrak m_{n_1}\cdots\mathfrak m_{n_r}
u_{n_1}(y)\cdots u_{n_r}(y)t^{-(n_1+\cdots+n_r)},
\qquad n_j\ge2.
\]
For fixed total degree~$k$ there are only finitely many such monomials, and
necessarily $2\le r\le\lfloor k/2\rfloor$.  Integrating the degree-$k$ part
against~$\omega$ gives exactly \eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient}.
Evaluation by ${\rm ev}_{\nu,k}$ is a homomorphism from the finite polynomial
algebra $\mathcal P_k$ to~$\RR$.  Applying it to
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient} gives
\eqref{eq:universal-coefficient-theorem-evaluated}, and applying it
coefficient-by-coefficient to the finite truncated formal generating identity
gives \eqref{eq:universal-coefficient-theorem-generating}.  This evaluation
uses only the centred moments through order~$k$.

It remains to prove the odd cancellation.  By
\eqref{eq:cayley-mode-parity}, $u_n(-y)=(-1)^n u_n(y)$, while
$\omega(\dd y)$ is even.  Hence the integrand in
$I(n_1,\dots,n_r)$ is odd whenever $n_1+\cdots+n_r$ is odd, and then the
integral vanishes.  Every summand in $A_k^{\rm form}$ has total index~$k$, so
all summands vanish when $k$ is odd.  Evaluating this formal identity gives
the asserted odd vanishing of $A_k(\nu)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Universal entropy coefficients: finite-moment layer]%
\label{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}
Let $k\ge0$, and let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean
$\bar\gamma$ and centred moments through order~$k$.  Then the quantity
$A_k(\nu)$ in \eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation} is a finite
polynomial in the actual centred moments of~$\nu$ and in the universal mode
integrals \eqref{eq:mode-integral-definition}.  If $k$ is odd, then
$A_k(\nu)=0$.  No measure-level value of $A_k(\nu)$ is asserted when the
required moments do not exist.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Evaluation by ${\rm ev}_{\nu,k}$ is a homomorphism from the finite polynomial
algebra $\mathcal P_k$ to~$\RR$.  Applying it to the identity of
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} gives exactly
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}.  The odd cancellation is
the image of \eqref{eq:universal-odd-coefficients-vanish} under the same
evaluation map.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Universal coefficient formula]%
\label{cor:universal-entropy-coefficient-package}
Let $k\ge0$, and let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean
$\bar\gamma$ and centred moments
$\mu_n=\int(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^n\dd\nu$ through order~$k$.  Then the formal
identity of Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} and the
evaluation rule of Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}
define the same coefficient: $A_k(\nu)$ is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-coefficient-package-evaluated}
A_k(\nu)
=
\sum_{r=2}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}
\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\substack{n_1,\dots,n_r\ge2\\ n_1+\cdots+n_r=k}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r}\,I(n_1,\dots,n_r),
\end{equation}
and the same number is the coefficient of $t^{-k}$ in the evaluated finite
formal expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-coefficient-package-generating}
\int_{\RR}
\left(
\left(1+\sum_{n=2}^{k}\mu_n\,u_n(y)t^{-n}\right)
\log\left(1+\sum_{n=2}^{k}\mu_n\,u_n(y)t^{-n}\right)
-\sum_{n=2}^{k}\mu_n\,u_n(y)t^{-n}
\right)\dd\omega,
\end{equation}
after formal truncation at total $t$-degree~$k$.  In particular
$A_k(\nu)=0$ for odd~$k$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The polynomial $A_k^{\rm form}$ is a finite sum in the algebra
$\mathcal P_k$, and ${\rm ev}_{\nu,k}$ is the homomorphism sending
$\mathfrak m_n$ to~$\mu_n$.  Applying this homomorphism to
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient} gives
\eqref{eq:universal-coefficient-package-evaluated}.  Applying the same
homomorphism coefficient-by-coefficient to the finite $t^{-k}$ part of the
formal identity in Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} gives
\eqref{eq:universal-coefficient-package-generating}.  Finally,
\eqref{eq:universal-odd-coefficients-vanish} evaluates to
$A_k(\nu)=0$ whenever $k$ is odd.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} gives the formal coefficient
identity for the entropy asymptotics, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation} gives its
finite-moment evaluation.  In this paper the asserted large-time expansion is
always the finite-moment statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders},
together with the remainder theorem below; the formal coefficient identity is
used there through Corollary~\ref{cor:universal-entropy-coefficient-package}
to identify the finite polynomial that the density and entropy remainders
justify.  Lemma~\ref{lem:coefficient-regime-separation} records the
formal, evaluated, analytic, and finite-moment meanings of this convention.

\begin{theorem}[Finite Fourier algorithm for mode integrals]%
\label{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}
For each $n\ge1$ define a Laurent polynomial $Q_n$ on the unit circle by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode-laurent-polynomial-definition}
Q_n(e^{2\ii\theta}):=u_n(\tan\theta),
\qquad -\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2.
\end{equation}
Explicitly, for $m\ge1$ and $m\ge0$, respectively,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode-laurent-even}
Q_{2m}(z)=(-1)^m2^{-2m}
\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}\binom{2m-1}{j}
\bigl(z^{j+1}+z^{-(j+1)}\bigr),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode-laurent-odd}
Q_{2m+1}(z)=(-1)^m\frac{2^{-2m}}{2\ii}
\sum_{j=0}^{2m}\binom{2m}{j}
\bigl(z^{j+1}-z^{-(j+1)}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every $r\ge2$ and $n_1,\dots,n_r\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode-integral-constant-term}
I(n_1,\dots,n_r)
=\bigl[z^0\bigr]\prod_{a=1}^{r}Q_{n_a}(z),
\end{equation}
where $[z^0]$ denotes constant Laurent coefficient.  Equivalently, if
$M>n_1+\cdots+n_r$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode-integral-root-of-unity-algorithm}
I(n_1,\dots,n_r)
=\frac1M\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_M}\prod_{a=1}^{r}Q_{n_a}(\zeta).
\end{equation}
The strict inequality $M>n_1+\cdots+n_r$ is the aliasing barrier: the
Laurent support of $\prod_{a=1}^rQ_{n_a}$ is contained in
$[-(n_1+\cdots+n_r),n_1+\cdots+n_r]$, so no nonzero exponent can be congruent
to zero modulo~$M$ under the root-of-unity average.
Thus every formal universal coefficient $A_k^{\rm form}$, and every evaluated
coefficient $A_k(\nu)$ when the required moments exist, is obtained by
finitely many Laurent-polynomial multiplications and constant-coefficient
extractions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equations \eqref{eq:mode-laurent-even} and \eqref{eq:mode-laurent-odd} are
exactly the finite Fourier formulas
\eqref{eq:cayley-mode-even-fourier} and
\eqref{eq:cayley-mode-odd-fourier} after writing
$z=e^{2\ii\theta}$,
\[
\cos(2\ell\theta)=\frac{z^\ell+z^{-\ell}}2,
\qquad
\sin(2\ell\theta)=\frac{z^\ell-z^{-\ell}}{2\ii}.
\]
The change of variables $y=\tan\theta$ gives
\[
\omega(\dd y)=\frac{\dd\theta}{\pi}
\qquad(-\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2),
\]
and $z=e^{2\ii\theta}$ pushes $\dd\theta/\pi$ to normalized Haar measure
on~$\TT$.  Hence $I(n_1,\dots,n_r)$ is the Haar integral of the Laurent
polynomial $\prod_a Q_{n_a}$, which is its constant coefficient.  The
support of $Q_n$ is contained in $\{-n,\dots,-1,1,\dots,n\}$; therefore
the product has Fourier support contained in
$\{-(n_1+\cdots+n_r),\dots,n_1+\cdots+n_r\}$.  Averaging over the $M$th
roots of unity with $M>n_1+\cdots+n_r$ kills every nonzero exponent in this
support and keeps the constant coefficient, proving
\eqref{eq:mode-integral-root-of-unity-algorithm}.  The last assertion
follows by substituting \eqref{eq:mode-integral-constant-term} into
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Below-barrier finite grids for the eighth-order jet]%
\label{cor:below-barrier-finite-grid-eighth-jet}
Let $L\ge9$.  For every product of Cayley modes appearing in the
coefficient extraction of Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} through
order~$t^{-8}$, and also for the odd total-index products whose integrals
give the discarded $t^{-5}$ and $t^{-7}$ coefficients, the Haar integral is
computed exactly by the root-of-unity average on~$\mu_L$.  Equivalently,
for every tuple $(n_1,\ldots,n_r)$ in this coefficient jet one has
\[
I(n_1,\ldots,n_r)
=\frac1L\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_L}\prod_{a=1}^rQ_{n_a}(\zeta),
\]
with no aliasing correction.  Hence the coefficients of $t^{-4}$, $t^{-6}$,
and $t^{-8}$ in Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} are recovered from exact
finite-grid constant-coefficient extraction below the Fourier-degree barrier;
the grid calculation is not a numerical quadrature approximation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient jet through order~$t^{-8}$ is assembled from products of
the modes $u_n$ whose total index $n_1+\cdots+n_r$ is at most~$8$: at the
even orders these are
\[
u_2^2,\quad
u_2u_4,\ u_3^2,\ u_2^3,\quad
u_2u_6,\ u_3u_5,\ u_4^2,\ u_2^2u_4,\ u_2u_3^2,\ u_2^4,
\]
and the odd orders use the analogous total-index $5$ and~$7$ products.
Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} with
$M=L\ge9$ gives $L>n_1+\cdots+n_r$ for every such tuple, so the
root-of-unity average kills all nonzero Laurent exponents in the product
and keeps exactly the constant coefficient.  Substituting these exact
integrals into the universal coefficient formula
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient} gives the displayed eighth-order
coefficient arithmetic in Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.  The odd
products have zero Haar integral by Lemma~\ref{lem:mode-parity-vanish}, and
the same below-barrier average computes that zero exactly.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite mode truncations]%
\label{prop:cayley-mode-remainder}
For every integer $N\ge 1$ there exists a rational function
$v_N:\RR^2\to\RR$, bounded on~$\RR^2$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayley-mode-remainder}
R_\varepsilon(y)
=\sum_{n=0}^{N}u_n(y)\varepsilon^n+\varepsilon^{N+1}v_N(y,\varepsilon)
\qquad(y,\varepsilon\in\RR).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every $x$ and $r$ one has the finite Chebyshev-sum identity
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{N}U_n(x)r^n
=\frac{1-r^{N+1}U_{N+1}(x)+r^{N+2}U_N(x)}{1-2xr+r^2}.
\]
\cite[Chapter~5]{MasonHandscomb2002}
Substituting $x=\sin\theta$ and $r=\varepsilon\cos\theta$ gives
\eqref{eq:cayley-mode-remainder} with
\[
v_N(\tan\theta,\varepsilon)
=\frac{\cos^{N+1}\theta\,U_{N+1}(\sin\theta)
-\varepsilon\cos^{N+2}\theta\,U_N(\sin\theta)}
{1-2(\varepsilon\cos\theta)\sin\theta+(\varepsilon\cos\theta)^2}.
\]
Equivalently, since
\[
1-2(\varepsilon\cos\theta)\sin\theta
   +(\varepsilon\cos\theta)^2
=\frac{1+(\tan\theta-\varepsilon)^2}{1+\tan^2\theta},
\]
we have
\[
v_N(y,\varepsilon)
=\frac{(1+y^2)u_{N+1}(y)-\varepsilon u_N(y)}
       {1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\]
so $v_N$ is rational in $(y,\varepsilon)$.

It remains to prove boundedness.  Put
$c=\cos\theta$, $s=\sin\theta$, and $d=\tan\theta-\varepsilon$.  Then
$\varepsilon c=s-dc$, and the preceding trigonometric formula becomes
\[
v_N(\tan\theta,\varepsilon)
=\frac{c^{N-1}\bigl(U_{N+1}(s)-sU_N(s)+dc\,U_N(s)\bigr)}
       {1+d^2}.
\]
For $|s|\le1$, the polynomials $U_N$ and $U_{N+1}$ are bounded by
constants depending only on~$N$.  Since $N\ge1$, $|c|^{N-1}\le1$, and
\[
\frac{|d|\,|c|^N}{1+d^2}\le \frac{|d|}{1+d^2}\le \frac12,
\]
the right-hand side is bounded uniformly in $\theta$ and~$\varepsilon$.
This proves that $v_N$ is bounded on~$\RR^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tail-uniform Cayley mode remainder]%
\label{lem:tail-uniform-cayley-mode-remainder}
Let $N\ge1$, let $X$ be a real random variable with
$\EE|X|^{N+1}<\infty$, and let
\[
\mathcal R_{N,t}(y):=
\EE\!\left[
R_{X/t}(y)-\sum_{n=0}^{N}u_n(y)\frac{X^n}{t^n}
\right]
\qquad(t>0).
\]
Then there is a constant $M_N<\infty$, depending only on~$N$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-uniform-cayley-mode-remainder}
\sup_{y\in\RR}|\mathcal R_{N,t}(y)|
\;+\;\|\mathcal R_{N,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le
M_N\,\EE|X|^{N+1}\,t^{-(N+1)},
\end{equation}
where $\omega(\dd y)=\pi^{-1}(1+y^2)^{-1}\dd y$.  Thus the remainder is
uniform in the phase variable after integration over the full, unbounded
tail of~$X$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:cayley-mode-remainder},
\[
R_{X/t}(y)-\sum_{n=0}^{N}u_n(y)\frac{X^n}{t^n}
=\frac{X^{N+1}}{t^{N+1}}v_N(y,X/t)
\]
for every $y$ and every value of~$X$.  Let
$M_N:=\|v_N\|_{L^\infty(\RR^2)}$.  Taking expectations gives
\[
|\mathcal R_{N,t}(y)|
\le M_N\,\EE|X|^{N+1}\,t^{-(N+1)}
\qquad(y\in\RR).
\]
This is the asserted uniform estimate.  Since $\omega$ is a probability
measure, the same bound also gives the displayed $L^1(\omega)$ estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-moment density remainder]%
\label{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder}
Let $L\ge2$, let $0<\eta\le1$, and let $\nu$ be a probability measure
on~$\RR$ with mean $\bar\gamma$ and finite centred $(L+\eta)$th absolute
moment.  Put
$\Delta=\gamma-\bar\gamma$,
$\mu_n=\int\Delta^n\dd\nu(\gamma)$, and
\[
\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty):=\frac{(P_t*\nu)(\bar\gamma+ty)}
{P_t(ty)}-1.
\]
Let
\[
B_L:=\max_{0\le n\le L}\|u_n\|_\infty,
\qquad
V_L:=\|v_L\|_\infty,
\qquad
C_L:=\max\{V_L,\,6+(L+1)B_L\}.
\]
Then \eqref{eq:finite-moment-density-remainder} holds with
$C_{\nu,L,\eta}=2C_L\EE|\Delta|^{L+\eta}$.  Moreover, with
\[
S_{\nu,L,\eta}:=
B_L\sum_{n=2}^{L}|\mu_n|+C_{\nu,L,\eta},
\qquad
T_{\nu,L,\eta}:=\max\{1,(2S_{\nu,L,\eta})^{1/2}\},
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-moment-density-remainder}
\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)
=\sum_{n=2}^{L}\mu_n u_n(y)t^{-n}+\rho_{L,t}(y),
\qquad
\sup_y|\rho_{L,t}(y)|+\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le C_{\nu,L,\eta}t^{-(L+\eta)},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-moment-density-smallness}
\sup_y|\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)|\le S_{\nu,L,\eta}t^{-2},
\qquad t\ge1,
\end{equation}
with $\sup_y|\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)|\le1/2$ for
$t\ge T_{\nu,L,\eta}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $x=\bar\gamma+ty$ and $g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma)=P_t(ty)$,
\[
\frac{(P_t*\nu)(x)}{g_t(x)}
=\int_{\RR}R_{\Delta/t}(y)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
For $|\varepsilon|\le1$, Proposition~\ref{prop:cayley-mode-remainder} and
the boundedness of $v_L$ give
\[
\left|R_\varepsilon(y)-\sum_{n=0}^{L}u_n(y)\varepsilon^n\right|
    \le V_L |\varepsilon|^{L+1}
    \le V_L |\varepsilon|^{L+\eta}.
\]
For $|\varepsilon|>1$, write $y=z+\varepsilon$.  Then
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+(z+\varepsilon)^2}{1+z^2}
\le 3(1+\varepsilon^2)
\le 6|\varepsilon|^{L+\eta},
\]
uniformly in~$z$, because $L+\eta\ge2$.  Also
\[
\left|\sum_{n=0}^{L}u_n(y)\varepsilon^n\right|
\le (L+1)B_L|\varepsilon|^L
\le (L+1)B_L|\varepsilon|^{L+\eta}.
\]
Thus the pointwise remainder is bounded by
$C_L|\varepsilon|^{L+\eta}$ uniformly in~$y$.
Taking $\varepsilon=\Delta/t$ and expectations yields the displayed
$t^{-(L+\eta)}$ bound in both $L^\infty$ and $L^1(\omega)$, since $\omega$
has total mass one.  Since $\mu_0=1$ and $\mu_1=0$, subtracting the
constant term gives \eqref{eq:finite-moment-density-remainder}.  Each
$u_n$ is bounded by Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode}; hence the
finite sum begins at order~$t^{-2}$.  The displayed definition of
$S_{\nu,L,\eta}$ therefore gives
\eqref{eq:finite-moment-density-smallness}, because $L+\eta\ge2$ and
$t\ge1$.  The displayed choice of $T_{\nu,L,\eta}$ gives the final
smallness assertion.
\end{proof}

\qquad
u_3(y)=\frac{4y(y^2-1)}{(1+y^2)^3},
\qquad
u_4(y)=\frac{5y^4-10y^2+1}{(1+y^2)^4},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cayley-next-modes}
u_5(y)=\frac{6y^5-20y^3+6y}{(1+y^2)^5},
\qquad
u_6(y)=\frac{7y^6-35y^4+21y^2-1}{(1+y^2)^6}.
\end{equation}

Propositions~\ref{prop:phase-coordinate-identification}

\begin{proposition}[Poisson--Cauchy moment inversion by phase modes]%
\label{prop:poisson-cauchy-moment-inversion-by-phase-modes}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$ and
finite centred fifth absolute moment.  Put
\[
\Delta:=\gamma-\bar\gamma,\qquad
\mu_n:=\int_{\RR}\Delta^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma),\qquad
\sigma^2:=\mu_2,
\]
and let $h_t:=P_t*\nu$ and $g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma)$.  For
$\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ define the phase profile
\[
F_t(\theta):=
\frac{h_t(\bar\gamma+t\tan\theta)}
     {g_t(\bar\gamma+t\tan\theta)}-1.
\]
For $k\ge1$ set
\[
\mathcal C_k(t):=
2\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}F_t(\theta)\cos(2k\theta)\,\frac{\dd\theta}{\pi},
\qquad
\mathcal S_k(t):=
2\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}F_t(\theta)\sin(2k\theta)\,\frac{\dd\theta}{\pi}.
\]
Then
\[
\sigma^2=\lim_{t\to\infty}-2t^2\bigl(\mathcal C_1(t)-\mathcal C_4(t)\bigr),
\qquad
\mu_3=\lim_{t\to\infty}-4t^3\mathcal S_3(t),
\qquad
\mu_4=\lim_{t\to\infty}8t^4\mathcal C_4(t).
\]
More precisely,
\[
\mathcal C_1(t)-\mathcal C_4(t)
=-\frac{\sigma^2}{2t^2}+O(t^{-5}),\qquad
\mathcal S_3(t)=-\frac{\mu_3}{4t^3}+O(t^{-5}),
\qquad
\mathcal C_4(t)=\frac{\mu_4}{8t^4}+O(t^{-5}).
\]
The $O(t^{-5})$ term in the middle estimate is an actual fifth-order
remainder: when the fifth centred moment is finite it contains the
$\sin(6\theta)$ coefficient of $u_5$, and no coefficient-free cancellation at
order $t^{-5}$ is asserted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $y=\tan\theta$.  For $x=\bar\gamma+ty$ one has
\[
\frac{P_t(x-\gamma)}{P_t(x-\bar\gamma)}
=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\Delta/t)^2}
=R_{\Delta/t}(y).
\]
Therefore
\[
F_t(\theta)=\int_{\RR}\bigl(R_{\Delta/t}(\tan\theta)-1\bigr)
\,\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:cayley-mode-remainder} gives the pointwise
order-four identity
\[
R_{\Delta/t}(y)-1
=\sum_{n=1}^{4}u_n(y)\frac{\Delta^n}{t^n}
+\frac{\Delta^5}{t^5}v_4(y,\Delta/t).
\]
Taking $\nu$-expectations and using $\mu_1=0$ gives, uniformly in
$\theta$,
\[
F_t(\theta)
=\frac{\sigma^2}{t^2}u_2(\tan\theta)
+\frac{\mu_3}{t^3}u_3(\tan\theta)
+\frac{\mu_4}{t^4}u_4(\tan\theta)
+O(t^{-5}).
\]
The finite fifth absolute moment is used only through
Lemma~\ref{lem:tail-uniform-cayley-mode-remainder}, with $X=\Delta$ and
truncation order~$4$, to bound the last expectation uniformly.
Thus the $O(t^{-5})$ term has not been discarded coefficientwise; it is the
full order-five tail produced by $v_4$.  If one instead expands through
$u_5$, the formula
\[
u_5(\tan\theta)=\frac1{16}\bigl(\sin2\theta+4\sin4\theta
+6\sin6\theta+4\sin8\theta+\sin10\theta\bigr)
\]
shows explicitly that the $\mathcal S_3$ channel has a fifth-order
contribution $3\mu_5/(8t^5)$ whenever $\mu_5$ is finite; the proposition uses
only the leading coefficient needed for the moment limit.

The first three nonconstant modes have the finite phase-Fourier
expansions
\[
u_2(\tan\theta)=-\frac12\bigl(\cos2\theta+\cos4\theta\bigr),
\]
\[
u_3(\tan\theta)
=-\frac14\bigl(\sin2\theta+2\sin4\theta+\sin6\theta\bigr),
\]
\[
u_4(\tan\theta)
=\frac18\bigl(\cos2\theta+3\cos4\theta+3\cos6\theta+\cos8\theta\bigr),
\]
which are the cases $n=2,3,4$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode}.  The functions
$\cos(2k\theta)$ and $\sin(2k\theta)$ are orthogonal on
$(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$, and the prefactor $2/\pi$ in the definitions of
$\mathcal C_k$ and $\mathcal S_k$ extracts the corresponding coefficient.
Thus the preceding uniform expansion gives
\[
\mathcal C_1(t)
=-\frac{\sigma^2}{2t^2}+\frac{\mu_4}{8t^4}+O(t^{-5}),
\qquad
\mathcal C_4(t)=\frac{\mu_4}{8t^4}+O(t^{-5}),
\]
and
\[
\mathcal S_3(t)=-\frac{\mu_3}{4t^3}+O(t^{-5}).
\]
The last displayed remainder includes the possible fifth-order sine-mode
contribution just identified.
Subtracting the two cosine coefficients and multiplying by the displayed
powers of~$t$ proves the three inversion formulas.
\end{proof}

The preceding proposition is a continuum phase-coefficient inversion.  The
finite-grid version below separates the exact alias-free part from the
large-time error; this is the additional estimate needed before the phase
coefficients can be read from finitely many exact samples.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-grid finite-time moment recovery]%
\label{thm:finite-grid-poisson-moment-recovery}
Let $\nu$ satisfy the hypotheses of
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-cauchy-moment-inversion-by-phase-modes},
and let $F_t$ be the corresponding phase profile.  Let $L\ge9$ be odd.
For each $\zeta\in\mu_L$, let
$\theta_\zeta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ be the unique angle with
$\zeta=e^{2\ii\theta_\zeta}$, and set, for $k\ge1$,
\begin{align*}
\widehat F_{t,L}(k)
&:=\frac1L\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_L}
F_t(\theta_\zeta)\zeta^{-k},\\
\mathcal C_{k,L}(t)&:=2\operatorname{Re}\widehat F_{t,L}(k),
\qquad
\mathcal S_{k,L}(t):=-2\operatorname{Im}\widehat F_{t,L}(k).
\end{align*}
Then there are constants $C_{\nu},T_{\nu}>0$ such that, for all
$t\ge T_{\nu}$,
\begin{align}
\left|
\mathcal C_{1,L}(t)-\mathcal C_{4,L}(t)
+\frac{\sigma^2}{2t^2}
\right|
&\le C_{\nu}t^{-5},\label{eq:finite-grid-sigma-recovery}\\
\left|
\mathcal S_{3,L}(t)+\frac{\mu_3}{4t^3}
\right|
&\le C_{\nu}t^{-5},\label{eq:finite-grid-mu-three-recovery}\\
\left|
\mathcal C_{4,L}(t)-\frac{\mu_4}{8t^4}
\right|
&\le C_{\nu}t^{-5}.\label{eq:finite-grid-mu-four-recovery}
\end{align}
Consequently the same fixed finite phase grid recovers the moment
coordinates in the large-time limit:
\[
\sigma^2=\lim_{t\to\infty}
-2t^2\bigl(\mathcal C_{1,L}(t)-\mathcal C_{4,L}(t)\bigr),
\qquad
\mu_3=\lim_{t\to\infty}-4t^3\mathcal S_{3,L}(t),
\]
\[
\mu_4=\lim_{t\to\infty}8t^4\mathcal C_{4,L}(t),
\]
with finite-time errors respectively $O(t^{-3})$, $O(t^{-2})$, and
$O(t^{-1})$ after the displayed renormalizations.  These are deterministic
estimates for exact phase samples.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-cauchy-moment-inversion-by-phase-modes},
using Lemma~\ref{lem:tail-uniform-cayley-mode-remainder} at order~$4$,
gives constants $C_0,T_0>0$ such that for $t\ge T_0$
\[
F_t(\theta)=Q_t(\theta)+r_t(\theta),
\qquad
\sup_{-\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2}|r_t(\theta)|\le C_0t^{-5},
\]
where
\[
Q_t(\theta)
:=\frac{\sigma^2}{t^2}u_2(\tan\theta)
+\frac{\mu_3}{t^3}u_3(\tan\theta)
+\frac{\mu_4}{t^4}u_4(\tan\theta).
\]
By the finite Fourier formulas displayed in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-cauchy-moment-inversion-by-phase-modes},
$Q_t$ is a Laurent polynomial in $z=e^{2\ii\theta}$ with Fourier support
contained in $\{-4,\ldots,4\}$.  For $k=1,3,4$, the shifted polynomial
$z^{-k}Q_t(z)$ has support contained in $\{-8,\ldots,8\}$.  Since
$L\ge9$, Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-grid-coefficient-recovery} applies
with no aliasing to the $Q_t$ part of each coefficient.  The parity and
orthogonality computation in the preceding proposition therefore gives
\[
\mathcal C_{1,L}^{Q}(t)-\mathcal C_{4,L}^{Q}(t)
=-\frac{\sigma^2}{2t^2},
\qquad
\mathcal S_{3,L}^{Q}(t)=-\frac{\mu_3}{4t^3},
\qquad
\mathcal C_{4,L}^{Q}(t)=\frac{\mu_4}{8t^4},
\]
where the superscript indicates the same finite-grid recovery applied to
$Q_t$.

For the remainder, the discrete averaging formula gives
\[
\left|\frac1L\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_L}
r_t(\theta_\zeta)\zeta^{-k}\right|
\le C_0t^{-5}
\qquad(k\ge1).
\]
Thus the corresponding cosine and sine recovery errors are at most
$2C_0t^{-5}$, and the difference
$\mathcal C_{1,L}-\mathcal C_{4,L}$ has error at most $4C_0t^{-5}$.
Increasing the constant to one number $C_\nu$ proves
\eqref{eq:finite-grid-sigma-recovery}--\eqref{eq:finite-grid-mu-four-recovery}.
Multiplication by $2t^2$, $4t^3$, and $8t^4$ gives the final
renormalized error orders and the three limits.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Mode parity cancellation]\label{lem:mode-parity-vanish}
For integers $n_1,\dots,n_k\ge 1$ let
\[
I_{n_1,\dots,n_k}:=
\int_{\RR}
\frac{u_{n_1}(y)\cdots u_{n_k}(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\,\dd y.
\]
Then $I_{n_1,\dots,n_k}=0$ whenever $n_1+\cdots+n_k$ is odd.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:cayley-mode-parity}, $u_n(-y)=(-1)^n u_n(y)$ for every
$n\ge1$, while $(1+y^2)^{-1}$ is even.  Hence the integrand is odd if
$n_1+\cdots+n_k$ is odd, so the integral vanishes.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Entropy asymptotics and finite-moment remainders}%
\label{subsec:finite-moment-entropy}

This subsection passes from formal coefficient algebra to genuine entropy
asymptotics.  The first lemma records a strong-moment power expansion that
fixes the algebraic order of operations for powers of the density ratio: one
truncates the one-body mode expansion additively, expands the resulting finite
polynomial exactly, and only then discards monomials whose total $t$-degree is
larger than the target degree.  The truncated polynomial is denoted
$P_{k,N,t}$, and the power remainder is defined by
\[
\varepsilon_{k,N,t}
:=\bigl(\delta_t(\bar\gamma+t\,\cdot)\bigr)^k-P_{k,N,t}.
\]
Thus the power formula below is a pointwise identity with an additive
remainder in $L^\infty(\RR)\cap L^1(\omega)$.

\begin{lemma}[Strong-moment power remainder]%
\label{lem:entropy-uniform-remainder}
Let $N\ge2$ and let $\nu$ have mean~$\bar\gamma$ and finite centred
$(2N+1)$st absolute moment.  Put
\[
\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty):=
\frac{h_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}{g_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}-1.
\]
Let $\omega(\dd y):=\pi^{-1}(1+y^2)^{-1}\dd y$.
This strong-moment statement is an algebraic power-expansion result, not the
final finite-moment entropy theorem.  It displays finite coefficient
expansions under the stronger moment hypothesis; the large-time entropy claim
used later is the sharper finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th moment statement of
Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}
and~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder}.
Then there are constants $C_{\nu,N},T_{\nu,N}>0$ and, for each
$2\le k\le N$, a constant $C_{\nu,N,k}>0$ with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*]
\item For every $t\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-delta-expansion}
\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)
=\sum_{n=2}^{2N}\frac{\mu_n}{t^n}u_n(y)+\rho_{N,t}(y),
\qquad
\sup_{y\in\RR}|\rho_{N,t}(y)|
\;+\;\|\rho_{N,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le C_{\nu,N} t^{-(2N+1)},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-delta-bound}
\sup_{y\in\RR}|\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)|\le C_{\nu,N} t^{-2}.
\end{equation}

\item For $t\ge T_{\nu,N}$ the function
\[
y\longmapsto
\Phi(\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty))
:=(1+\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty))
\log(1+\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty))
-\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)
\]
is integrable against $\omega$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-Phi-expansion}
\Phi(\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty))
=\sum_{j=2}^{N}\frac{(-1)^j}{j(j-1)}
\bigl(\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)\bigr)^j+\eta_{N,t}(y),
\end{equation}
where
\[
\sup_{y\in\RR}|\eta_{N,t}(y)|
\;+\;\|\eta_{N,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le C_{\nu,N} t^{-(2N+2)}.
\]

\item Put
\[
A_{N,t}(y):=\sum_{n=2}^{2N}\frac{\mu_n}{t^n}u_n(y),
\qquad
\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)=A_{N,t}(y)+\rho_{N,t}(y).
\]
For every $2\le k\le N$, define the truncated polynomial
\[
P_{k,N,t}(y):=
\sum_{m=2k}^{2N}\frac{1}{t^m}
\sum_{\substack{n_1,\dots,n_k\ge2\\ n_1+\cdots+n_k=m}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_k}
u_{n_1}(y)\cdots u_{n_k}(y).
\]
The power remainder is the exact additive difference
\[
\varepsilon_{k,N,t}(y):=
\bigl(\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)\bigr)^k-P_{k,N,t}(y).
\]
Thus the power expansion is the pointwise additive identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-power-expansion}
\begin{split}
\bigl(\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)\bigr)^k
\;=&
\sum_{m=2k}^{2N}\frac{1}{t^m}
\sum_{\substack{n_1,\dots,n_k\ge2\\ n_1+\cdots+n_k=m}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_k}
u_{n_1}(y)\cdots u_{n_k}(y)
\;+\;\varepsilon_{k,N,t}(y).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, in the truncation-polynomial notation this is
\[
\bigl(\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)\bigr)^k
=P_{k,N,t}(y)+\varepsilon_{k,N,t}(y).
\]
The last term is the whole difference between the exact power and the
truncated polynomial $P_{k,N,t}$.  In terms of the one-body truncation,
\[
\varepsilon_{k,N,t}
=\bigl((A_{N,t}+\rho_{N,t})^k-A_{N,t}^k\bigr)
+\bigl(A_{N,t}^k-P_{k,N,t}\bigr).
\]
For every $t\ge1$ the same additive remainder satisfies the uniform and
integrated bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-power-error-bound}
\sup_y |\varepsilon_{k,N,t}(y)|
+\|\varepsilon_{k,N,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le C_{\nu,N,k}t^{-(2N+1)}.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
\left|\int_{\RR}\varepsilon_{k,N,t}\,\dd\omega\right|
\le C_{\nu,N,k}t^{-(2N+1)}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Consequently all remainders in \eqref{eq:uniform-Phi-expansion} and
\eqref{eq:uniform-power-expansion} are $o(t^{-2N})$ in $L^1(\omega)$.
This strong-moment estimate justifies the displayed finite coefficient
extractions under a finite centred $(2N+1)$st moment, while the finite-moment
entropy expansion below uses Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder}
to lower the final remainder hypothesis to a finite centred
$(2N+\eta)$th moment.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Mode remainder.}
All constants below may depend on $\nu$ and $N$, and in the power estimate
also on $k$, but not on $y$ or $t$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:tail-uniform-cayley-mode-remainder} with
$X=\Delta$ and truncation order~$2N$.  Taking $\nu$-expectations in the
mode expansion, using $\mu_0=1$ and $\mu_1=0$, and subtracting the
constant term gives \eqref{eq:uniform-delta-expansion}.  The lemma gives
the displayed uniform and $L^1(\omega)$ bound for $\rho_{N,t}$ directly
from the finite centred $(2N+1)$st absolute moment.
Each fixed mode $u_n$ is bounded by
Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode}, so
\eqref{eq:uniform-delta-expansion} implies \eqref{eq:uniform-delta-bound}.

\emph{Entropy Taylor remainder.}
Choose $T_{\nu,N}$ so large that the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:uniform-delta-bound} is at most $1/2$ for $t\ge T_{\nu,N}$.  On
$|z|\le1/2$ the Taylor series
\[
\Phi(z)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{k(k-1)}z^k
\]
has a remainder after degree~$N$ bounded by a constant times
$|z|^{N+1}$.  Since $\delta_t=O(t^{-2})$ uniformly, this gives
\eqref{eq:uniform-Phi-expansion}.  The measure~$\omega$ has total mass
one, so the same supremum estimate gives the displayed $L^1(\omega)$
bound and the asserted integrability.

\emph{Power remainders.}
It remains to justify \eqref{eq:uniform-power-expansion} and
\eqref{eq:uniform-power-error-bound}.  From
\eqref{eq:uniform-delta-expansion},
\[
\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)=A_{N,t}(y)+\rho_{N,t}(y),
\qquad
\sup_y|\rho_{N,t}(y)|
\;+\;\|\rho_{N,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
=O(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\]
The modes are bounded, so $A_{N,t}=O(t^{-2})$ uniformly.  First expand the
finite product $A_{N,t}(y)^k$ exactly:
\[
A_{N,t}(y)^k
=
\sum_{m=2k}^{2Nk}\frac{1}{t^m}
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\dots,n_k\le 2N\\
n_1+\cdots+n_k=m}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_k}
u_{n_1}(y)\cdots u_{n_k}(y).
\]
No estimate has entered this step: it is a finite algebraic identity.
Splitting this exact finite sum at total degree $2N$ gives
\[
A_{N,t}(y)^k=P_{k,N,t}(y)+r_{k,N,t}(y),
\]
where
\[
r_{k,N,t}(y)=
\sum_{m=2N+1}^{2Nk}\frac{1}{t^m}
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\dots,n_k\le 2N\\
n_1+\cdots+n_k=m}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_k}
u_{n_1}(y)\cdots u_{n_k}(y).
\]
The sum is finite.  Since every mode in it is bounded and $t\ge1$,
if
\[
M_{N}:=\max_{2\le n\le 2N}\|u_n\|_\infty,
\qquad
M_{N,k}:=\max_{\substack{2\le n_1,\dots,n_k\le2N}}
\|u_{n_1}\cdots u_{n_k}\|_\infty\le M_N^k,
\]
then the discarded finite-product tail satisfies the concrete bound
\[
\sup_y|r_{k,N,t}(y)|
\le
t^{-(2N+1)}
M_{N,k}
\sum_{m=2N+1}^{2Nk}
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\dots,n_k\le2N\\
n_1+\cdots+n_k=m}}
|\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_k}|.
\]
Because $\omega(\RR)=1$, this also gives
\[
\|r_{k,N,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le
t^{-(2N+1)}
M_{N,k}
\sum_{m=2N+1}^{2Nk}
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\dots,n_k\le2N\\
n_1+\cdots+n_k=m}}
|\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_k}|.
\]
Second, compare the exact power of $\delta_t=A_{N,t}+\rho_{N,t}$ with the
finite product just expanded.  The binomial formula gives
\[
\bigl(A_{N,t}(y)+\rho_{N,t}(y)\bigr)^k-A_{N,t}(y)^k
=
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\binom{k}{\ell}
A_{N,t}(y)^{k-\ell}\rho_{N,t}(y)^\ell.
\]
For the term with index~$\ell$, the bounds $A_{N,t}=O(t^{-2})$ and
$\rho_{N,t}=O(t^{-(2N+1)})$ give
\[
O(t^{-2(k-\ell)})O(t^{-\ell(2N+1)})=O(t^{-(2N+1)})
\]
for $t\ge1$.  Hence
\[
\bigl(A_{N,t}(y)+\rho_{N,t}(y)\bigr)^k-A_{N,t}(y)^k
=O(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\]
For the corresponding $L^1(\omega)$ estimate, use
\[
\|A_{N,t}^{\,k-\ell}\rho_{N,t}^{\,\ell}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le
\|A_{N,t}\|_\infty^{k-\ell}
\|\rho_{N,t}\|_\infty^{\ell-1}
\|\rho_{N,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
=O(t^{-(2N+1)})
\]
for every $\ell\ge1$.
By the definition of $\varepsilon_{k,N,t}$, this proves the explicit
additive decomposition
\[
\varepsilon_{k,N,t}(y)
=r_{k,N,t}(y)
+\bigl(A_{N,t}(y)+\rho_{N,t}(y)\bigr)^k-A_{N,t}(y)^k
\]
for the additive remainder in \eqref{eq:uniform-power-expansion}.  The
displayed uniform and $L^1(\omega)$ estimates for $r_{k,N,t}$ and the
binomial difference give \eqref{eq:uniform-power-error-bound}.  The bounds
$t^{-(2N+2)}=o(t^{-2N})$ and
$t^{-(2N+1)}=o(t^{-2N})$ give the final claim.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-uniform-remainder} is a strong-moment coefficient
calculation for the finite polynomial algebra and the explicit low-order
coefficient computations.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} is the
all-order finite-moment asymptotic statement: its proof uses the sharper density
remainder from Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder} and therefore
requires only a finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment.  The separate
entropy remainder theorem is recorded immediately afterwards as
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder}.

The density-to-entropy passage is a finite operation at fixed order $2N$.
First the density ratio is written as a finite mode polynomial plus an
$L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)$ error.  Then
$\Phi(z)=(1+z)\log(1+z)-z$ is expanded only through degree~$N$, because the
density perturbation begins at order $t^{-2}$.  Products containing at least
one density error keep the $O(t^{-(2N+\eta)})$ $L^1(\omega)$ size, while the
discarded Taylor tail is $O(t^{-(2N+2)})$.  The former constant is inherited
from the finite density remainder and the finite mode bounds; the latter is
controlled by the eventual $t^{-2}$ bound for the density perturbation.  After
integration, the remaining finite polynomial is exactly the universal
coefficient polynomial.

\begin{lemma}[Density-to-entropy domination at finite moment order]%
\label{lem:density-to-entropy-domination}
Let $N\ge2$, $0<\eta\le1$, and suppose that, for all $t\ge T$, measurable
functions $\delta_t,A_t,\rho_t$ on~$\RR$ satisfy
\[
\delta_t=A_t+\rho_t,
\qquad
A_t(y)=\sum_{n=2}^{2N}\mu_n\,u_n(y)t^{-n},
\]
where $u_2,\dots,u_{2N}$ are bounded.  Then every product
$u_{n_1}\cdots u_{n_r}$ with $2\le r\le N$ and
$2\le n_j\le2N$ belongs to $L^1(\omega)$, and every coefficient integral
$I(n_1,\dots,n_r)$ appearing below is absolutely convergent.  Assume further
that, for fixed finite constants $C_\rho,M_A,S$,
\[
\|\rho_t\|_\infty+\|\rho_t\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le C_\rho t^{-(2N+\eta)},
\qquad
\|A_t\|_\infty\le M_A t^{-2},
\qquad
\|\delta_t\|_\infty\le \min\{1/2,S t^{-2}\}.
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal P_{N,t}:=
\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{m=2r}^{2N}t^{-m}
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\dots,n_r\le2N\\
n_1+\cdots+n_r=m}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r}\,I(n_1,
\dots,n_r).
\]
Then $\Phi(\delta_t)\in L^1(\omega)$ and, for all $t\ge\max\{1,T\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-to-entropy-domination-bound}
\left|
\int_{\RR}\Phi(\delta_t)\,\dd\omega-\mathcal P_{N,t}
\right|
\le
\mathfrak P_N(C_\rho,M_A)t^{-(2N+\eta)}
+\mathfrak Q_N(\mu,u)t^{-(2N+\eta)}
+2S^{N+1}t^{-(2N+2)},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
\mathfrak P_N(C_\rho,M_A)
&:=
\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{1}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\binom r\ell M_A^{r-\ell}C_\rho^\ell,\\
\mathfrak Q_N(\mu,u)
&:=
\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{1}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{m=2N+1}^{2Nr}
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\dots,n_r\le2N\\
n_1+\cdots+n_r=m}}
|\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r}|
\|u_{n_1}\cdots u_{n_r}\|_{L^1(\omega)} .
\end{align*}
In particular, the passage from the finite-moment density-ratio expansion to
the entropy integral loses only the density remainder
$O(t^{-(2N+\eta)})$ and the Taylor tail $O(t^{-(2N+2)})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The product integrability assertion follows first: if
$2\le n_j\le2N$, then
\[
\|u_{n_1}\cdots u_{n_r}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le\prod_{j=1}^r\|u_{n_j}\|_\infty<\infty,
\]
because $\omega$ is a probability measure.  Thus all coefficient integrals in
$\mathcal P_{N,t}$ are genuine absolutely convergent $L^1(\omega)$ integrals;
no conditional summation or tail cancellation is used in the entropy descent.

The bound $\|\delta_t\|_\infty\le1/2$ places the density ratio in the disk of
the Taylor expansion
\[
\Phi(z)=(1+z)\log(1+z)-z
=\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}z^r+R_{N+1}(z),
\qquad |R_{N+1}(z)|\le2|z|^{N+1}.
\]
Hence $\Phi(\delta_t)$ is bounded, and therefore belongs to $L^1(\omega)$,
because $\omega$ is a probability measure.  Moreover
\[
\|R_{N+1}(\delta_t)\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le2\|\delta_t\|_\infty^{N+1}
\le 2S^{N+1}t^{-(2N+2)} .
\]

For $2\le r\le N$ we have
\[
\delta_t^r-A_t^r
=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\binom r\ell A_t^{r-\ell}\rho_t^\ell.
\]
Using one factor $\rho_t$ in $L^1(\omega)$ and all remaining factors in
$L^\infty$ gives
\[
\|\delta_t^r-A_t^r\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le
\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\binom r\ell
M_A^{r-\ell}C_\rho^\ell
t^{-2(r-\ell)}t^{-\ell(2N+\eta)} .
\]
Since $t\ge1$ and $\ell\ge1$, each exponent on the right is at least
$2N+\eta$, so the whole expression is bounded by the first finite sum in
\eqref{eq:density-to-entropy-domination-bound} at order
$t^{-(2N+\eta)}$ after multiplication by the entropy coefficients.

It remains to compare the finite polynomial $A_t$ with the displayed
coefficient polynomial.  Expanding $A_t^r$ gives
\[
\int_{\RR}A_t(y)^r\,\dd\omega(y)
=
\sum_{m=2r}^{2Nr}t^{-m}
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\dots,n_r\le2N\\
n_1+\cdots+n_r=m}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r}\,I(n_1,\dots,n_r).
\]
The part with $m\le2N$ is exactly the $r$th part of $\mathcal P_{N,t}$.
The part with $m>2N$ has absolute value at most
\[
t^{-(2N+1)}
\sum_{m=2N+1}^{2Nr}
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\dots,n_r\le2N\\
n_1+\cdots+n_r=m}}
|\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r}|
\|u_{n_1}\cdots u_{n_r}\|_{L^1(\omega)} .
\]
Because $0<\eta\le1$ and $t\ge1$, this is bounded by the same finite sum
times $t^{-(2N+\eta)}$.  Combining the Taylor tail, the product error, and
the discarded finite-product degrees proves
\eqref{eq:density-to-entropy-domination-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite-moment density-to-entropy bounds]%
\label{lem:finite-moment-density-to-entropy-bounds}
Let $N\ge2$, $0<\eta\le1$, and let $\nu$ have mean $\bar\gamma$ and finite
centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment.  Put
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),
\qquad
\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)=h_t(\bar\gamma+ty)/g_t(\bar\gamma+ty)-1 .
\]
Then there is a threshold $T$ and finite fixed-law constants depending only on
$N,\eta$ and~$\nu$ such that, for every $t\ge T$, the following four assertions
hold simultaneously.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item The finite density expansion
\[
\delta_t=A_{N,t}+\rho_{N,t},
\qquad
A_{N,t}(y)=\sum_{n=2}^{2N}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n},
\]
holds in $L^\infty(\RR)\cap L^1(\omega)$ with
$\|\rho_{N,t}\|_\infty+\|\rho_{N,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
       =O_{\nu,N,\eta}(t^{-(2N+\eta)})$.
\item The logarithmic integrand is absolutely integrable and is dominated by a
fixed finite Taylor calculation:
\[
\Phi(\delta_t)
=\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}\delta_t^r
  +O_{L^1(\omega)}(t^{-(2N+2)}),
\qquad
\Phi(z)=(1+z)\log(1+z)-z .
\]
\item Replacing $\delta_t^r$ by $A_{N,t}^r$ in the preceding finite sum changes
the entropy integral by the error $O_{\nu,N,\eta}(t^{-(2N+\eta)})$.
\item The remaining finite polynomial is exactly
\[
\sum_{k=4}^{2N}A_k(\nu)t^{-k}
\]
with $A_k(\nu)$ defined by
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}; all monomials of total
degree greater than $2N$ contribute only
$O_{\nu,N}(t^{-(2N+1)})=O_{\nu,N,\eta}(t^{-(2N+\eta)})$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the finite-moment proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} uses only the finite
$(2N+\eta)$-moment density remainder, bounded Cayley--Chebyshev modes, and the
explicit smallness threshold; it does not invoke any stronger auxiliary moment
hypothesis.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder} with $L=2N$.  It gives
the decomposition in (i), the $O(t^{-2})$ bound for $A_{N,t}$, and a threshold
for which $\|\delta_t\|_\infty\le1/2$.  Since the modes $u_2,\dots,u_{2N}$ are
bounded by Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode}, all finite products of
these modes are in $L^1(\omega)$, because $\omega$ has total mass one.

The bound $\|\delta_t\|_\infty\le1/2$ gives the Taylor estimate
\[
\left|\Phi(z)-\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}z^r\right|
\le2|z|^{N+1}
\qquad(|z|\le1/2).
\]
Together with $\|\delta_t\|_\infty=O(t^{-2})$, this proves (ii).  For (iii),
expand
\[
\delta_t^r-A_{N,t}^r
=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\binom r\ell A_{N,t}^{r-\ell}\rho_{N,t}^{\ell}.
\]
Putting one factor $\rho_{N,t}$ in $L^1(\omega)$ and all other factors in
$L^\infty$ gives an $O(t^{-(2N+\eta)})$ contribution for each fixed
$2\le r\le N$.  Summing over the finite range of~$r$ proves (iii).

Finally expand $A_{N,t}^r$.  Each monomial has the form
\[
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r}
u_{n_1}\cdots u_{n_r}t^{-(n_1+\cdots+n_r)},
\qquad 2\le n_j\le2N .
\]
The monomials of total degree $k\le2N$ are precisely the finite-moment
coefficient formula \eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}; the
remaining finitely many monomials have total degree at least $2N+1$ and hence
are $O(t^{-(2N+1)})$.  Since $0<\eta\le1$, this is
$O(t^{-(2N+\eta)})$ for $t\ge1$.  These four estimates are exactly
the inputs used in Lemmas~\ref{lem:density-to-entropy-domination} and
\ref{lem:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}, and therefore close the stated
finite-moment descent without a stronger moment assumption.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Explicit finite-moment entropy constants]%
\label{lem:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}
Let $N\ge2$, $0<\eta\le1$, and let $\nu$ satisfy the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  Put
\[
C_\rho:=C_{\nu,2N,\eta},
\qquad
M_A:=\sum_{n=2}^{2N}|\mu_n|\,\|u_n\|_{\infty},
\qquad
S:=S_{\nu,2N,\eta},
\]
where $C_{\nu,2N,\eta}$ and $S_{\nu,2N,\eta}$ are the constants in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder}.  Define the finite sums
\begin{align}
\mathfrak P_{N,\eta}(\nu)
&:=
\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{1}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\binom r\ell M_A^{r-\ell}C_\rho^\ell,
\label{eq:entropy-constant-product-error}
\\
\mathfrak Q_N(\nu)
&:=
\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{1}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{m=2N+1}^{2Nr}
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\dots,n_r\le2N\\
n_1+\cdots+n_r=m}}
|\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r}|
\|u_{n_1}\cdots u_{n_r}\|_{L^1(\omega)} .
\label{eq:entropy-constant-truncation-error}
\end{align}
Then for every
\[
t\ge T_{N,\eta}^{\rm ent}(\nu):=
\max\{1,T_{\nu,2N,\eta}\}
\]
the error terms in the density-to-entropy passage may be chosen as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}
C_{N,\eta}^{\rm dens}(\nu)
=\mathfrak P_{N,\eta}(\nu)+\mathfrak Q_N(\nu),
\qquad
C_{N,\eta}^{\rm Tay}(\nu)=2S^{N+1}.
\end{equation}
In particular these are fixed-law finite expressions in the density-remainder
constant from Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder}, the moments
through order $2N$, and the universal mode norms through order $2N$.  The
lemma records usable bounds for each specified law; it does not assert
sharpness or uniform control over a moment class.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The density theorem with $L=2N$ gives
\[
\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)=A_{N,t}(y)+\rho_{N,t}(y),
\qquad
A_{N,t}(y):=\sum_{n=2}^{2N}\mu_n\,u_n(y)t^{-n},
\]
with
\[
\|\rho_{N,t}\|_\infty+\|\rho_{N,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le C_\rho t^{-(2N+\eta)},
\qquad
\|A_{N,t}\|_\infty\le M_A t^{-2},
\qquad
\|\delta_t\|_\infty\le S t^{-2}.
\]
For $2\le r\le N$,
\[
\delta_t^r-A_{N,t}^r
=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\binom r\ell A_{N,t}^{r-\ell}\rho_{N,t}^{\ell}.
\]
Using one $\rho_{N,t}$ factor in $L^1(\omega)$ and all remaining factors in
$L^\infty$, and using $t\ge1$, gives
\[
\|\delta_t^r-A_{N,t}^r\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le
\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\binom r\ell
M_A^{r-\ell}C_\rho^\ell t^{-(2N+\eta)}.
\]
After multiplication by the entropy Taylor coefficient $1/(r(r-1))$ and
summation in $r$, this is exactly the contribution
\eqref{eq:entropy-constant-product-error}.

Expanding $A_{N,t}^r$ gives monomials
\[
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r}
u_{n_1}\cdots u_{n_r}t^{-(n_1+\cdots+n_r)}.
\]
The monomials with total degree $m>2N$ contribute at most the finite quantity
in \eqref{eq:entropy-constant-truncation-error} times $t^{-(2N+1)}$.
Since $0<\eta\le1$ and $t\ge1$, $t^{-(2N+1)}\le t^{-(2N+\eta)}$, so this is
absorbed with constant $\mathfrak Q_N(\nu)$.

It remains only to record the Taylor remainder of
$\Phi(z)=(1+z)\log(1+z)-z$.  For $|z|\le1/2$,
\[
\left|\Phi(z)-\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}z^r\right|
\le \sum_{r=N+1}^{\infty}|z|^r
\le 2|z|^{N+1}.
\]
The threshold $T_{\nu,2N,\eta}$ ensures $\|\delta_t\|_\infty\le1/2$;
therefore the Taylor tail has $L^1(\omega)$ norm at most
$2S^{N+1}t^{-(2N+2)}$, because $\omega$ is a probability measure.  Combining
the three displayed estimates proves \eqref{eq:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Moment-ball domination of density and entropy constants]%
\label{lem:moment-ball-uniform-constant-domination}
Fix $N\ge2$, $0<\eta\le1$, and $M<\infty$, and put $p=2N+\eta$.  There are
finite constants
\[
\overline C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rho},\qquad
\overline S_{N,\eta,M},
\qquad
\overline C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm dens},
\qquad
\overline C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm Tay},
\qquad
\overline T_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm ent}
\]
depending only on $N$, $\eta$, $M$, and the universal norms of the modes
$u_2,\ldots,u_{2N}$, such that every probability measure $\nu$ with finite
mean $\bar\gamma$ and
\[
\int_{\RR}|\gamma-\bar\gamma|^p\dd\nu(\gamma)\le M
\]
satisfies
\begin{align*}
C_{\nu,2N,\eta}&\le \overline C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rho},
&
S_{\nu,2N,\eta}&\le \overline S_{N,\eta,M},
\\
C_{N,\eta}^{\rm dens}(\nu)&\le
\overline C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm dens},
&
C_{N,\eta}^{\rm Tay}(\nu)&\le
\overline C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm Tay},
&
T_{N,\eta}^{\rm ent}(\nu)&\le
\overline T_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm ent}.
\end{align*}
Consequently the density remainder
\eqref{eq:finite-moment-density-remainder}, the density smallness threshold
\eqref{eq:finite-moment-density-smallness}, and the entropy constants in
\eqref{eq:explicit-entropy-constant-bound} are uniformly dominated on the
centred $p$-moment ball.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $2\le n\le2N$, Lyapunov's inequality gives
$|\mu_n|\le M^{n/p}$.  The density theorem with $L=2N$ gives the explicit
constant
\[
C_{\nu,2N,\eta}=2C_{2N}\EE|\Delta|^p\le2C_{2N}M,
\]
where $C_{2N}$ is determined only by $2N$ and the universal bounded remainders
and mode norms.  Thus we may take
$\overline C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rho}=2C_{2N}M$.  Since
\[
S_{\nu,2N,\eta}=B_{2N}\sum_{n=2}^{2N}|\mu_n|+C_{\nu,2N,\eta},
\]
the same moment bounds give the uniform finite number
\[
\overline S_{N,\eta,M}:=
B_{2N}\sum_{n=2}^{2N}M^{n/p}+2C_{2N}M .
\]
The threshold definition then yields
\[
T_{N,\eta}^{\rm ent}(\nu)=\max\{1,T_{\nu,2N,\eta}\}
\le \max\{1,(2\overline S_{N,\eta,M})^{1/2}\}
=:\overline T_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm ent}.
\]

It remains only to dominate the finite entropy sums in
Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}.  Let
\[
\overline M_{A,N,\eta,M}:=
\sum_{n=2}^{2N}M^{n/p}\|u_n\|_\infty .
\]
Substituting the bounds
$M_A\le\overline M_{A,N,\eta,M}$ and
$C_\rho\le\overline C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rho}$ into the finite sum
\eqref{eq:entropy-constant-product-error} gives a uniform bound for
$\mathfrak P_{N,\eta}(\nu)$.  Substituting
$|\mu_{n_j}|\le M^{n_j/p}$ into the finite sum
\eqref{eq:entropy-constant-truncation-error} gives a uniform bound for
$\mathfrak Q_N(\nu)$; its remaining factors are the fixed universal quantities
$\|u_{n_1}\cdots u_{n_r}\|_{L^1(\omega)}$, bounded in turn by the displayed
mode norms because $\omega$ has mass one.  These two uniform finite bounds
define $\overline C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm dens}$.  Finally
$C_{N,\eta}^{\rm Tay}(\nu)=2S_{\nu,2N,\eta}^{N+1}$ is bounded by
\[
\overline C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm Tay}:=2\overline S_{N,\eta,M}^{N+1}.
\]
All constants are therefore controlled by the announced data alone.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite-moment entropy passage]%
\label{lem:finite-moment-entropy-passage}
Let $N\ge2$, $0<\eta\le1$, and let $\nu$ have mean $\bar\gamma$ and finite
centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment.  Define $h_t=P_t*\nu$,
$g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma)$,
\[
\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty):=\frac{h_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}{g_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}-1,
\qquad
A_{N,t}(y):=\sum_{n=2}^{2N}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}.
\]
Then the following implication holds entirely under this finite
$(2N+\eta)$-moment hypothesis.  The density remainder of
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder} gives constants
$C_\rho,M_A,S$ and a threshold $T$ such that, for $t\ge T$,
\[
\delta_t=A_{N,t}+\rho_{N,t},
\qquad
\|\rho_{N,t}\|_\infty+\|\rho_{N,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le C_\rho t^{-(2N+\eta)},
\]
\[
\|A_{N,t}\|_\infty\le M_A t^{-2},
\qquad
\|\delta_t\|_\infty\le \min\{1/2,S t^{-2}\}.
\]
Consequently Lemma~\ref{lem:density-to-entropy-domination} applies, equivalently
through the four estimates isolated in
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-moment-density-to-entropy-bounds}, and
gives
\begin{align*}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
&=\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{m=2r}^{2N}t^{-m}
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\dots,n_r\le2N\\
n_1+\cdots+n_r=m}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r}I(n_1,\dots,n_r)\\
&\qquad +O(t^{-(2N+\eta)})+O(t^{-(2N+2)}).
\end{align*}
The constants in the two error terms are fixed-law constants of the kind
recorded in Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}.  In particular
the strong-moment Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-uniform-remainder} is not an input to
this implication; it is a separate display of the same finite coefficient
algebra when a centred $(2N+1)$st absolute moment is available.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-moment-density-to-entropy-bounds}
records the four analytic estimates used below: the finite-moment density
expansion in $L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)$, absolute integrability of all finite
mode products, Taylor domination under the explicit smallness threshold, and
the loss estimates for density and high-degree polynomial remainders.

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder} with $L=2N$.  Its
finite-moment remainder gives the displayed decomposition in
$L^\infty(\RR)\cap L^1(\omega)$, with
$C_\rho=C_{\nu,2N,\eta}$, with
$M_A=\sum_{n=2}^{2N}|\mu_n|\|u_n\|_\infty$, and with $S$ and $T$ chosen from
the eventual $O(t^{-2})$ bound for $\delta_t$.  The hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:density-to-entropy-domination} are therefore satisfied with
$A_t=A_{N,t}$ and $\rho_t=\rho_{N,t}$.  Since
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\int_{\RR}\Phi(\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty))\,\omega(\dd y),
\]
The density-to-entropy lemma gives the displayed finite polynomial and the two
remainder orders.  The explicit constant bound is obtained by
substituting these same constants into the finite sums of
Lemma~\ref{lem:density-to-entropy-domination}, so the stated dependence of the
error constants follows.  No step in the argument invokes
Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-uniform-remainder}; that lemma assumes the stronger
$(2N+1)$st moment only to organize coefficient arithmetic, whereas the present
descent uses the finite $(2N+\eta)$ density remainder directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All-order finite-moment Poisson entropy expansion]%
\label{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}
Let $N\ge 2$, let $0<\eta\le1$, and let $\nu$ be a probability measure
on~$\RR$ with mean $\bar\gamma$ and finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute
moment.  Set
\[
\Delta:=\gamma-\bar\gamma,
\qquad
\mu_n:=\int_{\RR}\Delta^n\dd\nu(\gamma),
\]
and define
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma).
\]
Then the finite-moment evaluations $A_k(\nu)$ defined in
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation} satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-kl-even-orders}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{k=4}^{2N}A_k(\nu)t^{-k}+o(t^{-2N})
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N})
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\end{equation}
More precisely, with the constants and threshold from
Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}, for
$t\ge T_{N,\eta}^{\rm ent}(\nu)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-kl-even-orders-explicit-remainder}
\left|
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)-\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
\right|
\le
C_{N,\eta}^{\rm dens}(\nu)t^{-(2N+\eta)}
+C_{N,\eta}^{\rm Tay}(\nu)t^{-(2N+2)}.
\end{equation}
Here $C_{N,\eta}^{\rm dens}(\nu)$, $C_{N,\eta}^{\rm Tay}(\nu)$, and
$T_{N,\eta}^{\rm ent}(\nu)$ are the finite quantities fixed in
Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}.  They depend on the single
law~$\nu$, on $N$ and~$\eta$, and on the listed universal mode norms; no
uniformity over a class of laws and no sharpness of these constants is
asserted.
The finite-moment passage is precisely the reduction isolated in
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-moment-entropy-passage}: density-ratio
control in $L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)$, finite Taylor expansion of
$\Phi(z)=(1+z)\log(1+z)-z$, and integration of the remaining finite mode
polynomial.  The strong-moment Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-uniform-remainder} is not
part of the hypotheses of this theorem.
Equivalently, no odd power occurs in the expansion through order~$t^{-2N}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-moment-entropy-passage} is the proof
step: it performs the KL integration from the density-ratio expansion with
only the finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment, and it records the two
dominating errors separately.  The sharper density-to-entropy lemma,
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-moment-density-to-entropy-bounds},
spells out the $L^\infty/L^1$ descent, absolute convergence, Taylor domination,
and threshold dependence used by that passage.  Thus the strong-moment power
remainder is used only for coefficient display, not for the finite-moment
asymptotic claim.

Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder} with $L=2N$.
With $x=\bar\gamma+ty$ one has
\[
\delta_t(x):=\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}-1
=\int_{\RR}\bigl(R_{\Delta/t}(y)-1\bigr)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
The finite-moment density remainder gives
\[
A_{N,t}(y):=\sum_{n=2}^{2N}\mu_n\,u_n(y)t^{-n},
\qquad
\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)=A_{N,t}(y)+\rho_{N,t}(y),
\]
with
\[
\|\rho_{N,t}\|_\infty+\|\rho_{N,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
=O(t^{-(2N+\eta)})
\]
and $\|\delta_t\|_\infty=O(t^{-2})$.

Fix constants $C_\rho$, $M_A$, and $S$ so that,
for all larger $t$,
\[
\|\rho_{N,t}\|_\infty+\|\rho_{N,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le C_\rho t^{-(2N+\eta)},\qquad
\|A_{N,t}\|_\infty\le M_A t^{-2},\qquad
\|\delta_t\|_\infty\le \min\{1/2,S t^{-2}\}.
\]
Here $C_\rho$ is the constant $C_{\nu,2N,\eta}$ supplied by
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder}, and one may take
\[
M_A=\sum_{n=2}^{2N}|\mu_n|\,\|u_n\|_\infty .
\]
After increasing the threshold if necessary, such an $S$ exists by the
finite-moment density remainder.  Thus these constants depend only on the
specified law~$\nu$, on $N$ and~$\eta$, and on the listed universal mode
bounds; no class-uniform constant is used in this proof.

Lemma~\ref{lem:density-to-entropy-domination}, with the domain checks recorded
in Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-moment-density-to-entropy-bounds},
applied with
$A_t=A_{N,t}$ and $\rho_t=\rho_{N,t}$, gives the explicit
$L^1(\omega)$ domination needed for integration of the Taylor expansion.
In particular,
\begin{align*}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
&=\sum_{k=2}^{N}\frac{(-1)^k}{k(k-1)}
\sum_{m=2k}^{2N}\frac{1}{t^m}
\sum_{\substack{n_1,\dots,n_k\ge 2\\n_1+\cdots+n_k=m}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_k}\,I_{n_1,\dots,n_k} \\
&\qquad +O(t^{-(2N+\eta)})+O(t^{-(2N+2)}),
\end{align*}
where $I_{n_1,\dots,n_k}=I(n_1,\dots,n_k)$ is the mode integral from
\eqref{eq:mode-integral-definition}.  Comparing the coefficient of
$t^{-m}$ with the evaluated formal polynomial
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation} gives $A_m(\nu)$.
The parity part of Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} gives
$A_m(\nu)=0$ for odd~$m$, so only even powers remain.  Reindexing $m=2r$
gives
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{r=2}^{N} A_{2r}(\nu)\,t^{-2r}+o(t^{-2N}),
\]
with the sharper displayed remainder before the final $o(t^{-2N})$ notation.
Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-entropy-constant-bound} identifies the constants in
the last two error terms with the closed finite expressions in
\eqref{eq:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}.  This gives
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-even-orders-explicit-remainder} and proves
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-even-orders}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Strict finite-moment hierarchy for the entropy expansion]%
\label{prop:strict-finite-moment-entropy-regime}
Let $N\ge2$ and $0<\eta\le1$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}
has a finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th-moment hypothesis, separate from the
stronger $(2N+1)$st-moment coefficient algebra.  More precisely, its coefficients, constants, and
threshold are those supplied by Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder}
with $L=2N$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}.  The
strong-moment Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-uniform-remainder} is used only to display
the stronger-moment coefficient algebra.

If $0<\eta<1$, this distinction is strict: there are probability measures
with finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment and infinite centred
$(2N+1)$st absolute moment.  For every such measure, after translation to its
mean, Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder} still apply, while
Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-uniform-remainder} is not available.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the content of the displayed hypotheses and proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}: the density input is
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder} with $L=2N$, whose constant
is $C_{\nu,2N,\eta}=2C_{2N}\EE|\Delta|^{2N+\eta}$, and the entropy constants
are the finite expressions in Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}.
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} refers to
Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-uniform-remainder} only to identify the same finite
coefficient algebra under a stronger hypothesis; every estimate used for the
announced asymptotic remainder is then repeated with the finite
$(2N+\eta)$ density remainder.

It remains to prove strictness when $0<\eta<1$.  Put $p=2N+\eta$ and
$q=2N+1$, so $p<q$.  Let $X$ be the symmetric probability law on $\RR$ with a
bounded density near the origin and tails proportional to
$(1+|x|)^{-(q+1)}$ for large $|x|$.  Then $\EE X=0$, and the usual integral
test gives
\[
\EE|X|^r<\infty \quad\text{for } r<q,
\qquad
\EE|X|^q=\infty .
\]
In particular $\EE|X|^{2N+\eta}<\infty$ but
$\EE|X|^{2N+1}=\infty$.  This law satisfies the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder}, because those hypotheses
ask only for the finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment.  It fails the
hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-uniform-remainder}, whose input is a
finite centred $(2N+1)$st absolute moment.  Thus the lemma cannot be a hidden
assumption behind the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform finite-moment entropy remainder on moment balls]%
\label{cor:uniform-moment-ball-entropy-remainder}
Fix $N\ge2$, $0<\eta\le1$, and $M<\infty$.  There are finite constants
\[
C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm dens},\qquad C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm Tay},
\qquad T_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm ent}
\]
depending only on $N$, $\eta$, $M$, and the universal Cayley mode norms such
that the following holds uniformly for every probability measure $\nu$ on
$\RR$ with finite mean $\bar\gamma$ and centred moment bound
\[
\int_{\RR}|\gamma-\bar\gamma|^{2N+\eta}\dd\nu(\gamma)
\le M .
\]
With $h_t:=P_t*\nu$, $g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma)$, and the law-dependent
coefficients $A_{2m}(\nu)$ from
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}, one has, for every
$t\ge T_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm ent}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-moment-ball-entropy-remainder}
\left|
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)-\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
\right|
\le
C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm dens}t^{-(2N+\eta)}
+C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm Tay}t^{-(2N+2)} .
\end{equation}
Consequently the expansion in Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} is
uniform on every centred $(2N+\eta)$-moment ball, although the coefficients
remain the moment polynomials of the individual law.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:moment-ball-uniform-constant-domination}.  It gives
uniform dominating constants for the density remainder constant
$C_{\nu,2N,\eta}$, the smallness quantity $S_{\nu,2N,\eta}$, the entropy
constants $C_{N,\eta}^{\rm dens}(\nu)$ and $C_{N,\eta}^{\rm Tay}(\nu)$ from
\eqref{eq:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}, and the threshold
$T_{N,\eta}^{\rm ent}(\nu)$, all depending only on $N$, $\eta$, $M$, and the
universal mode norms.  Choose
$C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm dens}$, $C_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm Tay}$, and
$T_{N,\eta,M}^{\rm ent}$ to be those dominating constants.  The explicit
remainder estimate in Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}, with these
larger constants substituted, gives
\eqref{eq:uniform-moment-ball-entropy-remainder} for every law in the moment
ball.  The coefficients are still the evaluated moment polynomials of the
individual law, so only the remainder constants and threshold are made uniform.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-moment entropy remainder]%
\label{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder}
Under the hypotheses and notation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}, put
\[
\mathcal R_N(t;\nu)
:=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
-\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal R_N(t;\nu)
=O(t^{-(2N+\eta)})+O(t^{-(2N+2)})
\qquad(t\to\infty),
\]
with the fixed-law constants recorded in
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-even-orders-explicit-remainder}: the constants multiplying
$t^{-(2N+\eta)}$ and $t^{-(2N+2)}$ are the finite expressions in
Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-entropy-constant-bound}, depending on the specified
law~$\nu$, $N$, $\eta$, and the listed universal mode norms,
and in particular $\mathcal R_N(t;\nu)=o(t^{-2N})$.
Equivalently, after the universal even coefficient polynomial is subtracted,
the finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment hypothesis leaves no further
contribution at any order $t^{-k}$ with $k\le 2N$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite-moment passage used here is
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-moment-entropy-passage}: the density
remainder is integrated in $L^1(\omega)$ after a finite Taylor expansion, with
the density error and Taylor tail kept as the two displayed orders.  Hence the
present theorem is the finite centred $(2N+\eta)$-moment remainder statement.
The strong-moment Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-uniform-remainder} has no role in this
proof except as an optional strong-moment derivation of finite coefficient
arithmetic.

Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} gives
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-even-orders-explicit-remainder}, hence in particular
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\sum_{k=4}^{2N}A_k(\nu)t^{-k}
+O(t^{-(2N+\eta)})+O(t^{-(2N+2)}),
\]
with the stated two separate dependencies.
By the parity conclusion in the same theorem, $A_k(\nu)=0$ for every odd
$k\le2N$, while the smallest possible coefficient is $A_4$ because all mode
indices in \eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient} are at least~$2$ and the
entropy series starts with quadratic powers.  Hence the displayed polynomial
is exactly $\sum_{m=2}^N A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}$.  Subtracting it gives the stated
remainder bound, and the assumptions $0<\eta\le1$ and $N\ge2$ imply both
$t^{-(2N+\eta)}=o(t^{-2N})$ and $t^{-(2N+2)}=o(t^{-2N})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-moment closure of the coefficient calculus]%
\label{cor:finite-moment-coefficient-closure}
Let $N\ge2$, let $0<\eta\le1$, and assume that $\nu$ has mean
$\bar\gamma$ and finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment.  Then the
formal coefficient polynomial constructed in
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients}, after the finite-moment
evaluation of Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation},
is the actual large-time coefficient polynomial for the Poisson entropy
comparison through order~$t^{-2N}$.  Equivalently, for every $2\le m\le N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-moment-actual-coefficients}
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{2m}\left(
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
-\sum_{r=2}^{m-1}A_{2r}(\nu)t^{-2r}
\right)=A_{2m}(\nu),
\end{equation}
with the empty sum understood as zero.  In particular, the finite-moment
route in the entropy hierarchy is closed by
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder}; no centred
$(2N+1)$st moment is required for the entropy expansion itself.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} gives
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\sum_{r=2}^{N}A_{2r}(\nu)t^{-2r}+o(t^{-2N}).
\]
Fix $2\le m\le N$ and subtract the terms with $2\le r<m$.  Multiplying by
$t^{2m}$ gives
\begin{align*}
t^{2m}\Bigg(
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
-\sum_{r=2}^{m-1}A_{2r}(\nu)t^{-2r}
\Bigg)
&=A_{2m}(\nu) \\
&\quad +\sum_{r=m+1}^{N}A_{2r}(\nu)t^{-2(r-m)}+o(1).
\end{align*}
The finite sum on the right tends to zero except for the displayed
$A_{2m}(\nu)$ term, and the remainder also tends to zero.  The coefficients
are precisely the universal Cayley--Chebyshev coefficients by the comparison
step in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  That proof
uses Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder} with $L=2N$, whose
hypothesis is exactly the finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Analytic compact-support interchange]%
\label{thm:analytic-compact-support-interchange}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure with mean $\bar\gamma$ and compactly
supported centred variable $\Delta=\gamma-\bar\gamma$.  Then there is
$T_\nu\ge1$ such that, for $t\ge T_\nu$, the series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:analytic-density-series}
\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)
=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\mu_n\,u_n(y)t^{-n}
\end{equation}
converges absolutely and uniformly in $y\in\RR$.  The entropy Taylor series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:analytic-entropy-series}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\sum_{k=4}^{\infty}A_k(\nu)t^{-k}
\end{equation}
also converges absolutely for $t\ge T_\nu$, where $A_k(\nu)$ is the
finite-moment evaluation of $A_k^{\rm form}$ from
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}.  In particular the
all-order coefficients are the genuine analytic coefficients under this
strong compact-support hypothesis, while
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder} gives the separate
finite-moment asymptotic justification.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $|\Delta|\le M$ almost surely.  The generating function for the
Chebyshev polynomials gives
\[
R_\varepsilon(\tan\theta)
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\cos^n\theta\,U_n(\sin\theta)\varepsilon^n.
\]
Since $|U_n(s)|\le n+1$ for $|s|\le1$ and $|\cos\theta|\le1$, the series is
dominated uniformly by $\sum_{n\ge0}(n+1)|\varepsilon|^n$ whenever
$|\varepsilon|<1$.  Choose $T_\nu\ge2M$.  Then
$|\Delta|/t\le1/2$, so dominated convergence permits termwise integration
against~$\nu$ and gives the absolutely and uniformly convergent density
series.  The $n=0$ term is one and the $n=1$ term is zero because
$\EE\Delta=0$, which proves \eqref{eq:analytic-density-series}.

The uniform convergence gives $\delta_t=O(t^{-2})$ uniformly.  Enlarging
$T_\nu$ if needed, $|\delta_t|\le1/2$, and the Taylor series for
$\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s$ converges absolutely and uniformly after the
substitution $s=\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)$.  To make the interchange explicit,
write
\[
b_n(t):=|\mu_n|\,\|u_n\|_\infty t^{-n}
\qquad(n\ge2),
\qquad
B(t):=\sum_{n\ge2}b_n(t).
\]
The compact support bound and $\|u_n\|_\infty\le n+1$ give
$B(t)\le\sum_{n\ge2}(n+1)(M/t)^n<\infty$, and after increasing $T_\nu$ we
may assume $B(t)\le1/2$.  Hence every product occurring in the entropy Taylor
series has the summable absolute majorant
\[
\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac1{r(r-1)}
\sum_{n_1,\dots,n_r\ge2}
b_{n_1}(t)\cdots b_{n_r}(t)
=\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{B(t)^r}{r(r-1)}<\infty.
\]
Since $\omega$ has total mass one and
\[
\int\bigl|u_{n_1}(y)\cdots u_{n_r}(y)\bigr|\,\omega(\dd y)
\le \prod_{j=1}^r\|u_{n_j}\|_\infty,
\]
this single majorant justifies both Fubini/Tonelli for
$\Phi(\delta_t)=\sum_{r\ge2}(-1)^r\delta_t^r/(r(r-1))$ and termwise
multiplication of the absolutely convergent density series.  For a fixed
power $t^{-k}$, only finitely many tuples $r\ge2$, $n_j\ge2$, and
$n_1+\cdots+n_r=k$ occur.  The degree-$k$ coefficient obtained by multiplying
the density series is therefore the finite-moment evaluation of the formal
expression in
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient}; hence
\eqref{eq:analytic-entropy-series} follows.
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Formal and asymptotic coefficient regimes]%
\label{lem:coefficient-regime-separation}
Fix $N\ge2$ and $0<\eta\le1$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item The polynomials $A_k^{\rm form}$ for $k\le2N$ are determined entirely
by the finite Cayley--Chebyshev mode algebra and the mode integrals
\eqref{eq:mode-integral-definition}; no probability measure is involved.
\item If a law $\nu$ has centred moments through order $2N$, then
$A_k(\nu)$ for $k\le2N$ are precisely the finite evaluations of these formal
polynomials, and no value is asserted for a coefficient whose required
moments are absent.
\item If the centred law of $\nu$ is compactly supported, then for all large
$t$ the analytic entropy series
\eqref{eq:analytic-entropy-series} converges absolutely and has coefficients
$A_k(\nu)$.
\item If $\nu$ has finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment, then the
same evaluated coefficients are the actual Poisson entropy asymptotic
coefficients through order $t^{-2N}$, with the fixed-law remainder constants
of Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-entropy-constant-bound} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder}.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently every later use of an unsuperscripted coefficient $A_{2m}(\nu)$
as an entropy coefficient, with $2\le m\le N$, is justified by the finite
centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment theorem unless a stronger
compact-support hypothesis is explicitly stated.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is exactly the definition
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient} together with the mode formula of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode} and the finite Fourier algorithm of
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}.  The second is
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation} applied for
each $k\le2N$.  The third is
Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic-compact-support-interchange}.  The fourth is
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}, with its explicit remainder
constants supplied by Lemma~\ref{lem:explicit-entropy-constant-bound} and
repackaged in Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder}.  The final
sentence is therefore the combination of these four implications:
the notation $A_{2m}(\nu)$ is a finite evaluation once the moments exist, but
its interpretation as an entropy coefficient is supplied by either the
compact-support interchange theorem or the finite-moment theorem
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Explicit eighth-order coefficients and defects}%
\label{subsec:eighth-order-defects}

The preceding two subsections separate the formal coefficient polynomial from
the finite-moment remainder theorem.  This block specializes the same formal
definitions through order $t^{-8}$, verifies the required mode integrals by
finite phase averaging, and rewrites the sixth- and eighth-order coefficients in
non-negative defect coordinates.  The standing hypothesis here is the finite
centred seventh absolute moment used in the direct mode-remainder proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.  This is weaker than the $N=4$
hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}, which asks for a finite
centred $(8+\eta)$th absolute moment; compact-support analytic interchange is
not used in this subsection.  The finite phase calculations certify the
constants, and the direct seventh-moment estimate supplies their entropy
meaning.


\begin{corollary}[Direct seventh-moment eighth-order expansion]%
\label{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred seventh absolute moment.  Then,
with
\[
\Delta:=\gamma-\bar\gamma,
\qquad
\mu_n:=\int_{\RR}\Delta^n\dd\nu(\gamma),
\]
and
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),
\]
one has, as $t\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-kl-eighth}
\begin{split}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
+\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64\,t^6}
\\
\quad
+\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2}{256\,t^8}
+\frac{12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256\,t^8}
+O_\nu(t^{-9}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
More precisely, there are constants $B_{\nu,8},T_{\nu,8}<\infty$ such
that, for all $t\ge T_{\nu,8}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder}
\begin{split}
\Bigg|D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
&-\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
-\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64\,t^6}
\\
&-\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256\,t^8}
\Bigg|
\le B_{\nu,8}t^{-9}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
We prove the displayed expansion from the seventh-moment mode remainder.  The
$N=4$ case of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} gives the same
coefficient notation only under the stronger finite centred $(8+\eta)$th
absolute moment hypothesis; the remainder estimate in the present corollary is
the direct seventh-moment estimate below.

Let $\omega(\dd y)=\pi^{-1}(1+y^2)^{-1}\dd y$.  With
$x=\bar\gamma+ty$, Lemma~\ref{lem:tail-uniform-cayley-mode-remainder}
with $X=\Delta$ and truncation order~$6$ gives
\[
\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty)
=\frac{\sigma^2}{t^2}u_2(y)
+\frac{\mu_3}{t^3}u_3(y)
+\frac{\mu_4}{t^4}u_4(y)
+\frac{\mu_5}{t^5}u_5(y)
+\frac{\mu_6}{t^6}u_6(y)
+\theta_t(y),
\]
where, if $v_6$ is the bounded remainder from
Proposition~\ref{prop:cayley-mode-remainder}, then
\[
\|\theta_t\|_{L^\infty(\RR)}
+\|\theta_t\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le
\|v_6\|_{L^\infty(\RR^2)}\,\EE|\Delta|^7\,t^{-7}.
\]
Also
$g_t(\bar\gamma+ty)\dd(\bar\gamma+ty)=\omega(\dd y)$, hence
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\int_{\RR}\Phi(\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty))\,\omega(\dd y).
\]
Set
\[
a_2:=\sigma^2\,u_2,\qquad a_3:=\mu_3\,u_3,\qquad a_4:=\mu_4\,u_4,
\qquad a_5:=\mu_5\,u_5,\qquad a_6:=\mu_6\,u_6.
\]
Write
\[
d_t:=t^{-2}a_2+t^{-3}a_3+t^{-4}a_4+t^{-5}a_5+t^{-6}a_6,
\qquad
\delta_t=d_t+\theta_t,
\]
with the same $\theta_t$ as above.  The finite seventh absolute moment is
used exactly here: it makes the omitted mode $\theta_t$ small enough that
any occurrence of it lies past order~$t^{-8}$.  For the quantitative
remainder, set
\[
U_n:=\|u_n\|_{L^\infty(\RR)}\quad(2\le n\le6),\qquad
V_6:=\|v_6\|_{L^\infty(\RR^2)},
\]
\[
A_\nu:=\sum_{n=2}^{6}|\mu_n|U_n,\qquad
M_\nu:=V_6\,\EE|\Delta|^7,\qquad H_\nu:=A_\nu+M_\nu .
\]
Then, for $t\ge1$,
\[
\|d_t\|_\infty\le A_\nu t^{-2},\qquad
\|\theta_t\|_\infty+\|\theta_t\|_{L^1(\omega)}
\le M_\nu t^{-7},\qquad
\|\delta_t\|_\infty\le H_\nu t^{-2}.
\]
Let
\[
K_\Phi:=\sum_{j=5}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-(j-5)}}{j(j-1)},
\qquad
T_{\nu,8}:=\max\{1,(2H_\nu)^{1/2}\}.
\]
For $t\ge T_{\nu,8}$,
\[
\left|\Phi(\delta_t)
-\left(\frac12\delta_t^2-\frac16\delta_t^3
+\frac1{12}\delta_t^4\right)\right|
\le K_\Phi H_\nu^5t^{-10}.
\]
For $k=2,3,4$ put
\[
S_{k,\nu}:=
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\dots,n_k\le6\\ n_1+\cdots+n_k\ge9}}
\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\mu_{n_j}|U_{n_j}.
\]
The discarded terms in the exact finite product $d_t^k$ after total
$t$-degree eight are bounded by $S_{k,\nu}t^{-9}$ in $L^\infty$.  Moreover
\[
E_{2,\nu}:=2A_\nu M_\nu+M_\nu^2,\qquad
E_{3,\nu}:=3A_\nu^2M_\nu+3A_\nu M_\nu^2+M_\nu^3,
\]
\[
E_{4,\nu}:=4A_\nu^3M_\nu+6A_\nu^2M_\nu^2
+4A_\nu M_\nu^3+M_\nu^4
\]
give, for $t\ge1$, the following explicit bounds:
\begin{align*}
\|\delta_t^2-d_t^2\|_\infty
&\le 2A_\nu M_\nu t^{-9}+M_\nu^2t^{-14}
\le E_{2,\nu}t^{-9},\\
\|\delta_t^3-d_t^3\|_\infty
&\le 3A_\nu^2M_\nu t^{-11}+3A_\nu M_\nu^2t^{-16}
+M_\nu^3t^{-21}
\le E_{3,\nu}t^{-9},\\
\|\delta_t^4-d_t^4\|_\infty
&\le 4A_\nu^3M_\nu t^{-13}+6A_\nu^2M_\nu^2t^{-18}
+4A_\nu M_\nu^3t^{-23}+M_\nu^4t^{-28}
\le E_{4,\nu}t^{-9}.
\end{align*}
Thus the sole order-$t^{-9}$ contribution involving the seventh-moment
remainder is the quadratic cross term $2d_t\theta_t$; all higher powers that
contain $\theta_t$ are strictly smaller.
Consequently one may take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eighth-remainder-constant}
B_{\nu,8}:=
\frac12(S_{2,\nu}+E_{2,\nu})
+\frac16(S_{3,\nu}+E_{3,\nu})
+\frac1{12}(S_{4,\nu}+E_{4,\nu})
+K_\Phi H_\nu^5 .
\end{equation}
This bound is used in the quantitative large-time corollary below; the
following computation is the first explicit instance of the evaluated
universal coefficient formula \eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}.
Since
the preceding estimates imply $\delta_t=O(t^{-2})$, $d_t=O(t^{-2})$, and
$\theta_t=O(t^{-7})$ uniformly, we have
\[
\delta_t^2=d_t^2+2d_t\theta_t+\theta_t^2=d_t^2+O(t^{-9}),
\]
while
\[
\delta_t^3=d_t^3+O(t^{-11}),\qquad
\delta_t^4=d_t^4+O(t^{-13}).
\]
Combining these estimates with the Taylor remainder for~$\Phi$ gives the
following uniform rule for coefficients:
\[
\begin{aligned}
[t^{-m}]\Phi(\delta_t)
&=[t^{-m}]\bigl(\tfrac12d_t^2-\tfrac16d_t^3+\tfrac1{12}d_t^4\bigr),
\\
&\hspace{5em}4\le m\le8.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here coefficient extraction is understood modulo a uniform $O(t^{-9})$
remainder.  This is the only point in the proof where the seventh absolute
moment is needed beyond the lower moments appearing in the displayed
coefficients.  Equivalently,
\[
\Phi(\delta_t)
=\frac12d_t^2-\frac16d_t^3+\frac1{12}d_t^4+O(t^{-9})
=\sum_{m=4}^{8}t^{-m}B_m(y)+O(t^{-9})
\]
uniformly in $y$.  The coefficient functions are therefore obtained by
expanding only the following finite products in powers of~$t^{-1}$.  No
term containing $\theta_t$ can contribute to any displayed coefficient, and
all terms of total order larger than eight are absorbed in the uniform
$O(t^{-9})$ remainder:
\begin{align*}
d_t^2
&=t^{-4}a_2^2+2t^{-5}a_2a_3
+t^{-6}(2a_2a_4+a_3^2) \\
&\quad
+t^{-7}(2a_2a_5+2a_3a_4)
+t^{-8}(2a_2a_6+2a_3a_5+a_4^2)+O(t^{-9}),\\
d_t^3
&=t^{-6}a_2^3+3t^{-7}a_2^2a_3
+t^{-8}(3a_2^2a_4+3a_2a_3^2)+O(t^{-9}),\\
d_t^4
&=t^{-8}a_2^4+O(t^{-9}).
\end{align*}
Thus
\begin{align*}
B_4&=\frac12a_2^2,\\
B_5&=a_2a_3,\\
B_6&=a_2a_4+\frac12a_3^2-\frac16a_2^3,\\
B_7&=a_2a_5+a_3a_4-\frac12a_2^2a_3,\\
B_8&=a_2a_6+a_3a_5+\frac12a_4^2
-\frac12a_2^2a_4-\frac12a_2a_3^2+\frac1{12}a_2^4.
\end{align*}
The definitions of $S_{k,\nu}$, $E_{k,\nu}$, and $K_\Phi$ above give,
for $t\ge T_{\nu,8}$,
\[
\left\|
\Phi(\delta_t)-\sum_{m=4}^{8}t^{-m}B_m
\right\|_{L^\infty(\RR)}
\le B_{\nu,8}t^{-9}.
\]
Thus the extraction rule gives $[t^{-m}]\Phi(\delta_t)=B_m$ for
$4\le m\le8$.  In particular, the $t^{-8}$ coefficient is exactly the sum
of three contributions:
\[
\frac12[t^{-8}]d_t^2
=a_2a_6+a_3a_5+\frac12a_4^2,
\]
\[
-\frac16[t^{-8}]d_t^3
=-\frac12a_2^2a_4-\frac12a_2a_3^2,
\qquad
\frac1{12}[t^{-8}]d_t^4=\frac1{12}a_2^4.
\]
The functions $B_5$ and $B_7$ are sums of products
$u_{n_1}\cdots u_{n_k}$ with odd total index, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:mode-parity-vanish} gives
\[
\int_{\RR}B_5\,\dd\omega=\int_{\RR}B_7\,\dd\omega=0.
\]
The remaining coefficients are obtained by the finite Fourier algorithm,
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}; the resulting
constant-term arithmetic for these low orders is recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:entropy-integrals}:
\[
\int_{\RR}B_4\,\dd\omega
=\frac{\sigma^4}{2}\int_{\RR}u_2^2\,\dd\omega
=\frac{\sigma^4}{8},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}B_6\,\dd\omega
=\sigma^2\mu_4\!\int_{\RR}u_2u_4\,\dd\omega
+\frac{\mu_3^2}{2}\!\int_{\RR}u_3^2\,\dd\omega
-\frac{\sigma^6}{6}\!\int_{\RR}u_2^3\,\dd\omega
=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64},
\]
and
\begin{align*}
\int_{\RR}B_8\,\dd\omega
&=\sigma^2\mu_6\!\int_{\RR}u_2u_6\,\dd\omega
+\mu_3\mu_5\!\int_{\RR}u_3u_5\,\dd\omega
+\frac{\mu_4^2}{2}\!\int_{\RR}u_4^2\,\dd\omega\\
&\quad
-\frac{\sigma^4\mu_4}{2}\!\int_{\RR}u_2^2u_4\,\dd\omega
-\frac{\sigma^2\mu_3^2}{2}\!\int_{\RR}u_2u_3^2\,\dd\omega
+\frac{\sigma^8}{12}\!\int_{\RR}u_2^4\,\dd\omega\\
&=\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2}{256}
+\frac{12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\end{align*}
Since $\omega(\RR)=1$, the explicit uniform bound
\eqref{eq:eighth-remainder-constant} gives
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder}.  Substituting the three displayed
coefficients proves \eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Seventh-moment bridge to the coefficient chain]%
\label{cor:seventh-moment-eighth-bridge}
Under the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}, the coefficients
of $t^{-4}$, $t^{-6}$, and $t^{-8}$ in
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth} are precisely the universal evaluations
$A_4(\nu)$, $A_6(\nu)$, and $A_8(\nu)$ from
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}, while the odd
evaluations $A_5(\nu)$ and $A_7(\nu)$ vanish.  Consequently the defect
coordinates used in Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}
and~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification} lie on the same coefficient chain as
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}, but their eighth-order finite-moment
asymptotic justification is the direct seventh-moment remainder
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder}.  If, in addition,
$\int|\gamma-\bar\gamma|^{8+\eta}\dd\nu(\gamma)<\infty$ for some
$0<\eta\le1$, then the same eighth-order jet is the $N=4$ truncation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} gives, with an
$L^\infty(\RR)$ error bounded by $B_{\nu,8}t^{-9}$,
\[
\Phi(\delta_t(\bar\gamma+ty))
=\sum_{m=4}^{8}t^{-m}B_m(y)+O_\nu(t^{-9}),
\]
where the functions $B_m$ are the finite products displayed in that proof.
After integration against $\omega$, the definitions of $B_4$, $B_6$, and $B_8$
are exactly the degree-$4$, degree-$6$, and degree-$8$ terms obtained by
substituting the finite mode expansion $\sum_{n=2}^{6}\mu_n\,u_n\,t^{-n}$ into
\[
\Phi(z)=\sum_{r\ge2}\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}z^r
\]
and retaining total degree at most eight.  This is precisely the finite
evaluation formula \eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}; the
equality with the displayed polynomials follows from the finite Fourier
algorithm, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}, and the
low-order arithmetic recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:entropy-integrals}.  The
same comparison shows that the degree-five and degree-seven finite evaluations
are $\int B_5\,\dd\omega$ and $\int B_7\,\dd\omega$, and these vanish by
Lemma~\ref{lem:mode-parity-vanish}.  Hence the defect coordinates built from
the displayed $t^{-6}$ and $t^{-8}$ coefficients use the universal coefficient
polynomials while relying on the seventh-moment bound
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder} for the asymptotic remainder.  Under
the stronger finite $(8+\eta)$th absolute moment hypothesis,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} applies with $N=4$ and gives the same
coefficient evaluations through order $t^{-8}$, proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Entropy coefficient hypotheses]%
\label{thm:strict-entropy-coefficient-hierarchy}
The entropy coefficients used in this section involve the following distinct
hypotheses.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item The formal coefficients $A_k^{\rm form}$ and the parity cancellation are
      determined before any law is chosen by
      Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} and the finite Fourier
      algorithm of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}.
\item If $\nu$ has a finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment, then the
      evaluated coefficients $A_{2m}(\nu)$, $2\le m\le N$, are the actual
      entropy-asymptotic coefficients through order $t^{-2N}$, with the
      fixed-law finite-moment remainder of
      Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder}.  If the centred law is
      compactly supported, the same coefficients are the analytic Taylor
      coefficients of the convergent entropy series in
      Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic-compact-support-interchange}; this is a
      stronger analytic argument, not an extra hypothesis in the finite-moment
      theorem.
\item The eighth-order corollary and the defect ladder use the finite centred
      seventh absolute moment hypothesis of Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.
      Under this hypothesis, the displayed $t^{-4}$, $t^{-6}$, and $t^{-8}$
      coefficients are the universal evaluations $A_4(\nu)$, $A_6(\nu)$, and
      $A_8(\nu)$, although this assertion need not be an instance of
      Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} with $N=4$ and need not come
      from compact-support analytic interchange.
\end{enumerate}
The separation in \textup{(iii)} is sharp: for every $0<\eta\le1$ there are
centred probability laws with positive variance and finite seventh absolute
moment for which Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth},
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification} apply, while the $N=4$ case of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} with exponent $8+\eta$ and the compact
support theorem both do not apply.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part~\textup{(i)} is exactly the law-free construction of
\eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient}: the mode integrals are finite
Cauchy-weighted integrals of Cayley--Chebyshev modes, the finite Fourier
algorithm computes them by Laurent coefficient extraction, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} proves that the odd formal
coefficients vanish by parity.

For~\textup{(ii)}, Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}
evaluates the formal polynomials whenever the required moments exist.
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} then identifies these evaluations
with the actual finite-moment entropy coefficients through order $t^{-2N}$,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder} records the same
identification together with the explicit fixed-law remainder constants.  If
the centred law is compactly supported, the dominated-convergence and absolute
summability argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic-compact-support-interchange}
gives a convergent analytic entropy series with the same coefficient formula.
Thus the compact-support assertion is a separate analytic strengthening; the
finite-moment asymptotic theorem has already been proved without it.

For~\textup{(iii)}, Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} proves the
eighth-order expansion directly from the seventh-moment mode remainder, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:seventh-moment-eighth-bridge} identifies the three displayed
coefficients with $A_4(\nu)$, $A_6(\nu)$, and $A_8(\nu)$.  The defect ladder
and amplification theorems use only these coefficients and the same
seventh-moment hypothesis, so their coefficient input is supplied by this
direct route.

It remains only to prove that the separation is strict.  Fix
$0<\eta\le1$.  Let $X$ be a symmetric real random variable with a bounded
density near the origin and with two-sided tails proportional to
$(1+|x|)^{-9}$ for large $|x|$, normalized to total mass one.  The integral
test gives
\[
\EE |X|^r<\infty \quad\text{for } r<8,
\qquad
\EE |X|^r=\infty \quad\text{for } r\ge8.
\]
In particular $\EE X=0$, $0<\EE X^2<\infty$, and $\EE|X|^7<\infty$, while
\(\EE |X|^{8+\eta}=\infty\).  The law of $X$ is not compactly supported.
Therefore Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} applies to this law, and so do
Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder} and
\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}; however the $N=4$ hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} with exponent $8+\eta$ fails, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic-compact-support-interchange} is unavailable.  This
proves the announced sharp separation of the three routes.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Moment-jet factorization of the eighth-order consequence]%
\label{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization}
Let $\nu$ and $\widetilde\nu$ be probability measures on~$\RR$ with means
$\bar\gamma$ and $\widetilde{\bar\gamma}$, positive variances, and finite
centred seventh absolute moments.  Write
\[
\mu_n:=\int(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^n\dd\nu(\gamma),
\qquad
\widetilde\mu_n:=\int(\gamma-\widetilde{\bar\gamma})^n
\dd\widetilde\nu(\gamma).
\]
If
\[
\mu_n=\widetilde\mu_n,
\qquad 2\le n\le6,
\]
then the two matched-mean Poisson entropy expansions have the same
eighth-order jet:
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\widetilde\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\widetilde{\bar\gamma}))
=O_{\nu,\widetilde\nu}(t^{-9}).
\]
Equivalently, the coefficients $A_4$, $A_6$, and $A_8$ in
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}, and hence the defect coordinates
$C_6$, $C_8$, $\Delta_6$, and $\Delta_8$ used in
Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}
and~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}, factor through the finite
centred moment jet $(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6)$.  Higher moments enter this
eighth-order consequence only through the validity and size of the
order-nine remainder.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} to both laws.  The displayed
formula \eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth} expresses the $t^{-4}$ coefficient as
\(\mu_2^2/8\), the $t^{-6}$ coefficient as
\((\mu_2^3+6\mu_3^2-8\mu_2\mu_4)/64\), and the $t^{-8}$ coefficient as
\[
\frac{3\mu_2^4-12\mu_2^2\mu_4+9\mu_2\mu_3^2
+12\mu_2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
These three polynomials involve only the centred moments
\(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6\).  Under the hypothesis
\(\mu_n=\widetilde\mu_n\) for $2\le n\le6$, the retained coefficients in the
two expansions are therefore equal.  Subtracting the two estimates
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder}, and using
$\max\{B_{\nu,8},B_{\widetilde\nu,8}\}$ after increasing the lower time
threshold if necessary, gives the stated
$O_{\nu,\widetilde\nu}(t^{-9})$ difference.

The expressions defining $C_6$ and $C_8$ are precisely the $t^{-6}$ and
$t^{-8}$ coefficients just identified, and
\(\Delta_6=-7\mu_2^3/64-C_6\),
\(\Delta_8=C_8-23\mu_2^4/256\).  Hence these defect variables also depend
only on $(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6)$.  The seventh absolute moments are used by
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} to produce the remainders, so they
affect the constants and threshold but not the printed eighth-order jet.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Eighth-order finite phase examples]%
\label{prop:eighth-order-finite-phase-verification}
Let $n_1,\dots,n_k\ge2$ and put
\[
m:=n_1+\cdots+n_k.
\]
Define the circle function $P_{n_1,\dots,n_k}$ by
\[
P_{n_1,\dots,n_k}(e^{2\ii\theta})
:=\prod_{j=1}^{k}u_{n_j}(\tan\theta),
\qquad -\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2.
\]
Then $P_{n_1,\dots,n_k}$ is a Laurent polynomial in the circle variable
$z=e^{2\ii\theta}$ with Fourier support contained in
$\{-m,\dots,m\}$.  Hence, for every integer $L>m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-grid-mode-product-verification}
\int_{\RR}
\prod_{j=1}^{k}u_{n_j}(y)\,
\frac{\dd y}{\pi(1+y^2)}
=
\frac1L\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_L}P_{n_1,\dots,n_k}(\zeta).
\end{equation}
In particular, any phase grid $\mu_L$ with $L\ge9$ exactly computes all
mode-product integrals entering the coefficients of
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} through order~$t^{-8}$, including the
odd-order cancellations at orders $t^{-5}$ and $t^{-7}$.
Consequently the quantities
\[
\frac{\sigma^4}{8},\qquad C_6,\qquad C_8,
\]
and the defect coordinates used in
Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}
and~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification} are finite phase quantities
once the moment coordinates appearing in
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} are fixed.  Here $C_6$ and $C_8$
denote the displayed $t^{-6}$ and $t^{-8}$ coefficients in
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the finite Fourier formulas
\eqref{eq:cayley-mode-even-fourier} and
\eqref{eq:cayley-mode-odd-fourier}, each function
$u_n(\tan\theta)$ is a finite linear combination of
$z^r$ and $z^{-r}$ with $1\le r\le n$.  Multiplying the $k$ expansions
shows that $P_{n_1,\dots,n_k}$ is a Laurent polynomial whose exponents lie
between $-m$ and $m$.

The change of variables $y=\tan\theta$ gives
\[
\frac{\dd y}{\pi(1+y^2)}=\frac{\dd\theta}{\pi},
\]
and $z=e^{2\ii\theta}$ pushes $\dd\theta/\pi$ on
$(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ to normalized Haar measure on~$\TT$.  Therefore the
left-hand side of \eqref{eq:finite-grid-mode-product-verification} is the
constant Fourier coefficient of $P_{n_1,\dots,n_k}$.  If $L>m$, no
nonzero exponent in the support interval $\{-m,\dots,m\}$ is divisible
by~$L$.  The character average over $\mu_L$ is thus $1$ on the constant
term and $0$ on every other term, which proves
\eqref{eq:finite-grid-mode-product-verification}.

This is the specialization of
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} to the products
needed by Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.  That corollary uses only
mode products
whose total index is at most~$8$:
\[
u_2^2,\quad
u_2u_4,\ u_3^2,\ u_2^3,\quad
u_2u_6,\ u_3u_5,\ u_4^2,\ u_2^2u_4,\ u_2u_3^2,\ u_2^4,
\]
together with the odd-total-index products that form the discarded
$t^{-5}$ and $t^{-7}$ coefficients.  Taking $L\ge9$ puts all these
products below the aliasing barrier.  The same exact grid identity
therefore recovers the displayed even coefficients and also computes the
zero odd coefficients, since their Haar integrals vanish by
Lemma~\ref{lem:mode-parity-vanish}.  The final assertion follows because
the displayed coefficients and the later defect coordinates are algebraic
combinations of these computed integrals and the stated moment
coordinates.
\end{proof}

The next two defect theorems use $C_6$ and $C_8$ as entropy-asymptotic
coefficients under the finite centred seventh absolute moment hypothesis of
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.  The fourth-moment algebra in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:fourth-moment-stability} keeps separate superscripted
notation until that same hypothesis is imposed.

\begin{theorem}[A two-level defect ladder]%
\label{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred seventh absolute moment.  Define
\begin{align*}
C_6&:=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64}, \\
C_8&:=
\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2+12\sigma^2\mu_6}{256}
+\frac{-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\end{align*}
Let $X:=(\gamma-\bar\gamma)/\sigma$ on the probability space
$(\RR,\nu)$, set
\[
\beta_n:=\EE[X^n],\qquad n\in\NN,\ n\ge1,\qquad
\beta_3:=\EE[X^3],
\qquad
Q_2:=X^2-1-\beta_3X,
\]
and define
\[
Q_3:=XQ_2-\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2X-\frac{\beta_3}{4}Q_2.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-c6}
\frac{64}{\sigma^6}C_6
=-7-2\beta_3^2-8\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-c8}
\frac{256}{\sigma^8}C_8
=23
+12\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+32\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^4
+\frac{61}{4}\beta_3^2\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+52\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+2\beta_3^4
+13\beta_3^2.
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-c8-lower}
C_8\ge \frac{23}{256}\sigma^8,
\end{equation}
and equality holds if and only if
\[
\nu=\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma+\sigma}
+\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma-\sigma}.
\]
The identity \eqref{eq:defect-c6} recovers the classical Pearson
moment relation, while \eqref{eq:defect-c8} gives the next higher-order
component in the same defect coordinates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
\kappa:=\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2=\EE[Q_2^2],
\qquad
L:=\EE[XQ_2^2],
\qquad
M:=\EE[Q_2^3].
\]
Since $Q_2=X^2-1-\beta_3X$, one has
\[
\kappa=\beta_4-1-\beta_3^2,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{64}{\sigma^6}C_6
=1+6\beta_3^2-8\beta_4
=-7-2\beta_3^2-8\kappa,
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:defect-c6}.

The identity $X^2=1+\beta_3X+Q_2$ yields
\[
\beta_5=\beta_3(\beta_3^2+2+2\kappa)+L,
\]
\[
\beta_6=1+3\beta_3^2+3\kappa+\beta_3^4+3\beta_3^2\kappa+3\beta_3L+M.
\]
Hence, if
\[
P_8:=3-12\beta_4+9\beta_3^2+12\beta_6-30\beta_3\beta_5+20\beta_4^2,
\]
then a direct substitution gives
\[
P_8
=23+20\kappa^2+16\beta_3^2\kappa+64\kappa+2\beta_3^4+13\beta_3^2+6\beta_3L+12M.
\]
On the other hand,
using $\EE[XQ_2]=0$, $\EE[Q_2]=0$, $\EE[X^2Q_2^2]=\kappa+\beta_3L+M$, and
$\EE[XQ_2^2]=L$, the definition
$Q_3=XQ_2-\kappa X-(\beta_3/4)Q_2$ gives
\[
\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
=M+\frac{\beta_3}{2}L+\kappa-\kappa^2+\frac{\beta_3^2}{16}\kappa.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
12\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
=12M+6\beta_3L+12\kappa-12\kappa^2+\frac34\beta_3^2\kappa.
\]
Substituting this last line into the preceding expression for $P_8$ gives
the exact regrouping
\[
P_8=23+12\|Q_3\|_2^2+32\kappa^2
+\frac{61}{4}\beta_3^2\kappa+52\kappa+2\beta_3^4+13\beta_3^2,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:defect-c8}.  Since
$P_8=256C_8/\sigma^8$, inequality \eqref{eq:defect-c8-lower} follows.

If equality holds in \eqref{eq:defect-c8-lower}, then every non-negative
term on the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:defect-c8} vanishes.  In
particular $\beta_3=0$ and $\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}=0$, so $X^2=1$
$\nu$-almost everywhere.  Since $\EE[X]=0$ and $\EE[X^2]=1$, it follows
that $X=\pm 1$ with equal weights.  Thus
\[
\nu=\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma+\sigma}
+\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma-\sigma}.
\]
The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Defect amplification]\label{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred seventh absolute moment.  Define
\[
\Delta_6(\nu):=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-C_6,
\qquad
\Delta_8(\nu):=C_8-\frac{23}{256}\sigma^8,
\]
where $C_6$ and $C_8$ are the entropy coefficients from
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.  Set
\[
X:=\frac{\gamma-\bar\gamma}{\sigma},
\qquad
\beta_3:=\EE[X^3],
\qquad
Q_2:=X^2-1-\beta_3X,
\]
\[
\kappa:=\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2,
\qquad
Q_3:=XQ_2-\kappa X-\frac{\beta_3}{4}Q_2.
\]
Then
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2+\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa,
\]
and
\begin{align}
\Delta_8(\nu)
&=
\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu)
\notag\\
&\quad
+\frac{\sigma^8}{256}\left(
12\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+32\kappa^2
+\frac{61}{4}\beta_3^2\kappa
+2\beta_3^4
\right). \label{eq:defect-amplification}
\end{align}
In particular,
\[
\Delta_8(\nu)\ge \frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu),
\]
and equality holds if and only if
\[
\nu=\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma+\sigma}
+\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma-\sigma}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:defect-c6} gives
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)
=-\frac{\sigma^6}{64}\left(7+\frac{64}{\sigma^6}C_6\right)
=\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2+\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa.
\]
Likewise, subtracting $23$ from \eqref{eq:defect-c8} yields
\[
\Delta_8(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^8}{256}\Bigl(
12\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+32\kappa^2
+\frac{61}{4}\beta_3^2\kappa
+52\kappa
+2\beta_3^4
+13\beta_3^2
\Bigr).
\]
By the preceding formula for $\Delta_6(\nu)$,
\[
\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^8}{256}\bigl(52\kappa+13\beta_3^2\bigr),
\]
and substituting this identity proves \eqref{eq:defect-amplification}.

If equality holds in the lower bound, then every non-negative term in
\eqref{eq:defect-amplification} vanishes.  In particular,
$\beta_3=0$ and $\kappa=0$, hence $Q_2=X^2-1$ vanishes
$\nu$-almost everywhere.  Since $\EE[X]=0$ and $\EE[X^2]=1$, it follows
that $X=\pm 1$ with equal weights, which is exactly the stated
two-point law.  The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Symmetric two-point entropy-defect rigidity]%
\label{cor:poisson-two-point-defect-rigidity}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred seventh absolute moment.  Let
$C_6$ and $C_8$ be the entropy coefficients from
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}, and define
\[
\Delta_6(\nu):=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-C_6,
\qquad
\Delta_8(\nu):=C_8-\frac{23}{256}\sigma^8.
\]
Let $\nu_c$ be the centred law of $\gamma-\bar\gamma$ and set
\[
\rho_\sigma:=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma).
\]
Then, among all laws with the same mean and variance in this moment class,
\[
C_6\le -\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6,
\qquad
C_8\ge \frac{23}{256}\sigma^8.
\]
Moreover the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item $\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$, equivalently
$\nu=\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma-\sigma}
+\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma+\sigma}$;
\item $C_6=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6$;
\item $C_8=\frac{23}{256}\sigma^8$;
\item $\Delta_6(\nu)=\Delta_8(\nu)=0$;
\item the amplification bound is sharp,
\[
\Delta_8(\nu)=\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu).
\]
\end{enumerate}
Thus, at the proved sixth/eighth-order layer, the symmetric two-point law is
the unique entropy-defect extremizer and the unique rigidity class for the
amplification chain.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the notation of Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}
and~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}.  From
\eqref{eq:defect-c6},
\[
C_6+\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6
=-\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2-\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa
\le0,
\qquad
\kappa:=\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2.
\]
Hence $C_6$ is maximized by, and only by, laws for which
$\beta_3=0$ and $\kappa=0$.  In that case
$Q_2=X^2-1$ vanishes almost surely, so $X=\pm1$ almost surely; since
$\EE X=0$ and $\EE X^2=1$, the two signs have equal mass.  This is exactly
$\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$.  Conversely, for $\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$ one has
$\beta_3=0$ and $Q_2=0$, so equality holds in the displayed bound for
$C_6$.

The lower bound for $C_8$ and the characterization of equality in that
bound are precisely \eqref{eq:defect-c8-lower} and the equality statement
in Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}.  The definitions of
$\Delta_6$ and $\Delta_8$ convert equality in the two coefficient bounds
into $\Delta_6(\nu)=0$ and $\Delta_8(\nu)=0$, respectively.  Finally,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification} states that equality in
\[
\Delta_8(\nu)\ge\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu)
\]
holds if and only if $\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$.  Combining these equivalences
proves all the listed conditions equivalent.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Gaussian input]\label{ex:gaussian-defects}
Let $\nu$ be the centred Gaussian law with variance~$\sigma^2$.  For
$X=(\gamma-\bar\gamma)/\sigma$ one has
\[
\beta_3=\beta_5=0,\qquad \beta_4=3,\qquad \beta_6=15,
\qquad \kappa=\beta_4-1=2.
\]
Hence the defect formulas give
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)=\frac{\sigma^6}{4},
\qquad
\Delta_8(\nu)=\frac{19}{16}\sigma^8.
\]
Equivalently, inserting the Gaussian moments into the coefficient
formula for $C_8$ gives
\[
\frac{256}{\sigma^8}C_8
=3-12\cdot3+12\cdot15+20\cdot9=327,
\]
and subtracting the extremal value~$23$ yields the same
$\Delta_8=(327-23)\sigma^8/256$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Zero-inflated symmetric two-point family]%
\label{ex:zero-inflated-defects}
Fix $0<p\le1$ and let $X_p$ be the centred variance-one random variable
defined by
\[
\mathbb P(X_p=0)=1-p,
\qquad
\mathbb P(X_p=p^{-1/2})=\mathbb P(X_p=-p^{-1/2})=\frac p2.
\]
For the law of $\bar\gamma+\sigma X_p$ one has
\[
\beta_3=\beta_5=0,\qquad
\beta_4=\frac1p,\qquad
\beta_6=\frac1{p^2},\qquad
\kappa=\frac1p-1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\left(\frac1p-1\right),
\]
and
\[
\Delta_8(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^8}{256}
\left(\frac{32}{p^2}-\frac{12}{p}-20\right)
=\frac{\sigma^8}{64}\,
\frac{(1-p)(5p+8)}{p^2}.
\]
At $p=1$ the law is the symmetric two-point extremizer and both defects
vanish; for $0<p<1$ the extra atom at the mean produces a positive
sixth- and eighth-order defect.
\end{example}

\subsection{Fourth-moment algebra and coefficient-level stability}
\label{subsec:fourth-moment-stability}

The superscripted notation in this subsection marks a hypothesis boundary.
With a finite centred fourth moment, $C_6^{(4)}(\nu)$ and
$\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)$ are only the algebraic polynomials obtained from the
displayed $t^{-6}$ formula.  A finite centred seventh absolute moment is used
in Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} to define the genuine entropy
coefficient $C_6$; Lemma~\ref{lem:seventh-moment-bridge} then identifies
$C_6=C_6^{(4)}(\nu)$ and $\Delta_6(\nu)=\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)$.  Thus the
argument proceeds from fourth-moment algebra, to the seventh-moment bridge, to
coefficient-level stability for the actual asymptotic defect.

\paragraph{Fourth-moment algebra.}

\begin{lemma}[Fourth-moment algebraic defect]%
\label{lem:fourth-moment-algebraic-defect}
Let $\nu$ have mean~$\bar\gamma$, variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite
centred fourth moment.  Define
\[
C_6^{(4)}(\nu):=
\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64},
\qquad
\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu):=
-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-C_6^{(4)}(\nu).
\]
The superscript $(4)$ is part of the notation: it records that the
quantity is a fourth-moment polynomial and carries no asymptotic assertion
by itself.  On $(\RR,\nu)$ set
\begin{align*}
X&:=\frac{\gamma-\bar\gamma}{\sigma},
&
\beta_3&:=\EE[X^3],
&
\beta_4&:=\EE[X^4],
\\
Q_2&:=X^2-1-\beta_3X,
&
\kappa&:=\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2.
\end{align*}
Then the pure fourth-moment identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-sixth-fourth-moment}
\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2+\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa,
\qquad
\kappa=\beta_4-1-\beta_3^2\ge0.
\end{equation}
Moreover, $\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)=0$ if and only if the centred law of
$\gamma-\bar\gamma$ is $\rho_\sigma=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}
+\delta_\sigma)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identity for $\kappa$ follows by expanding $Q_2^2$ and using
$\EE[X]=0$, $\EE[X^2]=1$, and $\EE[X^3]=\beta_3$:
\[
\kappa
=\EE[(X^2-1-\beta_3X)^2]
=\beta_4-1-\beta_3^2.
\]
Since $\mu_3=\sigma^3\beta_3$ and $\mu_4=\sigma^4\beta_4$,
\[
-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-C_6^{(4)}(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^6}{64}\bigl(-7-1-6\beta_3^2+8\beta_4\bigr)
=\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2+\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:defect-sixth-fourth-moment} and non-negativity.  If
$\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)=0$, then $\beta_3=0$ and $\kappa=0$, so
$X^2=1$ almost surely.  The conditions $\EE[X]=0$ and $\EE[X^2]=1$ then
force $X=\pm1$ with equal probabilities, i.e. the centred law is
$\rho_\sigma$.  The converse is immediate by substitution.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fourth-moment algebraic coupling]%
\label{lem:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling}
Let $\nu$ have mean~$\bar\gamma$, variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite
centred fourth moment.  Let $\nu_c$ be the centred law of
$\Delta=\gamma-\bar\gamma$ and set
\[
\rho_\sigma:=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma).
\]
For the fourth-moment polynomial $\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)$ defined above,
\[
\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)\ge0
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling}
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, $\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)=0$ if and only if
$\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$.  This is an algebraic fourth-moment statement; it does
not introduce an entropy coefficient.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All random variables below live on $(\RR,\nu)$.  Set
\[
X:=\frac{\gamma-\bar\gamma}{\sigma},
\qquad
\beta_3:=\EE[X^3],
\qquad
Q_2:=X^2-1-\beta_3X,
\qquad
\kappa:=\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2 .
\]
By \eqref{eq:defect-sixth-fourth-moment},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algebraic-coupling-delta-six}
\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2+\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa .
\end{equation}
Define
\[
Y:=\mathbf{1}_{\{X\ge 0\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{X<0\}}\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
For every real $x$, with $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=1$,
\[
|x-\operatorname{sgn}(x)|\le |x^2-1|.
\]
Therefore
\[
\EE[(X-Y)^2]\le \EE[(X^2-1)^2].
\]
The decomposition $X^2-1=\beta_3X+Q_2$ is orthogonal in $L^2(\nu)$ because
\[
\EE[XQ_2]=\EE[X^3]-\EE[X]-\beta_3\EE[X^2]=0.
\]
Thus
\[
\EE[(X^2-1)^2]
=\beta_3^2+\kappa
\le 2\beta_3^2+8\kappa
=\frac{64}{\sigma^6}\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu),
\]
where the last equality is \eqref{eq:algebraic-coupling-delta-six}.  Since
$\EE[X]=0$,
\[
|\EE[Y]|=|\EE[Y-X]|
\le \EE[|Y-X|]
\le \EE[(Y-X)^2]^{1/2}
\le \frac{8}{\sigma^3}\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)^{1/2}.
\]
Let $Z$ be a symmetric Rademacher random variable.  If
$p=\mathbb P(Y=1)$, then $\EE[Y]=2p-1$ and the optimal transport between
$\mathcal L(Y)$ and the symmetric law on $\{-1,1\}$ moves
$|p-1/2|$ mass across distance~$2$.  Hence
\[
W_2(\mathcal L(Y),\mathcal L(Z))^2
=4|p-1/2|=2|\EE[Y]|
\le \frac{16}{\sigma^3}\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)^{1/2}.
\]
Also
\[
W_2(\mathcal L(X),\mathcal L(Y))
\le \EE[(X-Y)^2]^{1/2}
\le \frac{8}{\sigma^3}\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)^{1/2}.
\]
The laws satisfy $\nu_c=\mathcal L(\sigma X)$ and
$\rho_\sigma=\mathcal L(\sigma Z)$.  Taking square roots in the preceding
$W_2$ bound, then using scaling and the triangle inequality, gives
\eqref{eq:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling}.
The zero statement follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment-algebraic-defect}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Seventh-moment bridge.}

\begin{lemma}[Seventh-moment bridge from algebra to entropy]%
\label{lem:seventh-moment-bridge}
Assume that $\nu$ has mean~$\bar\gamma$, variance $\sigma^2>0$, and
\[
\int_{\RR}\abs{\gamma-\bar\gamma}^{7}\,\dd\nu(\gamma)<\infty.
\]
Let $C_6$ be the $t^{-6}$ entropy coefficient supplied by
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}, and let $C_6^{(4)}(\nu)$ and
$\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)$ be the fourth-moment polynomials defined in
Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment-algebraic-defect}.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-six-bridge}
C_6=C_6^{(4)}(\nu),
\qquad
\Delta_6(\nu)
:=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-C_6
=\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu).
\end{equation}
Thus the unsuperscripted entropy defect $\Delta_6$ is introduced only under
the finite seventh absolute moment hypothesis.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The finite seventh absolute moment implies the fourth moment needed to
define $C_6^{(4)}(\nu)$ and $\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)$.  It also gives the
uniform remainder in the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.
Comparing the $t^{-6}$ coefficient in
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth} with the definition of $C_6^{(4)}(\nu)$ gives
$C_6=C_6^{(4)}(\nu)$.  Substituting this identity into
$\Delta_6(\nu):=-7\sigma^6/64-C_6$ and using
\eqref{eq:defect-sixth-fourth-moment} gives the displayed bridge identity.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Entropy layer.}
With only a finite centred fourth moment, the algebraic lemmas prove
\eqref{eq:defect-sixth-fourth-moment} and
\eqref{eq:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling} for the superscripted
polynomial $\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)$.  Without the additional assumption
\[
\int_{\RR}\abs{\gamma-\bar\gamma}^{7}\,\dd\nu(\gamma)<\infty,
\]
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} is not available, so the
unsuperscripted coefficient $C_6$ and defect $\Delta_6$ are not defined.

\begin{theorem}[Coefficient-level stability of the sixth-order asymptotic defect]%
\label{thm:poisson-defect-stability}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred seventh absolute moment, i.e.
\[
\int_{\RR}\abs{\gamma-\bar\gamma}^{7}\,\dd\nu(\gamma)<\infty.
\]
Let
$C_6$ be the $t^{-6}$ coefficient in
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}, which exists under the displayed
seventh-moment hypothesis, and define the sixth-order entropy defect by
\[
\Delta_6(\nu):=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-C_6.
\]
This is a coefficient-level theorem.  The finite seventh absolute moment
hypothesis is part of the definition of the object used here:
$\Delta_6$ is the entropy coefficient defect supplied by
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.  The fourth-moment
polynomial is the superscripted quantity $\Delta_6^{(4)}$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment-algebraic-defect}, and the bridge identity
\eqref{eq:delta-six-bridge} identifies it with $\Delta_6$ only under the
seventh-moment hypothesis.  Under finite fourth moment alone this theorem
is not asserted; only the separate algebraic coupling estimate
\eqref{eq:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling} for $\Delta_6^{(4)}$ remains.
Let $\nu_c$ be the centred law of $\Delta=\gamma-\bar\gamma$ and set
\[
\rho_\sigma:=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma).
\]
Then $\Delta_6(\nu)\ge0$ and
\[
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/2}.
\]
The numerical constants $4$ and $8$ are universal.  At this coefficient
level the only non-universal parameters displayed in the stability modulus
are the variance scale $\sigma$ and the coefficient defect $\Delta_6(\nu)$
itself; no finite-time remainder constant is involved.
In particular, for fixed $\sigma$,
\[
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)=O\!\bigl(\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/4}\bigr)
\qquad\text{as }\Delta_6(\nu)\downarrow 0.
\]
Moreover $\Delta_6(\nu)=0$ if and only if $\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The displayed seventh absolute moment condition is exactly the hypothesis
of Lemma~\ref{lem:seventh-moment-bridge}.  Hence
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)=\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu).
\]
The finite fourth moment needed in
Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling} is automatic.  Substituting
the bridge identity into
\eqref{eq:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling} gives the stated $W_2$ estimate,
and the non-negativity and zero characterization are those of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment-algebraic-defect} after the same substitution.
The asymptotic
$O(\Delta_6^{1/4})$ statement for fixed $\sigma$ follows immediately from
the displayed estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Quantitative large-time sixth-order entropy-deficit stability]%
\label{cor:poisson-finite-time-defect-stability}
Let $\nu$ satisfy the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-stability}, let $C_8$ be the $t^{-8}$
coefficient from Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}, let
$B_{\nu,8},T_{\nu,8}$ be constants for which
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder} holds, and define
$h_t:=P_t*\nu$ and $g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma)$.  Let $\nu_c$ be the
centred law of $\gamma-\bar\gamma$ and let
$\rho_\sigma=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma)$.  Define the
renormalized sixth-order entropy deficit by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-time-sixth-deficit}
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)
:=
t^6\left(
\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
-\frac{7\sigma^6}{64t^6}
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
\right).
\end{equation}
Then, for every $t\ge T_{\nu,8}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-time-defect-remainder}
\left|
\Delta_6(\nu)-\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)-\frac{C_8}{t^2}
\right|
\le B_{\nu,8}t^{-3}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every $t\ge T_{\nu,8}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-time-w2-stability}
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}A_t^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}A_t^{1/2},
\end{equation}
where
\[
A_t
:=
\bigl|\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\bigr|
+|C_8|t^{-2}+B_{\nu,8}t^{-3}.
\]
Thus the coefficient-level stability estimate is visible at finite time
after subtracting the universal $t^{-4}$ term and the symmetric two-point
$t^{-6}$ coefficient: the $t^{-8}$ coefficient contributes the displayed
$C_8t^{-2}$ correction in the renormalized scale, and the remaining error
is bounded by $B_{\nu,8}t^{-3}$.  The constants $C_8$, $B_{\nu,8}$, and
$T_{\nu,8}$ in this corollary are attached to the single law~$\nu$; no
uniformity over a class of laws is asserted here.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $t\ge T_{\nu,8}$, the quantitative eighth-order expansion gives
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}+\frac{C_6}{t^6}
+\frac{C_8}{t^8}+E_{\nu,8}(t),
\qquad |E_{\nu,8}(t)|\le B_{\nu,8}t^{-9}.
\]
Substituting this identity into
\eqref{eq:finite-time-sixth-deficit} yields
\[
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)
=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-C_6-\frac{C_8}{t^2}-t^6E_{\nu,8}(t)
=\Delta_6(\nu)-\frac{C_8}{t^2}-t^6E_{\nu,8}(t),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:finite-time-defect-remainder}.  Since
$\Delta_6(\nu)\ge0$, the same estimate gives
\[
0\le \Delta_6(\nu)
\le
\bigl|\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\bigr|
+|C_8|t^{-2}+B_{\nu,8}t^{-3}
=A_t.
\]
Inserting this bound for $\Delta_6(\nu)$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-stability} proves
\eqref{eq:finite-time-w2-stability}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Constant regimes for defect stability]%
\label{cor:defect-stability-constant-regimes}
In the chain from coefficient stability to fixed-law finite-time stability
and then to shell-uniform finite-time stability,
the constants have the following four distinct regimes.
\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.19\linewidth}|p{0.28\linewidth}|p{0.24\linewidth}|p{0.16\linewidth}}
result & hypotheses & visible constants & dependence\\ \hline
coefficient stability &
\(\mu_2=\sigma^2>0\), \(\EE|\Delta|^7<\infty\) &
\(4,8\) & universal\\
variance scale &
same coefficient-level hypotheses; comparison with \(\rho_\sigma\) &
\(\sigma^{-1/2},\sigma^{-2}\) & fixed by scale\\
fixed-law finite time &
one fixed law \(\nu\) as above, \(t\ge T_{\nu,8}\) &
\(C_8,B_{\nu,8},T_{\nu,8}\) & \(\nu\)-dependent\\
shell finite time &
\(\mu_2=\sigma^2\), \(\EE|\Delta|^7\le M\),
\(t\ge T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\) &
\(C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\) &
\(\sigma,M\)-dependent
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
More explicitly:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The numerical constants $4$ and $8$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-stability} are universal coefficient-level
constants; they do not depend on the law, on time, or on a moment shell.
\item The powers of $\sigma$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-stability}
are not additional universal constants but the explicit variance-scale
normalization forced by comparison with the symmetric two-point law
$\rho_\sigma$.
\item In Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-finite-time-defect-stability}, the
finite-time constants $C_8$, $B_{\nu,8}$, and $T_{\nu,8}$ are fixed-law
constants.  They may depend on the centred moments of the particular law
through order seven and on the universal mode-remainder norms, and they are not
claimed to be uniform over all laws of variance $\sigma^2$.
\item In Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-finite-time-defect-stability}, after
fixing $\sigma$ and a seventh centred absolute moment bound $M$, the
dominating constants $B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}$, $T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}$, and
$C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}$ are shell-uniform: they depend only on $\sigma$, $M$, and
the universal mode-remainder norms.
\end{enumerate}
These regimes are compatible and no implication reverses them: universal
coefficient-level constants do not supply finite-time uniformity, variance
scaling is not a hidden law-uniform remainder bound, and fixed-law finite-time
constants become shell-uniform only after the variance and seventh-moment shell
is fixed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Part~(i) is exactly the displayed estimate in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-stability}; its proof uses only the algebraic
coupling estimate \eqref{eq:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling} after the
seventh-moment bridge, so no constants from an entropy remainder enter.
Part~(ii) is only the scale normalization already visible in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-stability}: the comparison measure is
$\rho_\sigma$, so the displayed Wasserstein estimates necessarily carry the
explicit powers $\sigma^{-1/2}$ and $\sigma^{-2}$ and introduce no new
law-uniform remainder constants.
Part~(iii) follows from the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-finite-time-defect-stability}: the finite-time step
substitutes the quantitative eighth-order expansion
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder} for the same single law~$\nu$, and its
error term is precisely controlled by the law's constants
$B_{\nu,8}$ and $T_{\nu,8}$ together with its eighth coefficient $C_8$.
Part~(iv) is the assertion and proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-finite-time-defect-stability}: on the class
specified by $\mu_2=\sigma^2$ and $\EE|\Delta|^7\le M$, Lyapunov's inequality
bounds the lower centred moments, the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth} bounds the law-dependent eighth-order
remainder data by finite functions of $\sigma$, $M$, and the universal norms,
and the displayed $C_8$ polynomial is bounded by
$C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}$.  Replacing the fixed-law constants by these dominating
constants gives the shell-uniform regime and proves the compatibility
statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform finite-time stability on controlled seventh-moment shells]%
\label{cor:uniform-finite-time-defect-stability}
Fix $\sigma>0$ and $M<\infty$.  There are finite constants
\[
B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},\qquad T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},
\qquad C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}
\]
depending only on $\sigma$, $M$, and the universal mode-remainder norms such
that the following holds for every law $\nu$ satisfying the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-stability}, with variance exactly $\sigma^2$
and seventh centred absolute moment at most $M$.  For
$h_t:=P_t*\nu$, $g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma)$, and the finite-time deficit
$\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)$ from \eqref{eq:finite-time-sixth-deficit}, every
$t\ge T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-finite-time-defect-shell}
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\mathcal A_t^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\mathcal A_t^{1/2},
\end{equation}
where
\[
\mathcal A_t:=
\bigl|\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\bigr|
+C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}t^{-2}+B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}t^{-3}.
\]
Thus the finite-time stability conclusion is uniform only after one fixes the
variance shell and controls the seventh centred moment; without such a
controlled shell the law-dependent constants in
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-finite-time-defect-stability} are the asserted
level of generality.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}, the quantitative
remainder is expressed through
\[
A_\nu=\sum_{n=2}^{6}|\mu_n|U_n,
\qquad M_\nu=V_6\EE|\Delta|^7,
\qquad H_\nu=A_\nu+M_\nu,
\]
and the finite polynomial quantities $S_{k,\nu}$ and $E_{k,\nu}$ in
\eqref{eq:eighth-remainder-constant}.  The assumptions give
$\mu_2=\sigma^2$ and, by Lyapunov's inequality, $|\mu_n|\le M^{n/7}$ for
$3\le n\le6$, while $M_\nu\le V_6M$.  Hence $A_\nu$, $M_\nu$, $H_\nu$,
all $S_{k,\nu}$, all $E_{k,\nu}$, the threshold $T_{\nu,8}$, and the
remainder constant $B_{\nu,8}$ are bounded by finite constants depending only
on $\sigma$, $M$, and the universal norms.  The displayed eighth coefficient
$C_8$ in \eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth} is a polynomial in
$\sigma^2,\mu_3,\ldots,\mu_6$, so $|C_8|$ is bounded by a finite constant
$C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}$ on the same shell.  Replacing
$B_{\nu,8}$, $T_{\nu,8}$, and $|C_8|$ in
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-finite-time-defect-stability} by these dominating
constants proves \eqref{eq:uniform-finite-time-defect-shell} uniformly over the
controlled class.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Fourth-moment coupling for the algebraic defect]\label{rem:fourth-moment-stability-algebraic}
The finite-fourth-moment conclusion is the algebraic estimate
\eqref{eq:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling} for the polynomial
$\Delta_6^{(4)}$.  It does not assert a $t^{-6}$ entropy coefficient under only
fourth moments, and it therefore uses neither $C_6$ nor the unsuperscripted
entropy defect $\Delta_6$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The estimate is a coefficient-level analogue of stability-to-extremizers
results for entropy deficits, such as the logarithmic
Hardy--Littlewood--Sobolev stability theorem of Carlen
\cite{Carlen2025LogHLS} and the logarithmic Sobolev stability theorem of
Brigati--Dolbeault--Simonov
\cite{BrigatiDolbeaultSimonov2024LogSobolev}.  Here the defect is not a
