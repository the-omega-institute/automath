\subsection{Eighth-order defect layer extracted from the finite-order coefficient calculus}%
\label{subsec:eighth-order-defects}

The analytic input for the eighth-order coefficient identity is the
exact-threshold theorem with \(N=4\), which requires a finite centred sixth
absolute moment.  The displayed quantitative \(O_\nu(t^{-9})\) ledger uses
the positive-margin retained-order theorem with \(N=4\) and \(\eta=1\),
and therefore assumes a finite centred seventh absolute moment.  The work
below is the explicit coefficient audit: it evaluates \(A_4(\nu)\),
\(A_6(\nu)\), and \(A_8(\nu)\) using the normalized Laurent representatives
and then records the defect consequences under the stated seventh-moment
hypotheses.

\begin{lemma}[Laurent audit for the fourth, sixth, and eighth entropy coefficients]%
\label{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}
Let \(\omega(\dd y)=\pi^{-1}(1+y^2)^{-1}\dd y\), let
\(I(n_1,\ldots,n_r)\) be the Cauchy mode integral
\eqref{eq:mode-integral-definition}, and define \(Q_n\) by
\eqref{eq:mode-laurent-polynomial-definition}.  For the modes needed through
order eight, with the normalization
\(\cos(2\ell\theta)=(z^\ell+z^{-\ell})/2\) and
\(\sin(2\ell\theta)=(z^\ell-z^{-\ell})/(2\ii)\),
\begin{align*}
Q_2(z)&=-\frac14\bigl(z+z^{-1}+z^2+z^{-2}\bigr),\\
Q_3(z)&=-\frac1{8\ii}\bigl((z-z^{-1})+2(z^2-z^{-2})+(z^3-z^{-3})\bigr),\\
Q_4(z)&=\frac1{16}\bigl((z+z^{-1})+3(z^2+z^{-2})
+3(z^3+z^{-3})+(z^4+z^{-4})\bigr),\\
Q_5(z)&=\frac1{32\ii}\bigl((z-z^{-1})+4(z^2-z^{-2})
+6(z^3-z^{-3})+4(z^4-z^{-4})+(z^5-z^{-5})\bigr),\\
Q_6(z)&=-\frac1{64}\bigl((z+z^{-1})+5(z^2+z^{-2})
+10(z^3+z^{-3})\\
&\hspace{6.5em}
+10(z^4+z^{-4})+5(z^5+z^{-5})+(z^6+z^{-6})\bigr).
\end{align*}
The required constant Laurent coefficients are the following two blocks.
In particular, the sixth-order entries are
\([z^0]Q_3^2=3/16\) and \([z^0]Q_2^3=-3/32\).
These are the Laurent constants used below in \(B_6\) and \(B_8\); no
additional root-of-unity normalization or trigonometric half-factor enters.
The first block is the fourth- and sixth-order arithmetic:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{product} & [z^0] \\
\hline
Q_2^2 & \frac14 \\
Q_2Q_4 & -\frac18 \\
Q_3^2 & \frac{3}{16} \\
Q_2^3 & -\frac{3}{32}
\end{array}
\]
The second block is the eighth-order arithmetic:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{product} & [z^0] \\
\hline
Q_2Q_6 & \frac{3}{64} \\
Q_3Q_5 & -\frac{15}{128} \\
Q_4^2 & \frac{5}{32} \\
Q_2^2Q_4 & \frac{3}{32} \\
Q_2Q_3^2 & -\frac{9}{128} \\
Q_2^4 & \frac{9}{64}
\end{array}
\]
Set \(a_j=\mu_j u_j\) for \(2\le j\le6\), with
\(\mu_2=\sigma^2\), and
\[
d_t=t^{-2}a_2+t^{-3}a_3+t^{-4}a_4+t^{-5}a_5+t^{-6}a_6 .
\]
If an actual density expansion has the seventh-moment remainder
\(\delta_t=d_t+\theta_t\) with
\(\|d_t\|_\infty=O(t^{-2})\) and
\(\|\theta_t\|_\infty=O(t^{-7})\), then
\[
\Phi(\delta_t)
=\frac12d_t^2-\frac16d_t^3+\frac1{12}d_t^4+O(t^{-9})
\]
for \(\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\): the quadratic cross term
\(d_t\theta_t\) is \(O(t^{-9})\), all higher products containing
\(\theta_t\) are \(O(t^{-11})\) or smaller, and \(d_t^5=O(t^{-10})\).
The purely finite product expansion through total \(t\)-degree eight is
\begin{align*}
d_t^2
&=t^{-4}a_2^2+2t^{-5}a_2a_3
+t^{-6}(2a_2a_4+a_3^2) \\
&\quad
+t^{-7}(2a_2a_5+2a_3a_4)
+t^{-8}(2a_2a_6+2a_3a_5+a_4^2)+O(t^{-9}),\\
d_t^3
&=t^{-6}a_2^3+3t^{-7}a_2^2a_3
+t^{-8}(3a_2^2a_4+3a_2a_3^2)+O(t^{-9}),\\
d_t^4
&=t^{-8}a_2^4+O(t^{-9}).
\end{align*}
Equivalently,
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{coefficient} & \text{polynomial before integration} &
\text{mode integrals used} \\
\hline
B_4 &
\frac12a_2^2 &
I(2,2)=\frac14 \\
\hline
B_6 &
a_2a_4+\frac12a_3^2-\frac16a_2^3 &
\begin{gathered}
I(2,4)=-\frac18,\quad I(3,3)=\frac{3}{16},\\
I(2,2,2)=-\frac{3}{32}
\end{gathered}
\\
\hline
B_8 &
\begin{gathered}
a_2a_6+a_3a_5+\frac12a_4^2\\
-\frac12a_2^2a_4-\frac12a_2a_3^2+\frac1{12}a_2^4
\end{gathered}
&
\begin{gathered}
I(2,6)=\frac{3}{64},\quad I(3,5)=-\frac{15}{128},\\
I(4,4)=\frac{5}{32},\quad I(2,2,4)=\frac{3}{32},\\
I(2,3,3)=-\frac{9}{128},\quad I(2,2,2,2)=\frac{9}{64}
\end{gathered}
\end{array}
\]
The omitted odd functions
\[
B_5=a_2a_3,\qquad
B_7=a_2a_5+a_3a_4-\frac12a_2^2a_3
\]
have zero \(\omega\)-integral by Lemma~\ref{lem:mode-parity-vanish}.  Hence
using the displayed Laurent polynomials,
\[
\int_{\RR}B_4\,\dd\omega=\frac{\sigma^4}{8},\qquad
\int_{\RR}B_6\,\dd\omega
=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64},
\]
and
\[
\int_{\RR}B_8\,\dd\omega
=\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The formulas for \(Q_2,\ldots,Q_6\) are the special cases
\(n=2,\ldots,6\) of
\eqref{eq:mode-laurent-even}--\eqref{eq:mode-laurent-odd}.  The normalization
is the one fixed in Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}:
after setting \(z=e^{2\ii\theta}\), each occurrence of
\(\cos(2\ell\theta)\) or \(\sin(2\ell\theta)\) contributes the additional
Laurent factor \(1/2\) or \(1/(2\ii)\).  For example
\[
u_2(\tan\theta)=-\frac12(\cos2\theta+\cos4\theta)
\]
therefore gives
\[
Q_2(z)=-\frac14(z+z^{-1}+z^2+z^{-2}),
\]
where the prefactor \(-1/4\) is forced by the Fourier coefficient
\(-1/2\) of \(u_2(\tan\theta)\) and the Laurent factor \(1/2\) in each
cosine.  The cases \(Q_3,\ldots,Q_6\) follow in the same way from
\eqref{eq:mode-laurent-even}--\eqref{eq:mode-laurent-odd}.

By Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm},
\[
I(n_1,\ldots,n_r)=[z^0]Q_{n_1}\cdots Q_{n_r}.
\]
If \(Q_j(z)=\sum_k q_{j,k}z^k\), the extraction is the finite convolution
\[
[z^0]Q_{n_1}\cdots Q_{n_r}
=\sum_{k_1+\cdots+k_r=0}q_{n_1,k_1}\cdots q_{n_r,k_r}.
\]
Thus, for instance,
\[
[z^0]Q_2^2
=2\left(\frac1{4^2}\right)+2\left(\frac1{4^2}\right)
=\frac14,
\]
which checks the Laurent normalization against the first entry of the table.
The two sixth-order entries that feed the central \(B_6\) coefficient are
also checked directly by the same convolution.  Since
\(Q_2\) has coefficient \(-1/4\) at exponents \(\pm1,\pm2\), the only
zero-sum triples are the permutations of \((1,1,-2)\) and
\((-1,-1,2)\), hence
\[
[z^0]Q_2^3
=3\left(-\frac1{4^3}\right)+3\left(-\frac1{4^3}\right)
=-\frac3{32}.
\]
Equivalently, with \(S=z+z^{-1}+z^2+z^{-2}\),
\[
[z^0]Q_2^3
=-\frac1{64}[z^0]S^3
=-\frac1{64}[z^0](z+z^{-1}+z^2+z^{-2})^3
=-\frac6{64}
=-\frac3{32}.
\]
For \(Q_3\), the coefficient moduli at exponents \(1,2,3\) are
\(1/8,2/8,1/8\), and the signs pair as
\((\ii a)(-\ii a)=a^2\).  Therefore
\[
[z^0]Q_3^2
=2\left(\frac1{8^2}\right)
+2\left(\frac2{8}\right)^2
+2\left(\frac1{8^2}\right)
=\frac3{16}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
[z^0]Q_3^2
=-\frac1{64}
[z^0]\bigl((z-z^{-1})+2(z^2-z^{-2})+(z^3-z^{-3})\bigr)^2
=\frac{12}{64}
=\frac3{16}.
\]
The same finite convolution applied to the displayed \(Q_2,\ldots,Q_6\)
gives the remaining constant terms listed above.  Substituting those
constants into \(B_4\), \(B_6\), and \(B_8\) gives
\[
\int_{\RR}B_4\,\dd\omega
=\frac{\sigma^4}{2}I(2,2)=\frac{\sigma^4}{8},
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\RR}B_6\,\dd\omega
&=\sigma^2\mu_4 I(2,4)
+\frac{\mu_3^2}{2}I(3,3)
-\frac{\sigma^6}{6}I(2,2,2)  \\
&=-\frac{\sigma^2\mu_4}{8}
+\frac{3\mu_3^2}{32}
+\frac{\sigma^6}{64}
=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64},
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\RR}B_8\,\dd\omega
&=\sigma^2\mu_6 I(2,6)+\mu_3\mu_5 I(3,5)
+\frac{\mu_4^2}{2}I(4,4)\\
&\quad-\frac{\sigma^4\mu_4}{2}I(2,2,4)
-\frac{\sigma^2\mu_3^2}{2}I(2,3,3)
+\frac{\sigma^8}{12}I(2,2,2,2)\\
&=\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Formal and evaluated eighth coefficient]%
\label{lem:formal-seventh-eighth-coefficient}
The formal degree-eight coefficient \(A^{\mathrm{form}}_8\) involves only
\(\mathfrak m_2,\ldots,\mathfrak m_6\).  Its evaluated polynomial is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formal-eighth-evaluated-polynomial}
{\rm ev}_{\nu,8}(A^{\mathrm{form}}_8)
=
\frac{3\mu_2^4-12\mu_2^2\mu_4+9\mu_2\mu_3^2
+12\mu_2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\end{equation}
If \(\nu\) has a finite centred sixth absolute moment, then
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} with \(N=4\) supplies the
corresponding eighth-order entropy coefficient identity.  The actual
\(t^{-8}\) coefficient is
\[
A_8(\nu)={\rm ev}_{\nu,8}(A^{\mathrm{form}}_8).
\]
If, in addition, \(\nu\) has a finite centred seventh absolute moment, then
the positive-margin ledger in
Corollary~\ref{cor:optimized-retained-order-entropy-bridge} gives an
\(O_\nu(t^{-9})\) remainder with this same coefficient \(A_8(\nu)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In the formal definition \eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient}, a
degree-eight monomial has the form
\(\mathfrak m_{n_1}\cdots\mathfrak m_{n_r}\) with \(r\ge2\),
\(n_j\ge2\), and \(n_1+\cdots+n_r=8\).  Hence every index satisfies
\(2\le n_j\le6\); no \(\mathfrak m_7\) or \(\mathfrak m_8\) can occur.
The degree-eight product before integration is
\[
a_2a_6+a_3a_5+\frac12a_4^2
-\frac12a_2^2a_4-\frac12a_2a_3^2+\frac1{12}a_2^4
\]
with \(a_j=\mu_j u_j\).  The audit table above gives its integral
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mu_2\mu_6 I(2,6)+\mu_3\mu_5 I(3,5)
+\frac12\mu_4^2 I(4,4)
-\frac12\mu_2^2\mu_4 I(2,2,4)\\
&\qquad
-\frac12\mu_2\mu_3^2 I(2,3,3)
+\frac1{12}\mu_2^4 I(2,2,2,2),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:formal-eighth-evaluated-polynomial} after
substitution of the listed mode integrals.

The last assertion is now only a reference to the exact-threshold
finite-moment theorem and its positive-margin ledger.  The finite centred
sixth absolute moment is the exact-threshold hypothesis \(2N-2=6\) in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} when \(N=4\), so it is enough for
the coefficient identity.  The finite centred seventh absolute moment is the
positive-margin hypothesis \(2N-2+\eta=7\) in
Corollary~\ref{cor:optimized-retained-order-entropy-bridge} when \(N=4\) and
\(\eta=1\), and it is used only for the \(O_\nu(t^{-9})\) remainder.  The
displayed formal computation identifies \(A_8(\nu)\) with
\({\rm ev}_{\nu,8}(A^{\mathrm{form}}_8)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit \(N=4\) coefficient identity at the sixth-moment threshold]%
\label{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred sixth absolute moment.  Then,
with
\[
\Delta:=\gamma-\bar\gamma,
\qquad
\mu_n:=\int_{\RR}\Delta^n\dd\nu(\gamma),
\]
and
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}, applied with \(N=4\), gives
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=A_4(\nu)t^{-4}+A_6(\nu)t^{-6}+A_8(\nu)t^{-8}+o(t^{-8}).
\]
The finite centred sixth absolute moment is the exact-threshold hypothesis
\(2N-2=6\).  No seventh moment is used in this coefficient identity.  The
only new content here is the explicit Laurent audit of the retained
coefficients:
\[
C_4=\frac{\sigma^4}{8},\qquad
C_6=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64},
\]
\[
A_8(\nu)=
\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
These final coefficients use the Laurent normalization of
\(Q_2,\ldots,Q_6\) displayed in
Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}; in particular
\([z^0]Q_2^2=1/4\), \([z^0]Q_3^2=3/16\), and
\([z^0]Q_2^3=-3/32\), consistent with
\(Q_2(1)=u_2(0)=-1\) and with the displayed \(B_6\) row.
With these coefficients one has, as $t\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-kl-eighth}
\begin{split}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
+\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64\,t^6}
\\
\quad
+\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2}{256\,t^8}
+\frac{12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256\,t^8}
+o(t^{-8}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} with \(N=4\) applies because
\(\nu\) has finite centred sixth absolute moment.  It gives the asymptotic
identity through order \(t^{-8}\) with remainder \(o(t^{-8})\).  It remains
only to evaluate the three retained coefficients.

The coefficient \(A_4(\nu)\) is the integral of
\(\frac12(\mu_2u_2)^2\).  Since \(\mu_2=\sigma^2\) and
Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic} gives
\(I(2,2)=1/4\), one obtains \(A_4(\nu)=\sigma^4/8\).  The same lemma gives
\(I(3,3)=3/16\) and \(I(2,2,2)=-3/32\), hence
\[
A_6(\nu)
=\mu_2\mu_4 I(2,4)+\frac12\mu_3^2I(3,3)
-\frac16\mu_2^3I(2,2,2)
=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64}.
\]
Finally Lemma~\ref{lem:formal-seventh-eighth-coefficient} evaluates
\[
A_8(\nu)
=\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
The odd coefficients through order eight vanish by
Lemma~\ref{lem:mode-parity-vanish}, or equivalently by the parity statement
in Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients}.  Substituting these
audited values into the \(N=4\) exact-threshold expansion proves
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative \(N=4\) remainder under the seventh moment]%
\label{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred seventh absolute moment.  Use the
notation of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}; in particular \(A_8(\nu)\)
is the displayed \(t^{-8}\) coefficient there.
Let
\[
B_{\nu,8}
=P_4^{\rm opt}(C_\rho^{\rm opt},M_A^{\rm opt})
+Q_4^{\rm opt}(\mu,u)+2(S^{\rm opt})^5,
\qquad
T_{\nu,8}=T^{\rm opt},
\]
where the optimized quantities are those in
Corollary~\ref{cor:optimized-retained-order-entropy-bridge} with
\(N=4\), \(L=6\), and \(\eta=1\).  Then for every \(t\ge T_{\nu,8}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder}
\begin{split}
\Bigg|D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
&-\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
-\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64\,t^6}
\\
&-\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256\,t^8}
\Bigg|
\le B_{\nu,8}t^{-9}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the exact-threshold expansion \eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth}
improves in this positive-margin regime to
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
+\frac{C_6}{t^6}
+\frac{A_8(\nu)}{t^8}
+O_\nu(t^{-9}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite centred seventh absolute moment implies the finite centred sixth
absolute moment needed for Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}, so the
coefficient identity is already available.  The same seventh moment is the
\(N=4,\eta=1\) positive-margin hypothesis \(2N-2+\eta=7\) in
Corollary~\ref{cor:optimized-retained-order-entropy-bridge}.  Its specialized
estimate \eqref{eq:optimized-n4-seventh-envelope} is exactly
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder} with the displayed definitions of
\(B_{\nu,8}\) and \(T_{\nu,8}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Moment-jet factorization of the eighth-order consequence]%
\label{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization}
Let $\nu$ and $\widetilde\nu$ be probability measures on~$\RR$ with means
$\bar\gamma$ and $\widetilde{\bar\gamma}$, positive variances, and finite
centred seventh absolute moments.  Write
\[
\mu_n:=\int(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^n\dd\nu(\gamma),
\qquad
\widetilde\mu_n:=\int(\gamma-\widetilde{\bar\gamma})^n
\dd\widetilde\nu(\gamma).
\]
If
\[
\mu_n=\widetilde\mu_n,
\qquad 2\le n\le6,
\]
then the two matched-mean Poisson entropy expansions have the same
eighth-order jet:
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\widetilde\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\widetilde{\bar\gamma}))
=O_{\nu,\widetilde\nu}(t^{-9}).
\]
Equivalently, the coefficients $C_4$, $C_6$, and \(A_8\) in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}, and hence the defect
coordinates $C_6$, $A_8$, $\Delta_6$, and $\Delta_8$ used in
Corollaries~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}
and~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}, factor through the finite
centred moment jet $(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6)$.  Higher moments enter this
eighth-order consequence only through the validity and size of the
order-nine remainder.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} to both laws.  The displayed
formula \eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth} expresses the $t^{-4}$ coefficient as
\(\mu_2^2/8\), the $t^{-6}$ coefficient as
\((\mu_2^3+6\mu_3^2-8\mu_2\mu_4)/64\), and the $t^{-8}$ coefficient as
\[
\frac{3\mu_2^4-12\mu_2^2\mu_4+9\mu_2\mu_3^2
+12\mu_2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
These three polynomials involve only the centred moments
\(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6\).  Under the hypothesis
\(\mu_n=\widetilde\mu_n\) for $2\le n\le6$, the retained coefficients in the
two expansions are therefore equal.  The finite centred seventh absolute
moments allow
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder} to be applied to both
laws.  Subtracting the two estimates \eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder},
and using
$\max\{B_{\nu,8},B_{\widetilde\nu,8}\}$ after increasing the lower time
threshold if necessary, gives the stated
$O_{\nu,\widetilde\nu}(t^{-9})$ difference.

The expressions defining $C_6$ and \(A_8\) are precisely the $t^{-6}$ and
$t^{-8}$ coefficients just identified, and, in the seventh-moment regime,
\(\Delta_6=-7\mu_2^3/64-C_6\),
\(\Delta_8=A_8-23\mu_2^4/256\).  Hence these defect variables also depend
only on $(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6)$.  The seventh absolute moments are used by
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder} to produce the
remainders, so they affect the constants and threshold but not this displayed
eighth-order polynomial.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:eighth-order-finite-phase-verification} records the
finite phase verification layer for the same normalized Laurent products and
constant terms displayed in Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.
It uses the same trigonometric/Laurent normalization as the coefficient audit
above.

The next two defect corollaries use \(C_6\) and \(A_8(\nu)\) from the
finite-sixth coefficient identity, under the finite centred seventh absolute
moment hypothesis needed for the quantitative eighth-order remainder.  These
are downstream defect consequences, not theorem-level replacements for the
eighth-order jet.

\begin{corollary}[A two-level defect ladder]%
\label{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred seventh absolute moment.  Define
\begin{align*}
C_6&:=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64}, \\
A_8(\nu)&:=
\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2+12\sigma^2\mu_6}{256}
+\frac{-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\end{align*}
Let $X:=(\gamma-\bar\gamma)/\sigma$ on the probability space
$(\RR,\nu)$, set
\[
\beta_n:=\EE[X^n],\qquad n\in\NN,\ n\ge1,\qquad
\beta_3:=\EE[X^3],
\qquad
Q_2:=X^2-1-\beta_3X,
\]
and define
\[
Q_3:=XQ_2-\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2X-\frac{\beta_3}{4}Q_2.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-c6}
\frac{64}{\sigma^6}C_6
=-7-2\beta_3^2-8\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-c8}
\frac{256}{\sigma^8}A_8(\nu)
=23
+12\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+32\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^4
+\frac{61}{4}\beta_3^2\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+52\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+2\beta_3^4
+13\beta_3^2.
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-c8-lower}
A_8(\nu)\ge \frac{23}{256}\sigma^8,
\end{equation}
and equality holds if and only if
\[
\nu=\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma+\sigma}
+\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma-\sigma}.
\]
The identity \eqref{eq:defect-c6} recovers the classical Pearson
moment relation, while \eqref{eq:defect-c8} gives the next higher-order
component in the same defect coordinates.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
\kappa:=\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2=\EE[Q_2^2],
\qquad
L:=\EE[XQ_2^2],
\qquad
M:=\EE[Q_2^3].
\]
Since $Q_2=X^2-1-\beta_3X$, one has
\[
\kappa=\beta_4-1-\beta_3^2,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{64}{\sigma^6}C_6
=1+6\beta_3^2-8\beta_4
=-7-2\beta_3^2-8\kappa,
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:defect-c6}.

The identity $X^2=1+\beta_3X+Q_2$ yields
\[
\beta_5=\beta_3(\beta_3^2+2+2\kappa)+L,
\]
\[
\beta_6=1+3\beta_3^2+3\kappa+\beta_3^4+3\beta_3^2\kappa+3\beta_3L+M.
\]
Hence, if
\[
P_8:=3-12\beta_4+9\beta_3^2+12\beta_6-30\beta_3\beta_5+20\beta_4^2,
\]
then a direct substitution gives
\[
P_8
=23+20\kappa^2+16\beta_3^2\kappa+64\kappa+2\beta_3^4+13\beta_3^2+6\beta_3L+12M.
\]
On the other hand,
using $\EE[XQ_2]=0$, $\EE[Q_2]=0$, $\EE[X^2Q_2^2]=\kappa+\beta_3L+M$, and
$\EE[XQ_2^2]=L$, the definition
$Q_3=XQ_2-\kappa X-(\beta_3/4)Q_2$ gives
\[
\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
=M+\frac{\beta_3}{2}L+\kappa-\kappa^2+\frac{\beta_3^2}{16}\kappa.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
12\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
=12M+6\beta_3L+12\kappa-12\kappa^2+\frac34\beta_3^2\kappa.
\]
Substituting this last line into the preceding expression for $P_8$ gives
the exact regrouping
\[
P_8=23+12\|Q_3\|_2^2+32\kappa^2
+\frac{61}{4}\beta_3^2\kappa+52\kappa+2\beta_3^4+13\beta_3^2,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:defect-c8}.  Since
$P_8=256A_8(\nu)/\sigma^8$, inequality
\eqref{eq:defect-c8-lower} follows.

If equality holds in \eqref{eq:defect-c8-lower}, then every non-negative
term on the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:defect-c8} vanishes.  In
particular $\beta_3=0$ and $\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}=0$, so $X^2=1$
$\nu$-almost everywhere.  Since $\EE[X]=0$ and $\EE[X^2]=1$, it follows
that $X=\pm 1$ with equal weights.  Thus
\[
\nu=\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma+\sigma}
+\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma-\sigma}.
\]
The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Defect amplification]\label{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred seventh absolute moment.  Define
\[
\Delta_6(\nu):=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-C_6,
\qquad
\Delta_8(\nu):=A_8(\nu)-\frac{23}{256}\sigma^8,
\]
where $C_6$ is the $t^{-6}$ entropy coefficient and
\(A_8(\nu)\) is the actual \(t^{-8}\) entropy coefficient from
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}.  Set
\[
X:=\frac{\gamma-\bar\gamma}{\sigma},
\qquad
\beta_3:=\EE[X^3],
\qquad
Q_2:=X^2-1-\beta_3X,
\]
\[
\kappa:=\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2,
\qquad
Q_3:=XQ_2-\kappa X-\frac{\beta_3}{4}Q_2.
\]
Then
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2+\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa,
\]
and
\begin{align}
\Delta_8(\nu)
&=
\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu)
\notag\\
&\quad
+\frac{\sigma^8}{256}\left(
12\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+32\kappa^2
+\frac{61}{4}\beta_3^2\kappa
+2\beta_3^4
\right). \label{eq:defect-amplification}
\end{align}
In particular,
\[
\Delta_8(\nu)\ge \frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu),
\]
and equality in this proved sixth/eighth-order defect layer holds if and only if
\[
\nu=\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma+\sigma}
+\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma-\sigma}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:defect-c6} gives
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)
=-\frac{\sigma^6}{64}\left(7+\frac{64}{\sigma^6}C_6\right)
=\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2+\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa.
\]
Likewise, subtracting $23$ from \eqref{eq:defect-c8} yields
\[
\Delta_8(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^8}{256}\Bigl(
12\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+32\kappa^2
+\frac{61}{4}\beta_3^2\kappa
+52\kappa
+2\beta_3^4
+13\beta_3^2
\Bigr).
\]
By the preceding formula for $\Delta_6(\nu)$,
\[
\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^8}{256}\bigl(52\kappa+13\beta_3^2\bigr),
\]
and substituting this identity proves \eqref{eq:defect-amplification}.

If equality holds in the lower bound, then every non-negative term in
\eqref{eq:defect-amplification} vanishes.  In particular,
$\beta_3=0$ and $\kappa=0$, hence $Q_2=X^2-1$ vanishes
$\nu$-almost everywhere.  Since $\EE[X]=0$ and $\EE[X^2]=1$, it follows
that $X=\pm 1$ with equal weights, which is exactly the stated
two-point law.  The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Symmetric two-point entropy-defect rigidity]%
\label{cor:poisson-two-point-defect-rigidity}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred seventh absolute moment.  Let
$C_6$ be the $t^{-6}$ entropy coefficient and let \(A_8(\nu)\) be the
actual \(t^{-8}\) coefficient from Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}.
Define
\[
\Delta_6(\nu):=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-C_6,
\qquad
\Delta_8(\nu):=A_8(\nu)-\frac{23}{256}\sigma^8.
\]
Let $\nu_c$ be the centred law of $\gamma-\bar\gamma$ and set
\[
\rho_\sigma:=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma).
\]
Then, among all laws with the same mean and variance in this moment class,
\[
C_6\le -\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6,
\qquad
A_8(\nu)\ge \frac{23}{256}\sigma^8.
\]
Moreover the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item $\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$, equivalently
$\nu=\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma-\sigma}
+\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma+\sigma}$;
\item $C_6=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6$;
\item $A_8(\nu)=\frac{23}{256}\sigma^8$;
\item $\Delta_6(\nu)=\Delta_8(\nu)=0$;
\item the amplification bound is sharp,
\[
\Delta_8(\nu)=\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu).
\]
\end{enumerate}
Thus, at the proved sixth/eighth-order layer, and with no assertion here about
an unproved all-order defect hierarchy, the symmetric two-point law is
the unique entropy-defect extremizer and the unique rigidity class for the
amplification chain.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the notation of Corollaries~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}
and~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}.  From
\eqref{eq:defect-c6},
\[
C_6+\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6
=-\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2-\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa
\le0,
\qquad
\kappa:=\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2.
\]
Hence $C_6$ is maximized by, and only by, laws for which
$\beta_3=0$ and $\kappa=0$.  In that case
$Q_2=X^2-1$ vanishes almost surely, so $X=\pm1$ almost surely; since
$\EE X=0$ and $\EE X^2=1$, the two signs have equal mass.  This is exactly
$\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$.  Conversely, for $\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$ one has
$\beta_3=0$ and $Q_2=0$, so equality holds in the displayed bound for
$C_6$.

The lower bound for \(A_8(\nu)\) and the characterization of equality
in that bound are precisely \eqref{eq:defect-c8-lower} and the equality
statement in Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}.  The definitions of
\(\Delta_6\) and \(\Delta_8\) convert equality in the two coefficient bounds
into \(\Delta_6(\nu)=0\) and \(\Delta_8(\nu)=0\), respectively.  Finally,
Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification} states that equality in
\[
\Delta_8(\nu)\ge\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu)
\]
holds if and only if $\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$.  Combining these equivalences
proves all the listed conditions equivalent.
\end{proof}
