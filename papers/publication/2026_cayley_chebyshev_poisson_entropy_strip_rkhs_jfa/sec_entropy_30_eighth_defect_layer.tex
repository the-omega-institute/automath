\subsection{Order-eight coefficient layer extracted from the finite-order coefficient calculus}%
\label{subsec:order-eight-coefficient-layer}
\label{subsec:eighth-order-defects}

The analytic input for the eighth-order coefficient identity is
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} with \(N=4\).  The finite centred
sixth absolute moment is the \(N=4\) instance of the coefficient-evaluability
scope fixed after Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  The displayed
quantitative \(O_\nu(t^{-9})\) remainder uses
the positive-margin retained-order theorem with \(N=4\) and \(\eta=1\),
and therefore assumes a finite centred seventh absolute moment.

The coefficient notation is the one fixed in
Table~\ref{tab:coefficient-notation}.  In this subsection \(A_6,A_8\) are
first computed as explicit finite moment polynomials.  The subsequent
self-contained coefficient theorem is the single point where those evaluated
polynomials are identified with the corresponding entropy coefficients; the
same unsuperscripted notation is then used throughout the defect layer.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform Laurent representatives through order six]%
\label{lem:n4-uniform-laurent-representatives}
For \(2\le n\le6\), the Laurent representative used in the eighth-order
coefficient calculation is the specialization of the single formula
\eqref{eq:mode-laurent-polynomial-definition}.  Equivalently, if
\(Q_n(z)=\sum_kq_{n,k}z^k\), then \(q_{n,0}=0\) and
\eqref{eq:mode-laurent-coefficients} gives every coefficient, including the
odd-mode signs on negative exponents.  Thus the relevant supports are
\[
S_j:=\operatorname{supp} Q_j=\{\pm1,\ldots,\pm j\},
\qquad 2\le j\le6.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} restricted
to \(n=2,\ldots,6\).  The factors \(1/2\) and \(1/(2\ii)\) from cosine and
sine have already been absorbed into
\eqref{eq:mode-laurent-polynomial-definition}; no separate even/odd Laurent
display is used in the coefficient calculation.
\end{proof}

The constant-term arithmetic is used only through total order eight, and is
therefore a finite Laurent certificate.  The coefficient convention is always
the all-sign formula \eqref{eq:mode-laurent-coefficients}; in particular, the
signs of negative exponents in odd modes are already present in \(q_{n,k}\)
and no later conjugation rule is applied.

\begin{lemma}[Laurent recurrence and coefficients through order eight]%
\label{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}
Use the Laurent representatives of
Lemma~\ref{lem:n4-uniform-laurent-representatives}.  If
\(Q_n(z)=\sum_kq_{n,k}z^k\), the constant term
\(I(\mathbf n)=[z^0]\prod_{j=1}^rQ_{n_j}(z)\) is produced directly from the
Laurent coefficients by the recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eighth-order-constant-term-recurrence}
C_0(s)=\mathbf 1_{\{s=0\}},\qquad
C_j(s)=\sum_{0<|k|\le n_j}q_{n_j,k}\,C_{j-1}(s-k)
\quad(1\le j\le r),\qquad I(\mathbf n)=C_r(0).
\end{equation}
Applying this recurrence to the rows entering \(A_4,A_6,A_8\) gives
\[
A_4=\frac{\sigma^4}{8},\qquad
A_6=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64},
\]
and
\[
A_8=
\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
The full row-by-row finite certificate is recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} identifies each
Cauchy mode integral with the zero Laurent coefficient of the corresponding
product.  Multiplying the Laurent polynomials one factor at a time gives
\eqref{eq:eighth-order-constant-term-recurrence}; this is finite because
\(q_{n,k}=0\) unless \(0<|k|\le n\).  Combining the recurrence values with
the universal coefficient formula gives the displayed \(A_4,A_6,A_8\)
polynomials.  Appendix~\ref{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate} displays
the finite certificate, including the sign-sensitive rows \(I(3,5)=-15/128\)
and \(I(2,3,3)=-9/128\), which use the same all-sign rule as the other rows.

The three zero rows are the complete list of odd total-degree products
appearing before the even-power projection:
\[
B_5=a_2a_3,\qquad
B_7=a_2a_5+a_3a_4-\frac12a_2^2a_3 .
\]
They vanish by parity, because each contains an odd number of odd modes and
is odd under \(y\mapsto-y\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Substitution into \(A_4\), \(A_6\), and \(A_8\)]%
\label{lem:n4-entropy-coefficient-substitution}
Let \(a_j=\mu_j u_j\) for \(2\le j\le6\), with \(\mu_2=\sigma^2\), and set
\[
d_t=t^{-2}a_2+t^{-3}a_3+t^{-4}a_4+t^{-5}a_5+t^{-6}a_6 .
\]
The coefficient polynomials before integration are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{coefficient} & \text{polynomial before integration}\\
\hline
B_4 & \frac12a_2^2\\[0.25em]
B_6 & a_2a_4+\frac12a_3^2-\frac16a_2^3\\[0.25em]
B_8 &
a_2a_6+a_3a_5+\frac12a_4^2
-\frac12a_2^2a_4-\frac12a_2a_3^2+\frac1{12}a_2^4 .
\end{array}
\]
Using the recurrence values from
Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}, with the row certificate
displayed in Appendix~\ref{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate}, their
Cauchy integrals are
\[
A_4=\frac12\mu_2^2I(2,2)=\frac{\sigma^4}{8},
\]
\[
A_6=\mu_2\mu_4I(2,4)+\frac12\mu_3^2I(3,3)
-\frac16\mu_2^3I(2,2,2)
=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64},
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_8={}&\mu_2\mu_6I(2,6)+\mu_3\mu_5I(3,5)
+\frac12\mu_4^2I(4,4)\\
&-\frac12\mu_2^2\mu_4I(2,2,4)
-\frac12\mu_2\mu_3^2I(2,3,3)
+\frac1{12}\mu_2^4I(2,2,2,2)\\
={}&\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Taylor expansion
\[
\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s
=\frac12s^2-\frac16s^3+\frac1{12}s^4+O(s^5)
\]
and the fact that \(d_t=O(t^{-2})\) show that only powers \(d_t^2,d_t^3,d_t^4\)
can contribute through total order eight.  Expanding these powers gives
\begin{align*}
d_t^2
&=t^{-4}a_2^2+2t^{-5}a_2a_3
+t^{-6}(2a_2a_4+a_3^2) \\
&\quad
+t^{-7}(2a_2a_5+2a_3a_4)
+t^{-8}(2a_2a_6+2a_3a_5+a_4^2)+O(t^{-9}),\\
d_t^3
&=t^{-6}a_2^3+3t^{-7}a_2^2a_3
+t^{-8}(3a_2^2a_4+3a_2a_3^2)+O(t^{-9}),\\
d_t^4
&=t^{-8}a_2^4+O(t^{-9}).
\end{align*}
Collecting the \(t^{-4}\), \(t^{-6}\), and \(t^{-8}\) terms gives the three
displayed \(B\)-polynomials.  The \(t^{-5}\) and \(t^{-7}\) terms have zero
integral by the parity rows recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate}.  Substituting the
recurrence values gives the displayed formulas for \(A_4,A_6,A_8\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Normalization and two-point checks]
The representative \(Q_2\) in
Lemma~\ref{lem:n4-uniform-laurent-representatives} satisfies
\[
Q_2(1)=-1,
\]
agreeing with \(u_2(0)=-1\).  For the symmetric two-point law
\(\rho_\sigma=\tfrac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma)\), the moments are
\(\mu_2=\sigma^2\), \(\mu_3=\mu_5=0\), \(\mu_4=\sigma^4\), and
\(\mu_6=\sigma^6\).  Substitution into
Lemma~\ref{lem:n4-entropy-coefficient-substitution} gives
\[
A_6(\rho_\sigma)=\frac{1-8}{64}\sigma^6
=-\frac{7\sigma^6}{64},
\qquad
A_8(\rho_\sigma)=\frac{3-12+12+20}{256}\sigma^8
=\frac{23\sigma^8}{256}.
\]
These identities are sanity checks on the arithmetic for the already stated
coefficient-identification theorem below.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Moment-polynomial notation for the coefficient layer through order eight.}%
\label{par:eighth-order-coefficient-notation}
For a centred moment jet
\[
\mu_j=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^j\dd\nu(\gamma),
\qquad \mu_2=\sigma^2>0,
\]
define the purely algebraic moment polynomials
\begin{align}
A_6(\nu)
&:=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64},
\label{eq:n4-notation-a6}\\
A_8(\nu)
&:=\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256},
\label{eq:n4-notation-a8}\\
\Delta_6(\nu)
&:=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-A_6(\nu),
\label{eq:n4-notation-delta6}\\
\mathcal E_8(\nu)
&:=A_8(\nu)-\frac{23}{256}\sigma^8.
\label{eq:n4-notation-e8}
\end{align}
These four quantities use only the displayed finite moment jet and carry no
entropy-coefficient assertion by themselves.  The next theorem is the sole
identification step in this subsection: under the stated fourth- and
sixth-moment hypotheses, the already defined \(A_6\) and \(A_8\) are the
large-time entropy coefficients, and \(\Delta_6,\mathcal E_8\) are their
sixth-order defect and order-eight coefficient excess.

\begin{lemma}[Eighth coefficient polynomial before coefficient identification]%
\label{lem:formal-seventh-eighth-coefficient}
The universal degree-eight coefficient polynomial involves only
\(\mathfrak m_2,\ldots,\mathfrak m_6\).  Its evaluation at the moments of
\(\nu\) is the polynomial fixed in
\eqref{eq:n4-notation-a8}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formal-eighth-evaluated-polynomial}
A_8(\nu)
:=
\frac{3\mu_2^4-12\mu_2^2\mu_4+9\mu_2\mu_3^2
+12\mu_2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\end{equation}
At this stage \(A_8(\nu)\) is only a moment polynomial; its
entropy-coefficient status is supplied by
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In the formal definition \eqref{eq:universal-entropy-coefficient}, a
degree-eight monomial has the form
\(\mathfrak m_{n_1}\cdots\mathfrak m_{n_r}\) with \(r\ge2\),
\(n_j\ge2\), and \(n_1+\cdots+n_r=8\).  Hence every index satisfies
\(2\le n_j\le6\); no \(\mathfrak m_7\) or \(\mathfrak m_8\) can occur.
The degree-eight product before integration is
\[
a_2a_6+a_3a_5+\frac12a_4^2
-\frac12a_2^2a_4-\frac12a_2a_3^2+\frac1{12}a_2^4
\]
with \(a_j=\mu_j u_j\).  The coefficient table above gives its integral
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mu_2\mu_6 I(2,6)+\mu_3\mu_5 I(3,5)
+\frac12\mu_4^2 I(4,4)
-\frac12\mu_2^2\mu_4 I(2,2,4)\\
&\qquad
-\frac12\mu_2\mu_3^2 I(2,3,3)
+\frac1{12}\mu_2^4 I(2,2,2,2),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:formal-eighth-evaluated-polynomial} after
substitution of the listed mode integrals.

Thus the displayed finite computation identifies the evaluated polynomial
\(A_8(\nu)\).  No entropy-coefficient status is asserted in
this lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Self-contained coefficient identification through order eight]%
\label{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
finite variance $\sigma^2\ge0$.  With
\[
X_c:=\gamma-\bar\gamma,
\qquad
\mu_n:=\int_{\RR}X_c^n\dd\nu(\gamma)
\]
whenever the displayed moment exists, define
\[
A_6(\nu)
:=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64}.
\]
When the moments through order six exist, define
\[
A_8(\nu)
:=
\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
Let
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma).
\]

\textup{(i) Sixth coefficient.}
If \(\nu\) has finite centred fourth absolute moment, then the \(t^{-6}\)
entropy coefficient exists and
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
+\frac{A_6(\nu)}{t^6}
+o(t^{-6}).
\]
Here \(A_6(\nu)\) is the \(t^{-6}\) entropy coefficient.

\textup{(ii) Eighth coefficient.}
If \(\nu\) has finite centred sixth absolute moment, then \(A_8(\nu)\) is the
\(t^{-8}\) entropy coefficient, and the expansion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-kl-eighth}
\begin{split}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
+\frac{A_6(\nu)}{t^6}
\\
\quad
+\frac{A_8(\nu)}{t^8}
+o(t^{-8}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
In this case \(A_6(\nu)\) is also the \(t^{-6}\) entropy coefficient.
The associated entropy-coefficient defects are
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)
:=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-A_6(\nu),
\qquad
\mathcal E_8(\nu)
:=A_8(\nu)-\frac{23}{256}\sigma^8.
\]
These are the same unsuperscripted quantities defined in
\eqref{eq:n4-notation-a6}--\eqref{eq:n4-notation-e8}, now with their
entropy-coefficient status established under the hypotheses above.

These coefficients use the Laurent normalization of
\(Q_2,\ldots,Q_6\) fixed by
Lemma~\ref{lem:n4-uniform-laurent-representatives}, the constant-term
recurrence in Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}, the
certificate in Appendix~\ref{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate}, and the
coefficient substitution in
Lemma~\ref{lem:n4-entropy-coefficient-substitution}.  The normalization and
symmetric two-point checks are recorded in the preceding remark.  The finite
centred sixth absolute moment is exactly the \(N=4\) moment level in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}; its scope is the boxed paragraph
following that theorem.

If \(\sigma^2=0\), then \(\nu=\delta_{\bar\gamma}\),
\(h_t=g_t\), all centred moments in the displayed formulas vanish, and both
coefficient expansions are the exact identity \(0=0\).  Positive variance is
therefore not needed for this raw coefficient identification.  It is reserved
below for normalized defect statements, where one fixes a nondegenerate
variance scale and uses \(\rho_\sigma\) or negative powers of \(\sigma\).
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Coefficient-evaluation note.}
This theorem separates the moment-polynomial display from entropy-coefficient
identification.  The fourth moment identifies \(A_6\) as the \(t^{-6}\)
coefficient, while the sixth moment identifies \(A_8\) as the \(t^{-8}\)
coefficient and supplies the coefficient ladder through \(t^{-8}\) with an
\(o(t^{-8})\) remainder.  The seventh moment is used only later, in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}, to make the finite-time
\(O(t^{-9})\) remainder quantitative.

\begin{proof}
If \(\sigma^2=0\), then \(\nu=\delta_{\bar\gamma}\), \(h_t=g_t\), and every
centred moment in the displayed polynomials is zero, so both asserted
coefficient expansions are exact.  Assume from now on that \(\sigma^2>0\).

For part \textup{(i)}, the finite centred fourth absolute moment is the
\(2N-2\) hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} with
\(N=3\).  That theorem identifies the retained evaluated coefficient
\(A_6(\nu)\) with the entropy coefficient.  Lemma~\ref{lem:n4-entropy-coefficient-substitution}
evaluates the same retained universal coefficient as
\[
A_4(\nu)=\frac{\sigma^4}{8},\qquad
A_6(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64}.
\]
This proves the sixth-order expansion and
the displayed formula for \(A_6\).

For part \textup{(ii)}, the finite centred sixth absolute moment is the
\(2N-2\) hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} with
\(N=4\).  It gives the asymptotic identity through order \(t^{-8}\) with
remainder \(o(t^{-8})\), and identifies the retained evaluated coefficients
with entropy coefficients.  Lemma~\ref{lem:n4-entropy-coefficient-substitution}
evaluates the degree-eight retained coefficient as
\[
A_8(\nu)
=\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
The odd coefficients through order eight vanish by
Lemma~\ref{lem:mode-parity-vanish}, or equivalently by the parity statement
in Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients}.  Substituting these
computed values into the \(N=4\) universal-coefficient expansion proves
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth}.  The displayed definitions of
\(\Delta_6\) and \(\mathcal E_8\) are the defect definitions from
\eqref{eq:n4-notation-delta6}--\eqref{eq:n4-notation-e8}, now used with
the identified entropy coefficients.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[\(N=4,\eta=1\) retained-order constants]
\label{def:n4-finite-time-constant-dictionary}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\),
centred moments \(\mu_n=\EE(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^n\), and finite
\[
M_7(\nu):=\EE|\gamma-\bar\gamma|^7 .
\]
For the retained density order \(L=6\), set
\[
\mathcal M_{\nu,8}:=\sum_{n=2}^{6}(n+1)|\mu_n|,
\qquad
\mathcal C_{\nu,8}:=110M_7(\nu),
\]
\[
\mathcal S_{\nu,8}:=7\sum_{n=2}^{6}|\mu_n|+110M_7(\nu).
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal P_{\nu,8}:=
\sum_{r=2}^{4}\frac{1}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\binom r\ell
\mathcal M_{\nu,8}^{\,r-\ell}\mathcal C_{\nu,8}^{\,\ell}
\]
and
\[
\mathcal Q_{\nu,8}:=
\sum_{r=2}^{4}\frac{1}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{m=9}^{6r}
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\ldots,n_r\le6\\
n_1+\cdots+n_r=m}}
\prod_{j=1}^{r}\bigl((n_j+1)|\mu_{n_j}|\bigr),
\]
where an empty inner sum is zero.  The fixed-law order-nine remainder and
threshold constants used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dictionary-fixed-law-n4-constants}
B_{\nu,8}:=\mathcal P_{\nu,8}+\mathcal Q_{\nu,8}
+2\mathcal S_{\nu,8}^{5},
\qquad
T_{\nu,8}:=\max\{1,(2\mathcal S_{\nu,8})^{1/2}\}.
\end{equation}

For the controlled shell \(\mu_2=\sigma^2\),
\(\EE|\gamma-\bar\gamma|^7\le M\), \(M\ge\sigma^7\), write
\(\widehat M=M/\sigma^7\).  For \(u\ge1\), set
\[
\mathcal M^{(8)}(u)
:=3+\sum_{n=3}^{6}(n+1)u^{n/7},
\qquad
\mathcal R^{(8)}(u):=110u,
\]
\[
\mathcal S^{(8)}(u)
:=7\left(1+\sum_{n=3}^{6}u^{n/7}\right)+110u .
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal P^{(8)}(u):=
\sum_{r=2}^{4}\frac{1}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\binom r\ell
\bigl(\mathcal M^{(8)}(u)\bigr)^{r-\ell}
\bigl(\mathcal R^{(8)}(u)\bigr)^\ell
\]
and
\[
\mathcal Q^{(8)}(u):=
\sum_{r=2}^{4}\frac{1}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{m=9}^{6r}
\sum_{\substack{2\le n_1,\ldots,n_r\le6\\
n_1+\cdots+n_r=m}}
\prod_{j=1}^{r}(n_j+1)\,\Lambda_{n_j}(u),
\]
with
\[
\Lambda_2(u):=1,\qquad
\Lambda_n(u):=u^{n/7}\quad(3\le n\le6).
\]
The normalized controlled-shell functions are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dictionary-normalized-shell-functions}
\begin{aligned}
C(u)
&:=\frac{1}{256}\left(
3+12u^{4/7}+21u^{6/7}+50u^{8/7}
\right),\\
B(u)
&:=\mathcal P^{(8)}(u)+\mathcal Q^{(8)}(u)
+2\bigl(\mathcal S^{(8)}(u)\bigr)^5,\\
T(u)
&:=\max\{1,\bigl(2\mathcal S^{(8)}(u)\bigr)^{1/2}\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Their dimensional scale restores are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dictionary-controlled-shell-c8}
C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}:=\sigma^8C(\widehat M),\qquad
B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}:=\sigma^9B(\widehat M),\qquad
T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}:=\sigma T(\widehat M).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

There are only three constant regimes in the \(N=4\) layer.  First,
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} is coefficient-level and uses
only \(\Delta_6\) and the universal constants in its \(W_2\) bound.  Second,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder} uses the fixed-law
seventh-moment constants \(B_{\nu,8}\) and \(T_{\nu,8}\) in
\eqref{eq:dictionary-fixed-law-n4-constants}.  Third, the controlled-shell
functions \(C(u),B(u),T(u)\) and their scaled versions
\(C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\) are used only
for conditional bookkeeping after the shell
\(\mu_2=\sigma^2\), \(\EE|X_c|^7\le M\), \(M\ge\sigma^7\), has been fixed.
An inserted finite-time KL value and its error radius never certify these
constants.

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative \(N=4\) remainder under the seventh moment]%
\label{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
finite variance $\sigma^2\ge0$, and finite centred seventh absolute moment.  Use the
notation of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}; in particular
\(A_6(\nu)\) and \(A_8(\nu)\) are the identified entropy coefficients there.
Let \(B_{\nu,8}\) and \(T_{\nu,8}\) be the explicit constants in
\eqref{eq:dictionary-fixed-law-n4-constants}.  Then for every
\(t\ge T_{\nu,8}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder}
\begin{split}
\Bigg|D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
&-\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
-\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64\,t^6}
\\
&-\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256\,t^8}
\Bigg|
\le B_{\nu,8}t^{-9}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the coefficient-identifying expansion
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth} improves in this positive-margin regime to
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
+\frac{A_6(\nu)}{t^6}
+\frac{A_8(\nu)}{t^8}
+O_\nu(t^{-9}).
\]
For \(\sigma^2=0\), this is again the exact degenerate identity \(0=0\);
the positive-variance restrictions in this subsection occur only in
normalized shells and defect inequalities.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The finite centred sixth absolute moment is the \(N=4\) hypothesis
\(2N-2=6\).  The seventh moment enters
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder} only for the
quantitative \(O(t^{-9})\) remainder.

The entropy proof uses the centred moment jet and, for the quantitative
remainder, the seventh absolute moment bound directly.  No finite-grid,
lattice-reconstruction, or finite-time approximation input is used in the
entropy theorem chain.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
The finite centred seventh absolute moment implies the finite centred sixth
absolute moment needed for Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}, so the
coefficient identity is already available.  The same seventh moment is the
\(N=4,\eta=1\) retained-order hypothesis \(2N-2+\eta=7\) in
Proposition~\ref{prop:density-to-entropy-descent}.  In that proposition
\(L=6\), \(p=7\), \(C_\rho=2C_6M_7(\nu)\), and
\[
M_A=\sum_{n=2}^{6}|\mu_n|\,\|u_n\|_\infty,\qquad
S=B_6\sum_{n=2}^{6}|\mu_n|+2C_6M_7(\nu).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:closed-cayley-mode-constant-envelopes} gives
\(B_6\le7\), \(C_6\le55\), and \(\|u_n\|_\infty\le n+1\); hence
\[
C_\rho\le\mathcal C_{\nu,8},\qquad
M_A\le\mathcal M_{\nu,8},\qquad
S\le\mathcal S_{\nu,8}.
\]
The finite retained-mode tail in Proposition~\ref{prop:density-to-entropy-descent}
is bounded by \(\mathcal Q_{\nu,8}\).  Substituting these three displayed
majorants in \eqref{eq:explicit-entropy-constant-bound} with \(N=4\) and
\(\eta=1\) gives exactly
\eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Moment-jet factorization of the eighth-order coefficients]%
\label{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization}
Let $\nu$ and $\widetilde\nu$ be probability measures on~$\RR$ with means
$\bar\gamma$ and $\widetilde{\bar\gamma}$, positive variances, and finite
centred sixth absolute moments.  Write
\[
\mu_n:=\int(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^n\dd\nu(\gamma),
\qquad
\widetilde\mu_n:=\int(\gamma-\widetilde{\bar\gamma})^n
\dd\widetilde\nu(\gamma).
\]
If
\[
\mu_n=\widetilde\mu_n,
\qquad 2\le n\le6,
\]
then the two matched-mean Poisson entropy expansions have the same retained
eighth-order coefficient jet:
\[
A_6(\nu)=A_6(\widetilde\nu),
\qquad
A_8(\nu)=A_8(\widetilde\nu).
\]
Equivalently, the entropy coefficients \(A_6\) and
\(A_8\) in Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}, and hence the
entropy-coefficient comparison coordinates introduced below after coefficient identification,
factor through the finite centred moment jet $(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} to both laws.  The displayed
formula \eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth} expresses the $t^{-4}$ coefficient as
\(\mu_2^2/8\), the $t^{-6}$ coefficient as
\((\mu_2^3+6\mu_3^2-8\mu_2\mu_4)/64\), and the $t^{-8}$ coefficient as
\[
\frac{3\mu_2^4-12\mu_2^2\mu_4+9\mu_2\mu_3^2
+12\mu_2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
These three polynomials involve only the centred moments
\(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6\).  Under the hypothesis
\(\mu_n=\widetilde\mu_n\) for $2\le n\le6$, the retained coefficients in the
two expansions are therefore equal.

The expressions defining \(A_6\) and
\(A_8\) are precisely the \(t^{-6}\) and \(t^{-8}\)
coefficients just identified.  The entropy-coefficient comparison variables below are
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)
:=-\frac{7}{64}\mu_2^3-A_6(\nu),
\qquad
\mathcal E_8(\nu)
:=A_8(\nu)-\frac{23}{256}\mu_2^4 .
\]
Hence these coefficient comparison variables also depend only on $(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Moment-jet equality with seventh-moment remainder]%
\label{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization-seventh-remainder}
Let $\nu$ and $\widetilde\nu$ satisfy the hypotheses of
Corollary~\ref{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization}.  Assume in addition that
both laws have finite centred seventh absolute moments.  If
\[
\mu_n=\widetilde\mu_n,
\qquad 2\le n\le6,
\]
then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\widetilde\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\widetilde{\bar\gamma}))
=O_{\nu,\widetilde\nu}(t^{-9}).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization} gives equality of the
retained \(t^{-4}\), \(t^{-6}\), and \(t^{-8}\) coefficients.  The additional
finite centred seventh absolute moments allow
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder} to be applied to both
laws.  Subtracting the two estimates \eqref{eq:poisson-kl-eighth-remainder},
and using
\(\max\{B_{\nu,8},B_{\widetilde\nu,8}\}\) after increasing the lower time
threshold if necessary, gives the stated
\(O_{\nu,\widetilde\nu}(t^{-9})\) difference.
\end{proof}

\(A_6(\nu)\) and \(A_8(\nu)\) from the
finite-sixth coefficient identity.  They are coefficient-level defect
consequences.

\begin{corollary}[A two-level defect ladder]%
\label{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred sixth absolute moment.  Use the
coefficient notation above, after the identifications of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}.
Let $X:=(\gamma-\bar\gamma)/\sigma$ on the probability space
$(\RR,\nu)$, set
\[
\beta_n:=\EE[X^n],\qquad n\in\NN,\ n\ge1,\qquad
\beta_3:=\EE[X^3],
\qquad
Q_2:=X^2-1-\beta_3X,
\]
and define
\[
Q_3:=XQ_2-\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2X-\frac{\beta_3}{4}Q_2.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-c6}
\frac{64}{\sigma^6}A_6(\nu)
=-7-2\beta_3^2-8\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-c8}
\frac{256}{\sigma^8}A_8(\nu)
=23
+12\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+32\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^4
+\frac{61}{4}\beta_3^2\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+52\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+2\beta_3^4
+13\beta_3^2.
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-c8-lower}
A_8(\nu)\ge \frac{23}{256}\sigma^8,
\end{equation}
and equality holds if and only if
\[
\nu=\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma+\sigma}
+\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma-\sigma}.
\]
The identity \eqref{eq:defect-c6} recovers the classical Pearson
moment relation, while \eqref{eq:defect-c8} gives the next higher-order
component in the same coefficient comparison coordinates.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
\kappa:=\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2=\EE[Q_2^2],
\qquad
L:=\EE[XQ_2^2],
\qquad
M:=\EE[Q_2^3].
\]
Since $Q_2=X^2-1-\beta_3X$, one has
\[
\kappa=\beta_4-1-\beta_3^2,
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{64}{\sigma^6}A_6(\nu)
=1+6\beta_3^2-8\beta_4
=-7-2\beta_3^2-8\kappa,
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:defect-c6}.

The identity $X^2=1+\beta_3X+Q_2$ yields
\[
\beta_5=\beta_3(\beta_3^2+2+2\kappa)+L,
\]
\[
\beta_6=1+3\beta_3^2+3\kappa+\beta_3^4+3\beta_3^2\kappa+3\beta_3L+M.
\]
Hence, if
\[
P_8:=3-12\beta_4+9\beta_3^2+12\beta_6-30\beta_3\beta_5+20\beta_4^2,
\]
then a direct substitution gives
\[
P_8
=23+20\kappa^2+16\beta_3^2\kappa+64\kappa+2\beta_3^4+13\beta_3^2+6\beta_3L+12M.
\]
On the other hand,
using $\EE[XQ_2]=0$, $\EE[Q_2]=0$, $\EE[X^2Q_2^2]=\kappa+\beta_3L+M$, and
$\EE[XQ_2^2]=L$, the definition
$Q_3=XQ_2-\kappa X-(\beta_3/4)Q_2$ gives
\[
\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
=M+\frac{\beta_3}{2}L+\kappa-\kappa^2+\frac{\beta_3^2}{16}\kappa.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
12\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
=12M+6\beta_3L+12\kappa-12\kappa^2+\frac34\beta_3^2\kappa.
\]
Substituting this last line into the preceding expression for $P_8$ gives
the exact regrouping
\[
P_8=23+12\|Q_3\|_2^2+32\kappa^2
+\frac{61}{4}\beta_3^2\kappa+52\kappa+2\beta_3^4+13\beta_3^2,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:defect-c8}.  Since
$P_8=256A_8(\nu)/\sigma^8$, inequality
\eqref{eq:defect-c8-lower} follows.

If equality holds in \eqref{eq:defect-c8-lower}, then every non-negative
term on the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:defect-c8} vanishes.  In
particular $\beta_3=0$ and $\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}=0$, so $X^2=1$
$\nu$-almost everywhere.  Since $\EE[X]=0$ and $\EE[X^2]=1$, it follows
that $X=\pm 1$ with equal weights.  Thus
\[
\nu=\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma+\sigma}
+\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma-\sigma}.
\]
The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Coefficient excess amplification]\label{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred sixth absolute moment.  Define
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)
:=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-A_6(\nu),
\qquad
\mathcal E_8(\nu)
:=A_8(\nu)-\frac{23}{256}\sigma^8,
\]
with \(A_6\) and \(A_8\) the entropy coefficients identified in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}.  Set
\[
X:=\frac{\gamma-\bar\gamma}{\sigma},
\qquad
\beta_3:=\EE[X^3],
\qquad
Q_2:=X^2-1-\beta_3X,
\]
\[
\kappa:=\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2,
\qquad
Q_3:=XQ_2-\kappa X-\frac{\beta_3}{4}Q_2.
\]
Then
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2+\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa,
\]
and
\begin{align}
\mathcal E_8(\nu)
&=
\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu)
\notag\\
&\quad
+\frac{\sigma^8}{256}\left(
12\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+32\kappa^2
+\frac{61}{4}\beta_3^2\kappa
+2\beta_3^4
\right). \label{eq:defect-amplification}
\end{align}
In particular,
\[
\mathcal E_8(\nu)\ge
\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu),
\]
and equality in this proved coefficient layer through order eight holds if and only if
\[
\nu=\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma+\sigma}
+\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma-\sigma}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:defect-c6} gives
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)
=-\frac{\sigma^6}{64}
\left(7+\frac{64}{\sigma^6}A_6(\nu)\right)
=\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2+\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa.
\]
Likewise, subtracting $23$ from \eqref{eq:defect-c8} yields
\[
\mathcal E_8(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^8}{256}\Bigl(
12\|Q_3\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2
+32\kappa^2
+\frac{61}{4}\beta_3^2\kappa
+52\kappa
+2\beta_3^4
+13\beta_3^2
\Bigr).
\]
By the preceding formula for \(\Delta_6(\nu)\),
\[
\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^8}{256}\bigl(52\kappa+13\beta_3^2\bigr),
\]
and substituting this identity proves \eqref{eq:defect-amplification}.

If equality holds in the lower bound, then every non-negative term in
\eqref{eq:defect-amplification} vanishes.  In particular,
$\beta_3=0$ and $\kappa=0$, hence $Q_2=X^2-1$ vanishes
$\nu$-almost everywhere.  Since $\EE[X]=0$ and $\EE[X^2]=1$, it follows
that $X=\pm 1$ with equal weights, which is exactly the stated
two-point law.  The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Symmetric two-point coefficient rigidity]%
\label{cor:poisson-two-point-defect-rigidity}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$,
variance $\sigma^2>0$, and finite centred sixth absolute moment.  Use
\(A_6(\nu)\) and \(A_8(\nu)\) as the entropy coefficients identified in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}.
Define
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)
:=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-A_6(\nu),
\qquad
\mathcal E_8(\nu)
:=A_8(\nu)-\frac{23}{256}\sigma^8.
\]
Let $\nu_c$ be the centred law of $\gamma-\bar\gamma$ and set
\[
\rho_\sigma:=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma).
\]
Then, among all laws with the same mean and variance in this moment class,
\[
A_6(\nu)\le -\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6,
\qquad
A_8(\nu)\ge \frac{23}{256}\sigma^8.
\]
Moreover the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item $\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$, equivalently
$\nu=\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma-\sigma}
+\frac12\delta_{\bar\gamma+\sigma}$;
\item \(A_6(\nu)=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6\);
\item \(A_8(\nu)=\frac{23}{256}\sigma^8\);
\item \(\Delta_6(\nu)=
\mathcal E_8(\nu)=0\);
\item the amplification bound is sharp,
\[
\mathcal E_8(\nu)
=\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
This rigidity statement is only for the coefficient layer through order eight proved in
this paper.  No all-order defect hierarchy is asserted here.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
Use the notation of Corollaries~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}
and~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}.  From
\eqref{eq:defect-c6},
\[
A_6(\nu)+\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6
=-\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2-\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa
\le0,
\qquad
\kappa:=\|Q_2\|_{L^2(\nu)}^2.
\]
Hence \(A_6(\nu)\) is maximized by, and only by, laws for which
$\beta_3=0$ and $\kappa=0$.  In that case
$Q_2=X^2-1$ vanishes almost surely, so $X=\pm1$ almost surely; since
$\EE X=0$ and $\EE X^2=1$, the two signs have equal mass.  This is exactly
$\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$.  Conversely, for $\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$ one has
$\beta_3=0$ and $Q_2=0$, so equality holds in the displayed bound for
\(A_6(\nu)\).

The lower bound for \(A_8(\nu)\) and the characterization of equality
in that bound are precisely \eqref{eq:defect-c8-lower} and the equality
statement in Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}.  The definitions of
\(\Delta_6\) and \(\mathcal E_8\) convert equality
in the two coefficient bounds into
\(\Delta_6(\nu)=0\) and
\(\mathcal E_8(\nu)=0\), respectively.  Finally,
Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification} states that equality in
\[
\mathcal E_8(\nu)\ge
\frac{13\sigma^2}{8}\Delta_6(\nu)
\]
holds if and only if $\nu_c=\rho_\sigma$.  Combining these equivalences
proves all the listed conditions equivalent.
\end{proof}
