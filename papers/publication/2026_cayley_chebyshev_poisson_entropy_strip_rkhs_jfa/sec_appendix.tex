\section{Related-work comparison}%
\label{app:related-work-scope}

This appendix separates the theorem statements in the paper from nearby
results that use similar words--entropy, transport, Poisson, heat profile,
or Hardy--Stein--for different objects.  The introduction gives the compact
recent-\emph{J. Funct. Anal.} comparison.  The longer table below records the
broader comparator landscape, including non-JFA heat-profile, Poisson-channel,
Cauchy-divergence, and entropy-representation references.  The distinctions
used in the table are structural: the present law \(\nu\) is fixed, the
reference is the mean-matched harmonic Poisson/Cauchy kernel, and the
coefficients are obtained by expanding the quotient before applying
\(\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\).

Closed formulas for divergences between Cauchy laws also do not imply the
coefficient hierarchy above.  Results such as Nielsen--Okamura
\cite{NielsenOkamura2023Cauchy}, Nielsen
\cite{Nielsen2020CauchyVoronoi}, and Verd{\'u}
\cite{Verdu2023Cauchy} evaluate \(f\)-divergences or information-geometric
quantities when both arguments are members of a finite-dimensional Cauchy
family.  In the present problem the numerator \(P_t*\nu\) is generally a
mixture of Cauchy translates, not a single Cauchy law; only the denominator is
the mean-matched Cauchy kernel.  Even when a law is approximated by a nearby
Cauchy profile, the KL coefficients are not obtained by differentiating the
two-parameter Cauchy divergence formula: after centering, the linear term in
the quotient integrates to zero, while the \(t^{-2m}\) coefficient collects
all products of quotient modes whose total order is \(2m\) through the
nonlinear series for \(\Phi\).  The fixed-law hierarchy is therefore a
moment-polynomial constant-term calculation for a Cauchy mixture quotient,
not an expansion inside the Cauchy statistical manifold.

The Poisson terminology also hides adjacent but different entropy theories.
Poisson-channel papers such as Guo--Shamai--Verd{\'u}
\cite{GuoShamaiVerdu2008PoissonChannel} and Atar--Weissman
\cite{AtarWeissman2012PoissonChannel} study conditional count outputs
\(Y|X\sim{\rm Poisson}(\gamma X)\) or continuous-time counting processes and
relate mutual information, relative entropy, and estimation loss.  These are
count-output models rather than harmonic smoothing by \(P_t\), so they do not
produce the quotient expansion used in Theorems~\ref{thm:main-A}
and~\ref{thm:main-B}.

Small-time heat-kernel entropy expansions have a different limiting regime.
For example, the expansions of
Menkovski--Portegies--Ravelonanosy
\cite{MenkovskiPortegiesRavelonanosy2024HeatKernelEntropy} treat the
relative entropy between a heat kernel and normalized Riemannian volume as the
heat time tends to zero; the coefficients are universal local geometric
invariants of the underlying manifold.  By contrast, here \(t\to\infty\), the
underlying space is Euclidean, the reference is the translated harmonic
Poisson/Cauchy kernel \(P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\), and the coefficients depend
on the fixed input law only through its centred moments and the Cayley Laurent
constant-term rule.  Process and discretization entropy asymptotics, such as
Fageot--Fallah--Horel
\cite{FageotFallahHorel2022EntropicCompressibilityLevy} and
Ghourchian--Amini--Gohari
\cite{GhourchianAminiGohari2018InnovationCompressibility}, concern
compressibility or innovation limits rather than a single fixed law smoothed
by a harmonic semigroup.  General representation formulas, including the
Gibbs--Donsker--Varadhan variational formula
\cite{DonskerVaradhan1975AsymptoticI,DupuisEllis1997WeakConvergence} and
semigroup de Bruijn identities such as
\cite{DeBruijn1953,HirataNemotoYoshida2012RelativeEntropy,
Yoshida2017RelativeEntropyDiffusion}, identify entropy through optimization
or dissipation.  They are structural identities, not coefficient evaluations
for the matched large-\(t\) functional in
\eqref{eq:intro-main-functional}.

\section{Fourth-moment coefficient-calculus example}%
\label{app:optional-fourth-moment-coefficient-algebra}
\label{app:fourth-moment-coupling-proof}
\label{subsec:coefficient-defect-coupling}
\label{subsec:fourth-moment-stability}

The fourth-moment material is not used in the three main theorems.  Its role
is only to illustrate how the sixth-order coefficient \(A_6\) can be read as
a simple algebraic defect after normalizing to unit variance.  The relevant
identity is
\[
\Delta_6(\nu):=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-A_6(\nu)
=\sigma^6\left(
\frac{\beta_3^2}{32}
+\frac{\EE(X^2-1-\beta_3X)^2}{8}
\right),
\]
where \(X=(\gamma-\bar\gamma)/\sigma\) and \(\beta_3=\EE X^3\).  Thus
\(\Delta_6(\nu)\ge0\), with equality exactly for the symmetric two-point
centred law.  The elementary sign-coupling proof gives the non-sharp bound
\[
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/2}.
\]
This is an illustrative fourth-moment algebra check, not an additional
finite-time stability theorem and not an input to Theorems~\ref{thm:main-A},
\ref{thm:main-B}, or~\ref{thm:main-C}.  The full normalized algebra and
coupling details have been moved to the paper-local supplementary
coefficient note.

\section{Weighted trigonometric normalization}%
\label{app:entropy-integrals}

This appendix records the change of variables behind the Laurent coefficient
normalization.  Set \(y=\tan\theta\),
\(-\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2\).  Then
\[
  \frac{\dd y}{\pi(1+y^2)}=\frac{\dd\theta}{\pi}.
\]
With \(z=e^{2\ii\theta}\),
\[
  \cos(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k+z^{-k}}2,\qquad
  \sin(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k-z^{-k}}{2\ii}.
\]
Thus the Cauchy mode integrals in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} are exactly Haar
constant terms:
\[
  \int_{\RR}\prod_j u_{n_j}(y)\,\omega(\dd y)
  =[z^0]\prod_j Q_{n_j}(z).
\]
For order eight, Appendix~\ref{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate} records
the row certificate as a finite coefficient-calculus check.

\section{Optional order-eight Laurent coefficient certificate}%
\label{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate}

The order-eight Laurent arithmetic is useful as an audit trail for the
coefficient calculation, but it is not an additional theorem of the paper.
The single convention is
\[
q_{n,k}=2^{-n}(-\ii)^n
\operatorname{sgn}(k)^{\,n\bmod2}
\binom{n-1}{|k|-1},
\qquad 0<|k|\le n,
\]
and
\[
J(n_1,\ldots,n_r)=[z^0]\prod_{j=1}^rQ_{n_j}(z).
\]
The rows entering the displayed \(A_8\) polynomial are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\mathbf n&J(\mathbf n)\\
\hline
(2,6)&3/64\\
(3,5)&-15/128\\
(4,4)&5/32\\
(2,2,4)&3/32\\
(2,3,3)&-9/128\\
(2,2,2,2)&9/64 .
\end{array}
\]
The sign-sensitive rows \((3,5)\) and \((2,3,3)\) use the same all-sign
coefficient rule as the other rows; no conjugation-as-inversion convention is
introduced.  Substitution of these six constants into the universal
coefficient formula gives
\[
A_8=
\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
This appendix is therefore a compact reproducibility certificate for the
finite Laurent rows only.  The complete row recurrence, root-of-unity
checks, and worked sign-sensitive examples have been moved to the
paper-local supplementary coefficient note.
