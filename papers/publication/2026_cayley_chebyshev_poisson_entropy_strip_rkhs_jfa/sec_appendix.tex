\section{Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness}%
\label{app:haar-pullback-uniqueness}

The following elementary statement fixes the chart normalization behind
\eqref{eq:haar-pullback}.  The entropy expansion uses only the pullback
identity itself; the uniqueness assertion is included here to remove any
ambiguity about the endpoint convention.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness]%
\label{prop:haar-pullback-uniqueness}
Let $\varphi:\overline\RR\to\TT$ be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
such that $\varphi|_{\RR}$ is of class $C^{1}$,
$\varphi(0)=1$, and $\varphi(\infty)=-1$.  Then the following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\varphi^{*}m_{\TT}=\omega$ on~$\RR$;
\item if $\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}$ with
      $\alpha:\RR\to(-\pi,\pi)$ increasing, then
      $\alpha'(x)=2/(1+x^{2})$ for every $x\in\RR$;
\item $\varphi=\iota$.
\end{enumerate}
If the orientation assumption is removed, the same conclusion holds up to
complex conjugation: $\varphi=\iota$ or $\varphi=\overline{\iota}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any orientation-preserving $\varphi$ satisfying the endpoint
normalization, there is a unique increasing $C^{1}$ lift
$\alpha:\RR\to(-\pi,\pi)$ with $\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}$ and
$\alpha(0)=0$.  Pullback of normalized arc length is
\[
\varphi^{*}m_{\TT}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\alpha'(x)\dd x .
\]
Hence $\varphi^{*}m_{\TT}=\omega$ is equivalent to
\(\alpha'(x)=2/(1+x^{2})\).  Integrating from \(0\) to \(x\) gives
\(\alpha(x)=2\arctan x\), hence \(\varphi=\iota\).  Conversely, for
\(\iota\), Proposition~\ref{prop:phase-derivative} gives
\[
\iota^{*}m_{\TT}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\vartheta'(x)\dd x
=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x
=\omega(\dd x).
\]
If orientation is reversed, the lift is decreasing and the same argument
applied to \(\overline{\varphi}\) gives the stated conjugate alternative.
\end{proof}

\section{Related work and theorem map}%
\label{app:related-work-scope}

The introduction contains the single theorem-by-theorem comparison table.  This
appendix records the broader citation clusters behind that table and the scope
boundary used throughout the paper.  The boundary is not a journal boundary:
the closest analytic comparators include the older and non-JFA theory of
finite-moment large-time profile expansions for heat, fractional heat, and
stable kernels.

Fixed-law Poisson entropy jets expand one scalar matched KL functional as the
Poisson scale grows.  Normalized-sum entropy theorems change the law with the
number of summands; flow asymptotics describe large-time profiles or evolution
limits; transport and functional-inequality stability compare near extremizers
or transport structures; and parametric Cauchy divergence formulae give closed
forms within Cauchy families.  In the present problem \(P_t*\nu\) is generally
not Cauchy, and \(\nu\) is not rescaled as a summand law.

The distinction from heat and fractional-heat profile expansions is more
specific.  The papers of Duoandikoetxea--Zuazua, Escobedo--Zuazua,
Karch--Schonbek, and Ishige--Kawakami--Michihisa
\cite{DuoandikoetxeaZuazua1992,EscobedoZuazua1991,KarchSchonbek2001,
IshigeKawakami2012,IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2017,
IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2022RefinedFractional} give finite-moment asymptotic
profiles for heat, viscous, fractional, or stable-kernel evolutions.  In that
lineage the coefficients multiply derivatives of the relevant kernel or
profile and describe the evolved density or solution.  The density-level
analogue in this paper is the quotient estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-profile-expansion-comparison}
u(t,x)=
\sum_{|\alpha|\le K}M_\alpha(\varphi)\,\partial^\alpha p_t(x)
+R_K(t,x),
\qquad
\frac{P_t*\nu}{P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)}-1
=\sum_{j=2}^{L}D_j(y)t^{-j}+\rho_L(t,y).
\end{equation}
The article isolates this quotient layer before the entropy theorem.  The main coefficients
\(A_{2m}(\nu)\), however, are obtained only after inserting that quotient into
the nonlinear Bregman integrand
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-kl-transfer-comparison}
D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=
\int
\Phi\!\left(\sum_{j=2}^{L}D_j(y)t^{-j}+\rho_L(t,y)\right)\dd\Omega(y),
\qquad
\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s,
\end{equation}
and retaining all products of modes of total order \(2m\).  Thus the final
coefficients are nonlinear KL coefficients, not density-profile coefficients.
This is why the coefficient-evaluation threshold in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} is stated for the displayed universal
moment-polynomial coefficient of the scalar entropy functional.

The distinction from formal Taylor extraction is also theorem-level.  Expanding
the quotient \(R_\varepsilon\) or its multivariable analogue gives candidate
modes only.  The entropy theorems prove that those modes may be averaged under
finite moments, that the quotient remainder is small in the weighted spaces
needed for KL, that centering removes the odd obstruction, and that the
Bregman Taylor expansion of \((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) has an integrable tail with
the stated \(o(t^{-2N})\) or finite-time remainder.  The explicit Laurent
calculus in dimension one is therefore the coefficient-evaluation layer of an
analytic finite-moment Poisson theorem, not the theorem by itself.

Entropic CLT and stable-law entropy asymptotics include
\cite{Barron1986,CarlenSoffer1991,BallBartheNaor2003,ArtsteinBallBartheNaor2004,JohnsonBarron2004,MadimanBarron2007,BobkovChistyakovGotze2013,BobkovChistyakovGotzeFisher2014,BobkovChistyakovGotzeEdgeworth2013,BobkovChistyakovGotzeBerryEsseen2014,BobkovGotzeEsscher2025}.

Here Chen--Lu--Tang's HLS stability paper is used as a non-JFA background

The Hardy--Stein and Bregman sources directly relevant to
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy} are
\cite{Stein1970SingularIntegrals,Bregman1967,

\section{Weighted trigonometric integrals}\label{app:entropy-integrals}

We collect the weighted identities used in the Laurent representatives and
constant-term table of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:n4-normalized-laurent-representatives} and
\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.  With
\(y=\tan\theta\), each identity is a zero-Fourier-mode extraction in an
elementary trigonometric product.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted Cayley mode integrals]\label{lem:entropy-integrals}
Let $u_2,\dots,u_6$ be the mode functions defined by
\eqref{eq:cayley-first-modes} and \eqref{eq:cayley-next-modes}.  Then
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac14,
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_3(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{3}{16},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_4(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac18,
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^3}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac{3}{32},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_6(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{3}{64},
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_3(y)u_5(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac{15}{128},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_4(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{5}{32},
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^2u_4(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{3}{32},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_3(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac{9}{128},
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^4}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{9}{64}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $y=\tan\theta$ with $\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$, so that
$\dd y/(1+y^2)=\dd\theta$, and write
\[
c_k(\theta):=\cos(2k\theta),
\qquad
s_k(\theta):=\sin(2k\theta).
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode},
\[
u_2(\tan\theta)=-\frac12(c_1+c_2),
\qquad
u_3(\tan\theta)=-\frac14(s_1+2s_2+s_3),
\]
\[
u_4(\tan\theta)=\frac18(c_1+3c_2+3c_3+c_4),
\qquad
u_5(\tan\theta)=\frac1{16}(s_1+4s_2+6s_3+4s_4+s_5),
\]
\[
u_6(\tan\theta)=-\frac1{32}(c_1+5c_2+10c_3+10c_4+5c_5+c_6).
\]
On $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ one has
\[
\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} c_k(\theta)^2\,\dd\theta
=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} s_k(\theta)^2\,\dd\theta
=\frac{\pi}{2}
\qquad(k\ge 1),
\]
and mixed products with different parity or different frequency
integrate to zero.  Thus each identity follows by expanding the
corresponding trigonometric polynomial and retaining only the constant
term.  Equivalently, if $Q_n(e^{2\ii\theta})=u_n(\tan\theta)$, then each
integral is the zero Laurent coefficient $[z^0]$ of the indicated product;
the coefficient-level constant terms are listed in
Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.
For instance,
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y
=\frac14\cdot\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}(c_1+c_2)^2\,\dd\theta
=\frac14,
\]
and
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_4(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y
=-\frac1{16}\cdot\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}
(c_1+c_2)(c_1+3c_2+3c_3+c_4)\,\dd\theta
=-\frac18.
\]
The remaining identities are accounted for by the same zero-Fourier-coefficient
computation.
\end{proof}

The coefficient-level use of these identities is consolidated in
Lemmas~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic} and
\ref{lem:n4-entropy-coefficient-substitution}; the computations above give
the elementary trigonometric verification of the weighted mode integrals.  The
finite Laurent certificate in
Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic} is intentionally
self-contained: recurrence
\eqref{eq:eighth-order-constant-term-recurrence} reconstructs each zero-sum
convolution row from the displayed all-sign coefficient vectors.  In
particular, the sign-sensitive rows \(I(3,5)=-15/128\) and
\(I(2,3,3)=-9/128\) are part of the displayed finite data and require no
external certificate.

\section{KL Doob quotient bounds}\label{app:doob-bregman-domain}

The general Sobolev--Bregman/Hardy--Stein identity is cited in
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}; it is not reproved here.  This appendix
records only the quotient-domain estimates for the Poisson comparison
quotient \(u_t=h_t/g_t\) that are used to apply that identity in the KL/Cauchy
case.

\begin{lemma}[KL quotient range and Bregman majorant]%
\label{lem:appendix-kl-quotient-domain-bounds}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\) and
finite centred second moment
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
Fix \(0<a<b<\infty\), and put
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t .
\]
Then there are constants \(0<m_{a,b}\le M_{a,b}<\infty\) and
\(L_{a,b}<\infty\) such that, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x\in\RR\),
\[
0<m_{a,b}\le u_t(x)\le M_{a,b}<\infty,
\qquad
|\partial_xu_t(x)|\le L_{a,b}.
\]
For
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=
\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta
\qquad(\alpha,\beta>0),
\]
there is \(C_{a,b,\nu}<\infty\) such that, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and
\(x\ne y\),
\[
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le
C_{a,b,\nu}g_t(x)\min\{1,|x-y|^{-2}\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma\), \(x=\bar\gamma+tz\), and
\[
R_\varepsilon(z):=\frac{1+z^2}{1+(z-\varepsilon)^2}.
\]
Then
\[
u_t(x)=
\int_{\RR}R_{X_c/t}\!\left(\frac{x-\bar\gamma}{t}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
The elementary inequalities
\[
R_\varepsilon(z)\le2(1+\varepsilon^2),
\qquad
R_\varepsilon(z)\ge\frac{1}{2(1+\varepsilon^2)}
\]
give
\[
\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)}
\le u_t(x)\le
2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2}.
\]
Thus one may take
\[
m_{a,b}:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},
\qquad
M_{a,b}:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2}.
\]
Differentiating \(R_\varepsilon\) gives
\[
\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)
=
\frac{2\varepsilon(1-w^2)-2\varepsilon^2w}{(1+w^2)^2},
\qquad w=z-\varepsilon,
\]
and hence \(|\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)|\le3(1+\varepsilon^2)\).  Since
\(\partial_x=t^{-1}\partial_z\),
\[
|\partial_xu_t(x)|
\le
\frac{3}{a}\left(1+\frac{\mu_2}{a^2}\right)
=:L_{a,b}.
\]

On the compact interval \([m_{a,b},M_{a,b}]\), the second derivative of
\(s\log s\) is bounded by \(m_{a,b}^{-1}\).  Therefore
\[
0\le\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)
\le \frac{1}{2m_{a,b}}|\alpha-\beta|^2
\qquad
(m_{a,b}\le\alpha,\beta\le M_{a,b}).
\]
Combining this with
\[
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L_{a,b}|x-y|,
\qquad
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le M_{a,b}-m_{a,b},
\]
yields
\[
\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le C'_{a,b,\nu}\min\{|x-y|^2,1\}.
\]
Dividing by \((x-y)^2\) and multiplying by \(g_t(x)\) gives the stated
majorant.
\end{proof}

\section{Certificate constants for finite-time sixth-defect bookkeeping}
\label{app:finite-time-defect-estimates}

Subsection~\ref{subsec:finite-time-bookkeeping} uses one ordered certificate
interface.  On a controlled shell
\[
\mu_2=\sigma^2,\qquad \EE|X_c|^7\le M,\qquad M\ge\sigma^7,
\]
write \(\widehat M=M/\sigma^7\ge1\) and first fix
\[
C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}=\sigma^8C(\widehat M),\qquad
B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}=\sigma^9B(\widehat M),\qquad
T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}=\sigma T(\widehat M).
\]
These constants, defined in
Definition~\ref{def:n4-finite-time-constant-dictionary}, supply bounds for
\(A_8\) and the order-nine remainder before a measured KL value is used.  Only
after this certificate has been fixed does one form the finite-time statistic
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-time-sixth-deficit}
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)
:=
t^6\left(
\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
-\frac{7\sigma^6}{64t^6}
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
\right).
\end{equation}
Here \(h_t=P_t*\nu\) and \(g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma)\).  The only identity used
after the statistic is formed is
\[
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)=\Delta_6(\nu)-A_8(\nu)t^{-2}-t^6E(t).
\]
The controlled-shell estimates
\eqref{eq:main-finite-time-defect-estimate}--\eqref{eq:main-finite-time-w2-stability}
are obtained by inserting the preassigned certificate bounds into this
identity.

The exact fixed-law constants \(B_{\nu,8},T_{\nu,8}\), normalized shell
functions \(C(u),B(u),T(u)\), and dimensional shell constants
\(C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\) are the entries
of Definition~\ref{def:n4-finite-time-constant-dictionary}, placed directly
before Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}.  In particular,
for \(\widehat M=M/\sigma^7\), the dimensional constants are exactly the
variance-one normalized constants with the powers of \(\sigma\) restored as
in \eqref{eq:dictionary-controlled-shell-c8}.

The constant regimes are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}, leftmargin=*]
\item The coefficient-level \(W_2\) constants \(4\) and \(8\) in
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} are universal algebraic
constants.  They do not use a finite-time entropy remainder.
\item The fixed-law conditional bookkeeping remark uses supplied bounds
\(C,B,T\).  If these data are supplied by
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}, then \(C\) is any
finite bound with \(C\ge |A_8(\nu)|\), while \(B=B_{\nu,8}\) and
\(T=T_{\nu,8}\); these may depend on the particular law.
\item The controlled-shell specialization uses the dictionary functions
\(C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\).  These are the
scale restores of normalized constants depending only on
\(\widehat M=M/\sigma^7\), together with the universal \(N=4\) envelopes from
Lemma~\ref{lem:closed-cayley-mode-constant-envelopes}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{corollary}[Constant dependence for moment-defect \(W_2\) estimates]%
\label{cor:defect-stability-constant-regimes}
The coefficient-level constants in
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} are universal apart from the
explicit variance powers forced by scaling against
\(\rho_\sigma=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma)\).  The finite-time
constants are separate inputs.  The controlled-shell certificate lemma uses
the constants defined for
Theorem~\ref{lem:controlled-shell-conditional-certificate};
the fixed-law alternative in
Remark~\ref{rem:fixed-law-finite-time-certificate} requires externally
supplied certificate bounds \(C,B,T\).  The measured value
\(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\) supplies only
\(\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This restates the hypotheses and conclusions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling},
Theorem~\ref{lem:controlled-shell-conditional-certificate}, and
Remark~\ref{rem:fixed-law-finite-time-certificate}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Appendix check of the controlled-shell certificate constants]%
\label{cor:uniform-finite-time-defect-stability}
Fix \(\sigma>0\) and \(M\ge\sigma^7\), and use the explicit constants
\(B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\), \(T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\), and
\(C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\) defined for
Theorem~\ref{lem:controlled-shell-conditional-certificate}.  These constants are
the only shell certificate inputs needed in the lemma.  Consequently, for
every law in the controlled shell \(\mu_2=\sigma^2\),
\(\EE|X_c|^7\le M\), and every \(t\ge T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\) after the KL value
has been inserted into \(\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-finite-time-defect-shell}
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\mathcal A_t^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\mathcal A_t^{1/2},
\end{equation}
where
\[
\mathcal A_t:=
\bigl|\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\bigr|
+C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}t^{-2}+B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}t^{-3}.
\]
Outside such a shell, supplied fixed-law certificate bounds \(C,B,T\) in
Remark~\ref{rem:fixed-law-finite-time-certificate} are the asserted level of
generality.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is exactly \eqref{eq:main-finite-time-w2-stability}.  The shell
control is proved inside
Theorem~\ref{lem:controlled-shell-conditional-certificate} by applying the
seventh-moment remainder theorem to the variance-one law
\((\gamma-\bar\gamma)/\sigma\) and restoring the factors of \(\sigma\).
\end{proof}
