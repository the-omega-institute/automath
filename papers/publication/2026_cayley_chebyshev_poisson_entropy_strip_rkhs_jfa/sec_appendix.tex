\section{Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness}%
\label{app:haar-pullback-uniqueness}

The entropy coefficient calculation uses only the pullback identity
\(\iota^*m_{\TT}=\omega\).  The following elementary normalization removes
the only possible ambiguity in the chosen Cayley chart.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness]%
\label{prop:haar-pullback-uniqueness}
Let \(\varphi:\overline\RR\to\TT\) be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
such that \(\varphi|_{\RR}\) is \(C^1\), \(\varphi(0)=1\), and
\(\varphi(\infty)=-1\).  Then \(\varphi^*m_{\TT}=\omega\) if and only if
\(\varphi=\iota\).  Without the orientation assumption, the only additional
possibility is complex conjugation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}\), where
\(\alpha:\RR\to(-\pi,\pi)\) is increasing and \(\alpha(0)=0\).  Pullback of
normalized arc length is \((2\pi)^{-1}\alpha'(x)\dd x\).  Therefore
\(\varphi^*m_{\TT}=\omega\) is equivalent to
\(\alpha'(x)=2/(1+x^2)\).  Integration gives
\(\alpha(x)=2\arctan x\), hence \(\varphi=\iota\).  The reversed-orientation
case follows by applying the same argument to \(\overline\varphi\).
\end{proof}

\section{Related work boundary note}%
\label{app:related-work-scope}

The theorem-level comparison in the introduction uses
\[
  D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)).
\]
None of the recent JFA papers cited here expands the fixed-input quotient
\(D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))\).  Bobkov--G{\"o}tze treat
Esscher transforms and R\'enyi-\(\infty\) convergence for normalized sums;
Anker--Papageorgiou--Zhang treat heat profiles; and
Bogdan--Gutowski--Pietruska-Pa{\l}uba provide the nearest
Hardy--Stein/Bregman identity framework.  The new contribution here is the
Cayley coefficient extraction for a fixed law and the finite-variance iff
threshold for the matched Cauchy quotient.

The direct baselines cited in the introduction are Cauchy information
formulae, heat/fractional-diffusion and Poisson-kernel moment expansions,
fractional-Laplacian calculus, and regular-variation tail calculus.  The
fixed-law matched Poisson KL functional and its radial multidimensional
analogue are the only entropy objects expanded here; no normalized-sum CLT,
changing-law limit, general slowly varying tail-replacement theorem,
finite-covariance-only multidimensional KL asymptotic, optimizer-stability
theorem, or single-value finite-time certification theorem is claimed.  The
finite-moment hierarchy evaluates the displayed universal polynomials only
from the moments present in its hypotheses, while the explicit power-tail and
conditional post-processing results use their own stated quotient,
remainder, threshold, and error assumptions.

\paragraph{Adjacent literature.}
Transport and stability papers enter only as scope markers.  Poisson
transport maps, entropy/Fisher--Stein--Wasserstein inequalities, unbalanced
optimal transport, and specific entropy of point processes
\cite{LopezRiveraShenfeld2025PoissonTransport,
ChengThalmaierWang2023EntropySteinTransport,
GallouetGhezziVialard2025UnbalancedOT,
ErbarHuesmannJalowyMuller2025StationaryPointTransport}
study geometric or transport structures not constructed here.  Logarithmic
Sobolev, Moser--Onofri, log-HLS, and Boltzmann stability results
\cite{BrigatiDolbeaultSimonov2024LogSobolev,
ChenLuTang2023LogSobolevStability,Carlen2025LogHLS,
CarlenCarvalho1992}
study quantitative deficits around optimizer or equilibrium classes.  The
present manuscript uses none of those hypotheses; its stability-looking
statements are coefficient identities for the matched Poisson KL expansion,
with the single \(W_2\) corollary derived from finite moment algebra.

\section{Weighted trigonometric normalization}%
\label{app:entropy-integrals}

This appendix records the change of variables behind the Laurent coefficient
normalization.  Set \(y=\tan\theta\),
\(-\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2\).  Then
\[
  \frac{\dd y}{\pi(1+y^2)}=\frac{\dd\theta}{\pi}.
\]
With \(z=e^{2\ii\theta}\),
\[
  \cos(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k+z^{-k}}2,\qquad
  \sin(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k-z^{-k}}{2\ii}.
\]
Thus the Cauchy mode integrals in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} are exactly Haar
constant terms:
\[
  \int_{\RR}\prod_j u_{n_j}(y)\,\omega(\dd y)
  =[z^0]\prod_j Q_{n_j}(z).
\]
The finite order-eight arithmetic used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} is the table in
Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.

\subsection{Optional differentiated-remainder consequence}
\label{app:conditional-post-processing}

This final subsection is optional bookkeeping.  It is not used in the proof of
the coefficient theorems.  The finite-moment quotient theorem gives
\(\rho_{L,t}=o(t^{-L})\) in \(L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)\), but it does not give
a corresponding estimate for \(t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\).  The conclusion below
therefore applies only after that differentiated remainder has been supplied
by an additional regularity or tail argument.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional differentiated-remainder a.e. dissipation]%
\label{lem:differentiated-quotient-remainder-ae-dissipation}
Let \(N\ge2\), put \(L=2N-2\), and set
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),\qquad
\delta_t(y):=\frac{h_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}{g_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}-1 .
\]
Assume that the KL tail identity
\eqref{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation} holds for the same
quotient path.  Assume also that
\[
\delta_t(y)=d_t(y)+\rho_{L,t}(y),
\qquad
d_t(y)=\sum_{n=2}^{L}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n},
\]
and that the undifferentiated and differentiated remainders satisfy, for
a.e. sufficiently large \(t\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:differentiated-quotient-remainder-bound}
\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^\infty}
+\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
=o(t^{-L}),
\qquad
\|t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^\infty}
+\|t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
=o(t^{-L}).
\end{equation}
Then, for a.e. \(t\to\infty\), the dissipation has the asymptotic expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ae-dissipation-asymptotic}
I_\nu(t)=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}2m\,A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-(2m+1)}
+o(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\).  For a.e. sufficiently large \(t\),
\(\|\delta_t\|_\infty+\|d_t\|_\infty=O(t^{-2})\), and
\[
H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\int_{\RR}\Phi(\delta_t(y))\,\omega(\dd y).
\]
The \(L^1\)-chain rule of Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-kl-l1-ac-chain-rule} gives
\[
H'(t)=\int_{\RR}\log(1+\delta_t(y))\,\partial_t\delta_t(y)\,\omega(\dd y)
\]
on the intersection of the corresponding full-measure set with the set where
\eqref{eq:differentiated-quotient-remainder-bound} holds.  Since
\(\partial_td_t=\sum_{n=2}^{L}(-n)\mu_nu_nt^{-(n+1)}\), one has
\(\|d_t\|_\infty=O(t^{-2})\) and
\(\|\partial_td_t\|_\infty=O(t^{-3})\).  The Lipschitz bound for
\(\log(1+s)\) on \(|s|\le1/2\), together with
\eqref{eq:differentiated-quotient-remainder-bound}, gives
\[
\int \log(1+\delta_t)\partial_t\delta_t\,\dd\omega
=
\int \log(1+d_t)\partial_td_t\,\dd\omega
+o(t^{-(L+3)}).
\]
Here \(L+3=2N+1\).  The finite-mode term is differentiated directly: the
Taylor series for \(\Phi\), the bound \(d_t=O(t^{-2})\), and the parity
cancellation in Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} give
\[
\int \log(1+d_t)\partial_td_t\,\dd\omega
=
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}
\int_{\RR}\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}d_t(y)^r\,\omega(\dd y)
+o(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\]
By the definition of the universal coefficients,
\[
\int_{\RR}\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}d_t(y)^r\,\omega(\dd y)
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
+O(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\]
Hence
\[
H'(t)=
-\sum_{m=2}^{N}2m\,A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-(2m+1)}
+o(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\]
Finally \(H'(t)=-I_\nu(t)\) a.e. by
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail}.  Removing the union of the relevant
null sets proves \eqref{eq:ae-dissipation-asymptotic}.  No continuity of
\(I_\nu\) or of \(t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\) is used or concluded.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[When the a.e. conclusion becomes pointwise]
The lemma deliberately stops at an a.e.-in-time conclusion.  A pointwise
large-time statement requires extra regularity turning the differentiated
calculation into an identity between continuous representatives.  For example,
it is enough to assume that
\[
t\mapsto \rho_{L,t},\qquad t\mapsto t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}
\]
are continuous as maps into
\(L^\infty(\omega)\cap L^1(\omega)\), and that on each compact
multiplicative window \([T,2T]\) the differentiated remainders are dominated
by an \(L^\infty(\omega)\cap L^1(\omega)\) majorant of order \(T^{-L}\)
compatible with \eqref{eq:differentiated-quotient-remainder-bound}.  Then
dominated convergence gives continuity of \(I_\nu(t)\) and of the finite-mode
derivative expression, so the a.e. identity extends to all sufficiently large
\(t\).  These assumptions are not consequences of the finite-moment quotient
theorem.
\end{remark}
