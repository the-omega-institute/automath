\section{Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness}%
\label{app:haar-pullback-uniqueness}

The entropy coefficient calculation uses only the pullback identity
\(\iota^*m_{\TT}=\omega\).  The following elementary normalization removes
the only possible ambiguity in the chosen Cayley chart.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness]%
\label{prop:haar-pullback-uniqueness}
Let \(\varphi:\overline\RR\to\TT\) be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
such that \(\varphi|_{\RR}\) is \(C^1\), \(\varphi(0)=1\), and
\(\varphi(\infty)=-1\).  Then \(\varphi^*m_{\TT}=\omega\) if and only if
\(\varphi=\iota\).  Without the orientation assumption, the only additional
possibility is complex conjugation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}\), where
\(\alpha:\RR\to(-\pi,\pi)\) is increasing and \(\alpha(0)=0\).  Pullback of
normalized arc length is \((2\pi)^{-1}\alpha'(x)\dd x\).  Therefore
\(\varphi^*m_{\TT}=\omega\) is equivalent to
\(\alpha'(x)=2/(1+x^2)\).  Integration gives
\(\alpha(x)=2\arctan x\), hence \(\varphi=\iota\).  The reversed-orientation
case follows by applying the same argument to \(\overline\varphi\).
\end{proof}

\section{Related-work comparison axes}%
\label{app:related-work-scope}

This appendix records the comparison axes used in the introduction without
restating the main theorems.  The central present functional in the
coefficient rows is the fixed-law matched Poisson quotient
\[
  D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)).
\]

\subsection*{Comparison axes}

\noindent\textbf{Esscher-information CLT comparison.}
Bobkov--G{\"o}tze
\cite{BobkovGotzeEsscher2025} study \(D_\infty\) and Esscher information for
Esscher transforms of normalized sums under CLT-type normalization and
moment/tail hypotheses.  The output is information convergence to Gaussian
profiles.  The present coefficient theorems keep one law fixed, vary only the
Poisson scale, identify the coefficients \(A_{2m}\), prove the finite-variance
threshold \(A_4=\mu_2^2/8\), and give top-mode replacements only for the
displayed tail classes.

\noindent\textbf{Heat-profile comparison.}
Anker--Papageorgiou--Zhang
\cite{AnkerPapageorgiouZhang2023HeatSymmetricSpaces} treat linear heat
profiles for the heat equation on \(G/K\), under the integrability, symmetry,
and geometric assumptions of the symmetric-space problem.  The output is a
large-time linear profile expansion.  The multidimensional Poisson results
here concern a nonlinear KL functional for one fixed law on \(\RR^d\), use
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\) or the finite-moment condition implying it, and
output tensor coefficients together with the isotropic/traceless covariance
contraction.

\noindent\textbf{Bregman-energy comparison.}
Bogdan--Gutowski--Pietruska-Pa{\l}uba
\cite{BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein} prove a
polarized Hardy--Stein identity for the relevant semigroup or Dirichlet-form
data under Markov-semigroup and convexity hypotheses.  The Cauchy result here
uses the same two-point Bregman algebra but proves the moving quotient-domain
bounds for \(u_t=(P_t*\nu)/P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\) directly, then obtains
\(D_{\rm KL}=\int_t^\infty\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s\).

\noindent\textbf{Stability and transport comparison.}
Recent logarithmic-Sobolev, Moser--Onofri, log-HLS, entropy--Stein, Poisson
transport, point-process transport, stationary entropy-geometry, and
unbalanced-transport papers
\cite{BrigatiDolbeaultSimonov2024LogSobolev,Carlen2025LogHLS,
ChenLuTang2023LogSobolevStability,
ChengThalmaierWang2023EntropySteinTransport,
LopezRiveraShenfeld2025PoissonTransport,
GozlanHerryPeccati2021PointMeasureTransport,
ErbarHuesmannJalowyMuller2025StationaryPointTransport,
GallouetGhezziVialard2025UnbalancedOT}
study optimizer stability, transport maps, point-process couplings, or
transport geometries under their own hypotheses.  In the present paper
\(W_2\) appears only in Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} as a
non-sharp finite-moment coupling consequence of the coefficient defect; no
transport theorem is used to prove the entropy asymptotics.

\section{Weighted trigonometric normalization}%
\label{app:entropy-integrals}

This appendix records the change of variables behind the Laurent coefficient
normalization.  Set \(y=\tan\theta\),
\(-\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2\).  Then
\[
  \frac{\dd y}{\pi(1+y^2)}=\frac{\dd\theta}{\pi}.
\]
With \(z=e^{2\ii\theta}\),
\[
  \cos(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k+z^{-k}}2,\qquad
  \sin(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k-z^{-k}}{2\ii}.
\]
Thus the Cauchy mode integrals in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} are exactly Haar
constant terms:
\[
  \int_{\RR}\prod_j u_{n_j}(y)\,\omega(\dd y)
  =[z^0]\prod_j Q_{n_j}(z).
\]
For order eight, Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic} gives
the recurrence; Appendix~\ref{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate} gives the
row certificate.

\section{Order-eight Laurent certificate}%
\label{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate}

This appendix gives the finite certificate behind
Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.  For
\(\mathbf n=(n_1,\ldots,n_r)\), put
\[
\mathcal K(\mathbf n)
:=\{(k_1,\ldots,k_r):0<|k_j|\le n_j,\ k_1+\cdots+k_r=0\}.
\]
With \(Q_n(z)=\sum_kq_{n,k}z^k\) and the uniform coefficients
\eqref{eq:mode-laurent-coefficients}, put
\[
\lambda_n:=2^{-n}(-\ii)^n .
\]
For each row set
\[
S(\mathbf n):=
\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathcal K(\mathbf n)}
\prod_{j=1}^r
\operatorname{sgn}(k_j)^{\,n_j\bmod2}
\binom{n_j-1}{|k_j|-1},
\qquad
\Lambda(\mathbf n):=\prod_{j=1}^r\lambda_{n_j}.
\]
Then the all-sign coefficient convention gives
\[
I(\mathbf n)=[z^0]\prod_{j=1}^rQ_{n_j}(z)
=\Lambda(\mathbf n)S(\mathbf n).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Deterministic exact Laurent recurrence]%
\label{lem:appendix-d-deterministic-laurent-recurrence}
Let \(\mathbf n=(n_1,\ldots,n_r)\) be any finite row of positive integers.
Define finitely supported maps \(C_j:\ZZ\to\QQ[\ii]\) by
\[
C_0(s)=\mathbf 1_{\{s=0\}},\qquad
C_j(s)=\sum_{0<|k|\le n_j}
2^{-n_j}(-\ii)^{n_j}\operatorname{sgn}(k)^{\,n_j\bmod2}
\binom{n_j-1}{|k|-1}\,C_{j-1}(s-k).
\]
Then
\[
  C_r(0)=[z^0]\prod_{j=1}^r Q_{n_j}(z)=I(\mathbf n).
\]
In particular \(C_r(0)\in\QQ\).  More explicitly,
\[
C_r(0)=
\prod_{j=1}^r\lambda_{n_j}
\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathcal K(\mathbf n)}
\prod_{j=1}^r
\operatorname{sgn}(k_j)^{\,n_j\bmod2}
\binom{n_j-1}{|k_j|-1}.
\]
The recurrence is deterministic and terminates after at most
\(\prod_{j=1}^r(2n_j)\) rational Gaussian-integer operations.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient formula \eqref{eq:mode-laurent-coefficients} says that
multiplication by \(Q_{n_j}\) sends a coefficient array \(C_{j-1}\) to the
convolution array displayed above.  Induction on \(j\) gives
\[
  C_j(s)=[z^s]\prod_{a=1}^jQ_{n_a}(z),
\]
and hence \(C_r(0)\) is the desired constant term.  Expanding the last
coefficient and collecting the common factor
\(\prod_j\lambda_{n_j}\) gives the stated zero-sum formula.

The value is rational because the constant term is the Haar integral of the
real-valued product
\(\prod_j u_{n_j}(\tan\theta)\), by
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}.  Equivalently,
when \(\sum_j n_j\) is odd, the integrand is odd and the value is zero; when
\(\sum_j n_j\) is even, \(\prod_j\lambda_{n_j}\) is real and the zero-sum
integer convolution is an integer.
\end{proof}

The paper-local script
\[
\texttt{certificates/laurent\_recurrence\_minicheck.py}
\]
is the exact-rational executable form of
Lemma~\ref{lem:appendix-d-deterministic-laurent-recurrence}.  With no
arguments it replays the order-eight rows below; with arguments such as
\(\texttt{2 6}\) or \(\texttt{2,3,3}\) it returns the exact constant term
for the requested row.

For the mode products entering \(A_4,A_6,A_8\), the finite zero-sum
convolution is:
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r|c|c}
\mathbf n&|\mathcal K(\mathbf n)|&S(\mathbf n)&\Lambda(\mathbf n)&I(\mathbf n)\\
\hline
(2,2)&4&4&1/16&1/4\\
(2,4)&4&8&-1/64&-1/8\\
(3,3)&6&-12&-1/64&3/16\\
(2,2,2)&6&6&-1/64&-3/32\\
(2,6)&4&12&1/256&3/64\\
(3,5)&6&-30&1/256&-15/128\\
(4,4)&8&40&1/256&5/32\\
(2,2,4)&12&24&1/256&3/32\\

(2,2,2,2)&36&36&1/256&9/64 .
\end{array}
\]
