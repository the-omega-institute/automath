Closed formulas for divergences between Cauchy laws also do not imply the
coefficient hierarchy above.  Results such as Nielsen--Okamura
\cite{NielsenOkamura2023Cauchy}, Nielsen
\cite{Nielsen2020CauchyVoronoi}, and Verd{\'u}
\cite{Verdu2023Cauchy} evaluate \(f\)-divergences or information-geometric
quantities when both arguments are members of a finite-dimensional Cauchy
family.  In the present problem the numerator \(P_t*\nu\) is generally a
mixture of Cauchy translates, not a single Cauchy law; only the denominator is
the mean-matched Cauchy kernel.  Even when a law is approximated by a nearby
Cauchy profile, the KL coefficients are not obtained by differentiating the
two-parameter Cauchy divergence formula: after centering, the linear term in
the quotient integrates to zero, while the \(t^{-2m}\) coefficient collects
all products of quotient modes whose total order is \(2m\) through the
nonlinear series for \(\Phi\).  The fixed-law hierarchy is therefore a
moment-polynomial constant-term calculation for a Cauchy mixture quotient,
not an expansion inside the Cauchy statistical manifold.

The Poisson terminology also hides adjacent but different entropy theories.
Poisson-channel papers such as Guo--Shamai--Verd{\'u}
\cite{GuoShamaiVerdu2008PoissonChannel} and Atar--Weissman
\cite{AtarWeissman2012PoissonChannel} study conditional count outputs
\(Y|X\sim{\rm Poisson}(\gamma X)\) or continuous-time counting processes and
relate mutual information, relative entropy, and estimation loss.  These are
count-output models rather than harmonic smoothing by \(P_t\), so they do not
produce the quotient expansion used in Theorems~\ref{thm:main-A}
and~\ref{thm:main-B}.

Small-time heat-kernel entropy expansions have a different limiting regime.
For example, the expansions of
Menkovski--Portegies--Ravelonanosy
\cite{MenkovskiPortegiesRavelonanosy2024HeatKernelEntropy} treat the
relative entropy between a heat kernel and normalized Riemannian volume as the
heat time tends to zero; the coefficients are universal local geometric
invariants of the underlying manifold.  By contrast, here \(t\to\infty\), the
underlying space is Euclidean, the reference is the translated harmonic
Poisson/Cauchy kernel \(P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\), and the coefficients depend
on the fixed input law only through its centred moments and the Cayley Laurent
constant-term rule.  Process and discretization entropy asymptotics, such as
Fageot--Fallah--Horel
\cite{FageotFallahHorel2022EntropicCompressibilityLevy} and
Ghourchian--Amini--Gohari
\cite{GhourchianAminiGohari2018InnovationCompressibility}, concern
compressibility or innovation limits rather than a single fixed law smoothed
by a harmonic semigroup.  General representation formulas, including the
Gibbs--Donsker--Varadhan variational formula
\cite{DonskerVaradhan1975AsymptoticI,DupuisEllis1997WeakConvergence} and
semigroup de Bruijn identities such as
\cite{DeBruijn1953,HirataNemotoYoshida2012RelativeEntropy,
Yoshida2017RelativeEntropyDiffusion}, identify entropy through optimization
or dissipation.  They are structural identities, not coefficient evaluations
for the matched large-\(t\) functional in
\eqref{eq:intro-main-functional}.

\section{Fourth-moment coefficient-calculus example}%
\label{app:optional-fourth-moment-coefficient-algebra}
\label{subsec:coefficient-defect-coupling}
\label{subsec:fourth-moment-stability}

This appendix illustrates the coefficient calculus developed in the main
text.  It records an algebraic consequence of the identified sixth-order
coefficient \(A_6\): after normalizing to unit variance, the difference
between the symmetric two-point value and \(A_6\) is a fourth-moment square
defect, and that square defect controls an elementary \(W_2\) coupling to the
symmetric two-point law.

The statement is first a normalized fourth-moment algebra lemma, independent
of Poisson kernels, entropy, and finite-time KL values.  This keeps the
terminology close to the fourth-moment-theorem literature, for example
Nourdin--Peccati--Swan
\cite{NourdinPeccatiSwan2014EntropyFourthMoment}, while separating the
present calculation from that theory.  After the normalized algebra is
recorded, the identified \(A_6\) coefficient from
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-sixth-coefficient} gives the scaled corollary.
The order-eight coefficient \(A_8\), the seventh-moment remainder, and any
finite-time KL post-processing are outside the statement below.

\begin{lemma}[Normalized fourth-moment defect algebra]%
\label{lem:normalized-fourth-moment-defect-algebra}
Let \(X\) be a real random variable with \(\EE X=0\), \(\EE X^2=1\), and
\(\EE X^4<\infty\).  Put
\[
\beta_3=\EE X^3,\qquad
Q_2=X^2-1-\beta_3X,\qquad
\kappa=\EE Q_2^2,\qquad
\eta=\frac{\beta_3^2}{32}+\frac{\kappa}{8}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-fourth-moment-algebra}
\eta
=\frac{\beta_3^2}{32}+\frac{\EE(X^2-1-\beta_3X)^2}{8}
=\frac{\EE X^4-1}{8}-\frac{3\beta_3^2}{32}.
\end{equation}
In particular \(\eta\ge0\).  Moreover \(\eta=0\) if and only if
\(\mathcal L(X)=\rho_1:=\frac12(\delta_{-1}+\delta_1)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding \(Q_2^2\) and using \(\EE X=0\), \(\EE X^2=1\), and
\(\EE X^3=\beta_3\) gives
\[
\kappa
=\EE[(X^2-1-\beta_3X)^2]
=\EE[(X^2-1)^2]-\beta_3^2
=\EE X^4-1-\beta_3^2.
\]
Substitution gives \eqref{eq:normalized-fourth-moment-algebra}.  The first
displayed form of \(\eta\) proves non-negativity.  If \(\eta=0\), then
\(\beta_3=0\) and \(Q_2=0\) almost surely, so \(X^2=1\) almost surely; since
\(\EE X=0\), the two signs have equal mass.  The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Elementary normalized \(W_2\) coupling]%
\label{lem:normalized-fourth-moment-w2-coupling}
Let \(X\) be a real random variable with \(\EE X=0\), \(\EE X^2=1\), and
\(\EE X^4<\infty\).  Put
\[
\beta_3=\EE X^3,\qquad
Q_2=X^2-1-\beta_3X,\qquad
\kappa=\EE Q_2^2,\qquad
\eta=\frac{\beta_3^2}{32}+\frac{\kappa}{8}.
\]
For \(\rho_1=\frac12(\delta_{-1}+\delta_1)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-sign-rademacher-coupling}
W_2(\mathcal L(X),\rho_1)
\le 4\eta^{1/4}+8\eta^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First record the defect size which is seen by the sign map.  Expanding
\(Q_2^2\) and using \(\EE X=0\), \(\EE X^2=1\), and
\(\EE X^3=\beta_3\) gives
\[
\kappa
=\EE[(X^2-1-\beta_3X)^2]
=\EE[(X^2-1)^2]-\beta_3^2.
\]
Thus, with
\[
M:=\EE[(X^2-1)^2]=\kappa+\beta_3^2,
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sign-defect-size-bound}
M\le 32\eta .
\end{equation}

Let \(S=1\) on \(\{X\ge0\}\) and \(S=-1\) on \(\{X<0\}\), and let
\(r_p=\mathcal L(S)=p\delta_1+(1-p)\delta_{-1}\).  For every real \(x\),
with this convention at \(x=0\),
\[
|x-\operatorname{sgn}x|\le |x^2-1|;
\]
indeed \(|x^2-1|=|x-1|(x+1)\) for \(x\ge0\), and
\(|x^2-1|=|x+1|(1-x)\) for \(x<0\).  Coupling \(X\) and \(S\) on the same
probability space therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:couple-x-to-sign}
W_2(\mathcal L(X),r_p)
\le \bigl(\EE|X-S|^2\bigr)^{1/2}
\le M^{1/2}.
\end{equation}

It remains to couple the biased sign law to the symmetric Rademacher law.
On the two-point set \(\{-1,1\}\) the optimal quadratic transport moves the
excess mass \(|p-\frac12|\) across distance \(2\), hence
\[
W_2(r_p,\rho_1)^2=4\left|p-\frac12\right|.
\]
Since \(\EE X=0\),
\[
2p-1=\EE S=\EE(S-X),
\]
and Cauchy--Schwarz together with \eqref{eq:couple-x-to-sign} gives
\[
\left|p-\frac12\right|
\le \frac12\EE|S-X|
\le \frac12 M^{1/2}.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:couple-biased-sign-to-symmetric}
W_2(r_p,\rho_1)\le \sqrt2\,M^{1/4}.
\end{equation}
The triangle inequality, followed by \eqref{eq:sign-defect-size-bound}, now
yields
\[
W_2(\mathcal L(X),\rho_1)
\le M^{1/2}+\sqrt2\,M^{1/4}
\le \sqrt{32}\,\eta^{1/2}+\sqrt2\,32^{1/4}\eta^{1/4}.
\]
Finally \(\sqrt{32}<8\) and \(\sqrt2\,32^{1/4}=2^{7/4}<4\), proving
\eqref{eq:normalized-sign-rademacher-coupling}.
\end{proof}

We use the following transport notation in the scaled corollary.  The centred
law is
\[
\nu_c:=(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})_\#\nu .
\]
For probability measures \(\mu,\rho\) on \(\RR\) with finite second moments,
\[
W_2^2(\mu,\rho)
:=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\rho)}
\int_{\RR\times\RR}|x-y|^2\,\dd\pi(x,y),
\]
where \(\Pi(\mu,\rho)\) is the set of couplings of \(\mu\) and \(\rho\).  If
\(S_a(x)=ax\), then pushing a coupling forward by \(S_a\times S_a\), and then
applying the same argument to \(a^{-1}\) when \(a\ne0\), gives the dilation
identity
\[
W_2\bigl((S_a)_\#\mu,(S_a)_\#\rho\bigr)=|a|\,W_2(\mu,\rho).
\]
In particular, with \(X=(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})/\sigma\),
\(\nu_c=(S_\sigma)_\#\mathcal L(X)\) and
\(\rho_\sigma=(S_\sigma)_\#\rho_1\).

\begin{corollary}[Scaled sixth-order coefficient consequence]%
\label{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}
Let \(\nu\) have mean \(\bar{\gamma}\), variance \(\sigma^2>0\), and finite
centred fourth absolute moment, and write
\(\mu_k=\EE(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^k\) for \(k=3,4\).  Let
\[
A_6(\nu)=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64}
\]
be the \(t^{-6}\) entropy coefficient identified in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-sixth-coefficient}, and define the scaled defect
from the symmetric two-point coefficient by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-defect-normalized-algebra}
\Delta_6(\nu)
:=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-A_6(\nu).
\end{equation}
For \(X=(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})/\sigma\), \(\beta_3=\EE X^3\), and
\(Q_2=X^2-1-\beta_3X\), the normalized fourth-moment lemma gives the
algebraic identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled-fourth-moment-defect}
\Delta_6(\nu)
=\sigma^6\left(
\frac{\beta_3^2}{32}+\frac{\EE Q_2^2}{8}
\right).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for the centred law \(\nu_c\) and
\(\rho_\sigma=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma)\),
\[
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/2}.
\]
Moreover \(\Delta_6(\nu)\ge0\), and equality holds if and only if
\(\nu_c=\rho_\sigma\).

This is a scaled corollary of the normalized fourth-moment algebra and the
elementary \(W_2\) coupling above.  The constants are non-sharp.  A
finite-time KL stability estimate would require separate coefficient and
remainder information; the corollary itself uses only the fourth moment and
the identified \(A_6\) coefficient.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
With \(X=(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})/\sigma\), \(\beta_3=\EE X^3\),
\(\beta_4=\EE X^4\), and \(Q_2=X^2-1-\beta_3X\), the displayed coefficient
formula gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma^{-6}\Delta_6(\nu)
&=-\frac7{64}
-\frac{1+6\beta_3^2-8\beta_4}{64}  \\
&=\frac{\beta_4-1}{8}-\frac{3\beta_3^2}{32}
=\frac{\beta_3^2}{32}+\frac{\EE Q_2^2}{8},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality is
Lemma~\ref{lem:normalized-fourth-moment-defect-algebra}.  Thus
\(\Delta_6(\nu)=\sigma^6\eta\).
Because quadratic Wasserstein distance is homogeneous under dilation,
\[
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
=\sigma\,W_2(\mathcal L(X),\rho_1)
\]
and Lemma~\ref{lem:normalized-fourth-moment-w2-coupling} gives
\[
\sigma\,W_2(\mathcal L(X),\rho_1)
\le \sigma\{4\eta^{1/4}+8\eta^{1/2}\},
\]
which is exactly
\[
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/2}.
\]
The constants in this displayed estimate are the deliberately non-sharp
constants from the elementary coupling above.  Non-negativity and the
equality case are exactly the equality statement in
Lemma~\ref{lem:normalized-fourth-moment-defect-algebra}, followed by the
dilation \(x\mapsto\sigma x\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Functional-inequality context]
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} is a scaled finite-moment
consequence of the normalized algebra
\eqref{eq:normalized-fourth-moment-algebra} and the coefficient
identification of \(A_6\), with an elementary transport coupling rather than a
sharp functional-inequality stability theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{Weighted trigonometric normalization}%
\label{app:entropy-integrals}

This appendix records the change of variables behind the Laurent coefficient
normalization.  Set \(y=\tan\theta\),
\(-\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2\).  Then
\[
  \frac{\dd y}{\pi(1+y^2)}=\frac{\dd\theta}{\pi}.
\]
With \(z=e^{2\ii\theta}\),
\[
  \cos(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k+z^{-k}}2,\qquad
  \sin(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k-z^{-k}}{2\ii}.
\]
Thus the Cauchy mode integrals in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} are exactly Haar
constant terms:
\[
  \int_{\RR}\prod_j u_{n_j}(y)\,\omega(\dd y)
  =[z^0]\prod_j Q_{n_j}(z).
\]
For order eight, Appendix~\ref{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate} records
the row certificate as a finite coefficient-calculus check.

\section{Optional order-eight Laurent coefficient certificate}%
\label{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate}

This optional appendix gives the finite certificate for the order-eight rows
used to audit the \(A_8\) substitution.  The main coefficient hierarchy has
already been proved in Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics}; the material
below is a reproducible check of one explicit example.
For
\(\mathbf n=(n_1,\ldots,n_r)\), put
\[
\mathcal K(\mathbf n)
:=\{(k_1,\ldots,k_r):0<|k_j|\le n_j,\ k_1+\cdots+k_r=0\}.
\]
With \(Q_n(z)=\sum_kq_{n,k}z^k\) and the uniform coefficients
\eqref{eq:mode-laurent-coefficients}, put
\[
\lambda_n:=2^{-n}(-\ii)^n .
\]
For each row set
\[
S(\mathbf n):=
\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathcal K(\mathbf n)}
\prod_{j=1}^r
\operatorname{sgn}(k_j)^{\,n_j\bmod2}
\binom{n_j-1}{|k_j|-1},
\qquad
\Lambda(\mathbf n):=\prod_{j=1}^r\lambda_{n_j}.
\]
Then the all-sign coefficient convention gives
\[
J(\mathbf n)=[z^0]\prod_{j=1}^rQ_{n_j}(z)
=\Lambda(\mathbf n)S(\mathbf n).
\]

The Laurent representatives used in every row of the \(A_6,A_8\)
substitution are:
\begin{align}
Q_2(z)
&=-\frac14\bigl(z+z^{-1}+z^2+z^{-2}\bigr),
\label{eq:appendix-d-explicit-q2}\\
Q_3(z)

\label{eq:appendix-d-explicit-q3}\\

\label{eq:appendix-d-explicit-q4}\\
Q_5(z)
&=-\frac{\ii}{32}\bigl((z-z^{-1})+4(z^2-z^{-2})
  +6(z^3-z^{-3})+4(z^4-z^{-4})+(z^5-z^{-5})\bigr),
\label{eq:appendix-d-explicit-q5}\\
Q_6(z)
&=-\frac1{64}\bigl((z+z^{-1})+5(z^2+z^{-2})
  +10(z^3+z^{-3})+10(z^4+z^{-4}) \nonumber\\

\begin{lemma}[Deterministic exact Laurent recurrence]%
\label{lem:appendix-d-deterministic-laurent-recurrence}
Let \(\mathbf n=(n_1,\ldots,n_r)\) be any finite row of positive integers.
Define finitely supported maps \(C_j:\ZZ\to\QQ[\ii]\) by
\[
C_0(s)=\mathbf 1_{\{s=0\}},\qquad
C_j(s)=\sum_{0<|k|\le n_j}
2^{-n_j}(-\ii)^{n_j}\operatorname{sgn}(k)^{\,n_j\bmod2}
\binom{n_j-1}{|k|-1}\,C_{j-1}(s-k).
\]
Then
\[
  C_r(0)=[z^0]\prod_{j=1}^r Q_{n_j}(z)=J(\mathbf n).
\]
In particular \(C_r(0)\in\QQ\).  More explicitly,
\[
C_r(0)=
\prod_{j=1}^r\lambda_{n_j}
\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathcal K(\mathbf n)}
\prod_{j=1}^r
\operatorname{sgn}(k_j)^{\,n_j\bmod2}
\binom{n_j-1}{|k_j|-1}.
\]
The recurrence is deterministic and terminates after at most
\(\prod_{j=1}^r(2n_j)\) rational Gaussian-integer operations.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient formula \eqref{eq:mode-laurent-coefficients} says that
multiplication by \(Q_{n_j}\) sends a coefficient array \(C_{j-1}\) to the
convolution array displayed above.  Induction on \(j\) gives
\[
  C_j(s)=[z^s]\prod_{a=1}^jQ_{n_a}(z),
\]
and hence \(C_r(0)\) is the desired constant term.  Expanding the last
coefficient and collecting the common factor
\(\prod_j\lambda_{n_j}\) gives the stated zero-sum formula.

The value is rational because the constant term is the Haar integral of the
real-valued product
\(\prod_j u_{n_j}(\tan\theta)\), by
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}.  Equivalently,
when \(\sum_j n_j\) is odd, the integrand is odd and the value is zero; when
\(\sum_j n_j\) is even, \(\prod_j\lambda_{n_j}\) is real and the zero-sum
integer convolution is an integer.
\end{proof}

The row certificate is the following finite table: \(S(\mathbf n)\) is the
integer from the zero-sum convolution in
Lemma~\ref{lem:appendix-d-deterministic-laurent-recurrence}, and
\(J(\mathbf n)=\Lambda(\mathbf n)S(\mathbf n)\).

For the mode products entering \(A_8\), the finite zero-sum
convolution is:
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r|c|c}
\mathbf n&|\mathcal K(\mathbf n)|&S(\mathbf n)&\Lambda(\mathbf n)&J(\mathbf n)\\
\hline
(2,6)&4&12&1/256&3/64\\
(3,5)&6&-30&1/256&-15/128\\
(4,4)&8&40&1/256&5/32\\
(2,2,4)&12&24&1/256&3/32\\
(2,3,3)&14&-18&1/256&-9/128\\
(2,2,2,2)&36&36&1/256&9/64 .
\end{array}
\]
Odd total-order entries through order eight vanish by
\(u_n(-y)=(-1)^nu_n(y)\).  The sign-sensitive rows
\((3,5)\) and \((2,3,3)\) use the same all-sign coefficient rule as the
other rows; no separate conjugation convention is applied.

As a model for the sign-sensitive rows, write
\[
w_n(k):=\operatorname{sgn}(k)^{\,n\bmod 2}
\binom{n-1}{|k|-1},\qquad 0<|k|\le n .
\]
For \((3,5)\), the zero-sum condition forces the second index to be \(-k\).
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
S(3,5)
&=\sum_{\substack{0<|k|\le3\\ l=-k}}
\operatorname{sgn}(k)\operatorname{sgn}(l)
\binom{2}{|k|-1}\binom{4}{|l|-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(l=-k\), one has
\(\operatorname{sgn}(l)=-\operatorname{sgn}(k)\).  The two choices
\(k=\pm r\), \(r=1,2,3\), therefore contribute the same negative weight, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
S(3,5)
&=-2\left[
\binom20\binom40+\binom21\binom41+\binom22\binom42
\right]  \\
&=-2(1+8+6)=-30.
\end{aligned}
\]
The rational prefactor is
\(\Lambda(3,5)=\lambda_3\lambda_5=(\ii/8)(-\ii/32)=1/256\).  Therefore
\[
J(3,5)=\Lambda(3,5)S(3,5)=-\frac{30}{256}
=-\frac{15}{128}.
\]

The remaining rows are obtained by the same recurrence.  The imaginary
factors cancel in the even total-order rows listed above, and odd total-order
rows vanish by parity.

\subsection*{Order-eight coefficient substitution}
\label{subsec:appendix-optional-order-eight-layer}

The coefficient theorem itself is only one line of analysis beyond the row
table: insert the displayed constants into the universal entropy-coefficient
formula.  The sixth-order arithmetic used in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:explicit-sixth-order-coefficients} is computed in
Lemma~\ref{lem:sixth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}; the appendix records the
eighth-order extension only to make the order-eight coefficient substitution
auditable.

\begin{proposition}[Order-eight coefficient from the row table]%
\label{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}
\label{lem:n4-entropy-coefficient-substitution}
\label{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}
\label{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}
Let \(\nu\) have mean \(\bar{\gamma}\), finite variance
\(\sigma^2\ge0\), and finite centred sixth absolute moment.  Then
\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}))
=\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}+\frac{A_6(\nu)}{t^6}
+\frac{A_8(\nu)}{t^8}+o(t^{-8}),
\]
where \(A_6\) is the sixth-order polynomial of
Lemma~\ref{lem:sixth-order-coefficient-arithmetic} and
\[
A_8=
\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
When \(\sigma^2=0\), this is the trivial exact identity for the point mass
\(\delta_{\bar{\gamma}}\).  If, in addition, the centred seventh absolute
moment is finite, then the same expansion has the quantitative remainder
\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}))
=\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}+\frac{A_6(\nu)}{t^6}
+\frac{A_8(\nu)}{t^8}+O_\nu(t^{-9}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The recurrence in
Lemma~\ref{lem:appendix-d-deterministic-laurent-recurrence}, applied to the
six total-order-eight rows in the table, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_8&=\mu_2\mu_6J(2,6)+\mu_3\mu_5J(3,5)
+\frac12\mu_4^2J(4,4)
-\frac12\mu_2^2\mu_4J(2,2,4)\\
&\hspace{2.5em}
-\frac12\mu_2\mu_3^2J(2,3,3)
+\frac1{12}\mu_2^4J(2,2,2,2).
\end{aligned}
\]
The row table gives the displayed rational polynomial for \(A_8\); the parity
identity \(u_n(-y)=(-1)^nu_n(y)\) eliminates the odd total-order products.
The coefficient-identifying expansion follows by applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} with \(N=4\), together with the
sixth-order constants of
Lemma~\ref{lem:sixth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.  The case
\(\sigma^2=0\) is immediate.  For the quantitative remainder, apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-margin-quotient-remainder} with \(N=4\), \(L=6\),
and \(\eta=1\).  The seventh absolute moment gives
\[
\delta_t(y)=\sum_{n=2}^{6}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}+\rho_{6,t}(y),
\qquad
\|\rho_{6,t}\|_\infty+\|\rho_{6,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}=O_\nu(t^{-7}),
\]
and \(\|\delta_t\|_\infty=O_\nu(t^{-2})\).  Every entropy term containing
\(\rho_{6,t}\) has one further density factor at the quadratic entropy scale,
so its contribution is \(O_\nu(t^{-9})\), while the Taylor tail after degree
\(4\) is \(O_\nu(t^{-10})\).  The coefficient theorem above supplies the
pure-mode terms \(A_4,A_6,A_8\), with the \(A_6\) constants taken from
Lemma~\ref{lem:sixth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.
\end{proof}
