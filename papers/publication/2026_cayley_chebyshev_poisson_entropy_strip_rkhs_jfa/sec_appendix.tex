\section{Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness}%
\label{app:haar-pullback-uniqueness}

The entropy coefficient calculation uses only the Haar/Cauchy transport
identity
\[
  \iota_{\#}\omega=m_{\TT},
  \qquad
  \iota^*m_{\TT}:=(\iota^{-1})_{\#}m_{\TT}=\omega .
\]
Thus pushforward is always written with \({}_{\#}\), and pullback is used
only for the inverse-image measure in the homeomorphic Cayley chart.  The
following elementary normalization removes the only possible ambiguity in the
chosen chart.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness]%
\label{prop:haar-pullback-uniqueness}
Let \(\varphi:\overline\RR\to\TT\) be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
such that \(\varphi|_{\RR}\) is \(C^1\), \(\varphi(0)=1\), and
\(\varphi(\infty)=-1\).  Then
\[
  \varphi_{\#}\omega=m_{\TT}
\]
if and only if \(\varphi=\iota\).  Equivalently, in the same chart
convention, \(\varphi^*m_{\TT}:=(\varphi^{-1})_{\#}m_{\TT}=\omega\) if and
only if \(\varphi=\iota\).  Without the orientation assumption, the only
additional possibility is complex conjugation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}\), where
\(\alpha:\RR\to(-\pi,\pi)\) is increasing and \(\alpha(0)=0\).  The
inverse-image measure \((\varphi^{-1})_{\#}m_{\TT}\), namely the pullback of
normalized arc length in this chart, has density
\((2\pi)^{-1}\alpha'(x)\dd x\).  Therefore
\(\varphi^*m_{\TT}=\omega\) is equivalent to
\(\alpha'(x)=2/(1+x^2)\).  Integration gives
\(\alpha(x)=2\arctan x\), hence \(\varphi=\iota\).  Since \(\varphi\) is a
homeomorphism, this pullback condition is equivalent to
\(\varphi_{\#}\omega=m_{\TT}\).  The reversed-orientation case follows by
applying the same argument to \(\overline\varphi\).
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Consolidated scope restrictions}

The KL functional used throughout is the extended nonnegative
Kullback--Leibler functional of \eqref{eq:extended-kl-definition}
\cite{KullbackLeibler1951,CoverThomas2006}.  Under finite centred second
moment in dimension one the matched-Poisson KL value is finite for every
\(t>0\); without that hypothesis the sharp-threshold theorem is interpreted
in \([0,\infty]\), so the conclusion
\(t^4D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))\to+\infty\) includes the
case where the KL value is already \(+\infty\) at some large times.  The
finite-moment hierarchy identifies universal polynomial coefficients only
when the required centred moments exist.

For the exact and perturbative symmetric power-tail density classes in
Theorems~\ref{prop:log-tail-counterexample-top-moment} and
\ref{thm:exact-perturbative-power-tail-log-replacement}, the missing top
finite moment is replaced by the truncated top moment \(M_p(t)\), giving the
stated logarithmic layers.  No theorem for arbitrary regularly varying tails
is asserted without the exact quotient expansion or quotient controls stated
in the relevant result.  The only slowly varying add-on is
Corollary~\ref{cor:conditional-slowly-varying-borderline-tail}, restricted to
eventually monotone borderline densities; there
Lemma~\ref{lem:slowly-varying-quotient-controls} proves the
Potter--Karamata quotient controls from slow variation and
\(M_p(t)\to\infty\).

In several dimensions, Proposition~\ref{prop:multidim-formal-covariance-layer}
is the quadratic covariance calculation, whereas the KL asymptotic requires
the separate quotient-jet hypothesis \((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\), for which
finite centred \(L\)-moment with integer \(L\ge d+1\) is a sufficient
condition.  Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} is a
finite-moment consequence of the coefficient algebra, not an
optimizer-stability theorem for a sharp functional inequality and not a
claim that a small matched-Poisson KL value alone controls \(W_2\).  The
dissipation identity is proved in Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy} by the
Cauchy cutoff Green formula.  Pointwise dissipation asymptotics would require
additional differentiated-remainder input; the coefficient theorems use only
tail integrals and fixed-window averages.

\section{Weighted trigonometric normalization}%
\label{app:entropy-integrals}

This appendix records the change of variables behind the Laurent coefficient
normalization.  Set \(y=\tan\theta\),
\(-\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2\).  Then
\[
  \frac{\dd y}{\pi(1+y^2)}=\frac{\dd\theta}{\pi}.
\]
With \(z=e^{2\ii\theta}\),
\[
  \cos(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k+z^{-k}}2,\qquad
  \sin(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k-z^{-k}}{2\ii}.
\]
Thus the Cauchy mode integrals in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} are exactly Haar
constant terms:
\[
  \int_{\RR}\prod_j u_{n_j}(y)\,\omega(\dd y)
  =[z^0]\prod_j Q_{n_j}(z).
\]
For order eight, Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic} gives
the recurrence; Appendix~\ref{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate} gives the
row certificate.

\section{Order-eight Laurent certificate}%
\label{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate}

This appendix gives the finite certificate behind
Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.  For
\(\mathbf n=(n_1,\ldots,n_r)\), put
\[
\mathcal K(\mathbf n)
:=\{(k_1,\ldots,k_r):0<|k_j|\le n_j,\ k_1+\cdots+k_r=0\}.
\]
With \(Q_n(z)=\sum_kq_{n,k}z^k\) and the uniform coefficients
\eqref{eq:mode-laurent-coefficients}, put
\[
\lambda_n:=2^{-n}(-\ii)^n .
\]
For each row set
\[
S(\mathbf n):=
\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathcal K(\mathbf n)}
\prod_{j=1}^r
\operatorname{sgn}(k_j)^{\,n_j\bmod2}
\binom{n_j-1}{|k_j|-1},
\qquad
\Lambda(\mathbf n):=\prod_{j=1}^r\lambda_{n_j}.
\]
Then the all-sign coefficient convention gives
\[
I(\mathbf n)=[z^0]\prod_{j=1}^rQ_{n_j}(z)
=\Lambda(\mathbf n)S(\mathbf n).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Deterministic exact Laurent recurrence]%
\label{lem:appendix-d-deterministic-laurent-recurrence}
Let \(\mathbf n=(n_1,\ldots,n_r)\) be any finite row of positive integers.
Define finitely supported maps \(C_j:\ZZ\to\QQ[\ii]\) by
\[
C_0(s)=\mathbf 1_{\{s=0\}},\qquad
C_j(s)=\sum_{0<|k|\le n_j}
2^{-n_j}(-\ii)^{n_j}\operatorname{sgn}(k)^{\,n_j\bmod2}
\binom{n_j-1}{|k|-1}\,C_{j-1}(s-k).
\]
Then
\[
  C_r(0)=[z^0]\prod_{j=1}^r Q_{n_j}(z)=I(\mathbf n).
\]
In particular \(C_r(0)\in\QQ\).  More explicitly,
\[
C_r(0)=
\prod_{j=1}^r\lambda_{n_j}
\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathcal K(\mathbf n)}
\prod_{j=1}^r
\operatorname{sgn}(k_j)^{\,n_j\bmod2}
\binom{n_j-1}{|k_j|-1}.
\]
The recurrence is deterministic and terminates after at most
\(\prod_{j=1}^r(2n_j)\) rational Gaussian-integer operations.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient formula \eqref{eq:mode-laurent-coefficients} says that
multiplication by \(Q_{n_j}\) sends a coefficient array \(C_{j-1}\) to the
convolution array displayed above.  Induction on \(j\) gives
\[
  C_j(s)=[z^s]\prod_{a=1}^jQ_{n_a}(z),
\]
and hence \(C_r(0)\) is the desired constant term.  Expanding the last
coefficient and collecting the common factor
\(\prod_j\lambda_{n_j}\) gives the stated zero-sum formula.

The value is rational because the constant term is the Haar integral of the
real-valued product
\(\prod_j u_{n_j}(\tan\theta)\), by
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}.  Equivalently,
when \(\sum_j n_j\) is odd, the integrand is odd and the value is zero; when
\(\sum_j n_j\) is even, \(\prod_j\lambda_{n_j}\) is real and the zero-sum
integer convolution is an integer.
\end{proof}

Thus the certificate used in the proof is completely finite: for each row
below, the integer \(S(\mathbf n)\) is obtained by the zero-sum convolution
in Lemma~\ref{lem:appendix-d-deterministic-laurent-recurrence}, multiplication
by the displayed rational factor \(\Lambda(\mathbf n)\) gives the exact
constant term \(I(\mathbf n)\), and no external computation is used in the
argument.

For the mode products entering \(A_4,A_6,A_8\), the finite zero-sum
convolution is:
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r|c|c}
\mathbf n&|\mathcal K(\mathbf n)|&S(\mathbf n)&\Lambda(\mathbf n)&I(\mathbf n)\\
\hline
(2,2)&4&4&1/16&1/4\\
(2,4)&4&8&-1/64&-1/8\\
(3,3)&6&-12&-1/64&3/16\\
(2,2,2)&6&6&-1/64&-3/32\\
(2,6)&4&12&1/256&3/64\\
(3,5)&6&-30&1/256&-15/128\\
(4,4)&8&40&1/256&5/32\\
(2,2,4)&12&24&1/256&3/32\\
(2,3,3)&14&-18&1/256&-9/128\\
(2,2,2,2)&36&36&1/256&9/64 .
\end{array}
\]
Odd total-order entries through order eight vanish by
\(u_n(-y)=(-1)^nu_n(y)\).  The sign-sensitive rows
\((3,5)\) and \((2,3,3)\) use the same all-sign coefficient rule as the
other rows; no separate conjugation convention is applied.
