\section{Weighted trigonometric integrals}\label{app:entropy-integrals}

For convenience we collect the weighted identities used in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}.  After the substitution
$y=\tan\theta$, each of them reduces to the extraction of the zero
Fourier mode in an elementary trigonometric product.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:entropy-integrals}
Let $u_2,\dots,u_6$ be the mode functions defined by
\eqref{eq:cayley-first-modes} and \eqref{eq:cayley-next-modes}.  Then
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac14,
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_3(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{3}{16},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_4(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac18,
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^3}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac{3}{32},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_6(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{3}{64},
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_3(y)u_5(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac{15}{128},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_4(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{5}{32},
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^2u_4(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{3}{32},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_3(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac{9}{128},
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^4}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{9}{64}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $y=\tan\theta$ with $\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$, so that
$\dd y/(1+y^2)=\dd\theta$, and write
\[
c_k(\theta):=\cos(2k\theta),
\qquad
s_k(\theta):=\sin(2k\theta).
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode},
\[
u_2(\tan\theta)=-\frac12(c_1+c_2),
\qquad
u_3(\tan\theta)=-\frac14(s_1+2s_2+s_3),
\]
\[
u_4(\tan\theta)=\frac18(c_1+3c_2+3c_3+c_4),
\qquad
u_5(\tan\theta)=\frac1{16}(s_1+4s_2+6s_3+4s_4+s_5),
\]
\[
u_6(\tan\theta)=-\frac1{32}(c_1+5c_2+10c_3+10c_4+5c_5+c_6).
\]
On $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ one has
\[
\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} c_k(\theta)^2\,\dd\theta
=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} s_k(\theta)^2\,\dd\theta
=\frac{\pi}{2}
\qquad(k\ge 1),
\]
and mixed products with different parity or different frequency
integrate to zero.  Thus each identity follows by expanding the
corresponding trigonometric polynomial and retaining only the constant
term.  Equivalently, if $Q_n(e^{2\ii\theta})=u_n(\tan\theta)$, then each
integral is the zero Laurent coefficient $[z^0]$ of the indicated product.
The required finite multiplications are:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{product} & [z^0]\,\text{product} \\
\hline
Q_2^2 & \frac14 \\
Q_3^2 & \frac{3}{16} \\
Q_2Q_4 & -\frac18 \\
Q_2^3 & -\frac{3}{32} \\
Q_2Q_6 & \frac{3}{64} \\
Q_3Q_5 & -\frac{15}{128} \\
Q_4^2 & \frac{5}{32} \\
Q_2^2Q_4 & \frac{3}{32} \\
Q_2Q_3^2 & -\frac{9}{128} \\
Q_2^4 & \frac{9}{64}
\end{array}
\]
For instance,
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y
=\frac14\cdot\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}(c_1+c_2)^2\,\dd\theta
=\frac14,
\]
and
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_4(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y
=-\frac1{16}\cdot\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}
(c_1+c_2)(c_1+3c_2+3c_3+c_4)\,\dd\theta
=-\frac18.
\]
The table records the remaining constant terms, so every integral listed in
the lemma is accounted for by a zero-Fourier-coefficient computation.
\end{proof}

\section{Background principal-symbol criterion}\label{app:background-symbol-criterion}

This appendix records the standard Fourier proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:background-principal-symbol-sampling}.  The argument is not a
new part of the Poisson-strip theorem chain; it is included only to make the
imported shift-invariant-space background self-contained for notation.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:background-principal-symbol-sampling}]
For finitely supported $c$ the Toeplitz Gram identity and Parseval give
\[
\|Cc\|_{\mathcal H}^2
=\sum_{m,n}c_m\overline{c_n}a_{m-n}
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
\sigma(\theta)|\widetilde c(\theta)|^2\dd\theta .
\]
The bounds on $\sigma$ yield the Riesz inequalities and therefore the
extension and closed-range isomorphism.  The same Fourier transform
diagonalizes $G$ by
\[
\widetilde{Gc}=\sigma\widetilde c,
\]
and $1/\sigma\in L^\infty$ gives the inverse multiplier.  If $R(Cc)=Gc$,
then $R$ is an isomorphism in the Riesz coordinates because $G$ is
invertible.  For finitely supported data $\alpha$ the interpolating
coefficient is $c=G^{-1}\alpha$, and therefore
\[
\|R^{-1}\alpha\|_{\mathcal H}^2
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
\sigma(\theta)\left|\frac{\widetilde\alpha(\theta)}{
\sigma(\theta)}\right|^2\dd\theta,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:imported-reciprocal-symbol-norm}.  Density in
$\ell^2$ gives the formula for all data, and taking
$\alpha=\delta_0$ gives $\widetilde d=\sigma^{-1}$.
\end{proof}
