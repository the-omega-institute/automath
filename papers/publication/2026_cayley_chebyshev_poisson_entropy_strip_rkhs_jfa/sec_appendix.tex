\section{Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness}%
\label{app:haar-pullback-uniqueness}

The entropy coefficient calculation uses only the pullback identity
\(\iota^*m_{\TT}=\omega\).  The following elementary normalization removes
the only possible ambiguity in the chosen Cayley chart.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness]%
\label{prop:haar-pullback-uniqueness}
Let \(\varphi:\overline\RR\to\TT\) be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
such that \(\varphi|_{\RR}\) is \(C^1\), \(\varphi(0)=1\), and
\(\varphi(\infty)=-1\).  Then \(\varphi^*m_{\TT}=\omega\) if and only if
\(\varphi=\iota\).  Without the orientation assumption, the only additional
possibility is complex conjugation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}\), where
\(\alpha:\RR\to(-\pi,\pi)\) is increasing and \(\alpha(0)=0\).  Pullback of
normalized arc length is \((2\pi)^{-1}\alpha'(x)\dd x\).  Therefore
\(\varphi^*m_{\TT}=\omega\) is equivalent to
\(\alpha'(x)=2/(1+x^2)\).  Integration gives
\(\alpha(x)=2\arctan x\), hence \(\varphi=\iota\).  The reversed-orientation
case follows by applying the same argument to \(\overline\varphi\).
\end{proof}

\section{Remarks and nonclaims}
\label{app:remarks-nonclaims}

This appendix records scope statements that are deliberately kept outside the
introductory theorem block.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item The paper studies the fixed-law matched Poisson KL functional
\[
  D_{\rm KL}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr),
\]
and its multidimensional radial analogue.  The input law is fixed as
\(t\to\infty\); it is not a normalized-sum or changing-law CLT problem.

\item The one-dimensional finite-moment hierarchy is a
coefficient-evaluability hierarchy for the displayed universal polynomials.
The finite \((2N-2)\)nd absolute moment is the first level at which the
polynomial \(A_{2N}\) can be evaluated from the finite moment jet used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  This is separate from the analytic
tail threshold proved for the leading scale: by
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-leading-kl-variance-threshold}, finite centred
variance is necessary and sufficient for a finite \(t^{-4}\) matched-Poisson
KL coefficient.

\item Missing top moments can create non-polynomial tail layers rather than
lower-moment evaluations of the same polynomial.  For \(N\ge3\),
Proposition~\ref{prop:log-tail-counterexample-top-moment} gives symmetric
power-tail laws where the missing \(A_{2N}\) layer is replaced by a
tail-dependent \(t^{-2N}\log t\) contribution; the \(N=2\) infinite-variance
borderline has the larger \(t^{-4}(\log t)^2\) scale.

\item The multidimensional covariance formula
Proposition~\ref{prop:multidim-formal-covariance-layer} is a covariance-only
quadratic-layer identity.  It does not assert a KL asymptotic under finite
covariance alone.  The leading multidimensional KL coefficient follows only
when the finite-\(L\) quotient-remainder hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets} is available; the
condition \(L\ge d+1\) is the large-translation integrability requirement in
that quotient theorem.

\item The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail} separates the
abstract Hardy--Stein/Sobolev--Bregman input from the specialization lemma
and from the paper's Poisson-quotient verification.  The \(L^1\) KL chain rule,
cutoff Green identity, bounded
quotient range, Bregman domination, and tail limit are checked in
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy} before the specialization is invoked.

\item The defect corollary is a coefficient-level statement.  It uses the
finite fourth absolute moment needed to identify \(A_6\), assumes
\(\sigma^2>0\) only for the normalized defect formulation, and does not use a
finite-time KL value, eighth-order remainder bound, sixth absolute moment, or
approximation radius.

\item Conditional finite-time post-processing is not part of the main proof
architecture.  It may be used only after the relevant coefficient,
undifferentiated remainder, differentiated remainder, and numerical error
inputs have been supplied independently.  Such bookkeeping is isolated in
Appendix~\ref{app:conditional-post-processing}; it is not an inference from a
single finite-time KL value, and the main asymptotic theorems use only the
displayed hypotheses and estimates stated in the article.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work and theorem map}%
\label{app:related-work-scope}

This appendix records the longer comparison behind the compact discussion in
the introduction.  The comparison is organized by object, not by priority.
Fixed parametric Cauchy information formulae concern Cauchy laws, whereas
\(P_t*\nu\) is generally not Cauchy.  Entropy, Fisher-information, de
Bruijn, and R\'enyi CLT papers concern changing-law normalized-sum or
stable-limit asymptotics.  Heat, fractional-heat, porous-medium, and
fast-diffusion papers concern linear or nonlinear large-time profile
expansions, often with entropy or entropy dissipation measuring convergence
to a self-similar or equilibrium profile.  Hardy--Stein and
Sobolev--Bregman papers supply the abstract Bregman identity used in
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}.  Stability, entropy-transport,
unbalanced-transport, and point-process papers concern functional-inequality
deficits, transport estimates, or geometry of entropy on a transport space.
None of these sources contains the fixed input-law Cauchy quotient, the
Cayley--Chebyshev coefficient algebra, the \(N\ge3\) borderline
\(t^{-2N}\log t\) power-tail layer, the \(N=2\) borderline
\(t^{-4}(\log t)^2\) infinite-variance layer, or the finite Laurent
constant-term evaluation used here.

The entropy-asymptotic papers closest in mechanism are the entropic CLT
papers \cite{Barron1986,ArtsteinBallBartheNaor2004,JohnsonBarron2004,
BobkovChistyakovGotzeEdgeworth2013,BobkovChistyakovGotzeBerryEsseen2014,
BobkovGotzeEsscher2025}, the stable-law and de Bruijn variants
\cite{BobkovChistyakovGotze2013,BobkovChistyakovGotzeStableFisher2014,
JohnsonStableDeBruijn2013,Toscani2015,Toscani2016}, and the fast-diffusion
entropy-method asymptotics
\cite{CarrilloToscani2000PorousMedium,CarrilloJungelMarkowichToscaniUnterreiter2001EntropyDissipation,
DolbeaultToscani2011FastDiffusion,BonforteGrilloVazquez2010SpecialFastDiffusion,
Vazquez2014NonlinearFractionalHeat}.  These works expand or estimate
information quantities along a changing family: normalized sums, stable-law
attractors, or nonlinear diffusion flows.  The present expansion instead
keeps the input law fixed and changes only the Cauchy/Poisson smoothing
scale.  Its coefficients are universal polynomials in the centred moments
after the matched translated Cauchy profile has been divided out.

\section{Weighted trigonometric normalization}%
\label{app:entropy-integrals}

This appendix records the change of variables behind the Laurent coefficient
normalization.  Set \(y=\tan\theta\),
\(-\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2\).  Then
\[
  \frac{\dd y}{\pi(1+y^2)}=\frac{\dd\theta}{\pi}.
\]
With \(z=e^{2\ii\theta}\),
\[
  \cos(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k+z^{-k}}2,\qquad
  \sin(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k-z^{-k}}{2\ii}.
\]
Thus the Cauchy mode integrals in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} are exactly Haar
constant terms:
\[
  \int_{\RR}\prod_j u_{n_j}(y)\,\omega(\dd y)
  =[z^0]\prod_j Q_{n_j}(z).
\]
The finite order-eight arithmetic used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} is the table in
Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.

\section{Conditional post-processing}
\label{app:conditional-post-processing}

This appendix contains optional statements that are not consequences of the
finite-moment quotient theorem alone and are not used to prove the main
asymptotic theorems.  In particular,
Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-moment-density-truncation} proves
\(\rho_{L,t}=o(t^{-L})\) in \(L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)\), but it does not
prove a corresponding estimate for \(t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\), nor a continuity
or local dominated-convergence hypothesis for the differentiated remainder.
The following dissipation expansion is therefore conditional and only
a.e.-in-time: it may be used after the differentiated quotient remainder has
been established by some additional regularity or tail input, but by itself it
does not upgrade the a.e. derivative identity to every large time.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional differentiated-remainder a.e. dissipation]%
\label{lem:differentiated-quotient-remainder-ae-dissipation}
Let \(N\ge2\), put \(L=2N-2\), and set
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),\qquad
\delta_t(y):=\frac{h_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}{g_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}-1 .
\]
Assume that the KL tail identity
\eqref{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation} holds for the same
quotient path.  Assume also that
\[
\delta_t(y)=d_t(y)+\rho_{L,t}(y),
\qquad
d_t(y)=\sum_{n=2}^{L}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n},
\]
and that the undifferentiated and differentiated remainders satisfy, for
a.e. sufficiently large \(t\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:differentiated-quotient-remainder-bound}
\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^\infty}
+\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
=o(t^{-L}),
\qquad
\|t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^\infty}
+\|t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
=o(t^{-L}).
\end{equation}
Then, for a.e. \(t\to\infty\), the dissipation has the asymptotic expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ae-dissipation-asymptotic}
I_\nu(t)=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}2m\,A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-(2m+1)}
+o(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\).  For a.e. sufficiently large \(t\),
\(\|\delta_t\|_\infty+\|d_t\|_\infty=O(t^{-2})\), and
\[
H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\int_{\RR}\Phi(\delta_t(y))\,\omega(\dd y).
\]
The \(L^1\)-chain rule of Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-kl-l1-ac-chain-rule}
identifies the absolutely continuous representative of the derivative as
\[
H'(t)=\int_{\RR}\log(1+\delta_t(y))\,\partial_t\delta_t(y)\,\omega(\dd y)
\]
for a.e. \(t\).  Intersect this full-measure set with the set on which
\eqref{eq:differentiated-quotient-remainder-bound} holds.  On the resulting
set \(E\), as \(t\to\infty\), the following estimates are valid.  Since
\[
\partial_td_t(y)=\sum_{n=2}^{L}(-n)\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-(n+1)},
\]
the finite modes give \(\|d_t\|_\infty=O(t^{-2})\) and
\(\|\partial_td_t\|_\infty=O(t^{-3})\).  Because \(\log(1+s)\) is Lipschitz
on \(|s|\le1/2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\int \log(1+\delta_t)\partial_t\delta_t\,\dd\omega
-\int \log(1+d_t)\partial_td_t\,\dd\omega
\right|\\
&\quad\le
C\|d_t\|_\infty\|\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
+C\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}\|\partial_td_t\|_\infty
+C\|\rho_{L,t}\|_\infty\|\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)} .
\end{aligned}
\]
The conditional bound \eqref{eq:differentiated-quotient-remainder-bound}
therefore makes this error
\[
O(t^{-2})o(t^{-L-1})+O(t^{-3})o(t^{-L})
+o(t^{-L})o(t^{-L-1})
=o(t^{-(L+3)})=o(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\]

It remains to differentiate the finite-mode entropy.  The Taylor series for
\(\Phi\) and the bound \(d_t=O(t^{-2})\) give
\[
\int \log(1+d_t)\partial_td_t\,\dd\omega
=
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}
\int_{\RR}\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}d_t(y)^r\,\omega(\dd y)
+o(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\]
The retained polynomial may be differentiated term by term.  By the
definition of the universal coefficients and the parity cancellation proved
in Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients},
\[
\int_{\RR}\sum_{r=2}^{N}\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}d_t(y)^r\,\omega(\dd y)
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
+O(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\]
Hence
\[
H'(t)=
-\sum_{m=2}^{N}2m\,A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-(2m+1)}
+o(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\]
Finally \(H'(t)=-I_\nu(t)\) for a.e. \(t\) by
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail}.  Removing another null set if
necessary, this identity holds on the same full-measure set \(E\).  Substituting
\(H'(t)=-I_\nu(t)\) on \(E\) proves exactly
\[
I_\nu(t)=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}2m\,A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-(2m+1)}
+o(t^{-(2N+1)})
\]
for a.e. \(t\to\infty\).  No continuity of \(I_\nu\) or of
\(t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\) is used or concluded.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[When the a.e. conclusion becomes pointwise]
The preceding lemma deliberately stops at an a.e.-in-time conclusion.  To
obtain the same expansion for every sufficiently large \(t\), one must add
regularity hypotheses that turn the differentiated calculation into an
identity between continuous representatives.  For example, it is enough to
assume that
\[
t\mapsto \rho_{L,t},\qquad t\mapsto t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}
\]
are continuous as maps into
\(L^\infty(\omega)\cap L^1(\omega)\), and that on each compact
multiplicative window \([T,2T]\) the differentiated remainders are dominated
by an \(L^\infty(\omega)\cap L^1(\omega)\) majorant of order \(T^{-L}\)
compatible with the two bounds in
\eqref{eq:differentiated-quotient-remainder-bound}.  Then dominated
convergence gives continuity of \(I_\nu(t)\) and of the finite-mode
derivative expression, so the a.e. identity extends to all sufficiently large
\(t\).  These continuity and domination assumptions are not consequences of
the finite-moment quotient theorem.
\end{remark}
