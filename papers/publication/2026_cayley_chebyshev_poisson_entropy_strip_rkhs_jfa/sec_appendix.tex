\section{Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness}%
\label{app:haar-pullback-uniqueness}

The following elementary statement fixes the chart normalization behind
\eqref{eq:haar-pullback}.  The entropy expansion uses only the pullback
identity itself; the uniqueness assertion is included here to remove any
ambiguity about the endpoint convention.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness]%
\label{prop:haar-pullback-uniqueness}
Let $\varphi:\overline\RR\to\TT$ be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
such that $\varphi|_{\RR}$ is of class $C^{1}$,
$\varphi(0)=1$, and $\varphi(\infty)=-1$.  Then the following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\varphi^{*}m_{\TT}=\omega$ on~$\RR$;
\item if $\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}$ with
      $\alpha:\RR\to(-\pi,\pi)$ increasing, then
      $\alpha'(x)=2/(1+x^{2})$ for every $x\in\RR$;
\item $\varphi=\iota$.
\end{enumerate}
If the orientation assumption is removed, the same conclusion holds up to
complex conjugation: $\varphi=\iota$ or $\varphi=\overline{\iota}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any orientation-preserving $\varphi$ satisfying the endpoint
normalization, there is a unique increasing $C^{1}$ lift
$\alpha:\RR\to(-\pi,\pi)$ with $\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}$ and
$\alpha(0)=0$.  Pullback of normalized arc length is
\[
\varphi^{*}m_{\TT}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\alpha'(x)\dd x .
\]
Hence $\varphi^{*}m_{\TT}=\omega$ is equivalent to
\(\alpha'(x)=2/(1+x^{2})\).  Integrating from \(0\) to \(x\) gives
\(\alpha(x)=2\arctan x\), hence \(\varphi=\iota\).  Conversely, for
\(\iota\), Proposition~\ref{prop:phase-derivative} gives
\[
\iota^{*}m_{\TT}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\vartheta'(x)\dd x
=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x
=\omega(\dd x).
\]
If orientation is reversed, the lift is decreasing and the same argument
applied to \(\overline{\varphi}\) gives the stated conjugate alternative.
\end{proof}

The table deliberately keeps five notions separate.  Fixed-law Poisson entropy
jets are the expansion of a single matched KL functional as the Poisson scale
grows.  Normalized-sum entropy theorems change the law with the number of
summands.  Flow asymptotics describe large-time profiles or evolution limits
rather than KL coefficients for a fixed input law.  Transport and
functional-inequality stability estimates compare near extremizers or
transport structures, whereas the present \(W_2\) estimates are
coefficient-defect consequences.  Parametric Cauchy divergence formulae give
closed forms within Cauchy families; here the smoothed law \(P_t*\nu\) is
generally not Cauchy unless \(\nu\) is a point mass or a matching Cauchy-type
special case.

The distinction from formal Taylor extraction is also theorem-level.  Expanding
the quotient \(R_\varepsilon\) or its multivariable analogue gives candidate
modes only.  The entropy theorems prove that those modes may be averaged under
finite moments, that the quotient remainder is small in the weighted spaces
needed for KL, that centering removes the odd obstruction, and that the
Bregman Taylor expansion of \((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) has an integrable tail with
the stated \(o(t^{-2N})\) or finite-time remainder.  The explicit Laurent
calculus in dimension one is therefore the coefficient-evaluation layer of an
analytic finite-moment Poisson theorem, not the theorem by itself.

\section{Weighted trigonometric integrals}\label{app:entropy-integrals}

We collect the weighted identities used in the Laurent representatives and
constant-term table of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:n4-normalized-laurent-representatives} and
\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.  With
\(y=\tan\theta\), each identity is a zero-Fourier-mode extraction in an
elementary trigonometric product.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted Cayley mode integrals]\label{lem:entropy-integrals}
Let $u_2,\dots,u_6$ be the mode functions defined by
\eqref{eq:cayley-first-modes} and \eqref{eq:cayley-next-modes}.  Then
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac14,
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_3(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{3}{16},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_4(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac18,
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^3}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac{3}{32},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_6(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{3}{64},
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_3(y)u_5(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac{15}{128},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_4(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{5}{32},
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^2u_4(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{3}{32},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_3(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac{9}{128},
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^4}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{9}{64}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $y=\tan\theta$ with $\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$, so that
$\dd y/(1+y^2)=\dd\theta$, and write
\[
c_k(\theta):=\cos(2k\theta),
\qquad
s_k(\theta):=\sin(2k\theta).
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode},
\[
u_2(\tan\theta)=-\frac12(c_1+c_2),
\qquad
u_3(\tan\theta)=-\frac14(s_1+2s_2+s_3),
\]
\[
u_4(\tan\theta)=\frac18(c_1+3c_2+3c_3+c_4),
\qquad
u_5(\tan\theta)=\frac1{16}(s_1+4s_2+6s_3+4s_4+s_5),
\]
\[
u_6(\tan\theta)=-\frac1{32}(c_1+5c_2+10c_3+10c_4+5c_5+c_6).
\]
On $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ one has
\[
\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} c_k(\theta)^2\,\dd\theta
=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} s_k(\theta)^2\,\dd\theta
=\frac{\pi}{2}
\qquad(k\ge 1),
\]
and mixed products with different parity or different frequency
integrate to zero.  Thus each identity follows by expanding the
corresponding trigonometric polynomial and retaining only the constant
term.  Equivalently, if $Q_n(e^{2\ii\theta})=u_n(\tan\theta)$, then each
integral is the zero Laurent coefficient $[z^0]$ of the indicated product;
the coefficient-level constant terms are listed in
Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.
For instance,
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y
=\frac14\cdot\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}(c_1+c_2)^2\,\dd\theta
=\frac14,
\]
and
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_4(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y
=-\frac1{16}\cdot\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}
(c_1+c_2)(c_1+3c_2+3c_3+c_4)\,\dd\theta
=-\frac18.
\]
The remaining identities are accounted for by the same zero-Fourier-coefficient
computation.
\end{proof}

The coefficient-level use of these identities is consolidated in
Lemmas~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic} and
\ref{lem:n4-entropy-coefficient-substitution}; the computations above give
the elementary trigonometric verification of the weighted mode integrals.

\section{KL Doob cutoff identity}\label{app:doob-bregman-domain}

This appendix contains only the domain input needed by the KL tail identity,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail}.  The compact quotient range,
Lipschitz bound, KL Bregman majorant, and product-cutoff convergence are
proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:kl-main-compact-domain}; the role of the appendix is
to fix the principal-value normalization that converts the localized KL chain
rule into the cutoff identity used in Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}.
The range-local convex-\(\Phi\) closure is independent of the coefficient
theorem and is separated from the main paper.

\begin{lemma}[Two-point KL algebra for the Doob derivative]%
\label{lem:doob-kl-algebra}
Let $g_x,g_y>0$ and $u_x,u_y>0$.  Let
$h_x=g_xu_x$, $h_y=g_yu_y$, and set
\[
A(u):=\log u+1,\qquad G(u):=-u,
\qquad
\Lambda(a,b):=a\log(a/b)-a+b .
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-kl-algebra}
\begin{split}
&(h_x-h_y)\bigl(A(u_x)-A(u_y)\bigr)
 +(g_x-g_y)\bigl(G(u_x)-G(u_y)\bigr)
\\
&\qquad
=g_x\Lambda(u_x,u_y)+g_y\Lambda(u_y,u_x).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Collect the $g_x$ and $g_y$ coefficients separately.  The coefficient of
$g_x$ on the left of \eqref{eq:doob-kl-algebra} is
\[
u_x\bigl(A(u_x)-A(u_y)\bigr)+G(u_x)-G(u_y).
\]
Substituting \(A(u)=\log u+1\) and \(G(u)=-u\) gives
\[
u_x\log(u_x/u_y)-u_x+u_y=\Lambda(u_x,u_y).
\]
The coefficient of $g_y$ is
\[
-u_y\bigl(A(u_x)-A(u_y)\bigr)-G(u_x)+G(u_y)
=u_y\log(u_y/u_x)-u_y+u_x
=\Lambda(u_y,u_x).
\]
This proves the identity.  No analytic domain assertion enters this step.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Quotient-kernel formulas and cutoff-ready bounds]%
\label{lem:appendix-poisson-quotient-kernel-calculus}
For
\[
R_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\qquad w:=y-\varepsilon,
\]
one has
\begin{align}
R_\varepsilon(y)
&=1+\frac{2\varepsilon w+\varepsilon^2}{1+w^2},
\label{eq:appendix-R-eps-zero}\\
\partial_yR_\varepsilon(y)
&=\frac{2\varepsilon(1-w^2)-2\varepsilon^2w}{(1+w^2)^2},
\label{eq:appendix-R-eps-first}\\
\partial_y^2R_\varepsilon(y)
&=\frac{4\varepsilon w(w^2-3)+2\varepsilon^2(3w^2-1)}
        {(1+w^2)^3}.
\label{eq:appendix-R-eps-second}
\end{align}
Consequently, for all \(y,\varepsilon\in\RR\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-R-eps-bounds}
0<R_\varepsilon(y)\le2(1+\varepsilon^2),\qquad
R_\varepsilon(y)\ge\frac{1}{2(1+\varepsilon^2)},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-R-eps-derivative-bounds}
|\partial_yR_\varepsilon(y)|\le3(1+\varepsilon^2),
\qquad
|\partial_y^2R_\varepsilon(y)|\le16(1+\varepsilon^2).
\end{equation}
Thus, if \(t\in[a,b]\), \(x=\bar\gamma+ty\), and
\(\varepsilon=(\gamma-\bar\gamma)/t\), then the corresponding Poisson quotient
obeys
\[
\|u_t\|_\infty\le2\left(1+\frac{\mu_2}{a^2}\right),\quad
\|\partial_xu_t\|_\infty
\le\frac{3}{a}\left(1+\frac{\mu_2}{a^2}\right),\quad
\|\partial_x^2u_t\|_\infty
\le\frac{16}{a^2}\left(1+\frac{\mu_2}{a^2}\right),
\]
where \(\mu_2=\int(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\dd\nu(\gamma)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(y=w+\varepsilon\),
\[
1+y^2=(1+w^2)+2\varepsilon w+\varepsilon^2,
\]
which gives \eqref{eq:appendix-R-eps-zero}.  Differentiating in \(w\) gives
\eqref{eq:appendix-R-eps-first} and \eqref{eq:appendix-R-eps-second}.  The
size bounds follow from
\[
1+y^2\le2\bigl(1+(y-\varepsilon)^2\bigr)+2\varepsilon^2,
\qquad
1+(y-\varepsilon)^2\le2(1+\varepsilon^2)(1+y^2).
\]
The derivative bounds follow from \eqref{eq:appendix-R-eps-first} and
\eqref{eq:appendix-R-eps-second} using
\[
\frac{|1-w^2|}{(1+w^2)^2},\
\frac{|w|}{(1+w^2)^2},\
\frac{|w|^3}{(1+w^2)^3},\
\frac{|w|}{(1+w^2)^3},\
\frac{w^2}{(1+w^2)^3},\
\frac{1}{(1+w^2)^3}
\le1
\]
and \(2|\varepsilon|\le1+\varepsilon^2\),
\(16|\varepsilon|+8\varepsilon^2\le16(1+\varepsilon^2)\).  Integrating
\(R_{(\gamma-\bar\gamma)/t}((x-\bar\gamma)/t)\) and its first two
\(x\)-derivatives against \(\nu\), and using \(t\ge a\), gives the stated
quotient bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cutoff justification for the Cauchy Doob calculus]%
\label{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus}
Let $[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)$ and let $\nu$ have finite centred second
moment.  For $t\in[a,b]$ put $h_t=P_t*\nu$, $g_t=P_t(\cdot-
\bar\gamma)$, and $u_t=h_t/g_t$.  Then the following assertions hold
uniformly for $t\in[a,b]$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item
      The functions $u_t$, $\partial_xu_t$, and $\partial_x^2u_t$ are
      bounded.  Moreover
      $h_t,g_t,\partial_xh_t,\partial_xg_t,\partial_th_t,\partial_tg_t$
      belong to $L^1(\RR)$, and
      \[
      \partial_th_t=Lh_t,
      \qquad
      \partial_tg_t=Lg_t
      \]
      pointwise, where $L$ is the principal-value Cauchy generator.
\item
      If $q$ is a bounded Lipschitz function and
      $p\in\{h_t,g_t\}$, then the bilinear Cauchy form
      \[
      \mathcal E(p,q)
      :=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
      \frac{(p(x)-p(y))(q(x)-q(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
      \]
      is absolutely convergent and
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff-cauchy-bilinear}
      \int_{\RR}q(x)Lp(x)\dd x=-\mathcal E(p,q).
      \end{equation}
      The identity is obtained as the limit of the compactly supported
      identities with $q$ replaced by $\chi_Rq$, where
      $\chi_R(x)=\chi(x/R)$, $\chi\in C_c^\infty(\RR)$,
      $\chi\equiv1$ on $[-1,1]$.  This fixes the domain convention used in
      the theorem: the left side is the $L^1$ pairing with the
      principal-value generator, and the right side is the absolutely
      convergent cutoff limit.
\item
      If $f$ is bounded $C^2$ with bounded first and second derivatives,
      in particular if $f=u_t$, then, with the same principal-value
      normalization,
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff-doob-product-rule}
      L(g_tf)(x)-f(x)Lg_t(x)
      =\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
      \frac{g_t(y)(f(y)-f(x))}{(x-y)^2}\dd y .
      \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:appendix-poisson-quotient-kernel-calculus} gives the asserted
uniform bounds for \(u_t\), \(\partial_xu_t\), and \(\partial_x^2u_t\) on
\([a,b]\).  The second-derivative bound is the input that puts \(u_t\) in the
pointwise product-rule domain below.

The $L^1$ assertions follow from convolution and scaling:
\[
\|\partial_xP_t\|_1\le C_a,
\qquad
\|\partial_tP_t\|_1\le C_a
\qquad(a\le t\le b).
\]
The identity $\partial_tP_t=LP_t$ is the one-dimensional Cauchy-semigroup
generator identity in the principal-value normalization used here
\cite[Definition~2.2 and Proposition~2.4]{Kwasnicki2017}.  Convolution with
the finite measure~$\nu$ and dominated convergence give
$\partial_th_t=Lh_t$; the same argument with the point mass at
$\bar\gamma$ gives $\partial_tg_t=Lg_t$.

For the bilinear identity, first take $q\in C_c^\infty(\RR)$.  The usual
principal-value formula for the Cauchy generator and symmetrization give
\eqref{eq:cutoff-cauchy-bilinear}.  The same compactly supported identity
holds for compactly supported Lipschitz tests by mollifying them and using the
estimates below.  For bounded Lipschitz $q$, replace it by
$q_R=\chi_Rq$, $R\ge1$.  Then
\[
\|q_R\|_\infty\le \|q\|_\infty,
\qquad
\|q_R\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}
\le \|q\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}+\|\chi'\|_\infty\|q\|_\infty,
\]
so the cutoff family has bounds independent of~$R$.  Since
$p,\partial_xp\in L^1(\RR)$ and $q$ is bounded and Lipschitz, the near
diagonal part satisfies, uniformly in~$R$,
\[
\iint_{|x-y|<1}
\frac{|p(x)-p(y)|\,|q_R(x)-q_R(y)|}{|x-y|^2}\dd x\dd y
\le 2\|q_R\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\|\partial_xp\|_1,
\]
because
$p(x)-p(y)=\int_0^1p'(y+s(x-y))(x-y)\dd s$.  The far part satisfies,
again uniformly in~$R$,
\[
\iint_{|x-y|\ge1}
\frac{|p(x)-p(y)|\,|q_R(x)-q_R(y)|}{|x-y|^2}\dd x\dd y
\le 8\|q\|_\infty\|p\|_1,
\]
since
$\int_{|x-y|\ge1}|x-y|^{-2}\dd y=2$ and the same estimate holds after
interchanging $x$ and~$y$.  It remains to show that the cutoff error tends to
zero.  Put $e_R:=q_R-q$.  Then $e_R=0$ on $[-R,R]$,
$\|e_R\|_\infty\le2\|q\|_\infty$, and
$\|e_R\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\le
\|q\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}+\|\chi'\|_\infty\|q\|_\infty$.  For the near
diagonal contribution, $e_R(x)-e_R(y)\ne0$ and $|x-y|<1$ imply that the line
segment between $x$ and~$y$ lies in $\{|z|>R-1\}$.  Therefore
\[
\iint_{|x-y|<1}
\frac{|p(x)-p(y)|\,|e_R(x)-e_R(y)|}{|x-y|^2}\dd x\dd y
\le C_q\int_{|z|>R-1}|p'(z)|\dd z\longrightarrow0.
\]
For the far contribution, using $|e_R(z)|\le2\|q\|_\infty\mathbf 1_{|z|>R}$
gives a sum of four terms.  The two terms in which the same variable carries
both $p$ and $e_R$ are bounded by
$4\|q\|_\infty\int_{|z|>R}|p(z)|\dd z$.  The cross terms are bounded by
\[
2\|q\|_\infty\int_{\RR}|p(x)|
\int_{\substack{|y|>R\\ |x-y|\ge1}}\frac{\dd y}{|x-y|^2}\dd x,
\]
and the analogous expression with $x$ and~$y$ interchanged; the inner integral
is bounded by~$2$ and tends to zero for each fixed outer variable, so dominated
convergence and $p\in L^1$ make the cross terms vanish.  Hence the bilinear
forms with $q_R$ converge to the form with~$q$.  On the one-variable side,
\[
|e_R(x)Lp(x)|\le 2\|q\|_\infty |Lp(x)|,
\qquad Lp=\partial_tp\in L^1(\RR),
\]
and $e_RLp\to0$ pointwise.  Hence the cutoff identities pass to the limit and
prove \eqref{eq:cutoff-cauchy-bilinear} for bounded Lipschitz~$q$.

Finally apply the principal-value formula to $g_tf$ and to $g_t$.  In the
truncated integrals over $|x-y|>\rho$ the difference is exactly
\[
\frac1\pi\int_{|x-y|>\rho}
\frac{g_t(y)(f(y)-f(x))}{(x-y)^2}\dd y .
\]
The integrand has a finite principal-value limit because near $y=x$ the
linear part contributes an odd kernel and the remaining term is bounded,
while away from $x$ it is integrable.  Letting $\rho\downarrow0$ gives
\eqref{eq:cutoff-doob-product-rule}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Non-autonomous Doob transform]%
\label{thm:poisson-doob-phi-entropy}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$ and
finite centred second moment.  For $t>0$ define
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),
\qquad
u_t(x):=\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}.
\]
Let
\[
(Lf)(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y
\]
be the one-dimensional Cauchy generator, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-generator-definition}
(\mathcal L_t f)(x)
:=\frac{1}{g_t(x)}\bigl(L(g_tf)(x)-f(x)Lg_t(x)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For the displayed formulae below the domain of \(\mathcal L_t\) is the class
of bounded \(C^2\) functions with bounded first and second derivatives; the
Green identity is asserted on \(C_c^\infty(\RR)\).  The cutoff interpretation
is the one fixed in Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus}.  Then the
following assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item
      The function $u_t$ is bounded and belongs to $C^\infty(\RR)$ for
      every $t>0$.  More precisely, for every $a>0$ and every $t\ge a$,
      \[
      \frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)}
      \le u_t(x)\le
      2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},
      \qquad
      \mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\dd\nu(\gamma).
      \]
      Moreover,
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-flow-equation}
      \partial_tu_t=\mathcal L_tu_t
      \qquad\text{on }\RR\times(0,\infty),
      \end{equation}
      and \eqref{eq:doob-generator-definition} may be written explicitly
      as
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-generator-kernel}
      (\mathcal L_tf)(x)
      =\frac{1}{\pi g_t(x)}\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
      \frac{g_t(y)\bigl(f(y)-f(x)\bigr)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y.
      \end{equation}
\item
      For every $f,\psi\in C_c^\infty(\RR)$ one has the weighted Green
      identity
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-green-identity}
      \begin{split}
      \int_{\RR}f(x)(\mathcal L_t\psi)(x)\,g_t(x)^2\dd x
      &=
      -\frac{1}{2\pi}
      \iint_{\RR^2}
      \frac{g_t(x)g_t(y)}{(x-y)^2}
      \\
      &\qquad\qquad\times
      \bigl(f(x)-f(y)\bigr)\bigl(\psi(x)-\psi(y)\bigr)\dd x\dd y.
      \end{split}
      \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $t>0$ the functions $h_t$ and $g_t$ are positive and belong to
$C^\infty(\RR)$, because they are obtained by convolving the smooth
Poisson kernel with a probability measure.  Write
$X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma$, $x=\bar\gamma+ty$, and
$\varepsilon=X_c/t$.  Then
\[
u_t(x)=\int_{\RR}R_{X_c/t}(y)\dd\nu(\gamma),
\qquad
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}.
\]
The elementary estimate
\[
1+y^2=1+\bigl((y-\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\bigr)^2
\le 2\bigl(1+(y-\varepsilon)^2\bigr)+2\varepsilon^2
\]
gives
\[
0<R_\varepsilon(y)\le 2+2\varepsilon^2
\qquad(y,\varepsilon\in\RR),
\]
and therefore
\[
0<u_t(x)\le 2+\frac{2\mu_2}{t^2},
\qquad
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
Likewise,
\[
1+(y-\varepsilon)^2
\le 2\bigl(1+y^2\bigr)+2\varepsilon^2
\le 2(1+\varepsilon^2)(1+y^2),
\]
so
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)\ge \frac{1}{2(1+\varepsilon^2)}
\qquad(y,\varepsilon\in\RR).
\]
Hence
\[
u_t(x)\ge \frac12\int_{\RR}\frac{1}{1+(X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
\ge \frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/t^2)},
\]
where the last inequality follows from Jensen's inequality applied to the
convex function $s\mapsto(1+s)^{-1}$ on $[0,\infty)$.  Thus $u_t$ is
bounded for every fixed $t>0$.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-semigroup-law},
$\partial_t h_t=-|D|h_t$ and $\partial_t g_t=-|D|g_t$.  The cutoff
calculus of Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus} identifies these
derivatives with $Lh_t$ and $Lg_t$ in the principal-value sense and permits
the product-rule passage below.  Since $h_t=u_tg_t$ and $g_t(x)>0$,
\[
\partial_tu_t(x)
=\frac{Lh_t(x)-u_t(x)Lg_t(x)}{g_t(x)}.
\]
Substituting $h_t=u_tg_t$ in
\eqref{eq:cutoff-doob-product-rule} yields
\[
\partial_tu_t(x)
=\frac{1}{\pi g_t(x)}\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{g_t(y)\bigl(u_t(y)-u_t(x)\bigr)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y.
\]
This is \eqref{eq:quotient-flow-equation} and proves
\eqref{eq:doob-generator-kernel}; part~\textup{(i)} follows.

For part~\textup{(ii)}, let $f,\psi\in C_c^\infty(\RR)$.  The truncated
integrals in Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus} justify using
\eqref{eq:doob-generator-kernel} under the outer integral, and hence
\[
\int_{\RR}f(x)(\mathcal L_t\psi)(x)\,g_t(x)^2\dd x
=
\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{f(x)g_t(x)g_t(y)\bigl(\psi(y)-\psi(x)\bigr)}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y.
\]
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ in the same integral and averaging the two
representations gives \eqref{eq:doob-green-identity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[KL cutoff domain identity extracted from the appendix]%
\label{prop:kl-appendix-cutoff-domain-identity}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\) and
finite centred second moment.  Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), and put
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t,
\qquad
H(t)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t).
\]
Set
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log(\alpha/\beta)-\alpha+\beta .
\]
Then \(H\in AC([a,b])\), and for a.e. \(t\in[a,b]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-kl-cutoff-domain-identity}
-H'(t)=
\lim_{R\to\infty}
\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}\chi_R(x)\chi_R(y)
\frac{
g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)\Lambda(u_t(y),u_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\end{equation}
with convergence in \(L^1([a,b])\).  Here
\(\chi_R(x)=\chi(x/R)\) for any fixed
\(\chi\in C_c^\infty(\RR)\), \(0\le\chi\le1\), \(\chi\equiv1\) on
\([-1,1]\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:kl-main-compact-domain} gives one compact range
\(m\le u_t\le M\) and one Lipschitz bound for \(u_t\) on
\([a,b]\times\RR\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus} gives
\(h,g,\partial_th,\partial_tg\in L^1([a,b]\times\RR)\) and
\(\partial_th_t=Lh_t\), \(\partial_tg_t=Lg_t\) in the principal-value
normalization used here.  The \(L^1\) KL chain rule,
Lemma~\ref{lem:l1-bounded-kl-quotient-chain-rule}, therefore gives
\(H\in AC([a,b])\) and, for a.e. \(t\),
\[
H'(t)=
\int_{\RR}\bigl(\log u_t+1\bigr)Lh_t\,\dd x
-\int_{\RR}u_t\,Lg_t\,\dd x .
\]

Fix a cutoff \(\chi_R\) and write
\[
H_R(t):=\int_{\RR}\chi_R(x)h_t(x)\log u_t(x)\dd x .
\]
The localized part of Lemma~\ref{lem:l1-bounded-kl-quotient-chain-rule} gives
\(H_R\in AC([a,b])\) and the same derivative formula with the factor
\(\chi_R\) inserted.  Put
\[
A_t(x):=\log u_t(x)+1,\qquad G_t(x):=-u_t(x).
\]
Since \(u_t\) is bounded away from \(0\), bounded above, and Lipschitz,
both \(A_t\) and \(G_t\) are bounded Lipschitz uniformly in
\(t\in[a,b]\).  Applying the cutoff Cauchy bilinear identity
\eqref{eq:cutoff-cauchy-bilinear} to the tests
\(\chi_RA_t\) and \(\chi_RG_t\) gives
\[
-H_R'(t)=
\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{\mathcal S_R(t,x,y)}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y,
\]
where
\begin{align*}
\mathcal S_R(t,x,y):=
&(h_t(x)-h_t(y))(\chi_R(x)A_t(x)-\chi_R(y)A_t(y))\\
&\quad
+(g_t(x)-g_t(y))(\chi_R(x)G_t(x)-\chi_R(y)G_t(y)).
\end{align*}
If \(\chi_R(x)\chi_R(y)\) is factored from the terms with
\(A_t(x)-A_t(y)\) and \(G_t(x)-G_t(y)\), the algebraic numerator is
\[
\begin{split}
&(h_t(x)-h_t(y))\bigl(A_t(x)-A_t(y)\bigr)
+(g_t(x)-g_t(y))\bigl(G_t(x)-G_t(y)\bigr)\\
&\quad=
g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)\Lambda(u_t(y),u_t(x)).
\end{split}
\]
This is Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-kl-algebra} with
\(h_t=g_tu_t\), \(A(u)=\log u+1\), and \(G(u)=-u\).

It remains only to remove the localization error.  The product-cutoff
Bregman term is controlled by the \(L^1([a,b]\times\RR^2)\) majorant in
Lemma~\ref{lem:kl-main-compact-domain}.  The remaining terms all contain
either \(\chi_R(x)(1-\chi_R(y))\) or \(\chi_R(x)-\chi_R(y)\).  Near the
diagonal, boundedness of \(A_t,G_t\), their uniform Lipschitz bounds, and the
identity
\[
p_t(x)-p_t(y)=(x-y)\int_0^1\partial_xp_t(y+s(x-y))\dd s,
\qquad p_t\in\{h_t,g_t\},
\]
dominate the absolute error by an \(L^1\) function involving
\(|\partial_xh_t|+|\partial_xg_t|\).  Away from the diagonal, boundedness of
\(A_t,G_t,u_t\) dominates it by a constant multiple of
\[
\frac{h_t(x)+h_t(y)+g_t(x)+g_t(y)}{|x-y|^2}\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|\ge1\}},
\]
which is integrable because \(h_t\) and \(g_t\) are probability densities.
The cutoff factors tend pointwise to zero in both regions, so dominated
convergence gives an error \(e_R\) with
\(\|e_R\|_{L^1([a,b])}\to0\).  Finally \(H_R\to H\) uniformly on \([a,b]\)
by the tail conclusion of Lemma~\ref{lem:l1-bounded-kl-quotient-chain-rule}.
Passing \(R\to\infty\) in the localized identities proves
\eqref{eq:appendix-kl-cutoff-domain-identity}.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite-time estimates and constant regimes for the sixth coefficient defect}
\label{app:finite-time-defect-estimates}

The primary finite-time result is
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}.  It fixes the
nonempty controlled shell \(\mu_2=\sigma^2\), \(\EE|X_c|^7\le M\), with
\(M\ge\sigma^7\), or equivalently the normalized radius
\(\widehat M=M/\sigma^7\ge1\).  It displays the normalized constants and
their dimensional scale restores
\(C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\), and only then
inserts the measured entropy value through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-time-sixth-deficit}
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)
:=
t^6\left(
\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
-\frac{7\sigma^6}{64t^6}
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
\right).
\end{equation}
Here \(h_t=P_t*\nu\) and \(g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma)\).  The controlled-shell
conclusion is
\eqref{eq:main-finite-time-defect-estimate}--\eqref{eq:main-finite-time-w2-stability}.
The fixed-law oracle variant,
Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle-finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}, instead uses
supplied certificate bounds \(C,B,T\) and gives
\eqref{eq:main-oracle-finite-time-defect-estimate}--\eqref{eq:main-oracle-finite-time-w2-stability}.

The constant regimes are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}, leftmargin=*]
\item The coefficient-level \(W_2\) constants \(4\) and \(8\) in
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} are universal algebraic
constants.  They do not use a finite-time entropy remainder.
\item The fixed-law finite-time theorem uses oracle certificate bounds
\(C,B,T\).  If these data are supplied by
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}, then \(C\) is any
finite bound with \(C\ge |A_8(\nu)|\), while \(B=B_{\nu,8}\) and
\(T=T_{\nu,8}\); these may depend on the particular law.
\item The controlled-shell theorem uses the displayed finite functions
\(C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\).  These are the
scale restores of normalized constants depending only on
\(\widehat M=M/\sigma^7\), together with the universal \(N=4\) envelopes from
Lemma~\ref{lem:closed-cayley-mode-constant-envelopes}; they are fixed before
the KL value at time \(t\) is used.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{corollary}[Constant dependence for moment-defect \(W_2\) estimates]%
\label{cor:defect-stability-constant-regimes}
The coefficient-level constants in
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} are universal apart from the
explicit variance powers forced by scaling against
\(\rho_\sigma=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma)\).  The finite-time
constants are separate inputs: either fixed-law oracle certificate bounds
\(C,B,T\), or the controlled-shell constants displayed in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}.  The measured value
\(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\) supplies only
\(\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This restates the hypotheses and conclusions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling},
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle-finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Appendix check of the controlled-shell finite-time estimate]%
\label{cor:uniform-finite-time-defect-stability}
Fix \(\sigma>0\) and \(M\ge\sigma^7\), and use the explicit constants
\(B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\), \(T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\), and
\(C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\) displayed in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}.  For every law in
the controlled shell \(\mu_2=\sigma^2\), \(\EE|X_c|^7\le M\), and every
\(t\ge T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-finite-time-defect-shell}
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\mathcal A_t^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\mathcal A_t^{1/2},
\end{equation}
where
\[
\mathcal A_t:=
\bigl|\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\bigr|
+C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}t^{-2}+B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}t^{-3}.
\]
Outside such a shell, supplied fixed-law certificate bounds \(C,B,T\) in
Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle-finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability} are the
asserted level of generality.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is exactly \eqref{eq:main-finite-time-w2-stability}.  The shell
control is proved inside
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability} by applying the
seventh-moment remainder theorem to the variance-one law
\((\gamma-\bar\gamma)/\sigma\) and restoring the factors of \(\sigma\).
\end{proof}
