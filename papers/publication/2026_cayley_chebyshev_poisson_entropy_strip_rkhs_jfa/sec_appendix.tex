\section{Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness}%
\label{app:haar-pullback-uniqueness}

The following elementary statement fixes the chart normalization behind
\eqref{eq:haar-pullback}.  The entropy expansion uses only the pullback
identity itself; the uniqueness assertion is included here to remove any
ambiguity about the endpoint convention.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness]%
\label{prop:haar-pullback-uniqueness}
Let $\varphi:\overline\RR\to\TT$ be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
such that $\varphi|_{\RR}$ is of class $C^{1}$,
$\varphi(0)=1$, and $\varphi(\infty)=-1$.  Then the following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\varphi^{*}m_{\TT}=\omega$ on~$\RR$;
\item if $\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}$ with
      $\alpha:\RR\to(-\pi,\pi)$ increasing, then
      $\alpha'(x)=2/(1+x^{2})$ for every $x\in\RR$;
\item $\varphi=\iota$.
\end{enumerate}
If the orientation assumption is removed, the same conclusion holds up to
complex conjugation: $\varphi=\iota$ or $\varphi=\overline{\iota}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any orientation-preserving $\varphi$ satisfying the endpoint
normalization, there is a unique increasing $C^{1}$ lift
$\alpha:\RR\to(-\pi,\pi)$ with $\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}$ and
$\alpha(0)=0$.  Pullback of normalized arc length is
\[
\varphi^{*}m_{\TT}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\alpha'(x)\dd x .
\]
Hence $\varphi^{*}m_{\TT}=\omega$ is equivalent to
\(\alpha'(x)=2/(1+x^{2})\).  Integrating from \(0\) to \(x\) gives
\(\alpha(x)=2\arctan x\), hence \(\varphi=\iota\).  Conversely, for
\(\iota\), Proposition~\ref{prop:phase-derivative} gives
\[
\iota^{*}m_{\TT}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\vartheta'(x)\dd x
=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x
=\omega(\dd x).
\]
If orientation is reversed, the lift is decreasing and the same argument
applied to \(\overline{\varphi}\) gives the stated conjugate alternative.
\end{proof}

\section{Weighted trigonometric normalization}\label{app:entropy-integrals}

This appendix records only the change of variables behind the Laurent
normalization used in
Lemmas~\ref{lem:n4-normalized-laurent-representatives} and
\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.  The actual coefficient
arithmetic, including the sign-sensitive \(A_8\) rows, is consolidated in the
single finite certificate of Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.

Set \(y=\tan\theta\) with \(\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\), so that
\[
\frac{\dd y}{\pi(1+y^2)}=\frac{\dd\theta}{\pi}.
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode}, if
\(c_k(\theta)=\cos(2k\theta)\) and \(s_k(\theta)=\sin(2k\theta)\), then
\[
u_2(\tan\theta)=-\frac12(c_1+c_2),
\qquad
u_3(\tan\theta)=-\frac14(s_1+2s_2+s_3),
\]
\[
u_4(\tan\theta)=\frac18(c_1+3c_2+3c_3+c_4),
\qquad
u_5(\tan\theta)=\frac1{16}(s_1+4s_2+6s_3+4s_4+s_5),
\]
\[
u_6(\tan\theta)=-\frac1{32}(c_1+5c_2+10c_3+10c_4+5c_5+c_6).
\]
With \(z=e^{2\ii\theta}\), the identities
\[
\cos(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k+z^{-k}}2,\qquad
\sin(2k\theta)=\frac{z^k-z^{-k}}{2\ii}
\]
give the Laurent representatives \(Q_n(z)=u_n(\tan\theta)\) displayed in
Lemma~\ref{lem:n4-normalized-laurent-representatives}.  Orthogonality on
\((-\pi/2,\pi/2)\) is exactly Haar constant-term extraction in \(z\):
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{\prod_j u_{n_j}(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\,\dd y
=[z^0]\prod_j Q_{n_j}(z).
\]
Therefore the weighted trigonometric integrals entering \(A_4,A_6,A_8\) are
not a second calculation; they are precisely the Laurent constant terms
certified by recurrence
\eqref{eq:eighth-order-constant-term-recurrence} in
Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.

\section{Elementary fourth-moment coupling}\label{app:fourth-moment-coupling-proof}

This appendix proves the transport estimate used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling}.  It is independent of the
entropy asymptotic machinery.

\begin{lemma}[Sign coupling for the sixth coefficient defect]%
\label{lem:appendix-fourth-moment-sign-coupling}
Let \(X\) be a real random variable with
\(\EE X=0\), \(\EE X^2=1\), and finite fourth moment.  Put
\[
\beta_3=\EE X^3,\qquad
Q_2=X^2-1-\beta_3X,\qquad
\kappa=\|Q_2\|_2^2,
\]
and let
\[
\Delta:=\frac{\sigma^6}{32}\beta_3^2+\frac{\sigma^6}{8}\kappa
\qquad(\sigma>0).
\]
If \(Z\) is a symmetric Rademacher random variable and
\(\rho_\sigma=\mathcal L(\sigma Z)\), then
\[
W_2(\mathcal L(\sigma X),\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\Delta^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\Delta^{1/2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
Y:=\mathbf 1_{\{X\ge0\}}-\mathbf 1_{\{X<0\}}\in\{-1,1\},
\]
with the convention \(\operatorname{sgn}(0)=1\).  For every real \(x\),
\[
|x-\operatorname{sgn}(x)|\le |x^2-1|,
\]
and hence
\[
\EE[(X-Y)^2]\le \EE[(X^2-1)^2].
\]
The decomposition \(X^2-1=\beta_3X+Q_2\) is orthogonal in \(L^2\), since
\[
\EE[XQ_2]=\EE[X^3]-\EE X-\beta_3\EE[X^2]=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
\EE[(X^2-1)^2]=\beta_3^2+\kappa
\le 2\beta_3^2+8\kappa
=\frac{64}{\sigma^6}\Delta .
\]
Thus
\[
W_2(\mathcal L(X),\mathcal L(Y))
\le \EE[(X-Y)^2]^{1/2}
\le \frac{8}{\sigma^3}\Delta^{1/2}.
\]
Since \(\EE X=0\),
\[
|\EE Y|=|\EE(Y-X)|
\le \EE[(Y-X)^2]^{1/2}
\le \frac{8}{\sigma^3}\Delta^{1/2}.
\]
If \(p=\mathbb P(Y=1)\), then \(\EE Y=2p-1\).  The optimal transport between
\(\mathcal L(Y)\) and the symmetric law on \(\{-1,1\}\) moves
\(|p-1/2|\) units of mass over distance \(2\), so
\[
W_2(\mathcal L(Y),\mathcal L(Z))^2
=4|p-1/2|=2|\EE Y|
\le \frac{16}{\sigma^3}\Delta^{1/2}.
\]
Taking square roots, multiplying both one-dimensional estimates by
\(\sigma\), and applying the triangle inequality gives the displayed bound.
\end{proof}

\section{KL Doob quotient bounds}\label{app:doob-bregman-domain}

The general Sobolev--Bregman/Hardy--Stein identity is cited in
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}; it is not reproved here.  This appendix
records only the quotient-domain estimates for the Poisson comparison
quotient \(u_t=h_t/g_t\) that are used to apply that identity in the KL/Cauchy
case.

\begin{lemma}[KL quotient range and Bregman majorant]%
\label{lem:appendix-kl-quotient-domain-bounds}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\) and
finite centred second moment
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
Fix \(0<a<b<\infty\), and put
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t .
\]
Then there are constants \(0<m_{a,b}\le M_{a,b}<\infty\) and
\(L_{a,b}<\infty\) such that, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x\in\RR\),
\[
0<m_{a,b}\le u_t(x)\le M_{a,b}<\infty,
\qquad
|\partial_xu_t(x)|\le L_{a,b}.
\]
For
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=
\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta
\qquad(\alpha,\beta>0),
\]
there is \(C_{a,b,\nu}<\infty\) such that, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and
\(x\ne y\),
\[
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le
C_{a,b,\nu}g_t(x)\min\{1,|x-y|^{-2}\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma\), \(x=\bar\gamma+tz\), and
\[
R_\varepsilon(z):=\frac{1+z^2}{1+(z-\varepsilon)^2}.
\]
Then
\[
u_t(x)=
\int_{\RR}R_{X_c/t}\!\left(\frac{x-\bar\gamma}{t}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
The elementary inequalities
\[
R_\varepsilon(z)\le2(1+\varepsilon^2),
\qquad
R_\varepsilon(z)\ge\frac{1}{2(1+\varepsilon^2)}
\]
give
\[
\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)}
\le u_t(x)\le
2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2}.
\]
Thus one may take
\[
m_{a,b}:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},
\qquad
M_{a,b}:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2}.
\]
Differentiating \(R_\varepsilon\) gives
\[
\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)
=
\frac{2\varepsilon(1-w^2)-2\varepsilon^2w}{(1+w^2)^2},
\qquad w=z-\varepsilon,
\]
and hence \(|\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)|\le3(1+\varepsilon^2)\).  Since
\(\partial_x=t^{-1}\partial_z\),
\[
|\partial_xu_t(x)|
\le
\frac{3}{a}\left(1+\frac{\mu_2}{a^2}\right)
=:L_{a,b}.
\]

On the compact interval \([m_{a,b},M_{a,b}]\), the second derivative of
\(s\log s\) is bounded by \(m_{a,b}^{-1}\).  Therefore
\[
0\le\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)
\le \frac{1}{2m_{a,b}}|\alpha-\beta|^2
\qquad
(m_{a,b}\le\alpha,\beta\le M_{a,b}).
\]
Combining this with
\[
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L_{a,b}|x-y|,
\qquad
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le M_{a,b}-m_{a,b},
\]
yields
\[
\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le C'_{a,b,\nu}\min\{|x-y|^2,1\}.
\]
Dividing by \((x-y)^2\) and multiplying by \(g_t(x)\) gives the stated
majorant.
\end{proof}

\section{Conditional post-processing}
\label{app:conditional-post-processing}

This appendix contains only deterministic post-processing of already certified
large-time data.  It does not provide a method for obtaining a finite-time KL
value, an error radius for such a value, an eighth-order coefficient bound, a
remainder bound, or a threshold time.  Those quantities must be supplied
independently before the statements below are applied.

\begin{corollary}[Deterministic finite-time sixth-defect propagation]%
\label{cor:finite-time-defect-bookkeeping}
\label{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\) with mean~\(\bar\gamma\),
variance \(\sigma^2>0\), and finite centred fourth absolute moment.  Let
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),
\qquad
\rho_\sigma=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma),
\]
and let \(A_6(\nu)\) be the sixth-order entropy coefficient from
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  Set
\[
\Delta_6(\nu):=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-A_6(\nu).
\]
Fix \(t>0\).  Assume that deterministic numbers \(C,B,T\), a coefficient
value \(A_8(\nu)\), and a remainder \(E(t)\) have been fixed so that
\(t\ge T\) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-controlled-shell-eighth-expansion}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
+\frac{A_6(\nu)}{t^6}
+\frac{A_8(\nu)}{t^8}
+E(t),
\qquad
|A_8(\nu)|\le C,\qquad |E(t)|\le Bt^{-9}.
\end{equation}
Let \(K_t^{\rm app}\) be any deterministic approximation to the KL value with
an independently supplied error radius \(\eta_t\ge0\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-approximation-error-radius}
\bigl|K_t^{\rm app}-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\bigr|\le \eta_t .
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-finite-time-sixth-deficit}
D_t^{(6),\mathrm{app}}
:=
t^6\left(
\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
-\frac{7\sigma^6}{64t^6}
-K_t^{\rm app}
\right)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-exact-finite-time-sixth-deficit}
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)
:=
t^6\left(
\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
-\frac{7\sigma^6}{64t^6}
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
\right).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-certified-shell-sixth-identity}
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)
=\Delta_6(\nu)-A_8(\nu)t^{-2}-t^6E(t),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-finite-time-defect-estimate}
0\le\Delta_6(\nu)
\le
\bigl|D_t^{(6),\mathrm{app}}\bigr|
+t^6\eta_t
+Ct^{-2}
+Bt^{-3}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for the centred law \(\nu_c\) of \(\gamma-\bar\gamma\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-finite-time-w2-stability}
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\mathcal A_t^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\mathcal A_t^{1/2},
\end{equation}
where
\[
\mathcal A_t
:=
\bigl|D_t^{(6),\mathrm{app}}\bigr|
+t^6\eta_t
+Ct^{-2}
+Bt^{-3}.
\]
In particular, if each summand in \(\mathcal A_t\) is bounded by
\(\varepsilon\), then
\[
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}(4\varepsilon)^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}(4\varepsilon)^{1/2}.
\]

The number \(K_t^{\rm app}\) and the error bound
\eqref{eq:kl-approximation-error-radius} are only inputs to
\eqref{eq:main-finite-time-defect-estimate}; they do not supply the constants
\(C,B,T\), the coefficient \(A_8(\nu)\), the remainder \(E(t)\), the bound
\(|E(t)|\le Bt^{-9}\), or the radius \(\eta_t\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient-level estimate is Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}.
Substituting \eqref{eq:main-controlled-shell-eighth-expansion} into
\eqref{eq:main-exact-finite-time-sixth-deficit} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)
&=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-A_6(\nu)
-A_8(\nu)t^{-2}-t^6E(t)\\
&=\Delta_6(\nu)-A_8(\nu)t^{-2}-t^6E(t),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is \eqref{eq:main-certified-shell-sixth-identity}.  Since
\(\Delta_6(\nu)\ge0\),
\[
0\le \Delta_6(\nu)
\le
\bigl|\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\bigr|+Ct^{-2}+Bt^{-3}.
\]
The deterministic input \eqref{eq:kl-approximation-error-radius} gives
\[
\bigl|\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)-D_t^{(6),\mathrm{app}}\bigr|
=t^6\bigl|K_t^{\rm app}-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\bigr|
\le t^6\eta_t,
\]
and hence \eqref{eq:main-finite-time-defect-estimate}.  Applying the
\(W_2\) bound in Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} with this
upper bound for \(\Delta_6(\nu)\) proves
\eqref{eq:main-finite-time-w2-stability}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Controlled-shell constants for finite-time propagation]%
\label{cor:controlled-shell-finite-time-oracle}
\label{cor:defect-stability-constant-regimes}
\label{cor:uniform-finite-time-defect-stability}
The deterministic propagation in
Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-time-defect-bookkeeping} can be made uniform on a
moment shell.  Assume, in addition to the hypotheses of that corollary, that
\[
\mu_2=\sigma^2,\qquad \EE|X_c|^7\le M,\qquad M\ge\sigma^7.
\]
Let \(\widehat M=M/\sigma^7\), and take
\[
C=C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}=\sigma^8C(\widehat M),\qquad
B=B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}=\sigma^9B(\widehat M),\qquad
T=T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}=\sigma T(\widehat M)
\]
from Definition~\ref{def:n4-finite-time-constant-dictionary}.  If
\(t\ge T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\) and
\eqref{eq:kl-approximation-error-radius} holds, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-finite-time-defect-shell}
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\mathcal A_t^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\mathcal A_t^{1/2},
\qquad
\mathcal A_t=
\bigl|D_t^{(6),\mathrm{app}}\bigr|+t^6\eta_t
+C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}t^{-2}+B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}t^{-3}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
After normalizing \(X=(\gamma-\bar\gamma)/\sigma\), one has
\(\EE X=0\), \(\EE X^2=1\), and
\(\EE|X|^7\le\widehat M=M/\sigma^7\).  The
\(N=4,\eta=1\) remainder theorem
\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}, with the constants recorded in
Definition~\ref{def:n4-finite-time-constant-dictionary}, supplies
\eqref{eq:main-controlled-shell-eighth-expansion} after restoring the powers
of \(\sigma\).  Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-time-defect-bookkeeping} then gives
the displayed shell form.
\end{proof}
