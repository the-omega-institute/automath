\section{Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness}%
\label{app:haar-pullback-uniqueness}

The following elementary statement fixes the chart normalization behind
\eqref{eq:haar-pullback}.  The entropy expansion uses only the pullback
identity itself; the uniqueness assertion is included here to remove any
ambiguity about the endpoint convention.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint-normalized Haar-pullback uniqueness]%
\label{prop:haar-pullback-uniqueness}
Let $\varphi:\overline\RR\to\TT$ be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
such that $\varphi|_{\RR}$ is of class $C^{1}$,
$\varphi(0)=1$, and $\varphi(\infty)=-1$.  Then the following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\varphi^{*}m_{\TT}=\omega$ on~$\RR$;
\item if $\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}$ with
      $\alpha:\RR\to(-\pi,\pi)$ increasing, then
      $\alpha'(x)=2/(1+x^{2})$ for every $x\in\RR$;
\item $\varphi=\iota$.
\end{enumerate}
If the orientation assumption is removed, the same conclusion holds up to
complex conjugation: $\varphi=\iota$ or $\varphi=\overline{\iota}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any orientation-preserving $\varphi$ satisfying the endpoint
normalization, there is a unique increasing $C^{1}$ lift
$\alpha:\RR\to(-\pi,\pi)$ with $\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}$ and
$\alpha(0)=0$.  Pullback of normalized arc length is
\[
\varphi^{*}m_{\TT}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\alpha'(x)\dd x .
\]
Hence $\varphi^{*}m_{\TT}=\omega$ is equivalent to
\(\alpha'(x)=2/(1+x^{2})\).  Integrating from \(0\) to \(x\) gives
\(\alpha(x)=2\arctan x\), hence \(\varphi=\iota\).  Conversely, for
\(\iota\), Proposition~\ref{prop:phase-derivative} gives
\[
\iota^{*}m_{\TT}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\vartheta'(x)\dd x
=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x
=\omega(\dd x).
\]
If orientation is reversed, the lift is decreasing and the same argument
applied to \(\overline{\varphi}\) gives the stated conjugate alternative.
\end{proof}

The table deliberately keeps five notions separate.  Fixed-law Poisson entropy
jets are the expansion of a single matched KL functional as the Poisson scale
grows.  Normalized-sum entropy theorems change the law with the number of
summands.  Flow asymptotics describe large-time profiles or evolution limits
rather than KL coefficients for a fixed input law.  Transport and
functional-inequality stability estimates compare near extremizers or
transport structures, whereas the present \(W_2\) estimates are
coefficient-defect consequences.  Parametric Cauchy divergence formulae give
closed forms within Cauchy families; here the smoothed law \(P_t*\nu\) is
generally not Cauchy unless \(\nu\) is a point mass or a matching Cauchy-type
special case.

The distinction from formal Taylor extraction is also theorem-level.  Expanding
the quotient \(R_\varepsilon\) or its multivariable analogue gives candidate
modes only.  The entropy theorems prove that those modes may be averaged under
finite moments, that the quotient remainder is small in the weighted spaces
needed for KL, that centering removes the odd obstruction, and that the
Bregman Taylor expansion of \((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) has an integrable tail with
the stated \(o(t^{-2N})\) or finite-time remainder.  The explicit Laurent
calculus in dimension one is therefore the coefficient-evaluation layer of an
analytic finite-moment Poisson theorem, not the theorem by itself.

\section{Weighted trigonometric integrals}\label{app:entropy-integrals}

We collect the weighted identities used in the Laurent representatives and
constant-term table of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:n4-normalized-laurent-representatives} and
\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.  With
\(y=\tan\theta\), each identity is a zero-Fourier-mode extraction in an
elementary trigonometric product.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted Cayley mode integrals]\label{lem:entropy-integrals}
Let $u_2,\dots,u_6$ be the mode functions defined by
\eqref{eq:cayley-first-modes} and \eqref{eq:cayley-next-modes}.  Then
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac14,
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_3(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{3}{16},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_4(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac18,
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^3}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac{3}{32},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_6(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{3}{64},
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_3(y)u_5(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac{15}{128},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_4(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{5}{32},
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^2u_4(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{3}{32},
\]
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_3(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=-\frac{9}{128},
\qquad
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^4}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y=\frac{9}{64}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $y=\tan\theta$ with $\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$, so that
$\dd y/(1+y^2)=\dd\theta$, and write
\[
c_k(\theta):=\cos(2k\theta),
\qquad
s_k(\theta):=\sin(2k\theta).
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode},
\[
u_2(\tan\theta)=-\frac12(c_1+c_2),
\qquad
u_3(\tan\theta)=-\frac14(s_1+2s_2+s_3),
\]
\[
u_4(\tan\theta)=\frac18(c_1+3c_2+3c_3+c_4),
\qquad
u_5(\tan\theta)=\frac1{16}(s_1+4s_2+6s_3+4s_4+s_5),
\]
\[
u_6(\tan\theta)=-\frac1{32}(c_1+5c_2+10c_3+10c_4+5c_5+c_6).
\]
On $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ one has
\[
\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} c_k(\theta)^2\,\dd\theta
=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} s_k(\theta)^2\,\dd\theta
=\frac{\pi}{2}
\qquad(k\ge 1),
\]
and mixed products with different parity or different frequency
integrate to zero.  Thus each identity follows by expanding the
corresponding trigonometric polynomial and retaining only the constant
term.  Equivalently, if $Q_n(e^{2\ii\theta})=u_n(\tan\theta)$, then each
integral is the zero Laurent coefficient $[z^0]$ of the indicated product;
the coefficient-level constant terms are listed in
Lemma~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}.
For instance,
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)^2}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y
=\frac14\cdot\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}(c_1+c_2)^2\,\dd\theta
=\frac14,
\]
and
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{u_2(y)u_4(y)}{\pi(1+y^2)}\dd y
=-\frac1{16}\cdot\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}
(c_1+c_2)(c_1+3c_2+3c_3+c_4)\,\dd\theta
=-\frac18.
\]
The remaining identities are accounted for by the same zero-Fourier-coefficient
computation.
\end{proof}

The coefficient-level use of these identities is consolidated in
Lemmas~\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic} and
\ref{lem:n4-entropy-coefficient-substitution}; the computations above give
the elementary trigonometric verification of the weighted mode integrals.

\section{Doob--Bregman domain closure}\label{app:doob-bregman-domain}

This appendix contains the technical Doob-transform and domain-closure
material behind the KL tail identity, Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail}.
The compact KL facts needed to read the main entropy identity directly--the
quotient range, Lipschitz bound, Bregman majorant, and product-cutoff
convergence--are stated and proved in
Lemma~\ref{lem:kl-main-compact-domain}.  The statements below retain the
longer principal-value normalization, localized cutoff commutator estimates,
and range-local convex approximation needed for the full
Doob--Bregman identity with general convex \(\Phi\).
This material is auxiliary to the entropy coefficient expansion:
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} is proved earlier by the quotient-mode
Taylor expansion.  The present appendix supplies the domain closure used in
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}; there it yields the tail identity and the
dissipation-tail coefficient consequences
\eqref{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-expansion}--\eqref{eq:bregman-window-average-coefficients}.

\begin{lemma}[Two-point Bregman algebra for the Doob derivative]%
\label{lem:doob-bregman-algebra}
Let $g_x,g_y>0$ and $u_x,u_y>0$.  Let
$\Phi\in C^1((0,\infty))$, set
\[
G(u):=\Phi(u)-u\Phi'(u),
\qquad
D_\Phi(a,b):=\Phi(a)-\Phi(b)-\Phi'(b)(a-b).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-bregman-algebra}
\begin{split}
&(g_xu_x-g_yu_y)\bigl(\Phi'(u_x)-\Phi'(u_y)\bigr)
 +(g_x-g_y)\bigl(G(u_x)-G(u_y)\bigr)
\\
&\qquad
=g_xD_\Phi(u_x,u_y)+g_yD_\Phi(u_y,u_x).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Collect the $g_x$ and $g_y$ coefficients separately.  The coefficient of
$g_x$ on the left of \eqref{eq:doob-bregman-algebra} is
\[
u_x\bigl(\Phi'(u_x)-\Phi'(u_y)\bigr)+G(u_x)-G(u_y).
\]
Substituting $G(u)=\Phi(u)-u\Phi'(u)$ gives
\[
\Phi(u_x)-\Phi(u_y)-\Phi'(u_y)(u_x-u_y)
=D_\Phi(u_x,u_y).
\]
The coefficient of $g_y$ is
\[
-u_y\bigl(\Phi'(u_x)-\Phi'(u_y)\bigr)-G(u_x)+G(u_y)
=\Phi(u_y)-\Phi(u_x)-\Phi'(u_x)(u_y-u_x)
=D_\Phi(u_y,u_x).
\]
This proves the identity.  No analytic domain assertion enters this step.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Quotient-kernel formulas and cutoff-ready bounds]%
\label{lem:appendix-poisson-quotient-kernel-calculus}
For
\[
R_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\qquad w:=y-\varepsilon,
\]
one has
\begin{align}
R_\varepsilon(y)
&=1+\frac{2\varepsilon w+\varepsilon^2}{1+w^2},
\label{eq:appendix-R-eps-zero}\\
\partial_yR_\varepsilon(y)
&=\frac{2\varepsilon(1-w^2)-2\varepsilon^2w}{(1+w^2)^2},
\label{eq:appendix-R-eps-first}\\
\partial_y^2R_\varepsilon(y)
&=\frac{4\varepsilon w(w^2-3)+2\varepsilon^2(3w^2-1)}
        {(1+w^2)^3}.
\label{eq:appendix-R-eps-second}
\end{align}
Consequently, for all \(y,\varepsilon\in\RR\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-R-eps-bounds}
0<R_\varepsilon(y)\le2(1+\varepsilon^2),\qquad
R_\varepsilon(y)\ge\frac{1}{2(1+\varepsilon^2)},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appendix-R-eps-derivative-bounds}
|\partial_yR_\varepsilon(y)|\le3(1+\varepsilon^2),
\qquad
|\partial_y^2R_\varepsilon(y)|\le16(1+\varepsilon^2).
\end{equation}
Thus, if \(t\in[a,b]\), \(x=\bar\gamma+ty\), and
\(\varepsilon=(\gamma-\bar\gamma)/t\), then the corresponding Poisson quotient
obeys
\[
\|u_t\|_\infty\le2\left(1+\frac{\mu_2}{a^2}\right),\quad
\|\partial_xu_t\|_\infty
\le\frac{3}{a}\left(1+\frac{\mu_2}{a^2}\right),\quad
\|\partial_x^2u_t\|_\infty
\le\frac{16}{a^2}\left(1+\frac{\mu_2}{a^2}\right),
\]
where \(\mu_2=\int(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\dd\nu(\gamma)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(y=w+\varepsilon\),
\[
1+y^2=(1+w^2)+2\varepsilon w+\varepsilon^2,
\]
which gives \eqref{eq:appendix-R-eps-zero}.  Differentiating in \(w\) gives
\eqref{eq:appendix-R-eps-first} and \eqref{eq:appendix-R-eps-second}.  The
size bounds follow from
\[
1+y^2\le2\bigl(1+(y-\varepsilon)^2\bigr)+2\varepsilon^2,
\qquad
1+(y-\varepsilon)^2\le2(1+\varepsilon^2)(1+y^2).
\]
The derivative bounds follow from \eqref{eq:appendix-R-eps-first} and
\eqref{eq:appendix-R-eps-second} using
\[
\frac{|1-w^2|}{(1+w^2)^2},\
\frac{|w|}{(1+w^2)^2},\
\frac{|w|^3}{(1+w^2)^3},\
\frac{|w|}{(1+w^2)^3},\
\frac{w^2}{(1+w^2)^3},\
\frac{1}{(1+w^2)^3}
\le1
\]
and \(2|\varepsilon|\le1+\varepsilon^2\),
\(16|\varepsilon|+8\varepsilon^2\le16(1+\varepsilon^2)\).  Integrating
\(R_{(\gamma-\bar\gamma)/t}((x-\bar\gamma)/t)\) and its first two
\(x\)-derivatives against \(\nu\), and using \(t\ge a\), gives the stated
quotient bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cutoff justification for the Cauchy Doob calculus]%
\label{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus}
Let $[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)$ and let $\nu$ have finite centred second
moment.  For $t\in[a,b]$ put $h_t=P_t*\nu$, $g_t=P_t(\cdot-
\bar\gamma)$, and $u_t=h_t/g_t$.  Then the following assertions hold
uniformly for $t\in[a,b]$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item
      The functions $u_t$, $\partial_xu_t$, and $\partial_x^2u_t$ are
      bounded.  Moreover
      $h_t,g_t,\partial_xh_t,\partial_xg_t,\partial_th_t,\partial_tg_t$
      belong to $L^1(\RR)$, and
      \[
      \partial_th_t=Lh_t,
      \qquad
      \partial_tg_t=Lg_t
      \]
      pointwise, where $L$ is the principal-value Cauchy generator.
\item
      If $q$ is a bounded Lipschitz function and
      $p\in\{h_t,g_t\}$, then the bilinear Cauchy form
      \[
      \mathcal E(p,q)
      :=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
      \frac{(p(x)-p(y))(q(x)-q(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
      \]
      is absolutely convergent and
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff-cauchy-bilinear}
      \int_{\RR}q(x)Lp(x)\dd x=-\mathcal E(p,q).
      \end{equation}
      The identity is obtained as the limit of the compactly supported
      identities with $q$ replaced by $\chi_Rq$, where
      $\chi_R(x)=\chi(x/R)$, $\chi\in C_c^\infty(\RR)$,
      $\chi\equiv1$ on $[-1,1]$.  This fixes the domain convention used in
      the theorem: the left side is the $L^1$ pairing with the
      principal-value generator, and the right side is the absolutely
      convergent cutoff limit.
\item
      If $f$ is bounded $C^2$ with bounded first and second derivatives,
      in particular if $f=u_t$, then, with the same principal-value
      normalization,
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff-doob-product-rule}
      L(g_tf)(x)-f(x)Lg_t(x)
      =\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
      \frac{g_t(y)(f(y)-f(x))}{(x-y)^2}\dd y .
      \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:appendix-poisson-quotient-kernel-calculus} gives the asserted
uniform bounds for \(u_t\), \(\partial_xu_t\), and \(\partial_x^2u_t\) on
\([a,b]\).  The second-derivative bound is the input that puts \(u_t\) in the
pointwise product-rule domain below.

The $L^1$ assertions follow from convolution and scaling:
\[
\|\partial_xP_t\|_1\le C_a,
\qquad
\|\partial_tP_t\|_1\le C_a
\qquad(a\le t\le b).
\]
The identity $\partial_tP_t=LP_t$ is the one-dimensional Cauchy-semigroup
generator identity in the principal-value normalization used here
\cite[Definition~2.2 and Proposition~2.4]{Kwasnicki2017}.  Convolution with
the finite measure~$\nu$ and dominated convergence give
$\partial_th_t=Lh_t$; the same argument with the point mass at
$\bar\gamma$ gives $\partial_tg_t=Lg_t$.

For the bilinear identity, first take $q\in C_c^\infty(\RR)$.  The usual
principal-value formula for the Cauchy generator and symmetrization give
\eqref{eq:cutoff-cauchy-bilinear}.  The same compactly supported identity
holds for compactly supported Lipschitz tests by mollifying them and using the
estimates below.  For bounded Lipschitz $q$, replace it by
$q_R=\chi_Rq$, $R\ge1$.  Then
\[
\|q_R\|_\infty\le \|q\|_\infty,
\qquad
\|q_R\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}
\le \|q\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}+\|\chi'\|_\infty\|q\|_\infty,
\]
so the cutoff family has bounds independent of~$R$.  Since
$p,\partial_xp\in L^1(\RR)$ and $q$ is bounded and Lipschitz, the near
diagonal part satisfies, uniformly in~$R$,
\[
\iint_{|x-y|<1}
\frac{|p(x)-p(y)|\,|q_R(x)-q_R(y)|}{|x-y|^2}\dd x\dd y
\le 2\|q_R\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\|\partial_xp\|_1,
\]
because
$p(x)-p(y)=\int_0^1p'(y+s(x-y))(x-y)\dd s$.  The far part satisfies,
again uniformly in~$R$,
\[
\iint_{|x-y|\ge1}
\frac{|p(x)-p(y)|\,|q_R(x)-q_R(y)|}{|x-y|^2}\dd x\dd y
\le 8\|q\|_\infty\|p\|_1,
\]
since
$\int_{|x-y|\ge1}|x-y|^{-2}\dd y=2$ and the same estimate holds after
interchanging $x$ and~$y$.  It remains to show that the cutoff error tends to
zero.  Put $e_R:=q_R-q$.  Then $e_R=0$ on $[-R,R]$,
$\|e_R\|_\infty\le2\|q\|_\infty$, and
$\|e_R\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\le
\|q\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}+\|\chi'\|_\infty\|q\|_\infty$.  For the near
diagonal contribution, $e_R(x)-e_R(y)\ne0$ and $|x-y|<1$ imply that the line
segment between $x$ and~$y$ lies in $\{|z|>R-1\}$.  Therefore
\[
\iint_{|x-y|<1}
\frac{|p(x)-p(y)|\,|e_R(x)-e_R(y)|}{|x-y|^2}\dd x\dd y
\le C_q\int_{|z|>R-1}|p'(z)|\dd z\longrightarrow0.
\]
For the far contribution, using $|e_R(z)|\le2\|q\|_\infty\mathbf 1_{|z|>R}$
gives a sum of four terms.  The two terms in which the same variable carries
both $p$ and $e_R$ are bounded by
$4\|q\|_\infty\int_{|z|>R}|p(z)|\dd z$.  The cross terms are bounded by
\[
2\|q\|_\infty\int_{\RR}|p(x)|
\int_{\substack{|y|>R\\ |x-y|\ge1}}\frac{\dd y}{|x-y|^2}\dd x,
\]
and the analogous expression with $x$ and~$y$ interchanged; the inner integral
is bounded by~$2$ and tends to zero for each fixed outer variable, so dominated
convergence and $p\in L^1$ make the cross terms vanish.  Hence the bilinear
forms with $q_R$ converge to the form with~$q$.  On the one-variable side,
\[
|e_R(x)Lp(x)|\le 2\|q\|_\infty |Lp(x)|,
\qquad Lp=\partial_tp\in L^1(\RR),
\]
and $e_RLp\to0$ pointwise.  Hence the cutoff identities pass to the limit and
prove \eqref{eq:cutoff-cauchy-bilinear} for bounded Lipschitz~$q$.

Finally apply the principal-value formula to $g_tf$ and to $g_t$.  In the
truncated integrals over $|x-y|>\rho$ the difference is exactly
\[
\frac1\pi\int_{|x-y|>\rho}
\frac{g_t(y)(f(y)-f(x))}{(x-y)^2}\dd y .
\]
The integrand has a finite principal-value limit because near $y=x$ the
linear part contributes an odd kernel and the remaining term is bounded,
while away from $x$ it is integrable.  Letting $\rho\downarrow0$ gives
\eqref{eq:cutoff-doob-product-rule}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Non-autonomous Doob transform]%
\label{thm:poisson-doob-phi-entropy}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$ and
finite centred second moment.  For $t>0$ define
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),
\qquad
u_t(x):=\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}.
\]
Let
\[
(Lf)(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y
\]
be the one-dimensional Cauchy generator, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-generator-definition}
(\mathcal L_t f)(x)
:=\frac{1}{g_t(x)}\bigl(L(g_tf)(x)-f(x)Lg_t(x)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For the displayed formulae below the domain of \(\mathcal L_t\) is the class
of bounded \(C^2\) functions with bounded first and second derivatives; the
Green identity is asserted on \(C_c^\infty(\RR)\).  The cutoff interpretation
is the one fixed in Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus}.  Then the
following assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item
      The function $u_t$ is bounded and belongs to $C^\infty(\RR)$ for
      every $t>0$.  More precisely, for every $a>0$ and every $t\ge a$,
      \[
      \frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)}
      \le u_t(x)\le
      2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},
      \qquad
      \mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\dd\nu(\gamma).
      \]
      Moreover,
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-flow-equation}
      \partial_tu_t=\mathcal L_tu_t
      \qquad\text{on }\RR\times(0,\infty),
      \end{equation}
      and \eqref{eq:doob-generator-definition} may be written explicitly
      as
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-generator-kernel}
      (\mathcal L_tf)(x)
      =\frac{1}{\pi g_t(x)}\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
      \frac{g_t(y)\bigl(f(y)-f(x)\bigr)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y.
      \end{equation}
\item
      For every $f,\psi\in C_c^\infty(\RR)$ one has the weighted Green
      identity
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-green-identity}
      \begin{split}
      \int_{\RR}f(x)(\mathcal L_t\psi)(x)\,g_t(x)^2\dd x
      &=
      -\frac{1}{2\pi}
      \iint_{\RR^2}
      \frac{g_t(x)g_t(y)}{(x-y)^2}
      \\
      &\qquad\qquad\times
      \bigl(f(x)-f(y)\bigr)\bigl(\psi(x)-\psi(y)\bigr)\dd x\dd y.
      \end{split}
      \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $t>0$ the functions $h_t$ and $g_t$ are positive and belong to
$C^\infty(\RR)$, because they are obtained by convolving the smooth
Poisson kernel with a probability measure.  Write
$X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma$, $x=\bar\gamma+ty$, and
$\varepsilon=X_c/t$.  Then
\[
u_t(x)=\int_{\RR}R_{X_c/t}(y)\dd\nu(\gamma),
\qquad
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}.
\]
The elementary estimate
\[
1+y^2=1+\bigl((y-\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\bigr)^2
\le 2\bigl(1+(y-\varepsilon)^2\bigr)+2\varepsilon^2
\]
gives
\[
0<R_\varepsilon(y)\le 2+2\varepsilon^2
\qquad(y,\varepsilon\in\RR),
\]
and therefore
\[
0<u_t(x)\le 2+\frac{2\mu_2}{t^2},
\qquad
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
Likewise,
\[
1+(y-\varepsilon)^2
\le 2\bigl(1+y^2\bigr)+2\varepsilon^2
\le 2(1+\varepsilon^2)(1+y^2),
\]
so
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)\ge \frac{1}{2(1+\varepsilon^2)}
\qquad(y,\varepsilon\in\RR).
\]
Hence
\[
u_t(x)\ge \frac12\int_{\RR}\frac{1}{1+(X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
\ge \frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/t^2)},
\]
where the last inequality follows from Jensen's inequality applied to the
convex function $s\mapsto(1+s)^{-1}$ on $[0,\infty)$.  Thus $u_t$ is
bounded for every fixed $t>0$.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-semigroup-law},
$\partial_t h_t=-|D|h_t$ and $\partial_t g_t=-|D|g_t$.  The cutoff
calculus of Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus} identifies these
derivatives with $Lh_t$ and $Lg_t$ in the principal-value sense and permits
the product-rule passage below.  Since $h_t=u_tg_t$ and $g_t(x)>0$,
\[
\partial_tu_t(x)
=\frac{Lh_t(x)-u_t(x)Lg_t(x)}{g_t(x)}.
\]
Substituting $h_t=u_tg_t$ in
\eqref{eq:cutoff-doob-product-rule} yields
\[
\partial_tu_t(x)
=\frac{1}{\pi g_t(x)}\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{g_t(y)\bigl(u_t(y)-u_t(x)\bigr)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y.
\]
This is \eqref{eq:quotient-flow-equation} and proves
\eqref{eq:doob-generator-kernel}; part~\textup{(i)} follows.

For part~\textup{(ii)}, let $f,\psi\in C_c^\infty(\RR)$.  The truncated
integrals in Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus} justify using
\eqref{eq:doob-generator-kernel} under the outer integral, and hence
\[
\int_{\RR}f(x)(\mathcal L_t\psi)(x)\,g_t(x)^2\dd x
=
\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{f(x)g_t(x)g_t(y)\bigl(\psi(y)-\psi(x)\bigr)}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y.
\]
Interchanging $x$ and $y$ in the same integral and averaging the two
representations gives \eqref{eq:doob-green-identity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Compact quotient range and Bregman integrability]%
\label{lem:doob-range-bregman-integrability}
Let $[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)$ and let $\nu$ be a probability measure
on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$ and finite centred second moment
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
Put $h_t=P_t*\nu$, $g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)$, and
$u_t=h_t/g_t$.  Then, for every $t\in[a,b]$ and every $x\in\RR$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-compact-range-bound}
\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)}
\le u_t(x)\le
2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2}.
\end{equation}
Moreover $u_t$ is uniformly Lipschitz in $x$ on
$\RR\times[a,b]$.  Consequently, if
$\Phi:(0,\infty)\to\RR$ is convex, of class $C^1$, and $\Phi'$ is
Lipschitz on compact subintervals of $(0,\infty)$, then there is a
constant $C_{\Phi,a,b}<\infty$ such that, for all $t\in[a,b]$ and
$x,y\in\RR$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-bregman-pointwise-bound}
0\le D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le C_{\Phi,a,b}\min\{|x-y|^2,1\}.
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doob-bregman-integrability-bound}
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}
\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{
g_t(x)D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)D_\Phi(u_t(y),u_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y<\infty,
\end{equation}
and $\int_{\RR}g_t(x)|\Phi(u_t(x))|\dd x<\infty$ for every
$t\in[a,b]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma$ and $y=(x-\bar\gamma)/t$.  Since
\[
\frac{P_t(x-\gamma)}{P_t(x-\bar\gamma)}
=R_{X_c/t}(y),
\qquad
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\]
we have $u_t(x)=\int R_{X_c/t}(y)\dd\nu(\gamma)$.  The elementary
inequality
\[
1+y^2\le 2\bigl(1+(y-\varepsilon)^2\bigr)+2\varepsilon^2
\]
gives $R_\varepsilon(y)\le2+2\varepsilon^2$, hence
\[
u_t(x)\le 2+2\mu_2/t^2\le 2+2\mu_2/a^2 .
\]
Conversely,
\[
1+(y-\varepsilon)^2
\le2(1+y^2)+2\varepsilon^2
\le2(1+\varepsilon^2)(1+y^2),
\]
so $R_\varepsilon(y)\ge[2(1+\varepsilon^2)]^{-1}$.  Therefore
\[
u_t(x)
\ge \frac12\int_{\RR}\frac{1}{1+(X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
\ge \frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/t^2)}
\ge \frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},
\]
where the middle inequality is Jensen's inequality for the convex
function $s\mapsto(1+s)^{-1}$ on $[0,\infty)$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:doob-compact-range-bound}.

Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus} gives a constant
$L_{a,b}$ such that $|\partial_xu_t(x)|\le L_{a,b}$ for
$t\in[a,b]$.  Let
\[
m_{a,b}:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},
\qquad
M_{a,b}:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2}.
\]
If $\Phi'$ has Lipschitz constant $L_\Phi$ on
$[m_{a,b},M_{a,b}]$, then the integral form of the Bregman remainder gives
\[
0\le D_\Phi(\alpha,\beta)\le \frac{L_\Phi}{2}|\alpha-\beta|^2
\qquad(m_{a,b}\le\alpha,\beta\le M_{a,b}).
\]
Combining this with the Lipschitz bound for $u_t$ and with the trivial
bound $|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le M_{a,b}-m_{a,b}$ proves
\eqref{eq:doob-bregman-pointwise-bound}.

Dividing \eqref{eq:doob-bregman-pointwise-bound} by $(x-y)^2$ gives the
majorant $C_{\Phi,a,b}\min\{1,|x-y|^{-2}\}$.  Since
\[
\int_{\RR}\min\{1,|x-y|^{-2}\}\dd y<\infty
\]
with a value independent of~$x$, and since $\int g_t=1$, the first term
in \eqref{eq:doob-bregman-integrability-bound} is bounded uniformly in
$t\in[a,b]$; the second term is identical after exchanging $x$ and~$y$.
Finally, \eqref{eq:doob-compact-range-bound} puts $u_t$ in a compact
subinterval of $(0,\infty)$, so $\Phi$ is bounded on the range and
$\int g_t|\Phi(u_t)|\le\sup_{[m_{a,b},M_{a,b}]}|\Phi|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Compact-range approximation for range-local $C^{1,1}$ entropies]%
\label{lem:compact-range-phi-approximation}
Let $[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)$, and let $h_t$, $g_t$, and $u_t=h_t/g_t$ be as
in Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-range-bregman-integrability}.  Let
$J=[m_{a,b},M_{a,b}]$ be the compact interval from its proof.  If
$\Phi:(0,\infty)\to\RR$ is convex, $C^1$, and $\Phi'$ is Lipschitz on compact
subintervals of $(0,\infty)$, then there are convex functions
$\Phi_\varepsilon\in C^\infty((0,\infty))$, defined for all sufficiently small
$\varepsilon>0$, such that
\[
\sup_{s\in J}|\Phi_\varepsilon(s)-\Phi(s)|
+\sup_{s\in J}|\Phi_\varepsilon'(s)-\Phi'(s)|\longrightarrow0,
\]
the functions $\Phi_\varepsilon'$ have a common Lipschitz constant on~$J$, and
\[
\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in J}
|D_{\Phi_\varepsilon}(\alpha,\beta)-D_\Phi(\alpha,\beta)|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently the entropy functionals and the Bregman dissipation integrands in
Proposition~\ref{prop:doob-bregman-domain-closure} may be obtained as limits
from the same formulas for the smooth convex functions $\Phi_\varepsilon$,
with the same compact-range majorants on every time interval $[a,b]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose an open interval $J_*$ with compact closure in $(0,\infty)$ and
$J\subset J_*$.  Since $\Phi$ is convex and $C^1$, $\Phi'$ is nondecreasing;
by hypothesis it has some Lipschitz constant $L_*$ on~$J_*$.  Extend
$\Phi'|_{J_*}$ to a globally defined nondecreasing Lipschitz function
$\Psi$ on~$\RR$ with Lipschitz constant $L_*$, for instance by continuing it
linearly with slope~$0$ outside $J_*$.  Let $\rho_\varepsilon$ be a standard
nonnegative even mollifier and set
$\Psi_\varepsilon=\rho_\varepsilon*\Psi$.  For $\varepsilon$ smaller than the
distance from $J$ to $\RR\setminus J_*$, the functions
$\Psi_\varepsilon$ are smooth, nondecreasing, have Lipschitz constant at most
$L_*$ on~$J$, and converge uniformly to $\Phi'$ on~$J$.
Define
\[
\Phi_\varepsilon(s):=\Phi(1)+\int_1^s\Psi_\varepsilon(r)\dd r,
\]
after any smooth convex extension away from $J_*$ if necessary.  Then
$\Phi_\varepsilon$ is smooth and convex on $(0,\infty)$, and the displayed
uniform convergence of $\Phi_\varepsilon$ and $\Phi_\varepsilon'$ on~$J$
follows by integrating the uniform convergence of the derivatives on the
compact interval.  The Bregman convergence is immediate from
\[
|D_{\Phi_\varepsilon}(\alpha,\beta)-D_\Phi(\alpha,\beta)|
\le 2\sup_J|\Phi_\varepsilon-\Phi|
 +(M_{a,b}-m_{a,b})\sup_J|\Phi_\varepsilon'-\Phi'|.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-range-bregman-integrability} gives the common bound
$0\le D_{\Phi_\varepsilon}(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le C\min\{|x-y|^2,1\}$ with $C$ independent of~$\varepsilon$, and it also
puts $u_t$ in~$J$.  Since $\int g_t=1$, dominated convergence applies to the
one-variable entropy integrals and to the two-variable Bregman dissipation
integrals.  This proves the limiting assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Smooth compactly supported Doob--Bregman identity]%
\label{prop:smooth-compact-doob-bregman}
Let $t\mapsto g_t$ be a positive $C^1$ curve of smooth densities on an open
time interval, and let $t\mapsto u_t$ be a positive $C^1$ curve of smooth
functions such that $u_t-1\in C_c^\infty(\RR)$ for each $t$, with one compact
support and one compact quotient range on compact time intervals.  Put
$h_t=g_tu_t$ and assume
\[
\partial_t h_t=Lh_t,\qquad \partial_tg_t=Lg_t,
\]
with all integrations below absolutely convergent; for instance this holds
after replacing the noncompact tests in Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus}
by fixed spatial cutoffs.  Let $\Phi\in C^2((0,\infty))$ with $\Phi(1)=0$.
Then
\[
\mathcal H_\Phi(t):=\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\Phi(u_t(x))\dd x
\]
is $C^1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth-compact-phi-entropy-dissipation}
-\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\mathcal H_\Phi(t)
=
\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{
g_t(x)D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)D_\Phi(u_t(y),u_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $u_t-1$ has compact support and $\Phi(1)=0$, the entropy integrand is
supported where $u_t\ne1$.  Differentiating under the integral and using
$h_t=g_tu_t$ gives
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\mathcal H_\Phi(t)
=
\int_{\RR}\Phi'(u_t)\,Lh_t\,\dd x
+
\int_{\RR}\bigl(\Phi(u_t)-u_t\Phi'(u_t)\bigr)Lg_t\,\dd x .
\]
The functions $\Phi'(u_t)-\Phi'(1)$ and
$Q_\Phi(u_t)-Q_\Phi(1)$ are smooth and compactly supported, and constants do
not change the bilinear form.  The constant pairings with $Lh_t$ and $Lg_t$
vanish because the generator preserves total mass.  Applying the compactly
supported Cauchy bilinear identity
\[
\int_{\RR}qLp\,\dd x
=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{(p(x)-p(y))(q(x)-q(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\]
first with $p=h_t=g_tu_t$, $q=\Phi'(u_t)$, and then with
$p=g_t$, $q=Q_\Phi(u_t)$, where
$Q_\Phi(s)=\Phi(s)-s\Phi'(s)$.  Thus
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\mathcal H_\Phi(t)
=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}\frac{E_t(x,y)}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y,
\]
where, with $u_x=u_t(x)$, $u_y=u_t(y)$, $g_x=g_t(x)$, and $g_y=g_t(y)$,
\[
E_t(x,y)
=(g_xu_x-g_yu_y)\bigl(\Phi'(u_x)-\Phi'(u_y)\bigr)
 +(g_x-g_y)\bigl(Q_\Phi(u_x)-Q_\Phi(u_y)\bigr).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-bregman-algebra} rewrites this exactly as
\[
E_t(x,y)=g_t(x)D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)D_\Phi(u_t(y),u_t(x)).
\]
Substitution gives \eqref{eq:smooth-compact-phi-entropy-dissipation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Doob--Bregman domain bounds]%
\label{lem:doob-bregman-domain-bounds}
Let $[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)$, let $\nu$ have finite centred second moment, and
use the notation
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma),
\qquad
u_t=h_t/g_t .
\]
Let $\Phi$ be convex on $(0,\infty)$ and assume that, on every compact
subinterval of $(0,\infty)$, $\Phi$ is $C^1$ and $\Phi'$ is Lipschitz.  Then,
uniformly for $t\in[a,b]$, the following analytic bounds hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item The principal-value identities for $Lh_t$, $Lg_t$, and
$L(g_tu_t)-u_tLg_t$ are the limits of the cutoff identities in
Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus}; the associated bilinear Cauchy
forms are absolutely convergent.
\item The quotient range $u_t(\RR)$ is contained in one compact interval
$J_{a,b}\Subset(0,\infty)$, and the Bregman integrand
\[
\frac{g_t(x)D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))
      +g_t(y)D_\Phi(u_t(y),u_t(x))}{(x-y)^2}
\]
is absolutely integrable on $\RR^2$, with a bound independent of
$t\in[a,b]$.  More explicitly, there is
$C_{\Phi,a,b}<\infty$ such that, for
\[
K_t^\Phi(x,y):=
\frac{g_t(x)D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2},
\qquad x\ne y,
\]
one has
\begin{align*}
0\le K_t^\Phi(x,y)
&\le C_{\Phi,a,b}g_t(x)\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|<1\}},\\
0\le K_t^\Phi(x,y)
&\le C_{\Phi,a,b}g_t(x)|x-y|^{-2}
\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|\ge1\}},
\end{align*}
and the same two bounds with \(x\) and \(y\) exchanged.  These are
\(L^1([a,b]\times\RR^2)\) majorants and therefore remove the symmetric
cutoffs \(\chi_R(x)\chi_R(y)\) by dominated convergence.
\item The map
\[
t\longmapsto \mathcal H_\Phi(t):=
\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\Phi(u_t(x))\dd x
\]
is absolutely continuous on $[a,b]$, and its derivative is obtained by
first proving the identity for the cutoff bilinear forms and then letting the
cutoff radius tend to infinity.
\item The passage from smooth convex entropies to the above range-local
$C^{1,1}$ class is justified by the approximants
$\Phi_\varepsilon$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:compact-range-phi-approximation}; on
$J_{a,b}$ the entropies, first derivatives, and Bregman divergences converge
under the same integrable majorants as in~\textup{(ii)}.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the dissipation formula
\eqref{eq:phi-entropy-dissipation} is a domain statement in the stated
principal-value normalization, not a formal application of a nonlocal product
rule.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Part (i) is exactly Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus}.  That lemma
first proves the compactly supported Cauchy-generator identities, then replaces
the test function by $\chi_Rq$ and passes to the limit using the near-diagonal
estimate controlled by $\partial_xp\in L^1$ and the far-field estimate
controlled by $p\in L^1$.  Thus the limiting forms are absolutely convergent
and fix the principal-value convention used here.

Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-range-bregman-integrability} proves the compact quotient
range.  Since \(\Phi'\) is Lipschitz on that range, the integral form of the
Bregman remainder gives
\[
0\le D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le C_{\Phi,a,b}|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|^2.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:appendix-poisson-quotient-kernel-calculus} give
\(|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L_{a,b}|x-y|\), while compactness of the quotient range
also gives \(|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le M_{a,b}-m_{a,b}\).  Hence
\[
0\le D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le C_{\Phi,a,b}\min\{|x-y|^2,1\}.
\]
Dividing by \((x-y)^2\) gives exactly the displayed near-diagonal and
far-field bounds for \(K_t^\Phi\), and the exchanged-variable estimates are
identical.  They are integrable because
\[
\int_a^b\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\dd x\dd t=b-a,\qquad
\int_{|z|<1}\dd z=2,\qquad
\int_{|z|\ge1}|z|^{-2}\dd z=2 .
\]
This proves (ii), including the cutoff convergence by dominated convergence.

For (iii), Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus} gives uniform
$L^1$ bounds for $\partial_th_t$ and $\partial_tg_t$ on $[a,b]$.  Thus
$h,g,\partial_th,\partial_tg\in L^1([a,b]\times\RR)$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-range-bregman-integrability} gives
\[
m_{a,b}\le h_t(x)/g_t(x)\le M_{a,b}
\qquad(t\in[a,b],\ x\in\RR).
\]
The Poisson kernels are \(C^1\) in \(t\) pointwise, so \(h_t(x)\) and
\(g_t(x)\) are absolutely continuous in \(t\) for every fixed \(x\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:l1-bounded-quotient-entropy-chain-rule}, applied with
\(X=\RR\), \(F(h,g)=g\Phi(h/g)\), and
\([m,M]=[m_{a,b},M_{a,b}]\), therefore gives
\(\mathcal H_\Phi\in AC([a,b])\) and, for a.e. \(t\),
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\mathcal H_\Phi(t)
=
\int_{\RR}\Phi'(u_t)\,\partial_th_t\dd x
+
\int_{\RR}\bigl(\Phi(u_t)-u_t\Phi'(u_t)\bigr)\partial_tg_t\dd x .
\]
Its localized version gives the same formula with any fixed spatial cutoff.
Applying the cutoff identity from part (i) to the bounded Lipschitz tests
\(\Phi'(u_t)\) and \(\Phi(u_t)-u_t\Phi'(u_t)\) gives the displayed derivative
at the cutoff level; the absolute majorants from parts (i) and (ii) justify
passage to the limit in the cutoff radius.

Finally, Lemma~\ref{lem:compact-range-phi-approximation} constructs smooth
convex $\Phi_\varepsilon$ that agree with $\Phi$ to first order on the compact
range in the limit and have uniformly bounded second derivatives there.  The
one-variable entropy terms are dominated by $g_t$ times a constant, and the
two-variable Bregman terms are dominated by the majorant from (ii).  Dominated
convergence therefore passes the smooth identity to the range-local
$C^{1,1}$ entropy.  This proves all four assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Domain closure of the Doob--Bregman identity]%
\label{prop:doob-bregman-domain-closure}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$ and
finite centred second moment, and use the notation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-doob-phi-entropy}.  Let
$\Phi:(0,\infty)\to\RR$ be convex, of class $C^1$, with range-local
$C^{1,1}$ control in the sense that $\Phi'$ is Lipschitz on every compact
subinterval of $(0,\infty)$, and assume $\Phi(1)=0$.  Define
\[
\mathcal H_\Phi(t):=\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\Phi(u_t(x))\dd x .
\]
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\).  Then there are constants
\[
0<m_{a,b}\le M_{a,b}<\infty,\qquad L_{a,b}<\infty,\qquad
C_{\Phi,a,b}<\infty,
\]
depending only on \(a,b,\Phi\) and the centred second moment of \(\nu\), such
that, for all \(t\in[a,b]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-domain-core-range}
m_{a,b}\le u_t(x)\le M_{a,b},\qquad
|\partial_xu_t(x)|\le L_{a,b}\qquad(x\in\RR),
\end{equation}
and, for \(x\ne y\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-domain-core-majorant}
\frac{g_t(x)D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{\Phi,a,b}g_t(x)\min\{1,|x-y|^{-2}\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\mathcal H_\Phi(t)<\infty$ for every $t>0$, and
$t\mapsto\mathcal H_\Phi(t)$ is absolutely continuous on compact
subintervals of $(0,\infty)$.  If
\(\chi\in C_c^\infty(\RR)\), \(0\le\chi\le1\), \(\chi\equiv1\) on
\([-1,1]\), and \(\chi_R(x)=\chi(x/R)\), then
\[
H_R(t):=\int_{\RR}\chi_R(x)g_t(x)\Phi(u_t(x))\dd x
\]
satisfies \(H_R\in AC([a,b])\), \(H_R\to\mathcal H_\Phi\) uniformly on
\([a,b]\), and there are \(e_R\in L^1([a,b])\) with
\(\|e_R\|_{L^1([a,b])}\to0\) such that, for a.e. \(t\in[a,b]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-domain-localized-cutoff-balance}
\begin{split}
-\frac{\dd}{\dd t}H_R(t)
&=
\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\chi_R(x)\chi_R(y)
\frac{
g_t(x)D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)D_\Phi(u_t(y),u_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
+e_R(t).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Passing \(R\to\infty\) gives, for a.e. \(t\in[a,b]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-entropy-dissipation}
-\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\mathcal H_\Phi(t)
=
\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{
g_t(x)D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)D_\Phi(u_t(y),u_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y,
\end{equation}
where
\[
D_\Phi(a,b):=\Phi(a)-\Phi(b)-\Phi'(b)(a-b)
\qquad(a,b>0).
\]
The right side is the finite cutoff-limit integral fixed by
Lemmas~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus},
\ref{lem:doob-range-bregman-integrability}, and
\ref{lem:doob-bregman-domain-bounds}.  The convergence of the two-variable
cutoff integrals to the right side of \eqref{eq:phi-entropy-dissipation}
holds in \(L^1([a,b])\).  In particular \(t\mapsto\mathcal H_\Phi(t)\) is
nonincreasing.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-range-bregman-integrability}
places $u_t(\RR)$, $t\in[a,b]$, in the compact interval
\[
J_{a,b}:=[m_{a,b},M_{a,b}]
\Subset(0,\infty),
\qquad
m_{a,b}:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\quad
M_{a,b}:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},
\]
and Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus} supplies
\[
|\partial_xu_t(x)|\le L_{a,b}\qquad(t\in[a,b]).
\]
Since \(\Phi'\) is Lipschitz on \(J_{a,b}\), the integral form of the
Bregman remainder and the Lipschitz bound give
\[
0\le D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le C_{\Phi,a,b}\min\{|x-y|^2,1\}.
\]
Dividing by \((x-y)^2\) proves the one-sided majorant
\eqref{eq:phi-domain-core-majorant}; the same estimate with \(x,y\)
exchanged controls the second Bregman term.  These bounds prove
\(\mathcal H_\Phi(t)<\infty\) and give the uniform Bregman double-integral
bound \eqref{eq:doob-bregman-integrability-bound}.
For the cutoff limit we shall use the following explicit decomposition of the
same majorant.  With
\[
K_t^\Phi(x,y):=
\frac{g_t(x)D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2},
\qquad x\ne y,
\]
one has, uniformly in \(t\in[a,b]\),
\begin{align}
0\le K_t^\Phi(x,y)
&\le C_{\Phi,a,b}g_t(x)\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|<1\}},
\label{eq:phi-cutoff-near-majorant}\\
0\le K_t^\Phi(x,y)
&\le C_{\Phi,a,b}g_t(x)|x-y|^{-2}
\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|\ge1\}} .
\label{eq:phi-cutoff-far-majorant}
\end{align}
The corresponding estimates for
\(g_t(y)D_\Phi(u_t(y),u_t(x))/(x-y)^2\) are obtained by exchanging the
variables.  These are actual \(L^1([a,b]\times\RR^2)\) majorants, since
\[
\int_a^b\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\dd x\dd t=b-a,\qquad
\int_{|z|<1}\dd z=2,\qquad
\int_{|z|\ge1}|z|^{-2}\dd z=2 .
\]
The closure argument below deliberately separates the proof into three
steps.  First,
Proposition~\ref{prop:smooth-compact-doob-bregman} proves the formula for
smooth compactly supported quotients, with
Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-bregman-algebra} as the only nontrivial algebraic
identity.  Second, the cutoff estimates of
Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus} pass the bilinear forms from
compactly supported tests to the Poisson quotient.  Third, the compact-range
approximation of Lemma~\ref{lem:compact-range-phi-approximation} passes from
smooth convex entropies to the stated range-local $C^{1,1}$ class.

Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus} gives uniform $L^1$ bounds for
$\partial_th_t$ and $\partial_tg_t$, so $t\mapsto h_t$ and $t\mapsto g_t$ are
absolutely continuous as $L^1$-valued maps on $[a,b]$.  Together with the
compact quotient range \(m_{a,b}\le h_t/g_t\le M_{a,b}\), these are precisely
the integrability and range hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:l1-bounded-quotient-entropy-chain-rule}.  That lemma gives
\(\mathcal H_\Phi\in AC([a,b])\) and, for a.e. \(t\),
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\mathcal H_\Phi(t)
=
\int_{\RR}\Phi'(u_t)\,\partial_th_t\dd x
+
\int_{\RR}\bigl(\Phi(u_t)-u_t\Phi'(u_t)\bigr)\partial_tg_t\dd x.
\]
It also gives the same derivative formula after inserting a fixed cutoff
\(\chi_R\), and
\[
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}
\left|
\int_{\RR}(1-\chi_R(x))g_t(x)\Phi(u_t(x))\dd x
\right|
\le
\sup_{J_{a,b}}|\Phi|
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\int_{\RR}(1-\chi_R(x))g_t(x)\dd x
\to0,
\]
where the final convergence follows from the explicit translated Poisson
kernel \(g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma)\), uniformly for \(t\in[a,b]\).
Substituting $\partial_th_t=L(u_tg_t)$ and $\partial_tg_t=Lg_t$, and applying
the cutoff bilinear identity \eqref{eq:cutoff-cauchy-bilinear} with the
bounded Lipschitz tests $\Phi'(u_t)$ and
$\Phi(u_t)-u_t\Phi'(u_t)$, gives the cutoff-limit version of the smooth
identity in Proposition~\ref{prop:smooth-compact-doob-bregman}.  The algebraic
step in that passage is precisely Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-bregman-algebra}, with
\[
u_x=u_t(x),\qquad u_y=u_t(y),\qquad g_x=g_t(x),\qquad g_y=g_t(y).
\]
Thus the cutoff limit is the Bregman integral in
\eqref{eq:phi-entropy-dissipation}.

The same argument with the cutoff inserted gives the localized statement.
For every \(R\ge1\),
\[
H_R(t):=\int_{\RR}\chi_R(x)g_t(x)\Phi(u_t(x))\dd x
\]
is absolutely continuous on \([a,b]\), and for a.e. \(t\)
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}H_R(t)
=
\int_{\RR}\chi_R(x)\Phi'(u_t(x))\,\partial_th_t(x)\dd x
+\int_{\RR}\chi_R(x)
\bigl(\Phi(u_t(x))-u_t(x)\Phi'(u_t(x))\bigr)\partial_tg_t(x)\dd x .
\]
Applying the cutoff bilinear identity to the bounded Lipschitz tests
\(\chi_R\Phi'(u_t)\) and
\(\chi_R(\Phi(u_t)-u_t\Phi'(u_t))\), and then using
Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-bregman-algebra}, gives the symmetric Bregman expression
with the product cutoff \(\chi_R(x)\chi_R(y)\), plus the commutator
\(e_R(t)\) whose exact formula is
\eqref{eq:phi-cutoff-commutator-error-formula}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-bregman-cutoff-commutator} splits this error into the
near-diagonal part \eqref{eq:phi-cutoff-commutator-near} and the far-field
part \eqref{eq:phi-cutoff-commutator-far}; the near estimate uses the
\(L^1\)-bounds for \(\partial_xh_t,\partial_xg_t\), and the far estimate uses
the probability-density bounds for \(h_t,g_t\) together with
\(\int_{|z|\ge1}|z|^{-2}\dd z<\infty\).  Its conclusion
\eqref{eq:phi-cutoff-commutator-l1-vanishing} gives
\(\|e_R\|_{L^1([a,b])}\to0\), and
\eqref{eq:phi-domain-localized-cutoff-balance} follows.  The same compact
range also gives
\[
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}|H_R(t)-\mathcal H_\Phi(t)|
\le \sup_{J_{a,b}}|\Phi|
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\int_{\RR}|1-\chi_R(x)|g_t(x)\dd x\to0,
\]
so \(H_R\to\mathcal H_\Phi\) uniformly on \([a,b]\).

The remaining passages are exactly the closure statements in
Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-bregman-domain-bounds}.  The cutoff convergence follows
from dominated convergence with the explicit decomposition
\[
K_t^\Phi=K_t^\Phi\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|<1\}}
        +K_t^\Phi\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|\ge1\}},
\]
the near-diagonal majorant \eqref{eq:phi-cutoff-near-majorant}, the far-field
majorant \eqref{eq:phi-cutoff-far-majorant}, and their exchanged-variable
counterparts.  These are the exact majorants used above to prove
\[
\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}(1-\chi_R(x)\chi_R(y))K_t^\Phi(x,y)
\,\dd x\,\dd y\,\dd t\longrightarrow0
\]
for the first Bregman term, with the same argument after exchanging \(x\) and
\(y\).  Absolute convergence is the bound
\eqref{eq:doob-bregman-integrability-bound}; and the passage from smooth
convex entropies to the present range-local $C^{1,1}$ class uses the
approximants $\Phi_\varepsilon$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:compact-range-phi-approximation}.  Their first derivatives have
a common Lipschitz constant on the compact quotient range, so the same
one-variable and Bregman majorants dominate both sides and dominated
convergence lets $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.  Convexity gives
$D_\Phi\ge0$, hence monotonicity.  Since $[a,b]$ was arbitrary, the proposition
follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Localized cutoff commutator for the Doob--Bregman identity]%
\label{lem:doob-bregman-cutoff-commutator}
Let the hypotheses and notation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-bregman-domain-bounds} hold on
\([a,b]\Subset(0,\infty)\).  Put
\[
A_t(x):=\Phi'(u_t(x)),\qquad
G_t(x):=\Phi(u_t(x))-u_t(x)\Phi'(u_t(x)),
\]
and, for \(x\ne y\),
\begin{align*}
\mathcal B_t^\Phi(x,y)
&:=
g_t(x)D_\Phi(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)D_\Phi(u_t(y),u_t(x)),\\
\mathcal M_t^\Phi(x,y)
&:=
\bigl(h_t(x)-h_t(y)\bigr)A_t(y)
+\bigl(g_t(x)-g_t(y)\bigr)G_t(y).
\end{align*}
Let \(\chi_R(x)=\chi(x/R)\), where
\(\chi\in C_c^\infty(\RR)\), \(0\le\chi\le1\), and
\(\chi\equiv1\) on \([-1,1]\).  Define
\[
\mathcal C_R^\Phi(t,x,y)
:=
\chi_R(x)(1-\chi_R(y))\mathcal B_t^\Phi(x,y)
+(\chi_R(x)-\chi_R(y))\mathcal M_t^\Phi(x,y).
\]
Then the cutoff error in the localized entropy balance is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-cutoff-commutator-error-formula}
e_R(t)=
\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{\mathcal C_R^\Phi(t,x,y)}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\end{equation}
with the absolutely convergent near/far splitting
\begin{align}
e_R^{<}(t)
&:=
\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{|x-y|<1}
\frac{\mathcal C_R^\Phi(t,x,y)}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y,
\label{eq:phi-cutoff-commutator-near}\\
e_R^{>}(t)
&:=
\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{|x-y|\ge1}
\frac{\mathcal C_R^\Phi(t,x,y)}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y.
\label{eq:phi-cutoff-commutator-far}
\end{align}
Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-cutoff-commutator-l1-vanishing}
\int_a^b\bigl(|e_R^{<}(t)|+|e_R^{>}(t)|\bigr)\dd t
\le
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{|\mathcal C_R^\Phi(t,x,y)|}{|x-y|^2}\dd x\dd y\dd t
\longrightarrow0 .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(u_x=u_t(x)\), \(u_y=u_t(y)\), \(g_x=g_t(x)\),
\(g_y=g_t(y)\), \(h_x=g_xu_x\), \(h_y=g_yu_y\),
\(A_x=A_t(x)\), \(A_y=A_t(y)\), \(G_x=G_t(x)\), and \(G_y=G_t(y)\).
The cutoff bilinear computation for
\(\frac{\dd}{\dd t}H_R(t)\) produces the numerator
\[
\mathcal S_R(t,x,y)
:=
(h_x-h_y)\bigl(\chi_R(x)A_x-\chi_R(y)A_y\bigr)
+(g_x-g_y)\bigl(\chi_R(x)G_x-\chi_R(y)G_y\bigr).
\]
The desired product-cutoff numerator is
\[
\chi_R(x)\chi_R(y)
\Bigl[(h_x-h_y)(A_x-A_y)+(g_x-g_y)(G_x-G_y)\Bigr].
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-bregman-algebra}, the bracket is
\(\mathcal B_t^\Phi(x,y)\).  Subtracting the product-cutoff numerator from
\(\mathcal S_R\) gives
\[
\begin{split}
&\mathcal S_R(t,x,y)-\chi_R(x)\chi_R(y)\mathcal B_t^\Phi(x,y)\\
&\qquad=
\chi_R(x)(1-\chi_R(y))\mathcal B_t^\Phi(x,y)
+(\chi_R(x)-\chi_R(y))
\bigl[(h_x-h_y)A_y+(g_x-g_y)G_y\bigr],
\end{split}
\]
which is exactly \(\mathcal C_R^\Phi(t,x,y)\).  This proves the formula for
the error once the bilinear identity is applied to the localized tests
\(\chi_R A_t\) and \(\chi_R G_t\).

It remains to justify the absolute convergence and the
\(L^1([a,b])\)-vanishing.  On the compact quotient range \(J_{a,b}\), the
functions \(\Phi\), \(\Phi'\), \(u_t\), \(A_t\), and \(G_t\) are uniformly
bounded, and \(A_t\), \(G_t\) are uniformly Lipschitz in \(x\).  The Bregman
part satisfies, by Lemma~\ref{lem:doob-bregman-domain-bounds},
\begin{align}
0\le \frac{\mathcal B_t^\Phi(x,y)}{|x-y|^2}
&\le C_{\Phi,a,b}(g_t(x)+g_t(y))\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|<1\}},
\label{eq:phi-commutator-near-bregman-bound}\\
0\le \frac{\mathcal B_t^\Phi(x,y)}{|x-y|^2}
&\le C_{\Phi,a,b}(g_t(x)+g_t(y))|x-y|^{-2}
\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|\ge1\}} .
\label{eq:phi-commutator-far-bregman-bound}
\end{align}
Both right-hand sides are integrable on
\([a,b]\times\RR^2\).

For the \(\mathcal M_t^\Phi\) part near the diagonal, the uniform boundedness
of \(A_t\) and \(G_t\) gives
\[
|\mathcal M_t^\Phi(x,y)|
\le C_{\Phi,a,b}\bigl(|h_t(x)-h_t(y)|+|g_t(x)-g_t(y)|\bigr).
\]
Using
\[
p_t(x)-p_t(y)
=(x-y)\int_0^1\partial_xp_t(y+s(x-y))\dd s,
\qquad p_t\in\{h_t,g_t\},
\]
and
\(|\chi_R(x)-\chi_R(y)|
\le \|\chi'\|_\infty |x-y|/R\le \|\chi'\|_\infty |x-y|\), we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-commutator-near-M-bound}
\frac{|(\chi_R(x)-\chi_R(y))\mathcal M_t^\Phi(x,y)|}{|x-y|^2}
\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|<1\}}
\le
C_{\Phi,a,b,\chi}\int_0^1
\sum_{p\in\{h,g\}}|\partial_xp_t(y+s(x-y))|\dd s\,
\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|<1\}} .
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is integrable on \([a,b]\times\RR^2\), because
Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-doob-cutoff-calculus} gives uniform \(L^1\)-bounds for
\(\partial_xh_t\) and \(\partial_xg_t\), and integration in the difference
variable contributes the finite factor \(\int_{|z|<1}\dd z=2\).
For each fixed \(t,x,y\), the factor
\(\chi_R(x)(1-\chi_R(y))\) and the factor
\(\chi_R(x)-\chi_R(y)\) tend to zero as \(R\to\infty\).  Dominated
convergence with
\eqref{eq:phi-commutator-near-bregman-bound} and
\eqref{eq:phi-commutator-near-M-bound} therefore gives
\[
\int_a^b\iint_{|x-y|<1}
\frac{|\mathcal C_R^\Phi(t,x,y)|}{|x-y|^2}
\dd x\dd y\dd t\longrightarrow0 .
\]

For the far field, boundedness of \(A_t\) and \(G_t\) gives
\[
|\mathcal M_t^\Phi(x,y)|
\le C_{\Phi,a,b}\bigl(h_t(x)+h_t(y)+g_t(x)+g_t(y)\bigr).
\]
Together with \(|\chi_R(x)-\chi_R(y)|\le1\) and
\eqref{eq:phi-commutator-far-bregman-bound}, this yields the integrable
majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-commutator-far-M-bound}
\frac{|\mathcal C_R^\Phi(t,x,y)|}{|x-y|^2}
\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|\ge1\}}
\le
C_{\Phi,a,b,\chi}
\frac{h_t(x)+h_t(y)+g_t(x)+g_t(y)}{|x-y|^2}
\mathbf 1_{\{|x-y|\ge1\}} .
\end{equation}
Indeed \(h_t\) and \(g_t\) are probability densities, so the right-hand side
has \([a,b]\times\RR^2\)-integral bounded by a constant times
\((b-a)\int_{|z|\ge1}|z|^{-2}\dd z\).  Pointwise again
\(\chi_R(x)(1-\chi_R(y))\to0\) and
\(\chi_R(x)-\chi_R(y)\to0\), so dominated convergence gives
\[
\int_a^b\iint_{|x-y|\ge1}
\frac{|\mathcal C_R^\Phi(t,x,y)|}{|x-y|^2}
\dd x\dd y\dd t\longrightarrow0 .
\]
Combining the near and far estimates proves
\eqref{eq:phi-cutoff-commutator-l1-vanishing}.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite-time estimates and constant regimes for the sixth coefficient defect}
\label{app:finite-time-defect-estimates}

The primary finite-time result is
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}.  It fixes the
controlled shell \(\mu_2=\sigma^2\), \(\EE|X_c|^7\le M\), displays the
finite constants \(C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\),
and only then inserts the measured entropy value through
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-time-sixth-deficit}
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)
:=
t^6\left(
\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
-\frac{7\sigma^6}{64t^6}
-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
\right).
\end{equation}
Here \(h_t=P_t*\nu\) and \(g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma)\).  The controlled-shell
conclusion is
\eqref{eq:main-finite-time-defect-estimate}--\eqref{eq:main-finite-time-w2-stability}.
The fixed-law oracle variant,
Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle-finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}, instead uses
supplied data \(A_8,B,T\) and gives
\eqref{eq:main-oracle-finite-time-defect-estimate}--\eqref{eq:main-oracle-finite-time-w2-stability}.

The constant regimes are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}, leftmargin=*]
\item The coefficient-level \(W_2\) constants \(4\) and \(8\) in
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} are universal algebraic
constants.  They do not use a finite-time entropy remainder.
\item The fixed-law finite-time theorem uses oracle data \(A_8,B,T\).  If
these data are supplied by
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}, then they are
\(A_8(\nu),B_{\nu,8},T_{\nu,8}\) and may depend on the particular law.
\item The controlled-shell theorem uses the displayed finite functions
\(C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\).  These depend
only on \((\sigma,M)\) and the universal \(N=4\) envelopes from
Lemma~\ref{lem:closed-cayley-mode-constant-envelopes}; they are fixed before
the KL value at time \(t\) is used.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{corollary}[Constant dependence for moment-defect \(W_2\) estimates]%
\label{cor:defect-stability-constant-regimes}
The coefficient-level constants in
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} are universal apart from the
explicit variance powers forced by scaling against
\(\rho_\sigma=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma)\).  The finite-time
constants are separate inputs: either fixed-law oracle data \(A_8,B,T\), or
the controlled-shell constants displayed in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}.  The measured value
\(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\) supplies only
\(\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This restates the hypotheses and conclusions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling},
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle-finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Appendix check of the controlled-shell finite-time estimate]%
\label{cor:uniform-finite-time-defect-stability}
Fix \(\sigma>0\) and \(M<\infty\), and use the explicit constants
\(B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\), \(T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\), and
\(C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\) displayed in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}.  For every law in
the controlled shell \(\mu_2=\sigma^2\), \(\EE|X_c|^7\le M\), and every
\(t\ge T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-finite-time-defect-shell}
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\mathcal A_t^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\mathcal A_t^{1/2},
\end{equation}
where
\[
\mathcal A_t:=
\bigl|\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\bigr|
+C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}t^{-2}+B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}t^{-3}.
\]
Outside such a shell, the fixed-law constants in
Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle-finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability} are the
asserted level of generality.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is exactly \eqref{eq:main-finite-time-w2-stability}.  The shell
domination of \(B_{\nu,8}\), \(T_{\nu,8}\), and \(A_8(\nu)\) is proved inside
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability} from the displayed
finite formulas there.
\end{proof}
