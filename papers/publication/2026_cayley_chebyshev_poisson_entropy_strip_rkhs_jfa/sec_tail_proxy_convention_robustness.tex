\begin{unnumberedproposition}[Robustness of the positive tail-jet convention]
Assume the hypotheses and notation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:stable-arbitrary-order-law-by-law-decomposition}.  Call a
pair \((\chi,H)\) an admissible tail-jet convention if there are constants
\(0<a<b<\infty\), independent of \(s\), such that
\[
 0\le\chi_s\le1,\qquad
 \chi_s(z)=0\quad(|z|\le a),\qquad
 \chi_s(z)=1\quad(|z|\ge b),
\]
where each \(\chi_s:\RR^d\to[0,1]\) is measurable, and if
\[
 \int H_s\dd\Omega_{\alpha,d}=0,
 \qquad
 \sup_s\bigl(\|H_s\|_\infty+
        \|H_s\|_{L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d})}\bigr)<\infty.
\]
The function \(H_s\) is common to the two laws.  Define
\begin{align*}
 \tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda
  &:=\int\chi_s(x/s)\lambda(\dd x),\\
 V_{s,\chi}^\lambda(y)
  &:=\int\chi_s(x/s)F_y^{(\alpha,d)}(x/s)\lambda(\dd x),\\
 A_s^{\lambda;\chi,H}
  &:=1+C_{r-1,s}+V_{s,\chi}^\lambda-
       \tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda+s^{-r}H_s.
\end{align*}
For all sufficiently large \(s\), these are positive probability densities
relative to \(\Omega_{\alpha,d}\), their relative entropy
\[
 \mathcal E_{r,s}^{\chi,H}(\nu,\eta)
 :=\int A_s^{\eta;\chi,H}
 \Phi\!\left(
  \frac{A_s^{\nu;\chi,H}-A_s^{\eta;\chi,H}}
       {A_s^{\eta;\chi,H}}
 \right)\dd\Omega_{\alpha,d}
\]
is finite, and
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\nu\|p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\eta)
 =\mathcal E_{r,s}^{\chi,H}(\nu,\eta)
  +\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)s^{-2r}+o(s^{-2r}).
\]
Consequently, for any two admissible conventions \((\chi,H)\) and
\((\widetilde\chi,\widetilde H)\),
\[
 \left|\mathcal E_{r,s}^{\chi,H}(\nu,\eta)
       -\mathcal E_{r,s}^{\widetilde\chi,\widetilde H}(\nu,\eta)\right|
 =o(s^{-2r}).
\]
Thus the vanishing criterion and the Hessian constant
\(\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)\) are independent of the convention in
this class.

The class contains the original hard cutoff, every hard cutoff
\(\mathbf1_{\{|x|>c_s\,s\}}\) with
\(0<\inf_sc_s\le\sup_sc_s<\infty\), and smooth radial cutoffs
\(\theta(|x|/(c_s\,s))\), where \(\theta=0\) on \([0,1]\) and \(\theta=1\)
on \([2,\infty)\).  It also permits arbitrary measurable, nonradial,
\(s\)-dependent transitions in a fixed annulus and the infinite-dimensional
family of common bounded zero-mass gauges \(H_s\).
\end{unnumberedproposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(F_y=F_y^{(\alpha,d)}\), let \(\mathsf B_j(y,z)\) be as in the proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:stable-arbitrary-order-law-by-law-decomposition}, and put
\(q_s^\lambda(y)=\int F_y(x/s)\lambda(\dd x)\).  Direct addition and
subtraction of the Taylor terms gives the exact split
\[
 q_s^\lambda=A_s^{\lambda;\chi,H}
  +s^{-r}(B_r^\lambda-H_s)+R_{s,\chi}^\lambda,
\]
where
\begin{align*}
 R_{s,\chi}^\lambda
 &=\int(1-\chi_s(x/s))
   \left\{F_\cdot(x/s)-\sum_{j=0}^r
                  \mathsf B_j(\cdot,x/s)\right\}\lambda(\dd x)\\
 &\quad-\sum_{j=1}^r\int\chi_s(x/s)
                  \mathsf B_j(\cdot,x/s)\lambda(\dd x).
\end{align*}
On \(|z|\le b\), the stable-kernel estimates and translation continuity
used in \eqref{eq:arbitrary-tail-local-remainder} give a bounded function
\(\omega_b\), with \(\omega_b(t)\to0\) as \(t\downarrow0\), such that the
sum of the \(L^\infty\) and \(L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d})\) norms of the Taylor
remainder is at most \(\omega_b(|z|)|z|^r\).  Since \(1-\chi_s(x/s)\)
vanishes for \(|x|\ge bs\), multiplication by \(s^r\) leaves a quantity
dominated by \(C_b|x|^r\) and converging pointwise to zero.  Dominated
convergence handles the first line.  For \(1\le j\le r\), the support
condition on \(\chi_s\) and \eqref{eq:arbitrary-tail-mode-bounds} give
\[
 \left\|\int\chi_s(x/s)\mathsf B_j(\cdot,x/s)
             \lambda(\dd x)\right\|_\infty
 +\left\|\int\chi_s(x/s)\mathsf B_j(\cdot,x/s)
             \lambda(\dd x)\right\|_1
 \le C_{a,r}s^{-r}\int_{|x|>as}|x|^r\lambda(\dd x).
\]
It follows that
\[
 \|R_{s,\chi}^\lambda\|_\infty+
 \|R_{s,\chi}^\lambda\|_{L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d})}=o(s^{-r}).
\]
The same tail estimate yields
\(\tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda=o(s^{-r})\).

Positivity and normalization are structural.  Indeed,
\(V_{s,\chi}^\lambda\ge0\),
\(\int V_{s,\chi}^\lambda\dd\Omega_{\alpha,d}
=\tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda\), and both \(C_{r-1,s}\) and \(H_s\) have zero
mean.  Moreover,
\[
 \inf_yA_s^{\lambda;\chi,H}(y)
 \ge1-\|C_{r-1,s}\|_\infty-
       \tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda-s^{-r}\|H_s\|_\infty\longrightarrow1.
\]
The entropy is finite by the same mixture argument as in
\eqref{eq:arbitrary-tail-normalized-proxy-entropy}.  More explicitly, if
\(\tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda>0\), convexity of relative entropy gives, for
\(Q_{s,\chi}^\lambda=V_{s,\chi}^\lambda/\tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda\),
\[
 \int Q_{s,\chi}^\lambda\log Q_{s,\chi}^\lambda\dd\Omega_{\alpha,d}
 \le\frac1{\tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda}
 \int\chi_s(x/s)\{C_{\alpha,d}+(d+\alpha)
              \log(1+|x|/s)\}\lambda(\dd x)<\infty.
\]
The finite first moment implied by the finite \(r\)th moment proves the last
inequality.  Adding the uniformly bounded terms
\(C_{r-1,s}-\tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda+s^{-r}H_s\) preserves finite positive
entropy.  The case \(\tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda=0\) is immediate.

Apply the two-background KL perturbation lemma with
\[
 A_{1,s}=A_s^{\nu;\chi,H},\quad
 A_{2,s}=A_s^{\eta;\chi,H},\quad
 B_{1,s}=B_r^\nu-H_s,\quad B_{2,s}=B_r^\eta-H_s.
\]
The backgrounds tend to one in \(L^1\), and their common jet and common
gauge cancel, so
\[
 \|A_s^{\nu;\chi,H}-A_s^{\eta;\chi,H}\|_1
 \le2(\tau_{s,\chi}^\nu+\tau_{s,\chi}^\eta)=o(s^{-r}).
\]
All remaining hypotheses have just been verified.  Finally,
\((B_r^\nu-H_s)-(B_r^\eta-H_s)=B_{\Delta_r}\), so the lemma's Hessian is
exactly \(\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)\).  This proves the expansion.
Subtracting the expansions for two conventions proves the asserted
\(o(s^{-2r})\) difference and the two invariant conclusions.
\end{proof}

\begin{unnumberedproposition}[Partial rigidity within the retained-tail ansatz]
Fix an admissible retention profile \(\chi\).  Consider positive normalized
proxies of the form
\[
 \widehat A_s^\lambda=1+J_s+V_{s,\chi}^\lambda-
                         \tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda,
 \qquad \lambda\in\{\nu,\eta\},
\]
where \(J_s\) is common to the two laws and has zero
\(\Omega_{\alpha,d}\)-mean.  Suppose the proxies are order-\(r\)
jet-faithful in the following precise sense: there are zero-mean modes
\(G_s^\lambda\), uniformly bounded in \(L^\infty\), and remainders
\(S_s^\lambda\) such that
\[
 q_s^\lambda=\widehat A_s^\lambda+s^{-r}G_s^\lambda+S_s^\lambda,
 \qquad
 \|S_s^\lambda\|_\infty+\|S_s^\lambda\|_1=o(s^{-r}).
\]
Then
\[
 H_s:=s^r(J_s-C_{r-1,s})
\]
is a common zero-mean family bounded in both \(L^\infty\) and \(L^1\), and
\[
 \|G_s^\lambda-(B_r^\lambda-H_s)\|_\infty
 +\|G_s^\lambda-(B_r^\lambda-H_s)\|_1=o(1).
\]
In particular,
\[
 \|G_s^\nu-G_s^\eta-B_{\Delta_r}\|_\infty
 +\|G_s^\nu-G_s^\eta-B_{\Delta_r}\|_1=o(1).
\]
Thus, within this ansatz, the lower-order common jet is forced modulo the
common order-\(r\) gauge already allowed by the robustness result, and the
law-difference mode, hence its Hessian, is forced asymptotically.  Also, if
one initially writes the normalized proxy as
\(1+J_s+V_{s,\chi}^\lambda+c_s^\lambda\) with a scalar counterterm, then
normalization forces \(c_s^\lambda=-\tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda\) exactly.
\end{unnumberedproposition}

\begin{proof}
The last assertion follows by integrating the proposed proxy.  For the
rigidity statement, compare the assumed split with the exact split in the
proof of the robustness proposition with \(H=0\).  Since the retained-tail
terms agree, subtraction gives
\[
 s^r(J_s-C_{r-1,s})+G_s^\lambda-B_r^\lambda
   =s^r(R_{s,\chi}^\lambda-S_s^\lambda).
\]
The right side tends to zero in both stated norms.  Boundedness of
\(B_r^\lambda\) and \(G_s^\lambda\) therefore proves the bounds on \(H_s\)
and the claimed approximation.  Subtracting the two law identities cancels
\(H_s\), and
\(B_r^\nu-B_r^\eta=B_{\Delta_r}\), which proves the final display.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope of the rigidity statement.}
The result does not select a retention profile, remove the common
order-\(r\) gauge, compare proxies outside the retained raw-tail/common-core
ansatz, or assert that the proxy minimizes relative entropy.  In particular,
it is a partial rigidity result and not a uniqueness or canonicity theorem
for all positive proxy constructions.
