Assume throughout the hypotheses and notation of
Corollary~\ref{thm:stable-arbitrary-order-law-by-law-decomposition}.  For
\(\lambda\in\{\nu,\eta\}\), let
\(\chi^\lambda=(\chi_s^\lambda)_{s>0}\).  Call
\((\boldsymbol\chi,H)=((\chi^\nu,\chi^\eta),H)\) an admissible fixed-scale
tail-jet convention if there are common constants
\(0<a<b<\infty\), independent of \(s\) and \(\lambda\), such that
\[
 0\le\chi_s^\lambda\le1,\qquad
 \chi_s^\lambda(z)=0\quad(|z|\le a),\qquad
 \chi_s^\lambda(z)=1\quad(|z|\ge b),
\]
where every \(\chi_s^\lambda:\RR^d\to[0,1]\) is measurable, and if
\[
 \int H_s\dd\Omega_{\alpha,d}=0,
 \qquad
 \sup_s\bigl(\|H_s\|_\infty+
        \|H_s\|_{L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d})}\bigr)<\infty.
\]
The function \(H_s\) is common to the two laws.  No relation between
\(\chi_s^\nu\) and \(\chi_s^\eta\) is required beyond their common fixed
inner and outer radii.

\paragraph{Sharpness of the inner exclusion.}
The condition that the profiles vanish near the origin is mathematically
necessary at the resolution under study.  Indeed, let a smooth profile
\(\chi\) equal a constant \(\kappa\in(0,1]\) on a neighbourhood of zero and
then rise to one, and let \(\nu,\eta\) be compactly supported, match below
order \(r\), and satisfy \(\Delta_r\ne0\).  For all large \(s\),
the corresponding additively normalized proxy with \(H=0\) satisfies
\[
 \tau_{s,\chi}^\lambda=\kappa,
 \qquad V_{s,\chi}^\lambda=\kappa q_s^\lambda,
 \qquad
 A_s^\lambda=1+C_{r-1,s}+\kappa(q_s^\lambda-1).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 A_s^\nu-A_s^\eta
 =\kappa s^{-r}B_{\Delta_r}+o(s^{-r})
 \quad\hbox{in }L^\infty\cap L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d}),
 \qquad \inf A_s^\eta\longrightarrow1.
\]
The quadratic KL expansion therefore gives
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(A_s^\nu\|A_s^\eta)
 =\kappa^2\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)s^{-2r}
   +o(s^{-2r}),
\]
whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:all-order-stable-first-unmatched-moment} gives the
actual smoothed entropy coefficient
\(\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)\).  Thus adding the universal quadratic
term to this proxy entropy would give the false coefficient
\((1+\kappa^2)\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)\).  The retained raw tail has
absorbed a fixed fraction of the local order-\(r\) energy; the admissible
class boundary is not a proof artifact.

\begin{unnumberedproposition}[Robustness of the positive tail-jet convention]
Define, for \(\lambda\in\{\nu,\eta\}\),
\begin{align*}
 \tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda
  &:=\int\chi_s^\lambda(x/s)\lambda(\dd x),\\
 V_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda(y)
  &:=\int\chi_s^\lambda(x/s)F_y^{(\alpha,d)}(x/s)\lambda(\dd x),\\
 A_s^{\lambda;\boldsymbol\chi,H}
  &:=1+C_{r-1,s}+V_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda-
       \tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda+s^{-r}H_s,\\
 \widetilde A_s^{\lambda;\boldsymbol\chi,H}
  &:=\frac{1+C_{r-1,s}+V_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda+s^{-r}H_s}
          {1+\tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda}.
\end{align*}
These are, respectively, the additive and multiplicative mass
normalizations.  For all sufficiently large \(s\), both pairs are positive
probability densities relative to \(\Omega_{\alpha,d}\).  Their relative
entropies
\[
 \mathcal E_{r,s}^{\boldsymbol\chi,H}(\nu,\eta)
 :=\int A_s^{\eta;\boldsymbol\chi,H}
 \Phi\!\left(
  \frac{A_s^{\nu;\boldsymbol\chi,H}
        -A_s^{\eta;\boldsymbol\chi,H}}
       {A_s^{\eta;\boldsymbol\chi,H}}
 \right)\dd\Omega_{\alpha,d},
\]
and
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal E}_{r,s}^{\boldsymbol\chi,H}(\nu,\eta)
 :=D_{\rm KL}(\widetilde A_s^{\nu;\boldsymbol\chi,H}
              \|\widetilde A_s^{\eta;\boldsymbol\chi,H})
\]
are finite and satisfy
\[
 \left|\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{r,s}^{\boldsymbol\chi,H}(\nu,\eta)
       -\mathcal E_{r,s}^{\boldsymbol\chi,H}(\nu,\eta)\right|
 =o(s^{-2r}).
\]
For either normalization, writing its energy as
\(\mathcal E_{r,s}^{\mathrm{norm}}\), one has
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\nu\|p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\eta)
 =\mathcal E_{r,s}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\nu,\eta)
  +\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)s^{-2r}+o(s^{-2r}).
\]
Consequently, the energies of any two admissible fixed-scale conventions,
with either of these two mass normalizations in either convention, differ by
\(o(s^{-2r})\).  In particular, the truth of the condition
\[
 s^{2r}\mathcal E_{r,s}^{\mathrm{norm}}(\nu,\eta)\longrightarrow0
\]
is independent of the convention and of these two normalization choices,
as is the Hessian constant \(\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)\).

The class contains the original hard cutoff, every hard cutoff
\(\mathbf1_{\{|x|>c_s\,s\}}\) with
\(0<\inf_sc_s\le\sup_sc_s<\infty\), and smooth radial cutoffs
\(\theta(|x|/(c_s\,s))\), where \(\theta=0\) on \([0,1]\) and \(\theta=1\)
on \([2,\infty)\).  It also permits arbitrary measurable, nonradial,
\(s\)-dependent transitions in a fixed annulus and the infinite-dimensional
family of common bounded zero-mass gauges \(H_s\).
No assertion is made for proxy constructions outside this prescribed
fixed-smoothing-scale tail/core class or for normalization rules other than
the two displayed above.
\end{unnumberedproposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(F_y=F_y^{(\alpha,d)}\), let \(\mathsf B_j(y,z)\) be as in the proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-positive-tail-jet-kernel}, and put
\(q_s^\lambda(y)=\int F_y(x/s)\lambda(\dd x)\).  Direct addition and
subtraction of the Taylor terms gives the exact split
\[
 q_s^\lambda=A_s^{\lambda;\boldsymbol\chi,H}
  +s^{-r}(B_r^\lambda-H_s)+R_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda,
\]
where
\begin{align*}
 R_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda
 &=\int(1-\chi_s^\lambda(x/s))
   \left\{F_\cdot(x/s)-\sum_{j=0}^r
                  \mathsf B_j(\cdot,x/s)\right\}\lambda(\dd x)\\
 &\quad-\sum_{j=1}^r\int\chi_s^\lambda(x/s)
                  \mathsf B_j(\cdot,x/s)\lambda(\dd x).
\end{align*}
On \(|z|\le b\), the stable-kernel estimates and translation continuity
used in \eqref{eq:abstract-tail-local-remainder} give a bounded function
\(\omega_b\), with \(\omega_b(t)\to0\) as \(t\downarrow0\), such that the
sum of the \(L^\infty\) and \(L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d})\) norms of the Taylor
remainder is at most \(\omega_b(|z|)|z|^r\).  Since
\(1-\chi_s^\lambda(x/s)\)
vanishes for \(|x|\ge bs\), multiplication by \(s^r\) leaves a quantity
dominated by \(C_b|x|^r\) and converging pointwise to zero.  Dominated
convergence handles the first line.  For \(1\le j\le r\), the support
condition on \(\chi_s\) and \eqref{eq:abstract-tail-mode-bounds} give
\[
 \left\|\int\chi_s^\lambda(x/s)\mathsf B_j(\cdot,x/s)
             \lambda(\dd x)\right\|_\infty
 +\left\|\int\chi_s^\lambda(x/s)\mathsf B_j(\cdot,x/s)
             \lambda(\dd x)\right\|_1
 \le C_{a,r}s^{-r}\int_{|x|>as}|x|^r\lambda(\dd x).
\]
It follows that
\[
 \|R_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda\|_\infty+
 \|R_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda\|_{L^1(\Omega_{\alpha,d})}=o(s^{-r}).
\]
The same tail estimate yields
\(\tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda=o(s^{-r})\).

Positivity and normalization are structural.  Indeed,
\(V_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda\ge0\),
\(\int V_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda\dd\Omega_{\alpha,d}
=\tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda\), and both \(C_{r-1,s}\) and \(H_s\) have zero
mean.  Moreover,
\[
 \inf_yA_s^{\lambda;\boldsymbol\chi,H}(y)
 \ge1-\|C_{r-1,s}\|_\infty-
       \tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda-s^{-r}\|H_s\|_\infty\longrightarrow1.
\]
The entropy is finite by the same mixture argument as in
\eqref{eq:abstract-tail-normalized-proxy-entropy}.  More explicitly, if
\(\tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda>0\), convexity of relative entropy gives,
for \(Q_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda
=V_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda/\tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda\),
\[
 \int Q_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda\log Q_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda
       \dd\Omega_{\alpha,d}
 \le\frac1{\tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda}
 \int\chi_s^\lambda(x/s)\{C_{\alpha,d}+(d+\alpha)
              \log(1+|x|/s)\}\lambda(\dd x)<\infty.
\]
The finite first moment implied by the finite \(r\)th moment proves the last
inequality.  Adding the uniformly bounded terms
\(C_{r-1,s}-\tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda+s^{-r}H_s\) preserves finite
positive entropy.  The case \(\tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda=0\) is
immediate.

Apply the two-background KL perturbation lemma with
\[
 A_{1,s}=A_s^{\nu;\boldsymbol\chi,H},\quad
 A_{2,s}=A_s^{\eta;\boldsymbol\chi,H},\quad
 B_{1,s}=B_r^\nu-H_s,\quad B_{2,s}=B_r^\eta-H_s.
\]
The backgrounds tend to one in \(L^1\), and their common jet and common
gauge cancel, so
\[
 \|A_s^{\nu;\boldsymbol\chi,H}
       -A_s^{\eta;\boldsymbol\chi,H}\|_1
 \le2(\tau_{s,\chi^\nu}^\nu+\tau_{s,\chi^\eta}^\eta)=o(s^{-r}).
\]
All remaining hypotheses have just been verified.  Finally,
\((B_r^\nu-H_s)-(B_r^\eta-H_s)=B_{\Delta_r}\), so the lemma's Hessian is
exactly \(\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)\).  This proves the expansion.
Subtracting the expansions for two additive conventions proves their
\(o(s^{-2r})\) equivalence.

It remains to compare the two normalizations.  Put \(\epsilon=s^{-r}\),
write \(A_s^\lambda=A_s^{\lambda;\boldsymbol\chi,H}\) and
\(\tau_s^\lambda=\tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda\), and interpolate by
\[
 X_{s,\theta}^\lambda
 :=\frac{A_s^\lambda+\theta\tau_s^\lambda}
         {1+\theta\tau_s^\lambda},\qquad 0\le\theta\le1.
\]
Thus \(X_{s,0}^\lambda=A_s^\lambda\) and
\(X_{s,1}^\lambda=\widetilde A_s^\lambda\).  We first record the entropy
estimate that replaces an unavailable \(L^\infty\) bound on the retained
potential.  The preceding mixture bound, the bounded negative part of
\(q\log q\), and \(\log(1+u)\le C_{a,r}u^r\) for \(u\ge a\) give
\begin{align*}
 \int V_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda
       \log^+V_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda\dd\Omega_{\alpha,d}
 &\le C\tau_s^\lambda
   +C\int\chi_s^\lambda(x/s)
          \{1+\log(1+|x|/s)\}\lambda(\dd x)\\
 &\le C\tau_s^\lambda
   +Cs^{-r}\int_{|x|>as}|x|^r\lambda(\dd x)
 =o(\epsilon).
\end{align*}
For fixed \(0<c<C<\infty\), the elementary pointwise bound
\[
 h(b+u,b'+v)\le C_{c,C}
 \{(b-b')^2+u+v+u\log^+u+v\log^+v\},
 \quad h(x,y)=x\log(x/y)-x+y,
\]
holds for \(c\le b,b'\le C\) and \(u,v\ge0\).  To see this, use the
quadratic Taylor bound for \(h\) when \(u+v\le1\); when \(u+v>1\), absorb
the constant in \(u+v\) and use the defining formula for \(h\).
The bounded cores of \(X_{s,\theta}^\nu\) and
\(X_{s,\theta}^\eta\) differ by
\(O(\tau_s^\nu+\tau_s^\eta)=o(\epsilon)\), uniformly in \(\theta\), while
their nonnegative retained parts obey the last display.  Consequently,
uniformly for \(0\le\theta\le1\),
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(X_{s,\theta}^\nu\|X_{s,\theta}^\eta)=o(\epsilon),
 \qquad
 \|X_{s,\theta}^\nu-X_{s,\theta}^\eta\|_1=o(\epsilon).
\]

Let \(K_s(\theta)=D_{\rm KL}(X_{s,\theta}^\nu
\|X_{s,\theta}^\eta)\) and
\(a_s^\lambda(\theta)=\tau_s^\lambda/(1+\theta\tau_s^\lambda)\).
Since
\(\partial_\theta X_{s,\theta}^\lambda
=a_s^\lambda(\theta)(1-X_{s,\theta}^\lambda)\), differentiation gives
\begin{align*}
 K_s'(\theta)
 &=a_s^\nu(\theta)\int(1-X_{s,\theta}^\nu)
       \log\frac{X_{s,\theta}^\nu}{X_{s,\theta}^\eta}
       \dd\Omega_{\alpha,d}\\
 &\quad-a_s^\eta(\theta)\int
       \frac{(X_{s,\theta}^\nu-X_{s,\theta}^\eta)
             (1-X_{s,\theta}^\eta)}{X_{s,\theta}^\eta}
       \dd\Omega_{\alpha,d}.
\end{align*}
The second integral is \(o(\epsilon)\), because all interpolants have a
common positive lower bound and \(|1-y|/y\) is bounded there.  For the first
integral, use
\[
 \int(1-x)\log(x/y)\dd\Omega
 =\int\log(x/y)\dd\Omega-D_{\rm KL}(x\|y),
 \qquad
 \int|\log(x/y)|\dd\Omega\le c^{-1}\|x-y\|_1.
\]
It too is \(o(\epsilon)\).  Since
\(a_s^\lambda(\theta)=o(\epsilon)\), uniformly in \(\theta\), we obtain
\(\sup_{0\le\theta\le1}|K_s'(\theta)|=o(\epsilon^2)\).
The preceding finite-entropy estimates justify the differentiation; one may
equivalently truncate the retained potentials and pass to the limit using
the displayed integrable bounds.  Integration from zero to one proves
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal E}_{r,s}^{\boldsymbol\chi,H}
 -\mathcal E_{r,s}^{\boldsymbol\chi,H}=o(s^{-2r}).
\]
Combining this with the additive expansion proves all remaining assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{unnumberedproposition}[Asymptotic coefficient rigidity inside a prescribed ansatz]
Fix an admissible pair \(\boldsymbol\chi=(\chi^\nu,\chi^\eta)\).  Consider
positive normalized
proxies of the form
\[
 \widehat A_s^\lambda=1+J_s+V_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda-
                         \tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda,
 \qquad \lambda\in\{\nu,\eta\},
\]
where \(J_s\) is common to the two laws and has zero
\(\Omega_{\alpha,d}\)-mean.  Suppose the proxies are order-\(r\)
jet-faithful in the following precise sense: there are zero-mean modes
\(G_s^\lambda\), uniformly bounded in \(L^\infty\), and remainders
\(S_s^\lambda\) such that
\[
 q_s^\lambda=\widehat A_s^\lambda+s^{-r}G_s^\lambda+S_s^\lambda,
 \qquad
 \|S_s^\lambda\|_\infty+\|S_s^\lambda\|_1=o(s^{-r}).
\]
Then
\[
 H_s:=s^r(J_s-C_{r-1,s})
\]
is a common zero-mean family bounded in both \(L^\infty\) and \(L^1\), and
\[
 \|G_s^\lambda-(B_r^\lambda-H_s)\|_\infty
 +\|G_s^\lambda-(B_r^\lambda-H_s)\|_1=o(1).
\]
In particular,
\[
 \|G_s^\nu-G_s^\eta-B_{\Delta_r}\|_\infty
 +\|G_s^\nu-G_s^\eta-B_{\Delta_r}\|_1=o(1).
\]
Thus, within this ansatz, the lower-order common jet is forced modulo the
common order-\(r\) gauge already allowed by the robustness result, and the
law-difference mode, hence its Hessian, is forced asymptotically.  Also, if
one initially writes the normalized proxy as
\(1+J_s+V_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda+c_s^\lambda\) with a scalar
counterterm, then normalization forces
\(c_s^\lambda=-\tau_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda\) exactly.
\end{unnumberedproposition}

\begin{proof}
The last assertion follows by integrating the proposed proxy.  For the
rigidity statement, compare the assumed split with the exact split in the
proof of the robustness proposition with \(H=0\).  Since the retained-tail
terms agree, subtraction gives
\[
 s^r(J_s-C_{r-1,s})+G_s^\lambda-B_r^\lambda
   =s^r(R_{s,\chi^\lambda}^\lambda-S_s^\lambda).
\]
The right side tends to zero in both stated norms.  Boundedness of
\(B_r^\lambda\) and \(G_s^\lambda\) therefore proves the bounds on \(H_s\)
and the claimed approximation.  Subtracting the two law identities cancels
\(H_s\), and
\(B_r^\nu-B_r^\eta=B_{\Delta_r}\), which proves the final display.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope of the coefficient-rigidity statement.}
This is asymptotic coefficient rigidity inside the prescribed ansatz.  The
result does not select a retention profile, remove the common
order-\(r\) gauge, compare proxies outside the retained raw-tail/common-core
ansatz, or assert that the proxy minimizes relative entropy.  In particular,
it is not a uniqueness, canonicity, or entropy-minimality theorem for all
positive proxy constructions.
