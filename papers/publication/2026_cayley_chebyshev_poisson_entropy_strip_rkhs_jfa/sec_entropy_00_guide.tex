\section{Poisson Smoothing and Entropy Asymptotics}%
\label{sec:entropy-asymptotics}

\subsection*{Main entropy theorem map}\label{sec:entropy-theorem-map}

Throughout this section we write
\[
X_c:=\gamma-\bar\gamma,\qquad
\mu_n:=\int_{\RR}X_c^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma)
\]
whenever the moment exists.  The section is organized around the two analytic
steps below; the later named results are the explicit coefficient layer,
defect corollaries, and finite-time consequences built from them.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem A: density quotient.}
Let \(L\ge2\), and assume \(\EE|X_c|^L<\infty\).  With
\(\omega(\dd y)=\pi^{-1}(1+y^2)^{-1}\dd y\), the Cauchy-scale density
quotient has the coefficient-identification threshold expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:guide-theorem-a-density-quotient}
\frac{(P_t*\nu)(\bar\gamma+ty)}{P_t(ty)}-1
=
\sum_{n=2}^{L}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}+\rho_{L,t}(y),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:guide-theorem-a-density-remainder}
\|\rho_{L,t}\|_\infty+\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}=o(t^{-L}).
\end{equation}
The quotient identity behind this statement is
\[
\frac{P_t(\bar\gamma+ty-\gamma)}{P_t(ty)}
=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}.
\]
Thus the constant mode cancels because the quotient is compared with \(1\),
and the linear mode cancels because \(X_c\) is centred.  In the body this is
proved as Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-moment-density-truncation}; the
Cayley--Chebyshev modes \(u_n\), the formal coefficient algebra, and the
Laurent constant-term rule are developed in
Theorems~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} and
\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem B: entropy transfer.}
Suppose that the expansion in Theorem A holds and put
\[
d_t(y):=\sum_{n=2}^{L}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}.
\]
Then, with
\[
I(n_1,\ldots,n_r):=
\int_{\RR}u_{n_1}(y)\cdots u_{n_r}(y)\,\omega(\dd y),
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:guide-theorem-b-entropy-transfer}
\begin{split}
D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
&=
\sum_{m=4}^{L+2}
\sum_{r=2}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\substack{n_1+\cdots+n_r=m\\ 2\le n_j\le L}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r} \\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad
{}\times I(n_1,\ldots,n_r)t^{-m}
+o(t^{-(L+2)}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The proof is the finite Taylor expansion of
\(\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\) after the change of variables
\(P_t(ty)t\,\dd y=\omega(\dd y)\).  Since \(\Phi(s)\) begins with
\(s^2/2\), a density error \(o(t^{-L})\) first appears in entropy multiplied
by the \(t^{-2}\) density scale, giving \(o(t^{-(L+2)})\).  This transfer is
isolated in Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-moment-entropy-transfer} and
Lemma~\ref{prop:coefficient-identification-poisson-entropy-transfer}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Corollaries and quantitative margins.}
Setting \(L=2N-2\) in Theorems A and B gives the
coefficient-identification threshold hierarchy
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  Setting \(L=6\) gives the explicit
eighth-order coefficient layer and the finite seventh-moment remainder,
Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}--\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}.
The positive-margin, moment-ball, stronger-truncation, optimized \(N=4\),
fixed-law, and controlled-shell estimates are not part of the
coefficient-identification statement; their constants are collected in
Appendices~\ref{subsubsec:finite-moment-constants-variants} and
\ref{app:finite-time-defect-estimates}.  The finite-time theorem
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability} is a conditional
certified-shell consequence: bounds on \(A_8\) and on the remainder are fixed
before the measured KL statistic is used.

The multidimensional radial Poisson analogue,
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets}, repeats the same
two-step structure with tensor density layers
\(D_j^{(d)}(y;\nu)\), but only after fixing an integer
\(L\ge\max\{2,d+1\}\) and assuming \(\EE|X_c|^L<\infty\).  Its odd
coefficients vanish by parity, its first nonzero layers are displayed
immediately after the theorem, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidim-leading-positive-defect} gives the coercive
all-dimensional leading covariance defect under the same
dimension-dependent \(L\ge d+1\) tail hypothesis.  In dimensions \(d\ge2\)
the displayed \(\mathcal A_4^{(d)}\) asymptotic is therefore not a
finite-covariance-only theorem.
