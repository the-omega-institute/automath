\section{Poisson Smoothing and Entropy Asymptotics}%
\label{sec:entropy-asymptotics}

\subsection*{Guide to hypotheses}\label{sec:entropy-theorem-map}

Throughout this section we write
\[
\Delta:=\gamma-\bar\gamma,\qquad
\mu_n:=\int_{\RR}\Delta^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma)
\]
whenever the moment exists.  The list below is only a theorem-by-theorem
assumption map.  It separates coefficient identities, remainder estimates,
finite observation inputs, and downstream \(W_2\) consequences.  The constants
used to turn the finite-time statements into fixed-law or controlled-shell
estimates are recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:finite-time-defect-estimates}.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}, leftmargin=*]
\item
The coefficient-algebra statements,
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients},
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}, assume only the
existence of the centred moments that occur in the displayed coefficient being
formed.  They produce the formal entropy polynomials, the law-level
coefficients after moment substitution, the parity cancellation of odd
orders, and the Laurent constant-term rule for the mode integrals.

\item
The finite-moment entropy hierarchy,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}, has three labelled parts.  Part
\textup{(E)} is the exact-threshold coefficient theorem under a finite
centred \((2N-2)\)th absolute moment and gives the retained-polynomial
\(o(t^{-2N})\) expansion.  Part \textup{(PM)} assumes the positive margin
\((2N-2+\eta)\) and gives the fixed-law quantitative retained-order estimate.
Part \textup{(MB)} assumes a \((2N+\eta)\)-moment shell and gives the
moment-ball uniform estimate.  The later statements
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder},
Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-margin-retained-order-entropy}, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-moment-ball-entropy-remainder} are only
reformulations or constant dictionaries for these three parts, not separate
moment hierarchies.  The companion sharpness assertion is about the
exact-threshold coefficient \(A_{2N}\): without the \((2N-2)\) moment
assumption the universal coefficient \(A_{2N}\) is not forced.

\item
The observation-channel results,
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-cauchy-moment-inversion-by-phase-modes} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-grid-poisson-moment-recovery}, are not entropy
remainder theorems.  The fourth-order phase recovery assumes a finite centred
fifth absolute moment, while the finite-grid theorem assumes grids above the
stated aliasing threshold.  Their output is recovery of the truncated
moment data, such as \((\sigma^2,\mu_3,\mu_4)\), together with the stated
finite-time residual estimates.

\item
The compact-support interchange theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic-compact-support-interchange}, assumes compact
support of the centred law.  It gives the absolutely convergent entropy series
and justifies termwise coefficient extraction in that stronger analytic
regime.

\item
The explicit eighth-order coefficient theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}, assumes a finite centred sixth absolute
moment.  The seventh-moment remainder theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}, assumes a finite
centred seventh absolute moment in order to obtain the \(O(t^{-9})\)
remainder.  Corollary~\ref{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization} uses the same
coefficient identity, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:eighth-order-finite-phase-verification} gives the
finite-phase verification of the normalized mode integrals on grids
\(\mathcal G_N\) with \(N\ge9\).

\item
The coefficient-defect consequences,
Corollaries~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder},
\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}, and
\ref{cor:poisson-two-point-defect-rigidity}, assume positive variance and the
finite centred sixth absolute moment needed for the eighth-order coefficient
identity.  They produce the non-negative defect coordinates, strict
eighth-order amplification, and the equality case for the symmetric two-point
law.

\item
The algebraic \(W_2\) layer,
Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling},
Lemma~\ref{lem:seventh-moment-bridge}, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}, assumes positive variance
and a finite centred fourth moment for the coefficient-level estimate.  The
seventh-moment bridge is invoked only when the finite-time entropy remainder
is also being used.

\item
The finite-time stability theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}, takes as inputs the
law-specific eighth-order coefficient and seventh-moment remainder data in
its displayed conditional expansion.  Thus the finite-time statement uses one
entropy observation plus preassigned eighth-order data.  The observation
contributes only the statistic \(\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\); it does not
determine those input constants.  The controlled-shell version fixes the
relevant seventh-moment shell and shell-uniform constants in advance, as
detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:finite-time-defect-estimates}.
\end{enumerate}

The section follows this order: normalization and formal coefficient algebra,
finite-moment entropy transfer, the explicit \(N=4\) sixth-moment coefficient
calculation, the seventh-moment quantitative remainder, and finally the
coefficient-level and finite-time \(W_2\) consequences.
