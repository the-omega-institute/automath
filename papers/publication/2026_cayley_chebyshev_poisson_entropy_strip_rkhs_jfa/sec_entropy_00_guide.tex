\section{Poisson Smoothing and Entropy Asymptotics}%
\label{sec:entropy-asymptotics}

\subsection*{Entropy proof architecture and hypothesis index}\label{sec:entropy-theorem-map}

Throughout this section we write
\[
X_c:=\gamma-\bar{\gamma},\qquad
\mu_n:=\int_{\RR}X_c^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma)
\]
whenever the moment exists.  This subsection is the single Section~3 map: it
records the proof architecture and the hypothesis status of the entropy
results.  Coefficient notation and the Cayley phase convention are fixed only
once, in Table~\ref{tab:coefficient-notation}.  The named introductory
theorems are not restated here; the body proves them by two analytic steps:
a density quotient expansion and a nonlinear KL transfer.  The paragraphs
below are a proof guide and hypothesis index, not additional theorem
statements.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proof architecture map.}
The one-dimensional and multidimensional entropy arguments have the same
three-stage form:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\hbox{finite density quotient}
\Longrightarrow
\hbox{finite Taylor transfer for }\Phi\\
\Longrightarrow
\hbox{coefficient consequences}.
\end{gathered}
\]
In one dimension the finite density quotient is
Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-moment-density-truncation}; the transfer is
Propositions~\ref{prop:finite-quotient-jet-implies-kl-coefficient-jet} and
\ref{prop:finite-density-jet-entropy-transfer}; the coefficient consequences
are Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders},
\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}, and
\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}.  In dimension \(d\), the
corresponding stages are
Theorems~\ref{thm:multidimensional-density-quotient} and
\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets}, followed by
Corollary~\ref{cor:multidim-coefficient-formula},
Proposition~\ref{prop:multidim-formal-covariance-layer}, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:multidim-leading-kl-coefficient}.  The proposition is the
finite-covariance formal quadratic-layer identity; the corollary is the
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\) leading KL coefficient statement.
The KL tail identity in Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail} is separate:
after the moving quotient has been verified to lie in the required domain, a
direct Cauchy cutoff-Green calculation converts the KL path into the
Bregman-dissipation representation.
Pointwise dissipation asymptotics are outside the proof architecture; the
main hierarchy uses only tail integrals and fixed-window averages, and any
differentiated-remainder input must be supplied independently.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Density quotient input.}
Let \(L\ge2\), and assume \(\EE|X_c|^L<\infty\).  With
\(\omega(\dd y)=\pi^{-1}(1+y^2)^{-1}\dd y\), the Cauchy-scale density
quotient has the finite-moment expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:guide-theorem-a-density-quotient}
\frac{(P_t*\nu)(\bar{\gamma}+ty)}{P_t(ty)}-1
=
\sum_{n=2}^{L}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}+\rho_{L,t}(y),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:guide-theorem-a-density-remainder}
\|\rho_{L,t}\|_\infty+\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}=o(t^{-L}).
\end{equation}
The quotient identity behind this statement is
\[
\frac{P_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty-\gamma)}{P_t(ty)}
=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}.
\]
Thus the constant mode cancels because the quotient is compared with \(1\),
and the linear mode cancels because \(X_c\) is centred.  In the body this is
proved as Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-moment-density-truncation}; the
Cayley--Chebyshev modes \(u_n\), the formal coefficient algebra, and the
Laurent constant-term rule are developed in
Theorems~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} and
\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Nonlinear entropy transfer.}
Suppose that the quotient expansion above holds and put
\[
d_t(y):=\sum_{n=2}^{L}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}.
\]
Then, with
\[
J(n_1,\ldots,n_r):=
\int_{\RR}u_{n_1}(y)\cdots u_{n_r}(y)\,\omega(\dd y),
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:guide-theorem-b-entropy-transfer}
\begin{split}
D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}))
&=
\sum_{m=4}^{L+2}
\sum_{r=2}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\substack{n_1+\cdots+n_r=m\\ 2\le n_j\le L}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r} \\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad
{}\times J(n_1,\ldots,n_r)t^{-m}
+o(t^{-(L+2)}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The proof is the finite Taylor expansion of
\(\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\) after the change of variables
\(P_t(ty)t\,\dd y=\omega(\dd y)\).  Since \(\Phi(s)\) begins with
\(s^2/2\), a density error \(o(t^{-L})\) first appears in entropy multiplied
by the \(t^{-2}\) density scale, giving \(o(t^{-(L+2)})\).  This transfer is
carried out in Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-density-jet-entropy-transfer}
and specialized to Poisson coefficients in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Corollaries and quantitative margins.}
Setting \(L=2N-2\) in Theorems A and B gives the
one-dimensional universal-polynomial hierarchy
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  Setting \(L=6\) gives the explicit
eighth-order coefficient layer and the finite seventh-moment remainder,
Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}--\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}.
The only quantitative entropy-transfer regime retained in this article uses
the same density order \(L=2N-2\), with a positive moment margin
\(\eta\in(0,1]\); its constants are collected in
  Appendix~\ref{subsubsec:finite-moment-constants-variants}.  The finite-time
  estimate is not part of the main entropy hierarchy.  It is deterministic
  conditional bookkeeping for the identity
\[
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}
=\Delta_6-A_8t^{-2}-t^6E(t)
\]
once bounds on \(A_8\) and on the remainder are fixed independently; an
inserted KL value contributes only the separately supplied \(t^6\)-scaled
error term.

The multidimensional radial Poisson analogue repeats the same two-step
structure after fixing an integer \(L\ge\max\{2,d+1\}\) and assuming
\(\EE|X_c|^L<\infty\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-density-quotient}
gives the tensor density quotient, hence proves
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\); Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets}
transfers that named quotient jet to entropy, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:multidim-coefficient-formula} records the
multiindex coefficient formula.  Lemma~\ref{lem:multidim-parity-low-order-display}
gives the odd cancellations and the displayed \(t^{-4}\) layer, while
Proposition~\ref{prop:multidim-formal-covariance-layer} evaluates the
corresponding algebraic quadratic covariance form as a positive
isotropic/traceless finite quantity under finite covariance alone.  Under
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\), Corollary~\ref{cor:multidim-leading-kl-coefficient}
identifies this same form as the leading \(t^{-4}\) coefficient of the
multidimensional KL jet.
The dimension-dependent moment requirement enters through the quotient
remainder, as quantified by
Proposition~\ref{prop:multidim-large-translation-obstruction}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Hypothesis index.}
Only four hypothesis levels are used in the entropy part.  The
multidimensional quotient theorem uses \(d\ge1\), \(L\ge\max\{2,d+1\}\), a
barycentre, and \(\EE|X_c|^L<\infty\) to prove
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\); that named quotient jet is then the input for the
corresponding KL jet.  The multidimensional covariance formula
uses only finite second moment, but it is only an algebraic quadratic-layer
identity until \((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\) is also available.  In one dimension,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} uses finite centred
\((2N-2)\)nd absolute moment for the universal coefficient hierarchy; its
coefficient-evaluability scope is recorded immediately before that theorem
and then proved by the obstruction propositions following it.  The sole sharp
analytic threshold theorem is
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-leading-kl-variance-threshold}, where finite variance
is necessary and sufficient for a finite leading \(t^{-4}\) KL coefficient.

For the explicit \(N=4\) layer, the fourth moment identifies \(A_6\) and the
sixth moment identifies \(A_8\).  The seventh moment is used only for the
quantitative \(O(t^{-9})\) remainder.  The coefficient-level \(W_2\) defect
corollary uses the sixth-order coefficient defect and no finite-time KL value.
The cutoff-Green tail theorem uses finite variance plus the moving-quotient
domain estimates in Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}.  Conditional
finite-time propagation assumes, rather than certifies, the approximate KL
value, its error radius, the bound on
\(A_8(\nu)\), the remainder bound, and the threshold time.
