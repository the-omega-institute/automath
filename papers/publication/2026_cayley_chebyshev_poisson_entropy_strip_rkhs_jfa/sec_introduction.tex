\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification of \(\RR\) with \(\TT\), and pulls
normalized Haar measure back to the Cauchy law
\[
\omega(\dd x)=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This is the endpoint-normalized form of the classical Cauchy/wrapped-Cauchy
M{\"o}bius correspondence in circular statistics
\cite{KentTyler1988WrappedCauchy,McCullagh1992CauchyModels,
McCullagh1996CauchyMobius,
KatoJones2010MobiusCircle,KatoMcCullagh2020SphereCauchyMobius}.
This normalization makes the fixed-law Poisson comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main-functional}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
\end{equation}
the object whose large-\(t\) behaviour is studied in Cayley--Chebyshev
modes.  The input law \(\nu\) is fixed, \(\bar\gamma\) is its mean, and
\(t\to\infty\) is the Poisson scale.

The basic calculation is a quotient calculation.  Let
\[
X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma
\]
be the centred translation variable.  With \(x=\bar\gamma+ty\), translation
by \(X_c\) is encoded by
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\qquad \varepsilon=X_c/t .
\]
The Cayley angle \(y=\tan\theta\) turns the Taylor coefficients of
\(R_\varepsilon\) into finite trigonometric, equivalently Laurent, modes.
After the matched Cauchy profile has been divided out, the quotient expansion
is therefore reduced to finite mode algebra; the KL expansion is then the
ordinary Taylor expansion of \((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) in
\(L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)\).

\subsection*{Main contribution and mechanism}

The new part has five pieces.  First, finite centred \((2N-2)\)th absolute
moment gives the one-dimensional coefficient expansion through order
\(t^{-2N}\), with each coefficient \(A_{2m}(\nu)\) computed by Laurent
constant-term extraction from the Cayley modes.  Second, the leading
coefficient has a sharp analytic threshold: finite centred variance is
equivalent to a finite \(t^{-4}\) matched-Poisson KL coefficient, and the
value is \(\mu_2^2/8\).  Third, admissible borderline power tails replace the
missing top finite moment by an explicit truncated top-mode quotient and give
the \(t^{-2N}\log t\) layer under stated quotient controls.  Fourth, in
\(\RR^d\) the multidimensional statement
is split into four layers: an abstract quotient hypothesis gives the tensor
entropy jet, a finite centred \(L\)-moment condition supplies that hypothesis,
finite covariance gives only the algebraic quadratic form
\(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\), and a separate finite \((d+1)\)-moment corollary
identifies that form as the actual leading KL coefficient.  Fifth,
under finite centred second moment, the same matched quotient satisfies an
exact Cauchy-generator Bregman dissipation identity whose tail integral
recovers the KL value.

The following theorem package is the main theorem package of the paper.  The
one-dimensional, tail, and Cauchy identities are single statements.  The
multidimensional theorem is deliberately split into four statements, because
the analytic quotient jet, the finite-moment route to that jet, the
covariance algebra, and the actual leading KL consequence are distinct
logical layers.

\subsection*{Consolidated theorem package}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem A: one-dimensional coefficient hierarchy]%
\label{thm:intro-main-1d-coefficients}
Let \(N\ge2\), put \(L=2N-2\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability law on
\(\RR\) with finite mean \(\bar\gamma\) and finite centred \(L\)th absolute
moment.  With
\[
\begin{aligned}
  X_c&=\gamma-\bar\gamma,\\
  \mu_n&=\int_{\RR} X_c^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma)\qquad(2\le n\le L),\\
  h_t&=P_t*\nu,\qquad
  g_t&=P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma),
\end{aligned}
\]
one has
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N}).
\]
Each coefficient \(A_{2m}(\nu)\) is a universal polynomial in
\(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{2m-2}\), evaluated by the Cayley--Chebyshev Laurent
constant-term rule; explicitly it is the coefficient formula of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  All retained odd coefficients
vanish, and the leading coefficient is
\[
A_4(\nu)=\frac{\mu_2^2}{8}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem B: sharp leading variance threshold]%
\label{thm:intro-main-variance-threshold}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability law on \(\RR\) with finite mean \(\bar\gamma\),
put \(X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma\), and set
\[
H(t)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
\]
with the extended nonnegative convention for relative entropy.  Then
\[
\EE X_c^2<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)<\infty .
\]
If the variance is finite, then \(H(t)<\infty\) for every \(t>0\) and
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=\frac{(\EE X_c^2)^2}{8}.
\]
If the variance is infinite, then
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=+\infty
\]
in the extended sense.  This is the only analytic if-and-only-if moment
threshold proved here.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem C: admissible borderline tails]%
\label{thm:intro-main-power-tail}
Let \(N\ge3\), put \(p=2N-2\), and let \(\nu\) be an even probability law
whose density has the form
\[
f(x)=g(x)+\bigl(c\lvert x\rvert^{-p-1}+r(x)\bigr)
      \mathbf1_{\{\lvert x\rvert\ge R\}},
\]
where \(c>0\), \(g\in L^1(\RR)\) is symmetric and nonnegative with
\(\operatorname{supp}g\subset(-R,R)\), and \(r\) is even.  Assume the
perturbative quotient controls
\[
\int_{\lvert x\rvert\ge R}\lvert x\rvert^q\lvert r(x)\rvert\,\dd x
<\infty\qquad(q<p),
\qquad
\int_{R\le\lvert x\rvert\le t}\lvert x\rvert^p r(x)\,\dd x=O(1),
\]
\[
t^{-(p+1)}
\int_{R\le\lvert x\rvert\le t}\lvert x\rvert^{p+1}\lvert r(x)\rvert\,\dd x
=O(t^{-p}),
\qquad
\int_{\lvert x\rvert>t}
\left(1+\frac{x^2}{t^2}\right)\lvert r(x)\rvert\,\dd x
=O(t^{-p}).
\]
Then \(\nu\) is centred, has finite absolute moments of every order \(q<p\),
and
\[
M_p(t):=\EE\!\left[X^p\mathbf1_{\{\lvert X\rvert\le t\}}\right]
=2c\log t+O(1).
\]
Moreover the matched quotient satisfies, in
\(L^\infty(\RR)\cap L^1(\omega)\),
\[
\frac{(P_t*\nu)(ty)}{P_t(ty)}-1
=\sum_{n=2}^{p-1}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}
+u_p(y)t^{-p}M_p(t)+E_t(y),
\qquad
\lVert E_t\rVert_\infty+\lVert E_t\rVert_{L^1(\omega)}=O(t^{-p}),
\]
and therefore
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N-1}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
+2c\,\kappa_N\mu_2\,t^{-2N}\log t
+o(t^{-2N}\log t),
\]
where
\[
\kappa_N=I(2,p)=(-1)^N(N-1)2^{-2N+2}.
\]
The exact power-tail theorem is the special case \(r=0\), with the quotient
expansion proved directly.  No theorem for arbitrary regularly varying tails
is asserted without the displayed quotient controls.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem D1: conditional jet transfer]%
\label{thm:intro-main-multidimensional}
\label{thm:intro-main-multidimensional-quotient}
Let \(d\ge1\) and let \(L\in\NN\) satisfy \(L\ge d+1\).  Assume that
\((\nu,d,L)\) satisfies the matched Poisson quotient jet
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_t^{(d)}(y)
&=\sum_{2\le\lvert\alpha\rvert\le L}
\mu_\alpha U_\alpha^{(d)}(y)t^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert}
+\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}(y),\\
\lVert\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}\rVert_\infty+
\lVert\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}\rVert_{L^1(\Omega_d)}
&=o(t^{-L}),\qquad
\lVert\delta_t^{(d)}\rVert_\infty=O(t^{-2}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Then, with \(h_t=P_t^{(d)}*\nu\) and
\(g_t=P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{m=4}^{L+2}\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)t^{-m}
+o(t^{-(L+2)}),
\]
where the tensor coefficients are those of
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets}; all retained odd
coefficients vanish.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem D2: moment supply]%
\label{thm:intro-main-multidimensional-finite-moment}
Let \(d\ge1\), let \(L\in\NN\) satisfy \(L\ge d+1\), and let \(\nu\) be a
probability measure on \(\RR^d\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\) and finite centred
\(L\)th absolute moment.  Then \((\nu,d,L)\) satisfies
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\), with the explicit quotient remainder estimate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-density-quotient}.  This theorem supplies
the analytic hypothesis in Theorem~D1; it is neither the KL transfer nor the
covariance calculation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Proposition D3: covariance algebra]%
\label{thm:intro-main-multidimensional-covariance}
Let \(d\ge1\), and suppose only that the centred covariance
\(\Sigma=\EE[X_cX_c^{\mathsf T}]\) is finite.  Define
\[
b_\Sigma(y)=
\frac{d+1}{2}\left(
-\frac{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma}{1+|y|^2}
+\frac{(d+3)y^{\mathsf T}\Sigma y}{(1+|y|^2)^2}
\right).
\]
Then the degree-two density mode has the algebraic contraction
\[
\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)
=\frac12\int_{\RR^d}b_\Sigma(y)^2\,\Omega_d(\dd y)
=c_d^{\rm iso}(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2
+c_d^{\rm tr}\|\Sigma_0\|_{\rm HS}^2,
\]
with
\[
c_d^{\rm iso}=
\frac{3(d+1)(7d+9)}
{4d(d+3)(d+5)(d+7)},
\qquad
c_d^{\rm tr}=
\frac{3(d+1)(d+3)}
{4(d+5)(d+7)} .
\]
This displayed identity is only covariance algebra.  It is not, by itself, a
KL asymptotic theorem.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Corollary D4: actual leading KL coefficient]%
\label{thm:intro-main-multidimensional-leading-coefficient}
Let \(d\ge1\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR^d\) with
mean \(\bar\gamma\).  If
\[
\EE|X_c|^{d+1}<\infty,\qquad X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma,
\]
then, with
\[
h_t=P_t^{(d)}*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar\gamma),
\qquad
\Sigma=\EE[X_cX_c^{\mathsf T}],
\]
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)t^{-4}+o(t^{-4}).
\]
Here \(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\) is the algebraic quadratic form in
Proposition~D3.  Thus finite covariance alone gives the number
\(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\), while the finite \((d+1)\)-moment hypothesis gives
the quotient jet through Theorem~D2 and only then turns that number into the
leading KL coefficient.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem E: Cauchy Bregman tail identity]%
\label{thm:intro-main-bregman}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\) and
finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_t&=P_t*\nu,\\
g_t&=P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma),\\
u_t&=h_t/g_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
The nonlocal domain is part of the statement.  For every \(0<a<b<\infty\),
write
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_2&:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\,\dd\nu(\gamma),\\
m&:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\\
M&:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
  L:=3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr).
\]
Then, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x,y\in\RR\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-bregman-domain-quotient-bounds}
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad
\lvert u_t(x)-u_t(y)\rvert\le L\lvert x-y\rvert .
\end{equation}
Define the Bregman kernel by
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\]
and set
\[
\Psi(z):=\mathbf1_{\{\lvert z\rvert<1\}}
\,+\,\lvert z\rvert^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{\lvert z\rvert\ge1\}},
\qquad
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\}.
\]
The compact-window bounds imply the pointwise Bregman majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-bregman-domain-majorant}
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}\,g_t(x)\Psi(x-y)
\qquad (x\ne y,\ t\in[a,b]),
\end{equation}
and the same estimate holds with \(x\) and \(y\) interchanged.  Hence the
symmetrized kernel is dominated on \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) by
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y),
\]
whose integral over \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) equals
\(8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\).  This domain clause makes the following nonlocal
double integral finite on compact time windows and independent of the
annular exhaustion used to construct it.

Define \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\) by annular cutoffs, equivalently by the
nonnegative Lebesgue integral
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t)
=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\]
with the diagonal ignored.  Then \(\mathcal I_\nu\) is measurable and locally
integrable on \((0,\infty)\), and for every \(t>0\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\,\dd s .
\]
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{Results and proof architecture}

The detailed theorem statements and proofs occur in the sections where their
hypotheses are proved or used.  Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} begins with the
Cayley mode calculation:
Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode} gives the modes \(u_n\),
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} defines the universal
coefficient polynomials by Cauchy-weighted mode integrals, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} evaluates those
integrals by a single Laurent constant-term rule.  The finite-moment
quotient remainder and the finite-jet entropy transfer then give the
one-dimensional expansion in Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.
For \(N\ge3\), its finite \((2N-2)\)-moment assumption is the finite data
needed to evaluate the retained universal polynomial.  The leading finite
value is \(A_4(\nu)=\mu_2^2/8\).

The power-tail replacements are proved in
Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} as four separate statements: a
conditional quotient-transfer proposition, an exact power-tail theorem, a
perturbative power-tail theorem, and a monotone slowly varying corollary.
Each statement records the quotient expansion or quotient controls it uses;
the logarithmic coefficient in Theorem~C is the output of that
transfer.

The multidimensional part is separated into algebra and analytic input.
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets} transfers an
abstract quotient jet \((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\) to tensor KL coefficients
\(\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-density-quotient} proves that finite centred
\(L\)-absolute moment, with integer \(L\ge d+1\), is one sufficient condition
for that quotient jet.  Proposition
~\ref{prop:multidim-formal-covariance-layer} computes the finite-covariance
quadratic layer and its isotropic/traceless contraction.

Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy} proves the Cauchy-generator tail identity.
The domain point is isolated there: on each compact time window
\([a,b]\subset(0,\infty)\), finite centred variance gives two-sided quotient
bounds, a Lipschitz bound, and an explicit integrable Bregman majorant for
the moving quotient \(u_t=(P_t*\nu)/P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\).  With that
domain theorem in place, Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail} follows from
\(H'(t)=-\mathcal I_\nu(t)\), nonnegativity of the dissipation, and
\(H(T)\to0\).

The order-eight calculation is stated once in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}.  Its Laurent certificate gives
\[
A_6(\nu)=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64},
\qquad
A_8(\nu)=
\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
The associated sixth-order defect \(\Delta_6\) is used only in
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}, where a non-sharp
one-line moment coupling bounds the distance to the symmetric two-point law
in \(W_2\).

\subsection*{Relation to recent JFA work and scope}

The paper uses the extended nonnegative Kullback--Leibler functional of
\eqref{eq:extended-kl-definition}.  Appendix~\ref{app:related-work-scope}
first gives a recent-\emph{J. Funct. Anal.} comparison table, organized by
the theorem package above, and explicitly marks the rows for which no close
JFA analogue appears to exist.  Older entropy, heat-kernel, Hardy--Stein,
stability, EPI-stability, and transport references are then used only as
background or proof language, not as substitutes for the JFA comparison.

\subsection*{Organization}

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation.  Sections~\ref{sec:cayley-gate}
and~\ref{sec:haar-pullback} record the Cayley and Haar-pullback
normalizations.  Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} proves the
multidimensional entropy jets, the one-dimensional coefficient hierarchy, the
sharp leading variance threshold, the explicit coefficient layer through
order eight, and the sixth-order coefficient-defect consequence.
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}
derives the Cauchy/KL Bregman tail identity by cutoff Green calculus,
verifies the Poisson quotient hypotheses, and then proves the dissipation-tail
coefficient expansion.
Appendix~\ref{app:related-work-scope} gives the theorem-level related-work
comparison and consolidated scope discussion;
Appendix~\ref{app:entropy-integrals} records the weighted trigonometric
normalization behind the Laurent arithmetic;
Appendix~\ref{app:haar-pullback-uniqueness}
records the endpoint-normalized Cayley uniqueness statement.
