\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification $\overline\RR$ of the line with
the circle~$\TT$.  Under this identification normalized Haar measure pulls
back to the Cauchy law
\[
\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This normalization is the common coordinate system of the paper.  It puts
Poisson smoothing on~$\RR$, circle Fourier analysis, Cauchy-weighted
entropy integrals, and the auxiliary secant observation kernel into one
calculus.  This places the paper in the information-theoretic Cauchy setting
surveyed by Verd{\'u}~\cite{Verdu2023Cauchy}, and it is directly adjacent to
closed-form parametric Cauchy divergence formulae: Chyzak--Nielsen compute
the Kullback--Leibler divergence between two Cauchy distributions
\cite{ChyzakNielsen2019CauchyKL}, and Nielsen--Okamura compute
\(f\)-divergences between Cauchy laws \cite{NielsenOkamura2023Cauchy}.
The object studied here is different.  The Cauchy law is used as the
Haar/Poisson normalization in which the smoothed comparison
\(D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))\) has a finite-moment
large-time coefficient expansion.  Thus the present coefficients are not
closed forms for a parametric Cauchy--Cauchy divergence; they are
coefficient-level entropy jets for the matched Poisson/Cauchy relative
entropy of an arbitrary input law with finite moments.

\subsection*{Main results}

The first main result is the finite-moment entropy expansion.  For every
\(N\ge2\), if a probability law~\(\nu\) has mean
$\bar\gamma$ and finite centred $(2N-2)$th absolute moment, then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N})
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\]
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}; the moment threshold for the
universal coefficient \(A_{2N}\) is sharp by
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-kl-moment-threshold-sharpness}.

The second main result is the explicit eighth-order layer.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}
computes the \(t^{-4}\), \(t^{-6}\), and \(t^{-8}\) coefficients at the
finite-sixth-moment threshold, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder} adds the finite-time
\(O(t^{-9})\) estimate under the corresponding seventh-moment hypothesis.
Those coefficients define the defect coordinates used in
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}, which bounds the
fixed-variance moment defect in \(W_2\).  The finite-time version,
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}, is an a posteriori
oracle estimate: its fixed-law statement uses supplied data
\(A_8^{\rm ent}(\nu),B_{\nu,8},T_{\nu,8}\), while the controlled-shell
statement is the single-observation version with constants fixed in advance.
The theorem-by-theorem hypotheses, constant regimes, and downstream outputs
are summarized before the entropy proofs in
Subsection~\ref{sec:entropy-theorem-map}.

Three supporting tools keep the argument checkable without changing this
hierarchy.  Finite root-of-unity sampling verifies the Laurent coefficient
arithmetic; the KL Doob-tail section gives the dissipation-tail
representation \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}=\int_t^\infty I_\nu(s)\dd s\); and the
secant/strip RKHS material gives Hilbert coordinates for the Poisson
observation channel.

\subsection*{Cayley coefficient mechanism}

The comparison kernel
\[
R_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
\]
measures the ratio between a translated Poisson profile and the reference
Cauchy density after the rescaling $x=\bar\gamma+ty$.  The Cayley angle
$y=\tan\theta$ converts the Taylor modes of $R_\varepsilon$ into finite
trigonometric expressions governed by Chebyshev polynomials of the second
kind.  Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode} records this mode formula.
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} then defines the formal
coefficient polynomials $A_k^{\rm form}$ by finite Cauchy-weighted mode
integrals and proves the odd-order cancellation.  At this stage the
calculation is algebraic: a law is evaluated only through the moment
interface in
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}.

The analytic content is the finite-moment passage from this algebraic
calculus to actual large-time entropy coefficients.  The exact retained
orders, the stronger-moment fixed-law rates, the moment-ball uniform version,
and the compact-support convergent-series variant are separated in the
hypothesis guide at the start of Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics}.

Finite phase sampling gives an exactness check for this coefficient calculus.
The main entropy proof uses Laurent constant-coefficient extraction.
Appendix~\ref{sec:finite-phase-sampling} identifies the same circle characters
on root-of-unity grids and records the alias-free recovery formula below the
explicit degree barrier.  The finite-grid moment recovery theorem and the
eighth-order finite-phase verification check the normalized Laurent
constant terms after the analytic asymptotic framework has been established.

\subsection*{Observation channel and strip RKHS}

The observation material gives Hilbert coordinates for a Poisson channel.  The
secant family
\[
\Psi_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{R_\varepsilon(y)-1}{\varepsilon}
\]
has Gram kernel
\[
K(a,b)=\frac{2}{4+(a-b)^2}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-gram-kernel} computes this kernel, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-space-rkhs} identifies the closed secant span with
$L^2_0(\omega)$ and its RKHS completion.  These results are the Hilbert
coordinate system for the appendix projection theorem.

The scalar centred observation functions in
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-central-two-channel} recover the centred law
from the central Poisson trace and its derivative by Laplace uniqueness and
characteristic-function inversion.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-channel-rkhs}
then places the centred fluctuation profile inside the secant RKHS and
identifies the observation values as evaluation functionals.
Appendix~\ref{sec:strip-lattice} transports this RKHS into the Poisson strip
space
$\sqrt{\pi}\,e^{-|D|}L^2(\RR)$ and records the integer-orbit projection
formula.  The resulting observation theorem is a projection theorem: integer
data recover exactly \(\Pi_{\ZZ}F_t\).  The same theorem translates full
channel recovery into a law-side lattice condition: for the observation time
\(t\), integer data recover \(F_t\) itself exactly when
\(q_{F_t}(\xi)=e^{2|\xi|}\widehat F_t(\xi)\) is \(2\pi\)-periodic,
equivalently when the centred law is supported on \(t\ZZ\).  When the full
profile \(q_{F_t}\) is available, a separate Fourier-inversion lemma recovers
the signed measure \(s\,\mu_t(\dd s)\), hence the scaled centred law
\(\mu_t\) away from zero and then its atom at zero by total mass.
The observation-to-entropy interface is
Theorem~\ref{thm:observation-entropy-interface}: the entropy-defect layer uses
only the centred moment jet \((\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6)\), which is supplied by the
full profile, or by integer samples only in the exact lattice-recovery case.
The projected data \(\Pi_{\ZZ}F_t\) alone are not used to infer
\(C_6^{\rm ent}\), \(A_8^{\rm ent}\), or the defect coordinates.

\subsection*{Theorem-level comparison with adjacent work}

The positioning references are not proof inputs.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}
is closest in spirit to entropic-asymptotic work such as
Bobkov--G{\"o}tze \cite{BobkovGotzeEsscher2025}, but it differs from CLT and
Esscher-transform results because the law is fixed and the time parameter is
Poisson smoothing.  Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability} are
coefficient-defect estimates, not functional-inequality stability theorems
of the log-Sobolev or log-HLS type.  The RKHS and lattice-observation
theorems are sampling/projection results for the Poisson secant channel, not
shift-invariant Gaussian sampling theorems.  Appendix~\ref{app:theorem-level-comparison}
contains the theorem-by-theorem comparison table with DOI and arXiv data and
keeps recent JFA comparators separate from older foundational and non-JFA
background.
