\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification $\overline\RR$ of the line with
the circle~$\TT$.  Under this identification normalized Haar measure pulls
back to the Cauchy law
\[
\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This normalization is the common coordinate system of the paper.  It puts
Poisson smoothing on~$\RR$, circle Fourier analysis, Cauchy-weighted
entropy integrals, and the auxiliary secant observation kernel into one
calculus.  This places the paper in the information-theoretic Cauchy setting
surveyed by Verd{\'u}~\cite{Verdu2023Cauchy}, but the object studied here is
not a parametric divergence between two Cauchy laws.  The Cauchy law is used
as the Haar/Poisson normalization in which the smoothed comparison
\(D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))\) has a finite-moment
large-time coefficient expansion.  Thus the explicit \(f\)-divergence
formulae for parametric Cauchy distributions of Nielsen--Okamura
\cite{NielsenOkamura2023Cauchy} are adjacent background for Cauchy
information geometry, while the present coefficients are smoothed-law
entropy jets for an arbitrary input law with finite moments.

\subsection*{Main results}

The contribution hierarchy is as follows.
\emph{Main result 1} is the exact-threshold finite-moment Poisson entropy
theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}, together with the
sharpness statement in
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-kl-moment-threshold-sharpness}.  It identifies
the least fixed-law moment order needed for the universal coefficient
through \(t^{-2N}\): finite centred \((2N-2)\)th absolute moment is enough,
and the coefficient \(A_{2N}\) is not determined below that threshold.
\emph{Main result 2} is the explicit \(N=4\) coefficient audit at the
finite-sixth-moment threshold, Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}, together
with its finite-seventh-moment quantitative ledger,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}.
The defect coordinates and the moment-defect \(W_2\) results are
coefficient-level consequences collected in
Corollaries~\ref{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization}--%
\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification},
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}.  The last of these
is a finite-time Poisson entropy stability readout: after the audited
\(A_8(\nu)t^{-8}\) correction, one large-time entropy value controls the
coefficient defect and hence \(W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)\).  The auxiliary
infrastructure is finite phase sampling, the Doob--Bregman tail identity, the
secant RKHS, and the integer-orbit projection theorem.  These tools provide
coefficient audits, a dissipation-tail representation, and observation
coordinates for the main entropy and observation statements.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{The exact-threshold finite-moment entropy theorem.}
For every $N\ge2$, if a probability law~$\nu$ has mean
$\bar\gamma$ and finite centred $(2N-2)$th absolute moment, then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N})
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\]
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  The coefficient \(A_{2N}\)
depends only on moments through order \(2N-2\), because every entropy monomial
has at least two density factors.  The exact-moment density lemma gives an
\(o(t^{-(2N-2)})\) density remainder, and the entropy quadratic scale turns
that into \(o(t^{-2N})\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-kl-moment-threshold-sharpness}
shows that this threshold is sharp: the top coefficient contains a nonzero
\(\mu_2\mu_{2N-2}\) term for \(N\ge3\), while \(A_4=\mu_2^2/8\) for
\(N=2\), and lower-moment heavy-tail examples make the required top moment
  infinite.  The exact-threshold remainder is restated in
  Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder}.  Positive-margin and
  uniform moment-ball versions, which add finite computable rates and
  thresholds, are recorded in
  Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-margin-retained-order-entropy},
  Corollary~\ref{cor:optimized-retained-order-entropy-bridge},
  Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-moment-entropy-constant-ledger}, and
  Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-moment-ball-entropy-remainder}.

\item \emph{The explicit \(N=4\) coefficient audit and seventh-moment ledger.}
The finite centred sixth absolute moment in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} is the \(N=4\) exact-threshold
hypothesis for the coefficient identity through \(t^{-8}\).  The finite
centred seventh absolute moment in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder} is the
\(N=4,\eta=1\) positive-margin hypothesis and records the quantitative
\(O(t^{-9})\) bound from
Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-margin-retained-order-entropy}, in the optimized
notation of Corollary~\ref{cor:optimized-retained-order-entropy-bridge}.
These two
theorems audit the coefficients \((C_4,C_6,A_8)\); the seventh-moment ledger
adds the quantitative remainder for the same coefficients.  These audited
coefficients then define the sixth- and
eighth-order defect coordinates.  The downstream consequences are the
non-negative defect ladder of
Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}, the strict eighth-order
amplification in Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}, and the
coefficient-defect coupling
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}.  The elementary
fourth-moment defect polynomial is identified with the genuine entropy
coefficient defect by
Lemma~\ref{lem:seventh-moment-bridge} before the \(W_2\) estimate is invoked.
\end{enumerate}

Three supporting tools keep the argument auditable.  Finite root-of-unity
sampling verifies the finite Fourier coefficient arithmetic below the explicit
aliasing barrier.  The KL Doob-tail section re-expresses the same Poisson
comparison quotient through the identity
\(D_{\mathrm{KL}}=\int_t^\infty I_\nu(s)\dd s\).  The secant Gram space and
strip RKHS appendix give the Hilbert-space model for integer-orbit observation
data, including the projection and law-side recovery criterion for that
channel.

\subsection*{Cayley coefficient mechanism}

The comparison kernel
\[
R_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
\]
measures the ratio between a translated Poisson profile and the reference
Cauchy density after the rescaling $x=\bar\gamma+ty$.  The Cayley angle
$y=\tan\theta$ converts the Taylor modes of $R_\varepsilon$ into finite
trigonometric expressions governed by Chebyshev polynomials of the second
kind.  Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode} records this mode formula.
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} then defines the formal
coefficient polynomials $A_k^{\rm form}$ by finite Cauchy-weighted mode
integrals and proves the odd-order cancellation.  At this stage the
calculation is algebraic: a law is evaluated only through the moment
interface in
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}.

The analytic content of the first main package is the exact-threshold
finite-moment density-to-entropy passage.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}
shows that finite centred $(2N-2)$th absolute moment is sufficient for the
evaluated even coefficients to be the actual coefficients of the large-time
Poisson relative entropy through order~$t^{-2N}$.  The proof uses the
little-oh exact-moment density truncation before Taylor expansion of
\(\Phi\), and
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-kl-moment-threshold-sharpness} proves that the
moment order cannot be lowered for the same universal coefficient.  The
  exact-threshold remainder theorem records the same \(o(t^{-2N})\)
  conclusion after subtracting the retained even polynomial.  The companion
  positive-margin ledgers record fixed-law constants under stronger moment
  hypotheses, while
Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-moment-ball-entropy-remainder} records the
corresponding uniform statement on centred moment balls.  The compact-support
analytic theorem
Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic-compact-support-interchange} records the
compact-support convergent-series version of the same coefficients.

Finite phase sampling gives an exactness check for this coefficient calculus.
The main entropy proof uses Laurent constant-coefficient extraction.
Appendix~\ref{sec:finite-phase-sampling} identifies the same circle characters
on root-of-unity grids and records the alias-free recovery formula below the
explicit degree barrier.  The finite-grid moment recovery theorem and the
eighth-order finite-phase verification check the normalized Laurent
constant terms after the analytic asymptotic framework has been established.

\subsection*{\texorpdfstring{Eighth-order defect and moment-defect \(W_2\) estimates}{Eighth-order defect and moment-defect W2 estimates}}

The second main package specializes the finite coefficient calculus to the
order-$t^{-8}$ layer.  The coefficient identity itself uses only a finite
centred sixth absolute moment, by Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}.  The
analytic $O(t^{-9})$ remainder uses the finite centred seventh absolute
moment in Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}, which is
Corollary~\ref{cor:optimized-retained-order-entropy-bridge} with
\(N=4,\eta=1\).  The subsequent corollaries use that audited jet.
Corollary~\ref{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization}
records that the displayed jet factors through centred moments through order
six.  From the sixth- and eighth-order coefficients one obtains non-negative
defect coordinates
(Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}) and strict eighth-order
amplification
(Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}).  The symmetric two-point
law at fixed variance is the equality case, as stated in
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-two-point-defect-rigidity}.

The \(W_2\) consequence is placed after the seventh-moment coefficient
identification.
With only a finite fourth moment, \(\Delta_6(\nu)\) is an algebraic
polynomial and satisfies the coupling
estimate of Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:seventh-moment-bridge} uses the seventh-moment entropy
expansion to identify that polynomial with the genuine sixth-order entropy
defect~$\Delta_6$.  With this identification,
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} gives the moment-defect
$W_2$ estimate
\[
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/2}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability} turns this into a
finite-time Poisson entropy stability readout.  With
\[
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)
=t^6\left(
\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}-\frac{7\sigma^6}{64t^6}
-D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
\right),
\]
it proves
\[
\left|\Delta_6(\nu)-\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)
-A_8(\nu)t^{-2}\right|
\le B_{\nu,8}t^{-3}
\]
and inserts this finite-time upper bound for \(\Delta_6(\nu)\) into the
displayed \(W_2\) modulus.  Appendix~\ref{app:finite-time-defect-readouts}
records the constant-dependence regimes and the controlled-moment-shell
uniform version.

\subsection*{Observation channel and strip RKHS}

The observation material gives Hilbert coordinates for a Poisson channel.  The
secant family
\[
\Psi_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{R_\varepsilon(y)-1}{\varepsilon}
\]
has Gram kernel
\[
K(a,b)=\frac{2}{4+(a-b)^2}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-gram-kernel} computes this kernel, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-space-rkhs} identifies the closed secant span with
$L^2_0(\omega)$ and its RKHS completion.  These results are the Hilbert
coordinate system for the appendix projection theorem.

The scalar centred observation functions in
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-central-two-channel} recover the centred law
from the central Poisson trace and its derivative by Laplace uniqueness and
characteristic-function inversion.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-channel-rkhs}
then places the centred fluctuation profile inside the secant RKHS and
identifies the observation values as evaluation functionals.
Appendix~\ref{sec:strip-lattice} transports this RKHS into the Poisson strip
space
$\sqrt{\pi}\,e^{-|D|}L^2(\RR)$ and records the integer-orbit projection
formula.  The resulting observation theorem is a projection theorem: integer
data recover exactly \(\Pi_{\ZZ}F_t\).  The same theorem translates full
channel recovery into a law-side lattice condition: for the observation time
\(t\), integer data recover \(F_t\) itself exactly when
\(q_{F_t}(\xi)=e^{2|\xi|}\widehat F_t(\xi)\) is \(2\pi\)-periodic,
equivalently when the centred law is supported on \(t\ZZ\).  When the full
profile \(q_{F_t}\) is available, a separate Fourier-inversion lemma recovers
the signed measure \(s\,\mu_t(\dd s)\), hence the scaled centred law
\(\mu_t\) away from zero and then its atom at zero by total mass.

\subsection*{Moment asymptotics for heat and fractional heat semigroups}

The structurally closest precedent is not only recent JFA work, but the older
moment-asymptotic PDE literature for heat-type semigroups.  The decomposition
of Duoandikoetxea--Zuazua~\cite{DuoandikoetxeaZuazua1992} writes a function
with finite weighted moments as a finite sum of derivatives of the Dirac mass,
with a remainder controlled in a way adapted to heat smoothing.  Applied to
the heat equation this gives the familiar profile
\[
e^{t\Delta}f
\sim
\sum_{|\alpha|\le K}
\frac{(-1)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}
\Bigl(\int x^\alpha f(x)\,dx\Bigr)\partial^\alpha G_t,
\]
with \(G_t\) the Gaussian kernel and with the order governed by weighted
\(L^1\)-moments of the initial datum.  Escobedo--Zuazua
\cite{EscobedoZuazua1991} use related moment profiles in
convection--diffusion large-time analysis.  Later heat-equation and nonlinear
parabolic expansions, for example Ishige--Kawakami
\cite{IshigeKawakami2012}, refine the profile hierarchy and remainder
estimates.  The fractional-heat analogue replaces \(G_t\) by the stable
self-similar kernel and the scaling \(t^{1/2}\) by \(t^{1/\alpha}\), as in
Ishige--Kawakami--Michihisa~\cite{IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2017}; the
normalization of \((-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}\) is recalled in
Kwa{\'s}nicki~\cite{Kwasnicki2017}.  Related information-theoretic stable
limits are treated by Johnson~\cite{JohnsonStableDeBruijn2013} and
Toscani~\cite{Toscani2015,Toscani2016}.  Recent geometric variants include
the heat-kernel asymptotics of Anker--Papageorgiou--Zhang
\cite{AnkerPapageorgiouZhang2023HeatSymmetricSpaces} and the
fractional-Laplacian operator asymptotics of
Papageorgiou~\cite{Papageorgiou2024FractionalLargeTime}.

This paper is close to that literature in using moments to organize a
large-time expansion, but the object being expanded is different.  The heat
and fractional-heat results are primarily density or profile expansions,
possibly with PDE nonlinearities or information functionals attached to a
stable self-similar limit.  The present expansion is for the relative entropy
\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
\]
of the one-dimensional Poisson semigroup against a translated Cauchy profile.
The proof does not expand \(P_t*\nu\) as a distributional derivative series.
It first divides by the comparison profile and uses the exact quotient
identity
\[
\frac{P_t(x-\gamma)}{P_t(x-\bar\gamma)}
=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\Delta/t)^2},
\qquad x=\bar\gamma+ty,\quad \Delta=\gamma-\bar\gamma,
\]
then integrates the nonlinear entropy density
\(\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\) against the fixed Cauchy weight
\(\omega(dy)=\pi^{-1}(1+y^2)^{-1}\,dy\).  The Cayley transform converts the
resulting rational modes into Laurent polynomials, so the entropy
coefficients are constant terms of products of quotient modes.

This quotient-and-entropy step is exactly what the heat/fractional-heat
moment expansions do not provide.  A density expansion through order \(K\)
does not by itself identify which products survive after applying
\(\Phi\), why the coefficient through \(t^{-2N}\) only needs the centred
\((2N-2)\)-moment, or why the top contribution contains the nonzero term
\(\kappa_N\mu_2\mu_{2N-2}\) in
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-kl-moment-threshold-sharpness}.  Nor does it
produce the explicit Poisson entropy jet
\(C_4,C_6,A_8\) in Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}; the seventh-moment
quantitative ledger controls the remainder for the same coefficients.
Conversely, the present theorem does not assert Gaussian or stable-kernel
asymptotics; it adds a Cauchy/Poisson relative-entropy coefficient calculus
and a Cayley--Laurent audit for one smoothed law.
