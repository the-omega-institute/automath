\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification of \(\RR\) with \(\TT\), and pulls
normalized Haar measure back to the Cauchy law
\[
\omega(\dd x)=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This is the endpoint-normalized form of the classical Cauchy/wrapped-Cauchy
M{\"o}bius correspondence in circular statistics
\cite{KentTyler1988WrappedCauchy,McCullagh1992CauchyModels,
McCullagh1996CauchyMobius,
KatoJones2010MobiusCircle,KatoMcCullagh2020SphereCauchyMobius}.
This normalization makes the fixed-law Poisson comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main-functional}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\bigr)
\end{equation}
the object whose large-\(t\) behaviour is studied in Cayley--Chebyshev
modes.  The input law \(\nu\) is fixed, \(\bar{\gamma}\) is its mean, and
\(t\to\infty\) is the Poisson scale.

The basic calculation is a quotient calculation.  Let
\[
X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}
\]
be the centred translation variable.  With \(x=\bar{\gamma}+ty\), translation
by \(X_c\) is encoded by
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\qquad \varepsilon=X_c/t .
\]
The Cayley angle \(y=\tan\theta\) turns the Taylor coefficients of
\(R_\varepsilon\) into finite trigonometric, equivalently Laurent, modes.
After the matched Cauchy profile has been divided out, the quotient expansion
is therefore reduced to finite mode algebra; the KL expansion is then the
ordinary Taylor expansion of \((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) in
\(L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)\).

\subsection*{Main contribution}

The paper has three main theorem statements about the matched Poisson KL
functional \eqref{eq:intro-main-functional}.  The first is the
one-dimensional Cayley--Chebyshev hierarchy together with the sharp
finite-variance threshold for the leading KL coefficient.  The second is the
multidimensional quotient-to-KL theorem: an assumed matched Poisson quotient
jet gives tensor KL coefficients, and finite centred \(L\)-absolute moment
with \(L\ge d+1\) is only one sufficient way to supply that quotient jet.  The
third is the exact Cauchy-generator Bregman tail identity.  The verified
borderline-tail subclasses, the multidimensional finite-moment and covariance
leading coefficient, and the normalized fourth-moment defect consequence are
recorded below as corollaries or algebraic consequences of these three
statements, not as additional main theorems.

The three main theorems are stated first and reserve the numbered internal
theorem labels for proof locations.  A separate ``Consequences'' subsection
then records the conditional transfer lemma, the
borderline-tail verification classes, the multidimensional finite-moment and
covariance consequences, and the fourth-moment defect corollary.  The scope
ledger after these statements records which hypotheses are assumptions, which
are verified consequences, and where the proof machinery enters.

\subsection*{The three main theorems}

\begin{introresult}{Main Theorem A: one-dimensional coefficient hierarchy and variance threshold}
Let \(N\ge2\), put \(L=2N-2\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability law on
\(\RR\) with finite mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and finite centred \(L\)th absolute
moment.  With
\[
\begin{aligned}
  X_c&=\gamma-\bar{\gamma},\\
  \mu_n&=\int_{\RR} X_c^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma)\qquad(2\le n\le L),\\
  h_t&=P_t*\nu,\qquad
  g_t&=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),
\end{aligned}
\]
one has
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N}).
\]
Each coefficient \(A_{2m}(\nu)\) is a universal polynomial in
\(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{2m-2}\), evaluated by the Cayley--Chebyshev Laurent
constant-term rule; explicitly it is the coefficient formula of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  All retained odd coefficients
vanish, and the leading coefficient is
\[
A_4(\nu)=\frac{\mu_2^2}{8}.
\]
For an arbitrary probability law on \(\RR\) with finite mean
\(\bar{\gamma}\), the leading coefficient has the sharp threshold below.
Put \(X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}\) and set
\[
H(t)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\bigr)
\]
with the extended nonnegative convention for relative entropy.  Then
\[
\EE X_c^2<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)<\infty .
\]
If the variance is finite, then \(H(t)<\infty\) for every \(t>0\) and
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=\frac{(\EE X_c^2)^2}{8}.
\]
If the variance is infinite, then
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=+\infty
\]
in the extended sense.  This is the only analytic if-and-only-if moment
threshold proved here.
\end{introresult}

\begin{introresult}{Main Theorem B: conditional multidimensional quotient-to-KL transfer}
Let \(d\ge1\) and let \(L\in\NN\) satisfy \(L\ge2\).  Assume that
\((\nu,d,L)\) satisfies the full matched Poisson quotient jet
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\), including the large-\(t\) entropy-existence clause
\((\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_t^{(d)}(y)
&=\sum_{2\le\lvert\alpha\rvert\le L}
\mu_\alpha U_\alpha^{(d)}(y)t^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert}
+\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}(y),\\
\lVert\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}\rVert_\infty+
\lVert\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}\rVert_{L^1(\Omega_d)}
&=o(t^{-L}),\qquad
\lVert\delta_t^{(d)}\rVert_\infty=O(t^{-2}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Then, with \(h_t=P_t^{(d)}*\nu\) and
\(g_t=P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{m=4}^{L+2}\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)t^{-m}
+o(t^{-(L+2)}),
\]
where the tensor coefficients are those of
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets}; all retained odd
coefficients vanish.

The finite-moment supply result is separate from this transfer theorem.  If
\(L\ge d+1\) and \(\nu\) has finite centred \(L\)th absolute moment, then
Corollary~II.1 verifies \((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\) and
\((\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\); inserting that verified quotient jet into the
conditional transfer above gives the finite-moment tensor KL expansion.
\end{introresult}

\begin{introresult}{Main Theorem C: Cauchy Bregman tail identity}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and
finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_t&=P_t*\nu,\\
g_t&=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\\
u_t&=h_t/g_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
The nonlocal domain is part of the statement.  For every \(0<a<b<\infty\),
write
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_2&:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^2\,\dd\nu(\gamma),\\
m&:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\\
M&:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
  L:=3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr).
\]
Then, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x,y\in\RR\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-bregman-domain-quotient-bounds}
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad
\lvert u_t(x)-u_t(y)\rvert\le L\lvert x-y\rvert .
\end{equation}
Define the Bregman kernel by
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\]
and set
\[
\Psi(z):=\mathbf1_{\{\lvert z\rvert<1\}}
\,+\,\lvert z\rvert^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{\lvert z\rvert\ge1\}},
\qquad
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\}.
\]
The compact-window bounds imply the pointwise Bregman majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-bregman-domain-majorant}
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}\,g_t(x)\Psi(x-y)
\qquad (x\ne y,\ t\in[a,b]),
\end{equation}
and the same estimate holds with \(x\) and \(y\) interchanged.  Hence the
symmetrized kernel is dominated on \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) by
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y),
\]
whose integral over \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) equals
\(8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\).  The same one-sided majorant gives the compact-window
dissipation bound
\[
\int_a^b\mathcal I_\nu(s)\,\dd s
\le \frac{4C_{a,b,\nu}}{\pi}(b-a)
\]
once \(\mathcal I_\nu\) is defined below.  This domain clause makes the
following nonlocal double integral finite on compact time windows and
independent of the annular exhaustion used to construct it.

Define \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\) by annular cutoffs, equivalently by the
nonnegative Lebesgue integral
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t)
=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\]
with the diagonal ignored.  Then \(\mathcal I_\nu\) is measurable and locally
integrable on \((0,\infty)\), and for every \(t>0\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\,\dd s .
\]
\end{introresult}

\subsection*{Consequences}

\begin{introresult}{Lemma I.1: conditional missing-top-moment transfer}
Let \(N\ge3\), let \(p=2N-2\ge4\), and put
\[
  \Delta_t(y):=\frac{(P_t*\nu)(ty)}{P_t(ty)}-1 .
\]
Assume first that \(\nu\) is symmetric, has finite absolute moments of every
order \(q<p\), and satisfies the conditional quotient expansion
\[
  \Delta_t(y)
  =A_t(y)
  +u_p(y)t^{-p}M_p(t)+e_t(y),
  \qquad
  A_t(y):=\sum_{n=2}^{p-1}\mu_n u_n(y)t^{-n},
\]
with
\[
  \|e_t\|_\infty+\|e_t\|_{L^1(\omega)}
  =o(t^{-p}M_p(t)),
\]
and the growth bounds
\[
  M_p(t)\to\infty,\qquad M_p(t)=o(t^{p-2}).
\]
The growth condition \(M_p(t)\to\infty\) is not a substitute for the displayed
quotient expansion; without that quotient error estimate no entropy conclusion
is asserted.
Then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N-1}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
+J(2,p)\mu_2t^{-2N}M_p(t)
+o(t^{-2N}M_p(t)),
\]
where
\[
\mu_2=\int x^2\,\nu(\dd x),\qquad
J(2,p)=(-1)^N(N-1)2^{-2N+2}.
\]
\end{introresult}

\begin{introresult}{Corollary I.2: exact power-tail verification}
Let \(N\ge3\), put \(p=2N-2\), fix \(R>0\) and \(c>0\), and let \(\nu\)
have symmetric probability density
\[
f(x)=g(x)+c|x|^{-p-1}\mathbf1_{\{|x|\ge R\}},
\]
where \(g\ge0\) is symmetric and compactly supported in \((-R,R)\),
\(f\ge0\), and \(\int_{\RR}f=1\).  Put
\[
M_p(t)=\int_{|x|\le t}x^pf(x)\,\dd x=2c\log t+O(1).
\]
Then, for
\[
  \Delta_t(y)=\frac{(P_t*\nu)(ty)}{P_t(ty)}-1,
  \qquad
  \mu_n=\int x^nf(x)\,\dd x\quad(2\le n<p),
\]
the quotient estimate is the first conclusion:
\[
\Delta_t(y)=
\sum_{n=2}^{p-1}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}
+2c\,u_p(y)t^{-p}\log t+E_t(y),
\qquad
\|E_t\|_\infty+\|E_t\|_{L^1(\omega)}=O(t^{-p}).
\]
This verifies the quotient hypothesis of Lemma~I.1.  Consequently,
after this quotient verification,
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N-1}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
+2c\,J(2,p)\mu_2t^{-2N}\log t
+o(t^{-2N}\log t).
\]
\end{introresult}

\begin{introresult}{Corollary I.3: perturbative power-tail verification}
Let \(N\ge3\), put \(p=2N-2\), fix \(R>0\) and \(c>0\), and let \(\nu\)
have even probability density
\[
f(x)=g(x)+\bigl(c\lvert x\rvert^{-p-1}+r(x)\bigr)
      \mathbf1_{\{\lvert x\rvert\ge R\}},
\]
where \(g\ge0\) is symmetric and compactly supported in \((-R,R)\),
\(f\ge0\), \(\int_{\RR}f=1\), and \(r\) is even.  Require
\[
\int_{\lvert x\rvert\ge R}\lvert x\rvert^q\lvert r(x)\rvert\,\dd x
<\infty\qquad(0\le q<p),
\]
together with the three perturbative controls
\[
\int_{R\le\lvert x\rvert\le t}\lvert x\rvert^p r(x)\,\dd x=O(1),
\]
\[
t^{-(p+1)}
\int_{R\le\lvert x\rvert\le t}\lvert x\rvert^{p+1}\lvert r(x)\rvert\,\dd x
=O(t^{-p}),
\qquad
\int_{\lvert x\rvert>t}
\left(1+\frac{x^2}{t^2}\right)\lvert r(x)\rvert\,\dd x
=O(t^{-p}).
\]
Put
\[
M_p(t)=\int_{|x|\le t}x^pf(x)\,\dd x=2c\log t+O(1),
\]
and
\[
  \Delta_t(y)=\frac{(P_t*\nu)(ty)}{P_t(ty)}-1,\qquad
  \mu_n=\int x^nf(x)\,\dd x\quad(2\le n<p).
\]
Then the quotient estimate is the first conclusion:
\[
\Delta_t(y)=
\sum_{n=2}^{p-1}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}
+u_p(y)t^{-p}M_p(t)+E_t(y),
\qquad
\|E_t\|_\infty+\|E_t\|_{L^1(\omega)}=O(t^{-p}).
\]
This verifies the quotient hypothesis of Lemma~I.1, and therefore,
after this quotient verification,
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N-1}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
+2c\,J(2,p)\mu_2t^{-2N}\log t
+o(t^{-2N}\log t).
\]
No conclusion is asserted for perturbations that fail these quotient-scale
controls.
\end{introresult}

\begin{introresult}{Corollary I.4: monotone slowly varying tail verification}
Let \(N\ge3\), put \(p=2N-2\), fix \(R>0\) and \(c>0\), and let \(\nu\)
have symmetric probability density
\[
f(x)=g(x)+c|x|^{-p-1}L(|x|)\mathbf1_{\{|x|\ge R\}},
\]
where \(g\ge0\) is symmetric and compactly supported in \((-R,R)\),
\(f\ge0\), \(\int_{\RR}f=1\), and \(L\) is nonnegative, eventually positive,
eventually monotone, and slowly varying.  If
\[
M_p(t)=\int_{|x|\le t}x^pf(x)\,\dd x
\]
tends to \(+\infty\), then the monotone slowly varying estimates verify the
conditional quotient hypothesis only by first proving the quotient estimate.
Indeed, with
\[
  \Delta_t(y)=\frac{(P_t*\nu)(ty)}{P_t(ty)}-1,\qquad
  \mu_n=\int x^nf(x)\,\dd x\quad(2\le n<p),
\]
one has
\[
\Delta_t(y)=
\sum_{n=2}^{p-1}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}
+u_p(y)t^{-p}M_p(t)+e_t(y),
\qquad
\|e_t\|_\infty+\|e_t\|_{L^1(\omega)}
=o(t^{-p}M_p(t)).
\]
This verifies the quotient hypothesis of Lemma~I.1 with this
\(M_p(t)\).  Only after that quotient verification does one obtain the entropy
layer \(J(2,p)\mu_2t^{-2N}M_p(t)\):
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N-1}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
+J(2,p)\mu_2t^{-2N}M_p(t)
+o(t^{-2N}M_p(t)).
\]
\end{introresult}

\begin{introresult}{Corollary II.1: finite-moment supply of the quotient jet}
Let \(d\ge1\), let \(L\in\NN\) satisfy \(L\ge d+1\), and let \(\nu\) be a
probability measure on \(\RR^d\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and finite centred
\(L\)th absolute moment.  Then \((\nu,d,L)\) satisfies
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\), including \((\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\), with the explicit
quotient remainder estimate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-density-quotient}.  This theorem supplies
the analytic and existence hypotheses in Theorem~II; it is neither the KL
transfer nor the covariance calculation.
\end{introresult}

\begin{introresult}{Proposition II.2: covariance algebra}
Let \(d\ge1\), and suppose only that the centred covariance
\(\Sigma=\EE[X_cX_c^{\mathsf T}]\) is finite.  Define
\[
b_\Sigma(y)=
\frac{d+1}{2}\left(
-\frac{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma}{1+|y|^2}
+\frac{(d+3)y^{\mathsf T}\Sigma y}{(1+|y|^2)^2}
\right).
\]
Then the degree-two density mode has the algebraic contraction
\[
\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)
=\frac12\int_{\RR^d}b_\Sigma(y)^2\,\Omega_d(\dd y)
=c_d^{\rm iso}(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2
+c_d^{\rm tr}\|\Sigma_0\|_{\rm HS}^2,
\]
with
\[
c_d^{\rm iso}=
\frac{3(d+1)(7d+9)}
{4d(d+3)(d+5)(d+7)},
\qquad
c_d^{\rm tr}=
\frac{3(d+1)(d+3)}
{4(d+5)(d+7)} .
\]
This displayed identity is only covariance algebra.  It is not, by itself, a
KL asymptotic theorem and does not imply \((\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\) or
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\).
\end{introresult}

\begin{introresult}{Proposition II.2bis: second-order quotient criterion}
Under the finite-covariance assumptions of Proposition~II.2, finite
covariance gives the algebraic quantity
\[
\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)=\frac12\int_{\RR^d}b_\Sigma(y)^2\,\Omega_d(\dd y).
\]
This is the whole finite-covariance conclusion.  If, in addition, the separate
second-order quotient criterion
\[
  \delta_t^{(d)}(y)=t^{-2}b_\Sigma(y)+r_t(y),\qquad
  \|r_t\|_\infty+\|r_t\|_{L^1(\Omega_d)}=o(t^{-2}),\qquad
  \|\delta_t^{(d)}\|_\infty=O(t^{-2}).
\]
holds, then
\[
D_{\rm KL}(h_t\|g_t)=\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)t^{-4}+o(t^{-4}),
\]
because \(\Phi(\delta_t^{(d)})=\frac12t^{-4}b_\Sigma^2+o(t^{-4})\) in
\(L^1(\Omega_d)\).
For \(d\ge2\), finite covariance alone does not imply the full
\(L^\infty\)-based second-order quotient criterion.  What remains open is
whether a weaker \(L^1(\Omega_d)\)-based criterion, strengthened by the
appropriate nonlinear entropy-integrability control, is sufficient for the
leading KL coefficient.  Thus this proposition is a one-way conditional KL
transfer, not a finite-covariance KL theorem.
\end{introresult}

\begin{introresult}{Corollary II.3: actual leading multidimensional KL coefficient}
Let \(d\ge1\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR^d\) with
mean \(\bar{\gamma}\).  If
\[
\EE|X_c|^{d+1}<\infty,\qquad X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma},
\]
then, with
\[
h_t=P_t^{(d)}*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),
\qquad
\Sigma=\EE[X_cX_c^{\mathsf T}],
\]
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)t^{-4}+o(t^{-4}).
\]
Here \(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\) is the algebraic quadratic form in
Proposition~II.2.  Thus finite covariance alone gives the number
\(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\), while the finite \((d+1)\)-moment hypothesis gives
the quotient jet and \((\mathrm{EX}_{d,d+1})\) through Corollary~II.1 and only
then turns that number into the leading KL coefficient.
Equivalently: by Corollary~II.1, \(\EE|X_c|^{d+1}<\infty\) implies the
displayed quotient criterion, and by Proposition~II.2 the coefficient is
\(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\).
\end{introresult}

\begin{introresult}{Corollary I.5: normalized fourth-moment defect and scaled coefficient consequence}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability law on \(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\),
positive variance \(\sigma^2>0\), and finite centred fourth absolute moment.
Set
\[
  X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma},\qquad
  \mu_k=\EE X_c^k\quad (k=3,4),\qquad
  X=\frac{X_c}{\sigma},\qquad
  \beta_3=\EE X^3,\qquad
  Q_2=X^2-1-\beta_3X,
\]
and put
\[
\eta=\frac{\beta_3^2}{32}+\frac{\EE Q_2^2}{8}.
\]
The normalized fourth-moment algebra gives
\[
\eta
=\frac{\EE X^4-1}{8}-\frac{3\beta_3^2}{32}\ge0,
\qquad
\eta=0\Longleftrightarrow
\mathcal L(X)=\frac12(\delta_{-1}+\delta_1).
\]
The elementary sign--Rademacher coupling gives
\[
W_2\!\left(\mathcal L(X),\frac12(\delta_{-1}+\delta_1)\right)
\le 4\eta^{1/4}+8\eta^{1/2}.
\]
Now define the sixth-order coefficient identified by the coefficient
hierarchy through order \(t^{-6}\),
\[
A_6(\nu)=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64}.
\]
The scaled two-point coefficient defect is then
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)
:=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-A_6(\nu)
=\sigma^6\left(\frac{\beta_3^2}{32}
+\frac{\EE Q_2^2}{8}\right).
\]
If
\[
  \nu_c=(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})_\#\nu,\qquad
  \rho_\sigma=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma),
\]
then
\[
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/2}.
\]
Moreover \(\Delta_6(\nu)\ge0\), and equality holds if and only if
\(\nu_c=\rho_\sigma\).  This is a scaled coefficient corollary of the
normalized fourth-moment algebra, proved as
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}.  It gives no finite-time KL
stability: a single finite-\(t\) KL value does not determine the coefficient
\(A_6\), the quotient remainder, or the defect \(\Delta_6(\nu)\).
\end{introresult}

The order-eight certificate is supplied in
Appendix~\ref{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate} for verification.

\subsection*{Hypotheses and scope}
\label{subsec:intro-hypotheses-scope}

This subsection is the single scope ledger for the theorem statements and
their consequences above.
The formal hypotheses remain in the statements; the paragraphs here record
which hypotheses are conditional, which are verified sufficient conditions,
and which non-sharpness or non-converse claims are intentionally not made.

The paper uses the extended nonnegative Kullback--Leibler functional of
\eqref{eq:extended-kl-definition}.  Under finite centred second moment in
dimension one the matched-Poisson KL value is finite for every \(t>0\);
without that hypothesis the sharp-threshold theorem is interpreted in
\([0,\infty]\).  Thus the conclusion
\[
t^4D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}))\to+\infty
\]
includes the case where the KL value is already \(+\infty\) at large times.
The only analytic if-and-only-if moment threshold proved here is the leading
one-dimensional variance threshold in Theorem~I.

For Theorem~I, the finite \((2N-2)\)-moment assumption is the finite-moment
level used to evaluate the retained universal coefficient polynomial
\(A_{2N}\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-kl-moment-threshold-sharpness}
proves an algebraic lower-moment obstruction for that universal polynomial:
because \(A_{2N}^{\rm form}\) has a nonzero
\(\mathfrak m_2\mathfrak m_{2N-2}\) term, this same polynomial cannot be
evaluated from centred moments of orders at most \(2N-3\) on the full
finite-moment class.  This is not asserted to be a sharp analytic tail
threshold for every possible non-polynomial asymptotic regime.

Lemma~I.1 is conditional.  It assumes the displayed quotient expansion and
the error estimate in \(L^\infty(\RR)\cap L^1(\omega)\); lower moments
together with \(M_p(t)\to\infty\) do not imply its conclusion by themselves.
Corollaries~I.2--I.4 are verification classes for that quotient input:
exact power tails, perturbative power tails with the stated controls on
\(r\), and eventually monotone slowly varying borderline tails with
\(M_p(t)\to\infty\).  They are not an unrestricted regular-variation theorem.

Theorem~II is also conditional: \((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\), including
\((\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\), is the analytic input.  The lower bound \(L\ge d+1\)
is not part of this abstract transfer; it enters only through
Corollary~II.1, where finite centred \(L\)-absolute moment is proved to be a
sufficient way to obtain the quotient jet.  No sharp dimension-\(d\) moment
threshold, converse, or finite-covariance sufficiency theorem is asserted.

Proposition~II.2 is only covariance algebra.  It computes
\[
\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)=\frac12\int b_\Sigma^2\,\dd\Omega_d
\]
from finite covariance, but it does not supply a KL asymptotic.  The leading
KL coefficient \(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)t^{-4}\) follows under the separate
second-order quotient criterion in Proposition~II.2bis, and Corollary~II.3 uses
finite \((d+1)\)-moment only as a sufficient condition to supply that
criterion through the multidimensional quotient theorem.  In dimensions
\(d\ge2\), finite covariance alone does not imply the full \(L^\infty\)-based
second-order quotient criterion: it does not prevent quotient peaks from far
sparse atoms, so it cannot give
\(\|\delta_t^{(d)}\|_\infty=O(t^{-2})\) or
\(\|r_t\|_\infty=o(t^{-2})\) by itself.  What remains open is whether a
weaker \(L^1(\Omega_d)\)-based criterion, with the nonlinear
entropy-integrability needed to pass through
\(\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\), is sufficient for the leading KL coefficient.

Theorem~III has a nonlocal domain hypothesis rather than a coefficient
evaluability issue.  On every compact time window, finite centred variance
gives the two-sided quotient bounds, the Lipschitz bound, and the Bregman
majorant stated in the theorem.  The identity is proved by the Cauchy cutoff
Green formula and the chain rule for the bounded moving quotient.  Pointwise
dissipation asymptotics would require additional differentiated-remainder
input; the coefficient theorems here use only tail integrals and fixed-window
averages.

Corollary~I.5 is first a normalized fourth-moment algebra statement and
elementary sign--Rademacher \(W_2\) coupling.  The entropy coefficient enters
only afterwards, through the identified \(A_6\) coefficient, to scale the
defect by \(\sigma^6\).  It is not an optimizer-stability theorem for a sharp
functional inequality, does not claim sharp constants, and gives no
finite-time KL stability: a single finite-\(t\) value of
\(D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))\) does not determine
\(A_6\), the quotient remainder, or the defect.

\subsection*{Recent JFA comparison at theorem level}
\label{subsec:intro-recent-jfa-comparison}

The closest recent \emph{J. Funct. Anal.} neighbours are adjacent in
method or vocabulary, but not in theorem type.  Bobkov--G{\"o}tze's
Esscher-transform CLT paper \cite{BobkovGotzeEsscher2025} concerns changing
normalized sums and normal approximation.  Theorem~I instead fixes one input
law and expands the single-scale continuous Cauchy-kernel quantity
\[
  D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
\]
as \(t\to\infty\), including explicit Cayley--Chebyshev coefficients and the
finite-variance threshold for the leading KL coefficient.  The heat-profile
paper \cite{AnkerPapageorgiouZhang2023HeatSymmetricSpaces} proves linear
large-time profiles on noncompact symmetric spaces, while Theorem~II is a
conditional nonlinear quotient-to-KL transfer for Euclidean radial
Poisson-kernel quotients with tensor coefficients.  The polarized
Hardy--Stein paper
\cite{BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein} supplies an
abstract nonlocal identity; Theorem~III proves the compact-window domain
bounds for the moving quotient
\((P_t*\nu)/P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\) and the exact Cauchy-scale KL tail
identity needed to transfer coefficient asymptotics to dissipation tails.
The functional-inequality papers of Chen--Chewi--Niles-Weed
\cite{ChenChewiNilesWeed2021MixtureLogSobolev} and
Cheng--Thalmaier--Wang \cite{ChengThalmaierWang2023EntropySteinTransport}
locate the transport/log-Sobolev side of the landscape, but they do not
compute the explicit Cayley--Chebyshev Laurent coefficient hierarchy, the
radial-Poisson covariance contraction \(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\), or the
normalized fourth-moment coefficient consequence recorded here.

The word ``Poisson'' also brings up L{\'o}pez-Rivera--Shenfeld's
\emph{The Poisson transport map}
\cite{LopezRiveraShenfeld2025PoissonTransport}.  That paper constructs a
contraction transport from Poisson point processes to ultra-log-concave
measures on \(\mathbb N\) and derives discrete functional inequalities.  It
is therefore not a competitor for the present continuous Cauchy-kernel
problem: here \(P_t\) is the real-line Poisson kernel, the object is the
large-\(t\) coefficient asymptotics of a matched KL quotient for
\(P_t*\nu\), and the main hypotheses are moment or quotient-jet hypotheses,
not discrete transport contractivity or log-Sobolev constants.

\subsection*{Proof-layer guide}
\label{subsec:intro-guide-hypotheses}

The proof has three recurring layers.  Moment-supply lemmas give density
quotient expansions, quotient-transfer propositions convert those expansions
to KL by Taylor expansion of \(\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\), and algebraic
coefficient statements evaluate the resulting Laurent or tensor constants.
The scope restrictions for these layers are collected in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:intro-hypotheses-scope}.

For Theorem~I the route is finite-dimensional.  The Cayley mode
identity for \(R_\varepsilon\), the universal entropy polynomial, and the
Laurent constant-term rule produce the coefficients.  The finite
\((2N-2)\)-moment assumption in Theorem~I supplies the quotient remainder
needed to retain terms through \(t^{-2N}\).  The threshold clause in
Theorem~I is the one place where
the paper proves an analytic if-and-only-if moment threshold: finite variance
is exactly the condition for the leading finite KL coefficient, and that
coefficient is \(A_4(\nu)=\mu_2^2/8\).

Transfer Lemma~I.1 is the conditional quotient-transfer step for the
borderline missing-top-moment layer.  The exact power, perturbative power,
and monotone slowly varying results are the places where that quotient input
is verified.

Theorem~II and its corollaries separate the multidimensional logic.
Theorem~II assumes the matched Poisson quotient jet \((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\),
including the large-time entropy-existence clause \((\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\),
and transfers it to tensor KL coefficients.  Corollary~II.1 proves that
finite centred \(L\)-absolute moment, \(L\ge d+1\), is one sufficient route to
that analytic input.  Proposition~II.2 computes
\(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\), Proposition~II.2bis records the separate
second-order quotient criterion that turns it into a KL coefficient, and
Corollary~II.3 verifies that criterion from finite \((d+1)\)-moment.

Theorem~III has a different domain issue.  On each compact time window
\([a,b]\subset(0,\infty)\), finite centred variance gives two-sided bounds
and a Lipschitz bound for the moving quotient
\(u_t=(P_t*\nu)/P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\).  Those bounds produce the
Bregman majorant in the statement: the symmetrized kernel has integral at
most \(8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\), while the one-sided dissipation satisfies
\(\int_a^b\mathcal I_\nu(s)\,\dd s\le4C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)/\pi\).  With this
nonlocal domain under control, the tail identity follows from the cutoff
Green formula and \(H'(t)=-\mathcal I_\nu(t)\).

Corollary~I.5 uses the coefficient algebra through \(A_6\) and a finite
fourth absolute moment.  The main sixth-order coefficient theorem gives
\[
A_6(\nu)=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64}.
\]
After normalization, this identity yields the nonnegative defect
\(\Delta_6\) and then the \(W_2\) coupling estimate in
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}.
