\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification of \(\RR\) with \(\TT\), and pulls
normalized Haar measure back to the Cauchy law
\[
\omega(\dd x)=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This normalization makes the fixed-law Poisson comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main-functional}
D_{\rm KL}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
\end{equation}
a finite coefficient problem in Cayley--Chebyshev modes.  The input law
\(\nu\) is fixed, \(\bar\gamma\) is its mean, and \(t\to\infty\) is the
Poisson scale.

\subsection*{Main theorem statements}

\textbf{Radial Poisson entropy jets.}
Let \(d\ge1\), let \(L\ge\max\{2,d+1\}\) be an integer, and let
\(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR^d\) with finite centred \(L\)th
absolute moment.  Theorems~\ref{thm:multidimensional-density-quotient} and
\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets} prove the quotient expansion
for \(P_t^{(d)}*\nu/P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\) and its nonlinear transfer
to the matched KL expansion
\[
D_{\rm KL}\bigl(P_t^{(d)}*\nu\,\|\,P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
=\sum_{m=4}^{L+2}\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)t^{-m}
+o(t^{-(L+2)}),
\]
with the coefficients given by finite tensor-moment integrals of the Taylor
modes of
\[
\left(\frac{1+|y|^2}{1+|y-z|^2}\right)^{(d+1)/2}.
\]
The leading coefficient has the positive decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-multidim-leading}
\mathcal A_4^{(d)}(\nu)
=
c_d^{\rm iso}(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2
+c_d^{\rm tr}\|\Sigma_0\|_{\rm HS}^2,
\end{equation}
where
\[
c_d^{\rm iso}=
\frac{3(d+1)(7d+9)}
{4d(d+3)(d+5)(d+7)},
\qquad
c_d^{\rm tr}=
\frac{3(d+1)(d+3)}
{4(d+5)(d+7)}.
\]
Here \(\Sigma\) is the centred covariance matrix and
\(\Sigma_0=\Sigma-d^{-1}(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)I_d\).  This is
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidim-leading-positive-defect}.

\textbf{One-dimensional finite-moment entropy transfer.}
For \(d=1\), write \(X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma\) and
\(\mu_n=\int X_c^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma)\) when the moment exists.  For every
\(N\ge2\), Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} proves that a finite
centred \((2N-2)\)th absolute moment gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-one-dimensional-transfer}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N}).
\end{equation}
The coefficients are the evaluations of universal moment polynomials obtained
from the Cayley mode integrals; all retained odd coefficients vanish.
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-kl-moment-threshold-sharpness} identifies the
top-moment term in the displayed universal coefficient \(A_{2N}\), so the
centred \((2N-2)\)nd moment is exactly the evaluation threshold for that
coefficient polynomial.

\textbf{Explicit sixth/eighth-order layer.}
Assume now that \(\nu\) has variance \(\sigma^2>0\).  When the centred moments
through order six exist, Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} identifies
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
=
\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
+\frac{A_6(\nu)}{t^6}
+\frac{A_8(\nu)}{t^8}
+o(t^{-8}),
\]
where
\[
A_6(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64},
\]
and
\[
A_8(\nu)
=
\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
The computation uses the explicit Laurent representatives
\[
Q_n(w)=\sum_{1\le |k|\le n}q_{n,k}w^k,\qquad
q_{n,k}=\lambda_n\,\operatorname{sgn}(k)^{\,n\bmod 2}
\binom{n-1}{|k|-1}
\]
from Lemma~\ref{lem:n4-normalized-laurent-representatives}, together with the
zero-sum convolution rule for Laurent constant terms.  Under one additional
centred absolute moment, Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}
upgrades the last remainder to \(O(t^{-9})\).

\textbf{Defects, finite-time certificates, and the KL tail identity.}
The explicit \(N=4\) layer defines the sixth/eighth-order defect coordinates.
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} converts the fixed-variance
sixth defect into a \(W_2\) bound.  Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}
is a conditional certificate statement: after the controlled
seventh-moment shell has fixed bounds for \(A_8\) and the order-nine
remainder, the identity
\[
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)=\Delta_6(\nu)-A_8(\nu)t^{-2}-t^6E(t)
\]
gives finite-time bounds from the subsequently inserted KL observation and
its independent error radius.  Finally,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail} proves the Cauchy/KL Hardy--Stein
tail representation
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
=\int_t^\infty I_\nu(s)\dd s,
\]
and Proposition~\ref{prop:dissipation-tail-coefficient-expansion} transfers
the coefficient expansion to the dissipation tail.

\textbf{What is not claimed.}
The results above concern the fixed-law matched Poisson KL functional
\eqref{eq:intro-main-functional}.  They are not normalized-sum entropy
asymptotics, parametric Cauchy-family divergence formulae, or
functional-inequality stability theorems.  The moment thresholds are
coefficient-evaluation thresholds for the displayed universal polynomials,
not assertions that every possible weaker-tail or renormalized entropy
asymptotic fails; Proposition~\ref{prop:coefficient-evaluation-not-analytic-necessity}
spells out that distinction.  The finite-time estimates are conditional
bookkeeping after independent coefficient and remainder bounds have already
been fixed.

\subsection*{Cayley coefficient mechanism}

After the rescaling \(x=\bar\gamma+ty\), a translate by \(X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma\)
is encoded by
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\qquad \varepsilon=X_c/t .
\]
The Cayley angle \(y=\tan\theta\) converts the Taylor modes of
\(R_\varepsilon\) into finite trigonometric, equivalently Laurent, modes.
Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode} gives the mode formula,
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} defines the formal entropy
coefficients by finite Cauchy-weighted mode integrals, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} reduces those
integrals to Laurent constant-term extraction.

The analytic input is separate from the algebra.  The finite-moment quotient
remainder is proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-density-remainder} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-moment-density-truncation}; the retained-order nonlinear
transfer through \((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) is
Proposition~\ref{prop:density-to-entropy-descent} and
Theorem~\ref{prop:coefficient-identification-poisson-entropy-transfer}.  The
finite Laurent arithmetic needed for the explicit \(N=4\) layer is displayed
in Lemmas~\ref{lem:n4-normalized-laurent-representatives}--\ref{lem:n4-entropy-coefficient-substitution}.

\subsection*{Position relative to existing work}

The closest analytic background is finite-moment large-time profile expansion
for heat, fractional heat, and stable kernels
\cite{DuoandikoetxeaZuazua1992,EscobedoZuazua1991,KarchSchonbek2001,
IshigeKawakami2012,IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2017,
IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2022RefinedFractional,
AnkerPapageorgiouZhang2023HeatSymmetricSpaces,Papageorgiou2024FractionalLargeTime}.
Those results expand an evolved density, kernel, or operator profile.  Here
the density quotient expansion is only the input to a nonlinear scalar KL
expansion, whose coefficients contain products of moment modes.

The heavy-tail functional-inequality literature is used only for comparison
unless it supplies the specific nonlocal Bregman identity invoked in
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}.  Weighted Poincar\'e, weighted
logarithmic Sobolev, weak functional-inequality, and stable-law
entropy-dissipation results
\cite{BobkovLedoux2009WeightedCauchyPoincare,
CattiauxGozlanGuillinRoberto2010HeavyTailFunctionalIneq,
CattiauxGuillinWu2011WeightedLogSobolev,
Huguet2024GeneralisedCauchyPoincare,
Huguet2026GeneralisedCauchyLogSobolev,
BobkovChistyakovGotzeStableFisher2014,
JohnsonStableDeBruijn2013,Toscani2015,Toscani2016,
Toscani2019StablePoincare}
are therefore background references: they locate the heavy-tail Cauchy/stable
setting and the entropy-production vocabulary, but no stability or
log-Sobolev-type theorem is deduced from them.  The genuinely adjacent
references for the paper's KL-tail identity are the Hardy--Stein and
Sobolev--Bregman identities cited in the last row of
Table~\ref{tab:intro-jfa-theorem-comparison}.

\subsection*{Profile expansions versus nonlinear KL transfer}

The distinction from fractional heat profile expansions is structural rather
than terminological.  In the finite-moment profile literature, a typical
large-time statement has the density or solution form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-heat-profile-comparison}
u(t,x)=
\sum_{|\alpha|\le K}M_\alpha(\varphi)\,\partial^\alpha p_t(x)
+R_K(t,x),
\end{equation}
under weighted \(L^1\) moment assumptions on the initial datum.  The
coefficients \(M_\alpha(\varphi)\) multiply derivatives of the linear kernel,
and the theorem controls the remaining profile \(R_K\).

The present paper first proves a quotient expansion and then transfers it
through the nonlinear KL integrand.  In the notation used below, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-kl-transfer-comparison}
D_{\rm KL}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
=
\int_{\RR}
\Phi\!\left(
\sum_{j=2}^{L}D_j(y)t^{-j}+\rho_L(t,y)
\right)\,\dd\Omega(y),
\qquad
\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s .
\end{equation}
The novelty used in the coefficient theorem is therefore not a new linear
heat-kernel profile expansion, but the nonlinear KL transfer from this quotient
expansion, where \(\Phi\) forces products of the retained modes.
Thus the novelty claimed here is not a new heat-kernel profile expansion.
It is the finite-moment transfer from a matched Poisson quotient expansion to
a scalar KL asymptotic.  The nonlinearity of \(\Phi\) is what forces the
coefficient products, for example the \(D_2D_4\), \(D_3^2\), and \(D_2^3\)
terms in the sixth-order coefficient and the longer product list in the
eighth-order coefficient.
