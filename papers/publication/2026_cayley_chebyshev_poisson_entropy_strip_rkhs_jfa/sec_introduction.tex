\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification $\overline\RR$ of the line with
the circle~$\TT$.  Under this identification normalized Haar measure pulls
back to the Cauchy law
\[
\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This normalization is the common coordinate system of the paper.  It puts
Poisson smoothing on~$\RR$, circle Fourier analysis, Cauchy-weighted
entropy integrals, and the auxiliary secant observation kernel into one
calculus.  The Cauchy law is used here as the Haar/Poisson normalization for
the single matched fixed-law functional
\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)).
\]
This is the paper's scope.  The input law is fixed, the time parameter
\(t\) tends to infinity, and the coefficients are universal
moment-polynomial entropy jets for this Poisson comparison.  The comparison
with normalized-sum entropy asymptotics, heat and fractional-flow profile
asymptotics, transport or functional-inequality stability, and parametric
Cauchy divergence formulae is made theorem by theorem below.

\subsection*{Main results}

The first main result extends beyond the Cayley coordinate.  For the radial
Poisson kernel on \(\RR^d\), Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets}
proves a finite-moment entropy-jet expansion for the fixed-law comparison
between \(P_t^{(d)}*\nu\) and the translate of \(P_t^{(d)}\) matched to the
mean of~\(\nu\).  The coefficients are universal tensor-moment polynomials
formed from the multivariable Taylor coefficients of
\[
\left(\frac{1+|y|^2}{1+|y-z|^2}\right)^{(d+1)/2}.
\]
Thus the analytic finite-moment passage is a multidimensional Poisson theorem;
the scalar Laurent computation below is its explicit one-dimensional
specialization.

The one-dimensional Cayley theorem is the explicit computable specialization
of that entropy-jet mechanism for precisely the matched KL functional used in
the rest of the paper, with the input law kept fixed.  For every
\(N\ge2\), if a probability law~\(\nu\) has mean $\bar\gamma$ and finite
centred $(2N-2)$th absolute moment, then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N})
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\]
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  The threshold statement is
only a coefficient-identification statement: by
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-kl-moment-threshold-sharpness}, the displayed
universal moment-polynomial coefficient \(A_{2N}\) contains a nonzero
top-moment term and therefore cannot be evaluated from lower moments alone.
No claim is made there that an unrelated analytic asymptotic under weaker
hypotheses must fail or have the same coefficient.

The second main result is the explicit eighth-order layer.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}
computes the \(t^{-4}\), \(t^{-6}\), and \(t^{-8}\) coefficients at the
finite-sixth-moment threshold, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder} adds the finite-time
\(O(t^{-9})\) estimate under the corresponding seventh-moment hypothesis.
Those coefficients define the defect coordinates used in
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}, which bounds the
fixed-variance moment defect in \(W_2\).  The finite-time version,
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}, is deliberately
stated first on controlled seventh-moment shells: once the shell parameters
\((\sigma,M)\) are fixed, the theorem displays the constants
\(C_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},B_{\sigma,M}^{(8)},T_{\sigma,M}^{(8)}\), and the
measured KL value is used only through
\(\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)\).  A separate oracle fixed-law variant,
Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle-finite-time-poisson-entropy-stability}, records the
same algebra when \(A_8,B,T\) are supplied for a particular law.
The theorem-by-theorem hypotheses, constant regimes, and downstream outputs
are summarized before the entropy proofs in
Subsection~\ref{sec:entropy-theorem-map}.

Thus the new ingredient has two layers.  The analytic layer is the
finite-moment Poisson entropy-jet theorem, valid for radial Poisson kernels in
all dimensions.  The one-dimensional layer is explicit finite-moment Laurent
arithmetic for arbitrary fixed input laws under Poisson smoothing: the
\(t^{-6}\) and \(t^{-8}\) coefficient table, the resulting sixth/eighth-order
defect ladder, and the conditional finite-time estimate obtained once
eighth-order data are supplied.

Three supporting tools keep the argument checkable without changing this
hierarchy.  Finite root-of-unity sampling verifies the Laurent coefficient
arithmetic; the KL Doob-tail section gives the dissipation-tail
representation \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}=\int_t^\infty I_\nu(s)\dd s\) and the
corresponding tail-window coefficient expansion; and a final remark records
the direct full-profile formula by which external Poisson observation data
can supply the finite moment jet used by the entropy layer.

\subsection*{Cayley coefficient mechanism}

The comparison kernel
\[
R_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
\]
measures the ratio between a translated Poisson profile and the reference
Cauchy density after the rescaling $x=\bar\gamma+ty$.  The Cayley angle
$y=\tan\theta$ converts the Taylor modes of $R_\varepsilon$ into finite
trigonometric expressions governed by Chebyshev polynomials of the second
kind.  Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode} records this mode formula.
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} then defines the formal
coefficient polynomials $A_k^{\rm form}$ by finite Cauchy-weighted mode
integrals and proves the odd-order cancellation.  At this stage the
calculation is algebraic: a law is evaluated only through the moment
interface in
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}.

The analytic content is the finite-moment passage from this algebraic
calculus to actual large-time entropy coefficients.  The exact retained
orders, the stronger-moment fixed-law rates, the moment-ball uniform version,
and the compact-support convergent-series variant are separated in the
hypothesis guide at the start of Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics}.

Finite phase sampling gives an exactness check for this coefficient calculus.
The main entropy proof uses Laurent constant-coefficient extraction.
Appendix~\ref{sec:finite-phase-sampling} identifies the same circle characters
on root-of-unity grids and records the alias-free recovery formula below the
explicit degree barrier.  The finite-grid moment recovery theorem and the
eighth-order finite-phase verification check the normalized Laurent
constant terms after the analytic asymptotic framework has been established.

\subsection*{Observation data}

The entropy expansion and defect theorems are moment theorems.  External
observation data enter only by supplying the moment jet
\((\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6)\).  The direct full-profile interface is the following.
At a fixed observation time \(t_0\), write
\[
\kappa_{t_0}:=\operatorname{Law}((\gamma-\bar\gamma)/t_0),
\qquad
q_{t_0}(\xi)=\pi\int_{\RR}s e^{-\ii s\xi}\kappa_{t_0}(\dd s).
\]
Full knowledge of \(q_{t_0}\) determines the finite signed measure
\(s\,\kappa_{t_0}(\dd s)\) by Fourier uniqueness, hence determines
\(\kappa_{t_0}\) away from zero and the atom at zero by total mass.  If the
corresponding moments are finite, then
\[
\mu_j
=
t_0^j\,\frac{\ii^{\,j-1}}{\pi}\,
q_{t_0}^{(j-1)}(0),
\qquad 2\le j\le6 .
\]

A separate exact lattice reconstruction exists only under the additional
condition
\(\nu\{\gamma:\gamma-\bar\gamma\in t_0\ZZ\}=1\).  This is a non-generic
certificate, not a practical one-observation theorem.  Without that support
condition, integer samples are projection data and do not reconstruct the
full continuous profile; the detailed lattice calculation is therefore kept
out of the main entropy argument and appears only as a final remark.

\subsection*{Organization}

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation and conventions.
Subsection~\ref{sec:cayley-gate} records the Cayley formulas, and
Subsection~\ref{sec:haar-pullback} records the Haar-pullback normalization.
Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} proves the main entropy packages: the
multidimensional Poisson entropy-jet theorem, its one-dimensional
Cayley--Chebyshev coefficient specialization, and the seventh-moment
eighth-order defect layer, followed by the coefficient-level and finite-time
moment-defect \(W_2\) consequences.  Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}
records the KL dissipation-tail identity for the same Poisson comparison
quotient and derives the dissipation-tail coefficient expansion from the
main coefficient theorem.  Appendix~\ref{sec:strip-lattice} is a short final
remark on optional observation-to-moment input and on the limited exact
lattice case.  Appendix~\ref{app:related-work-scope} collects the related-work
comparison.  Appendix~\ref{app:entropy-integrals}
records the weighted trigonometric integrals and coefficient arithmetic used
in the eighth-order expansion.  Appendix~\ref{sec:finite-phase-sampling}
records the post-theorem root-of-unity finite check for the same normalized
Cayley coefficients.
Appendix~\ref{app:haar-pullback-uniqueness} records the endpoint-normalized
uniqueness statement for the Cayley chart.
Appendix~\ref{app:finite-time-defect-estimates} records the constant-regime
bookkeeping and controlled-shell uniform version for the finite-time
moment-defect estimates.
