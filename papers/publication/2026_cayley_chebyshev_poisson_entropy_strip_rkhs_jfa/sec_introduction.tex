\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification of \(\RR\) with \(\TT\), and pulls
normalized Haar measure back to the Cauchy law
\[
\omega(\dd x)=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This is the endpoint-normalized form of the classical Cauchy/wrapped-Cauchy
M{\"o}bius correspondence in circular statistics
\cite{KentTyler1988WrappedCauchy,McCullagh1992CauchyModels,
McCullagh1996CauchyMobius,
KatoJones2010MobiusCircle,KatoMcCullagh2020SphereCauchyMobius}.
This normalization makes the fixed-law Poisson comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main-functional}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\bigr)
\end{equation}
the object whose large-\(t\) behaviour is studied in Cayley--Chebyshev
modes.  The input law \(\nu\) is fixed, \(\bar{\gamma}\) is its mean, and
\(t\to\infty\) is the Poisson scale.

The basic calculation is a quotient calculation.  Let
\[
X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}
\]
be the centred translation variable.  With \(x=\bar{\gamma}+ty\), translation
by \(X_c\) is encoded by
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\qquad \varepsilon=X_c/t .
\]
The Cayley angle \(y=\tan\theta\) turns the Taylor coefficients of
\(R_\varepsilon\) into finite trigonometric, equivalently Laurent, modes.
After the matched Cauchy profile has been divided out, the quotient expansion
is therefore reduced to finite mode algebra; the KL expansion is then the
ordinary Taylor expansion of \((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) in
\(L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)\).

\subsection*{Contribution}

The first theorem is one-dimensional and computes the full finite-moment KL
coefficient hierarchy.  It also identifies the exact threshold at which the
leading \(t^{-4}\) scale exists: finite centred variance gives coefficient
\(\mu_2^2/8\), while infinite centred variance forces
\(t^4D_{\rm KL}\to+\infty\).

The second theorem is multidimensional.  Its analytic hypothesis is the
two-region quotient-tail condition that closes the large-translation
remainder in the matched radial Poisson quotient.  Under that condition the
quotient has an explicit
\(L^\infty\cap L^1(\Omega_d)\) jet, the entropy-existence package holds for
large \(t\), and the tensor Taylor algebra gives the KL expansion through
order \(L+2\).  Finite centred \(L\)-moment, with \(L\ge d+1\), is recorded
immediately afterward as a corollary verifying this tail input.

The third theorem is dynamic rather than asymptotic.  Under finite centred
variance the moving matched Cauchy quotient lies in the domain of the
Cauchy-generator Bregman form on every compact time window, and the KL
functional is exactly the tail integral of that nonlocal dissipation.
The point is not merely to quote a Hardy--Stein identity for a fixed
semigroup orbit.  Here both the quotient
\[
u_t=\frac{h_t}{g_t}
\]
and the reference density \(g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\) move with \(t\).
The logarithmic chain rule therefore first gives the two-pair identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-two-pair-chain-rule}
H'(t)=\langle \log u_t,\partial_t h_t\rangle
      -\langle u_t-1,\partial_t g_t\rangle,
\qquad H(t)=D_{\rm KL}(h_t\|g_t),
\end{equation}
not a single fixed-reference Dirichlet form.  Applying the Cauchy Green
formula separately to the pairs \((h_t,\log u_t)\) and \((g_t,u_t)\), and
using \(\int\partial_tg_t=0\), produces the algebraic difference
\[
(\rho_xa-\rho_yb)(\log a-\log b)-(\rho_x-\rho_y)(a-b)
=\rho_x\Lambda(a,b)+\rho_y\Lambda(b,a),
\]
where \(a=u_t(x)\), \(b=u_t(y)\), \(\rho_x=g_t(x)\),
\(\rho_y=g_t(y)\), and
\(\Lambda(a,b)=a\log(a/b)-a+b\).  This two-Green-pairing closure, together
with the compact-window cutoff and measurability lemma in
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}, is the extra step that is not supplied
by existing Hardy--Stein formulas alone.

\subsection*{Theorems}\label{subsec:main-results}

\begin{maintheorem}{One-dimensional coefficient hierarchy and variance threshold}
\label{thm:main-A}
Let \(N\ge2\), put \(L=2N-2\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability law on
\(\RR\) with finite mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and finite centred \(L\)th absolute
moment.  With
\[
\begin{aligned}
  X_c&=\gamma-\bar{\gamma},\\
  \mu_n&=\int_{\RR} X_c^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma)\qquad(2\le n\le L),\\
  h_t&=P_t*\nu,\qquad
  g_t&=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),
\end{aligned}
\]
one has
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N}).
\]
Each coefficient \(A_{2m}(\nu)\) is a universal polynomial in
\(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{2m-2}\), evaluated by the Cayley--Chebyshev Laurent
constant-term rule; explicitly it is the coefficient formula of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  All retained odd coefficients
vanish, and the leading coefficient is
\[
A_4(\nu)=\frac{\mu_2^2}{8}.
\]
For an arbitrary probability law on \(\RR\) with finite mean
\(\bar{\gamma}\), the leading coefficient has the sharp threshold below.
Put \(X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}\) and set
\[
H(t)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\bigr)
\]
with the extended nonnegative convention for relative entropy.  Then
\[
\EE X_c^2<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)<\infty .
\]
If the variance is finite, then \(H(t)<\infty\) for every \(t>0\) and
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=\frac{(\EE X_c^2)^2}{8}.
\]
If the variance is infinite, then
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=+\infty
\]
in the extended sense.
\end{maintheorem}

\begin{maintheorem}{Two-region multidimensional Poisson entropy jet}
\label{thm:main-B}
Let \(d\ge1\), let \(L\in\NN\) satisfy \(L\ge d+1\), and let \(\nu\) be a
probability measure on \(\RR^d\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\).  Put
\[
X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma .
\]
Assume the two-region quotient-tail condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main-B-two-region-tail}
\begin{aligned}
&t^{-(L+1)}\EE\!\left[
|X_c|^{L+1}\mathbf1_{\{|X_c|\le t\}}\right]
t^{-(d+1)}\EE\!\left[
|X_c|^{d+1}\mathbf1_{\{|X_c|>t\}}\right]  \\
&\hspace{7em}
\sum_{k=d+2}^{L}t^{-k}\EE\!\left[
|X_c|^k\mathbf1_{\{|X_c|>t\}}\right]
=o(t^{-L}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the sum is empty when \(L=d+1\).  Then the centred moments
\[
\mu_\alpha=\EE[X_c^\alpha]\qquad(|\alpha|\le L)
\]
are finite.  With
\[
h_t=P_t^{(d)}*\nu,\qquad
g_t=P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar\gamma),\qquad
\delta_t^{(d)}(y)=\frac{h_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}{g_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}-1,
\]
one has the quotient jet
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_t^{(d)}(y)
&=\sum_{2\le\lvert\alpha\rvert\le L}
\mu_\alpha U_\alpha^{(d)}(y)t^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert}
+\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}(y),\\
\lVert\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}\rVert_\infty+
\lVert\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}\rVert_{L^1(\Omega_d)}
&\le C_{d,L}\Bigg(
t^{-(L+1)}\EE\!\left[
|X_c|^{L+1}\mathbf1_{\{|X_c|\le t\}}\right]
+t^{-(d+1)}\EE\!\left[
|X_c|^{d+1}\mathbf1_{\{|X_c|>t\}}\right]\\
&\hspace{7em}
+\sum_{k=d+2}^{L}t^{-k}\EE\!\left[
|X_c|^k\mathbf1_{\{|X_c|>t\}}\right]\Bigg)\\
&=o(t^{-L}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover \(\|\delta_t^{(d)}\|_\infty=O(t^{-2})\), and for all sufficiently
large \(t\) the following entropy-existence package holds:
\[
1+\delta_t^{(d)}(y)>0,\qquad
\int_{\RR^d}\delta_t^{(d)}\,\dd\Omega_d=0,\qquad
\int_{\RR^d}\Phi(\delta_t^{(d)})\,\dd\Omega_d<\infty,
\]
where \(\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\), and
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\int_{\RR^d}\Phi(\delta_t^{(d)}(y))\,\Omega_d(\dd y).
\]
In the notation of Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics}, this says that
\eqref{eq:intro-main-B-two-region-tail} supplies both the quotient jet
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\) and the large-\(t\) entropy-existence clause
\((\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\).  Consequently
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{m=4}^{L+2}\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)t^{-m}
+o(t^{-(L+2)}),
\]
where the tensor coefficients are those of
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidim-conditional-tail-poisson-kl-asymptotic}; all retained
odd coefficients vanish.  The symbol \((\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\) names the
displayed positivity, mass-zero, integrability, and KL-representation
statements needed before the algebraic Taylor transfer may be read as an
entropy expansion.
\end{maintheorem}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-moment route to Theorem~\ref{thm:main-B}]
\label{cor:main-B-finite-moment-route}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:main-B}, the two-region tail condition
\eqref{eq:intro-main-B-two-region-tail} holds whenever
\(\EE|X_c|^L<\infty\).  Thus finite centred \(L\)-moment, with
\(L\ge d+1\), gives the same matched Poisson KL coefficient expansion through
order \(L+2\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{maintheorem}{Cauchy Bregman tail identity}
\label{thm:main-C}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and
finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_t&=P_t*\nu,\\
g_t&=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\\
u_t&=h_t/g_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
The nonlocal domain is part of the statement.  For every \(0<a<b<\infty\),
write
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_2&:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^2\,\dd\nu(\gamma),\\
m&:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\\
M&:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
  L:=3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr).
\]
Then, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x,y\in\RR\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-bregman-domain-quotient-bounds}
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad
\lvert u_t(x)-u_t(y)\rvert\le L\lvert x-y\rvert .
\end{equation}
Define the Bregman kernel by
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\]
and set
\[
\Psi(z):=\mathbf1_{\{\lvert z\rvert<1\}}
\,+\,\lvert z\rvert^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{\lvert z\rvert\ge1\}},
\qquad
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\}.
\]
The compact-window bounds imply the pointwise Bregman majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-bregman-domain-majorant}
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}\,g_t(x)\Psi(x-y)
\qquad (x\ne y,\ t\in[a,b]),
\end{equation}
and the same estimate holds with \(x\) and \(y\) interchanged.  Hence the
symmetrized kernel is dominated on \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) by
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y),
\]
whose integral over \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) equals
\(8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\).  The same one-sided majorant gives the compact-window
dissipation bound
\[
\int_a^b\mathcal I_\nu(s)\,\dd s
\le \frac{4C_{a,b,\nu}}{\pi}(b-a)
\]
once \(\mathcal I_\nu\) is defined below.  This domain clause makes the
following nonlocal double integral finite on compact time windows and
independent of the annular exhaustion used to construct it.

Define \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\) by annular cutoffs, equivalently by the
nonnegative Lebesgue integral
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t)
=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\]
with the diagonal ignored.  Then \(\mathcal I_\nu\) is measurable and locally
integrable on \((0,\infty)\), and for every \(t>0\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\,\dd s .
\]
\end{maintheorem}

\subsection*{Theorem dependency diagram}
\label{subsec:intro-hypotheses-scope}

The proof chain is the following.
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\EE|X_c|^{2N-2}<\infty
&\Longrightarrow&
\text{one-dimensional quotient jet}\\
&\Longrightarrow&
\text{finite Taylor transfer for }\Phi\\
&\Longrightarrow&
\text{Theorem~\ref{thm:main-A} coefficient hierarchy},\\[0.7em]
\EE X_c^2<\infty
&\Longleftrightarrow&
\text{finite leading }t^{-4}\text{ KL coefficient}\\
&\Longrightarrow&
\text{Theorem~\ref{thm:main-A} threshold},\\[0.7em]
\text{two-region quotient-tail condition}
&\Longrightarrow&
(\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})+(\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\\
&\Longrightarrow&
\text{Theorem~\ref{thm:main-B} tensor KL jet},\\[0.7em]
\EE|X_c|^L<\infty,\ L\ge d+1
&\Longrightarrow&
\text{two-region quotient-tail condition},\\[0.7em]
\EE X_c^2<\infty
&\Longrightarrow&
\text{compact-window domain bounds}\\
&\Longrightarrow&
\text{two Green pairings}\\
&\Longrightarrow&
\text{Theorem~\ref{thm:main-C} Bregman tail identity}.
\end{array}
\]
The proof locations are
Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} and
\ref{thm:sharp-leading-kl-variance-threshold} for
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-A},
Theorems~\ref{thm:multidimensional-conditional-tail-density-quotient} and
\ref{thm:multidim-conditional-tail-poisson-kl-asymptotic} for
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-B}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-window-bregman-identity} together with
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail} for
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-C}.  Finite \(L\)-moment verifies the
multidimensional tail condition in
Corollary~\ref{thm:multidim-finite-moment-poisson-kl-asymptotic}.  The
truncated-top-mode entropy layer is isolated in
Corollary~\ref{cor:main-body-conditional-missing-top-moment-tail} and
Appendix~\ref{app:conditional-missing-top-moment-tails}; the covariance
quadratic form is evaluated in
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidim-covariance-algebra} and becomes a KL leading term
only through the quotient criteria stated afterward.  The fourth-moment square
defect following \(A_6\) is recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:optional-fourth-moment-coefficient-algebra}.

\subsection*{Related theorem-level comparison}
\label{subsec:intro-recent-jfa-comparison}

Recent \emph{J. Funct. Anal.} papers closest in method treat
Esscher-transform CLT, heat-profile asymptotics, Poisson transport,
functional inequalities, and Hardy--Stein identities.  Poisson transport and
Poisson-channel results are adjacent because they combine Poisson laws with
entropy, but their Poisson object is respectively a count/point-process
measure or a counting-output channel, not the harmonic Cauchy kernel
\(P_t(x)=\pi^{-1}t/(t^2+x^2)\) smoothing a fixed Euclidean law.  The
fixed-law matched functional \eqref{eq:intro-main-functional} and its Laurent
coefficient hierarchy are therefore separate from those count-Poisson
problems.  Appendix~\ref{app:related-work-scope} gives the theorem-level
comparator table and the entropy-asymptotic context beyond JFA, including
small-time heat-kernel relative entropy expansions and general
relative-entropy representation formulas.
