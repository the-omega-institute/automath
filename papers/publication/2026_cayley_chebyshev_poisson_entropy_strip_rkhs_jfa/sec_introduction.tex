\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification $\overline\RR$ of the line with
the circle~$\TT$.  Under this identification the normalized Haar measure
on~$\TT$ pulls back to the Cauchy law
\[
\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x.
\]
This identity fixes the functional-analytic normalization of the paper.  It
selects the Cayley chart among normalized $C^1$ orientation-preserving
homeomorphisms $\overline\RR\to\TT$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:haar-pullback-uniqueness}), places large-time Poisson
comparison on~$\RR$ in Cauchy coordinates, and identifies the secant profiles
whose Gram completion becomes the strip RKHS observation channel.

The main results separate formal Cayley--Chebyshev coefficient algebra,
finite-moment entropy asymptotics under a finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute
moment, compact-support analytic convergence, and a seventh-moment derivation of
the eighth-order entropy jet.  They lead to order-$t^{-8}$ defect coordinates,
rigidity of the symmetric two-point law at fixed variance, strict eighth-order
amplification, coefficient-level $W_2$ stability, and finite-time stability.

The results are organized as follows:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.25\textwidth}p{0.66\textwidth}@{}}
Entropy asymptotics & finite-moment expansion; compact-support analytic series\\
Eighth-order theory & seventh-moment entropy jet; defect rigidity,
amplification, and stability\\
Coefficient calculus & finite phase recovery; Doob--$\Phi$ identity;
higher-secant germs\\
Observation channel & strip RKHS, Hardy splitting, integer-orbit projection and
cardinal formulas
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The strip and lattice material is the observation-channel interpretation of the
same coefficient calculus.  The secant profiles generate a strip RKHS whose
integer orbit has an explicit hyperbolic symbol and an exact projection/quotient
theorem: integer samples recover the minimal-norm lattice component of the
Poisson observation channel, with full recovery exactly on the lattice subspace.

The same visible phase factor also supplies the finite recovery mechanism.
Section~\ref{sec:finite-phase-sampling} records the root-of-unity
averaging used for Cayley Fourier coefficients.
Proposition~\ref{prop:phase-coordinate-identification} identifies the finite grids
and the analytic Fourier modes with the same circle characters.  Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-grid-coefficient-recovery}
then gives coefficient recovery: below the explicit $K<N$ aliasing barrier
the grid coefficient is exact, and in general the error is the Fourier
aliasing tail.  This finite phase calculus is used later in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-grid-poisson-moment-recovery}, which reads the first
nontrivial centred moment coordinates from exact samples on a fixed finite
phase grid with explicit large-time errors, and in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm}, which reduces
every coefficient integral $I(n_1,\ldots,n_r)$ to constant Laurent
coefficient extraction.  Proposition~\ref{prop:eighth-order-finite-phase-verification}
is the low-order specialization: a phase grid $\mu_L$ with $L\ge9$ exactly
computes the finite mode integrals appearing in the printed expansion
through order~$t^{-8}$ once the moment coordinates are fixed.

The analytic consequences are driven by the comparison kernel
\[
R_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\]
which measures the ratio between a translated Poisson profile and the reference
Cauchy density after the rescaling $x=\bar\gamma+ty$.  The Cayley angle
$y=\tan\theta$ reveals a finite Fourier structure governed by Chebyshev
polynomials of the second kind, giving the Cayley--Chebyshev mode calculus of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode}: every Taylor mode of
$R_\varepsilon$ admits an explicit trigonometric formula.  The formal
universal coefficient theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients},
defines $A_k^{\rm form}$ by summing Cauchy-weighted mode integrals, proves
$A_k^{\rm form}=0$ for odd~$k$, and is evaluated at a law only by
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}.  The first
asymptotic result is Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}, together with
the explicit remainder theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder}:
for each order covered by the finite centred $(2N+\eta)$ moment hypothesis,
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N}).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic-compact-support-interchange} gives the
compact-support analytic series.  The finite-moment asymptotic theorem has
fixed-law constants determined by the stated moment data and universal mode
bounds, while the stronger $(2N+1)$st-moment remainder is used for finite
coefficient identities and displayed expansions.
Proposition~\ref{prop:strict-finite-moment-entropy-regime} records the gap when
$0<\eta<1$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:strict-entropy-coefficient-hierarchy}
summarizes the formal, finite-moment, compact-support, and seventh-moment
hypotheses used below.
At finite order, the same phase-Fourier coefficients recover the first
nontrivial centred moments
$(\sigma^2,\mu_3,\mu_4)$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-cauchy-moment-inversion-by-phase-modes});
the finite-grid version requires the aliasing statement and the uniform
large-time error estimate recorded in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-grid-poisson-moment-recovery}.  Once those moment
coordinates are fixed, the same finite phase calculus provides exact
finite-grid verification for the eighth-order coefficient examples
(Proposition~\ref{prop:eighth-order-finite-phase-verification}).
The local higher-secant germs in
Theorem~\ref{thm:higher-secant-kernels} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:higher-secant-entropy-polynomial} organize the finite
coefficient arithmetic of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-secant-entropy-jet-obstruction}
records the complementary obstruction: every fixed entropy coefficient jet
factors through finitely many centred moments and cannot reconstruct the law by
itself.

This coefficient calculus is complemented by a dissipative layer.
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-doob-phi-entropy}
conjugates the Cauchy semigroup by the moving Poisson profile and proves an
exact nonlocal $\Phi$-entropy identity: for every admissible convex~$\Phi$,
the decay is the symmetric Bregman form of the Doob jump kernel.  In the
Kullback--Leibler case this gives
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\int_t^\infty\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s,
\]
where $\mathcal I_\nu$ is the nonlocal Bregman dissipation rate.
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-bregman-tail-coefficients} then identifies the
even Cayley--Chebyshev entropy coefficients as the successive tail
coefficients of this dissipation integral.  Thus the paper has two coupled
descriptions of the same entropy asymptotics: a finite Fourier coefficient
calculus and a Doob--Bregman dissipation mechanism.

The first explicit consequence is the eighth-order entropy corollary for a
Poisson average relative to the translated Poisson kernel with matching mean
(Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-kl-eighth}).
Corollary~\ref{cor:seventh-moment-eighth-bridge} identifies its displayed jet
with the formal coefficient notation under a finite centred seventh moment; the
stronger $N=4$ finite-moment theorem and the compact-support analytic theorem
give stronger-hypothesis consistency checks.  Corollary~\ref{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization}
records that this finite expansion depends only on the centred moment jet
through order six.  Writing
the coefficients of orders $t^{-6}$ and $t^{-8}$ as
$C_6$ and $C_8$, we then recenter them at their symmetric two-point extremal
values.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder} identifies explicit
non-negative defect coordinates, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification} proves that the eighth-order
defect strictly amplifies the sixth-order one.
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-two-point-defect-rigidity} records the rigidity
conclusion: at fixed mean and variance, the symmetric two-point law is the
unique law attaining both extremal coefficient values, zero entropy defect, and
equality in the amplification bound.  The fourth-moment polynomial extracted
from the sixth-order formula is kept separate from asymptotic coefficient
notation.  Under the same finite centred seventh absolute moment hypothesis,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-stability} gives coefficient-level $W_2$
control of the sixth-order asymptotic defect relative to the symmetric
two-point extremizer.
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-finite-time-defect-stability} converts this into
a quantitative large-time statement for the renormalized sixth-order
entropy deficit, with the explicit $C_8t^{-2}$ correction and an
order~$t^{-3}$ residual in the renormalized scale.  The finite-fourth-moment
consequence remains the separate algebraic coupling bound.

The strip RKHS and lattice results give the Hilbert-space form of the same
normalized Poisson observation channel whose finite-dimensional mode
coefficients drive the entropy expansion.  The functions
\[
\Psi_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{R_\varepsilon(y)-1}{\varepsilon}
\]
have exact Gram kernel
\[
K(a,b)=\frac{2}{4+(a-b)^2}
\]
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-gram-kernel}).  Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-space-rkhs}
shows that the closed span of the secant family is exactly the zero-mean
space $L^2_0(\omega)$ and is unitarily equivalent to the
reproducing-kernel Hilbert space generated by~$K$.  Through
Proposition~\ref{prop:gram-space-poisson-image}, this RKHS becomes the
Hardy-type Poisson image space $\sqrt{\pi}\,e^{-|D|}L^2(\RR)$, hence a
translation-invariant strip space with explicit Fourier-side norm and
holomorphic extension to $\{z\in\CC:\ |\Im z|<1\}$.  The strip model
splits orthogonally into two Hardy summands
(Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-hardy-splitting}).  This identifies the
ambient RKHS in which the fluctuation profiles from the entropy comparison
become evaluation functionals (Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-channel-rkhs}).
In this channel picture,
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-central-two-channel} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-symmetrization} separate the even and odd
observation data: the value channel sees the centred symmetrization, while
the derivative channel is precisely the obstruction to symmetry and hence
to recovery from the value channel alone.

The discrete part is the integer secant orbit.
Corollary~\ref{cor:integer-orbit-projection} gives the projection
used in the main observation-channel statement; the more general
uniform-lattice fiberization formula in Section~\ref{sec:strip-lattice} is
an auxiliary derivation of that integer case.  The Poisson-strip
integer-orbit calculation starts with
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-lattice-symbol}, which
computes the explicit positive symbol
\[
\sigma(\theta)=\frac{\pi\cosh(2(\pi-|\theta|))}{\sinh(2\pi)},
\qquad -\pi\le\theta\le\pi,
\]
with sharp constants, for the secant Gram kernel
$K(a,b)=\pi P_2(a-b)$.  With this symbol in hand, the following two
results are the standard principal-symbol consequences of this computation:
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-lattice-sampling} records the resulting
Riesz and stable sampling bounds for
$\{K(\cdot,n)\}_{n\in\ZZ}$ and $\{\Psi_n\}_{n\in\ZZ}$, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-cardinal-reconstruction} records the
corresponding cardinal reconstruction: there is a unique cardinal kernel
$L\in\mathcal H_K(\ZZ)$ with $L(n)=\delta_{0n}$, every lattice datum in
$\ell^2(\ZZ)$ admits a unique interpolant
\[
F_\alpha(z)=\sum_{n\in\ZZ}\alpha_nL(z-n),
\]
and the interpolant norm is given exactly by the reciprocal lattice
symbol.  In Fourier realization, $\mathcal H_K(\ZZ)$ is therefore a
principal shift-invariant space generated by the Poisson strip kernel.
The second Poisson-specific step is
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-observation-lattice-sampling}, which specializes
that standard reconstruction to the observation profiles produced earlier:
integer samples recover the lattice projection of the RKHS observation
channel, and they recover the full channel exactly when that channel belongs
to $\mathcal H_K(\ZZ)$.  Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-integer-observation-quotient}
sharpens this into an exact quotient obstruction: the integer restriction map
has kernel $\mathcal H_K(\ZZ)^\perp$, its quotient is isomorphic to
$\ell^2(\ZZ)$, and the cardinal reconstruction is the unique minimal-norm
representative of the observed quotient class.
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-integer-invisible-obstruction} then gives the budget
form of the same lattice-projection obstruction: inside any ambient RKHS ball
compatible with the observed data, the integer fiber is precisely a ball in the
invisible subspace, and the cardinal representative is the sharp minimax center
of that fiber.
The final normal form,
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-lattice-normal-form}, combines these inputs in
an explicit energy identity: the integer data determine the cardinal
lattice component, the remaining energy is exactly the norm of the invisible
component, and vanishing of this invisible energy is equivalent to full
Poisson-channel reconstruction.  Theorem~\ref{thm:integer-poisson-observation-classification}
then closes the lattice endpoint of the theorem chain by classifying every
ambient strip RKHS channel with the same integer data, proving the exact
excess-energy budget, and showing that every datum-dependent reconstruction or
invariant factors through the quotient by the integer-invisible subspace.
Thus the lattice material gives the integer-orbit reconstruction theory of the
same Poisson observation channel: the explicit hyperbolic symbol, observation
profile embedding, lattice projection, and quotient obstruction generated by
the integer-invisible subspace.

The centered Poisson trace proposition
(Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-central-two-channel}) provides the scalar
reconstruction input for this RKHS interpretation.  It shows that
the central Poisson trace and its central derivative recover the
Laplace transform of the centred characteristic function; by Laplace
uniqueness and characteristic-function inversion they determine the
centred law, and together with the mean they determine the original
measure.  This proposition fits naturally after the mode-space/RKHS construction,
where the two observation channels become evaluation functionals.

The background is used in three roles.  First, the analytic toolkit is
classical: Poisson semigroups and Fourier representation
\cite{Folland1995,Grafakos2014,Stein1971}, Laplace transforms
\cite{Widder1941}, characteristic functions and inversion
\cite{Lukacs1970,Feller1971}, reproducing kernels \cite{Aronszajn1950},
and Hardy--Poisson boundary theory \cite{Duren1970,Rudin1987}.  The
Haar-pullback rigidity and entropy change-of-variables identities below only
fix the normalized Cayley chart.

Second, the entropy layer is adjacent to, but distinct from, the entropic CLT,
stable-law de Bruijn, and entropy-production literatures
\cite{Barron1986,JohnsonBarron2004,ArtsteinBallBartheNaor2004,CarlenCarvalho1992,BobkovChistyakovGotzeEdgeworth2013,BobkovChistyakovGotzeBerryEsseen2014,BobkovChistyakovGotze2013,Toscani2015,Toscani2016,JohnsonStableDeBruijn2013}.
Recent stability results for logarithmic HLS and logarithmic Sobolev deficits
\cite{Carlen2025LogHLS,BrigatiDolbeaultSimonov2024LogSobolev} give the
nearest functional-inequality comparison: they control distance from
extremizers by entropy deficits.  The present stability statement is narrower
and coefficient-level.  It concerns finite-moment Poisson-shift asymptotic
defects, with universal algebraic constants at coefficient level, variance
factors displayed explicitly, fixed-law finite-time constants, and
shell-uniform constants only after a variance and seventh-moment shell is
fixed.  Recent Cauchy information work
\cite{Verdu2023Cauchy,NielsenOkamura2023Cauchy} is relevant because the
Cauchy law supplies the Cayley pullback and the Poisson comparison kernel, not
because the paper studies divergence geometry inside the Cauchy family.

Third, the lattice layer imports the principal shift-invariant/RKHS sampling
criterion.  Riesz translates and principal generators are standard
\cite{deBoorDeVoreRon1994,RonShen1995,Bownik2000}; nonuniform and stable
sampling are treated in \cite{AldroubiGrochenig2001}; and RKHS, localized,
derivative, and Galerkin sampling frameworks appear in
\cite{NashedWalter1991,NashedSun2010,ChengJiangSun2016,GrochenigRomeroStockler2020,FuhrGrochenigHaimiKlotzRomero2018}.
Explicit Gaussian, Lorentz/Cauchy, and radial-basis cardinal constructions
\cite{KiselevMininNovikov2016,KiselevMininNovikov2019,Buhmann2003} provide
nearby examples.  What is Poisson-specific here is the strip realization of
the secant mode space, the closed hyperbolic lattice symbol, the cardinal
kernel and norm formula, and the exact quotient/minimax obstruction for the
observation profiles above.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation and conventions.
Subsection~\ref{sec:cayley-gate} records the Cayley formulas, and
Subsection~\ref{sec:haar-pullback} proves the rigidity of the Haar
pullback.  Section~\ref{sec:finite-phase-sampling} records the finite
root-of-unity sampling facts used by the Cayley Fourier coefficients.
Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} develops the
entropy identity, the Cayley--Chebyshev mode calculus, the entropy
asymptotics, the defect amplification, and the coefficient-level and
quantitative large-time $W_2$ control theorems.
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy} proves the non-autonomous Cauchy Doob
transform and the $\Phi$-entropy/Bregman dissipation theorem, and relates
its Kullback--Leibler tail identity back to the formal entropy
coefficient algebra.
Section~\ref{sec:gram-space} constructs the mode Gram kernel and its
reproducing-kernel Hilbert space, proves the centered reconstruction theorem,
and records local higher-secant germs for the entropy polynomial.
Section~\ref{sec:strip-lattice} starts by fixing
the complex-Hilbert-space convention and the closed-span notation for the
lattice subspaces, identifies the strip RKHS with a Poisson image space,
establishes the Hardy splitting, and develops the lattice projection machinery
yielding the explicit integer-orbit symbol,
cardinal kernel, norm formula, and reconstruction theorem for Poisson
observation channels.
The appendix records the weighted trigonometric integrals used in the
eighth-order expansion.
