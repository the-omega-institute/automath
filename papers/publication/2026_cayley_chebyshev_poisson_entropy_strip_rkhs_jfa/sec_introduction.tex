\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification of \(\RR\) with \(\TT\), and pulls
normalized Haar measure back to the Cauchy law
\[
\omega(\dd x)=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This is the endpoint-normalized form of the classical Cauchy/wrapped-Cauchy
M{\"o}bius correspondence in circular statistics
\cite{KentTyler1988WrappedCauchy,McCullagh1992CauchyModels,
McCullagh1996CauchyMobius,
KatoJones2010MobiusCircle,KatoMcCullagh2020SphereCauchyMobius}.
This normalization makes the fixed-law Poisson comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main-functional}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\bigr)
\end{equation}
the object whose large-\(t\) behaviour is studied in Cayley--Chebyshev
modes.  The input law \(\nu\) is fixed, \(\bar{\gamma}\) is its mean, and
\(t\to\infty\) is the Poisson scale.

The basic calculation is a quotient calculation.  Let
\[
X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}
\]
be the centred translation variable.  With \(x=\bar{\gamma}+ty\), translation
by \(X_c\) is encoded by
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\qquad \varepsilon=X_c/t .
\]
The Cayley angle \(y=\tan\theta\) turns the Taylor coefficients of
\(R_\varepsilon\) into finite trigonometric, equivalently Laurent, modes.
After the matched Cauchy profile has been divided out, the quotient expansion
is therefore reduced to finite mode algebra; the KL expansion is then the
ordinary Taylor expansion of \((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) in
\(L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)\).

\subsection*{Main contribution}

The paper has three main theorem statements about the matched Poisson KL
functional \eqref{eq:intro-main-functional}.  Main Theorem A gives the
one-dimensional Cayley--Chebyshev hierarchy and the sharp finite-variance
threshold for the leading KL coefficient.  Main Theorem B gives the
finite-moment multidimensional quotient jet and the resulting tensor KL
coefficients.  Main Theorem C gives the exact Cauchy-generator Bregman tail
identity.  The subsequent consequences record the verified borderline-tail
classes, the conditional quotient-transfer proposition, covariance algebra,
and the normalized fourth-moment defect algebra.

\subsection*{The three main theorems}

\begin{introresult}{Main Theorem A: one-dimensional coefficient hierarchy and variance threshold}
Let \(N\ge2\), put \(L=2N-2\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability law on
\(\RR\) with finite mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and finite centred \(L\)th absolute
moment.  With
\[
\begin{aligned}
  X_c&=\gamma-\bar{\gamma},\\
  \mu_n&=\int_{\RR} X_c^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma)\qquad(2\le n\le L),\\
  h_t&=P_t*\nu,\qquad
  g_t&=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),
\end{aligned}
\]
one has
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N}).
\]
Each coefficient \(A_{2m}(\nu)\) is a universal polynomial in
\(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{2m-2}\), evaluated by the Cayley--Chebyshev Laurent
constant-term rule; explicitly it is the coefficient formula of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  All retained odd coefficients
vanish, and the leading coefficient is
\[
A_4(\nu)=\frac{\mu_2^2}{8}.
\]
For an arbitrary probability law on \(\RR\) with finite mean
\(\bar{\gamma}\), the leading coefficient has the sharp threshold below.
Put \(X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}\) and set
\[
H(t)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\bigr)
\]
with the extended nonnegative convention for relative entropy.  Then
\[
\EE X_c^2<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)<\infty .
\]
If the variance is finite, then \(H(t)<\infty\) for every \(t>0\) and
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=\frac{(\EE X_c^2)^2}{8}.
\]
If the variance is infinite, then
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=+\infty
\]
in the extended sense.
\end{introresult}

\begin{introresult}{Main Theorem B: finite-moment multidimensional Poisson entropy jet}
Let \(d\ge1\), let \(L\in\NN\) satisfy \(L\ge d+1\), and let \(\nu\) be a
probability measure on \(\RR^d\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\).  Put
\[
X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma,\qquad
\mu_\alpha=\EE[X_c^\alpha].
\]
Assume that \(\EE|X_c|^L<\infty\).  Then, with
\[
h_t=P_t^{(d)}*\nu,\qquad
g_t=P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar\gamma),\qquad
\delta_t^{(d)}(y)=\frac{h_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}{g_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}-1,
\]
one has the finite quotient jet
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_t^{(d)}(y)
&=\sum_{2\le\lvert\alpha\rvert\le L}
\mu_\alpha U_\alpha^{(d)}(y)t^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert}
+\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}(y),\\
\lVert\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}\rVert_\infty+
\lVert\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}\rVert_{L^1(\Omega_d)}
&\le C_{d,L}t^{-L}\EE\!\left[
|X_c|^L\frac{|X_c|}{t}\mathbf1_{\{|X_c|\le t\}}
 +|X_c|^L\mathbf1_{\{|X_c|>t\}}
\right]\\
&=o(t^{-L}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover \(\|\delta_t^{(d)}\|_\infty=O(t^{-2})\), the matched entropy is
finite for all sufficiently large \(t\), and
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{m=4}^{L+2}\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)t^{-m}
+o(t^{-(L+2)}),
\]
where the tensor coefficients are those of
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidim-finite-moment-poisson-kl-asymptotic}; all retained
odd coefficients vanish.  Equivalently, the finite \(L\)-moment hypothesis
first proves the matched quotient package \((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\) and the
large-\(t\) entropy-existence clause \((\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\), and the
algebraic Taylor transfer then converts that quotient jet into the displayed
KL expansion.
\end{introresult}

\begin{introresult}{Main Theorem C: Cauchy Bregman tail identity}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and
finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_t&=P_t*\nu,\\
g_t&=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\\
u_t&=h_t/g_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
The nonlocal domain is part of the statement.  For every \(0<a<b<\infty\),
write
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_2&:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^2\,\dd\nu(\gamma),\\
m&:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\\
M&:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
  L:=3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr).
\]
Then, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x,y\in\RR\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-bregman-domain-quotient-bounds}
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad
\lvert u_t(x)-u_t(y)\rvert\le L\lvert x-y\rvert .
\end{equation}
Define the Bregman kernel by
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\]
and set
\[
\Psi(z):=\mathbf1_{\{\lvert z\rvert<1\}}
\,+\,\lvert z\rvert^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{\lvert z\rvert\ge1\}},
\qquad
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\}.
\]
The compact-window bounds imply the pointwise Bregman majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-bregman-domain-majorant}
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}\,g_t(x)\Psi(x-y)
\qquad (x\ne y,\ t\in[a,b]),
\end{equation}
and the same estimate holds with \(x\) and \(y\) interchanged.  Hence the
symmetrized kernel is dominated on \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) by
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y),
\]
whose integral over \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) equals
\(8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\).  The same one-sided majorant gives the compact-window
dissipation bound
\[
\int_a^b\mathcal I_\nu(s)\,\dd s
\le \frac{4C_{a,b,\nu}}{\pi}(b-a)
\]
once \(\mathcal I_\nu\) is defined below.  This domain clause makes the
following nonlocal double integral finite on compact time windows and
independent of the annular exhaustion used to construct it.

Define \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\) by annular cutoffs, equivalently by the
nonnegative Lebesgue integral
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t)
=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\]
with the diagonal ignored.  Then \(\mathcal I_\nu\) is measurable and locally
integrable on \((0,\infty)\), and for every \(t>0\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\,\dd s .
\]
\end{introresult}

\subsection*{Consequences and complements}

The remaining statements are organized as consequences of the three main
theorems rather than as additional main results.  Their full hypotheses are
kept with the formal results in
Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} and
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}; the following list records only the role
of each layer.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Missing-top-moment tails.}
For \(N\ge3\), \(p=2N-2\), a symmetric quotient expansion of the form
\[
  \frac{(P_t*\nu)(ty)}{P_t(ty)}-1
  =\sum_{n=2}^{p-1}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}
  +u_p(y)t^{-p}M_p(t)+e_t(y),
\]
with \(M_p(t)\to\infty\), \(M_p(t)=o(t^{p-2})\), and
\(\|e_t\|_\infty+\|e_t\|_{L^1(\omega)}=o(t^{-p}M_p(t))\), transfers to
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N-1}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
+J(2,p)\mu_2t^{-2N}M_p(t)
+o(t^{-2N}M_p(t)).
\]
Exact power tails, perturbative power tails, and monotone slowly varying tails
are verified subclasses of this quotient hypothesis.

\item \emph{Conditional transfer behind Main Theorem B.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets} records the
reusable implication
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})+(\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\Rightarrow\) tensor KL expansion.
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-density-quotient} supplies this package
from \(\EE|X_c|^L<\infty\) when \(L\ge d+1\), which is the finite-moment
route stated in Main Theorem B.

\item \emph{Covariance algebra and the second-order coefficient.}
Finite covariance gives the degree-two contraction
\[
\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)=\frac12\int_{\RR^d}b_\Sigma(y)^2\,\Omega_d(\dd y)
=c_d^{\rm iso}(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2
+c_d^{\rm tr}\|\Sigma_0\|_{\rm HS}^2,
\]
where \(b_\Sigma\), \(c_d^{\rm iso}\), and \(c_d^{\rm tr}\) are displayed in
Proposition~\ref{prop:multidim-formal-covariance-layer}.  It becomes the
leading KL coefficient once either the stated \(L^\infty\cap L^1(\Omega_d)\)
second-order quotient criterion or the non-uniform \(L^2\)-plus-nonlinear-integrand
criterion of Lemma~\ref{lem:l2-second-order-entropy-criterion} is available.
In particular, finite \((d+1)\)-moment supplies the needed quotient jet and
gives
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t^{(d)}*\nu\|P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)t^{-4}+o(t^{-4}).
\]

\item \emph{Optional normalized fourth-moment defect.}
For positive variance and finite centred fourth moment, put
\(X=X_c/\sigma\), \(\beta_3=\EE X^3\), and
\(Q_2=X^2-1-\beta_3X\).  The normalized algebraic defect
\[
\eta=\frac{\beta_3^2}{32}+\frac{\EE Q_2^2}{8}
=\frac{\EE X^4-1}{8}-\frac{3\beta_3^2}{32}
\]
controls an elementary sign--Rademacher \(W_2\) coupling.  Scaling this
identity and inserting the identified \(A_6\) coefficient gives
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}, a complement to the KL
coefficient hierarchy.
\end{itemize}

The order-eight Laurent certificate is supplied in
Appendix~\ref{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate} for verification.

\subsection*{Related theorem-level comparison}
\label{subsec:intro-recent-jfa-comparison}

Recent \emph{J. Funct. Anal.} papers closest in method treat
Esscher-transform CLT, heat-profile asymptotics, functional inequalities, and
Hardy--Stein identities.  None computes fixed-law matched Cauchy/Poisson KL
Laurent coefficients.  Bobkov--G{\"o}tze
\cite{BobkovGotzeEsscher2025} study changing normalized sums and normal
approximation; Main Theorem A fixes one input law and expands the continuous
Cauchy-kernel quantity
\[
  D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
\]
as \(t\to\infty\).  Anker--Papageorgiou--Zhang
\cite{AnkerPapageorgiouZhang2023HeatSymmetricSpaces} prove linear large-time
profiles on noncompact symmetric spaces; Main Theorem B proves a finite-moment
Euclidean radial Poisson quotient jet and converts it into nonlinear tensor KL
coefficients.  Bogdan--Gutowski--Pietruska-Pa{\l}uba
\cite{BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein} supply an
abstract nonlocal identity; Main Theorem C proves compact-window domain
bounds for the moving quotient
\((P_t*\nu)/P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\) and the exact Cauchy-scale KL tail
identity.
Appendix~\ref{app:related-work-scope} gives the compact comparator table and
entropy-asymptotic context beyond JFA.

\subsection*{Convention and organization}

The paper uses the extended nonnegative Kullback--Leibler functional of
\eqref{eq:extended-kl-definition}.

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation.  Sections~\ref{sec:cayley-gate}
and~\ref{sec:haar-pullback} record the Cayley and Haar-pullback
normalizations.  Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} proves the
multidimensional entropy jets, the one-dimensional coefficient hierarchy, the
sharp leading variance threshold, the explicit coefficient layer through
order six, and the coefficient-defect consequence.
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}
derives the Cauchy/KL Bregman tail identity by cutoff Green calculus,
verifies the Poisson quotient hypotheses, and then proves the dissipation-tail
coefficient expansion.

Appendices~\ref{app:entropy-integrals} and
\ref{app:order-eight-laurent-certificate} record the Laurent normalization
and order-eight certificate.
