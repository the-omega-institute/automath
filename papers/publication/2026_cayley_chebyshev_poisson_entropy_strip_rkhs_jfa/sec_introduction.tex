\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification of \(\RR\) with \(\TT\), and pulls
normalized Haar measure back to the Cauchy law
\[
\omega(\dd x)=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This is the endpoint-normalized form of the classical Cauchy/wrapped-Cauchy
M{\"o}bius correspondence in circular statistics
\cite{KentTyler1988WrappedCauchy,McCullagh1992CauchyModels,
McCullagh1996CauchyMobius,
KatoJones2010MobiusCircle,KatoMcCullagh2020SphereCauchyMobius}.
This normalization makes the fixed-law Poisson comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main-functional}
D_{\rm KL}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
\end{equation}
the object whose large-\(t\) behaviour is studied in Cayley--Chebyshev
modes.  The input law \(\nu\) is fixed, \(\bar\gamma\) is its mean, and
\(t\to\infty\) is the Poisson scale.  The convention for extended-valued KL
and the scope of the finite-moment and tail statements are collected in the
final scope subsection of the introduction.

\subsection*{Main contribution and mechanism}

The paper proves a coefficient theorem for the fixed-law matched Cauchy
quotient, not a CLT theorem and not a parametric Cauchy-distance formula.
The new part has three pieces.
First, after the Cauchy translate matched to the barycentre has been divided
out, the density quotient admits a Cayley--Chebyshev expansion whose
coefficients are finite Laurent constant terms.  This identifies the
one-dimensional KL coefficients \(A_{2m}(\nu)\) as universal polynomial
functions of the centred moments whenever the required moments exist.
Second, the leading coefficient has a sharp analytic threshold:
finite centred variance is equivalent to a finite \(t^{-4}\) matched-Poisson
KL coefficient, and the value is \(\mu_2^2/8\).  Third, the paper separates
this finite-moment polynomial hierarchy from explicit power-tail replacement
layers.
For the explicit symmetric power-tail laws treated here, the missing top
moment gives a logarithmic term in the quotient and hence in the entropy:
\(t^{-2N}\log t\) for \(N\ge3\), while the borderline infinite-variance
\(N=2\) example has leading scale \(t^{-4}(\log t)^2\).  These are
class-specific calculations; they are not a general slowly varying or
regularly varying replacement theorem.

The mechanism is elementary but rigid.  With \(x=\bar\gamma+ty\), a translate
by the centred variable \(X_c:=\gamma-\bar\gamma\) is encoded by
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\qquad \varepsilon=X_c/t .
\]
The Cayley angle \(y=\tan\theta\) converts the Taylor modes of
\(R_\varepsilon\) into finite trigonometric, equivalently Laurent, modes.
Thus quotient expansion is an analytic estimate, while passage from the
quotient to KL is an algebraic expansion of
\((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) in \(L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)\).  The finite-moment
remainder, the explicit power-tail replacement quotient, and the
multidimensional moment jet are therefore stated separately below.

\subsection*{Theorem-level comparison with recent JFA work}

The comparison object throughout the paper is
\[
  D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)),
\]
where the input law \(\nu\) is fixed and only the Poisson scale tends to
infinity.  None of the recent JFA papers cited below expands this
fixed-input quotient.  Bobkov--G{\"o}tze treat Esscher transforms and
R\'enyi-\(\infty\) convergence for normalized sums;
Anker--Papageorgiou--Zhang treat heat profiles; Bogdan--Gutowski--
Pietruska-Pa{\l}uba provide the nearest Hardy--Stein/Bregman identity
framework.  The new contribution here is the Cayley coefficient extraction
for a fixed law and the finite-variance iff threshold for the matched Cauchy
quotient.

\subsection*{Formal theorem statements}

The formal statements below give the exact assumptions and coefficients used
later.  The multidimensional coefficients are denoted
\(\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)\); the one-dimensional coefficients use the
unsuperscripted symbols \(A_k(\nu)\).

\begin{theorem}[One-dimensional coefficient identification at finite \((2N-2)\)-moment level]%
\label{thm:intro-one-dimensional-universal-expansion}
Let \(N\ge2\), let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR\) with mean
\(\bar\gamma\), and put \(X_c:=\gamma-\bar\gamma\).  Assume that \(X_c\)
has finite \((2N-2)\)th absolute moment, and put
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),\qquad
\mu_n=\int X_c^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-one-dimensional-transfer}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N}).
\end{equation}
Each \(A_{2m}\) is the finite-moment evaluation of a universal polynomial in
the centred moments, computed by the Cayley mode integrals in
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients}; all retained odd
coefficients vanish.  More explicitly, for \(2\le m\le N\),
\[
A_{2m}(\nu)=
\sum_{r=2}^{m}
\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\substack{n_1+\cdots+n_r=2m\\2\le n_j\le 2N-2}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r}
\int_{\RR}u_{n_1}\cdots u_{n_r}\,\dd\omega .
\]
Prior object:
\(D_{\rm KL}(\mu_n\|\gamma)\) for normalized sums.  Current object:
\(D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))\).  The exact new item is the
coefficient extraction formula displayed above, with the Cauchy/Cayley mode
integrals reduced to finite Laurent constant terms.
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp leading tail threshold in dimension one]%
\label{thm:intro-sharp-leading-threshold}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR\) with finite mean
\(\bar\gamma\), and put
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),\qquad
H(t)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t).
\]
The last quantity is the extended KL functional of
\eqref{eq:extended-kl-definition}, so \(H(t)\in[0,\infty]\) for every
\(t>0\).
If the centred variance \(\mu_2=\int(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\,\dd\nu(\gamma)\)
is finite, then \(H(t)<\infty\) for every \(t>0\) and
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=\frac{\mu_2^2}{8}.
\]
If the centred variance is infinite, then
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=+\infty.
\]
The infinite-variance assertion is an identity in the extended interval:
it includes the possibility that \(H(t)=+\infty\) at some, or all,
large times.
Equivalently, finite centred second moment is necessary and sufficient for a
finite leading \(t^{-4}\) matched-Poisson KL coefficient, and the finite value
is the universal coefficient \(A_4(\nu)=\mu_2^2/8\).  This is
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-leading-kl-variance-threshold}.
\end{theorem}

Theorems~\ref{thm:intro-one-dimensional-universal-expansion}
and~\ref{thm:intro-sharp-leading-threshold} are the finite-moment coefficient
theorem and the sharp finite-variance threshold, respectively.  They do not
assert any replacement rule for arbitrary regularly varying or slowly varying
tails.  The only logarithmic tail replacements proved in this paper are the
explicit symmetric power-tail class
Proposition~\ref{prop:log-tail-counterexample-top-moment}, the perturbative
class Corollary~\ref{cor:perturbative-power-tail-log-replacement}, and the
\(N=2\) borderline Proposition~\ref{prop:n2-borderline-infinite-variance-layer};
the conditional transfer
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional-truncated-top-moment-replacement} must have
its quotient hypotheses verified separately for each tail class.
For the explicitly displayed symmetric density
\[
f(x)=g(x)+c|x|^{-p-1}{\bf1}_{|x|\ge R},
\]
and for perturbations satisfying
\eqref{eq:perturbative-power-tail-lower-moments}--\eqref{eq:perturbative-power-tail-far-remainder},
the quotient has a top-mode truncated-moment term
\(u_p(y)t^{-p}M_p(t)\); Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional-truncated-top-moment-replacement}
then transfers this to the entropy layer
\(\kappa_N\mu_2t^{-2N}M_p(t)\).  No general regularly varying replacement
theorem is claimed.

\begin{theorem}[Multidimensional finite-moment Poisson KL asymptotic]%
\label{thm:intro-multidimensional-jet}
\label{cor:intro-multidimensional-finite-moment-entropy-jet}
Let \(d\ge1\), let \(L\ge\max\{2,d+1\}\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability
measure on \(\RR^d\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\) and finite centred
\(L\)-absolute moment.  Here
\[
P_t^{(d)}(x)=
\frac{\Gamma((d+1)/2)}{\pi^{(d+1)/2}}\,
\frac{t}{(t^2+|x|^2)^{(d+1)/2}}
\]
is the normalized radial Poisson kernel on \(\RR^d\).  Put
\[
h_t=P_t^{(d)}*\nu,\qquad
g_t=P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar\gamma),\qquad
X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma,
\]
write
\[
\mu_\alpha=\EE[X_c^\alpha]\qquad(|\alpha|\le L),
\]
and let
\[
\Sigma=\EE[X_cX_c^{\mathsf T}],\qquad
\Sigma_0=\Sigma-d^{-1}(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)I_d .
\]
Then
\[
D_{\rm KL}\bigl(P_t^{(d)}*\nu\,\|\,P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
=\sum_{m=4}^{L+2}\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)t^{-m}
+o(t^{-(L+2)}),
\]
where the tensor coefficients are
\[
\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)=
\sum_{r=2}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\substack{|\alpha_1|,\ldots,|\alpha_r|\ge2\\
|\alpha_1|+\cdots+|\alpha_r|=m\\
|\alpha_j|\le L}}
\mu_{\alpha_1}\cdots\mu_{\alpha_r}
\int_{\RR^d}\prod_{j=1}^rU_{\alpha_j}^{(d)}(y)\,\Omega_d(\dd y).
\]
All odd retained coefficients vanish:
\[
\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)=0
\qquad\text{for every odd }m,\quad 4\le m\le L+2.
\]
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-multidim-leading}
\mathcal A_4^{(d)}(\nu)
=
c_d^{\rm iso}(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2
+c_d^{\rm tr}\|\Sigma_0\|_{\rm HS}^2,
\end{equation}
where
\[
c_d^{\rm iso}=
\frac{3(d+1)(7d+9)}
{4d(d+3)(d+5)(d+7)},
\qquad
c_d^{\rm tr}=
\frac{3(d+1)(d+3)}
{4(d+5)(d+7)} .
\]
Finite covariance alone gives only the displayed quadratic form; it is not
asserted to imply the KL asymptotic.  The proof in the main text first proves
the finite-moment quotient expansion
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-density-quotient}, then applies the
entropy transfer Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets}, and
finally inserts the covariance contraction from
Proposition~\ref{prop:multidim-formal-covariance-layer}.  This combined
statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:multidim-finite-moment-poisson-kl-asymptotic}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Poisson quotient KL tail identity]%
\label{thm:intro-kl-tail-identity}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\) and
finite centred second moment.  For
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t(x)=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t,
\]
define
\[
I_\nu(t)=
\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\left(
u_t(x)\log\!\bigl(u_t(x)/u_t(y)\bigr)-u_t(x)+u_t(y)
\right)}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\]
Then \(I_\nu\in L^1_{\rm loc}(0,\infty)\) and
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
=\int_t^\infty I_\nu(s)\dd s,
\]
for every \(t>0\).  This is Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit \(N=4\) coefficient layer through order eight]%
\label{thm:intro-explicit-n4-layer}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\),
finite variance \(\sigma^2\ge0\), and finite centred sixth absolute moment.
Write
\[
X_c:=\gamma-\bar\gamma,\qquad
\mu_j:=\int_{\RR}X_c^j\,\dd\nu(\gamma)\quad (j\le6),
\qquad
\sigma^2=\mu_2 .
\]
Then
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} identifies
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
=
\frac{\sigma^4}{8t^4}
+\frac{A_6(\nu)}{t^6}
+\frac{A_8(\nu)}{t^8}
+o(t^{-8}),
\]
where
\[
A_6(\nu)
=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64},
\]
and
\[
A_8(\nu)
=
\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
If the centred seventh absolute moment is finite, then
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder} improves the last
remainder to \(O(t^{-9})\).  When \(\sigma^2=0\),
\(\nu=\delta_{\bar\gamma}\) and the expansion is the trivial identity
\(0=0\).  These are Theorems~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} and
\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder}.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{Cayley coefficient mechanism}

After the rescaling \(x=\bar\gamma+ty\), a translate by the centred variable
\(X_c:=\gamma-\bar\gamma\) is encoded by
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\qquad \varepsilon=X_c/t .
\]
The Cayley angle \(y=\tan\theta\) converts the Taylor modes of
\(R_\varepsilon\) into finite trigonometric, equivalently Laurent, modes.
Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode} gives the mode formula,
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} defines the entropy
coefficients by finite Cauchy-weighted mode integrals, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} reduces those
integrals to Laurent constant-term extraction.

The finite-moment quotient remainder is proved in
Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-moment-density-truncation}; the retained-order nonlinear
transfer through \((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) is
Proposition~\ref{prop:density-to-entropy-descent} and
Theorem~\ref{prop:coefficient-identification-poisson-entropy-transfer}.  The
finite Laurent arithmetic needed for the explicit \(N=4\) layer is displayed
in Lemmas~\ref{lem:n4-normalized-laurent-representatives}--\ref{lem:n4-entropy-coefficient-substitution}.

\subsection*{Further consequences}

\begin{corollary}[Coefficient-defect coupling at the fourth-moment layer]%
\label{cor:intro-defect-certificate}
Let \(\nu\) have mean \(\bar\gamma\), variance \(\sigma^2>0\), and finite
centred fourth absolute moment.  Put
\[
X_c:=\gamma-\bar\gamma,\qquad
\mu_j:=\int_{\RR}X_c^j\,\dd\nu(\gamma)\quad (j\le4),
\qquad
\sigma^2=\mu_2 .
\]
The \(N=3\) case of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-one-dimensional-universal-expansion} identifies
\[
A_6(\nu)=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64}
\]
as the genuine \(t^{-6}\) coefficient.  Define the sixth-order defect
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)=-\frac{7}{64}\sigma^6-A_6(\nu).
\]
Equivalently, for \(X=X_c/\sigma\), \(\beta_3=\EE X^3\), and
\(Q_2=X^2-1-\beta_3X\),
\[
\Delta_6(\nu)=
\sigma^6\left(\frac{\beta_3^2}{32}+\frac{\|Q_2\|_2^2}{8}\right).
\]
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} couples this fixed-variance
coefficient defect to
\(\rho_\sigma=\frac12(\delta_{-\sigma}+\delta_\sigma)\) in \(W_2\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Leading multidimensional KL coefficient under a finite jet hypothesis]%
\label{cor:intro-multidimensional-leading-kl-coefficient}
Let \(d\ge1\), let \(L\ge\max\{2,d+1\}\), and assume that
\(\nu\) has mean \(\bar\gamma\), centred tensor moments
\(\mu_\alpha\) for \(2\le|\alpha|\le L\), and satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L}):\qquad
\delta_t^{(d)}(y)
&=\sum_{2\le|\alpha|\le L}
\mu_\alpha U_\alpha^{(d)}(y)t^{-|\alpha|}
+\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}(y),\\
\|\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}\|_\infty+
\|\rho_{L,t}^{(d)}\|_{L^1(\Omega_d)}
&=o(t^{-L}),\qquad
\|\delta_t^{(d)}\|_\infty=O(t^{-2}).
\end{aligned}
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-density-quotient}, the finite
centred \(L\)th absolute moment hypothesis is a sufficient condition for
this jet.
With \(A_{4,\mathrm{quad}}^{(d)}(\nu)\) as in
\eqref{eq:intro-multidim-leading},
\[
D_{\rm KL}\bigl(P_t^{(d)}*\nu\,\|\,P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
=A_{4,\mathrm{quad}}^{(d)}(\nu)t^{-4}+o(t^{-4}).
\]
This is the abstract-jet version of the leading coefficient statement.  The
finite-\(L\)-moment version is already included in
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-multidimensional-jet}.  In both statements the
finite-covariance proposition supplies only the value of the quadratic form;
the quotient remainder is the additional hypothesis that makes it an
asymptotic coefficient.
\end{corollary}

\subsection*{Scope}

The KL functional used throughout is the extended nonnegative
Kullback--Leibler functional of \eqref{eq:extended-kl-definition}
\cite{KullbackLeibler1951,CoverThomas2006}.  Under finite centred second
moment in dimension one the matched-Poisson KL value is finite for every
\(t>0\); without that hypothesis the sharp-threshold theorem is interpreted
in \([0,\infty]\), so the conclusion
\(t^4D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))\to+\infty\) includes the
case where the KL value is already \(+\infty\) at some large times.  The
finite-moment hierarchy identifies universal polynomial coefficients only
when the needed centred moments exist; for the explicit symmetric power-tail
laws treated in the main text, missing top moments belong instead to the
proved logarithmic replacement layers, and no general regularly or slowly
varying replacement theorem is asserted.  In several dimensions, the
finite-covariance proposition is the quadratic coefficient calculation,
whereas the KL asymptotic is the separate finite-quotient-remainder
corollary.  The dissipation identity is proved in
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy} by the Cauchy cutoff Green formula, and
the conditional finite-time post-processing in
Subsection~\ref{app:conditional-post-processing} is a bookkeeping layer whose
coefficient, remainder, threshold, and numerical-error inputs must be supplied
independently.
