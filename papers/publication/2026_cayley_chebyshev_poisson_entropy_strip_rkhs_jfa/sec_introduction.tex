\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification of \(\RR\) with \(\TT\), and pulls
normalized Haar measure back to the Cauchy law
\[
\omega(\dd x)=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This is the endpoint-normalized form of the classical Cauchy/wrapped-Cauchy
M{\"o}bius correspondence in circular statistics
\cite{KentTyler1988WrappedCauchy,McCullagh1992CauchyModels,
McCullagh1996CauchyMobius,
KatoJones2010MobiusCircle,KatoMcCullagh2020SphereCauchyMobius}.
This normalization makes the fixed-law Poisson comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main-functional}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\bigr)
\end{equation}
the object whose large-\(t\) behaviour is studied in Cayley--Chebyshev
modes.  The input law \(\nu\) is fixed, \(\bar{\gamma}\) is its mean, and
\(t\to\infty\) is the Poisson scale.

The basic calculation is a quotient calculation.  Let
\[
X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}
\]
be the centred translation variable.  With \(x=\bar{\gamma}+ty\), translation
by \(X_c\) is encoded by
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\qquad \varepsilon=X_c/t .
\]
The Cayley angle \(y=\tan\theta\) turns the Taylor coefficients of
\(R_\varepsilon\) into finite trigonometric, equivalently Laurent, modes.
After the matched Cauchy profile has been divided out, the quotient expansion
is therefore reduced to finite mode algebra; the KL expansion is then the
ordinary Taylor expansion of \((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) in
\(L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)\).

\subsection*{Contribution}

The first theorem is one-dimensional and computes the full finite-moment KL
coefficient hierarchy.  It also identifies the exact threshold at which the
leading \(t^{-4}\) scale exists: finite centred variance gives coefficient
\(\mu_2^2/8\), while infinite centred variance forces
\(t^4D_{\rm KL}\to+\infty\).

The second theorem is multidimensional and finite-moment.  For
\(L\ge d+1\), finite centred \(L\)-moment closes the large-translation
remainder in the matched radial Poisson quotient, gives an explicit
\(L^\infty\cap L^1(\Omega_d)\) jet, supplies the large-\(t\)
entropy-existence package, and hence gives the tensor KL expansion through
order \(L+2\).  The two-region expression is retained as the quantitative
remainder bound; in this range its \(o(t^{-L})\) decay is equivalent to the
finite \(L\)-moment assumption.
The leading multidimensional covariance formula is deliberately read through
this analytic route.  Finite covariance alone determines the bounded quadratic
mode \(b_\Sigma\) and the algebraic number \(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\), but it
does not by itself make \(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\) the coefficient of
\eqref{eq:intro-main-functional}.  It becomes the leading KL coefficient only
after a quotient/existence bridge is in force, for example the
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})+(\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\) package supplied here by
finite \(L\)-moment with \(L\ge d+1\), or the separate second-order quotient
criterion stated in Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics}.

The third theorem is dynamic rather than asymptotic.  Under finite centred
variance the moving matched Cauchy quotient lies in the domain of the
Cauchy-generator Bregman form on every compact time window, and the KL
functional is exactly the tail integral of that nonlocal dissipation.
The point is not merely to quote a Hardy--Stein identity for a fixed
semigroup orbit.  Here both the quotient
\[
u_t=\frac{h_t}{g_t}
\]
and the reference density \(g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\) move with \(t\).
The logarithmic chain rule therefore first gives the two-pair identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-two-pair-chain-rule}
H'(t)=\langle \log u_t,\partial_t h_t\rangle
      -\langle u_t-1,\partial_t g_t\rangle,
\qquad H(t)=D_{\rm KL}(h_t\|g_t),
\end{equation}
not a single fixed-reference Dirichlet form.  Applying the Cauchy Green
formula separately to the pairs \((h_t,\log u_t)\) and \((g_t,u_t)\), and
using \(\int\partial_tg_t=0\), produces the algebraic difference
\[
(\rho_xa-\rho_yb)(\log a-\log b)-(\rho_x-\rho_y)(a-b)
=\rho_x\Lambda(a,b)+\rho_y\Lambda(b,a),
\]
where \(a=u_t(x)\), \(b=u_t(y)\), \(\rho_x=g_t(x)\),
\(\rho_y=g_t(y)\), and
\(\Lambda(a,b)=a\log(a/b)-a+b\).  This two-Green-pairing closure, together
with the compact-window cutoff and measurability lemma in
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}, is the extra step that is not supplied
by existing Hardy--Stein formulas alone.

\subsection*{Theorems}\label{subsec:main-results}

\begin{maintheorem}{One-dimensional coefficient hierarchy and variance threshold}
\label{thm:main-A}
Let \(N\ge2\), put \(L=2N-2\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability law on
\(\RR\) with finite mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and finite centred \(L\)th absolute
moment.  With
\[
\begin{aligned}
  X_c&=\gamma-\bar{\gamma},\\
  \mu_n&=\int_{\RR} X_c^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma)\qquad(2\le n\le L),\\
  h_t&=P_t*\nu,\qquad
  g_t&=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),
\end{aligned}
\]
one has
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N}).
\]
Each coefficient \(A_{2m}(\nu)\) is a universal polynomial in
\(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{2m-2}\), evaluated by the Cayley--Chebyshev Laurent
constant-term rule; explicitly it is the coefficient formula of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  All retained odd coefficients
vanish, and the leading coefficient is
\[
A_4(\nu)=\frac{\mu_2^2}{8}.
\]
For an arbitrary probability law on \(\RR\) with finite mean
\(\bar{\gamma}\), the leading coefficient has the sharp threshold below.
Put \(X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}\) and set
\[
H(t)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\bigr)
\]
with the extended nonnegative convention for relative entropy.  Then
\[
\EE X_c^2<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)<\infty .
\]
If the variance is finite, then \(H(t)<\infty\) for every \(t>0\) and
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=\frac{(\EE X_c^2)^2}{8}.
\]
If the variance is infinite, then
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=+\infty
\]
in the extended sense.
\end{maintheorem}

\begin{maintheorem}{Finite-moment multidimensional Poisson entropy jet}
\label{thm:main-B}
Let \(d\ge1\), let \(L\in\NN\) satisfy \(L\ge d+1\), and let \(\nu\) be a
probability measure on \(\RR^d\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\).  Put
\[
X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma .
\]
Assume \(\EE |X_c|^L<\infty\).  Then the centred moments
\[
\mu_\alpha=\EE[X_c^\alpha]\qquad(|\alpha|\le L)
\]
are finite.  With
\[
h_t=P_t^{(d)}*\nu,\qquad
g_t=P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar\gamma),
\]
one has, for all sufficiently large \(t\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{m=4}^{L+2}\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)t^{-m}
+o(t^{-(L+2)}),
\]
where the tensor coefficients are those of
Corollary~\ref{thm:multidim-finite-moment-poisson-kl-asymptotic}; all retained
odd coefficients vanish.
For the leading retained coefficient, let
\[
\Sigma=\EE[X_cX_c^{\mathsf T}],\qquad
\Sigma_0=\Sigma-\frac{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma}{d}I_d .
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal A_4^{(d)}(\nu)=\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)
=c_d^{\rm iso}(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2
+c_d^{\rm tr}\|\Sigma_0\|_{\rm HS}^2,
\]
with \(c_d^{\rm iso}\) and \(c_d^{\rm tr}\) as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidim-covariance-algebra}.
\end{maintheorem}

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-B}.}
The finite \(L\)-moment hypothesis first supplies the quotient jet and the
large-\(t\) entropy representation.  In the body this is expressed by the
remainder
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_{d,L}(t)
&=t^{-(L+1)}\EE\!\left[
|X_c|^{L+1}\mathbf1_{\{|X_c|\le t\}}\right] \\
&\quad{}+t^{-(d+1)}\EE\!\left[
|X_c|^{d+1}\mathbf1_{\{|X_c|>t\}}\right] \\
&\quad{}+\sum_{k=d+2}^{L}t^{-k}\EE\!\left[
|X_c|^k\mathbf1_{\{|X_c|>t\}}\right],
\end{aligned}
\]
where the final sum is empty when \(L=d+1\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:two-region-remainder-equivalent-finite-moment}
shows that \(\mathcal R_{d,L}(t)=o(t^{-L})\) is equivalent to
\(\EE|X_c|^L<\infty\) in the range \(L\ge d+1\), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-density-quotient} converts it into the
quotient expansion.  Proposition~\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets}
then gives the KL coefficients.  The covariance formula for
\(\mathcal A_4^{(d)}\) uses Theorem~\ref{thm:multidim-covariance-algebra};
finite covariance alone gives only the algebraic quantity
\(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\), not a KL coefficient.

\begin{maintheorem}{Cauchy Bregman tail identity}
\label{thm:main-C}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and
finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_t&=P_t*\nu,\\
g_t&=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\\
u_t&=h_t/g_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
Define the Bregman kernel by
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\]
and define the nonlocal Bregman dissipation by the nonnegative Lebesgue
integral
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t)
=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\]
with the diagonal ignored.  Then \(\mathcal I_\nu\) is measurable and locally
integrable on \((0,\infty)\), and for every \(t>0\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\,\dd s .
\]
\end{maintheorem}

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-C}.}
The compact-window constants are used only to justify the nonlocal domain and
the two Green pairings.  For \(0<a<b<\infty\), put
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^2\,\dd\nu(\gamma),\qquad
m:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\qquad
M:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},
\]
and \(L_a:=3a^{-1}(1+\mu_2/a^2)\).  Then
\[
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad
\lvert u_t(x)-u_t(y)\rvert\le L_a\lvert x-y\rvert
\qquad(t\in[a,b]).
\]
With
\[
\Psi(z)=\mathbf1_{\{\lvert z\rvert<1\}}
\,+\,\lvert z\rvert^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{\lvert z\rvert\ge1\}},
\qquad
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L_a^2,(M-m)^2\},
\]
the one-sided Bregman kernel is bounded by
\[
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}\,g_t(x)\Psi(x-y).
\]
This compact-window majorant is the input for
Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-window-bregman-identity}; the endpoint decay
then gives the tail formula stated in Theorem~\ref{thm:main-C}.

\subsection*{Related theorem-level comparison}
\label{subsec:intro-recent-jfa-comparison}

Recent \emph{J. Funct. Anal.} papers closest in method treat
Esscher-transform CLT, heat-profile asymptotics, Poisson transport,
functional inequalities, and Hardy--Stein identities.  Poisson transport and
Poisson-channel results are adjacent because they combine Poisson laws with
entropy, but their Poisson object is respectively a count/point-process
measure or a counting-output channel, not the harmonic Cauchy kernel
\(P_t(x)=\pi^{-1}t/(t^2+x^2)\) smoothing a fixed Euclidean law.  The
fixed-law matched functional \eqref{eq:intro-main-functional} and its Laurent
coefficient hierarchy are therefore separate from those count-Poisson
problems.

The closest Euclidean fractional-heat asymptotic reference is V{\'a}zquez's
large-time theory for the fractional heat equation
\cite{Vazquez2018FractionalHeatEuclidean}, which proves convergence of
fractional heat solutions to the fundamental kernel and rates under moment
assumptions.  The present paper differs in two ways: it specializes to the
\(\alpha=1\) Poisson/Cauchy kernel and studies the nonlinear matched relative
entropy
\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)),
\]
extracting explicit moment-dependent KL coefficients rather than only
kernel-profile convergence.
Thus the new information is not convergence to the Poisson or stable profile,
but the explicit moment polynomial hierarchy produced by the logarithmic
entropy functional.  Refined fractional and Poisson-kernel profile expansions
\cite{IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2017,
IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2022RefinedFractional,
FilaIshigeKawakami2012PoissonKernel} are used in the same comparator role:
they estimate evolved profiles, whereas the present expansion is a nonlinear
moving-reference entropy expansion.  Appendix~\ref{app:related-work-scope}
gives the
theorem-level comparator table and the entropy-asymptotic context beyond JFA,
including small-time heat-kernel relative entropy expansions and general
relative-entropy representation formulas.

\subsection*{Convention and organization}

The paper uses the extended nonnegative Kullback--Leibler functional of
\eqref{eq:extended-kl-definition}.

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation.  Sections~\ref{sec:cayley-gate}
and~\ref{sec:haar-pullback} record the Cayley and Haar-pullback
normalizations, including the notation table used in the entropy arguments.
Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} proves the coefficient hierarchy, the
variance threshold, and the multidimensional quotient criteria.  It begins
with the detailed proof map, so the introduction need not carry the internal
hypothesis diagram.  Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy} proves the
compact-window Bregman domain bounds and the Cauchy/KL tail identity.  The
appendices contain the related-work table, normalization details, and optional
coefficient and tail checks used only for auditability of examples.
