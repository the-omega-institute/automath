\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification $\overline\RR$ of the line with
the circle~$\TT$.  Under this identification normalized Haar measure pulls
back to the Cauchy law
\[
\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This normalization is the common coordinate system of the paper.  It puts
Poisson smoothing on~$\RR$, circle Fourier analysis, Cauchy-weighted
entropy integrals, and Laurent coefficient extraction into one calculus.
The Cauchy law is used here as the Haar/Poisson normalization for
the single matched fixed-law functional
\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)).
\]
This is the paper's scope.  The input law is fixed, the time parameter
\(t\) tends to infinity, and the coefficients are universal
moment-polynomial entropy jets for this Poisson comparison.  The comparison
with normalized-sum entropy asymptotics, heat and fractional-flow profile
asymptotics, transport or functional-inequality stability, and parametric
Cauchy divergence formulae is made theorem by theorem below.

\textbf{Scope and nonclaims.}
All coefficient statements below concern this fixed-law matched Poisson KL
functional.  ``Threshold'' refers to identification of the displayed
universal moment-polynomial coefficient under the stated finite-moment
hypothesis; it is not an assertion that every weaker-tail, tail-dependent, or
renormalized entropy asymptotic must fail.  The finite-time estimates use one
observed scalar only after independent bounds for the next coefficient and the
remainder have been fixed in advance.  All coefficient arithmetic invoked by
the entropy proof is the finite Laurent arithmetic displayed in the article.

\subsection*{Main results}

The first main result extends beyond the Cayley coordinate.  For the radial
Poisson kernel on \(\RR^d\), Theorems
\ref{thm:multidimensional-density-quotient},
\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets}, and
\ref{thm:multidim-leading-positive-defect} prove a finite-moment
density-to-entropy jet under an integer truncation
\(L\ge\max\{2,d+1\}\) and the corresponding finite centred \(L\)th absolute
moment for the fixed-law comparison between
\(P_t^{(d)}*\nu\) and the translate of \(P_t^{(d)}\) matched to the mean of
\(\nu\).  The leading \(t^{-4}\) coefficient is not just an extracted Taylor
term: it splits as a positive sum of the squared isotropic second-moment
defect and the squared traceless covariance.  For \(d\ge2\), this proved
leading \(\mathcal A_4^{(d)}\) asymptotic is still part of the same
\(L\ge d+1\) finite-moment theorem and is not a finite-covariance-only
statement.  The remaining coefficients are universal tensor-moment
polynomials formed from the multivariable Taylor coefficients of
\[
\left(\frac{1+|y|^2}{1+|y-z|^2}\right)^{(d+1)/2}.
\]
Thus the analytic finite-moment passage is a multidimensional Poisson theorem
with a dimension-dependent tail hypothesis and an explicit positive
covariance-defect structure at the first entropy level; the scalar Laurent
computation below is its explicit one-dimensional specialization.

The one-dimensional Cayley theorem is the explicit computable specialization
of that entropy-jet mechanism for precisely the matched KL functional used in
the rest of the paper, with the input law kept fixed.  For every
\(N\ge2\), if a probability law~\(\nu\) has mean $\bar\gamma$ and finite
centred $(2N-2)$th absolute moment, then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N})
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\]
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  In the sense fixed in the
scope paragraph, the threshold is coefficient-identification: by
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-kl-moment-threshold-sharpness}, the displayed
universal moment-polynomial coefficient \(A_{2N}\) contains a nonzero
top-moment term and therefore cannot be evaluated from the centred moments
of orders at most \(2N-3\) alone; the centred \((2N-2)\)nd moment is part of
the displayed universal coefficient.

The second main result is the explicit eighth-order layer.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}
computes the \(t^{-4}\), \(t^{-6}\), and \(t^{-8}\) coefficients at the
finite-sixth-moment threshold, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth-seventh-remainder} adds the finite-time
\(O(t^{-9})\) estimate under the corresponding seventh-moment hypothesis.
Those coefficients define the defect coordinates used in
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}, which bounds the
fixed-variance moment defect in \(W_2\).  The finite-time statements are
collected later as applications/bookkeeping: they combine the identity
\[
\mathfrak D_t^{(6)}(\nu)
=\Delta_6(\nu)-A_8(\nu)t^{-2}-t^6E(t)
\]
with constants for \(A_8\) and \(E(t)\) fixed in advance, either from a
controlled seventh-moment shell or from supplied fixed-law data.
The density quotient expansion, entropy transfer theorem, explicit \(N=4\)
coefficient theorem, defect corollary, and Doob-tail theorem are summarized
before the entropy proofs in Subsection~\ref{sec:entropy-theorem-map}.

Thus the new ingredient has two layers.  The analytic layer is the
finite-moment Poisson entropy-jet theorem for radial Poisson kernels in
dimension \(d\), stated under \(L\ge\max\{2,d+1\}\) and
\(\EE|X_c|^L<\infty\), together with the exact positive decomposition of the
leading coefficient into isotropic and traceless covariance defects under
that same hypothesis.  The
one-dimensional layer is explicit finite-moment Laurent arithmetic for
arbitrary fixed input laws under Poisson smoothing: the
\(t^{-6}\) and \(t^{-8}\) coefficient table, the resulting sixth/eighth-order
defect ladder, and the resulting coefficient-defect \(W_2\) consequence.

Two supporting tools keep the argument checkable without changing this
hierarchy.  The KL Doob-tail section gives the dissipation-tail
representation \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}=\int_t^\infty I_\nu(s)\dd s\) and the
corresponding tail-window coefficient expansion.  The finite arithmetic used
for the one-dimensional \(N=4\) layer is completely contained in the displayed
Laurent coefficient vectors, the zero-sum convolution formula, and the
coefficient table.  Root-of-unity averaging is mentioned only as an elementary
way to view the same constant-term identity after the finite Laurent
polynomial has already been specified.  No observation, sampling, or lattice
interface is an input to the entropy proof or to any reproducibility claim made
in the article.

\subsection*{Cayley coefficient mechanism}

The comparison kernel
\[
R_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
\]
measures the ratio between a translated Poisson profile and the reference
Cauchy density after the rescaling $x=\bar\gamma+ty$.  The Cayley angle
$y=\tan\theta$ converts the Taylor modes of $R_\varepsilon$ into finite
trigonometric expressions governed by Chebyshev polynomials of the second
kind.  Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode} records this mode formula.
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} then defines the formal
coefficient polynomials $A_k^{\rm form}$ by finite Cauchy-weighted mode
integrals and proves the odd-order cancellation.  At this stage the
calculation is algebraic: a law is evaluated only through the moment
interface in
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}.

The analytic content is the finite-moment passage from this algebraic
calculus to actual large-time entropy coefficients.
The main entropy-transfer theorem is the coefficient-identification statement.
Stronger-moment fixed-law rates, moment-ball uniformity, and the associated
constant bookkeeping are collected in the appendix; the scope paragraph above
fixes how these auxiliary regimes are to be read.

Finite phase sampling is only an exactness check for finite Laurent
polynomials after the coefficient calculus has already been formed.  The main
entropy proof uses Haar/Laurent constant-coefficient extraction directly.
The short final auxiliary-data remark records the root-of-unity averaging
identity below the explicit Fourier-support barrier.

\subsection*{Organization}

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation and conventions.
Subsection~\ref{sec:cayley-gate} records the Cayley formulas, and
Subsection~\ref{sec:haar-pullback} records the Haar-pullback normalization.
Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} proves the main entropy results: the
multidimensional Poisson entropy-jet theorem, its one-dimensional
Cayley--Chebyshev coefficient specialization, and the seventh-moment
eighth-order defect layer, followed by the coefficient-level \(W_2\)
consequence and the finite-time applications/bookkeeping estimates.
Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy}
records the Cauchy/KL Hardy--Stein tail identity for the same Poisson
comparison quotient and derives the dissipation-tail coefficient expansion
from the main coefficient theorem.  Appendix~\ref{app:related-work-scope}
collects the related-work
comparison.  Appendix~\ref{app:entropy-integrals}
records the weighted trigonometric integrals and coefficient arithmetic used
in the eighth-order expansion.
Appendix~\ref{app:haar-pullback-uniqueness} records the endpoint-normalized
uniqueness statement for the Cayley chart.
Appendix~\ref{app:finite-time-defect-estimates} records the constant-regime
bookkeeping and controlled-shell uniform version for the finite-time
moment-defect estimates.  The exact Laurent arithmetic certificate is the
finite data displayed immediately after the eighth-order coefficient table:
the coefficient vectors, the zero-sum convolution formula, and the resulting
constant terms.
