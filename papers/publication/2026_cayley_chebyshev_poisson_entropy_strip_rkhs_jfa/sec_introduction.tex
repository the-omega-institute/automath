\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification $\overline\RR$ of the line with
the circle~$\TT$.  Under this identification normalized Haar measure pulls
back to the Cauchy law
\[
\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This normalization is the common coordinate system of the paper.  It puts
Poisson smoothing on~$\RR$, circle Fourier analysis, Cauchy-weighted
entropy integrals, and the auxiliary secant observation kernel into one
calculus.  This places the paper in the information-theoretic Cauchy setting
surveyed by Verd{\'u}~\cite{Verdu2023Cauchy}, but the object studied here is
not a parametric divergence between two Cauchy laws.  The Cauchy law is used
as the Haar/Poisson normalization in which the smoothed comparison
\(D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))\) has a finite-moment
large-time coefficient expansion.  Thus the explicit \(f\)-divergence
formulae for parametric Cauchy distributions of Nielsen--Okamura
\cite{NielsenOkamura2023Cauchy} are adjacent background for Cauchy
information geometry, while the present coefficients are smoothed-law
entropy jets for an arbitrary input law with finite moments.

\subsection*{Main results}

The contribution hierarchy is as follows.
\emph{Main result 1} is the finite-moment Poisson entropy expansion,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  \emph{Main result 2} is the
explicit \(N=4\) coefficient audit with a seventh-moment remainder ledger,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}.
The defect coordinates and the moment-defect \(W_2\) corollaries are
coefficient-level consequences collected in
Corollaries~\ref{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization}--%
\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification},
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}.  Appendix~\ref{app:finite-time-defect-readouts}
records finite-time readouts obtained by inserting the eighth-order remainder
into that coefficient estimate.  The auxiliary infrastructure is finite phase
sampling, the Doob--Bregman tail identity, the
secant RKHS, and the integer-orbit projection theorem.  These tools provide
coefficient audits, a dissipation-tail representation, and observation
coordinates; they are not the main novelty claims.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{The arbitrary finite-order finite-moment entropy expansion.}
For every $N\ge2$, if a probability law~$\nu$ has mean
$\bar\gamma$ and finite centred $(2N-2+\eta)$th absolute moment for some
\(0<\eta\le1\), then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N})
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\]
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  The coefficient \(A_{2N}\)
depends only on moments through order \(2N-2\), because every entropy monomial
has at least two density factors; the positive margin \(\eta\) controls the
omitted density modes after multiplication by the quadratic entropy scale.
Finite computable remainder and uniform moment-ball versions under the
convenient stronger \((2N+\eta)\)-moment hypothesis are recorded in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-moment-ball-entropy-remainder}.  The result is the
finite-moment passage from the universal Cayley--Chebyshev coefficient algebra
to an actual Poisson relative-entropy asymptotic expansion at the prescribed
order.  It is not a single asymptotic series under one fixed moment
hypothesis; increasing the retained order requires the corresponding finite
centred \((2N-2+\eta)\)-moment margin, while stronger moment balls are used
only for the uniform ledgers.

\item \emph{The explicit \(N=4\) coefficient audit with a seventh-moment ledger.}
The finite centred seventh absolute moment in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} is the \(N=4,\eta=1\) instance of the
general positive-margin theorem and also records the quantitative
\(O(t^{-9})\) ledger from
Corollary~\ref{cor:optimized-retained-order-entropy-bridge}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} audits the resulting coefficients
$(C_4,C_6,C_8^{(7)})$.  These audited coefficients then define the sixth- and
eighth-order defect coordinates.  The downstream consequences are the
non-negative defect ladder of
Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}, the strict eighth-order
amplification in Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}, and the
coefficient-defect coupling
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}.  The finite fourth-moment
polynomial is identified with the genuine entropy coefficient defect by
Lemma~\ref{lem:seventh-moment-bridge} before the \(W_2\) estimate is invoked.
\end{enumerate}

Three supporting tools keep the argument auditable.  Finite root-of-unity
sampling verifies the finite Fourier coefficient arithmetic below the explicit
aliasing barrier.  The KL Doob-tail section re-expresses the same Poisson
comparison quotient through the identity
\(D_{\mathrm{KL}}=\int_t^\infty I_\nu(s)\dd s\).  The secant Gram space and
strip RKHS appendix give the Hilbert-space model for integer-orbit observation
data, including the projection and law-side recovery criterion for that
channel.

\subsection*{Cayley coefficient mechanism}

The comparison kernel
\[
R_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
\]
measures the ratio between a translated Poisson profile and the reference
Cauchy density after the rescaling $x=\bar\gamma+ty$.  The Cayley angle
$y=\tan\theta$ converts the Taylor modes of $R_\varepsilon$ into finite
trigonometric expressions governed by Chebyshev polynomials of the second
kind.  Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode} records this mode formula.
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} then defines the formal
coefficient polynomials $A_k^{\rm form}$ by finite Cauchy-weighted mode
integrals and proves the odd-order cancellation.  At this stage the
calculation is algebraic: a law is evaluated only through the moment
interface in
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}.

The analytic content of the first main package is the finite-moment
density-to-entropy passage.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}
shows that, under a finite centred $(2N-2+\eta)$th absolute moment, the
evaluated even coefficients are the actual coefficients of the large-time
Poisson relative entropy through order~$t^{-2N}$.  The companion remainder
theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder}, records fixed-law
constants under the convenient stronger \((2N+\eta)\)-moment hypothesis,
while Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-moment-ball-entropy-remainder}
records the corresponding uniform statement on those centred moment balls.  The
compact-support analytic theorem
Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic-compact-support-interchange} records the
compact-support convergent-series version of the same coefficients.

Finite phase sampling gives an exactness check for this coefficient calculus.
The main entropy proof uses Laurent constant-coefficient extraction.
Appendix~\ref{sec:finite-phase-sampling} identifies the same circle characters
on root-of-unity grids and records the alias-free recovery formula below the
explicit degree barrier.  The finite-grid moment recovery theorem and the
eighth-order finite-phase verification check the normalized Laurent
constant terms after the analytic asymptotic framework has been established.

\subsection*{\texorpdfstring{Eighth-order defect and moment-defect \(W_2\) estimates}{Eighth-order defect and moment-defect W2 estimates}}

The second main package specializes the finite coefficient calculus to the
order-$t^{-8}$ layer under a finite centred seventh absolute moment.  The
analytic expansion and $O(t^{-9})$ remainder come from
Corollary~\ref{cor:optimized-retained-order-entropy-bridge} with
\(N=4,\eta=1\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} is the explicit
coefficient audit for this specialization.  The subsequent corollaries use
that audited jet.  Corollary~\ref{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization}
records that the displayed jet factors through centred moments through order
six.  From the sixth- and eighth-order coefficients one obtains non-negative
defect coordinates
(Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}) and strict eighth-order
amplification
(Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}).  The symmetric two-point
law at fixed variance is the equality case, as stated in
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-two-point-defect-rigidity}.

The \(W_2\) consequence is placed after the seventh-moment coefficient
identification.
With only a finite fourth moment the formula
$\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)$ is an algebraic polynomial and satisfies the coupling
estimate of Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:seventh-moment-bridge} uses the seventh-moment entropy
expansion to identify that polynomial with the genuine sixth-order entropy
defect~$\Delta_6$.  With this identification,
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} gives the moment-defect
$W_2$ estimate
\[
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/2}.
\]
Appendix~\ref{app:finite-time-defect-readouts} gives finite-time readouts by
displaying the $C_8^{(7)}t^{-2}$ correction and the fixed-law residual in the
renormalized sixth-order scale.  These readouts are consequences of the
coefficient estimate and the eighth-order remainder, not a separate entropy
stability theory.

\subsection*{Observation channel and strip RKHS}

The observation material gives Hilbert coordinates for a Poisson channel.  The
secant family
\[
\Psi_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{R_\varepsilon(y)-1}{\varepsilon}
\]
has Gram kernel
\[
K(a,b)=\frac{2}{4+(a-b)^2}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-gram-kernel} computes this kernel, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-space-rkhs} identifies the closed secant span with
$L^2_0(\omega)$ and its RKHS completion.  These results are the Hilbert
coordinate system for the appendix projection theorem.

The scalar centred observation functions in
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-central-two-channel} recover the centred law
from the central Poisson trace and its derivative by Laplace uniqueness and
characteristic-function inversion.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-channel-rkhs}
then places the centred fluctuation profile inside the secant RKHS and
identifies the observation values as evaluation functionals.
Appendix~\ref{sec:strip-lattice} transports this RKHS into the Poisson strip
space
$\sqrt{\pi}\,e^{-|D|}L^2(\RR)$ and records the integer-orbit projection
formula.  The resulting observation theorem is a projection theorem: integer
data recover exactly \(\Pi_{\ZZ}F_t\).  The same theorem translates full
channel recovery into a law-side lattice condition: for the observation time
\(t\), integer data recover \(F_t\) itself exactly when
\(q_{F_t}(\xi)=e^{2|\xi|}\widehat F_t(\xi)\) is \(2\pi\)-periodic,
equivalently when the centred law is supported on \(t\ZZ\).  When the full
profile \(q_{F_t}\) is available, a separate Fourier-inversion lemma recovers
the signed measure \(s\,\mu_t(\dd s)\), hence the scaled centred law
\(\mu_t\) away from zero and then its atom at zero by total mass.

\subsection*{Theorem-level comparison with adjacent work}

The references in this subsection are positioning references, not proof
inputs.  The detailed bibliographic table, with DOI and arXiv identifiers, is
Appendix~\ref{app:theorem-level-comparison}; the short comparison here records
only the theorem-level distinction.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*]
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} concerns one law observed
through the Cauchy/Poisson quotient
\(D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))\) under a finite centred
\((2N-2+\eta)\)-moment.  Bobkov--G{\"o}tze's Esscher CLT
\cite{BobkovGotzeEsscher2025}
(\nolinkurl{doi:10.1016/j.jfa.2025.110999},
\nolinkurl{arXiv:2407.20726}) and the Anker--Papageorgiou--Zhang heat-kernel
asymptotics \cite{AnkerPapageorgiouZhang2023HeatSymmetricSpaces}
(\nolinkurl{doi:10.1016/j.jfa.2022.109828},
\nolinkurl{arXiv:2112.01323}) treat
different asymptotic objects.  They do not yield the quotient identity
\(R_{\Delta/t}\), the Laurent extraction of \(A_{2m}(\nu)\), or the finite
moment threshold in Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.

\item Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} is the explicit \(N=4\),
\(\eta=1\) coefficient audit for an arbitrary smoothed law with finite
centred seventh absolute moment.  Verd{\'u}'s Cauchy information survey
\cite{Verdu2023Cauchy} (\nolinkurl{doi:10.3390/e25020346}) and
Nielsen--Okamura's Cauchy--Cauchy \(f\)-divergence formulae
\cite{NielsenOkamura2023Cauchy}
(\nolinkurl{doi:10.1109/TIT.2022.3231645},
\nolinkurl{arXiv:2101.12459}) give the relevant
Cauchy information-theory context, but they do not compute the
\(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6\) coefficient jet of \(P_t*\nu\) or the
\(O_\nu(t^{-9})\) seventh-moment ledger.

\item Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling} is a
coefficient-defect consequence: after the seventh-moment coefficient
identification fixes the sixth entropy coefficient, a fourth-moment algebraic
identity gives a \(W_2\) modulus to the symmetric two-point law.  This is not a finite-time
entropy stability theorem and is not compared as such with logarithmic
Sobolev/HLS stability or point-process transport results such as
Brigati--Dolbeault--Simonov
\cite{BrigatiDolbeaultSimonov2024LogSobolev}
(\nolinkurl{doi:10.1016/j.jfa.2024.110562},
\nolinkurl{arXiv:2303.12926}), Carlen \cite{Carlen2025LogHLS}
(\nolinkurl{doi:10.1016/j.jfa.2024.110818},
\nolinkurl{arXiv:2312.00614}), Gozlan--Herry--Peccati
\cite{GozlanHerryPeccati2021PointMeasureTransport}
(\nolinkurl{doi:10.1016/j.jfa.2021.109141},
\nolinkurl{arXiv:2002.04923}), L{\'o}pez-Rivera--Shenfeld
\cite{LopezRiveraShenfeld2025PoissonTransport}
(\nolinkurl{doi:10.1016/j.jfa.2025.110864},
\nolinkurl{arXiv:2407.02359}), and
Erbar--Huesmann--Jalowy--M{\"u}ller
\cite{ErbarHuesmannJalowyMuller2025StationaryPointTransport}
(\nolinkurl{doi:10.1016/j.jfa.2025.110974},
\nolinkurl{arXiv:2304.11145}).
Those JFA papers prove finite-time functional-inequality stability,
logarithmic-HLS stability, or transport stability statements for their own
functionals and metric structures.  The present corollary proves only a
coefficient-level moment-defect readout after the Poisson entropy expansion
has identified the relevant coefficient.

\item Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-observation-lattice-sampling} is the
Poisson-channel sampling statement for the secant kernel
\(K(a,b)=2/(4+(a-b)^2)\).  General RKHS sampling results such as
Nashed--Walter \cite{NashedWalter1991}
(\nolinkurl{doi:10.1007/BF02570568}) and Gaussian shift-invariant sampling
results such as Romero--Ulanovskii--Zlotnikov
\cite{RomeroUlanovskiiZlotnikov2024}
(\nolinkurl{doi:10.1016/j.jfa.2024.110600},
\nolinkurl{arXiv:2306.13619}) do not imply the hyperbolic symbol, the
integer-orbit projection, or the law-side \(t\ZZ\)-support criterion.
\end{enumerate}
