\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification $\overline\RR$ of the line with
the circle~$\TT$.  Under this identification normalized Haar measure pulls
back to the Cauchy law
\[
\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This normalization is the common coordinate system of the paper.  It puts
Poisson smoothing on~$\RR$, circle Fourier analysis, Cauchy-weighted
entropy integrals, and the secant kernels of the strip RKHS into one
calculus.

\subsection*{Main contributions}

The paper has three main theorem packages.  All other named results are used
as normalization, coefficient algebra, domain justification, or standard
functional-analytic infrastructure for these packages.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Finite-moment Poisson entropy expansion.}
For every $N\ge2$ and $0<\eta\le1$, if a probability law~$\nu$ has mean
$\bar\gamma$ and finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment, then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N})
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\]
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}, with explicit remainder
and uniform moment-ball versions in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-moment-ball-entropy-remainder}.  Its novelty is
the finite-moment passage from the Cayley--Chebyshev coefficient algebra to
an actual Poisson relative-entropy asymptotic expansion, with explicit
fixed-law and moment-ball remainder constants.

\item \emph{Seventh-moment eighth-order defect and stability.}
Under a finite centred seventh absolute moment, the order-$t^{-8}$ entropy
jet is computed directly in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}, including the coefficient jet
$(C_4,C_6,C_8)$ and a quantitative $O(t^{-9})$ remainder.  This theorem is
then used to define sixth- and eighth-order defect coordinates.  The
downstream consequences are the non-negative defect ladder of
Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}, the strict eighth-order
amplification in Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}, the
coefficient-level $W_2$ stability consequence
Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-stability}, and the finite-time translation
in Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-finite-time-defect-stability}.  The finite
fourth-moment polynomial is identified with the genuine entropy coefficient
defect by Lemma~\ref{lem:seventh-moment-bridge} before the stability
corollaries are invoked.

\item \emph{Poisson strip observation projection.}
The secant profiles generated by the same Cayley quotient form a Poisson
strip RKHS.  The observation theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-observation-lattice-sampling}, states that integer
samples of the Poisson observation profile reconstruct exactly the
minimal-norm lattice projection in the integer-orbit subspace and reconstruct
the full channel precisely when the channel already belongs to that lattice
subspace.  The Poisson-specific ingredients are the strip realization of
Proposition~\ref{prop:gram-space-poisson-image}, the explicit integer-orbit
symbol of Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-lattice-symbol}, and the cardinal
kernel and norm formula of
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-cardinal-reconstruction}.  The abstract passage
from a positive principal symbol to Riesz bounds and cardinal interpolation
is imported classical shift-invariant/RKHS sampling theory, not a new
theorem of the paper.
\end{enumerate}

This hierarchy is part of the statement of the article.  The finite phase
sampling formulas, the Cayley--Chebyshev mode formulas, the Doob
$\Phi$-entropy identity, the higher-secant germs, the Hardy splitting, and the
principal shift-invariant criterion are included because they make the three
packages explicit and checkable in a single normalization.  They are not
separate claims of comparable novelty.

\subsection*{Cayley coefficient mechanism}

The comparison kernel
\[
R_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
\]
measures the ratio between a translated Poisson profile and the reference
Cauchy density after the rescaling $x=\bar\gamma+ty$.  The Cayley angle
$y=\tan\theta$ converts the Taylor modes of $R_\varepsilon$ into finite
trigonometric expressions governed by Chebyshev polynomials of the second
kind.  Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode} records this mode formula.
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} then defines the formal
coefficient polynomials $A_k^{\rm form}$ by finite Cauchy-weighted mode
integrals and proves the odd-order cancellation.  At this stage the
calculation is algebraic: a law is evaluated only through the moment
interface in
Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-entropy-coefficient-evaluation}.

The analytic content of the first main package is the finite-moment
density-to-entropy passage.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}
shows that, under a finite centred $(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment, the
evaluated even coefficients are the actual coefficients of the large-time
Poisson relative entropy through order~$t^{-2N}$.  The companion remainder
theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-moment-entropy-remainder}, records fixed-law
constants, while Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform-moment-ball-entropy-remainder}
records the corresponding uniform statement on centred moment balls.  The
compact-support analytic theorem
Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic-compact-support-interchange} records the
compact-support convergent-series version of the same coefficients.

Finite phase sampling is an auxiliary exactness check for this coefficient
calculus.  Proposition~\ref{prop:phase-coordinate-identification} identifies
the finite grids with the same circle characters used by the Cayley chart,
and Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-grid-coefficient-recovery} gives the
root-of-unity recovery and aliasing formula below the explicit degree
barrier.  The finite-grid moment recovery theorem and the eighth-order
finite-phase verification are used only after the analytic asymptotic
framework has been established.

\subsection*{Eighth-order defect and stability}

The second main package specializes the finite coefficient calculus to the
order-$t^{-8}$ jet under a finite centred seventh absolute moment.
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth} is the central result: it gives the
displayed coefficient jet and the $O(t^{-9})$ remainder.  The subsequent
corollaries use that jet.  Corollary~\ref{cor:eighth-order-jet-factorization}
records that the printed jet factors through centred moments through order
six.  From the sixth- and eighth-order coefficients one obtains non-negative
defect coordinates
(Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-ladder}) and strict eighth-order
amplification
(Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-amplification}).  The symmetric two-point
law at fixed variance is the equality case, as stated in
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-two-point-defect-rigidity}.

The stability consequence is deliberately placed after the seventh-moment bridge.
With only a finite fourth moment the formula
$\Delta_6^{(4)}(\nu)$ is an algebraic polynomial and satisfies the coupling
estimate of Lemma~\ref{lem:fourth-moment-algebraic-coupling}.  It is not yet
an entropy coefficient.  Lemma~\ref{lem:seventh-moment-bridge} uses the
seventh-moment entropy expansion to identify that polynomial with the genuine
sixth-order entropy defect~$\Delta_6$.  Only then does
Corollary~\ref{thm:poisson-defect-stability} give the coefficient-level
$W_2$ control
\[
W_2(\nu_c,\rho_\sigma)
\le
4\,\sigma^{-1/2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/4}
+8\,\sigma^{-2}\Delta_6(\nu)^{1/2}.
\]
Corollary~\ref{cor:poisson-finite-time-defect-stability} converts the
coefficient estimate into a large-time statement by displaying the
$C_8t^{-2}$ correction and the fixed-law residual in the renormalized
sixth-order scale.

\subsection*{Observation channel and strip RKHS}

The third main package is Hilbert-space and observational rather than
entropy-asymptotic.  The secant family
\[
\Psi_\varepsilon(y):=\frac{R_\varepsilon(y)-1}{\varepsilon}
\]
has Gram kernel
\[
K(a,b)=\frac{2}{4+(a-b)^2}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-gram-kernel} computes this kernel, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:mode-space-rkhs} identifies the closed secant span with
$L^2_0(\omega)$ and its RKHS completion.  These results are the Hilbert
coordinate system for the observation theorem.  The higher-secant kernels in
Theorem~\ref{thm:higher-secant-kernels} are subordinate to the entropy
coefficient calculus: they repackage the finite Cauchy-weighted mode
integrals and do not form an additional inverse problem.

The scalar centred observation functions in
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-central-two-channel} recover the centred law
from the central Poisson trace and its derivative by Laplace uniqueness and
characteristic-function inversion.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-channel-rkhs}
then places the centred fluctuation profile inside the secant RKHS and
identifies the observation values as evaluation functionals.
Section~\ref{sec:strip-lattice} transports this RKHS into the Poisson strip
space
$\sqrt{\pi}\,e^{-|D|}L^2(\RR)$, computes the integer-orbit symbol, and applies
the classical principal shift-invariant criterion to the unshifted integer
orbit.  The resulting observation theorem is a projection theorem:
integer data recover the lattice projection and the quotient class; they do
not recover the orthogonal invisible component unless the channel is already
in the lattice subspace.

\subsection*{Relation to prior work}

The analytic background is classical: Poisson semigroups and Fourier
representation
\cite{Folland1995,Grafakos2014,Stein1971}, Laplace transforms
\cite{Widder1941}, characteristic functions and inversion
\cite{Lukacs1970,Feller1971}, reproducing kernels \cite{Aronszajn1950},
and Hardy--Poisson boundary theory \cite{Duren1970,Rudin1987}.  The
Haar-pullback normalization and entropy change-of-variables identities below
only fix the normalized Cayley chart used throughout.

The entropy expansion is adjacent to, but distinct from, the entropic CLT,
stable-law de Bruijn, and entropy-production literatures
\cite{Barron1986,JohnsonBarron2004,ArtsteinBallBartheNaor2004,CarlenCarvalho1992,BobkovChistyakovGotzeEdgeworth2013,BobkovChistyakovGotzeBerryEsseen2014,BobkovChistyakovGotze2013,Toscani2015,Toscani2016,JohnsonStableDeBruijn2013}.
Recent stability results for logarithmic HLS and logarithmic Sobolev deficits
\cite{Carlen2025LogHLS,BrigatiDolbeaultSimonov2024LogSobolev} give the
nearest functional-inequality comparison: they control distance from
extremizers by entropy deficits.  The present stability consequence is narrower
and coefficient-level.  It concerns finite-moment Poisson-shift asymptotic
defects, with universal algebraic constants at coefficient level and
fixed-law finite-time constants unless an explicit seventh-moment shell is
imposed.  Recent Cauchy information work
\cite{Verdu2023Cauchy,NielsenOkamura2023Cauchy} is relevant because the
Cauchy law supplies the Cayley pullback and Poisson comparison kernel, not
because the paper studies divergence geometry inside the Cauchy family.

The lattice analysis imports the principal shift-invariant/RKHS sampling
criterion needed for the unshifted integer orbit.  Riesz translates and
principal generators are standard
\cite{deBoorDeVoreRon1994,RonShen1995,Bownik2000}; nonuniform and stable
sampling are treated in \cite{AldroubiGrochenig2001}.  RKHS, localized,
derivative, and Galerkin sampling frameworks appear in
\cite{NashedWalter1991,NashedSun2010,ChengJiangSun2016,GrochenigRomeroStockler2020,FuhrGrochenigHaimiKlotzRomero2018}.
Explicit Gaussian and radial-basis cardinal constructions
\cite{BaranovBelovGrochenig2022,RomeroUlanovskiiZlotnikov2024,KiselevMininNovikov2016,KiselevMininNovikov2019,Buhmann2003}
provide nearby examples.  What is Poisson-specific here is the strip
realization of the secant mode space, the closed hyperbolic lattice symbol,
the cardinal kernel and norm formula, and the integer-orbit projection
statement for the observation profiles above.

\subsection*{Organization}

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation and conventions.
Subsection~\ref{sec:cayley-gate} records the Cayley formulas, and
Subsection~\ref{sec:haar-pullback} records the Haar-pullback normalization.
Section~\ref{sec:finite-phase-sampling} records the finite
root-of-unity sampling facts used by the Cayley Fourier coefficients.
Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} proves the first two main packages:
the finite-moment entropy expansion and the seventh-moment eighth-order
defect/stability theory.  Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy} gives the
Doob--Bregman dissipation interpretation of the same Poisson comparison
quotient.  Section~\ref{sec:gram-space} constructs the secant Gram space,
records the subordinate higher-secant coefficient packaging, and proves the
centred Poisson reconstruction statement.  Section~\ref{sec:strip-lattice}
identifies the strip RKHS and proves the integer-orbit observation projection
theorem, using the imported principal-symbol criterion recalled in
Appendix~\ref{app:background-symbol-criterion}.  The remaining appendix
records the weighted trigonometric integrals and coefficient arithmetic used
in the eighth-order expansion.
