\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification of \(\RR\) with \(\TT\), and pulls
normalized Haar measure back to the Cauchy law
\[
\omega(\dd x)=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This is the endpoint-normalized form of the classical Cauchy/wrapped-Cauchy
M{\"o}bius correspondence in circular statistics
\cite{KentTyler1988WrappedCauchy,McCullagh1992CauchyModels,
McCullagh1996CauchyMobius,
KatoJones2010MobiusCircle,KatoMcCullagh2020SphereCauchyMobius}.
This normalization makes the fixed-law Poisson comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main-functional}
D_{\rm KL}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
\end{equation}
the object whose large-\(t\) behaviour is studied in Cayley--Chebyshev
modes.  The input law \(\nu\) is fixed, \(\bar\gamma\) is its mean, and
\(t\to\infty\) is the Poisson scale.

The basic calculation is a quotient calculation.  Let
\[
X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma
\]
be the centred translation variable.  With \(x=\bar\gamma+ty\), translation
by \(X_c\) is encoded by
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\qquad \varepsilon=X_c/t .
\]
The Cayley angle \(y=\tan\theta\) turns the Taylor coefficients of
\(R_\varepsilon\) into finite trigonometric, equivalently Laurent, modes.
After the matched Cauchy profile has been divided out, the quotient expansion
is therefore reduced to finite mode algebra; the KL expansion is then the
ordinary Taylor expansion of \((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) in
\(L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)\).

\subsection*{Main contribution and mechanism}

The new part has four pieces.  First, finite centred \((2N-2)\)th absolute
moment gives the one-dimensional coefficient expansion through order
\(t^{-2N}\), with each coefficient \(A_{2m}(\nu)\) computed by Laurent
constant-term extraction from the Cayley modes.  Second, the leading
coefficient has a sharp analytic threshold: finite centred variance is
equivalent to a finite \(t^{-4}\) matched-Poisson KL coefficient, and the
value is \(\mu_2^2/8\).  Third, in \(\RR^d\) the multidimensional statement
is split into three layers: an abstract quotient hypothesis gives the tensor
entropy jet, a finite centred \(L\)-moment condition supplies that hypothesis,
and the degree-two covariance contraction gives the explicit quadratic form
\(Q_d(\Sigma)\).  Fourth,
under finite centred second moment, the same matched quotient satisfies an
exact Cauchy-generator Bregman dissipation identity whose tail integral
recovers the KL value.

The one-dimensional coefficient theorem, sharp threshold, multidimensional
jet, and dissipation identity are the main formal statements in the body.
The finite-moment quotient remainder, the displayed symmetric power-tail
examples, and the multidimensional moment jet are stated separately there so
that the analytic input and the algebraic entropy transfer remain visible.

\subsection*{Results and proof architecture}

The theorem statements occur once, in the sections where their hypotheses are
proved or used.  Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} begins with the
Cayley mode calculation:
Theorem~\ref{thm:cayley-chebyshev-mode} gives the modes \(u_n\),
Theorem~\ref{thm:universal-entropy-coefficients} defines the universal
coefficient polynomials by Cauchy-weighted mode integrals, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-fourier-mode-integral-algorithm} evaluates those
integrals by a single Laurent constant-term rule.  The finite-moment
quotient remainder and the finite-jet entropy transfer then give the
one-dimensional expansion in Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.
For \(N\ge3\), its finite \((2N-2)\)-moment assumption is the finite data
needed to evaluate the retained universal polynomial.  The leading finite
value is \(A_4(\nu)=\mu_2^2/8\).

The power-tail replacements are intentionally narrower.  The exact
perturbative theorem is
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-perturbative-power-tail-log-replacement}: for
\(N\ge3\), \(p=2N-2\), and an even density
\[
f(x)=g(x)+\{c|x|^{-p-1}+r(x)\}\mathbf1_{\{|x|\ge R\}},
\]
where \(g\in L^1(\RR)\) is symmetric and nonnegative with
\(\operatorname{supp}g\subset(-R,R)\), and
\[
\int |x|^q|r(x)|\,\dd x<\infty\ (q<p),\qquad
\int_{R\le |x|\le t}|x|^pr(x)\,\dd x=O(1),
\]
\[
t^{-(p+1)}\int_{R\le |x|\le t}|x|^{p+1}|r(x)|\,\dd x=O(t^{-p}),
\qquad
\int_{|x|>t}\left(1+\frac{x^2}{t^2}\right)|r(x)|\,\dd x=O(t^{-p}),
\]
one has
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\|P_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N-1}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
+2c\,\kappa_N\mu_2\,t^{-2N}\log t
+o(t^{-2N}\log t).
\]
No regularly varying theorem is claimed without the quotient controls above.
Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics} separates this tail discussion into
four blocks: the conditional quotient-transfer proposition
Proposition~\ref{thm:tail-replacements-proved-here}, the exact power-tail theorem
Theorem~\ref{prop:log-tail-counterexample-top-moment}, the perturbative
power-tail theorem
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-perturbative-power-tail-log-replacement}, and the
monotone slowly varying corollary
Corollary~\ref{cor:conditional-slowly-varying-borderline-tail}.  Each
result repeats the quotient expansion or quotient controls used in its
entropy transfer; the Potter--Karamata estimates for the slowly varying
corollary are proved separately before the corollary.

The multidimensional part is separated into algebra and analytic input.
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-poisson-entropy-jets} transfers an
abstract quotient jet \((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\) to tensor KL coefficients
\(\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:multidimensional-density-quotient} proves that finite centred
\(L\)-absolute moment, with integer \(L\ge d+1\), is one sufficient condition
for that quotient jet.  Proposition
~\ref{prop:multidim-formal-covariance-layer} computes the finite-covariance
quadratic layer and its isotropic/traceless contraction.

Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy} proves the Cauchy-generator tail identity.
The domain point is isolated there: on each compact time window
\([a,b]\subset(0,\infty)\), finite centred variance gives two-sided quotient
bounds, a Lipschitz bound, and an explicit integrable Bregman majorant for
the moving quotient \(u_t=(P_t*\nu)/P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\).  With that
domain theorem in place, Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail} follows from
\(H'(t)=-\mathcal I_\nu(t)\), nonnegativity of the dissipation, and
\(H(T)\to0\).

The order-eight calculation is stated once in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-eighth}.  Its Laurent certificate gives
\[
A_6(\nu)=\frac{\sigma^6+6\mu_3^2-8\sigma^2\mu_4}{64},
\qquad
A_8(\nu)=
\frac{3\sigma^8-12\sigma^4\mu_4+9\sigma^2\mu_3^2
+12\sigma^2\mu_6-30\mu_3\mu_5+20\mu_4^2}{256}.
\]
The associated sixth-order defect \(\Delta_6\) is used only in
Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}, where a non-sharp
one-line moment coupling bounds the distance to the symmetric two-point law
in \(W_2\).

\subsection*{Scope and limitations}

The KL functional used throughout is the extended nonnegative
Kullback--Leibler functional of \eqref{eq:extended-kl-definition}
\cite{KullbackLeibler1951,CoverThomas2006}.  Under finite centred second
moment in dimension one the matched-Poisson KL value is finite for every
\(t>0\); without that hypothesis the sharp-threshold theorem is interpreted
in \([0,\infty]\), so the conclusion
\(t^4D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))\to+\infty\) includes the
case where the KL value is already \(+\infty\) at some large times.  The
finite-moment hierarchy identifies universal polynomial coefficients only
when the needed centred moments exist.  For the exact and perturbative
symmetric power-tail density classes in
Theorems~\ref{prop:log-tail-counterexample-top-moment} and
\ref{thm:exact-perturbative-power-tail-log-replacement}, the missing top
finite moment is replaced by the truncated top moment \(M_p(t)\), giving the
stated logarithmic layers.  No assertion is made for arbitrary regularly
varying tails.  No regularly varying theorem is claimed without the quotient
controls above.  The only slowly varying add-on is
Corollary~\ref{cor:conditional-slowly-varying-borderline-tail}, restricted to
eventually monotone borderline densities; there
Lemma~\ref{lem:slowly-varying-quotient-controls} proves the Potter--Karamata
quotient controls from slow variation and \(M_p(t)\to\infty\).  In several
dimensions, the finite-covariance
proposition is the quadratic coefficient calculation, whereas the KL
asymptotic requires the separate quotient-jet hypothesis
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})\), for which finite centred \(L\)-moment with integer
\(L\ge d+1\) is a sufficient condition.  Corollary~\ref{cor:coefficient-defect-coupling}
is a finite-moment consequence of the coefficient algebra, not an
optimizer-stability theorem for a sharp functional inequality and not a
claim that a small matched-Poisson KL value alone controls \(W_2\).  The
dissipation identity is proved in Section~\ref{sec:doob-phi-entropy} by the
Cauchy cutoff Green formula.  That section also records that pointwise
dissipation asymptotics need additional differentiated-remainder input; the
coefficient theorems use only tail integrals and fixed-window averages.

\subsection*{Organization}

and~\ref{sec:haar-pullback} record the Cayley and Haar-pullback

coefficient expansion.
