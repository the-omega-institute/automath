\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Let \((p_t)_{t>0}\) be the symmetric \(\alpha\)-stable heat kernel on
\(\RR^d\), normalized by
\[
 \widehat p_t(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^\alpha},\qquad 0<\alpha<2.
\]
For probability measures \(\mu\) and \(\nu\), consider the two solutions
\[
 f_t=p_t*\mu,\qquad g_t=p_t*\nu.
\]
The first problem of this paper is to justify, for measure-valued initial
data, the formal entropy calculation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-formal-entropy-derivative}
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}D_{\rm KL}(f_t\|g_t)
 =-\frac{c_{d,\alpha}}2
 \iint_{\RR^d\times\RR^d}
 \Lambda\!\left(
  \frac{f_t(x)}{g_t(x)},\frac{f_t(y)}{g_t(y)}
 \right)
 \frac{g_t(x)}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}\dd y\dd x,
\end{equation}
where
\[
 \Lambda(a,b)=a\log(a/b)-a+b\ge0.
\]
The same identity for smooth compactly supported data follows from the
symmetry of the fractional generator and the two-solution Bregman algebra.
Two analytic obstructions arise for the present continuum problem.  First,
the jump kernel \(|x-y|^{-d-\alpha}\) is singular on the diagonal, so the
logarithmic Bregman form must be shown finite before it can represent the
entropy derivative.  Second, the integrations by parts occur on all of
\(\RR^d\).  The two resulting Green pairings have different weights and
cannot be closed by writing a single formally vanishing boundary term.

We resolve both points by combining compact-time quotient estimates with an
annular truncation of the fractional generator.  Stable density bounds imply
that, on each window \(t\in[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)\), the quotient
\(f_t/g_t\) is bounded above and below and its logarithm is globally
Lipschitz.  This controls the diagonal singularity.  The annular argument
then closes the two Green pairings separately in \(L^1\).  Finally, a
transport estimate gives decay of the entropy at the infinite-time endpoint,
turning the local derivative identity into a tail formula.

\begin{unnumberedtheorem}[Stable-flow dissipation and endpoint representation]
Assume that \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) have finite \((d+\alpha)\)-th absolute
moments.  Then \(D_{\rm KL}(f_t\|g_t)\) is finite for every \(t>0\), locally
absolutely continuous, and its derivative is given almost everywhere by
\eqref{eq:intro-formal-entropy-derivative}.  The dissipation is finite for
every positive time and locally integrable, and
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(f_t\|g_t)=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\alpha(f_q\|g_q)\dd q.
\]
If the initial law \(\mu\) has finite \((d+\alpha)\)-th absolute moment, a
moving stable reference \(p_{\mathord\cdot+s_0}\) yields the corresponding
dissipation identity and tail representation in every dimension.  In dimension
one, if \(\mu\) has a density and \(D_{\rm KL}(\mu\|p_{s_0})<\infty\), the latter
becomes an exact Bregman representation along Johnson's symmetric-stable
interpolation.
\end{unnumberedtheorem}

If instead \(D_{\rm KL}(\mu\|\nu)<\infty\), the local absolute continuity and
dissipation identity hold without any moment assumption.  If the two measures
also have finite first moments, the endpoint representation holds as well.
The full statements are Theorems
\ref{thm:two-stable-heat-flow-relative-entropy} and
\ref{thm:stable-flow-finite-entropy-domain}.  They separate the
compact-quotient domain, which gives pointwise finiteness and endpoint decay,
from the finite-entropy domain, which closes the nonnegative jump form by
convex truncation.

\subsection*{Large-scale stable smoothing}

The second problem concerns the amount of moment information required to
turn a density expansion into a relative-entropy expansion.  The relevant
quantity is the polynomial tail exponent of the smoothing density.  We first
use the stable spatial scale
\[
 p_s^{(\alpha,d)}(x)=s^{-d}p_1^{(\alpha,d)}(x/s).
\]
Let \(\nu\) and \(\eta\) have matching tensor moments below an integer order
\(r\ge1\), and write
\[
 \Delta_r=\int x^{\otimes r}(\nu-\eta)(\dd x)
 \in\operatorname{Sym}^r(\RR^d).
\]
The normalized translate quotient
\[
 F_y(z)=\frac{p_1^{(\alpha,d)}(y-z)}{p_1^{(\alpha,d)}(y)}
\]
has bounded Taylor jets but grows in the critical \(L^q\) space when
\(|z|\) is large.  Balancing that growth with the quadratic order of relative
entropy gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-critical-moment-exponent}
 q_{r,\alpha,d}=\min\left\{2,1+\frac{2r}{d+\alpha}\right\},
 \qquad
 p_{r,\alpha,d}=\frac{2r}{q_{r,\alpha,d}}
 =\max\left\{r,\frac{2r(d+\alpha)}{d+\alpha+2r}\right\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{unnumberedtheorem}[Optimal first-unmatched-moment asymptotics]
Suppose \(\Delta_r\ne0\) and both laws have finite
\(p_{r,\alpha,d}\)-th absolute moments.  Then
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\nu\|p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\eta)
 =\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)s^{-2r}+o(s^{-2r}),
\]
where \(\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}\) is an orthogonally invariant
positive-definite quadratic form on symmetric order-\(r\) tensors.  The
endpoint in \eqref{eq:intro-critical-moment-exponent} is sufficient.  If
\(d+\alpha>2r\), every exponent
\(\rho\in[r,p_{r,\alpha,d})\) fails uniformly, even among pairs for which
\(\Delta_r\) is finite and nonzero.  If \(d+\alpha\le2r\), the threshold is
\(r\), while smaller moment assumptions do not define \(\Delta_r\) on the
whole moment class.
\end{unnumberedtheorem}

The order-two instance is
Theorem~\ref{thm:high-dimensional-kl-moment-threshold}; the arbitrary-order
statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:all-order-stable-first-unmatched-moment}.
Their endpoint proof rests on the critical translate-remainder estimate in
Lemma~\ref{lem:all-order-stable-critical-remainder} and a two-background
Bregman transfer that needs only \(L^1\), rather than uniform, convergence of
the moving denominator.  Sharpness is established by finite-difference
moment blocks placed on recursively separated radii.  At the smoothing scale
attached to one block, a moving ball detects probability of order equal to
the block weight under one law and only a fixed smaller fraction under the
other.  Data processing converts that separation into the required entropy
lower bound.

The stable theorem is a specialization of a sharp polynomial-tail principle.
For \(\beta>d\), set
\[
 \kappa_{r,\beta}=\max\left\{r,\frac{2r\beta}{\beta+2r}\right\},
 \qquad m=\lfloor\kappa_{r,\beta}\rfloor.
\]
If \(p\) is a strictly positive \(C^{m+1}\) density satisfying
\[
 p(y)\asymp(1+|y|)^{-\beta},\qquad
 \max_{1\le|\gamma|\le m+1}
 \left\|\frac{\partial^\gamma p}{p}\right\|_\infty<\infty,
\]
then this \(\kappa_{r,\beta}\) is the optimal exponent.
The endpoint is sufficient, and every exponent in
\([r,\kappa_{r,\beta})\) fails uniformly when \(\beta>2r\).  This gives, in
particular, the first sharp moment theorem for relative-entropy asymptotics
under multivariate Student smoothing.  The full result is
Theorem~\ref{thm:polynomial-tail-optimal-moment-exponent}.

The same translate estimates reveal a second phase for superlinear
information divergences.  For the power divergence \(D_\theta\) of order
\(1<\theta\le2\), Theorem~\ref{thm:stable-power-divergence-moment-phase}
identifies the optimal exponent
\[
 p_{r,\alpha,d}^{(\theta)}
 =\max\left\{p_{r,\alpha,d},
 \frac{(d+\alpha)(\theta-1)}\theta\right\}.
\]
The leading quadratic form remains
\(\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}\), because the generators are normalized by
\(\phi_\theta''(1)=1\).  The additional term is not a proof artifact:
below it the power divergence can be infinite at every smoothing scale,
while between it and the KL-type barrier the divergence is finite but the
scaled asymptotic fails uniformly.  At \(\theta=2\), this gives the sharp
stable-kernel moment exponent
\(\max\{r,(d+\alpha)/2\}\) for the Pearson chi-square divergence.

\subsection*{Positive tail jets}

The exponent is optimal over moment classes, but it is not a law-by-law
criterion.  Under finite \(r\)-th moments alone, the Taylor jet
cannot absorb all mass at distances comparable with or larger than \(s\).
Rather than discard that mass, we retain it in a positive proxy.  Each proxy
contains the common Taylor jet below order \(r\), together with the original
law's unexpanded translate mixture outside a fixed multiple of the smoothing
scale.  Its small mass correction makes it a probability density.  The
bounded normalized stable jets make the core uniformly positive, while the
retained mixture is nonnegative.

For these proxies \(A_s^\nu,A_s^\eta\), the exact asymptotic transaction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-positive-tail-jet-preview}
 D_{\rm KL}(p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\nu\|p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\eta)
 =D_{\rm KL}(A_s^\nu\|A_s^\eta)
  +\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)s^{-2r}+o(s^{-2r}).
\end{equation}
The first term is nonnegative and retains the nonlinear interaction of the
two tails.  Consequently, the universal first-unmatched coefficient is
attained precisely when the rescaled proxy entropy vanishes.  This
complements the moment-class theorem: the latter gives a checkable uniform
hypothesis, whereas \eqref{eq:intro-positive-tail-jet-preview} identifies the
remaining obstruction for each fixed pair of laws.

Corollary~\ref{thm:stable-arbitrary-order-law-by-law-decomposition} proves
\eqref{eq:intro-positive-tail-jet-preview}.  Its mechanism is not specific to
regularly varying kernels.  Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-positive-tail-jet-kernel}
requires bounded normalized derivatives through order \(r\), bounded score,
and translation continuity of the top normalized derivative.  Under these
conditions the local tail-jet remainder is small simultaneously in
\(L^\infty\) and \(L^1\), translate entropy grows at most linearly, and both
proxies have finite relative entropy.  Stable kernels, the Poisson kernel,
and multivariate Student kernels satisfy the criterion.  It is also preserved
by invertible affine maps, finite products, and finite positive mixtures.

Hard and smooth fixed-scale retention, and additive and multiplicative mass
normalization, give defects that differ by \(o(s^{-2r})\).  The precise
robustness statement is Proposition~\ref{prop:stable-proxy-convention-robustness};
its proof is included in the appendix.  No classification of all positive
proxy conventions is intended.

\subsection*{Relation to prior work and scope}

The two-solution Bregman algebra behind
\eqref{eq:intro-formal-entropy-derivative} is classical.  Nonlocal
Hardy--Stein identities and entropy balances for jump processes provide the
closest general framework
\cite{BanuelosBogdanLuks2016HardyStein,Gutowski2023HardySteinPureJump,
KlimsiakRozkosz2025ConditionalHardyStein}.  The contribution of
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-stable-heat-flow-relative-entropy} is the
stable-continuum measure-data domain, the separate closure of the noncompact
Green pairings, and the endpoint identity.  Stable entropy methods and the
symmetric-stable interpolation motivate the moving-reference specialization
\cite{BobkovChistyakovGotze2013StableEntropy,Johnson2013StableDeBruijn,
Toscani2016StableEntropy}.

First-unmatched-moment asymptotics under Gaussian smoothing are prior work.
Chen and Niles-Weed also obtained the Gaussian chi-square asymptotic under
exponential-square tail assumptions
\cite[Theorem~2.5]{ChenNilesWeed2022SmoothedWasserstein}.
All-order density expansions for fractional diffusion are also known
\cite{IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2017,
IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2022RefinedFractional}.  Our asymptotic contribution is
the polynomial-tail critical exponent, its endpoint, uniform sharpness, and
the positive law-by-law proxy decomposition.  The sharp theorem covers both
stable and Student smoothing, as well as every kernel satisfying its explicit
tail and derivative hypotheses.  The abstract proxy theorem covers the wider
bounded-score additive class; it does not cover Gaussian smoothing, whose
normalized derivatives are unbounded.

A separate article treats the Cauchy kernel through a Cayley--Laurent
coefficient calculus.  It includes Gaussian quadrature and a Poisson
observation-channel RKHS; none of those results is used here.
