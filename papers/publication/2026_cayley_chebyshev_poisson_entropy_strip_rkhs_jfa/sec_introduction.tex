\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The Cayley transform
\[
\iota(x)=\frac{1+\ii x}{1-\ii x}
\]
identifies the one-point compactification of \(\RR\) with \(\TT\), and pulls
normalized Haar measure back to the Cauchy law
\[
\omega(\dd x)=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x=P_1(x)\dd x .
\]
This is the endpoint-normalized form of the classical Cauchy/wrapped-Cauchy
M{\"o}bius correspondence in circular statistics
\cite{KentTyler1988WrappedCauchy,McCullagh1992CauchyModels,
McCullagh1996CauchyMobius,
KatoJones2010MobiusCircle,KatoMcCullagh2020SphereCauchyMobius}.
This normalization makes the fixed-law Poisson comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-main-functional}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\bigr)
\end{equation}
the object whose large-\(t\) behaviour is studied in Cayley--Chebyshev
modes.  The input law \(\nu\) is fixed, \(\bar{\gamma}\) is its mean, and
\(t\to\infty\) is the Poisson scale.

The basic calculation is a quotient calculation.  Let
\[
X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}
\]
be the centred translation variable.  With \(x=\bar{\gamma}+ty\), translation
by \(X_c\) is encoded by
\[
R_\varepsilon(y)=\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2},
\qquad \varepsilon=X_c/t .
\]
The Cayley angle \(y=\tan\theta\) turns the Taylor coefficients of
\(R_\varepsilon\) into finite trigonometric, equivalently Laurent, modes.
After the matched Cauchy profile has been divided out, the quotient expansion
is therefore reduced to finite mode algebra; the KL expansion is then the
ordinary Taylor expansion of \((1+u)\log(1+u)-u\) in
\(L^\infty\cap L^1(\omega)\).

\subsection*{Contribution}

The principal unconditional result is
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-leading-kl-variance-threshold}.  For every law with
finite mean and without a tail-class assumption, finite centred variance is
equivalent to bounded \(t^4D_{\rm KL}\); the limit is \(\mu_2^2/8\) in the
finite-variance case and \(+\infty\) otherwise.  The infinite-variance proof
pairs the quotient with the bounded second Cayley mode and therefore does not
use a formal Taylor expansion.

The other principal analytic result is the regularly varying boundary theorem,
Theorem~\ref{cor:main-body-conditional-missing-top-moment-tail}.  For \(N\ge3\),
a centred law with two-sided regularly varying tails of index \(p=2N-2\) and
divergent integrated slowly varying factor
\(\ell_L(t)=\int^tL(s)\dd s/s\) satisfies
\[
t^{2N}\left\{H(t)-\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}A_{2j}(\nu)t^{-2j}\right\}
\sim p(c_++c_-)\kappa_N\mu_2\ell_L(t),
\qquad
\kappa_N=(-1)^N(N-1)2^{-2N+2}.
\]
Equivalently, the residual normalized by the truncated boundary moment has
limit \(\kappa_N\mu_2\).  This is a class-conditional analytic statement,
without a density, symmetry, or monotonicity assumption; it is not an
unconditional higher-order converse.

The all-order coefficient algebra has a different logical status.  For every
\(m\ge3\), the reduced universal polynomial satisfies
\[
A_{2m}^{\rm form}
=\kappa_m\mathfrak m_2\mathfrak m_{2m-2}
+P_m(\mathfrak m_2,\ldots,\mathfrak m_{2m-3}),
\qquad \kappa_m=(-1)^m(m-1)2^{-2m+2}\ne0.
\]
At \(m=2\), one instead has \(A_4^{\rm form}=\mathfrak m_2^2/8\).
Thus \(\EE|X_c|^{2m-2}<\infty\) is sufficient to evaluate every monomial and
is the minimal absolute-moment assumption that guarantees such evaluation
uniformly over all centred laws.  Proposition~\ref{thm:sharp-moment-equivalence-hierarchy}
is only this algebraic polynomial-domain minimality statement.  In particular,
for \(m\ge3\) it is not an unconditional converse for the entropy remainder.

The second theorem is multidimensional and finite-moment.  For
\(L\ge d+1\), finite centred \(L\)-moment closes the large-translation
remainder in the matched radial Poisson quotient, gives an explicit
\(L^\infty\cap L^1(\Omega_d)\) jet, supplies the large-\(t\)
entropy-existence package, and hence gives the tensor KL expansion through
order \(L+2\).  The two-region expression is retained as the quantitative
remainder bound; in this range the precise statement is the equivalence
\(\mathcal R_{d,L}(t)=o(t^{-L})\) for the displayed functional
\eqref{eq:conditional-multidim-two-region-tail} and the finite
\(L\)-moment assumption.
The restriction \(L\ge d+1\) is sharp for this \(L^\infty\) quotient
topology, as shown by the spike construction below.  It is not claimed to be
necessary for the leading KL coefficient in a weaker \(L^2(\Omega_d)\) or
entropy topology.  Section~\ref{sec:verified-A2-results} treats those weaker
topologies separately: the sharp moment exponent for the \(L^2\) quotient is
\(\max\{2,(d+1)/2\}\); finite covariance is the exact leading-KL threshold
for \(d\le3\); and finite covariance fails to control the leading KL scale
for every \(d\ge4\).  The optimal sufficient KL moment exponent in the latter
dimensions remains open.
The leading multidimensional covariance formula is deliberately read through
this analytic route.  Finite covariance alone determines the bounded quadratic
mode \(b_\Sigma\) and the algebraic number \(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\), but it
does not by itself make \(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\) the coefficient of
\eqref{eq:intro-main-functional}.  It becomes the leading KL coefficient only
after a quotient/existence bridge is in force, for example the
\((\mathrm{MPJ}_{d,L})+(\mathrm{EX}_{d,L})\) package supplied here by
finite \(L\)-moment with \(L\ge d+1\), or the separate second-order quotient
criterion stated in Section~\ref{sec:entropy-asymptotics}.

The third theorem is a domain-and-endpoint result for the standard
simultaneous-semigroup relative-entropy calculus.  For a symmetric jump
generator
\[
(L_Jf)(x)=\int\{f(y)-f(x)\}J(x,y)\,\dd y,
\qquad J(x,y)=J(y,x),
\]
and sufficiently regular positive solutions
\(\partial_t h_t=L_Jh_t\), \(\partial_t g_t=L_Jg_t\), set
\(u_t=h_t/g_t\).  The general generator identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-general-two-solution-generator-identity}
-\frac{\dd}{\dd t}D_{\rm KL}(h_t\|g_t)
=\frac12\iint J(x,y)
\left[g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)\Lambda(u_t(y),u_t(x))\right]\dd x\dd y.
\end{equation}
By exchanging \(x\) and \(y\) in the second term, the right side equals
\[
\iint J(x,y)g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))\dd x\dd y.
\]
This is the logarithmic relative-\(\Phi\)-entropy identity for two solutions
of the same symmetric jump semigroup; its Bregman differentiation is not
claimed as new \cite{Chafai2004PhiEntropies,BakryCoulhonLedouxSaloffCoste1995}.

Under finite centred variance, Theorem~\ref{thm:main-C} verifies that the
moving matched Cauchy quotient lies in the domain of this identity on every
compact time window, with the generator normalized as the symmetric
\(1\)-stable Cauchy generator
\cite{Feller1971,Bertoin1996LevyProcesses,Sato1999LevyProcesses,
Applebaum2009LevyProcesses,Kwasnicki2017}.  The KL functional is then the
tail integral of the resulting nonlocal dissipation.  Here both the quotient
\[
u_t=\frac{h_t}{g_t}
\]
and the reference density \(g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\) move with \(t\).
The logarithmic chain rule therefore first gives the two-pair identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-two-pair-chain-rule}
H'(t)=\langle \log u_t,\partial_t h_t\rangle
      -\langle u_t-1,\partial_t g_t\rangle,
\qquad H(t)=D_{\rm KL}(h_t\|g_t),
\end{equation}
not a single fixed-reference Dirichlet form.  Applying the Cauchy Green
formula separately to the pairs \((h_t,\log u_t)\) and \((g_t,u_t)\), and
using \(\int\partial_tg_t=0\), produces the algebraic difference
\[
(\rho_xa-\rho_yb)(\log a-\log b)-(\rho_x-\rho_y)(a-b)
=\rho_x\Lambda(a,b)+\rho_y\Lambda(b,a),
\]
where \(a=u_t(x)\), \(b=u_t(y)\), \(\rho_x=g_t(x)\),
\(\rho_y=g_t(y)\), and
\(\Lambda(a,b)=a\log(a/b)-a+b\).  This is the logarithmic case of the
Csisz{\'a}r--Morimoto--Ali--Silvey \(f\)-divergence/Bregman calculus
\cite{AliSilvey1966Divergence,Csiszar1967,Morimoto1963Markov,
Vajda1989TheoryDivergence,Bregman1967}.  The actual increment is to justify
these formal pairings on the noncompact Cauchy state space: finite variance
yields compact-window upper and lower quotient bounds, valid Green cutoffs,
and integrable domination of the Bregman kernel.  The separate endpoint proof
\(H(T)\to0\) then permits integration to infinity.

The coefficient-domain and boundary-layer increments are separate from
that dissipation identity.  Stable-semigroup de Bruijn and Hardy--Stein formulas identify an
entropy derivative or an integrated Bregman/Dirichlet form
\cite{DeBruijn1953,HirataNemotoYoshida2012RelativeEntropy,
Yoshida2017RelativeEntropyDiffusion,BanuelosBogdanLuks2016HardyStein,
Gutowski2023HardySteinPureJump,
BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein,
Johnson2013StableDeBruijn,Toscani2016StableEntropy,
BobkovChistyakovGotze2013StableEntropy}.  They do not determine the large-time
coefficient residual for a fixed input law against a moving matched Cauchy
reference.  The analytic increments here are the unconditional bounded-mode
converse at the variance threshold and the signed,
truncated-moment-normalized residual within the stated regularly varying
class.  The all-order Cayley--Laurent calculation instead supplies a finite
polynomial-evaluation algorithm and its minimal universal absolute-moment
assumption.

\subsection*{Theorems}\label{subsec:main-results}

\begin{maintheorem}{Analytic variance threshold and coefficient-domain minimality}
\label{thm:main-A}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability law on \(\RR\) with finite mean
\(\bar\gamma\), and put \(X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma\).  No variance or tail
assumption is imposed at this point.  Set
\[
H(t)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
\]
with the extended nonnegative convention for relative entropy.  Then
\[
\EE X_c^2<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)<\infty .
\]
If the variance is finite, then \(H(t)<\infty\) for every \(t>0\) and
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=\frac{(\EE X_c^2)^2}{8};
\]
if it is infinite, then
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4H(t)=+\infty
\]
in the extended sense.  No compact-support, exponential-tail, or
regular-variation hypothesis is used in this equivalence.

For the higher universal coefficients, the conclusion is algebraic rather
than an unconditional analytic converse.  For every \(m\ge3\), let
\(A_{2m}^{\rm form}\) be the reduced universal polynomial defined by the
constant-term rule below.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-A-moment-equivalence}
A_{2m}^{\rm form}
=\kappa_m\mathfrak m_2\mathfrak m_{2m-2}
+P_m(\mathfrak m_2,\ldots,\mathfrak m_{2m-3}),
\qquad
\kappa_m=(-1)^m(m-1)2^{-2m+2}\ne0,
\end{equation}
At \(m=2\), \(A_4^{\rm form}=\mathfrak m_2^2/8\).  Consequently, for every
\(m\ge2\),
\(\EE|X_c|^{2m-2}<\infty\) is sufficient for the canonical Lebesgue
evaluation of every monomial, and is minimal among absolute-moment
assumptions that guarantee evaluation uniformly over all centred probability
laws.  This statement uses the deliberately specified polynomial-evaluation
domain of Definition~\ref{def:canonical-coefficient-finiteness}; for
\(m\ge3\) it is not an unconditional converse for the entropy remainder.

The supporting coefficient expansion has the exact moment hypothesis as
follows.  Let \(N\ge2\), put \(L=2N-2\), and assume now that
\(\EE|X_c|^L<\infty\).  With
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mu_n&=\int_{\RR} X_c^n\,\dd\nu(\gamma)\qquad(2\le n\le L),\\
  h_t&=P_t*\nu,\qquad
  g_t&=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),
\end{aligned}
\]
one has
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N}).
\]
The coefficients are part of the statement.  For \(2\le m\le N\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-A-intro-coefficients}
A_{2m}(\nu)=
\sum_{r=2}^{m}
\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\substack{n_1+\cdots+n_r=2m\\ n_j\ge2}}
\mu_{n_1}\cdots\mu_{n_r}
\,[z^0]\prod_{j=1}^r Q_{n_j}(z),
\end{equation}
where \([z^0]\) denotes constant Laurent coefficient and the
Cayley--Chebyshev Laurent representative is fixed by the convention
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-A-intro-Qn}
Q_n(z)=2^{-n}(-\ii)^n
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{\ell-1}
\bigl(z^\ell+(-1)^nz^{-\ell}\bigr)
\qquad(n\ge1).
\end{equation}
Thus \(A_{2m}(\nu)\) is a universal polynomial in
\(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{2m-2}\); the proof that
\eqref{eq:main-A-intro-coefficients} is the entropy coefficient is supplied
by Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.  All retained odd coefficients
vanish, and the leading coefficient is
\[
A_4(\nu)=\frac{\mu_2^2}{8}.
\]
\end{maintheorem}

\begin{maintheorem}{Finite-moment multidimensional Poisson entropy jet}
\label{thm:main-B}
Let \(d\ge1\), let \(L\in\NN\) satisfy \(L\ge d+1\), and let \(\nu\) be a
probability measure on \(\RR^d\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\).  Put
\[
X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma .
\]
Assume \(\EE |X_c|^L<\infty\).  Then the centred moments
\[
\mu_\alpha=\EE[X_c^\alpha]\qquad(|\alpha|\le L)
\]
are finite.  The explicit two-region condition used in the quotient proof is
only the following one:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_{d,L}(t)
&:=t^{-(L+1)}\EE\!\left[
|X_c|^{L+1}{\bf1}_{\{|X_c|\le t\}}\right] \\
&\quad{}+ t^{-L}\EE\!\left[
|X_c|^L{\bf1}_{\{|X_c|>t\}}\right],
\end{aligned}
\]
one has, in the stated range \(L\ge d+1\),
\[
\mathcal R_{d,L}(t)=o(t^{-L})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\EE |X_c|^L<\infty .
\]
No broader equivalence between arbitrary two-region estimates and moment
hypotheses is asserted.  The restriction \(L\ge d+1\) is sharp for this
\(L^\infty\) quotient topology: for every integer \(1\le L<d+1\), the spike
construction following Theorem~\ref{thm:second-order-quotient-kl-coefficient}
gives a centred law with finite \(L\)th absolute moment whose quotient does
not converge uniformly to one.  This does not establish necessity for a
leading KL coefficient under a weaker \(L^2(\Omega_d)\) or entropy topology;
the separate sharp \(L^2\) result and the dimension-dependent KL statements
are Theorems~\ref{thm:multidim-l2-moment-threshold} and
\ref{thm:low-dimensional-finite-covariance-threshold}, together with
Proposition~\ref{prop:high-dimensional-finite-covariance-obstruction}.
With
\[
h_t=P_t^{(d)}*\nu,\qquad
g_t=P_t^{(d)}(\cdot-\bar\gamma),
\]
one has, for all sufficiently large \(t\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\sum_{m=4}^{L+2}\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)t^{-m}
+o(t^{-(L+2)}),
\]
where, for \(4\le m\le L+2\),
\[
\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)=
\sum_{r=2}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
\frac{(-1)^r}{r(r-1)}
\sum_{\substack{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r\in\NN_0^d\\
|\alpha_1|+\cdots+|\alpha_r|=m\\
2\le|\alpha_j|\le L\ \mathrm{for}\ 1\le j\le r}}
\mu_{\alpha_1}\cdots\mu_{\alpha_r}
\int_{\RR^d}\prod_{j=1}^r
U_{\alpha_j}^{(d)}(y)\,\Omega_d(\dd y).
\]
Here \(U_\alpha^{(d)}\) denotes the radial Poisson Taylor mode and
\[
\Omega_d(\dd y)
=\frac{\Gamma((d+1)/2)}{\pi^{(d+1)/2}}
(1+|y|^2)^{-(d+1)/2}\,\dd y
\]
is the \(t=1\) \(d\)-dimensional Poisson law.  The displayed formula is a
finite universal tensor polynomial in the centred moments of orders
\(2,\ldots,m-2\), and all retained odd coefficients vanish.
For the leading retained coefficient, let
\[
\Sigma=\EE[X_cX_c^{\mathsf T}],\qquad
\Sigma_0=\Sigma-\frac{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma}{d}I_d .
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal A_4^{(d)}(\nu)=\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)
=c_d^{\rm iso}(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2
+c_d^{\rm tr}\|\Sigma_0\|_{\rm HS}^2,
\]
with
\[
c_d^{\rm iso}=
\frac{3(d+1)(7d+9)}
{4d(d+3)(d+5)(d+7)},
\qquad
c_d^{\rm tr}=
\frac{3(d+1)(d+3)}
{4(d+5)(d+7)} .
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)
=\frac12\int_{\RR^d}b_\Sigma(y)^2\,\Omega_d(\dd y),
\quad
b_\Sigma(y)=
\frac{d+1}{2}\left(
-\frac{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma}{1+|y|^2}
+\frac{(d+3)y^{\mathsf T}\Sigma y}{(1+|y|^2)^2}
\right).
\]
\end{maintheorem}

\begin{maintheorem}{Domain and endpoint closure for Cauchy relative entropy}
\label{thm:main-C}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and
finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_t&=P_t*\nu,\\
g_t&=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\\
u_t&=h_t/g_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
Define the Bregman kernel by
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\]
and define the nonlocal Bregman dissipation by the nonnegative Lebesgue
integral
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t)
=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\]
with the diagonal ignored.  For the Cauchy jump kernel
\(J(x,y)=\{\pi(x-y)^2\}^{-1}\), this is exactly the right side of the
general two-solution identity
\eqref{eq:intro-general-two-solution-generator-identity}.  The assertion is
that the matched quotient belongs to the required domain: \(\mathcal I_\nu\)
is measurable and locally integrable on \((0,\infty)\).  More precisely, on every compact window
\([a,b]\Subset(0,\infty)\), the quotient \(u_t\) satisfies the bounded
Lipschitz estimates, Green-cutoff convergence, and integrable Bregman-kernel
domination recorded in
Proposition~\ref{prop:main-C-domain-differentiation-package}.  These bounds
justify the general generator identity, while the separate endpoint result
\(D_{\rm KL}(h_T\|g_T)\to0\) justifies passage from a finite time window to
infinity.  Consequently, for every \(t>0\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\,\dd s .
\]
\end{maintheorem}

\subsection*{Comparison by mechanism}
\label{subsec:intro-recent-jfa-comparison}

Classical moment decompositions expand a function or convolution solution
into masses and derivatives of a fundamental kernel; see
Duoandikoetxea--Zuazua \cite{DuoandikoetxeaZuazua1992} and the subsequent
large-time diffusion literature
\cite{EscobedoZuazua1991,IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2017,
IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2022RefinedFractional}.  The density-level template is
\(P_t*\nu=\sum_{|\alpha|\le L}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\mu_\alpha
\partial^\alpha P_t/\alpha!+\text{remainder}\).  The increment in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-B} is the uniform matched quotient estimate, including
large translations, followed by a justified KL transfer; it is not the
moment decomposition by itself.

Relative \(\Phi\)-entropy and pure-jump semigroup calculus already provide
the formal two-solution identity
\eqref{eq:intro-general-two-solution-generator-identity}; see Chafa{\"\i}
\cite{Chafai2004PhiEntropies} and, for early nonlinear/nonlocal energy forms,
Bakry--Coulhon--Ledoux--Saloff-Coste
\cite{BakryCoulhonLedouxSaloffCoste1995}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main-C} therefore
claims only the finite-variance domain verification for the noncompact Cauchy
kernel and the endpoint passage that converts the window identity into a
tail integral.

At formula level, the polarized Hardy--Stein identity
\cite{BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein} states, for its
symmetric Markov semigroup and \(p\ge2\),
\[
\int f g^{\langle p-1\rangle}\dd x
=\int_0^\infty\!\iint
\mathcal J_p(P_s\Phi(x),P_s\Phi(y))J(x,y)\dd x\dd y\dd s,
\qquad \Phi=(f,g),
\]
where, for \(w,z\in\RR^2\),
\[
\mathcal J_p(w,z)=z_1z_2^{\langle p-1\rangle}
-w_1w_2^{\langle p-1\rangle}-w_2^{\langle p-1\rangle}(z_1-w_1)
-(p-1)w_1|w_2|^{p-2}(z_2-w_2).
\]
This is a fixed power pairing.  It does not specialize directly to
\[
\int h_t\log(h_t/g_t)\dd x,
\]
nor does its \(p\ge2\) statement supply the logarithmic endpoint.  More
importantly for the present theorem,
it does not by specialization verify the compact-window bounds and noncompact
Green pairings for \(h_t/g_t\), or prove \(H(T)\to0\).  Those are precisely
the conclusions isolated in Theorem~\ref{thm:main-C}.  A broader comparison
by mechanism is given in Appendix~\ref{app:related-work-scope}.
