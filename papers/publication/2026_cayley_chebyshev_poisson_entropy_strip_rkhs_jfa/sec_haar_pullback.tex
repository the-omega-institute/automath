\subsection{Pullback of Haar measure}%
\label{sec:haar-pullback}

The Jacobian of the Cayley parametrization identifies normalized arc
length on the circle with the Cauchy weight on the line.  Since this is
the only role played by the circle model in the sequel, Proposition~\ref{prop:phase-derivative}
records just the change-of-variables formula used in the coefficient
computations.  The same Cauchy/wrapped-Cauchy M{\"o}bius normalization is a
standard one in circular statistics
\cite{KentTyler1988WrappedCauchy,McCullagh1992CauchyModels,
McCullagh1996CauchyMobius,
KatoJones2010MobiusCircle,KatoMcCullagh2020SphereCauchyMobius}.

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cauchy-measure}
\omega(\dd x):=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})}\dd x.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Cauchy phase Jacobian]\label{prop:phase-derivative}
The phase coordinate \eqref{eq:phase-coord} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-deriv}
\upsilon'(x)=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^{2})},
\qquad
\vartheta'(x)=\frac{2}{1+x^{2}}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, $\upsilon:(\RR,\omega)\to(\UU,\dd u)$ is measure preserving.
In particular, for every non-negative Borel function $f$ on~$\UU$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-integral}
\int_{\RR}f(\upsilon(x))\,\omega(\dd x)=\int_{\UU}f(u)\dd u.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \eqref{eq:phase-coord}.  The change-of-variables formula
then gives \eqref{eq:weighted-integral}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Endpoint-normalized chart]\label{rem:haar-pullback-normalization}
For the Cayley map itself the measure statement can be written in the two
equivalent forms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:haar-pullback}
\iota_{\#}\omega=m_{\TT},
\qquad
\iota^{*}m_{\TT}:=(\iota^{-1})_{\#}m_{\TT}=\omega.
\end{equation}
Here \(\iota_{\#}\) denotes pushforward from the line compactification to
the circle, while the displayed pullback notation is used only in this
homeomorphic chart sense.
This identity is the normalization used later for every Cauchy-weighted
mode integral and Laurent constant-term extraction.  It also fixes the
endpoint-normalized chart: if an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
\(\varphi:\overline\RR\to\TT\) has \(C^1\) restriction to \(\RR\),
\(\varphi(0)=1\), \(\varphi(\infty)=-1\), and
\(\varphi_{\#}\omega=m_{\TT}\), then writing
\(\varphi(x)=e^{\ii\alpha(x)}\) with \(\alpha(0)=0\) gives
\((2\pi)^{-1}\alpha'(x)\dd x=\omega(\dd x)\), hence
\(\alpha(x)=2\arctan x\) and \(\varphi=\iota\).  Reversing orientation only
adds the conjugate chart.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Notation guide for entropy coefficients}
\label{subsec:notation-guide-entropy}

The entropy arguments use several symbols that look similar but live in
different layers of the proof.  The following table fixes the convention used
after the Cayley--Haar preliminaries.  In particular, \(Q_n\) is always a
one-dimensional Laurent mode, while \(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\) is a covariance
quadratic form; the subscript \(d\) in the latter denotes dimension, not a
Laurent index.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.28\textwidth}p{0.22\textwidth}X@{}}
\textbf{Role} & \textbf{Notation} & \textbf{Meaning and scope} \\
\hline
One-dimensional coefficient &
\(A_k^{\rm form}\), \(A_k(\nu)\) &
\(A_k^{\rm form}\) is the universal polynomial obtained from the
Cayley--Chebyshev mode integrals.  \(A_k(\nu)\) is its evaluation at the
centred moments of the one-dimensional input law, when those moments exist. \\
Multidimensional coefficient &
\(\mathcal A_m^{(d)}(\nu)\) &
The retained coefficient in the \(d\)-dimensional radial Poisson KL expansion.
We use the calligraphic symbol throughout and do not introduce a separate
\(A_m^{(d)}\) alias. \\
Laurent mode &
\(Q_n(z)\) &
The Laurent representative of the one-dimensional mode \(u_n\), defined by
\(Q_n(e^{2\ii\theta})=u_n(\tan\theta)\).  Its index \(n\) is a mode order. \\
Covariance quadratic form &
\(\mathcal Q_d(\Sigma)\) &
The algebraic quadratic contraction determined by a \(d\)-dimensional
covariance matrix \(\Sigma\).  It becomes a KL coefficient only after a
sufficient quotient jet has supplied the leading \(t^{-2}\) density layer. \\
Dissipation &
\(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\) &
The nonlocal Bregman dissipation of the moving matched Cauchy quotient,
defined by annular cutoffs and used in the tail identity
\(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\int_t^\infty\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s\). \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
