\section{Regularly varying missing-moment boundary layer}%
\label{app:conditional-missing-top-moment-tails}

This appendix proves
Theorem~\ref{cor:main-body-conditional-missing-top-moment-tail}.  The proof
has two distinct inputs.  Karamata theory identifies the divergent truncated
boundary moment and controls all omitted tails.  The Cayley quotient estimate
then transfers that single truncated mode through the entropy Taylor map.
Neither an absolute-continuity hypothesis nor a symmetry hypothesis is
needed.

Throughout the appendix, let \(N\ge3\), \(p=2N-2\), and let \(X\) be
centred.  Write
\[
 \overline F(t):=\mathbb P(|X|>t),\qquad
 C:=c_++c_->0,
\]
and suppose that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rv-boundary-absolute-tail}
 \overline F(t)\sim Ct^{-p}L(t).
\end{equation}
The two one-sided hypotheses in the theorem imply
\eqref{eq:rv-boundary-absolute-tail}; their balance will not otherwise enter
the proof.

\begin{lemma}[Tauberian boundary-moment package]%
\label{lem:rv-boundary-moment-package}
Let \(p>2\), let \(L\) be measurable, eventually positive, and slowly
varying, and assume \eqref{eq:rv-boundary-absolute-tail}.  Put
\[
 \ell_L(t):=\int_{x_0}^t\frac{L(s)}s\,\dd s
\]
and suppose \(\ell_L(t)\to\infty\).  Then \(\ell_L\) is slowly
varying, \(L(t)=o(\ell_L(t))\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rv-truncated-p-moment}
 M_p(t):=\EE[|X|^p\mathbf1_{\{|X|\le t\}}]
 \sim pC\ell_L(t).
\end{equation}
In particular, \(\EE|X|^q<\infty\) for \(q<p\),
\(\EE|X|^p=\infty\), \(M_p(t)\to\infty\), and
\(M_p(t)=o(t^{p-2})\).  Moreover, for each \(0\le q<p\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rv-lower-omitted-tail}
 t^{-q}\EE[|X|^q\mathbf1_{\{|X|>t\}}]
 =O(t^{-p}L(t)),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rv-local-p-plus-one}
 t^{-(p+1)}
 \EE[|X|^{p+1}\mathbf1_{\{|X|\le t\}}]
 =O(t^{-p}L(t)).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The integrated slowly varying assertions
\[
 \ell_L\in\mathrm{SV},\qquad L(t)=o(\ell_L(t))
\]
are the divergent-integral case of the standard integrated-slow-variation
theorem; see
\cite[Proposition~1.5.9a]{BinghamGoldieTeugels1987RegularVariation}.
Karamata's direct and tail theorems are used below in the normalization of
\cite[Theorem~1.5.11]{BinghamGoldieTeugels1987RegularVariation}.

For a nonnegative random variable \(Y\), integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rv-truncated-layer-cake}
 \EE[Y^r\mathbf1_{\{Y\le t\}}]
 =r\int_0^t s^{r-1}\mathbb P(Y>s)\,\dd s
 -t^r\mathbb P(Y>t).
\end{equation}
Apply this with \(Y=|X|\) and \(r=p\).  Since
\(s^{p-1}\overline F(s)\sim Cs^{-1}L(s)\), the index \(-1\) case of
Karamata's direct theorem
\cite[Theorem~1.5.11]{BinghamGoldieTeugels1987RegularVariation}, together
with the divergence of \(\ell_L\), gives
\[
 \int_{x_0}^t s^{p-1}\overline F(s)\,\dd s
 \sim C\ell_L(t).
\]
Here the choice of \(x_0\) changes \(\ell_L\) only by an additive constant,
which is absorbed by the divergent slowly varying scale
\(\ell_L\in\mathrm{SV}\).
The terminal term in \eqref{eq:rv-truncated-layer-cake} is asymptotic to
\(CL(t)=o(\ell_L(t))\), proving
\eqref{eq:rv-truncated-p-moment}.  The same layer-cake identity without
truncation shows that moments of order \(q<p\) are finite and that the
\(p\)th moment diverges.  Since every slowly varying function is
\(o(t^\varepsilon)\) for each \(\varepsilon>0\),
\(M_p(t)=o(t^{p-2})\).

For \(0\le q<p\), another integration by parts yields
\[
 \EE[|X|^q\mathbf1_{\{|X|>t\}}]
 =t^q\overline F(t)
 +q\int_t^\infty s^{q-1}\overline F(s)\,\dd s
 =O(t^{q-p}L(t)),
\]
where Karamata's tail theorem applies because \(q-p-1<-1\).  This is
\eqref{eq:rv-lower-omitted-tail}.  Finally, taking \(r=p+1\) in
\eqref{eq:rv-truncated-layer-cake} and using Karamata's direct theorem at
index zero gives
\[
 \EE[|X|^{p+1}\mathbf1_{\{|X|\le t\}}]=O(tL(t)),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:rv-local-p-plus-one}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Unconditional regularly varying quotient jet]%
\label{lem:rv-boundary-quotient-jet}
Under the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{cor:main-body-conditional-missing-top-moment-tail}, define
\[
 \Delta_t(y):=\frac{(P_t*\nu)(ty)}{P_t(ty)}-1,\qquad
 A_t(y):=\sum_{n=2}^{p-1}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rv-boundary-quotient-jet}
 \Delta_t(y)=A_t(y)+u_p(y)t^{-p}M_p(t)+e_t(y),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rv-boundary-quotient-error}
 \|e_t\|_\infty+\|e_t\|_{L^1(\omega)}
 =O(t^{-p}L(t))
 =o(t^{-p}M_p(t)).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identity
\[
 \Delta_t(y)=\EE\{R_{X/t}(y)-1\}
\]
holds for every probability law.  On \(|X|\le t\), expand
\(R_{X/t}\) through order \(p\).  The uniform Cayley remainder in
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-one-dimensional-cayley-taylor-remainder} and
\eqref{eq:rv-local-p-plus-one} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rv-local-quotient-error}
 \left\|
 \EE\!\left[
 \left\{R_{X/t}(y)-\sum_{n=0}^pu_n(y)(X/t)^n\right\}
 \mathbf1_{\{|X|\le t\}}\right]
 \right\|_\infty
 =O(t^{-p}L(t)).
\end{equation}
The same estimate holds in \(L^1(\omega)\), because \(\omega\) is a
probability measure.

The centred first moment vanishes.  For each \(1\le n<p\), completing the
truncated moment to \(\mu_n\) costs, by
\eqref{eq:rv-lower-omitted-tail} and the boundedness of \(u_n\),
\[
 \left\|u_nt^{-n}
 \EE[X^n\mathbf1_{\{|X|>t\}}]\right\|_\infty
 =O(t^{-p}L(t)).
\]
There are only finitely many such terms.  The top local term is exactly
\(u_pt^{-p}M_p(t)\), since \(p\) is even.

It remains to bound the unexpanded far quotient.  The elementary estimate
\[
 |R_\varepsilon(y)-1|\le C_0(1+\varepsilon^2),
 \qquad y,\varepsilon\in\RR,
\]
and \eqref{eq:rv-lower-omitted-tail} at \(q=0,2\) give
\[
 \left\|\EE[
 \{R_{X/t}(y)-1\}\mathbf1_{\{|X|>t\}}]\right\|_\infty
 \le C_0\left\{\mathbb P(|X|>t)
 +t^{-2}\EE[|X|^2\mathbf1_{\{|X|>t\}}]\right\}
 =O(t^{-p}L(t)).
\]
Again the uniform bound implies the same \(L^1(\omega)\) bound.
Collecting the local remainder, lower-moment completions, and far quotient
proves the first equality in \eqref{eq:rv-boundary-quotient-error}.  Its
little-oh form follows from
\(L(t)=o(\ell_L(t))\) and
\(M_p(t)\sim pC\ell_L(t)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of
Theorem~\ref{cor:main-body-conditional-missing-top-moment-tail}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:rv-boundary-moment-package} proves all moment assertions and
the asymptotic \(M_p(t)\sim pC\ell_L(t)\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:rv-boundary-quotient-jet}, the quotient has the form required
by the moving-scale Taylor transfer
Lemma~\ref{lem:moving-scale-taylor-transfer}, with
\[
 a_2=\mu_2u_2,\qquad b=u_p.
\]
The finite second moment supplied by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rv-boundary-moment-package} also gives the finite KL
representation \(H(t)=\int\Phi(\Delta_t)\,\dd\omega\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:kl-bounded-test-and-poisson-quotient}.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rv-entropy-transfer}
 H(t)=\int_\RR\Phi(A_t)\,\dd\omega
 +\mu_2J(2,p)t^{-(p+2)}M_p(t)
 +o(t^{-(p+2)}M_p(t)).
\end{equation}
The finite Laurent Taylor algebra and its odd-parity cancellation give
\[
 \int_\RR\Phi(A_t)\,\dd\omega
 =\sum_{j=2}^{N-1}A_{2j}(\nu)t^{-2j}+O(t^{-2N}).
\]
The final error is
\(o(t^{-2N}M_p(t))\), because \(M_p(t)\to\infty\).  Finally,
Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson-kl-moment-threshold-sharpness} computes
\[
 J(2,p)=(-1)^N(N-1)2^{-2N+2}=\kappa_N.
\]
Substitution into \eqref{eq:rv-entropy-transfer} proves the expansion and
the first normalized limit.  Division by
\(\ell_L(t)\), followed by
\(M_p(t)/\ell_L(t)\to p(c_++c_-)\), proves the second.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Sharp scope of the boundary hypothesis.}
The regular-variation index alone does not imply that the \(p\)th moment
diverges.  For example,
\[
 \mathbb P(|X|>t)\sim Ct^{-p}(\log t)^{-a}
\]
has an infinite \(p\)th moment for \(a\le1\), with boundary corrections
\[
 \ell_L(t)\sim
 \begin{cases}
 (\log t)^{1-a}/(1-a),&a<1,\\
 \log\log t,&a=1,
 \end{cases}
\]
but has a finite \(p\)th moment for \(a>1\).  In the latter case the
ordinary coefficient theorem applies and there is no divergent
slowly varying replacement.  For tails that are not regularly varying, or
for second-order remainders beyond the leading \(\ell_L\) scale, the
present theorem makes no assertion; such refinements require corresponding
first- or second-order Tauberian hypotheses.
