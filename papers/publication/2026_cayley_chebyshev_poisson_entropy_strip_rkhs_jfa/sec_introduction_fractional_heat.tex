\subsection*{Stable-flow dissipation and endpoint representation}

The field-facing theorem that organizes the paper concerns the entire
positive-time evolution of relative entropy.  For two isotropic symmetric
stable heat flows issuing from probability measures with finite
\((d+\alpha)\)-th absolute moments,
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-stable-heat-flow-relative-entropy} proves that their
relative entropy is locally absolutely continuous and dissipates by the
logarithmic fractional Dirichlet form.  It closes the endpoint at infinity,
gives the exact tail-integral formula, and treats a stable moving reference.
We provide an exact nonlocal Bregman integral representation along Johnson's
symmetric-stable interpolation, under the stated finite-moment and
finite-initial-entropy domain.  Johnson's Theorem~5.1 instead expresses the
derivative along this interpolation as an inner product involving an
MMSE-type score and a Fisher-score difference
\cite{Johnson2013StableDeBruijn}; no identification of that score pairing
with the present jump form is asserted.

The two-solution entropy-production identity has important precedents.  For
two positive solutions \(\mu_t,\rho_t\) of the same forward Kolmogorov
equation on a finite or countable state space, Hilder--Peletier--Sharma--Tse
write, with \(v_t=\mu_t/\rho_t\),
\[
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}H(\mu_t\mid\rho_t)
 =-\sum_{x,y}\rho_t(x)L(x,y)
 \Lambda\bigl(v_t(x),v_t(y)\bigr),
\]
and trace entropy contraction to Voigt's 1981 stochastic-operator theorem
\cite{HilderPeletierSharmaTse2020EntropyDistance,
Voigt1981StochasticOperators}.  Hirata--Nemoto--Yoshida give a Gaussian
moving-reference de Bruijn identity and an integral representation
\cite{HirataNemotoYoshida2012IntegralRepresentation}.  Thus neither a moving
reference nor the one-sided Bregman structure is itself the novelty.

More broadly, relative \(\Phi\)-entropy calculus already gives the formal
simultaneous-semigroup identity \cite{Chafai2004PhiEntropies}.  Hardy--Stein
identities for L\'evy semigroups and pure-jump Dirichlet forms, nonlinear
Douglas identities, polarized Hardy--Stein identities, and Beurling--Deny
formulas for Sobolev--Bregman forms already establish broad nonlocal Bregman
algebra
\cite{BanuelosBogdanLuks2016HardyStein,
BogdanGrzywnyPietruskaPalubaRutkowski2023NonlinearNonlocalDouglas,
Gutowski2023HardySteinPureJump,
BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein,
GutowskiKwasnicki2025BeurlingDenySobolevBregman}.  Most closely, conditional
Hardy--Stein identities treat ratios of harmonic functions and general
convex functions \cite{KlimsiakRozkosz2025ConditionalHardyStein}.  We claim
neither the two-point logarithmic algebra nor a general Dirichlet-form
identity.  The contribution is the stable-continuum measure-data domain and
endpoint theorem for the classical two-solution entropy-production identity:
the compact-time quotient bounds, separate noncompact Green-pairing limits,
and the \(t=\infty\) and stable-reference endpoint closures.

The exponent \(d+\alpha\) is a transparent sufficient hypothesis, not an
optimality claim.  Its \(d+\alpha\)-order content enters only in the upper
compact-window quotient bound, because the stable translate-to-reference
quotient grows in the worst case like \((1+|z|)^{d+\alpha}\).  The endpoint
argument also uses the hypothesis through its finite-first-moment
consequence, which makes \(W_1(\mu,\nu)\) finite, but not through the
\(d+\alpha\) exponent.  Whether finite first moment, finite initial relative
entropy, or an intrinsic Dirichlet-form domain suffices is left open.  No
theorem is claimed for all isotropic unimodal L\'evy semigroups or all
subordinate Brownian motions.
