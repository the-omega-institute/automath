\subsection*{Two simultaneous stable heat flows}

The coefficient results above concern large-scale asymptotics.  A separate
field-facing consequence concerns the entire positive-time evolution of
relative entropy.  Theorem~\ref{thm:two-stable-heat-flow-relative-entropy}
proves, for two isotropic symmetric stable heat flows issuing from
probability measures with finite \((d+\alpha)\)-th absolute moments, that
their relative entropy is locally absolutely continuous and dissipates by
the logarithmic fractional Dirichlet form.  It also closes the endpoint at
infinity, gives the exact tail-integral formula, and yields the stable
reference representation along Johnson's interpolation
\cite[Open Problem~4]{Johnson2013StableDeBruijn}.  The proof begins with the
quotient calculation in Lemma~\ref{lem:stable-quotient-pilot}; the critical
local domination is \( |x-y|^{2-d-\alpha}\), so no moment margin beyond the
stated hypothesis is introduced.

The novelty boundary is deliberately narrow.  Relative
\(\Phi\)-entropy calculus already gives the formal simultaneous-semigroup
identity \cite{Chafai2004PhiEntropies}.  Hardy--Stein identities for L\'evy
semigroups and pure-jump Dirichlet forms, nonlinear Douglas identities,
polarized Hardy--Stein identities, and Beurling--Deny formulas for
Sobolev--Bregman forms already establish broad nonlocal Bregman algebra
\cite{BanuelosBogdanLuks2016HardyStein,
BogdanGrzywnyPietruskaPalubaRutkowski2023NonlinearNonlocalDouglas,
Gutowski2023HardySteinPureJump,
BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein,
GutowskiKwasnicki2025BeurlingDenySobolevBregman}.  Most closely, conditional
Hardy--Stein identities now treat ratios of harmonic functions and general
convex functions \cite{KlimsiakRozkosz2025ConditionalHardyStein}.  We claim
neither the two-point logarithmic algebra nor a general Dirichlet-form
identity.  The increment is the moving-denominator parabolic theorem for
stable heat flows from measure data, the explicit sufficient moment domain,
the separate noncompact Green-pairing limits, the endpoint \(t=\infty\), and
the connection to Johnson's integral-representation problem.

The moment exponent \(d+\alpha\) is not called optimal.  It is the natural
sufficient exponent for this proof because the stable density decays like
\((1+|x|)^{-d-\alpha}\), and hence its translate-to-reference quotient grows
in the worst case like \((1+|z|)^{d+\alpha}\).  The upper compact-window
quotient bound is exactly where this moment is used.  Whether finite first
moment, finite initial relative entropy, or an intrinsic Dirichlet-form
condition suffices is left open.
