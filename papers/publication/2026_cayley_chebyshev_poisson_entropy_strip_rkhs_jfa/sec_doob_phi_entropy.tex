\section{KL Tail Identity for the Poisson Doob Quotient}%
\label{sec:doob-phi-entropy}

Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\).
For \(t>0\) put
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad
u_t:=h_t/g_t .
\]
The Cauchy generator is normalized by
\[
(Lf)(x)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y,
\]
so that \(\partial_tP_t=LP_t\).  This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail}
in the Cauchy normalization displayed above, namely the KL-specialized
two-point Bregman dissipation identity for the moving Poisson quotient
\(u_t=h_t/g_t\), and then records the coefficient consequence.

\noindent\textbf{The quotient-domain input.}
The only point not supplied by the abstract Hardy--Stein/Bregman formalism is
the moving matched-Poisson domain verification.
Theorem~\ref{thm:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} is the
domain theorem: on every compact time window \(0<a<b<\infty\), it records
\[
\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)}\le u_t\le
2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},\qquad
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le
3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr)|x-y|,
\]
and the logarithmic Bregman kernel divided by the Cauchy singular kernel is
dominated by an explicit integrable majorant
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}
\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{|x-y|<1\}}+|x-y|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|x-y|\ge1\}}\bigr).
\]
The cutoff theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification}, then records the \(L^1\) chain rule and the two
annular Cauchy Green formulae for \((h_t,\log u_t)\) and \((g_t,u_t)\).
This is the new Cauchy/KL closure step for the nonstationary quotient
\((P_t*\nu)/P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\); the preceding lemmas only prove the
pieces that the domain theorem assembles.

The identity is in the de Bruijn/Stam and relative-entropy dissipation
lineage
\cite{DeBruijn1953,Stam1959,Blachman1965,Barron1986,
JohnsonBarron2004,MadimanBarron2007,Verdu2010Mismatched,
HirataNemotoYoshida2012RelativeEntropy,Yoshida2017RelativeEntropyDiffusion},
and it uses the same Bregman algebra that underlies nonlocal
Hardy--Stein/Sobolev--Bregman formulas
\cite{Bregman1967,BanuelosBogdanLuks2016HardyStein,
BanuelosKim2019HardySteinNonsymmetric,
BogdanGrzywnyPietruskaPalubaRutkowski2023NonlinearNonlocalDouglas,
Gutowski2023HardySteinPureJump,GutowskiKwasnicki2025BeurlingDenySobolevBregman,
BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein}.  Here no abstract
Hardy--Stein theorem is invoked as a black box.  The proof is split into
four self-contained inputs:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(1)}&
\text{an }L^1\text{ chain rule for a bounded moving quotient},\\
\text{(2)}&
\text{the annular Cauchy Green formula for bounded Lipschitz tests},\\
\text{(3)}&
\text{the matched-Poisson quotient-domain theorem above},\\
\text{(4)}&
\text{the endpoint decay }D_{\rm KL}(h_T\|g_T)\to0 .
\end{array}
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:abstract-cutoff-bregman-kl} combines the first two
inputs with the elementary logarithmic Bregman identity.
Theorem~\ref{thm:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} supplies the
only distribution-dependent estimates for the nonstationary quotient
\((P_t*\nu)/P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification} verifies the abstract cutoff
hypotheses on the compact time window.  The final theorem then follows from
\(H'(t)=-\mathcal I_\nu(t)\), nonnegativity of \(\mathcal I_\nu\), and the
separate decay \(H(T)\to0\).  The coefficient expansion itself remains the
Cayley quotient-mode result of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}; the
identity below only converts those KL tails into dissipation tails.

\begin{lemma}[Second-moment KL decay to the translated Poisson profile]%
\label{lem:poisson-second-moment-kl-decay}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean $\bar{\gamma}$ and
finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar{\gamma}).
\]
Then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}$ and $x=\bar{\gamma}+ty$.  Since
$g_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty)=(\pi t)^{-1}(1+y^2)^{-1}$,
\[
u_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty):=\frac{h_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty)}{g_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty)}
=\int_{\RR}\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
For $A>0$ decompose $\nu=\nu_A+\nu_A^c$ according to
$|X_c|\le A$ and $|X_c|>A$.  The elementary kernel bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-moment-kl-kernel-bound}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
\le 2(1+\varepsilon^2)
\qquad(y,\varepsilon\in\RR)
\end{equation}
implies
\[
0\le u_{t,A}^c(y):=\int_{|X_c|>A}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
\le 2\int_{|X_c|>A}\left(1+\frac{X_c^2}{t^2}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
Hence, for $t\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-moment-kl-tail-sup}
\sup_y u_{t,A}^c(y)
\le 2\nu(|X_c|>A)+2\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu .
\end{equation}

The bounded part has total mass $m_A:=\nu(|X_c|\le A)$ and first centred
moment $b_A:=\int_{|X_c|\le A}X_c\dd\nu$.  The exact expansion
\[
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
=1+\frac{2y}{1+y^2}\varepsilon
+\frac{(3y^2-1)\varepsilon^2-2y\varepsilon^3}
{(1+y^2)(1+(y-\varepsilon)^2)}
\]
shows that, for each fixed $A$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-truncated-quotient-limit}
u_{t,A}(y):=\int_{|X_c|\le A}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
=m_A+\frac{2yb_A}{t(1+y^2)}+O_A(t^{-2})
\end{equation}
uniformly in~$y$.  Since
$b_A=-\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c\dd\nu$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
|b_A|\le \left(\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Combining this with
\eqref{eq:second-moment-kl-tail-sup} and
\eqref{eq:bounded-truncated-quotient-limit} yields
\[
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|u_t-1\|_{L^\infty(\RR)}
\le 1-m_A+2\nu(|X_c|>A)+2\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu .
\]
Letting $A\to\infty$ gives $\|u_t-1\|_\infty\to0$.

For all sufficiently large $t$ we therefore have
$|u_t-1|\le1/2$.  With $\delta_t=u_t-1$ and using
$\int g_t\delta_t=\int(h_t-g_t)=0$,
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\int_{\RR}g_t\bigl((1+\delta_t)\log(1+\delta_t)-\delta_t\bigr)\dd x
\le 2\|\delta_t\|_\infty^2,
\]
because $(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\le2s^2$ for $|s|\le1/2$.  The right-hand side
tends to zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Abstract cutoff--Bregman KL identity]%
\label{prop:abstract-cutoff-bregman-kl}
\label{lem:cauchy-kl-cutoff-green-identity}
\label{thm:external-cauchy-kl-hardystein-input}
\label{prop:self-contained-cauchy-kl-hardystein}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\).  Let \(p_t\) and \(r_t\) be positive probability
densities on \(\RR\), put \(v_t=p_t/r_t\), and define
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta ,
\qquad
K(t):=\int_{\RR}p_t(x)\log v_t(x)\dd x .
\]
Assume the following hypotheses on \([a,b]\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(B\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \(t\mapsto p_t,r_t\) are absolutely continuous as \(L^1(\RR)\)-valued
probability-density paths, \(K\in AC([a,b])\), and, for a.e. \(t\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:external-kl-chain-rule}
K'(t)=
\int_{\RR}\log v_t\,\partial_tp_t\,\dd x
-\int_{\RR}(v_t-1)\partial_tr_t\,\dd x .
\end{equation}
\item For \(q_t=\log v_t\) with \(s_t=p_t\), and for \(q_t=v_t\) with
\(s_t=r_t\), the annular Cauchy Green formula holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:external-cutoff-pairing}
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)\partial_ts_t(x)\dd x
=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(s_t(x)-s_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\end{equation}
for a.e. \(t\), where \(\Omega_R=\{(x,y):R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}\).
\item The symmetrized logarithmic Bregman kernel
\[
\frac{r_t(x)\Lambda(v_t(x),v_t(y))
+r_t(y)\Lambda(v_t(y),v_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}
\]
has an \(L^1([a,b]\times\RR^2)\) majorant.  In particular the annular
cutoffs may be removed by dominated convergence.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
\mathcal I(t):=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{r_t(x)\Lambda(v_t(x),v_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\]
belongs to \(L^1([a,b])\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:external-cauchy-kl-hardystein-input}
K'(t)=-\mathcal I(t)\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in[a,b].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At a time for which the chain rule and the two cutoff pairings hold,
write
\[
a=v_t(x),\qquad b=v_t(y),\qquad \rho_x=r_t(x),\qquad \rho_y=r_t(y),
\]
and put \(p_t=r_tv_t\).  By \eqref{eq:external-kl-chain-rule} and the mass
identity for \(r_t\), which follows from
\(\int r_t=1\) and \(t\mapsto r_t\in AC([a,b];L^1)\),
\[
K'(t)=\int_{\RR}\log v_t\,\partial_tp_t
      -\int_{\RR}v_t\,\partial_tr_t .
\]
Applying the assumed cutoff Green formula
\eqref{eq:external-cutoff-pairing} first to
\((s_t,q_t)=(p_t,\log v_t)\) and then to
\((s_t,q_t)=(r_t,v_t)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
K'(t)
&=-\frac1{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(\log v_t(x)-\log v_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y\\
&\quad+\frac1{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(r_t(x)-r_t(y))(v_t(x)-v_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\end{aligned}
\]
The numerator in the difference of the two Green forms is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(\rho_xa-\rho_yb)(\log a-\log b)-(\rho_x-\rho_y)(a-b)\\
&\qquad
=\rho_x\left(a\log\frac ab-a+b\right)
 +\rho_y\left(b\log\frac ba-b+a\right)\\
&\qquad
=r_t(x)\Lambda(v_t(x),v_t(y))
 +r_t(y)\Lambda(v_t(y),v_t(x)).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the only Bregman algebra used in the proposition.  Hypothesis
\textup{(B3)} supplies an \(L^1([a,b]\times\RR^2)\) majorant for the last
display divided by \((x-y)^2\).  Therefore the two annular limits may be
combined and their cutoffs removed by dominated convergence.  Hence, for
the present time \(t\),
\[
-K'(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{r_t(x)\Lambda(v_t(x),v_t(y))
+r_t(y)\Lambda(v_t(y),v_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\]
Finally,
\[
\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{r_t(y)\Lambda(v_t(y),v_t(x))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
=
\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{r_t(x)\Lambda(v_t(x),v_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\]
after exchanging \(x\) and \(y\).  Therefore the symmetric coefficient
\(1/(2\pi)\) becomes the one-sided coefficient \(1/\pi\), proving
\eqref{eq:external-cauchy-kl-hardystein-input}.  The same majorant gives
\(\mathcal I\in L^1([a,b])\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cauchy cutoff estimates for bounded Lipschitz tests]%
\label{lem:kl-main-generator-convention-domain}
\label{lem:poisson-cauchy-generator-l1-domain}
\label{lem:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), let \(\mu\) be a finite signed measure on \(\RR\),
and set \(p_t=P_t*\mu\).  For \(R>1\) put
\[
(L_R f)(x):=\frac1\pi\int_{R^{-1}<|z|<R}
\frac{f(x+z)-f(x)}{z^2}\dd z,
\qquad
\Omega_R:=\{(x,y):R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-generator-annular-l1-estimate}
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t\|_1
\le
\frac{\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}{\pi R}
\left(\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1+4\right),
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-generator-annular-l1}
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t\|_1\longrightarrow0
\qquad(R\to\infty).
\end{equation}
Moreover, let \(q_t:\RR\to\RR\), \(t\in[a,b]\), be any family of bounded
Lipschitz functions satisfying
\[
M_*:=\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|q_t\|_\infty<\infty,\qquad
L_*:=\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\operatorname{Lip}(q_t)<\infty .
\]
For each \(t\in[a,b]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-finite}
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)L_Rp_t(x)\dd x
=-\frac1{2\pi}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\end{equation}
and
\begin{align}
&\sup_{\substack{t\in[a,b]\\R>1}}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\left|
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\right|\dd x\dd y \notag\\
&\qquad\le
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\left(2L_*\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_xP_s\|_1+8M_*\right).
\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-total-variation-bound}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{align}
&\sup_{t\in[a,b]}
\left|
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)\partial_tp_t(x)\dd x
+\frac1{2\pi}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\right| \notag\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{M_*\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}{\pi R}
\left(\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1+4\right),
\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-time-dependent-cutoff-error}
\end{align}
and therefore, uniformly for such a family \(q_t\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-limit}
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)\partial_tp_t(x)\dd x
=-\frac1{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\end{equation}
The same limit is independent of the annular exhaustion: if
\(0\le\chi_n\le1\) are measurable cutoffs with \(\chi_n(x,y)\to1\) for
\(x\ne y\), each \(\chi_n\) vanishing near the diagonal and outside a bounded
separation annulus, then for every \(t\in[a,b]\)
\[
\iint_{\RR^2}\chi_n(x,y)
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\longrightarrow
\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Fourier normalization is
\[
\widehat P_t(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|},\qquad
\widehat{\partial_tP_t}(\xi)=-|\xi|e^{-t|\xi|}.
\]
Thus \(\partial_tP_t=LP_t\), where
\[
(Lf)(x)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y .
\]
It remains to record the cutoff removal in \(L^1\).  Translation invariance
gives
\[
L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t=(L_RP_t-\partial_tP_t)*\mu .
\]
The near-diagonal omission is controlled by symmetric Taylor expansion:
for \(|z|<R^{-1}\),
\[
P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t(\cdot)
=z\,\partial_xP_t+\int_0^z(z-s)\partial_x^2P_t(\cdot+s)\dd s,
\]
and the odd first-order term integrates to zero over the symmetric annulus.
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-green-near-diagonal-bound}
\left\|\frac1\pi\int_{|z|<R^{-1}}
\frac{P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t(\cdot)}{z^2}\dd z\right\|_1
\le \frac{1}{\pi R}\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1 .
\end{equation}
The far-tail omission uses only \(\|P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t\|_1\le2\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-green-far-tail-bound}
\left\|\frac1\pi\int_{|z|>R}
\frac{P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t(\cdot)}{z^2}\dd z\right\|_1
\le \frac{4}{\pi R}.
\end{equation}
Combining the near and far estimates and convolving with \(\mu\) proves
\eqref{eq:poisson-generator-annular-l1-estimate}; in particular
\eqref{eq:poisson-generator-annular-l1} follows.

Fix \(t\) and write \(q=q_t\).  For bounded \(q\), Fubini is legitimate on
every fixed annulus because
\[
\int_{R^{-1}<|z|<R}\frac{\|q\|_\infty
\|p_t(\cdot+z)-p_t\|_1}{z^2}\dd z<\infty .
\]
Changing variables in one copy of the integral and symmetrizing in \(z\)
therefore give \eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-finite}.  The
Lipschitz hypothesis enters in the limiting annuli.  Let
\(M_q:=\|q\|_\infty\) and \(L_q:=\sup_{x\ne y}|q(x)-q(y)|/|x-y|\).  The
singular kernel is dominated, uniformly in \(R>1\), by
\begin{align}
&\iint_{\Omega_R}
\left|\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q(x)-q(y))}{(x-y)^2}\right|
\dd x\dd y \notag\\
&\qquad\le
\int_{|z|<1}
\frac{\|p_t(\cdot+z)-p_t\|_1\,L_q|z|}{z^2}\dd z
+2M_q\int_{|z|\ge1}
\frac{\|p_t(\cdot+z)-p_t\|_1}{z^2}\dd z \notag\\
&\qquad\le
2L_q\|\partial_xp_t\|_1+8M_q\|p_t\|_1 .
\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-singular-kernel-domination}
\end{align}
Here \(\|p_t(\cdot+z)-p_t\|_1\le |z|\|\partial_xp_t\|_1\) for
\(|z|<1\) and \(\|p_t(\cdot+z)-p_t\|_1\le2\|p_t\|_1\) for \(|z|\ge1\).
Using
\[
\|p_t\|_1\le\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}},\qquad
\|\partial_xp_t\|_1\le
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\|\partial_xP_t\|_1,
\]
and then taking the supremum over \(t\in[a,b]\) gives
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-total-variation-bound}.  This estimate
is the needed domination of the singular kernel for time-dependent bounded
noncompact Lipschitz tests.  Finally,
\[
\left|\int_{\RR}q_t\,(L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t)\dd x\right|
\le \|q_t\|_\infty\|L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t\|_1,
\]
and the explicit generator estimate
\eqref{eq:poisson-generator-annular-l1-estimate} give
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-time-dependent-cutoff-error}.  Letting
\(R\to\infty\) in the finite annular identity proves
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-limit}.  The full off-diagonal
kernel is absolutely integrable by
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-total-variation-bound}; dominated
convergence therefore gives the same value for every cutoff exhaustion
\(\chi_n\) described in the statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[\(L^1\) chain rule for a bounded moving quotient]%
\label{lem:poisson-kl-l1-ac-chain-rule}
Let \((X,\mathcal A,\mu)\) be a sigma-finite measure space and let
\(I=[a,b]\).  Let \(h,g:I\times X\to(0,\infty)\) be measurable functions
such that, for a.e. \(x\), \(t\mapsto h_t(x)\) and \(t\mapsto g_t(x)\) are
absolutely continuous on \(I\).  Assume
\[
h,g,\partial_th,\partial_tg\in L^1(I\times X),
\qquad
0<m\le u_t(x):=\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}\le M<\infty
\]
for a.e. \((t,x)\), and assume also that
\(\int_Xh_t\,\dd\mu=\int_Xg_t\,\dd\mu=1\) for every \(t\in I\).  Set
\[
H(t):=\int_X h_t(x)\log\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}\,\dd\mu(x).
\]
Then \(H\in AC(I)\), and for a.e. \(t\in I\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-moving-quotient-l1-chain-rule}
H'(t)=
\int_X \log u_t(x)\,\partial_th_t(x)\,\dd\mu(x)
-\int_X (u_t(x)-1)\,\partial_tg_t(x)\,\dd\mu(x).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(F(r,s)=r\log(r/s)\) on the cone
\(\{(r,s):s>0,\ m\le r/s\le M\}\).  On this cone,
\[
\partial_rF(r,s)=\log(r/s)+1,\qquad
\partial_sF(r,s)=-r/s,
\]
and these two partial derivatives are bounded there by a constant depending
only on \(m\) and \(M\).  Hence, for a.e. \(x\), the ordinary
one-dimensional chain rule gives
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}F(h_t(x),g_t(x))
=\bigl(\log u_t(x)+1\bigr)\partial_th_t(x)
-u_t(x)\partial_tg_t(x)
\]
for a.e. \(t\).

The same bounded-derivative estimate supplies an \(L^1\) majorant for time
increments.  More explicitly, if two points \((r_0,s_0)\) and \((r_1,s_1)\)
lie in the cone \(s>0\), \(m\le r/s\le M\), then the whole line segment lies
in the same cone, because its quotient is an \(s\)-weighted average of
\(r_0/s_0\) and \(r_1/s_1\).  Therefore, for \(s,t\in I\) and a.e. \(x\),
\[
\left|F(h_t(x),g_t(x))-F(h_s(x),g_s(x))\right|
\le C_{m,M}\left(|h_t(x)-h_s(x)|+|g_t(x)-g_s(x)|\right),
\]
and, using the absolute continuity of the pointwise paths,
\[
|h_t(x)-h_s(x)|+|g_t(x)-g_s(x)|
\le \int_s^t\left(|\partial_\tau h_\tau(x)|
                 +|\partial_\tau g_\tau(x)|\right)\dd\tau
\]
when \(s<t\).  Since \(\partial_th,\partial_tg\in L^1(I\times X)\),
Fubini implies that \(t\mapsto F(h_t,g_t)\) is absolutely continuous as an
\(L^1(X)\)-valued map and
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}H(t)
=\int_X\bigl(\log u_t+1\bigr)\partial_th_t\,\dd\mu
-\int_X u_t\,\partial_tg_t\,\dd\mu
\]
for a.e. \(t\in I\).

Finally \(h,g\in AC(I;L^1(X))\), because the same integral estimate applied to
\(h\) and \(g\) separately gives their \(L^1\)-absolute continuity.  Therefore
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int_X h_t\,\dd\mu=\int_X\partial_th_t\,\dd\mu,\qquad
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int_X g_t\,\dd\mu=\int_X\partial_tg_t\,\dd\mu
\]
for a.e. \(t\).  Since \(\int h_t=\int g_t=1\), the extra
\(\int\partial_th_t\) term vanishes, and subtracting
\(\int\partial_tg_t=0\) from the second term gives
\eqref{eq:kl-moving-quotient-l1-chain-rule}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Matched Poisson compact-window domain theorem]%
\label{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain}
\label{thm:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\)
with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and finite centred second moment
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^2\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
For
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t,
\qquad t\in[a,b],
\]
put
\[
m:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\qquad
M:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},\qquad
L:=3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr).
\]
Then, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x,y\in\RR\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML}
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L|x-y|.
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\log u_t\) is bounded and Lipschitz uniformly on
\([a,b]\).  Moreover, with
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\qquad
\Psi(z):=\mathbf1_{\{|z|<1\}}+|z|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|z|\ge1\}},
\]
and
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\},
\]
the one-sided logarithmic Bregman kernel satisfies, for \(x\ne y\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-one-sided-bregman-majorant}
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}\,g_t(x)\Psi(x-y),
\end{equation}
and the exchanged term satisfies the same estimate with \(x\) and \(y\)
interchanged.  Hence the symmetrized Bregman kernel in
Proposition~\ref{prop:abstract-cutoff-bregman-kl} is dominated on
\([a,b]\times\RR^2\) by the integrable majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-sym-bregman-integrable-majorant}
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y),
\end{equation}
whose integral over \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) is \(8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With \(z=(x-\bar{\gamma})/t\), \(X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}\), and
\[
R_\varepsilon(z):=\frac{1+z^2}{1+(z-\varepsilon)^2},
\]
one has
\[
u_t(x)=\int_{\RR}R_{X_c/t}\!\left(\frac{x-\bar{\gamma}}{t}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
The elementary inequalities
\[
R_\varepsilon(z)\le2(1+\varepsilon^2),\qquad
R_\varepsilon(z)\ge\frac{1}{2(1+\varepsilon^2)}
\]
give the displayed lower and upper bounds in
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML}, uniformly on
\([a,b]\times\RR\).  Indeed, the upper bound follows immediately from
\(\int X_c^2\dd\nu=\mu_2\).  For the lower bound, Jensen's inequality applied
to the convex function \(r\mapsto 1/r\) gives
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{1}{2(1+X_c^2/t^2)}\dd\nu(\gamma)
\ge
\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/t^2)}
\ge
\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)}.
\]
Also
\[
\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)
=\frac{2\varepsilon(1-w^2)-2\varepsilon^2w}{(1+w^2)^2},
\qquad w=z-\varepsilon.
\]
Since
\[
\sup_w\frac{2|1-w^2|}{(1+w^2)^2}\le2,\qquad
\sup_w\frac{2|w|}{(1+w^2)^2}\le1,
\]
we have
\[
|\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)|\le 2|\varepsilon|+\varepsilon^2
\le 3(1+\varepsilon^2).
\]
After dividing by \(t\) and using \(t\ge a\),
\[
|\partial_xu_t(x)|
\le \frac{3}{t}\int_{\RR}\left(1+\frac{X_c^2}{t^2}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma)
\le 3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr)=L.
\]
This proves the Lipschitz bound in
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML}.  Since
\((\log r)'=1/r\le1/m\) on \([m,M]\), the same bounds also make
\(\log u_t\) uniformly bounded and Lipschitz.

For fixed \(\beta\in[m,M]\), the function
\(\alpha\mapsto \Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\) has first derivative zero at
\(\alpha=\beta\) and second derivative \(1/\alpha\le1/m\) on \([m,M]\).
Taylor's formula therefore gives the compact-range quadratic estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-compact-quadratic-bregman}
0\le \Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\le \frac{1}{2m}|\alpha-\beta|^2
\qquad(\alpha,\beta\in[m,M]).
\end{equation}
Combining this with the Lipschitz estimate gives
\[
\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le \frac{L^2}{2m}|x-y|^2\qquad(|x-y|<1).
\]
The same quadratic estimate and the range bound
\(|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le M-m\) give
\[
\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le \frac{(M-m)^2}{2m}\qquad(|x-y|\ge1).
\]
Dividing by \((x-y)^2\) proves
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-one-sided-bregman-majorant}.  The exchanged term is
identical after switching \(x\) and \(y\).  Finally
\[
\int_{\RR}\Psi(z)\dd z
=\int_{|z|<1}\dd z+\int_{|z|\ge1}|z|^{-2}\dd z=4,
\]
and \(\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\dd x=1\), so
\[
\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}
\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y)\dd x\dd y\dd t
=8(b-a)<\infty .
\]
This proves the stated integrable majorant.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Consolidated compact-window Bregman constants]%
\label{lem:single-compact-window-bregman-majorant-constants}
Under the hypotheses and notation of
Theorem~\ref{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain}, define
\[
\Phi(t,x,y):=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2},&x\ne y,\\[1.2ex]
0,&x=y .
\end{cases}
\]
Then the one-sided kernel satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-lemma-one-sided-pointwise}
0\le\Phi(t,x,y)\le C_{a,b,\nu}g_t(x)\Psi(x-y)
\qquad(t\in[a,b],\ x,y\in\RR),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-lemma-psi-mass}
\int_{\RR}\Psi(z)\dd z=4 .
\end{equation}
Consequently, if
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t):=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}\Phi(t,x,y)\dd x\dd y
\]
is defined by the nonnegative Lebesgue integral, with the diagonal ignored,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-lemma-one-sided-majorant-integral}
\int_a^b\mathcal I_\nu(t)\dd t
\le \frac{1}{\pi}\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}
C_{a,b,\nu}g_t(x)\Psi(x-y)\dd x\dd y\dd t
=\frac{4C_{a,b,\nu}}{\pi}(b-a).
\end{equation}
The same estimate with \(x\) and \(y\) interchanged controls
\(g_t(y)\Lambda(u_t(y),u_t(x))/(x-y)^2\).  Thus the symmetrized
kernel
\[
\Phi(t,x,y)+\Phi(t,y,x)
=\frac{
g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)\Lambda(u_t(y),u_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}
\]
used in the cutoff Green formula is dominated by
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y),
\]
and this majorant has the compact-window integral
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-lemma-sym-bregman-integral}
\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y)\dd x\dd y\dd t
=8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a).
\end{equation}
Hence the one-sided dissipation estimate carries the factor \(1/\pi\),
whereas the displayed \(8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\) is only the integral of the
two-sided Green-formula majorant before the Green prefactor is applied.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The pointwise one-sided estimate is
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-one-sided-bregman-majorant}.  Since
\(\int_\RR g_t=1\) and
\[
\int_{\RR}\Psi(z)\dd z
=\int_{|z|<1}\dd z+\int_{|z|\ge1}|z|^{-2}\dd z=4,
\]
we get \eqref{eq:single-lemma-psi-mass}.  Tonelli's theorem and
\(\int_\RR g_t=1\) give
\[
\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}
C_{a,b,\nu}g_t(x)\Psi(x-y)\dd x\dd y\dd t
=4C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:single-lemma-one-sided-majorant-integral}.  The
exchanged estimate is identical after switching \(x\) and \(y\), so the
two copies of the same computation give
\eqref{eq:single-lemma-sym-bregman-integral}.
\end{proof}

The next theorem adds only the cutoff calculus to the preceding domain theorem:
the quotient bounds and the Bregman domination are those of
Theorem~\ref{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain}, while the
remaining assertions are the \(L^1\) chain rule and the annular Green
formulae needed to identify the derivative of the KL functional.

\begin{theorem}[Compact-window domain theorem for the Cauchy Bregman identity]%
\label{thm:kl-domain-verification}
\label{lem:poisson-quotient-cutoff-bregman-verification}
\label{lem:kl-tail-chain-rule-cutoff-passage}
\label{prop:kl-tail-cutoff-inputs}
\label{thm:kl-local-doob-bregman-domain-closure}
\label{prop:kl-local-doob-bregman-domain-closure}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\).  Let
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t
\]
on \(t\in[a,b]\), where \(\nu\) is a probability measure with mean
\(\bar{\gamma}\) and finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^2\dd\nu(\gamma),
\qquad
H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t),
\]
and
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t):=\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{
g_t(x)\left(
u_t(x)\log\!\bigl(u_t(x)/u_t(y)\bigr)-u_t(x)+u_t(y)
\right)}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item With
\[
m:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\qquad
M:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},\qquad
L:=3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr),
\]
the matched quotient has the compact-window range and Lipschitz bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domain-theorem-quotient-mml}
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L|x-y|
\end{equation}
for every \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x,y\in\RR\).  Consequently
\(\log u_t\) is bounded and Lipschitz uniformly on \([a,b]\).

\item With
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\qquad
\Psi(z):=\mathbf1_{\{|z|<1\}}+|z|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|z|\ge1\}},
\]
and
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\},
\]
the one-sided Bregman majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domain-theorem-one-sided-bregman-majorant}
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}g_t(x)\Psi(x-y)
\end{equation}
holds for \(x\ne y\), and the same bound holds with \(x\) and \(y\)
interchanged.  Hence the symmetrized kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domain-theorem-sym-bregman-kernel}
\frac{
g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)\Lambda(u_t(y),u_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}
\end{equation}
is dominated on \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) by the integrable majorant
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y),
\]
whose integral over \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) is \(8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\).
Equivalently, Lemma~\ref{lem:single-compact-window-bregman-majorant-constants}
records in one place that the one-sided kernel defining
\(\mathcal I_\nu\) has the compact-window estimate
\[
\int_a^b\mathcal I_\nu(t)\dd t
\le \frac{4C_{a,b,\nu}}{\pi}(b-a),
\]
while the separate symmetrized Green-formula majorant integrates to
\(8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\) before any Green prefactor is applied.

\item \(H\in AC([a,b])\) and satisfies the quotient chain rule
\[
H'(t)=
\int_{\RR}\log u_t\,\partial_th_t\,\dd x
-\int_{\RR}(u_t-1)\partial_tg_t\,\dd x
\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in[a,b].
\]

\item For a.e. \(t\in[a,b]\), both annular Cauchy Green formulae hold:
\begin{align}
\int_{\RR}\log u_t(x)\,\partial_th_t(x)\dd x
&=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(h_t(x)-h_t(y))(\log u_t(x)-\log u_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y, \label{eq:domain-theorem-h-logu-green}\\
\int_{\RR}u_t(x)\,\partial_tg_t(x)\dd x
&=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(g_t(x)-g_t(y))(u_t(x)-u_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y, \label{eq:domain-theorem-g-u-green}
\end{align}
where \(\Omega_R=\{(x,y):R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}\).  More precisely, put
\[
K_1:=\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_xP_s\|_1,\qquad
K_2:=\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1,\qquad
M_{\log}:=\max\{|\log m|,|\log M|\}.
\]
Then, for every \(R>1\),
\begin{align}
&\sup_{t\in[a,b]}
\left|
\int_{\RR}\log u_t\,\partial_th_t\,\dd x
+\frac1{2\pi}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(h_t(x)-h_t(y))(\log u_t(x)-\log u_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\right| \notag\\
&\qquad\le \frac{M_{\log}}{\pi R}(K_2+4),
\label{eq:domain-logu-cutoff-error}\\
&\sup_{t\in[a,b]}
\left|
\int_{\RR}u_t\,\partial_tg_t\,\dd x
+\frac1{2\pi}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(g_t(x)-g_t(y))(u_t(x)-u_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\right| \notag\\
&\qquad\le \frac{M}{\pi R}(K_2+4).
\label{eq:domain-u-cutoff-error}
\end{align}
The corresponding annular integrands are uniformly absolutely controlled by
\begin{align}
&\sup_{\substack{t\in[a,b]\\R>1}}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\left|
\frac{(h_t(x)-h_t(y))(\log u_t(x)-\log u_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}
\right|\dd x\dd y
\le 2(L/m)K_1+8M_{\log}, \label{eq:domain-logu-annular-tv-bound}\\
&\sup_{\substack{t\in[a,b]\\R>1}}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\left|
\frac{(g_t(x)-g_t(y))(u_t(x)-u_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}
\right|\dd x\dd y
\le 2LK_1+8M . \label{eq:domain-u-annular-tv-bound}
\end{align}
Consequently these two limits are unchanged if the symmetric annuli are
replaced by any cofinal annular exhaustion.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently \(\mathcal I_\nu\in L^1([a,b])\) and
\[
H'(t)=-\mathcal I_\nu(t)\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in[a,b].
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} gives the
compact quotient range, the uniform Lipschitz bounds for \(u_t\) and
\(\log u_t\), and the integrable majorant for the symmetrized Bregman kernel.
This proves \textup{(i)} and \textup{(ii)}, with
\eqref{eq:domain-theorem-quotient-mml} repeating
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML} and
\eqref{eq:domain-theorem-one-sided-bregman-majorant} repeating
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-one-sided-bregman-majorant}.

Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing}, applied first to
\((p_t,q_t)=(h_t,\log u_t)\) with \(\mu=\nu\), and then to
\((p_t,q_t)=(g_t,u_t)\) with \(\mu=\delta_{\bar{\gamma}}\), gives the two
annular Green pairings \eqref{eq:domain-theorem-h-logu-green} and
\eqref{eq:domain-theorem-g-u-green}.  Here
\(\|\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=\|\delta_{\bar{\gamma}}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=1\),
\[
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|\log u_t\|_\infty=M_{\log},\qquad
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\operatorname{Lip}(\log u_t)\le L/m,
\]
and
\[
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|u_t\|_\infty\le M,\qquad
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\operatorname{Lip}(u_t)\le L.
\]
The explicit estimates
\eqref{eq:domain-logu-cutoff-error}--\eqref{eq:domain-u-annular-tv-bound}
are exactly
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-time-dependent-cutoff-error} and
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-total-variation-bound} with these
constants.  The annular-exhaustion independence in \textup{(iv)} is the
cutoff-independence conclusion of the same lemma.  The same lemma also fixes
the Cauchy-generator convention
\[
(Lf)(x)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y,\qquad \partial_tP_t=LP_t,
\]
and supplies the cutoff convergence in \(L^1\).  This proves
\textup{(iv)}.

The maps \(t\mapsto h_t,g_t,\partial_th_t,\partial_tg_t\) are locally
absolutely continuous in \(L^1\), since
\(\partial_t^kP_t(x)=t^{-k-1}Q_k(x/t)\), \(k=0,1,2\), with
\(Q_k\in L^1(\RR)\).  In particular
\(h,g,\partial_th,\partial_tg\in L^1([a,b]\times\RR)\), and the Poisson
kernels are pointwise \(C^1\) in \(t\).  Applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-kl-l1-ac-chain-rule} on the compact quotient range
from Theorem~\ref{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} gives
\(H\in AC([a,b])\) and the displayed formula in \textup{(iii)}.

The three hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:abstract-cutoff-bregman-kl}
are now verified for \((p_t,r_t,v_t)=(h_t,g_t,u_t)\): the chain rule is
\textup{(iii)}, the cutoff Green pairings are \textup{(iv)}, and Bregman
domination is \textup{(ii)}.  Applying that proposition gives
\(\mathcal I_\nu\in L^1([a,b])\) and \(H'=-\mathcal I_\nu\) a.e.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The KL tail identity has two logically separate inputs.  The local input is
the compact-window domain theorem
\(H'(t)=-\mathcal I_\nu(t)\), Theorem~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification},
which includes the annular cutoff passage and the explicit Bregman majorant.
The infinite-tail input is the independent second-moment decay
\(H(T)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_T\|g_T)\to0\) from
Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-second-moment-kl-decay}.  The passage to
\([t,\infty)\) below uses only these two facts and monotone convergence; it
does not use any additional cancellation in the singular double integral.

\begin{unnumberedlemma}[Annular construction of the Bregman dissipation]
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\)
and finite centred second moment, and keep
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t .
\]
For \(t>0\) and \(R>1\), set
\[
\mathcal I_{\nu,R}(t):=
\frac1\pi\iint_{\{R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y,
\qquad
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta .
\]
Then the limit
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t):=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal I_{\nu,R}(t)
\]
exists in \([0,\infty)\), is independent of the annular exhaustion, and is a
measurable locally integrable function on \((0,\infty)\).  Equivalently,
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t)=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\]
as a Lebesgue integral of the nonnegative kernel, with the diagonal value
ignored.
\end{unnumberedlemma}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
\Phi(t,x,y):=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2},&x\ne y,\\[1.2ex]
0,&x=y .
\end{cases}
\]
The Poisson kernels are continuous and strictly positive, hence
\((t,x,y)\mapsto\Phi(t,x,y)\) is Borel measurable and nonnegative.  For fixed
\(t\), the annuli
\(\Omega_R=\{(x,y):R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}\) increase to
\(\RR^2\setminus\{x=y\}\).  Therefore
\(\mathcal I_{\nu,R}(t)\) is increasing in \(R\), and monotone convergence
gives
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal I_{\nu,R}(t)
=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}\Phi(t,x,y)\dd x\dd y .
\]
The same argument shows that every cofinal annular exhaustion gives this
same value.  More generally, if \(0\le\chi_n\le1\) are measurable cutoffs
which vanish near the diagonal and at spatial infinity and satisfy
  \(\chi_n(x,y)\to1\) for \(x\ne y\), then the increasing envelopes
  \(\widehat\chi_n:=\sup_{k\le n}\chi_k\) give the same limit by monotone
  convergence; if the original cutoffs are dominated by an annular envelope,
  the convergence also holds for \(\chi_n\) itself by dominated convergence on
  each compact time window, using the majorant below.  Thus the definition is
  independent of the annular cutoff convention used in the Green formulae.

Measurability follows from the representation
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t)=\sup_{n\ge2}\frac1\pi\iint_{\Omega_n}\Phi(t,x,y)\dd x\dd y,
\]
because each finite-annulus integral is measurable by Tonelli.  Finally fix
\(0<a<b<\infty\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:single-compact-window-bregman-majorant-constants}
gives the one-sided bound
\eqref{eq:single-lemma-one-sided-majorant-integral}.  Thus
\(\mathcal I_\nu\in L^1([a,b])\), and the same pointwise majorant in that
lemma gives \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)<\infty\) for every \(t\in[a,b]\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Poisson quotient KL tail identity]%
\label{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail}
\label{cor:poisson-kl-doob-bregman-tail}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar{\gamma}$ and finite
centred second moment.  Put
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t,
\]
and let \(\mathcal I_\nu\) be the locally integrable Bregman dissipation
constructed above.  The domain estimates are part of the theorem: for every
\(0<a<b<\infty\), with
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^2\dd\nu(\gamma),\qquad
m=\frac1{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\quad
M=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},\quad
L=3a^{-1}(1+\mu_2/a^2),
\]
one has, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x,y\in\RR\),
\[
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad |u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L|x-y|.
\]
Moreover, for
\begin{align*}
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\qquad&
\Psi(z)=\mathbf1_{\{|z|<1\}}+|z|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|z|\ge1\}},\\
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\},
\end{align*}
one has, for \(x\ne y\) and \(t\in[a,b]\),
\[
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}g_t(x)\Psi(x-y).
\]
The same constants also give the symmetrized estimate
\[
\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{
g_s(x)\Lambda(u_s(x),u_s(y))
+g_s(y)\Lambda(u_s(y),u_s(x))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y\dd s
\le 8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a),
\]
and the one-sided dissipation estimate
\[
\int_a^b\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
\le \frac{4C_{a,b,\nu}}{\pi}(b-a).
\]
Consequently, for every \(t>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set \(H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\).  On every compact interval
\([a,b]\subset(0,\infty)\),
Theorem~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification} gives
\[
m\le u_s(x)\le M,\qquad |u_s(x)-u_s(y)|\le L|x-y|
\]
with the displayed values of \(m,M,L\), the one-sided Bregman domination
displayed in the theorem, \(H\in AC([a,b])\), \(\mathcal I_\nu\in L^1([a,b])\),
and
\[
H'(s)=-\mathcal I_\nu(s)\qquad\text{for a.e. }s\in[a,b].
\]
This proves the compact-window domain-to-derivative step.  Hence, for
\(0<t<T\),
\[
\int_t^T \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s=H(t)-H(T).
\]
The integrand \(\mathcal I_\nu\) is nonnegative because
\(\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=
\alpha\log(\alpha/\beta)-\alpha+\beta\ge0\).  Therefore the finite-window
integrals increase with \(T\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-second-moment-kl-decay}
gives the separate endpoint decay \(H(T)\to0\) as \(T\to\infty\), and
monotone convergence gives
\[
\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_t^T \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=H(t).
\]
This is the promised three-step chain:
compact-window domain gives \(H'=-\mathcal I_\nu\), endpoint decay gives
\(H(T)\to0\), and monotone convergence sends \([t,T]\) to \([t,\infty)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No pointwise conclusion from finite moments alone]
\label{rem:no-pointwise-dissipation-from-finite-moments}
The coefficient transfer below concerns only tail integrals
\(\int_t^\infty\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s\) and fixed multiplicative-window
averages.  It does not assert a pointwise expansion of
\(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\).  A tail expansion for the primitive
\(H(t)=\int_t^\infty\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s\), even with a full finite-moment
coefficient hierarchy, does not by itself justify differentiating the
asymptotic series.  Pointwise asymptotics for \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\) require
extra differentiated-remainder input.

For example, if the centred law is compactly supported, say
\(|\gamma-\bar{\gamma}|\le M\), then for each fixed retained order \(L\) and
all \(t>2M\) the Cayley generating function gives the uniformly convergent
expansion, with \(\mu_n=\EE(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^n\),
\[
\frac{(P_t*\nu)(\bar{\gamma}+ty)}{P_t(ty)}-1
=\sum_{n=2}^{L}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}+\rho_{L,t}(y),
\qquad
\rho_{L,t}=\sum_{n>L}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}.
\]
The same uniform majorant gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^\infty}
+\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
+\|t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^\infty}
+\|t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
=O_\nu(t^{-(L+1)}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Under such an additional differentiated remainder bound, one may
differentiate the finite quotient expansion and combine it with
\eqref{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation}; this auxiliary
observation is not part of the finite-moment coefficient hierarchy.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Dissipation-tail and window-average coefficient transfer]%
\label{prop:dissipation-tail-coefficient-expansion}
Let \(N\ge2\), and assume that \(\nu\) has finite centred
\((2N-2)\)th absolute moment.  Then the KL dissipation tail has the same
universal coefficient polynomial layer as the entropy expansion:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-expansion}
\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N})
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for every \(2\le M\le N\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-recovery}
A_{2M}(\nu)
=
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{2M}
\left(
\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
-\sum_{m=2}^{M-1}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
\right),
\end{equation}
with the sum interpreted as zero when \(M=2\).

More locally, for each fixed \(\lambda>1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-finite-window-coefficients}
\int_t^{\lambda t} \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)(1-\lambda^{-2m})t^{-2m}
+o(t^{-2N}).
\end{equation}
Thus the averaged dissipation on a multiplicative window satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-window-average-coefficients}
\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)t}\int_t^{\lambda t} \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}
\frac{1-\lambda^{-2m}}{\lambda-1}\,A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-(2m+1)}
+o(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\end{equation}
If, in addition, \(0<\eta\le1\) and the centred
\((2N-2+\eta)\)th absolute moment is finite, then the error terms above have
the following positive-margin bounds:
\[
\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+O_\nu(t^{-(2N+\eta)})
\]
and, for each fixed \(\lambda>1\),
\[
\int_t^{\lambda t} \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)(1-\lambda^{-2m})t^{-2m}
 +O_{\nu,N,\eta,\lambda}(t^{-(2N+\eta)}).
\]
Consequently the averaged-window remainder is
\[
O_{\nu,N,\eta,\lambda}(t^{-(2N+1+\eta)}).
\]
No pointwise expansion of \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\) is asserted.

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is a coefficient transfer, not an additional Hardy--Stein theorem.
The tail identity \eqref{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation} identifies
\(\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu\) with \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\).  Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} gives
\eqref{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-expansion} and hence the coefficient
recovery formula \eqref{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-recovery}.

For the finite window, use the already proved tail identity twice:
\[
H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t),
\qquad
\int_t^{\lambda t}\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s=H(t)-H(\lambda t).
\]
Substitute the expansion of \(H\) at \(t\) and at \(\lambda t\).  The
coefficient of \(t^{-2m}\) is
\(A_{2m}(\nu)-A_{2m}(\nu)\lambda^{-2m}\), which gives
\eqref{eq:bregman-finite-window-coefficients}; division by
\((\lambda-1)t\) gives \eqref{eq:bregman-window-average-coefficients}.
The positive-margin assertion follows from the explicit remainder estimate
\eqref{eq:positive-margin-retained-order-theorem} for \(H(t)\) and
\(H(\lambda t)\).
\end{proof}
