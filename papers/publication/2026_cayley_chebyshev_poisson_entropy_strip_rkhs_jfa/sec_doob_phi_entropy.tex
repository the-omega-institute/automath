\section{KL Tail Identity for the Poisson Doob Quotient}%
\label{sec:doob-phi-entropy}

Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\) with mean \(\bar\gamma\).
For \(t>0\) put
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma),\qquad
u_t:=h_t/g_t .
\]
The Cauchy generator is normalized by
\[
(Lf)(x)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y,
\]
so that \(\partial_tP_t=LP_t\).  This section proves, in the Cauchy
normalization displayed above, the KL-specialized two-point Bregman
dissipation identity for the moving Poisson quotient \(u_t=h_t/g_t\), and
then records the coefficient consequence.
The calculation belongs to the Hardy--Stein/Bregman lineage.  The classical
local identity is the chain-rule/Green-formula Hardy--Stein identity for the
Laplacian \cite[pp.~86--88]{Stein1970SingularIntegrals}; the
convex-divergence language starts with Bregman's
construction~\cite{Bregman1967}.  For jump semigroups, the nonlocal
Hardy--Stein identity and Sobolev--Bregman forms were developed in
\cite{BanuelosBogdanLuks2016HardyStein,
BanuelosKim2019HardySteinNonsymmetric,
BogdanGrzywnyPietruskaPalubaRutkowski2023NonlinearNonlocalDouglas,
Gutowski2023HardySteinPureJump,GutowskiKwasnicki2025BeurlingDenySobolevBregman}.
The Beurling--Deny Sobolev--Bregman formula of
Gutowski--Kwa\'snicki is cited here in its published
\emph{Nonlinear Analysis} form, with DOI and arXiv identifier recorded in the
bibliography.  In conceptual terms, the present identity is the
Cauchy-generator, relative-entropy specialization of the polarized
Hardy--Stein/Sobolev--Bregman identities for Markov semigroups: the convex
function is
\(\Phi(r)=r\log r\), the two-point co-divergence is
\(\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\log(\alpha/\beta)-\alpha+\beta\), and the
reference density is the moving Poisson profile \(g_t\).  The general
polarized Bregman framework is due to
Bogdan--Gutowski--Pietruska-Pa{\l}uba~\cite{BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein}.
No abstract theorem is imported as a black box in the proof below.  The
external papers explain the provenance of the identity; the operative
Cauchy/KL step is Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-kl-cutoff-green-identity}, which is
proved directly from the \(L^1\) chain rule, the annular Cauchy Green formula,
and a one-line logarithmic Bregman algebra.  Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification}
then checks, for the nonstationary Cauchy pair
\((h_t,g_t)=(P_t*\nu,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))\), every hypothesis used by that
cutoff lemma: generator convention, quotient bounds, cutoff convergence, and
Bregman domination.
The coefficient expansion itself is proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} by the Cayley quotient-mode Taylor
calculus; the Doob identity below supplies an equivalent dissipation-tail
representation after that coefficient theorem is known.

\begin{lemma}[Second-moment KL decay to the translated Poisson profile]%
\label{lem:poisson-second-moment-kl-decay}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean $\bar\gamma$ and
finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar\gamma).
\]
Then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma$ and $x=\bar\gamma+ty$.  Since
$g_t(\bar\gamma+ty)=(\pi t)^{-1}(1+y^2)^{-1}$,
\[
u_t(\bar\gamma+ty):=\frac{h_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}{g_t(\bar\gamma+ty)}
=\int_{\RR}\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
For $A>0$ decompose $\nu=\nu_A+\nu_A^c$ according to
$|X_c|\le A$ and $|X_c|>A$.  The elementary kernel bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-moment-kl-kernel-bound}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
\le 2(1+\varepsilon^2)
\qquad(y,\varepsilon\in\RR)
\end{equation}
implies
\[
0\le u_{t,A}^c(y):=\int_{|X_c|>A}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
\le 2\int_{|X_c|>A}\left(1+\frac{X_c^2}{t^2}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
Hence, for $t\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-moment-kl-tail-sup}
\sup_y u_{t,A}^c(y)
\le 2\nu(|X_c|>A)+2\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu .
\end{equation}

The bounded part has total mass $m_A:=\nu(|X_c|\le A)$ and first centred
moment $b_A:=\int_{|X_c|\le A}X_c\dd\nu$.  The exact expansion
\[
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
=1+\frac{2y}{1+y^2}\varepsilon
+\frac{(3y^2-1)\varepsilon^2-2y\varepsilon^3}
{(1+y^2)(1+(y-\varepsilon)^2)}
\]
shows that, for each fixed $A$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-truncated-quotient-limit}
u_{t,A}(y):=\int_{|X_c|\le A}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
=m_A+\frac{2yb_A}{t(1+y^2)}+O_A(t^{-2})
\end{equation}
uniformly in~$y$.  Since
$b_A=-\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c\dd\nu$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
|b_A|\le \left(\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Combining this with
\eqref{eq:second-moment-kl-tail-sup} and
\eqref{eq:bounded-truncated-quotient-limit} yields
\[
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|u_t-1\|_{L^\infty(\RR)}
\le 1-m_A+2\nu(|X_c|>A)+2\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu .
\]
Letting $A\to\infty$ gives $\|u_t-1\|_\infty\to0$.

For all sufficiently large $t$ we therefore have
$|u_t-1|\le1/2$.  With $\delta_t=u_t-1$ and using
$\int g_t\delta_t=\int(h_t-g_t)=0$,
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\int_{\RR}g_t\bigl((1+\delta_t)\log(1+\delta_t)-\delta_t\bigr)\dd x
\le 2\|\delta_t\|_\infty^2,
\]
because $(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\le2s^2$ for $|s|\le1/2$.  The right-hand side
tends to zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[\(L^1\) Cauchy generator domain for Poisson smoothings]%
\label{lem:poisson-cauchy-generator-l1-domain}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), let \(\mu\) be a finite signed Borel measure on
\(\RR\), and set \(p_t=P_t*\mu\).  For \(R>1\) define
\[
(L_Rp)(x):=\frac1\pi\int_{R^{-1}<|z|<R}
\frac{p(x+z)-p(x)}{z^2}\dd z .
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-cauchy-generator-l1-domain}
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t\|_{L^1(\RR)}
\longrightarrow0\qquad(R\to\infty).
\end{equation}
Consequently, if \(q_t\) is uniformly bounded on
\([a,b]\times\RR\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-cauchy-generator-bounded-pairing}
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\left|
\int_{\RR}q_tL_Rp_t\,\dd x
-\int_{\RR}q_t\,\partial_tp_t\,\dd x
\right|\longrightarrow0 .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Cauchy kernel is translation invariant, hence
\[
L_R(P_t*\mu)-\partial_t(P_t*\mu)
=(L_RP_t-\partial_tP_t)*\mu .
\]
Young's inequality reduces \eqref{eq:poisson-cauchy-generator-l1-domain} to
the case \(\mu=\delta_0\), up to the factor \(\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\).  For
the Poisson kernel, \(\widehat P_t(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|}\), and therefore the
principal-value Cauchy generator \(L\) satisfies \(LP_t=\partial_tP_t\).
Thus \(L_RP_t-\partial_tP_t\) is the sum of the omitted near-zero and
far-field parts of the principal-value integral.

For the near-zero part, the symmetric annulus cancels the odd linear term:
for each \(z\),
\[
P_t(x+z)-P_t(x)=z\,\partial_xP_t(x)
+z^2\int_0^1(1-\theta)\partial_x^2P_t(x+\theta z)\dd\theta .
\]
After integration over \(|z|<R^{-1}\), the \(z\,\partial_xP_t(x)\) term
vanishes.  Hence
\[
\left\|\frac1\pi\int_{|z|<R^{-1}}
\frac{P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t(\cdot)}{z^2}\dd z\right\|_1
\le \frac{2}{\pi R}\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1 .
\]
The supremum is finite because \(t\ge a>0\).  For the far-field part,
\[
\left\|\frac1\pi\int_{|z|>R}
\frac{P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t(\cdot)}{z^2}\dd z\right\|_1
\le \frac{2\|P_t\|_1}{\pi}\int_{|z|>R}\frac{\dd z}{z^2}
=\frac{4}{\pi R}.
\]
Both bounds are uniform for \(t\in[a,b]\), proving
\eqref{eq:poisson-cauchy-generator-l1-domain}.  The pairing convergence
\eqref{eq:poisson-cauchy-generator-bounded-pairing} follows by multiplying by
\(\sup_{t,x}|q_t(x)|\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bounded-Lipschitz cutoff pairing for Poisson smoothings]%
\label{lem:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), let \(p_t=P_t*\mu\) with \(\mu\) a finite signed
Borel measure, and define \(L_R\) as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-cauchy-generator-l1-domain}.  If \(q\) is bounded and
Lipschitz on \(\RR\), then, for every \(t\in[a,b]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing}
\int_{\RR}q(x)\partial_tp_t(x)\dd x
=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q(x)-q(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\end{equation}
where \(\Omega_R=\{(x,y):R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}\).  The truncated bilinear forms
are absolutely convergent.  If \(q_t\) is a family with uniformly bounded
\(\|q_t\|_\infty+\operatorname{Lip}(q_t)\) on \([a,b]\), the same convergence
is uniform for \(t\in[a,b]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(B_R(p,q)\) for the double integral in
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing}.  For a Poisson smoothing
\(p=p_t\) one has \(p,\partial_xp\in L^1(\RR)\), uniformly for
\(t\in[a,b]\).  In the variable \(z=y-x\),
\[
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\left|\frac{(p(x)-p(y))(q(x)-q(y))}{(x-y)^2}\right|\dd x\dd y
\]
is bounded by
\[
\int_{|z|<1}
\frac{\|p(\cdot+z)-p\|_1\operatorname{Lip}(q)|z|}{z^2}\dd z
+4\|p\|_1\|q\|_\infty\int_{|z|\ge1}\frac{\dd z}{z^2}.
\]
Since \(\|p(\cdot+z)-p\|_1\le |z|\|\partial_xp\|_1\), this gives absolute
convergence, with a bound depending only on the displayed uniform norms on a
compact time interval.

For \(q\in C_c^\infty(\RR)\), Fubini on the finite annulus gives
\[
\int_{\RR}q\,L_Rp\,\dd x=-\frac1{2\pi}B_R(p,q).
\]
For a bounded Lipschitz \(q\), choose an even mollifier \(\rho_\varepsilon\)
and a cutoff \(\chi\in C_c^\infty(\RR)\) with \(\chi=1\) on \([-1,1]\), and
put
\[
q_{S,\varepsilon}:=\rho_\varepsilon*(\chi(\,\cdot\,/S)q).
\]
The functions \(q_{S,\varepsilon}\) are smooth and compactly supported,
bounded uniformly by \(\|q\|_\infty\), and have Lipschitz constants bounded in
terms of \(\operatorname{Lip}(q)\), \(\|q\|_\infty\), and \(\chi\), but not
\(\varepsilon\).  The majorant above therefore permits
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and then \(S\to\infty\) in the bilinear side.  The
same two limits on the single integral side follow from
\(L_Rp\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}\) on each fixed annulus and from the far-field
bound already used above.  Hence the finite-annulus identity holds for the
bounded Lipschitz test \(q\).

Finally, Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-cauchy-generator-l1-domain} gives
\[
\int q\,L_Rp_t\dd x\longrightarrow \int q\,\partial_tp_t\dd x
\]
in the sense of the \(L^1\) generator-domain limit, because multiplication by
a bounded \(q\) is continuous on \(L^1\).  This identifies the cutoff bilinear
limit with the distributional generator pairing and proves
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing}.  The same estimates give
the stated uniform version.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The following lemma is the consolidated domain verification used in the KL
tail identity.  It packages the cutoff generator convergence, the
bounded-Lipschitz Green formula, the quotient range, and the Bregman
domination in one place, with all constants uniform on a fixed compact time
interval.

\begin{lemma}[Quotient domain and cutoff calculus]%
\label{thm:kl-domain-verification}
\label{lem:kl-tail-chain-rule-cutoff-passage}
\label{prop:kl-tail-cutoff-inputs}
\label{thm:kl-local-doob-bregman-domain-closure}
\label{prop:kl-local-doob-bregman-domain-closure}
\label{lem:kl-main-generator-convention-domain}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\) with
mean~\(\bar\gamma\), put \(X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma\), assume
\(\EE|X_c|^2<\infty\), and put
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t,
\qquad
H(t)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t).
\]
For \(R>1\) define the symmetric Cauchy cutoff
\[
(L_Rp)(x):=\frac1\pi\int_{R^{-1}<|x-y|<R}
\frac{p(y)-p(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y ,
\qquad
\Omega_R:=\{(x,y):R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}.
\]
Then there exist \(0<m<M<\infty\) and \(L<\infty\) such that
\[
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad |u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L|x-y|
\]
for \(x,y\in\RR\) and \(t\in[a,b]\).  Moreover, for \(p_t=P_t*\mu\) with finite signed
\(\mu\),
\[
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|L_Rp_t-\partial_t p_t\|_1\to0,
\]
and for every family \(q_t\) with uniformly bounded sup norm and Lipschitz
constant on \([a,b]\),
\[
\int q_t\partial_t p_t =
-\frac1{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\,dx\,dy .
\]
Finally, with
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta ,
\]
\[
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le Cg_t(x)\min\{1,|x-y|^{-2}\}.
\]
All limits in this paragraph are uniform for \(t\in[a,b]\), and the last
bound is used together with the identical estimate obtained by exchanging
\(x\) and \(y\).

More precisely, the following facts hold on the fixed compact time interval
\([a,b]\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item The generator convention is
\[
(Lf)(x)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y,\qquad \widehat{Lf}(\xi)=-|\xi|\widehat f(\xi),
\]
so \(\partial_tP_t=LP_t\).  If \(p_t=P_t*\mu\), where \(\mu\) is a finite
signed Borel measure, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-consolidated-generator-l1}
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t\|_{L^1(\RR)}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(R\to\infty).
\end{equation}
Consequently every uniformly bounded family \(q_t\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-consolidated-bounded-pairing}
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\left|
\int_{\RR}q_t L_Rp_t-\int_{\RR}q_t\,\partial_tp_t
\right|\longrightarrow0 .
\end{equation}
\item For \(p\in\{h_t,g_t\}\) and for every bounded Lipschitz \(q\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-consolidated-pv-pairing}
\int_{\RR}q(x)\partial_tp(x)\dd x
=
-\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p(x)-p(y))(q(x)-q(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\end{equation}
The truncated bilinear forms are absolutely convergent, and the displayed
pairing is the bounded-Lipschitz cutoff closure of the same
principal-value generator.
\item The quotient domain is explicit.  With
\(X_c=\gamma-\bar\gamma\), \(z=(x-\bar\gamma)/t\), and
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\dd\nu(\gamma),
\]
\[
R_\varepsilon(z):=\frac{1+z^2}{1+(z-\varepsilon)^2},
\]
one has
\[
u_t(x)=\int_{\RR}R_{X_c/t}\!\left(\frac{x-\bar\gamma}{t}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma),
\]
and
\[
\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)}
\le u_t(x)\le 2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},
\qquad
|\partial_xu_t(x)|\le
\frac{3}{a}\left(1+\frac{\mu_2}{a^2}\right).
\]
\item With
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta ,
\]
there is \(C_{a,b,\nu}<\infty\) such that, for \(x\ne y\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-consolidated-bregman-majorant}
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}g_t(x)\min\{1,|x-y|^{-2}\},
\end{equation}
and the same estimate holds after exchanging \(x\) and \(y\).
\item The Bregman majorant is integrable uniformly in time:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-consolidated-bregman-l1-dominator}
C_{a,b,\nu}
\left(g_t(x)+g_t(y)\right)\min\{1,|x-y|^{-2}\}
\in L^1([a,b]\times\RR^2),
\end{equation}
and it dominates the symmetrized kernel
\[
\frac{
g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)\Lambda(u_t(y),u_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}.
\]
Consequently the one-sided and symmetrized Bregman double integrals are
finite, and annular cutoffs may be removed by dominated convergence in
\(L^1([a,b])\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Fourier transform of the Poisson kernel is
\(\widehat P_t(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|}\), hence
\(\partial_tP_t=LP_t\) for the displayed principal-value normalization.  If
\(p_t=P_t*\mu\), translation invariance gives
\[
L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t=(L_RP_t-\partial_tP_t)*\mu .
\]
It is therefore enough to control the omitted parts of the principal-value
formula for \(P_t\).  The near-diagonal term is estimated by Taylor expansion:
the odd first-order term cancels on the symmetric interval and
\[
\left\|\frac1\pi\int_{|z|<R^{-1}}
\frac{P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t(\cdot)}{z^2}\dd z\right\|_1
\le \frac{2}{\pi R}\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1 .
\]
The far-field term satisfies
\[
\left\|\frac1\pi\int_{|z|>R}
\frac{P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t(\cdot)}{z^2}\dd z\right\|_1
\le \frac{4}{\pi R}.
\]
After convolution with \(\mu\), these two estimates prove
\eqref{eq:kl-consolidated-generator-l1}; multiplication by
\(\sup_{t,x}|q_t(x)|\) gives
\eqref{eq:kl-consolidated-bounded-pairing}.

For the bounded-Lipschitz cutoff formula, write \(B_R(p,q)\) for the double
integral in \eqref{eq:kl-consolidated-pv-pairing}.  On a finite annulus,
Fubini and symmetry give \(\int q\,L_Rp=-B_R(p,q)/(2\pi)\) first for
\(q\in C_c^\infty\).  The extension to bounded Lipschitz \(q\) uses the
absolute bound
\[
\int_{|z|<1}
\frac{\|p(\cdot+z)-p\|_1\operatorname{Lip}(q)|z|}{z^2}\dd z
+4\|p\|_1\|q\|_\infty\int_{|z|\ge1}\frac{\dd z}{z^2}<\infty,
\]
with \(\|p(\cdot+z)-p\|_1\le |z|\|\partial_xp\|_1\), followed by standard
mollifier and spatial cutoff approximation.  Finally
\eqref{eq:kl-consolidated-generator-l1} identifies the annular limit with
\(\int q\,\partial_tp\).  This proves the stated principal-value pairing for
\(p=h_t\) and \(p=g_t\), uniformly on \([a,b]\) whenever the bounded and
Lipschitz norms of \(q\) are uniform.

The quotient formula follows by writing \(x=\bar\gamma+tz\).  The elementary
bounds
\[
R_\varepsilon(z)\le2(1+\varepsilon^2),\qquad
R_\varepsilon(z)\ge\frac{1}{2(1+\varepsilon^2)}
\]
give the displayed upper and lower bounds after integration, the lower bound
using Jensen's inequality for \(s\mapsto(1+s)^{-1}\).  Differentiating gives
\[
\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)
=\frac{2\varepsilon(1-w^2)-2\varepsilon^2w}{(1+w^2)^2},
\qquad w=z-\varepsilon,
\]
so \(|\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)|\le3(1+\varepsilon^2)\), and the Lipschitz
bound follows from \(\partial_x=t^{-1}\partial_z\).

The quotient range is a compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\).  On that
interval, the second derivative of \(s\log s\) is bounded; hence
\(\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\le C|\alpha-\beta|^2\).  Combining this with the
Lipschitz bound and with the bounded quotient range gives
\(\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))\le C\min\{|x-y|^2,1\}\), proving
\eqref{eq:kl-consolidated-bregman-majorant}.  The same estimate with
\((x,y)\) exchanged dominates the symmetrized kernel.  Since
\(\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\dd x=1\) for every \(t>0\), the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:kl-consolidated-bregman-l1-dominator} has finite
\([a,b]\times\RR^2\) integral:
\[
\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}
g_t(x)\min\{1,|x-y|^{-2}\}\dd x\dd y\dd t
\le (b-a)\int_{\RR}\min\{1,|z|^{-2}\}\dd z<\infty,
\]
and the term with \(g_t(y)\) is identical.  This proves (v), including the
cutoff convergence by dominated convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[\(L^1\)-absolute-continuity chain rule for the Poisson KL path]%
\label{lem:poisson-kl-l1-ac-chain-rule}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), let \(\mu\) be a finite signed Borel measure on
\(\RR\), and put \(p_t=P_t*\mu\).  Then
\[
t\longmapsto p_t,\qquad
t\longmapsto \partial_tp_t
\]
are absolutely continuous maps from \([a,b]\) into \(L^1(\RR)\).  In
particular, for
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t,
\]
the four maps
\[
t\mapsto h_t,\qquad t\mapsto g_t,\qquad
t\mapsto\partial_th_t,\qquad t\mapsto\partial_tg_t
\]
are locally \(L^1\)-absolutely continuous on \((0,\infty)\).

Assume in addition that, on \([a,b]\),
\[
0<m\le u_t(x)\le M<\infty
\qquad\text{for a.e. }(t,x)\in[a,b]\times\RR .
\]
Then
\[
H(t):=\int_{\RR}h_t(x)\log\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}\dd x
\]
is absolutely continuous on \([a,b]\), and for a.e. \(t\in[a,b]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-kl-l1-chain-rule}
H'(t)=
\int_{\RR}\log u_t(x)\,\partial_th_t(x)\dd x
-\int_{\RR}(u_t(x)-1)\,\partial_tg_t(x)\dd x .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Poisson kernel has the scaling form
\[
P_t(x)=t^{-1}P_1(x/t),
\]
and, for \(k=0,1,2\),
\[
\partial_t^kP_t(x)=t^{-k-1}Q_k(x/t)
\]
with \(Q_k\) a rational function satisfying
\(|Q_k(y)|\le C_k(1+|y|)^{-2}\).  Hence \(Q_k\in L^1(\RR)\), and
\(t\mapsto\partial_t^kP_t\) is continuous as an \(L^1(\RR)\)-valued function
on every compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\).  The Bochner fundamental
theorem of calculus gives, for \(s,t\in[a,b]\),
\[
\partial_\tau^kP_\tau\big|_{\tau=t}
-\partial_\tau^kP_\tau\big|_{\tau=s}
=\int_s^t\partial_r^{k+1}P_r\,\dd r
\quad\text{in }L^1(\RR),\qquad k=0,1 .
\]
Convolution with a finite signed measure is bounded on \(L^1\), so the same
identities hold for \(p_t=P_t*\mu\).  This proves the asserted
\(L^1\)-absolute continuity of \(p_t\) and \(\partial_tp_t\), and applying it
to \(\mu=\nu\) and to the point mass \(\delta_{\bar\gamma}\) gives the four
Poisson paths in the statement.

It remains to justify differentiating the entropy integral.  Set
\[
F(r,s):=r\log(r/s),\qquad r,s>0 .
\]
On the closed quotient cone \(m\le r/s\le M\),
\[
\partial_rF(r,s)=\log(r/s)+1,\qquad
\partial_sF(r,s)=-r/s
\]
are bounded.  Thus
\[
|F(r,s)|\le C_{m,M}s,
\]
and the mean-value theorem on the same cone gives
\[
|F(r_1,s_1)-F(r_0,s_0)|
\le C_{m,M}(|r_1-r_0|+|s_1-s_0|)
\]
whenever both pairs lie in the cone.  Hence \(H(t)\) is finite and, for
\(s<t\) in \([a,b]\),
\[
|H(t)-H(s)|
\le C_{m,M}\bigl(\|h_t-h_s\|_1+\|g_t-g_s\|_1\bigr).
\]
Since \(t\mapsto h_t\) and \(t\mapsto g_t\) are \(L^1\)-absolutely
continuous, this already proves \(H\in AC([a,b])\).

For the derivative formula, the partial derivatives satisfy
\[
|\partial_rF(r,s)|+|\partial_sF(r,s)|\le C_{m,M}
\]
throughout the cone.  For each fixed \(x\), the scalar functions
\(t\mapsto h_t(x)\) and \(t\mapsto g_t(x)\) are absolutely continuous on
\([a,b]\): their time derivatives are obtained by differentiating the
Poisson kernel under the finite measure, and the derivative majorants are
integrable on \([a,b]\times\RR\).  Therefore the ordinary one-variable chain
rule gives, for a.e. \(t\) and a.e. \(x\),
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}F(h_t(x),g_t(x))
=\bigl(\log u_t(x)+1\bigr)\partial_th_t(x)
-u_t(x)\partial_tg_t(x).
\]
The right-hand side belongs to \(L^1([a,b]\times\RR)\), with bound
\[
C_{m,M}\bigl(|\partial_th_t(x)|+|\partial_tg_t(x)|\bigr),
\]
because the first part of the proof gives
\[
\int_a^b\|\partial_th_t\|_1\dd t
+\int_a^b\|\partial_tg_t\|_1\dd t<\infty .
\]
Integrating the scalar chain rule over \(x\), and using Fubini together with
the preceding \(L^1([a,b]\times\RR)\) bound, gives
\[
H'(t)=
\int_{\RR}(\log u_t+1)\partial_th_t\dd x
-\int_{\RR}u_t\,\partial_tg_t\dd x
\quad\text{for a.e. }t .
\]
Finally, \(\int h_t=\int g_t=1\) for all \(t\).  Since
\(t\mapsto h_t\) and \(t\mapsto g_t\) are \(L^1\)-absolutely continuous,
differentiating these mass identities gives
\(\int\partial_th_t=\int\partial_tg_t=0\) for a.e. \(t\).  Substituting this
into the previous display yields \eqref{eq:poisson-kl-l1-chain-rule}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cauchy/KL cutoff Green identity]%
\label{lem:cauchy-kl-cutoff-green-identity}
\label{thm:external-cauchy-kl-hardystein-input}
The following is the internal Cauchy/KL identity used in this paper.  It is
not an imported abstract theorem: the proof below starts from the stated
\(L^1\) chain rule and the stated annular Green formula, then removes the
cutoff by the stated Bregman majorant.  The Hardy--Stein and
Sobolev--Bregman references above are provenance for this form of the
calculation; the hypotheses below are the exact hypotheses used here and are
checked for the Poisson quotient in Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification}.
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\).  Let \(p_t\) and \(r_t\) be positive probability
densities on \(\RR\).  Put \(v_t=p_t/r_t\) and suppose that the following
five hypotheses hold on \([a,b]\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(HS\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
\item The Cauchy generator is normalized by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:external-cauchy-generator-normalization}
(Lf)(x)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y,
\qquad \widehat{Lf}(\xi)=-|\xi|\widehat f(\xi),
\end{equation}
and \(p_t,r_t\) lie in its \(L^1\)-trajectory domain:
\[
t\mapsto p_t,\ r_t\in AC([a,b];L^1(\RR)),\qquad
\partial_t p_t=Lp_t,\quad \partial_t r_t=Lr_t
\quad\text{in }L^1 .
\]
\item The quotient is an admissible positive Bregman coordinate: there are
\(0<m<M<\infty\) such that
\[
m\le v_t(x)\le M\qquad(t,x)\in[a,b]\times\RR,
\]
and \(v_t\) is uniformly Lipschitz in \(x\).  Consequently \(v_t\) and
\(\log v_t\) are bounded Lipschitz test functions, uniformly in \(t\).
\item The KL path
\[
K(t):=\int_{\RR}p_t(x)\log v_t(x)\dd x
\]
is absolutely continuous and satisfies the \(L^1\) quotient chain rule
\begin{equation}\label{eq:external-kl-chain-rule}
K'(t)=
\int_{\RR}\log v_t(x)\,\partial_tp_t(x)\dd x
-\int_{\RR}(v_t(x)-1)\,\partial_tr_t(x)\dd x
\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in[a,b].
\end{equation}
\item With
\[
\Omega_R:=\{(x,y):R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\},
\]
the cutoff generator pairing converges for exactly the two tests used in
\eqref{eq:external-kl-chain-rule}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:external-cutoff-pairing}
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)\partial_t s_t(x)\dd x
=
-\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(s_t(x)-s_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\end{equation}
for \((s_t,q_t)=(p_t,\log v_t)\) and
\((s_t,q_t)=(r_t,v_t)\), for a.e. \(t\in[a,b]\).
\item The logarithmic Bregman kernels are integrable and the annular cutoffs
may be removed.  Equivalently, for
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta ,
\]
the symmetrized kernel
\[
\frac{r_t(x)\Lambda(v_t(x),v_t(y))
+r_t(y)\Lambda(v_t(y),v_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}
\]
has an \(L^1([a,b]\times\RR^2)\) majorant.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
I(t):=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{r_t(x)\left(
v_t(x)\log\!\bigl(v_t(x)/v_t(y)\bigr)-v_t(x)+v_t(y)
\right)}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\]
belongs to \(L^1([a,b])\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:external-cauchy-kl-hardystein-input}
K'(t)=-I(t)\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in[a,b].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At a time for which the chain rule and the two cutoff pairings hold,
write
\[
a=v_t(x),\qquad b=v_t(y),\qquad \rho_x=r_t(x),\qquad \rho_y=r_t(y),
\]
and put \(p_t=r_tv_t\).  By \eqref{eq:external-kl-chain-rule} and the mass
identity for \(r_t\), which follows from
\(\int r_t=1\) and \(t\mapsto r_t\in AC([a,b];L^1)\),
\[
K'(t)=\int_{\RR}\log v_t\,\partial_tp_t
      -\int_{\RR}v_t\,\partial_tr_t .
\]
Applying the assumed cutoff Green formula
\eqref{eq:external-cutoff-pairing} first to
\((s_t,q_t)=(p_t,\log v_t)\) and then to
\((s_t,q_t)=(r_t,v_t)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
K'(t)
&=-\frac1{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(\log v_t(x)-\log v_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y\\
&\quad+\frac1{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(r_t(x)-r_t(y))(v_t(x)-v_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\end{aligned}
\]
The numerator in the difference of the two Green forms is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(\rho_xa-\rho_yb)(\log a-\log b)-(\rho_x-\rho_y)(a-b)\\
&\qquad
=\rho_x\left(a\log\frac ab-a+b\right)
 +\rho_y\left(b\log\frac ba-b+a\right)\\
&\qquad
=r_t(x)\Lambda(v_t(x),v_t(y))
 +r_t(y)\Lambda(v_t(y),v_t(x)).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the only Bregman algebra used in the lemma.  Hypothesis
\textup{(HS5)} supplies an \(L^1([a,b]\times\RR^2)\) majorant for the last
display divided by \((x-y)^2\).  Therefore the two annular limits may be
combined and their cutoffs removed by dominated convergence.  Hence, for
the present time \(t\),
\[
-K'(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{r_t(x)\Lambda(v_t(x),v_t(y))
+r_t(y)\Lambda(v_t(y),v_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\]
Finally,
\[
\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{r_t(y)\Lambda(v_t(y),v_t(x))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
=
\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{r_t(x)\Lambda(v_t(x),v_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\]
after exchanging \(x\) and \(y\).  Therefore the symmetric coefficient
\(1/(2\pi)\) becomes the one-sided coefficient \(1/\pi\), proving
\eqref{eq:external-cauchy-kl-hardystein-input}.  The same majorant gives
\(I\in L^1([a,b])\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Poisson quotient logarithmic Bregman calculation]%
\label{prop:self-contained-cauchy-kl-hardystein}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\).  Let
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t
\]
on \(t\in[a,b]\), where \(\nu\) is a probability measure with mean
\(\bar\gamma\) and finite centred second moment.  Define
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta .
\]
Set
\[
H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
\]
and
\[
I_\nu(t):=\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\]
Then \(H\in AC([a,b])\), \(I_\nu\in L^1([a,b])\), and, for a.e.
\(t\in[a,b]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-tail-input-unlocalized}
H'(t)=-I_\nu(t).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, before applying the Cauchy Green formula, the entropy path
satisfies the one-variable quotient chain rule
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-chain-rule-before-pairing}
H'(t)=
\int_{\RR}\log u_t(x)\,\partial_th_t(x)\dd x
-\int_{\RR}(u_t(x)-1)\,\partial_tg_t(x)\dd x
\end{equation}
for a.e. \(t\in[a,b]\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-kl-l1-ac-chain-rule} gives \(H\in AC([a,b])\) and the
one-variable quotient chain rule \eqref{eq:kl-chain-rule-before-pairing}.
It remains only to verify the hypotheses of the Cauchy/KL cutoff Green
identity, Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-kl-cutoff-green-identity}.
Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification}\textup{(i)} gives the generator
normalization and the \(L^1\)-trajectory identities
\(\partial_th_t=Lh_t\), \(\partial_tg_t=Lg_t\), which is \textup{(HS1)}.
Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification}\textup{(iii)} gives the compact
positive quotient range and the uniform Lipschitz bound for \(u_t\); since
\(\log\) is smooth on that compact range, both \(u_t\) and \(\log u_t\) are
bounded Lipschitz tests, giving \textup{(HS2)}.  The chain rule just cited is
\textup{(HS3)}.  Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification}\textup{(ii)} gives
the two cutoff generator pairings required in \textup{(HS4)}, applied to
\((s_t,q_t)=(h_t,\log u_t)=(g_tu_t,\log u_t)\) and
\((s_t,q_t)=(g_t,u_t)\).  Finally,
Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification}\textup{(iv)}--\textup{(v)} gives the
integrable logarithmic-Bregman dominator and the cutoff removal in
\textup{(HS5)}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-kl-cutoff-green-identity}, applied with
\((p_t,r_t,v_t)=(h_t,g_t,u_t)\), therefore gives
\(I_\nu\in L^1([a,b])\) and
\eqref{eq:kl-tail-input-unlocalized}.  The displayed chain rule was already
obtained from Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-kl-l1-ac-chain-rule}.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The KL tail identity is therefore proved from the quotient-domain
verification above and the Cauchy/KL cutoff Green identity: Lemma~
\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification} supplies the admissible moving quotient, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-second-moment-kl-decay} converts the resulting
differential identity into a tail formula.

\begin{theorem}[Poisson quotient KL tail identity]%
\label{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail}
\label{cor:poisson-kl-doob-bregman-tail}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar\gamma$ and finite
centred second moment.  Define
\[
I_\nu(s):=
\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{
g_s(x)\left(
u_s(x)\log\!\bigl(u_s(x)/u_s(y)\bigr)-u_s(x)+u_s(y)
\right)}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\]
Then \(I_\nu\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty)\), and for every \(t>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\int_t^\infty I_\nu(s)\dd s.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set \(H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\).  On every compact interval
\([a,b]\subset(0,\infty)\),
Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification} verifies the quotient bounds, the
cutoff generator pairing, and the \(L^1\) Bregman dominator used by
Proposition~\ref{prop:self-contained-cauchy-kl-hardystein}.  That proposition
therefore gives \(H\in AC([a,b])\),
\(I_\nu\in L^1([a,b])\), and \(H'(s)=-I_\nu(s)\) for a.e.
\(s\in[a,b]\).  Hence, for \(0<t<T\),
\[
\int_t^T I_\nu(s)\dd s=H(t)-H(T).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-second-moment-kl-decay} gives \(H(T)\to0\) as
\(T\to\infty\).  Since \(I_\nu\ge0\), monotone convergence yields
\eqref{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Dissipation-tail and window-average coefficient transfer]%
\label{prop:dissipation-tail-coefficient-expansion}
Let \(N\ge2\), and assume that \(\nu\) has finite centred
\((2N-2)\)th absolute moment.  Then the KL dissipation tail has the same
universal coefficient polynomial layer as the entropy expansion:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-expansion}
\int_t^\infty I_\nu(s)\dd s
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N})
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for every \(2\le M\le N\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-recovery}
A_{2M}(\nu)
=
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{2M}
\left(
\int_t^\infty I_\nu(s)\dd s
-\sum_{m=2}^{M-1}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
\right),
\end{equation}
with the sum interpreted as zero when \(M=2\).

More locally, for each fixed \(\lambda>1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-finite-window-coefficients}
\int_t^{\lambda t} I_\nu(s)\dd s
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)(1-\lambda^{-2m})t^{-2m}
+o(t^{-2N}).
\end{equation}
Thus the averaged dissipation on a multiplicative window satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-window-average-coefficients}
\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)t}\int_t^{\lambda t} I_\nu(s)\dd s
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}
\frac{1-\lambda^{-2m}}{\lambda-1}\,A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-(2m+1)}
+o(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\end{equation}
If, in addition, \(0<\eta\le1\) and the centred
\((2N-2+\eta)\)th absolute moment is finite, then the error terms above have
the following positive-margin bounds:
\[
\int_t^\infty I_\nu(s)\dd s
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+O_\nu(t^{-(2N+\eta)})
\]
and, for each fixed \(\lambda>1\),
\[
\int_t^{\lambda t} I_\nu(s)\dd s
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)(1-\lambda^{-2m})t^{-2m}
 +O_{\nu,N,\eta,\lambda}(t^{-(2N+\eta)}).
\]
Consequently the averaged-window remainder is
\[
O_{\nu,N,\eta,\lambda}(t^{-(2N+1+\eta)}).
\]

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is a coefficient transfer, not an additional Hardy--Stein theorem.
The tail identity \eqref{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation} identifies
\(\int_t^\infty I_\nu\) with \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\).  Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} gives
\eqref{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-expansion} and hence the coefficient
recovery formula \eqref{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-recovery}.

For the finite window, use the already proved tail identity twice:
\[
H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t),
\qquad
\int_t^{\lambda t}I_\nu(s)\dd s=H(t)-H(\lambda t).
\]
Substitute the expansion of \(H\) at \(t\) and at \(\lambda t\).  The
coefficient of \(t^{-2m}\) is
\(A_{2m}(\nu)-A_{2m}(\nu)\lambda^{-2m}\), which gives
\eqref{eq:bregman-finite-window-coefficients}; division by
\((\lambda-1)t\) gives \eqref{eq:bregman-window-average-coefficients}.
The positive-margin assertion follows from the explicit remainder estimate
\eqref{eq:positive-margin-retained-order-theorem} for \(H(t)\) and
\(H(\lambda t)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No pointwise conclusion from finite moments alone]
Proposition~\ref{prop:dissipation-tail-coefficient-expansion} gives only
tail integrals and fixed-window averages.  Pointwise asymptotics for
\(I_\nu(t)\) require extra differentiated remainder input; the precise
conditional result is isolated in
Appendix~\ref{app:conditional-post-processing}.
\end{remark}
