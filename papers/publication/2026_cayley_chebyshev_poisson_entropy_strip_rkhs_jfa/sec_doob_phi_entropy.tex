\section{KL Tail Identity for the Poisson Doob Quotient}%
\label{sec:doob-phi-entropy}

Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\).
For \(t>0\) put
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad
u_t:=h_t/g_t .
\]
The Cauchy generator is normalized by
\[
(Lf)(x)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y,
\]
so that \(\partial_tP_t=LP_t\).  This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail}
in the Cauchy normalization displayed above, namely the KL-specialized
two-point Bregman dissipation identity for the moving Poisson quotient
\(u_t=h_t/g_t\), and then records the coefficient consequence.

The new point is the moving-reference differentiation.  If
\(H(t)=D_{\rm KL}(h_t\|g_t)\), then the \(L^1\) chain rule first gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:section4-two-pair-chain-rule}
H'(t)=\int_{\RR}\log u_t\,\partial_t h_t\,\dd x
-\int_{\RR}(u_t-1)\partial_tg_t\,\dd x .
\end{equation}
The first term is paired by the Cauchy Green formula with
\((h_t,\log u_t)\); the second is paired with \((g_t,u_t)\).  Thus the
argument is not a direct citation of a static Hardy--Stein identity.  Writing
\[
g_x=g_t(x),\quad g_y=g_t(y),\quad
u_x=u_t(x),\quad u_y=u_t(y),
\]
the difference of the two Green numerators is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:section4-two-green-numerator-difference}
(g_xu_x-g_yu_y)(\log u_x-\log u_y)
-(g_x-g_y)(u_x-u_y)
=g_x\Lambda(u_x,u_y)+g_y\Lambda(u_y,u_x),
\end{equation}
where \(\Lambda(a,b)=a\log(a/b)-a+b\).  This elementary identity is the
precise closure step that converts two moving Green pairings into the
one-sided nonlocal Bregman integrand used below.

\noindent\textbf{The quotient-domain input.}
The only point not supplied by the abstract Hardy--Stein/Bregman formalism is
the moving matched-Poisson domain verification.
Theorem~\ref{thm:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} is the
domain theorem: on every compact time window \(0<a<b<\infty\), it records
\[
\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)}\le u_t\le
2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},\qquad
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le
3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr)|x-y|,
\]
and the logarithmic Bregman kernel divided by the Cauchy singular kernel is
dominated by an explicit integrable majorant
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}
\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{|x-y|<1\}}+|x-y|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|x-y|\ge1\}}\bigr).
\]
Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification} is the standalone \(L^1\)
Nemytskii chain rule for bounded moving quotients.
Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-window-bregman-identity} then consolidates the exact
local hypothesis package: two Poisson solutions \(h_t=P_t*\mu\) and
\(g_t=P_t*\eta\), a quotient \(u_t=h_t/g_t\) satisfying one bounded
Lipschitz estimate on a compact time window, the annular Cauchy Green
formula for the two pairs \((h_t,\log u_t)\) and \((g_t,u_t)\), and the
Bregman cutoff passage.  Under precisely those hypotheses it proves
\(H'(t)=-\mathscr I(t)\) for a.e. time on the window.
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail} applies that proposition with
\(\mu=\nu\) and \(\eta=\delta_{\bar\gamma}\).

The identity is in the de Bruijn/Stam and relative-entropy dissipation
lineage
\cite{DeBruijn1953,Stam1959,Blachman1965,Barron1986,
JohnsonBarron2004,MadimanBarron2007,Verdu2010Mismatched,
HirataNemotoYoshida2012RelativeEntropy,Yoshida2017RelativeEntropyDiffusion},
and it uses the same Bregman algebra that underlies nonlocal
Hardy--Stein/Sobolev--Bregman formulas
\cite{Bregman1967,BanuelosBogdanLuks2016HardyStein,
BanuelosKim2019HardySteinNonsymmetric,
BogdanGrzywnyPietruskaPalubaRutkowski2023NonlinearNonlocalDouglas,
Gutowski2023HardySteinPureJump,GutowskiKwasnicki2025BeurlingDenySobolevBregman,
BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein}.  Here no abstract
Hardy--Stein theorem is invoked as a black box.  The proof uses four local
lemmas, followed by the endpoint decay lemma:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(1)}&
\text{compact-window quotient bounds and the single Bregman majorant},\\
\text{(2)}&
\text{annular cutoff, measurability, and compact-window cutoff removal},\\
\text{(3)}&
\text{the Cauchy Green formula and its constants},\\
\text{(4)}&
\text{the }L^1\text{ Nemytskii chain rule},\\
\text{(5)}&
\text{the endpoint decay }D_{\rm KL}(h_T\|g_T)\to0 .
\end{array}
\]
Lemma~\ref{thm:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} supplies the
only distribution-dependent estimates for the nonstationary quotient
\((P_t*\nu)/P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\), namely the bounded Lipschitz quotient
hypothesis of Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-window-bregman-identity}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:annular-cutoff-measurable-bregman-dissipation} defines
\(\mathcal I_\nu=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal I_{\nu,R}\), proves its
measurability by monotone limits, and removes the annular or auxiliary
cutoffs on compact time windows.
Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing} fixes the generator
sign and the \(1/\pi,1/(2\pi)\) constants once.
Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-window-bregman-identity} is the consolidated local
statement that combines the chain rule, the two Green pairings, the annular
cutoff limit, and the Bregman algebra.  The final tail identity follows from
this derivative identity, nonnegativity of \(\mathcal I_\nu\), and the
separate decay \(H(T)\to0\).  The coefficient expansion itself remains the
Cayley quotient-mode result of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}; the
identity below only converts those KL tails into dissipation tails.

\begin{lemma}[Second-moment KL decay to the translated Poisson profile]%
\label{lem:poisson-second-moment-kl-decay}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean $\bar{\gamma}$ and
finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar{\gamma}).
\]
Then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}$ and $x=\bar{\gamma}+ty$.  Since
$g_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty)=(\pi t)^{-1}(1+y^2)^{-1}$,
\[
u_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty):=\frac{h_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty)}{g_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty)}
=\int_{\RR}\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
For $A>0$ decompose $\nu=\nu_A+\nu_A^c$ according to
$|X_c|\le A$ and $|X_c|>A$.  The elementary kernel bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-moment-kl-kernel-bound}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
\le 2(1+\varepsilon^2)
\qquad(y,\varepsilon\in\RR)
\end{equation}
implies
\[
0\le u_{t,A}^c(y):=\int_{|X_c|>A}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
\le 2\int_{|X_c|>A}\left(1+\frac{X_c^2}{t^2}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
Hence, for $t\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-moment-kl-tail-sup}
\sup_y u_{t,A}^c(y)
\le 2\nu(|X_c|>A)+2\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu .
\end{equation}

The bounded part has total mass $m_A:=\nu(|X_c|\le A)$ and first centred
moment $b_A:=\int_{|X_c|\le A}X_c\dd\nu$.  The exact expansion
\[
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
=1+\frac{2y}{1+y^2}\varepsilon
+\frac{(3y^2-1)\varepsilon^2-2y\varepsilon^3}
{(1+y^2)(1+(y-\varepsilon)^2)}
\]
shows that, for each fixed $A$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-truncated-quotient-limit}
u_{t,A}(y):=\int_{|X_c|\le A}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
=m_A+\frac{2yb_A}{t(1+y^2)}+O_A(t^{-2})
\end{equation}
uniformly in~$y$.  Since
$b_A=-\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c\dd\nu$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
|b_A|\le \left(\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Combining this with
\eqref{eq:second-moment-kl-tail-sup} and
\eqref{eq:bounded-truncated-quotient-limit} yields the needed two-sided
uniform estimate explicitly.  Put
\[
q_A:=\nu(|X_c|>A),\qquad
r_A:=\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu,\qquad
\eta_A(t):=\sup_y |u_{t,A}(y)-m_A|.
\]
Then \(\eta_A(t)\to0\) for each fixed \(A\), and for \(t\ge1\)
\[
-\eta_A(t)-q_A
\le u_t(\bar\gamma+ty)-1
\le \eta_A(t)+q_A+\sup_z u_{t,A}^c(z)
\le \eta_A(t)+3q_A+2r_A
\]
uniformly in \(y\).  Hence
\[
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|u_t-1\|_{L^\infty(\RR)}
\le 3\nu(|X_c|>A)+2\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu .
\]
Letting $A\to\infty$ gives $\|u_t-1\|_\infty\to0$.

For all sufficiently large $t$ we therefore have
$|u_t-1|\le1/2$.  With $\delta_t=u_t-1$ and using
$\int g_t\delta_t=\int(h_t-g_t)=0$,
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\int_{\RR}g_t\bigl((1+\delta_t)\log(1+\delta_t)-\delta_t\bigr)\dd x
\le 2\|\delta_t\|_\infty^2,
\]
because $(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\le2s^2$ for $|s|\le1/2$.  The right-hand side
tends to zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Compact-window quotient bounds and Bregman majorant]%
\label{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain}
\label{thm:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain}
\label{lem:single-compact-window-bregman-majorant-constants}
\label{lem:bregman-kernel-l1-cutoff-removal}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\)
with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and finite centred second moment
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^2\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
For
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t,
\qquad t\in[a,b],
\]
put
\[
m:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\qquad
M:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},\qquad
L:=3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr).
\]
Then, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x,y\in\RR\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML}
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L|x-y|.
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\log u_t\) is bounded and Lipschitz uniformly on
\([a,b]\).  Moreover, with
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\qquad
\Psi(z):=\mathbf1_{\{|z|<1\}}+|z|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|z|\ge1\}},
\]
and
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\},
\]
the one-sided logarithmic Bregman kernel satisfies, for \(x\ne y\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-one-sided-bregman-majorant}
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}\,g_t(x)\Psi(x-y),
\end{equation}
and the exchanged term satisfies the same estimate with \(x\) and \(y\)
interchanged.  Hence the symmetrized Bregman kernel used in the local
Green-pairing argument below is dominated on
\([a,b]\times\RR^2\) by the integrable majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-sym-bregman-integrable-majorant}
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y),
\end{equation}
whose integral over \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) is \(8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\).  In
particular, if
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t):=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\]
is understood as the nonnegative Lebesgue integral off the diagonal, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-lemma-one-sided-majorant-integral}
\int_a^b\mathcal I_\nu(t)\dd t
\le \frac{4C_{a,b,\nu}}{\pi}(b-a).
\end{equation}
The one-sided and symmetrized Bregman kernels are in
\(L^1([a,b]\times\RR^2)\); hence every measurable cutoff
\(0\le\chi_n\le1\) with \(\chi_n(t,x,y)\to1\) off the diagonal may be removed
by dominated convergence.  For each fixed \(t>0\) the same cutoff statement
holds on \(\RR^2\), after choosing any compact window containing \(t\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With \(z=(x-\bar{\gamma})/t\), \(X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}\), and
\[
R_\varepsilon(z):=\frac{1+z^2}{1+(z-\varepsilon)^2},
\]
one has
\[
u_t(x)=\int_{\RR}R_{X_c/t}\!\left(\frac{x-\bar{\gamma}}{t}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
The two-sided part of \eqref{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML} is
exactly the compact-window Poisson quotient bound
\eqref{eq:prelim-poisson-quotient-two-sided-bound} from
Lemma~\ref{lem:kl-bounded-test-and-poisson-quotient}.  It remains in this
lemma only to add the Lipschitz and Bregman estimates needed for the Green
pairing.
Also
\[
\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)
=\frac{2\varepsilon(1-w^2)-2\varepsilon^2w}{(1+w^2)^2},
\qquad w=z-\varepsilon.
\]
Since
\[
\sup_w\frac{2|1-w^2|}{(1+w^2)^2}\le2,\qquad
\sup_w\frac{2|w|}{(1+w^2)^2}\le1,
\]
we have
\[
|\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)|\le 2|\varepsilon|+\varepsilon^2
\le 3(1+\varepsilon^2).
\]
After dividing by \(t\) and using \(t\ge a\),
\[
|\partial_xu_t(x)|
\le \frac{3}{t}\int_{\RR}\left(1+\frac{X_c^2}{t^2}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma)
\le 3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr)=L.
\]
This proves the Lipschitz bound in
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML}.  Since
\((\log r)'=1/r\le1/m\) on \([m,M]\), the same bounds also make
\(\log u_t\) uniformly bounded and Lipschitz.

For fixed \(\beta\in[m,M]\), the function
\(\alpha\mapsto \Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\) has first derivative zero at
\(\alpha=\beta\) and second derivative \(1/\alpha\le1/m\) on \([m,M]\).
Taylor's formula therefore gives the compact-range quadratic estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-compact-quadratic-bregman}
0\le \Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\le \frac{1}{2m}|\alpha-\beta|^2
\qquad(\alpha,\beta\in[m,M]).
\end{equation}
Combining this with the Lipschitz estimate gives
\[
\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le \frac{L^2}{2m}|x-y|^2\qquad(|x-y|<1).
\]
The same quadratic estimate and the range bound
\(|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le M-m\) give
\[
\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le \frac{(M-m)^2}{2m}\qquad(|x-y|\ge1).
\]
Dividing by \((x-y)^2\) proves
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-one-sided-bregman-majorant}.  The exchanged term is
identical after switching \(x\) and \(y\).  Finally
\[
\int_{\RR}\Psi(z)\dd z
=\int_{|z|<1}\dd z+\int_{|z|\ge1}|z|^{-2}\dd z=4,
\]
and \(\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\dd x=1\).  Thus the one-sided majorant integrates over
\([a,b]\times\RR^2\) to \(4C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\), while adding the exchanged
majorant gives
\[
\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y)\dd x\dd y\dd t
=8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)<\infty .
\]
Consequently the one-sided integral estimate is exactly
\eqref{eq:single-lemma-one-sided-majorant-integral}.  The Bregman kernels are
dominated by the displayed \(L^1\) functions, so all stated cutoff removals
follow from dominated convergence; the diagonal is a null set.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cauchy Green formula and constants]%
\label{lem:kl-main-generator-convention-domain}
\label{lem:poisson-cauchy-generator-l1-domain}
\label{lem:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing}
\label{lem:cauchy-normalization-bounded-test-green}
Use the Fourier convention
\[
\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\RR}f(x)e^{-\ii x\xi}\dd x .
\]
Then
\[
\widehat{P_t}(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|},\qquad \partial_tP_t=LP_t,
\]
where
\[
Lf(x)=\frac1\pi\,\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y .
\]
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), let \(\mu\) be a finite signed measure on \(\RR\), and
put \(p_t=P_t*\mu\).  For \(R>1\) define
\[
(L_R f)(x):=\frac1\pi\int_{R^{-1}<|z|<R}
\frac{f(x+z)-f(x)}{z^2}\dd z,
\qquad
\Omega_R:=\{(x,y):R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-generator-annular-l1-estimate}
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t\|_1
\le
\frac{\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}{\pi R}
\left(\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1+4\right).
\end{equation}
If \(q_t:\RR\to\RR\) is a possibly time-dependent bounded Lipschitz test
family with no compact-support assumption and with
\[
M_*:=\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|q_t\|_\infty<\infty,\qquad
L_*:=\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\operatorname{Lip}(q_t)<\infty ,
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-finite}
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)L_Rp_t(x)\dd x
=-\frac1{2\pi}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\end{equation}
and
\begin{align}
&\sup_{\substack{t\in[a,b]\\R>1}}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\left|
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\right|\dd x\dd y \notag\\
&\qquad\le
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\left(2L_*\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_xP_s\|_1+8M_*\right).
\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-total-variation-bound}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-limit}
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)\partial_tp_t(x)\dd x
=-\frac1{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\end{equation}
and the cutoff error is bounded by
\begin{align}
&\sup_{t\in[a,b]}
\left|
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)\partial_tp_t(x)\dd x
+\frac1{2\pi}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\right| \notag\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{M_*\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}{\pi R}
\left(\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1+4\right).
\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-time-dependent-cutoff-error}
\end{align}
Thus the constants are: \(L\) carries the prefactor \(1/\pi\);
each symmetrized Green pairing carries \(-1/(2\pi)\); after the two
logarithmic Bregman pairings are combined and the variables are exchanged,
the final one-sided dissipation carries \(1/\pi\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Fourier transform of the Cauchy kernel is
\(\widehat{P_t}(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|}\), and the displayed principal-value
operator has multiplier \(-|\xi|\).  Hence
\[
\widehat{\partial_tP_t}(\xi)=-|\xi|e^{-t|\xi|}
=\widehat{LP_t}(\xi).
\]
Translation invariance gives
\(L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t=(L_RP_t-\partial_tP_t)*\mu\).  The near-diagonal
omission is controlled by the symmetric Taylor expansion of
\(P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t\), whose odd first-order term integrates to zero, giving
\[
\left\|\frac1\pi\int_{|z|<R^{-1}}
\frac{P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t(\cdot)}{z^2}\dd z\right\|_1
\le \frac{1}{\pi R}\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1 .
\]
The far-tail omission uses \(\|P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t\|_1\le2\), and is at most
\(4/(\pi R)\).  Convolving with \(\mu\) proves
\eqref{eq:poisson-generator-annular-l1-estimate}.

For fixed \(R\), Fubini is legitimate because \(q_t\) is bounded and
\(\int_{R^{-1}<|z|<R}\|p_t(\cdot+z)-p_t\|_1z^{-2}\dd z<\infty\).  Changing
variables in one copy and symmetrizing gives
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-finite}.  The limiting annuli are
controlled by
\[
\iint_{\Omega_R}\left|
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\right|\dd x\dd y
\le 2L_*\|\partial_xp_t\|_1+8M_*\|p_t\|_1 ,
\]
using \(\|p_t(\cdot+z)-p_t\|_1\le |z|\|\partial_xp_t\|_1\) for \(|z|<1\)
and \(\le2\|p_t\|_1\) for \(|z|\ge1\).  Since
\(\|p_t\|_1\le\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\) and
\(\|\partial_xp_t\|_1\le\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\|\partial_xP_t\|_1\), this proves
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-total-variation-bound}.  Combining the
finite-annulus identity with
\[
\left|\int_{\RR}q_t(L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t)\dd x\right|
\le \|q_t\|_\infty\|L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t\|_1
\]
gives \eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-time-dependent-cutoff-error}, and then
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-limit}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Absolutely continuous logarithmic Nemytskii chain rule]%
\label{prop:abstract-cutoff-bregman-kl}
\label{lem:cauchy-kl-cutoff-green-identity}
\label{thm:external-cauchy-kl-hardystein-input}
\label{prop:self-contained-cauchy-kl-hardystein}
\label{lem:poisson-kl-l1-ac-chain-rule}
\label{thm:kl-domain-verification}
\label{lem:poisson-quotient-cutoff-bregman-verification}
\label{lem:kl-tail-chain-rule-cutoff-passage}
\label{prop:kl-tail-cutoff-inputs}
\label{thm:kl-local-doob-bregman-domain-closure}
\label{prop:kl-local-doob-bregman-domain-closure}
Let \(I=[a,b]\) with \(0<a<b<\infty\).  Let \(h_t\) and \(g_t\) be
nonnegative probability densities on \(\RR\), with \(g_t>0\) a.e., such that
\(t\mapsto h_t\) and \(t\mapsto g_t\) are absolutely continuous as
\(L^1(\RR)\)-valued maps.  Assume that there are constants
\(0<m\le M<\infty\) for which
\[
m\le u_t(x):=\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}\le M
\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in\RR,\ t\in I .
\]
Put
\[
H(t):=\int_{\RR}h_t(x)\log\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}\dd x .
\]
Then \(H\in AC(I)\), and for a.e. \(t\in I\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-moving-quotient-l1-chain-rule}
H'(t)=
\int_{\RR}\log u_t\,\partial_th_t\,\dd x
-\int_{\RR}(u_t-1)\partial_tg_t\,\dd x
\end{equation}
where the derivatives are the \(L^1\)-a.e. time derivatives.  The quotient
\(u_t\) may move with \(t\); no pointwise time derivative of \(u_t\) is used.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
K:=\{(r,s):s>0,\ m\le r/s\le M\}
\]
and \(F(r,s):=r\log(r/s)\) on \(K\).  On this cone
\[
\partial_rF(r,s)=\log(r/s)+1,\qquad
\partial_sF(r,s)=-r/s
\]
are bounded by constants depending only on \(m\) and \(M\).  If
\((r_0,s_0),(r_1,s_1)\in K\), then each point on the line segment between
them stays in \(K\), because
\[
\frac{(1-\theta)r_0+\theta r_1}
{(1-\theta)s_0+\theta s_1}
=
\frac{(1-\theta)s_0(r_0/s_0)+\theta s_1(r_1/s_1)}
{(1-\theta)s_0+\theta s_1}
\in[m,M].
\]
Hence \(F\) is Lipschitz on \(K\) in the sense
\[
|F(r_1,s_1)-F(r_0,s_0)|
\le C_{m,M}(|r_1-r_0|+|s_1-s_0|)
\qquad ((r_j,s_j)\in K).
\]
Therefore \(t\mapsto F(h_t,g_t)\) is an absolutely continuous
\(L^1(\RR)\)-valued map, and \(H(t)=\int F(h_t,g_t)\) is absolutely
continuous.

To identify the derivative, choose a time \(t\) outside the null set where the
\(L^1\)-derivatives \(\partial_th_t\) and \(\partial_tg_t\) fail to exist.
At such a time the Banach-space fundamental theorem gives, in \(L^1\),
\[
\frac{h_{t+\varepsilon}-h_t}{\varepsilon}\to\partial_th_t,\qquad
\frac{g_{t+\varepsilon}-g_t}{\varepsilon}\to\partial_tg_t
\]
along \(\varepsilon\to0\) with \(t+\varepsilon\in I\).  The mean-value formula
on \(K\) gives pointwise
\[
\frac{F(h_{t+\varepsilon},g_{t+\varepsilon})-F(h_t,g_t)}{\varepsilon}
=A_\varepsilon\,\frac{h_{t+\varepsilon}-h_t}{\varepsilon}
+ B_\varepsilon\,\frac{g_{t+\varepsilon}-g_t}{\varepsilon},
\]
where \(A_\varepsilon\) and \(B_\varepsilon\) are convex averages of
\(\partial_rF\) and \(\partial_sF\) along the segment joining
\((h_t,g_t)\) to \((h_{t+\varepsilon},g_{t+\varepsilon})\).  These
coefficients are uniformly bounded, and
\[
A_\varepsilon\to \log u_t+1,\qquad B_\varepsilon\to -u_t
\]
in measure.  Indeed \(h_{t+\varepsilon}\to h_t\) and
\(g_{t+\varepsilon}\to g_t\) in \(L^1\), hence in measure along every
vanishing sequence after taking subsequences; since \(g_t>0\) a.e. and the
quotients remain in \([m,M]\), the corresponding convex-combination quotients
converge to \(u_t\) in measure.  The difference quotients above are uniformly
integrable because they converge in \(L^1\).  Consequently bounded
coefficient convergence in measure, combined with this uniform
integrability, allows passage to the limit in the two integrals.  Thus
\[
H'(t)
=\int_{\RR}(\log u_t+1)\partial_th_t\dd x
-\int_{\RR}u_t\partial_tg_t\dd x
\]
for a.e. \(t\).  Since \(h_t\) and \(g_t\) are probability densities and are
absolutely continuous in \(L^1\),
\(\int\partial_th_t=\int\partial_tg_t=0\) for a.e. \(t\).  This is
\eqref{eq:kl-moving-quotient-l1-chain-rule}.
\end{proof}

\noindent
For a probability measure \(\nu\) on~\(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and
finite centred second moment, keep
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t .
\]
Set
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\qquad
\Psi(z):=\mathbf1_{\{|z|<1\}}+|z|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|z|\ge1\}}.
\]
For \(R>1\) put
\[
\Omega_R:=\{(x,y)\in\RR^2:R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}.
\]
The Bregman dissipation used below is first defined by annular cutoffs,
then by monotone convergence:
\[
\chi_R(x,y):=\mathbf1_{\{R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}},
\qquad
\mathcal I_{\nu,R}(t):=\frac1\pi\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y,
\]
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t):=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal I_{\nu,R}(t)
=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2\setminus\{x=y\}}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y.
\]
The second expression means the nonnegative Lebesgue integral off the
diagonal.  Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-window-bregman-identity} gives the
compact-window integrability and identifies the local entropy derivative.
The following lemma fixes once and for all the cutoff independence,
measurability in \(t\), and cutoff passage used in the Green-pairing proof.

\begin{lemma}[Annular cutoff and measurable Bregman dissipation]%
\label{lem:annular-cutoff-measurable-bregman-dissipation}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\).  Let \(g_t(x)\) and \(u_t(x)\) be Borel functions on
\([a,b]\times\RR\), with \(g_t\ge0\) and
\(\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\dd x=1\) for each \(t\).  Assume the compact-window bounds
\[
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad |u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L|x-y|
\qquad(t\in[a,b],\ x,y\in\RR)
\]
for constants \(0<m\le M<\infty\) and \(L<\infty\).  Put
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,\qquad
\Psi(z)=\mathbf1_{\{|z|<1\}}+|z|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|z|\ge1\}},
\]
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\},
\]
and, for \(R>1\),
\[
I_{\nu,R}(t)=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}\chi_R(x,y)
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\]
Then \(t\mapsto I_{\nu,R}(t)\) is Borel measurable for each \(R>1\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-cutoff-monotone-definition}
I_\nu(t):=\lim_{R\to\infty}I_{\nu,R}(t)
=\sup_{R>1}I_{\nu,R}(t)
\end{equation}
exists for every \(t\in[a,b]\) and equals the nonnegative Lebesgue integral
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-cutoff-full-integral}
I_\nu(t)=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2\setminus\{x=y\}}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\end{equation}
In particular \(I_\nu\) is Borel measurable and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-cutoff-compact-window-bound}
\int_a^b I_\nu(t)\dd t\le \frac{4C_{a,b,\nu}}{\pi}(b-a).
\end{equation}
Moreover, every uniformly bounded measurable cutoff converging pointwise to
\(1\) off the diagonal may be removed by dominated convergence using the
majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-cutoff-reusable-majorant}
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}
\left(\mathbf1_{\{|x-y|<1\}}+|x-y|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|x-y|\ge1\}}\right).
\end{equation}
Precisely, if \(\chi_n\) are measurable on \([a,b]\times\RR^2\),
\(\sup_n\|\chi_n\|_\infty<\infty\), and
\(\chi_n(t,x,y)\to1\) for a.e. \((t,x,y)\) with \(x\ne y\), then
\begin{align}
&\int_a^b\left|
\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}\chi_n(t,x,y)
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
-I_\nu(t)\right|\dd t \longrightarrow0, \label{eq:bregman-cutoff-l1-time-removal}\\
&\frac1\pi\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}\chi_n(t,x,y)
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y\dd t
\longrightarrow
\int_a^b I_\nu(t)\dd t . \label{eq:bregman-cutoff-integral-removal}
\end{align}
The value in \eqref{eq:annular-cutoff-monotone-definition} is independent of
the annular exhaustion: if \(E_n\uparrow\RR^2\setminus\{x=y\}\) is any
increasing measurable exhaustion, then
\[
\frac1\pi\iint_{E_n}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\uparrow I_\nu(t)
\qquad(n\to\infty)
\]
for every \(t\in[a,b]\).

Applied to the Poisson quotient displayed above, this gives
\[
\mathcal I_{\nu,R}(t)=I_{\nu,R}(t),\qquad
\mathcal I_\nu(t)=I_\nu(t)
\]
on the compact window \([a,b]\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The compact-range Taylor estimate
\[
0\le\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\le\frac{1}{2m}|\alpha-\beta|^2
\qquad(\alpha,\beta\in[m,M])
\]
and the Lipschitz bound give, for \(x\ne y\),
\[
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}g_t(x)\Psi(x-y).
\]
The exchanged kernel satisfies the same estimate with \(x\) and \(y\)
interchanged, and hence the symmetrized majorant is
\eqref{eq:annular-cutoff-reusable-majorant}.  Since
\[
\int_{\RR}\Psi(z)\dd z
=\int_{|z|<1}\dd z+\int_{|z|\ge1}|z|^{-2}\dd z=4
\]
and \(\int_{\RR}g_t=1\), the one-sided majorant has integral
\[
\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}C_{a,b,\nu}g_t(x)\Psi(x-y)\dd x\dd y\dd t
=4C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:annular-cutoff-compact-window-bound} after the factor
\(1/\pi\).

The function
\[
K(t,x,y):=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2},&x\ne y,\\[1ex]
0,&x=y,
\end{cases}
\]
is Borel and nonnegative.  Tonelli's theorem gives Borel measurability of
\(t\mapsto I_{\nu,R}(t)\).

The annuli \(\Omega_R\) increase to \(\RR^2\setminus\{x=y\}\) as
\(R\to\infty\), so the monotone convergence theorem gives
\eqref{eq:annular-cutoff-monotone-definition} and
\eqref{eq:annular-cutoff-full-integral}.  Taking the supremum over rational
\(R>1\) gives measurability of \(I_\nu\).  The same monotone convergence
argument proves independence of any increasing measurable exhaustion.

With \(B:=\sup_n\|\chi_n\|_\infty\), dominated convergence on
\([a,b]\times\RR^2\), using \(|\chi_nK|\le BK\), proves
\eqref{eq:bregman-cutoff-integral-removal}.  Applying dominated convergence
to
\[
\left|\iint_{\RR^2}(\chi_n(t,x,y)-1)K(t,x,y)\dd x\dd y\right|
\le \iint_{\RR^2}|1-\chi_n(t,x,y)|K(t,x,y)\dd x\dd y
\]
with \(|1-\chi_n|K\le(1+B)K\) also gives the \(L^1([a,b])\) convergence in
\eqref{eq:bregman-cutoff-l1-time-removal}.  For the displayed Poisson
quotient, \(g_t\) is smooth in \((t,x)\), and \(h_t=P_t*\nu\) is the
parameter integral of a continuous kernel, so the Borel hypotheses hold;
Lemma~\ref{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} supplies the
required compact-window bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Annular Green limits close to the full Bregman form]%
\label{lem:annular-green-limits-close-to-full-bregman}
Let \(I=[a,b]\Subset(0,\infty)\).  Let \(g_t(x)\ge0\) and
\(u_t(x)\in[m,M]\), put \(h_t=g_tu_t\), and set
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta .
\]
For \(R>1\) define the two annular Green numerators
\begin{align*}
G_R^{h,\log u}(t)
&:=\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(h_t(x)-h_t(y))(\log u_t(x)-\log u_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y,\\
G_R^{g,u}(t)
&:=\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(g_t(x)-g_t(y))(u_t(x)-u_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\end{align*}
Assume that these finite-annulus integrals are well defined, and that, for
every \(t\) in a set \(E\subset I\), both limits
\[
G^{h,\log u}(t):=\lim_{R\to\infty}G_R^{h,\log u}(t),
\qquad
G^{g,u}(t):=\lim_{R\to\infty}G_R^{g,u}(t)
\]
exist, and that the symmetrized Bregman kernel
\[
B_t(x,y):=
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)\Lambda(u_t(y),u_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}\mathbf1_{\{x\ne y\}}
\]
is dominated by an \(L^1(\RR^2)\) majorant for each \(t\in E\).  Then, for
each \(t\in E\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-green-limits-full-sym-bregman}
G^{h,\log u}(t)-G^{g,u}(t)
=\iint_{\RR^2}B_t(x,y)\dd x\dd y .
\end{equation}
Consequently, if \(B_t\) is dominated on the compact window by an
\(L^1(I\times\RR^2)\) majorant, the annular differences converge to the full
symmetrized Bregman integral in \(L^1(I)\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-green-difference-l1-full-bregman}
\int_a^b\left|
\iint_{\Omega_R}B_t(x,y)\dd x\dd y
-\iint_{\RR^2}B_t(x,y)\dd x\dd y
\right|\dd t\longrightarrow0 .
\end{equation}
In particular, whenever the full one-sided Bregman integral is finite,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-green-limits-one-sided-bregman}
\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigl(G^{h,\log u}(t)-G^{g,u}(t)\bigr)
=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For finite \(R\), put
\[
\rho_x=g_t(x),\qquad \rho_y=g_t(y),\qquad
\alpha=u_t(x),\qquad \beta=u_t(y).
\]
Since \(h_t=g_tu_t\), the difference of the two Green numerators is
pointwise equal on \(\Omega_R\) to
\[
(\rho_x\alpha-\rho_y\beta)(\log\alpha-\log\beta)
-(\rho_x-\rho_y)(\alpha-\beta).
\]
The elementary identity
\[
(\rho_x\alpha-\rho_y\beta)(\log\alpha-\log\beta)
-(\rho_x-\rho_y)(\alpha-\beta)
=\rho_x\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)+\rho_y\Lambda(\beta,\alpha)
\]
therefore gives
\[
G_R^{h,\log u}(t)-G_R^{g,u}(t)
=\iint_{\Omega_R}B_t(x,y)\dd x\dd y .
\]
Letting \(R\to\infty\) and using the assumed existence of the two Green
limits gives the left side of
\eqref{eq:annular-green-limits-full-sym-bregman}.  The right side converges
to the full nonnegative Bregman integral by dominated convergence, because
\(\mathbf1_{\Omega_R}\to1\) off the diagonal and \(B_t\) is integrably
dominated.  The compact-window \(L^1(I)\) convergence
\eqref{eq:annular-green-difference-l1-full-bregman} is the same dominated
convergence argument on \(I\times\RR^2\).  Finally, exchanging \(x\) and
\(y\) in the second term of the symmetrized integral gives
\[
\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(y)\Lambda(u_t(y),u_t(x))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
=\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:annular-green-limits-one-sided-bregman}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Poisson kernel \(L^1\) absolute continuity and derivative bounds]%
\label{lem:poisson-kernel-l1-ac-bounds}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\) and let \(\mu\) be a finite signed measure on
\(\RR\).  With
\[
P_t(x)=\frac1\pi\frac{t}{t^2+x^2},
\]
the map
\[
t\longmapsto P_t*\mu
\]
belongs to \(AC([a,b];L^1(\RR))\).  Its \(L^1\)-derivative is, for every
\(t\in(a,b)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-convolution-time-derivative-l1}
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}(P_t*\mu)=(\partial_tP_t)*\mu
\qquad\text{in }L^1(\RR).
\end{equation}
Moreover the differentiated kernels have the scaled forms
\begin{align}
\partial_tP_t(x)&=t^{-2}Q_0(x/t),
&Q_0(y)&:=\frac1\pi\frac{y^2-1}{(1+y^2)^2}, \label{eq:poisson-Qt-scaled}\\
\partial_xP_t(x)&=t^{-2}Q_1(x/t),
&Q_1(y)&:=-\frac{2y}{\pi(1+y^2)^2}, \label{eq:poisson-Qx-scaled}\\
\partial_x^2P_t(x)&=t^{-3}Q_2(x/t),
&Q_2(y)&:=\frac{6y^2-2}{\pi(1+y^2)^3}. \label{eq:poisson-Qxx-scaled}
\end{align}
These profile functions are in \(L^1(\RR)\), with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-Q-profile-l1-norms}
\|Q_0\|_1=\frac2\pi,\qquad
\|Q_1\|_1=\frac2\pi,\qquad
\|Q_2\|_1=\frac{3\sqrt3}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, uniformly for \(t\in[a,b]\),
\begin{align}
\|\partial_tP_t\|_1&\le \frac{2}{\pi a},
&
\|(\partial_tP_t)*\mu\|_1&\le \frac{2}{\pi a}\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}},
\label{eq:poisson-dt-uniform-l1-bound}\\
\|\partial_xP_t\|_1&\le \frac{2}{\pi a},
&
\|(\partial_xP_t)*\mu\|_1&\le \frac{2}{\pi a}\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}},
\label{eq:poisson-dx-uniform-l1-bound}\\
\|\partial_x^2P_t\|_1&\le \frac{3\sqrt3}{2\pi a^2},
&
\|(\partial_x^2P_t)*\mu\|_1&\le
\frac{3\sqrt3}{2\pi a^2}\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\label{eq:poisson-dxx-uniform-l1-bound}
\end{align}
The same kernels are dominated on the compact window by fixed \(L^1\)
majorants:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-derivative-pointwise-majorants}
|\partial_tP_t(x)|+|\partial_xP_t(x)|
\le \frac{2}{\pi(x^2+a^2)},\qquad
|\partial_x^2P_t(x)|
\le \frac{6b}{\pi(x^2+a^2)^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The derivative formulas follow by direct differentiation of
\(P_t(x)=t^{-1}P_1(x/t)\).  For \(Q_0\), use
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd y}\frac{y}{1+y^2}
=\frac{1-y^2}{(1+y^2)^2}.
\]
Splitting at \(y=1\) and using evenness gives
\[
\|Q_0\|_1
=\frac2\pi\left\{
\int_0^1\frac{1-y^2}{(1+y^2)^2}\dd y
+\int_1^\infty\frac{y^2-1}{(1+y^2)^2}\dd y
\right\}
=\frac2\pi.
\]
Similarly,
\[
\|Q_1\|_1
=\frac4\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{y}{(1+y^2)^2}\dd y
=\frac2\pi.
\]
Finally \(Q_2=Q_1'\).  On \([0,\infty)\) the function \(Q_1\) decreases
from \(0\) to its minimum at \(y=1/\sqrt3\) and then increases back to
\(0\).  Hence the total variation on both half-lines is
\[
\|Q_2\|_1
=4\left|Q_1(1/\sqrt3)\right|
=\frac{3\sqrt3}{2\pi}.
\]
The scaling relations give
\[
\|\partial_tP_t\|_1=t^{-1}\|Q_0\|_1,\qquad
\|\partial_xP_t\|_1=t^{-1}\|Q_1\|_1,\qquad
\|\partial_x^2P_t\|_1=t^{-2}\|Q_2\|_1,
\]
and Young's inequality for convolution with a finite signed measure gives
the convolution bounds.

It remains only to justify absolute continuity as a Banach-space statement.
The pointwise estimates
\[
|\partial_tP_t(x)|\le\frac{1}{\pi(x^2+a^2)},\qquad
|\partial_xP_t(x)|\le\frac{1}{\pi(x^2+a^2)}
\]
follow from
\(|x^2-t^2|\le x^2+t^2\) and \(2t|x|\le x^2+t^2\).  Also
\[
|\partial_x^2P_t(x)|
=\frac{2t|3x^2-t^2|}{\pi(t^2+x^2)^3}
\le \frac{6t}{\pi(t^2+x^2)^2}
\le \frac{6b}{\pi(x^2+a^2)^2}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:poisson-derivative-pointwise-majorants}; the right
sides are integrable.  Dominated convergence therefore makes
\(t\mapsto\partial_tP_t\) continuous from \([a,b]\) to \(L^1(\RR)\), and
the pointwise fundamental theorem gives, as a Bochner integral in \(L^1\),
\[
P_t-P_r=\int_r^t\partial_sP_s\,\dd s\qquad(a\le r,t\le b).
\]
Convolving with \(\mu\) preserves this identity in \(L^1\), because
\(\|f*\mu\|_1\le\|f\|_1\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\).  Thus
\[
P_t*\mu-P_r*\mu
=\int_r^t(\partial_sP_s)*\mu\,\dd s
\]
in \(L^1(\RR)\), and the integrand is continuous in \(L^1\).  This proves
\(t\mapsto P_t*\mu\in AC([a,b];L^1)\) and
\eqref{eq:poisson-convolution-time-derivative-l1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded Lipschitz quotient implies \(H'=-I\)]%
\label{prop:compact-window-bregman-identity}
Let \(I=[a,b]\Subset(0,\infty)\).  Let \(\mu\) and \(\eta\) be probability
measures on~\(\RR\), and put
\[
h_t=P_t*\mu,\qquad g_t=P_t*\eta,\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t .
\]
Assume that the quotient has one compact range and one Lipschitz constant on
the whole window:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-quotient-hypothesis}
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad |u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L|x-y|
\qquad(t\in I,\ x,y\in\RR),
\end{equation}
for constants \(0<m\le M<\infty\) and \(L<\infty\).  Define
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\qquad
\Psi(z):=\mathbf1_{\{|z|<1\}}+|z|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|z|\ge1\}},
\]
and
\[
C_I:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\}.
\]
For \(R>1\), let
\[
\Omega_R:=\{(x,y):R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\},
\qquad
\mathscr I_R(t):=\frac1\pi\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\]
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-full-dissipation}
\mathscr I(t):=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathscr I_R(t)
=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2\setminus\{x=y\}}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y,
\end{equation}
where the second expression is the nonnegative Lebesgue integral.  Then all
of the following conclusions hold on the same window \(I\).

\begin{enumerate}
\item For \(x\ne y\) and \(t\in I\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-majorant}
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_I g_t(x)\Psi(x-y),
\end{equation}
and the same estimate holds with \(x\) and \(y\) interchanged.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-sym-integral-bound}
\int_I\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{
g_s(x)\Lambda(u_s(x),u_s(y))
+g_s(y)\Lambda(u_s(y),u_s(x))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y\dd s
\le 8C_I|I|,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-one-sided-bound}
\int_I \mathscr I(s)\dd s\le \frac{4C_I}{\pi}|I|.
\end{equation}
The function \(\mathscr I\) is Borel measurable, locally integrable on the
window, and independent of the increasing annular exhaustion.  More generally, if
\(\chi_n\) are measurable on \(I\times\RR^2\),
\(\sup_n\|\chi_n\|_\infty<\infty\), and
\(\chi_n(t,x,y)\to1\) for a.e. \(x\ne y\), then the cutoff integrals converge
to \(\mathscr I\) in \(L^1(I,\dd t)\), with the single majorant
\[
C_I\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y).
\]

\item Let
\[
H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\int_{\RR}h_t(x)\log u_t(x)\dd x .
\]
Then \(H\) is finite and absolutely continuous on \(I\).  There is a single
null set \(N_I\subset I\) such that, for every \(s\in I\setminus N_I\), the
\(L^1\)-time derivatives of \(h_s\) and \(g_s\) exist and the moving-quotient
chain rule holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-window-chain-rule-single-null-set}
H'(s)=\int_{\RR}\log u_s\,\partial_sh_s\,\dd x
-\int_{\RR}(u_s-1)\partial_sg_s\,\dd x .
\end{equation}
At the same times the two noncompact Cauchy Green formulas hold:
\begin{align}
\int_{\RR}\log u_s(x)\,\partial_sh_s(x)\dd x
&=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(h_s(x)-h_s(y))(\log u_s(x)-\log u_s(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y, \label{eq:compact-window-h-logu-green}\\
\int_{\RR}u_s(x)\,\partial_sg_s(x)\dd x
&=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(g_s(x)-g_s(y))(u_s(x)-u_s(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y. \label{eq:compact-window-g-u-green}
\end{align}

\item The annular one-sided dissipations converge to the full one-sided
Bregman integral in \(L^1(I)\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-window-annular-l1-convergence}
\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_I
\left|\mathscr I_R(s)-\mathscr I(s)\right|\dd s=0.
\end{equation}
For every \(s\in I\setminus N_I\), the two Green limits, the chain rule, and
the Bregman algebra combine to give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-window-bregman-derivative}
-H'(s)
=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_s(x)\Lambda(u_s(x),u_s(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
=\mathscr I(s).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The compact-range Taylor estimate
\[
0\le\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\le\frac{1}{2m}|\alpha-\beta|^2
\qquad(\alpha,\beta\in[m,M])
\]
and \eqref{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-quotient-hypothesis} give
\eqref{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-majorant}; for \(|x-y|<1\) use the
Lipschitz part, and for \(|x-y|\ge1\) use \(|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le M-m\).  The
exchanged estimate is identical.  Since
\[
\int_{\RR}\Psi(z)\dd z
=\int_{|z|<1}\dd z+\int_{|z|\ge1}|z|^{-2}\dd z=4
\]
and \(\int_{\RR}g_t=1\), the one-sided and symmetrized estimates integrate to
\eqref{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-sym-integral-bound} and
\eqref{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-one-sided-bound}.  Lemma
\ref{lem:annular-cutoff-measurable-bregman-dissipation} gives the monotone
definition of \(\mathscr I\), measurability, exhaustion independence, and the
stated cutoff removal, with \(C_I\) replacing \(C_{a,b,\nu}\).

Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-kernel-l1-ac-bounds}, applied once to \(\mu\) and
once to \(\eta\), gives
\[
h,g\in AC(I;L^1(\RR)),\qquad
\partial_s h_s=(\partial_sP_s)*\mu,\qquad
\partial_s g_s=(\partial_sP_s)*\eta
\]
for a.e. \(s\in I\), and supplies uniform \(L^1\) bounds for
\(\partial_sP_s\), \(\partial_xP_s\), and \(\partial_x^2P_s\) on the same
window.  The quotient bounds give \(m g_s\le h_s\le M g_s\), hence
\(H(s)=\int h_s\log u_s\,\dd x\) is finite.  Lemma
\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification} therefore applies on \(I\), proving absolute
continuity of \(H\) and the chain rule outside a null set.  Let \(N_I\) be
the union of that null set with the null set on which the \(L^1\)-time
derivatives of \(h_s\) or \(g_s\) fail to exist.

For \(s\in I\setminus N_I\), Lemma
\ref{lem:cauchy-normalization-bounded-test-green} applies to the Poisson
solutions \(h_s=P_s*\mu\) and \(g_s=P_s*\eta\); its hypotheses are exactly
the finite-measure convolution structure and the compact-window kernel
bounds just supplied by Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-kernel-l1-ac-bounds}.  The
tests \(\log u_s\) and \(u_s\) are bounded Lipschitz uniformly on \(I\), because
\eqref{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-quotient-hypothesis} holds and
\((\log r)'\le1/m\) on \([m,M]\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:compact-window-h-logu-green} and
\eqref{eq:compact-window-g-u-green}.  Since \(\int_{\RR}\partial_sg_s\,\dd x=0\)
at such a time, the second term in
\eqref{eq:compact-window-chain-rule-single-null-set} may be written as
\(\int_{\RR}u_s\partial_sg_s\,\dd x\).

The \(L^1(I)\) annular convergence
\eqref{eq:compact-window-annular-l1-convergence} is the cutoff-removal
statement from the first paragraph applied to \(\mathbf1_{\Omega_R}\).  To
identify the derivative, apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:annular-green-limits-close-to-full-bregman} at a fixed
\(s\in I\setminus N_I\).  The two Green limits exist by the preceding
paragraph, and the symmetrized Bregman kernel is dominated by the majorant in
\eqref{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-majorant} plus its exchanged copy.  Thus
\[
\frac1{2\pi}
\left[
\lim_{R\to\infty}G_R^{h,\log u}(s)
-\lim_{R\to\infty}G_R^{g,u}(s)
\right]
=\mathscr I(s).
\]
The chain rule and the two Green formulas give the same left side as
\(-H'(s)\).  This proves \eqref{eq:compact-window-bregman-derivative}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This proposition is the compact-window domain and derivative part of Main
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-C}.  The following theorem adds the endpoint decay
\(D_{\rm KL}(h_T\|g_T)\to0\) and integrates the derivative identity to obtain
the tail formula.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Poisson quotient KL tail identity]%
\label{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail}
\label{cor:poisson-kl-doob-bregman-tail}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar{\gamma}$ and finite
centred second moment.  With \(h_t,g_t,u_t\) and \(\mathcal I_\nu\) as above,
\(\mathcal I_\nu\) is locally integrable on \((0,\infty)\), and for every
\(t>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set \(H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\).  For \(0<t<T\), apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} on the compact
window \([t,T]\).  It gives the bounded Lipschitz quotient hypothesis
\eqref{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-quotient-hypothesis} of
Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-window-bregman-identity}, with
\(\mu=\nu\), \(\eta=\delta_{\bar\gamma}\), and
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\dd\nu(\gamma),\qquad
m=\frac1{2(1+\mu_2/t^2)},\qquad
M=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{t^2},\qquad
L=3t^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/t^2\bigr).
\]
The abstract dissipation \(\mathscr I\) in
\eqref{eq:abstract-bounded-lipschitz-full-dissipation} is exactly
\(\mathcal I_\nu\) by the annular definition preceding
Lemma~\ref{lem:annular-cutoff-measurable-bregman-dissipation}.  Hence
Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-window-bregman-identity} gives
\(H\in AC([t,T])\), \(\mathcal I_\nu\in L^1([t,T])\), and
\(H'=-\mathcal I_\nu\) outside a single null set on that window.  Therefore
\[
H(t)-H(T)=\int_t^T \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-second-moment-kl-decay} gives \(H(T)\to0\) as
\(T\to\infty\).  Since \(\mathcal I_\nu\ge0\), monotone convergence yields
\[
H(t)=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s .
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Together with Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-window-bregman-identity}, this
proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main-C}.  The coefficient transfer after this point
combines Theorem~\ref{thm:main-A} with the tail identity.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Tail-integral coefficient transfer]
\label{rem:no-pointwise-dissipation-from-finite-moments}
The coefficient transfer below is formulated for tail integrals
\(\int_t^\infty\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s\) and fixed multiplicative-window
averages.  Passing from a tail expansion for the primitive
\(H(t)=\int_t^\infty\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s\), even with a full finite-moment
coefficient hierarchy, to pointwise asymptotics for \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\)
requires extra differentiated-remainder input.

For example, if the centred law is compactly supported, say
\(|\gamma-\bar{\gamma}|\le M\), then for each fixed retained order \(L\) and
all \(t>2M\) the Cayley generating function gives the uniformly convergent
expansion, with \(\mu_n=\EE(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^n\),
\[
\frac{(P_t*\nu)(\bar{\gamma}+ty)}{P_t(ty)}-1
=\sum_{n=2}^{L}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}+\rho_{L,t}(y),
\qquad
\rho_{L,t}=\sum_{n>L}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}.
\]
The same uniform majorant gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^\infty}
+\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
+\|t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^\infty}
+\|t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
=O_\nu(t^{-(L+1)}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Under such a differentiated remainder bound, one may
differentiate the finite quotient expansion and combine it with
\eqref{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation}; this auxiliary
observation requires more than the finite-moment coefficient hierarchy.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Dissipation-tail and window-average coefficient transfer]%
\label{prop:dissipation-tail-coefficient-expansion}
Let \(N\ge2\), and assume that \(\nu\) has finite centred
\((2N-2)\)th absolute moment.  Then the KL dissipation tail has the same
universal coefficient polynomial layer as the entropy expansion:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-expansion}
\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N})
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for every \(2\le M\le N\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-recovery}
A_{2M}(\nu)
=
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{2M}
\left(
\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
-\sum_{m=2}^{M-1}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
\right),
\end{equation}
with the sum interpreted as zero when \(M=2\).

More locally, for each fixed \(\lambda>1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-finite-window-coefficients}
\int_t^{\lambda t} \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)(1-\lambda^{-2m})t^{-2m}
+o(t^{-2N}).
\end{equation}
Thus the averaged dissipation on a multiplicative window satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-window-average-coefficients}
\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)t}\int_t^{\lambda t} \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}
\frac{1-\lambda^{-2m}}{\lambda-1}\,A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-(2m+1)}
+o(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\end{equation}
If, in addition, \(0<\eta\le1\) and the centred
\((2N-2+\eta)\)th absolute moment is finite, then the error terms above have
the following positive-margin bounds:
\[
\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+O_\nu(t^{-(2N+\eta)})
\]
and, for each fixed \(\lambda>1\),
\[
\int_t^{\lambda t} \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)(1-\lambda^{-2m})t^{-2m}
 +O_{\nu,N,\eta,\lambda}(t^{-(2N+\eta)}).
\]
Consequently the averaged-window remainder is
\[
O_{\nu,N,\eta,\lambda}(t^{-(2N+1+\eta)}).
\]
No pointwise expansion of \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\) is asserted.

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The tail identity \eqref{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation} identifies
\(\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu\) with \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\).  Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} gives
\eqref{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-expansion} and hence the coefficient
recovery formula \eqref{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-recovery}.

For the finite window, use the already proved tail identity twice:
\[
H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t),
\qquad
\int_t^{\lambda t}\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s=H(t)-H(\lambda t).
\]
Substitute the expansion of \(H\) at \(t\) and at \(\lambda t\).  The
coefficient of \(t^{-2m}\) is
\(A_{2m}(\nu)-A_{2m}(\nu)\lambda^{-2m}\), which gives
\eqref{eq:bregman-finite-window-coefficients}; division by
\((\lambda-1)t\) gives \eqref{eq:bregman-window-average-coefficients}.
The positive-margin assertion follows from the explicit remainder estimate
\eqref{eq:positive-margin-retained-order-theorem} for \(H(t)\) and
\(H(\lambda t)\).
\end{proof}
