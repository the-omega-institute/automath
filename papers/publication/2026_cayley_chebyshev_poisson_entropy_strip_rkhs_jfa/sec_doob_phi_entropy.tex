\section{KL Tail Identity for the Poisson Doob Quotient}%
\label{sec:doob-phi-entropy}

Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\).
For \(t>0\) put
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad
u_t:=h_t/g_t .
\]
The Cauchy generator is normalized by
\[
(Lf)(x)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y,
\]
so that \(\partial_tP_t=LP_t\).  This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail}
in the Cauchy normalization displayed above, namely the KL-specialized
two-point Bregman dissipation identity for the moving Poisson quotient
\(u_t=h_t/g_t\), and then records the coefficient consequence.

\noindent\textbf{The quotient-domain input.}
The only point not supplied by the abstract Hardy--Stein/Bregman formalism is
the moving matched-Poisson domain verification.
Theorem~\ref{thm:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} is the
domain theorem: on every compact time window \(0<a<b<\infty\), it records
\[
\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)}\le u_t\le
2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},\qquad
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le
3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr)|x-y|,
\]
and the logarithmic Bregman kernel divided by the Cauchy singular kernel is
dominated by an explicit integrable majorant
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}
\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{|x-y|<1\}}+|x-y|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|x-y|\ge1\}}\bigr).
\]
The cutoff theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification}, then records the \(L^1\) chain rule and the two
annular Cauchy Green formulae for \((h_t,\log u_t)\) and \((g_t,u_t)\).
This is the new Cauchy/KL closure step for the nonstationary quotient
\((P_t*\nu)/P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\); the preceding lemmas only prove the
pieces that the domain theorem assembles.

The identity is in the de Bruijn/Stam and relative-entropy dissipation
lineage
\cite{DeBruijn1953,Stam1959,Blachman1965,Barron1986,
JohnsonBarron2004,MadimanBarron2007,Verdu2010Mismatched,
HirataNemotoYoshida2012RelativeEntropy,Yoshida2017RelativeEntropyDiffusion},
and it uses the same Bregman algebra that underlies nonlocal
Hardy--Stein/Sobolev--Bregman formulas
\cite{Bregman1967,BanuelosBogdanLuks2016HardyStein,
BanuelosKim2019HardySteinNonsymmetric,
BogdanGrzywnyPietruskaPalubaRutkowski2023NonlinearNonlocalDouglas,
Gutowski2023HardySteinPureJump,GutowskiKwasnicki2025BeurlingDenySobolevBregman,
BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein}.  Here no abstract
Hardy--Stein theorem is invoked as a black box.  The proof uses three local
lemmas, followed by the endpoint decay lemma:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(1)}&
\text{compact-window quotient bounds and the single Bregman majorant},\\
\text{(2)}&
\text{the Cauchy Green formula, including the normalization ledger},\\
\text{(3)}&
\text{the }L^1\text{ chain rule and the two-point Bregman algebra},\\
\text{(4)}&
\text{the endpoint decay }D_{\rm KL}(h_T\|g_T)\to0 .
\end{array}
\]
Lemma~\ref{thm:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} supplies the
only distribution-dependent estimates for the nonstationary quotient
\((P_t*\nu)/P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing} fixes the generator
sign and the \(1/\pi,1/(2\pi)\) constants once.
Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification} then combines the chain rule, those two
Green pairings, and the elementary logarithmic Bregman identity to give
\(H'(t)=-\mathcal I_\nu(t)\) on compact windows.  The final theorem follows
from this derivative identity, nonnegativity of \(\mathcal I_\nu\), and the
separate decay \(H(T)\to0\).  The coefficient expansion itself remains the
Cayley quotient-mode result of Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}; the
identity below only converts those KL tails into dissipation tails.

\begin{lemma}[Second-moment KL decay to the translated Poisson profile]%
\label{lem:poisson-second-moment-kl-decay}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean $\bar{\gamma}$ and
finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar{\gamma}).
\]
Then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}$ and $x=\bar{\gamma}+ty$.  Since
$g_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty)=(\pi t)^{-1}(1+y^2)^{-1}$,
\[
u_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty):=\frac{h_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty)}{g_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty)}
=\int_{\RR}\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
For $A>0$ decompose $\nu=\nu_A+\nu_A^c$ according to
$|X_c|\le A$ and $|X_c|>A$.  The elementary kernel bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-moment-kl-kernel-bound}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
\le 2(1+\varepsilon^2)
\qquad(y,\varepsilon\in\RR)
\end{equation}
implies
\[
0\le u_{t,A}^c(y):=\int_{|X_c|>A}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
\le 2\int_{|X_c|>A}\left(1+\frac{X_c^2}{t^2}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
Hence, for $t\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-moment-kl-tail-sup}
\sup_y u_{t,A}^c(y)
\le 2\nu(|X_c|>A)+2\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu .
\end{equation}

The bounded part has total mass $m_A:=\nu(|X_c|\le A)$ and first centred
moment $b_A:=\int_{|X_c|\le A}X_c\dd\nu$.  The exact expansion
\[
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
=1+\frac{2y}{1+y^2}\varepsilon
+\frac{(3y^2-1)\varepsilon^2-2y\varepsilon^3}
{(1+y^2)(1+(y-\varepsilon)^2)}
\]
shows that, for each fixed $A$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-truncated-quotient-limit}
u_{t,A}(y):=\int_{|X_c|\le A}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
=m_A+\frac{2yb_A}{t(1+y^2)}+O_A(t^{-2})
\end{equation}
uniformly in~$y$.  Since
$b_A=-\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c\dd\nu$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
|b_A|\le \left(\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Combining this with
\eqref{eq:second-moment-kl-tail-sup} and
\eqref{eq:bounded-truncated-quotient-limit} yields
\[
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|u_t-1\|_{L^\infty(\RR)}
\le 1-m_A+2\nu(|X_c|>A)+2\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu .
\]
Letting $A\to\infty$ gives $\|u_t-1\|_\infty\to0$.

For all sufficiently large $t$ we therefore have
$|u_t-1|\le1/2$.  With $\delta_t=u_t-1$ and using
$\int g_t\delta_t=\int(h_t-g_t)=0$,
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\int_{\RR}g_t\bigl((1+\delta_t)\log(1+\delta_t)-\delta_t\bigr)\dd x
\le 2\|\delta_t\|_\infty^2,
\]
because $(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\le2s^2$ for $|s|\le1/2$.  The right-hand side
tends to zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Compact-window quotient bounds and Bregman majorant]%
\label{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain}
\label{thm:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain}
\label{lem:single-compact-window-bregman-majorant-constants}
\label{lem:bregman-kernel-l1-cutoff-removal}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\)
with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and finite centred second moment
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^2\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
For
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t,
\qquad t\in[a,b],
\]
put
\[
m:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\qquad
M:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},\qquad
L:=3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr).
\]
Then, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x,y\in\RR\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML}
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L|x-y|.
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\log u_t\) is bounded and Lipschitz uniformly on
\([a,b]\).  Moreover, with
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\qquad
\Psi(z):=\mathbf1_{\{|z|<1\}}+|z|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|z|\ge1\}},
\]
and
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\},
\]
the one-sided logarithmic Bregman kernel satisfies, for \(x\ne y\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-one-sided-bregman-majorant}
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}\,g_t(x)\Psi(x-y),
\end{equation}
and the exchanged term satisfies the same estimate with \(x\) and \(y\)
interchanged.  Hence the symmetrized Bregman kernel in
Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification} is dominated on
\([a,b]\times\RR^2\) by the integrable majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-sym-bregman-integrable-majorant}
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y),
\end{equation}
whose integral over \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) is \(8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\).  In
particular, if
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t):=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\]
is understood as the nonnegative Lebesgue integral off the diagonal, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-lemma-one-sided-majorant-integral}
\int_a^b\mathcal I_\nu(t)\dd t
\le \frac{4C_{a,b,\nu}}{\pi}(b-a).
\end{equation}
The one-sided and symmetrized Bregman kernels are in
\(L^1([a,b]\times\RR^2)\); hence every measurable cutoff
\(0\le\chi_n\le1\) with \(\chi_n(t,x,y)\to1\) off the diagonal may be removed
by dominated convergence.  For each fixed \(t>0\) the same cutoff statement
holds on \(\RR^2\), after choosing any compact window containing \(t\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With \(z=(x-\bar{\gamma})/t\), \(X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}\), and
\[
R_\varepsilon(z):=\frac{1+z^2}{1+(z-\varepsilon)^2},
\]
one has
\[
u_t(x)=\int_{\RR}R_{X_c/t}\!\left(\frac{x-\bar{\gamma}}{t}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
The elementary inequalities
\[
R_\varepsilon(z)\le2(1+\varepsilon^2),\qquad
R_\varepsilon(z)\ge\frac{1}{2(1+\varepsilon^2)}
\]
give the displayed lower and upper bounds in
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML}, uniformly on
\([a,b]\times\RR\).  Indeed, the upper bound follows immediately from
\(\int X_c^2\dd\nu=\mu_2\).  For the lower bound, Jensen's inequality applied
to the convex function \(r\mapsto 1/r\) gives
\[
\int_{\RR}\frac{1}{2(1+X_c^2/t^2)}\dd\nu(\gamma)
\ge
\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/t^2)}
\ge
\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)}.
\]
Also
\[
\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)
=\frac{2\varepsilon(1-w^2)-2\varepsilon^2w}{(1+w^2)^2},
\qquad w=z-\varepsilon.
\]
Since
\[
\sup_w\frac{2|1-w^2|}{(1+w^2)^2}\le2,\qquad
\sup_w\frac{2|w|}{(1+w^2)^2}\le1,
\]
we have
\[
|\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)|\le 2|\varepsilon|+\varepsilon^2
\le 3(1+\varepsilon^2).
\]
After dividing by \(t\) and using \(t\ge a\),
\[
|\partial_xu_t(x)|
\le \frac{3}{t}\int_{\RR}\left(1+\frac{X_c^2}{t^2}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma)
\le 3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr)=L.
\]
This proves the Lipschitz bound in
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML}.  Since
\((\log r)'=1/r\le1/m\) on \([m,M]\), the same bounds also make
\(\log u_t\) uniformly bounded and Lipschitz.

For fixed \(\beta\in[m,M]\), the function
\(\alpha\mapsto \Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\) has first derivative zero at
\(\alpha=\beta\) and second derivative \(1/\alpha\le1/m\) on \([m,M]\).
Taylor's formula therefore gives the compact-range quadratic estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-compact-quadratic-bregman}
0\le \Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\le \frac{1}{2m}|\alpha-\beta|^2
\qquad(\alpha,\beta\in[m,M]).
\end{equation}
Combining this with the Lipschitz estimate gives
\[
\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le \frac{L^2}{2m}|x-y|^2\qquad(|x-y|<1).
\]
The same quadratic estimate and the range bound
\(|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le M-m\) give
\[
\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le \frac{(M-m)^2}{2m}\qquad(|x-y|\ge1).
\]
Dividing by \((x-y)^2\) proves
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-one-sided-bregman-majorant}.  The exchanged term is
identical after switching \(x\) and \(y\).  Finally
\[
\int_{\RR}\Psi(z)\dd z
=\int_{|z|<1}\dd z+\int_{|z|\ge1}|z|^{-2}\dd z=4,
\]
and \(\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\dd x=1\).  Thus the one-sided majorant integrates over
\([a,b]\times\RR^2\) to \(4C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\), while adding the exchanged
majorant gives
\[
\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y)\dd x\dd y\dd t
=8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)<\infty .
\]
Consequently the one-sided integral estimate is exactly
\eqref{eq:single-lemma-one-sided-majorant-integral}.  The Bregman kernels are
dominated by the displayed \(L^1\) functions, so all stated cutoff removals
follow from dominated convergence; the diagonal is a null set.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cauchy Green formula and normalization ledger]%
\label{lem:kl-main-generator-convention-domain}
\label{lem:poisson-cauchy-generator-l1-domain}
\label{lem:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing}
\label{lem:cauchy-normalization-bounded-test-green}
\label{lem:cauchy-bregman-normalization-ledger}
Use the Fourier convention
\[
\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\RR}f(x)e^{-\ii x\xi}\dd x .
\]
Then
\[
\widehat{P_t}(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|},\qquad \partial_tP_t=LP_t,
\]
where
\[
Lf(x)=\frac1\pi\,\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y .
\]
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), let \(\mu\) be a finite signed measure on \(\RR\), and
put \(p_t=P_t*\mu\).  For \(R>1\) define
\[
(L_R f)(x):=\frac1\pi\int_{R^{-1}<|z|<R}
\frac{f(x+z)-f(x)}{z^2}\dd z,
\qquad
\Omega_R:=\{(x,y):R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-generator-annular-l1-estimate}
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t\|_1
\le
\frac{\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}{\pi R}
\left(\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1+4\right).
\end{equation}
If \(q_t:\RR\to\RR\) is a bounded Lipschitz test family with
\[
M_*:=\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|q_t\|_\infty<\infty,\qquad
L_*:=\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\operatorname{Lip}(q_t)<\infty ,
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-finite}
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)L_Rp_t(x)\dd x
=-\frac1{2\pi}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\end{equation}
and
\begin{align}
&\sup_{\substack{t\in[a,b]\\R>1}}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\left|
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\right|\dd x\dd y \notag\\
&\qquad\le
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\left(2L_*\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_xP_s\|_1+8M_*\right).
\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-total-variation-bound}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-limit}
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)\partial_tp_t(x)\dd x
=-\frac1{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\end{equation}
and the cutoff error is bounded by
\begin{align}
&\sup_{t\in[a,b]}
\left|
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)\partial_tp_t(x)\dd x
+\frac1{2\pi}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\right| \notag\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{M_*\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}{\pi R}
\left(\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1+4\right).
\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-time-dependent-cutoff-error}
\end{align}
Thus the normalization ledger is: \(L\) carries the prefactor \(1/\pi\);
each symmetrized Green pairing carries \(-1/(2\pi)\); after the two
logarithmic Bregman pairings are combined and the variables are exchanged,
the final one-sided dissipation carries \(1/\pi\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Fourier transform of the Cauchy kernel is
\(\widehat{P_t}(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|}\), and the displayed principal-value
operator has multiplier \(-|\xi|\).  Hence
\[
\widehat{\partial_tP_t}(\xi)=-|\xi|e^{-t|\xi|}
=\widehat{LP_t}(\xi).
\]
Translation invariance gives
\(L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t=(L_RP_t-\partial_tP_t)*\mu\).  The near-diagonal
omission is controlled by the symmetric Taylor expansion of
\(P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t\), whose odd first-order term integrates to zero, giving
\[
\left\|\frac1\pi\int_{|z|<R^{-1}}
\frac{P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t(\cdot)}{z^2}\dd z\right\|_1
\le \frac{1}{\pi R}\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1 .
\]
The far-tail omission uses \(\|P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t\|_1\le2\), and is at most
\(4/(\pi R)\).  Convolving with \(\mu\) proves
\eqref{eq:poisson-generator-annular-l1-estimate}.

For fixed \(R\), Fubini is legitimate because \(q_t\) is bounded and
\(\int_{R^{-1}<|z|<R}\|p_t(\cdot+z)-p_t\|_1z^{-2}\dd z<\infty\).  Changing
variables in one copy and symmetrizing gives
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-finite}.  The limiting annuli are
controlled by
\[
\iint_{\Omega_R}\left|
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\right|\dd x\dd y
\le 2L_*\|\partial_xp_t\|_1+8M_*\|p_t\|_1 ,
\]
using \(\|p_t(\cdot+z)-p_t\|_1\le |z|\|\partial_xp_t\|_1\) for \(|z|<1\)
and \(\le2\|p_t\|_1\) for \(|z|\ge1\).  Since
\(\|p_t\|_1\le\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\) and
\(\|\partial_xp_t\|_1\le\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\|\partial_xP_t\|_1\), this proves
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-total-variation-bound}.  Combining the
finite-annulus identity with
\[
\left|\int_{\RR}q_t(L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t)\dd x\right|
\le \|q_t\|_\infty\|L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t\|_1
\]
gives \eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-time-dependent-cutoff-error}, and then
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-limit}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Chain rule and logarithmic Bregman algebra]%
\label{prop:abstract-cutoff-bregman-kl}
\label{lem:cauchy-kl-cutoff-green-identity}
\label{thm:external-cauchy-kl-hardystein-input}
\label{prop:self-contained-cauchy-kl-hardystein}
\label{lem:poisson-kl-l1-ac-chain-rule}
\label{thm:kl-domain-verification}
\label{lem:poisson-quotient-cutoff-bregman-verification}
\label{lem:kl-tail-chain-rule-cutoff-passage}
\label{prop:kl-tail-cutoff-inputs}
\label{thm:kl-local-doob-bregman-domain-closure}
\label{prop:kl-local-doob-bregman-domain-closure}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability measure with mean
\(\bar{\gamma}\) and finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t,
\qquad H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t),
\]
and
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,\qquad
\mathcal I_\nu(t):=\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\]
Then \(H\in AC([a,b])\), \(\mathcal I_\nu\in L^1([a,b])\), and for a.e.
\(t\in[a,b]\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-moving-quotient-l1-chain-rule}
H'(t)=
\int_{\RR}\log u_t\,\partial_th_t\,\dd x
-\int_{\RR}(u_t-1)\partial_tg_t\,\dd x
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:external-cauchy-kl-hardystein-input}
H'(t)=-\mathcal I_\nu(t).
\end{equation}
More explicitly, the two Green pairings used in the last identity are
\begin{align}
\int_{\RR}\log u_t(x)\,\partial_th_t(x)\dd x
&=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(h_t(x)-h_t(y))(\log u_t(x)-\log u_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y, \label{eq:domain-theorem-h-logu-green}\\
\int_{\RR}u_t(x)\,\partial_tg_t(x)\dd x
&=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(g_t(x)-g_t(y))(u_t(x)-u_t(y))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y. \label{eq:domain-theorem-g-u-green}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^2\dd\nu(\gamma),\quad
m:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\quad
M:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},\quad
L:=3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} gives
\[
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad |u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L|x-y|
\]
and the same bounds for \(\log u_t\) on the compact quotient range.
The maps \(t\mapsto h_t,g_t,\partial_th_t,\partial_tg_t\) are locally
absolutely continuous in \(L^1\), since
\(\partial_t^kP_t(x)=t^{-k-1}Q_k(x/t)\), \(k=0,1,2\), with
\(Q_k\in L^1(\RR)\).

The chain rule is elementary on the cone \(s>0\), \(m\le r/s\le M\).  For
\(F(r,s)=r\log(r/s)\), the derivatives
\[
\partial_rF=\log(r/s)+1,\qquad \partial_sF=-r/s
\]
are bounded there, and line segments stay in the cone because the quotient is
an \(s\)-weighted average of endpoint quotients.  Therefore
\(t\mapsto F(h_t,g_t)\) is absolutely continuous as an \(L^1\)-valued map, and
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}H(t)
=\int_{\RR}(\log u_t+1)\partial_th_t\dd x
-\int_{\RR}u_t\partial_tg_t\dd x
\]
for a.e. \(t\).  Since \(h_t\) and \(g_t\) are probability densities,
\(\int\partial_th_t=\int\partial_tg_t=0\) a.e.; this gives
\eqref{eq:kl-moving-quotient-l1-chain-rule}.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing} first to
\((p_t,q_t)=(h_t,\log u_t)\) with \(\mu=\nu\), and then to
\((p_t,q_t)=(g_t,u_t)\) with \(\mu=\delta_{\bar{\gamma}}\).  The bounded
Lipschitz hypotheses follow from the compact-window quotient bounds, so the
two Green formulae \eqref{eq:domain-theorem-h-logu-green} and
\eqref{eq:domain-theorem-g-u-green} hold.

At a time where the chain rule and both Green formulae hold, write
\[
a=u_t(x),\qquad b=u_t(y),\qquad \rho_x=g_t(x),\qquad \rho_y=g_t(y).
\]
Since \(h_t=g_tu_t\), the difference of the two Green numerators is the single
algebraic identity
\[
(\rho_xa-\rho_yb)(\log a-\log b)-(\rho_x-\rho_y)(a-b)
=\rho_x\Lambda(a,b)+\rho_y\Lambda(b,a).
\]
The compact-window Bregman majorant from
Lemma~\ref{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} lets us combine
the two annular limits and remove the cutoff by dominated convergence.  Hence
\[
-H'(t)=\frac1{2\pi}\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)\Lambda(u_t(y),u_t(x))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y .
\]
Exchanging \(x\) and \(y\) in the second term converts the symmetrized
\(1/(2\pi)\) expression into the one-sided \(1/\pi\) expression
\(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\).  The same majorant gives
\(\mathcal I_\nu\in L^1([a,b])\).
\end{proof}

\noindent
The KL tail identity has two logically separate inputs.  The local input is
the compact-window derivative identity
\(H'(t)=-\mathcal I_\nu(t)\), Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification}, together
with the explicit Bregman majorant from
Lemma~\ref{thm:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain}.
The infinite-tail input is the independent second-moment decay
\(H(T)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_T\|g_T)\to0\) from
Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-second-moment-kl-decay}.  The passage to
\([t,\infty)\) below uses only these two facts and monotone convergence; it
does not use any additional cancellation in the singular double integral.

\noindent
For a probability measure \(\nu\) on~\(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and
finite centred second moment, keep
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t .
\]
For \(t>0\) and \(R>1\), set
\[
\mathcal I_{\nu,R}(t):=
\frac1\pi\iint_{\{R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y,
\qquad
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta .
\]
The Bregman dissipation used below is the nonnegative Lebesgue integral
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t):=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y,
\]
with the diagonal ignored.  Indeed, if
\[
\Phi(t,x,y):=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2},&x\ne y,\\[1.2ex]
0,&x=y .
\end{cases}
\]
then \(\Phi\) is Borel measurable and nonnegative.  For fixed \(t\), the
annuli
\(\Omega_R=\{(x,y):R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}\) increase to
\(\RR^2\setminus\{x=y\}\), and monotone convergence gives
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal I_{\nu,R}(t)
=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}\Phi(t,x,y)\dd x\dd y .
\]
The same monotone-convergence argument applies to every increasing cofinal
annular exhaustion.  For nonmonotone or nonsymmetric cutoffs one uses the
absolute integrability of the kernel rather than cancellation: Lemma
\ref{lem:bregman-kernel-l1-cutoff-removal} proves that, on every compact time
window, \(\Phi\in L^1([a,b]\times\RR^2)\), and that for each fixed \(t>0\)
every measurable family \(0\le\chi_n\le1\) with
\(\chi_n(x,y)\to1\) for \(x\ne y\) satisfies
\[
\iint_{\RR^2}\chi_n(x,y)\Phi(t,x,y)\dd x\dd y
\longrightarrow
\iint_{\RR^2}\Phi(t,x,y)\dd x\dd y .
\]
Thus the value of \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\) is independent of the annular cutoff
convention used in the Green formulae; no symmetry of the approximating
cutoffs is needed after the kernel has been identified as an \(L^1\) kernel.
Measurability follows from
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t)=\sup_{n\ge2}\frac1\pi\iint_{\Omega_n}\Phi(t,x,y)\dd x\dd y,
\]
and Lemma~\ref{lem:bregman-kernel-l1-cutoff-removal} gives, for
\(0<a<b<\infty\),
\[
\int_a^b\mathcal I_\nu(t)\dd t
\le \frac{4C_{a,b,\nu}}{\pi}(b-a),
\]
and also gives \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)<\infty\) for every \(t\in[a,b]\).

\begin{theorem}[Poisson quotient KL tail identity]%
\label{thm:poisson-kl-doob-tail}
\label{cor:poisson-kl-doob-bregman-tail}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean~$\bar{\gamma}$ and finite
centred second moment.  Put
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t,
\]
and let \(\mathcal I_\nu\) be the locally integrable Bregman dissipation
constructed above.  The domain estimates are part of the theorem: for every
\(0<a<b<\infty\), with
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^2\dd\nu(\gamma),\qquad
m=\frac1{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\quad
M=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},\quad
L=3a^{-1}(1+\mu_2/a^2),
\]
one has, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x,y\in\RR\),
\[
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad |u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L|x-y|.
\]
Moreover, for
\begin{align*}
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\qquad&
\Psi(z)=\mathbf1_{\{|z|<1\}}+|z|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|z|\ge1\}},\\
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\},
\end{align*}
one has, for \(x\ne y\) and \(t\in[a,b]\),
\[
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}g_t(x)\Psi(x-y).
\]
The same constants also give the symmetrized estimate
\[
\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{
g_s(x)\Lambda(u_s(x),u_s(y))
+g_s(y)\Lambda(u_s(y),u_s(x))}
{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y\dd s
\le 8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a),
\]
and the one-sided dissipation estimate
\[
\int_a^b\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
\le \frac{4C_{a,b,\nu}}{\pi}(b-a).
\]
Consequently, for every \(t>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set \(H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\).  The proof uses only the following
five inputs, with the constant ledger confined to
Lemmas~\ref{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} and
\ref{lem:cauchy-normalization-bounded-test-green}.

First, on every compact window \([a,b]\Subset(0,\infty)\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} gives
\[
m\le u_s(x)\le M,\qquad |u_s(x)-u_s(y)|\le L|x-y|
\]
with the constants displayed in the theorem, as well as the stated Bregman
majorants and \(L^1\) cutoff removal.

Second, Lemma~\ref{lem:cauchy-normalization-bounded-test-green} supplies the
Cauchy Green formula
\[
\int_{\RR} q_s\,\partial_s p_s
=-\frac1{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_s(x)-p_s(y))(q_s(x)-q_s(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\]
for the two pairs \((p_s,q_s)=(h_s,\log u_s)\) and \((g_s,u_s)\).

Third, the \(L^1\) chain rule in Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification} gives
\[
H'(s)=\int_{\RR}\log u_s\,\partial_sh_s\,\dd x
-\int_{\RR}(u_s-1)\partial_sg_s\,\dd x
\qquad\text{for a.e. }s\in[a,b].
\]

Fourth, with
\[
a=u_s(x),\quad b=u_s(y),\quad \rho_x=g_s(x),\quad \rho_y=g_s(y),
\]
the Bregman algebra
\[
(\rho_xa-\rho_yb)(\log a-\log b)-(\rho_x-\rho_y)(a-b)
=\rho_x\Lambda(a,b)+\rho_y\Lambda(b,a)
\]
turns the two Green pairings into \(H'(s)=-\mathcal I_\nu(s)\).  The
one-sided definition of \(\mathcal I_\nu\) follows by exchanging \(x\) and
\(y\) in the second Bregman term; the preceding majorant justifies the cutoff
limit and gives \(\mathcal I_\nu\in L^1([a,b])\).

Fifth, integrating the derivative identity over \([t,T]\) gives
\[
H(t)-H(T)=\int_t^T \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s.
\]
The endpoint lemma, Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson-second-moment-kl-decay}, gives
\(H(T)\to0\).  Since \(\mathcal I_\nu\ge0\), monotone convergence yields
\[
H(t)=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s .
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No pointwise conclusion from finite moments alone]
\label{rem:no-pointwise-dissipation-from-finite-moments}
The coefficient transfer below concerns only tail integrals
\(\int_t^\infty\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s\) and fixed multiplicative-window
averages.  It does not assert a pointwise expansion of
\(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\).  A tail expansion for the primitive
\(H(t)=\int_t^\infty\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s\), even with a full finite-moment
coefficient hierarchy, does not by itself justify differentiating the
asymptotic series.  Pointwise asymptotics for \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\) require
extra differentiated-remainder input.

For example, if the centred law is compactly supported, say
\(|\gamma-\bar{\gamma}|\le M\), then for each fixed retained order \(L\) and
all \(t>2M\) the Cayley generating function gives the uniformly convergent
expansion, with \(\mu_n=\EE(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^n\),
\[
\frac{(P_t*\nu)(\bar{\gamma}+ty)}{P_t(ty)}-1
=\sum_{n=2}^{L}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}+\rho_{L,t}(y),
\qquad
\rho_{L,t}=\sum_{n>L}\mu_nu_n(y)t^{-n}.
\]
The same uniform majorant gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^\infty}
+\|\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
+\|t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^\infty}
+\|t\partial_t\rho_{L,t}\|_{L^1(\omega)}
=O_\nu(t^{-(L+1)}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Under such an additional differentiated remainder bound, one may
differentiate the finite quotient expansion and combine it with
\eqref{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation}; this auxiliary
observation is not part of the finite-moment coefficient hierarchy.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Dissipation-tail and window-average coefficient transfer]%
\label{prop:dissipation-tail-coefficient-expansion}
Let \(N\ge2\), and assume that \(\nu\) has finite centred
\((2N-2)\)th absolute moment.  Then the KL dissipation tail has the same
universal coefficient polynomial layer as the entropy expansion:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-expansion}
\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N})
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for every \(2\le M\le N\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-recovery}
A_{2M}(\nu)
=
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{2M}
\left(
\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
-\sum_{m=2}^{M-1}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}
\right),
\end{equation}
with the sum interpreted as zero when \(M=2\).

More locally, for each fixed \(\lambda>1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-finite-window-coefficients}
\int_t^{\lambda t} \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)(1-\lambda^{-2m})t^{-2m}
+o(t^{-2N}).
\end{equation}
Thus the averaged dissipation on a multiplicative window satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bregman-window-average-coefficients}
\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)t}\int_t^{\lambda t} \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}
\frac{1-\lambda^{-2m}}{\lambda-1}\,A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-(2m+1)}
+o(t^{-(2N+1)}).
\end{equation}
If, in addition, \(0<\eta\le1\) and the centred
\((2N-2+\eta)\)th absolute moment is finite, then the error terms above have
the following positive-margin bounds:
\[
\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+O_\nu(t^{-(2N+\eta)})
\]
and, for each fixed \(\lambda>1\),
\[
\int_t^{\lambda t} \mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s
=
\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)(1-\lambda^{-2m})t^{-2m}
 +O_{\nu,N,\eta,\lambda}(t^{-(2N+\eta)}).
\]
Consequently the averaged-window remainder is
\[
O_{\nu,N,\eta,\lambda}(t^{-(2N+1+\eta)}).
\]
No pointwise expansion of \(\mathcal I_\nu(t)\) is asserted.

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is a coefficient transfer, not an additional Hardy--Stein theorem.
The tail identity \eqref{eq:relative-entropy-integrated-dissipation} identifies
\(\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu\) with \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\).  Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders} gives
\eqref{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-expansion} and hence the coefficient
recovery formula \eqref{eq:bregman-tail-coefficient-recovery}.

For the finite window, use the already proved tail identity twice:
\[
H(t):=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t),
\qquad
\int_t^{\lambda t}\mathcal I_\nu(s)\dd s=H(t)-H(\lambda t).
\]
Substitute the expansion of \(H\) at \(t\) and at \(\lambda t\).  The
coefficient of \(t^{-2m}\) is
\(A_{2m}(\nu)-A_{2m}(\nu)\lambda^{-2m}\), which gives
\eqref{eq:bregman-finite-window-coefficients}; division by
\((\lambda-1)t\) gives \eqref{eq:bregman-window-average-coefficients}.
The positive-margin assertion follows from the explicit remainder estimate
\eqref{eq:positive-margin-retained-order-theorem} for \(H(t)\) and
\(H(\lambda t)\).
\end{proof}
