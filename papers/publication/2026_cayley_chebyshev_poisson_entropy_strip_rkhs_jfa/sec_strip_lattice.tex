\section{Final Remarks on Auxiliary Data}%
\label{sec:strip-lattice}

The entropy expansion, the coefficient-defect identities, the Doob-tail
identity, and the finite-time stability estimate are moment and coefficient
theorems.  They do not require finite phase grids, integer observation
samples, or an RKHS reconstruction theorem.  Two auxiliary data interfaces are
nevertheless useful for checking or supplying the finite inputs used above.

First, the Laurent constant terms in the coefficient calculation can be
checked by finite phase averages.  If
\(Q(z)=\sum_{|m|\le K}a_mz^m\) is one of the Laurent polynomials obtained
from a finite product of Cayley modes and \(N>K\), then
\[
  a_0=\int_{\TT}Q(z)\,\dd m_{\TT}(z)
      =\frac1N\sum_{\zeta^N=1}Q(\zeta).
\]
Thus any root-of-unity grid above the Fourier-support barrier gives an exact
post-theorem verification of the displayed Laurent arithmetic.  For the
eighth-order layer, the largest total mode index is \(8\), so grids
\(\{\zeta:\zeta^N=1\}\) with \(N\ge9\) exactly check all constant terms used
in Lemmas~\ref{lem:n4-normalized-laurent-representatives},
\ref{lem:eighth-order-coefficient-arithmetic}, and
\ref{lem:n4-entropy-coefficient-substitution}.  This finite check is only a
certificate for the already-proved coefficient table; the entropy proof uses
Haar/Laurent extraction directly.

Second, if moment data are supplied from an external observation channel, the
only direct input needed by the entropy layer is the centred jet
\[
  \mu_j=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^j\,\dd\nu(\gamma),
  \qquad 2\le j\le6,
\]
and, for the finite-time remainder theorem, the corresponding seventh
absolute moment bound fixed before the KL value is inserted.

The direct full-profile moment-supply interface assumes full continuous
knowledge of the law-side Fourier profile at a fixed time; it is not a
finite-sample reconstruction assertion.  The profile is only invoked after the
needed moment hypotheses have been fixed.  For \(t_0>0\), write
\[
  \kappa_{t_0}:=\operatorname{Law}((\gamma-\bar\gamma)/t_0).
\]
If \(\nu\) has a finite first centred absolute moment, then
\[
  \lambda_{t_0}(\dd s):=s\,\kappa_{t_0}(\dd s)
\]
is a finite signed measure.  Only under this hypothesis do we define the
continuous full profile
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observation-profile-definition}
  q_{t_0}(\xi):=
  \pi\int_{\RR} e^{-\ii s\xi}\,\lambda_{t_0}(\dd s)
  =
  \pi\int_{\RR}s e^{-\ii s\xi}\,\kappa_{t_0}(\dd s).
\end{equation}
Thus \(q_{t_0}=\pi\widehat{\lambda_{t_0}}\), with a genuine finite signed
measure on the right.  Full knowledge of \(q_{t_0}\) determines
\(\lambda_{t_0}\) by Fourier uniqueness.  On Borel sets not containing the
origin it then determines
\[
  \kappa_{t_0}(B)=\int_B s^{-1}\lambda_{t_0}(\dd s),
\]
and the remaining atom at the origin is fixed by total mass.

\begin{proposition}[Full-profile moment-supply interface]%
\label{prop:observation-profile-finite-jet}
Fix \(t_0>0\).  Assume that \(\nu\) has a finite sixth centred absolute
moment and define \(\kappa_{t_0}\), \(\lambda_{t_0}\), and \(q_{t_0}\) as
above.  Then \(q_{t_0}\in C^5(\RR)\), and, for \(2\le j\le6\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observation-interface-full-profile-moments}
  \mu_j
  =
  t_0^j\,\frac{\ii^{\,j-1}}{\pi}\,
  q_{t_0}^{(j-1)}(0).
\end{equation}
Consequently a full continuous profile at time \(t_0\), together with the
finite sixth centred absolute moment hypothesis, supplies exactly the
centred moment jet needed for \(A_6(\nu)\), \(A_8(\nu)\), \(\Delta_6(\nu)\),
and \(\Delta_8(\nu)\).  This is a full-profile assertion about the continuous
Fourier transform of the finite signed measure
\(\lambda_{t_0}=s\,\kappa_{t_0}\); finite or lattice samples do not supply
the input unless the separate support-restricted lattice hypothesis below is
also imposed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The sixth centred absolute moment gives
\[
  \int_{\RR}\abs{s}^6\,\kappa_{t_0}(\dd s)
  =
  t_0^{-6}\int_{\RR}\abs{\gamma-\bar\gamma}^6\,\nu(\dd\gamma)
  <\infty .
\]
In particular \(\lambda_{t_0}=s\,\kappa_{t_0}\) is a finite signed measure.
For \(0\le r\le5\), the function \(s\mapsto s^r e^{-\ii s\xi}\) is
integrable with respect to \(\lambda_{t_0}\), uniformly for \(\xi\) in
compact sets after domination by \(\abs{s}^{r+1}\).  Differentiation under
the integral therefore gives
\[
  q_{t_0}^{(r)}(\xi)
  =
  \pi(-\ii)^r\int_{\RR}s^{r+1}e^{-\ii s\xi}\,\kappa_{t_0}(\dd s),
  \qquad 0\le r\le5 .
\]
Setting \(r=j-1\) and \(\xi=0\) yields
\[
  q_{t_0}^{(j-1)}(0)
  =
  \pi(-\ii)^{j-1}\int_{\RR}s^j\,\kappa_{t_0}(\dd s).
\]
Since \(s=(\gamma-\bar\gamma)/t_0\), the last integral is
\(t_0^{-j}\mu_j\).  Multiplying by \(t_0^j\ii^{j-1}/\pi\) proves
\eqref{eq:observation-interface-full-profile-moments}.
\end{proof}

There is also a purely exact, non-generic lattice certificate.  If, in
addition to integer-orbit samples, one already knows the support condition
\[
  \nu\{\gamma:\gamma-\bar\gamma\in t_0\ZZ\}=1,
\]
then the integer convolution with \(K(a,b)=2/(4+(a-b)^2)\) can be inverted by
the positive symbol
\[
  \sum_{k\in\ZZ}\frac{2}{4+k^2}e^{-\ii k\theta}
  =
  \pi\frac{\cosh(2(\pi-|\theta|))}{\sinh(2\pi)},
  \qquad -\pi\le\theta\le\pi .
\]
This recovers the lattice masses and hence the same moment jet.  This lattice
route is deliberately support-restricted and non-generic: without the
displayed support condition, the integer samples are only projection data and
do not reconstruct the full continuous profile required by the full-profile
moment-supply interface.  For that reason the lattice calculation is kept
outside the entropy argument.  The separate supplementary note
\path{observation_lattice_reconstruction_note.tex} contains the longer
integer-lattice reconstruction statement.
