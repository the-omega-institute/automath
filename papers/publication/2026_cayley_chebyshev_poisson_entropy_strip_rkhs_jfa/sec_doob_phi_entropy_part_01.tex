\section{Domain and Endpoint Closure for the Moving Poisson Quotient}%
\label{sec:doob-phi-entropy}

Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\).
For \(t>0\) put
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad
u_t:=h_t/g_t .
\]
Thus \(u_t\) is the moving Poisson quotient against the matched Cauchy
reference \(g_t\).  The domain bounds below provide the Doob--Bregman
calculus needed to differentiate its KL divergence.
The Cauchy generator is normalized by
\[
(Lf)(x)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y,
\]
so that \(\partial_tP_t=LP_t\).  This is the usual generator of the symmetric
\(1\)-stable Cauchy semigroup under the Fourier convention fixed in
Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}
\cite{Feller1971,Bertoin1996LevyProcesses,Sato1999LevyProcesses,
Applebaum2009LevyProcesses,Kwasnicki2017}.

We first separate the formal semigroup identity from the domain question.
For a symmetric jump generator
\[
(L_Jf)(x)=\int\{f(y)-f(x)\}J(x,y)\,\dd y,
\qquad J(x,y)=J(y,x),
\]
and positive solutions \(\partial_t h_t=L_Jh_t\),
\(\partial_t g_t=L_Jg_t\), put \(u_t=h_t/g_t\).  Whenever differentiation,
the two Green pairings, and Fubini are justified, the general logarithmic
relative-\(\Phi\)-entropy identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:section4-general-two-solution-jump-identity}
-\frac{\dd}{\dd t}D_{\rm KL}(h_t\|g_t)
=\frac12\iint J(x,y)
\left[g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
+g_t(y)\Lambda(u_t(y),u_t(x))\right]\dd x\dd y.
\end{equation}
Symmetry of \(J\) turns the right side into
\[
\iint J(x,y)g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))\dd x\dd y.
\]
This simultaneous-semigroup Bregman identity belongs to the general
relative-\(\Phi\)-entropy calculus; see Chafa{\"\i}
\cite{Chafai2004PhiEntropies} and the earlier nonlinear energy forms of
Bakry--Coulhon--Ledoux--Saloff-Coste
\cite{BakryCoulhonLedouxSaloffCoste1995}.  The result of this section is not
the formal differentiation.  It verifies its finite-variance domain for
\(J(x,y)=\{\pi(x-y)^2\}^{-1}\) and proves the separate endpoint needed to
integrate the identity to infinity.

\subsection*{Proof roadmap}

The moving reference is handled by a single differentiation-and-Green
calculation.  If
\(H(t)=D_{\rm KL}(h_t\|g_t)\), then the \(L^1\) chain rule first gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:section4-two-pair-chain-rule}
H'(t)=\int_{\RR}\log u_t\,\partial_t h_t\,\dd x
-\int_{\RR}(u_t-1)\partial_tg_t\,\dd x .
\end{equation}
The first term is paired by the Cauchy Green formula with
\((h_t,\log u_t)\); the second is paired with \((g_t,u_t)\).  Thus the
argument is not a direct citation of a static Hardy--Stein identity.  Writing
\[
g_x=g_t(x),\quad g_y=g_t(y),\quad
u_x=u_t(x),\quad u_y=u_t(y),
\]
the difference of the two Green numerators is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:section4-two-green-numerator-difference}
(g_xu_x-g_yu_y)(\log u_x-\log u_y)
-(g_x-g_y)(u_x-u_y)
=g_x\Lambda(u_x,u_y)+g_y\Lambda(u_y,u_x),
\end{equation}
where \(\Lambda(a,b)=a\log(a/b)-a+b\).  This elementary identity is the
precise closure step that converts two moving Green pairings into the
one-sided nonlocal Bregman integrand used below.  The function
\(\Lambda\) is the two-point Bregman remainder for
\(\Phi(s)=s\log s-s+1\), i.e. the logarithmic
\(f\)-divergence kernel in the Csisz{\'a}r--Morimoto--Ali--Silvey framework
\cite{AliSilvey1966Divergence,Csiszar1967,Morimoto1963Markov,
Vajda1989TheoryDivergence,Bregman1967}.  The only distributional input is the
compact-window domain lemma:
Lemma~\ref{thm:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain} records, for
every \(0<a<b<\infty\),
\[
\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)}\le u_t\le
2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},\qquad
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le
3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr)|x-y|,
\]
and the logarithmic Bregman kernel divided by the Cauchy singular kernel is
dominated by an explicit integrable majorant
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}
\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{|x-y|<1\}}+|x-y|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|x-y|\ge1\}}\bigr).
\]
The remaining local steps are the \(L^1\) Nemytskii chain rule
(Lemma~\ref{thm:kl-domain-verification}), the annular cutoff and
measurability lemma
(Lemma~\ref{lem:annular-cutoff-measurable-bregman-dissipation}), the Cauchy
Green formula with constants
(Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing}), and the
consolidated compact-window identity
(Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-window-bregman-identity}).  Applying that
proposition with \(\mu=\nu\) and \(\eta=\delta_{\bar\gamma}\), then using
the endpoint decay \(D_{\rm KL}(h_T\|g_T)\to0\), gives the tail formula.
The coefficient consequence still comes from the Cayley quotient-mode
expansion in Theorem~\ref{thm:poisson-kl-even-orders}.

The identity is in the de Bruijn/Stam and relative-entropy dissipation
lineage
\cite{DeBruijn1953,Stam1959,Blachman1965,Barron1986,
JohnsonBarron2004,MadimanBarron2007,Verdu2010Mismatched,
HirataNemotoYoshida2012IntegralRepresentation,
Yoshida2017RelativeEntropyDiffusion},
and it uses the same Bregman algebra that underlies nonlocal
Hardy--Stein/Sobolev--Bregman formulas
\cite{Bregman1967,BanuelosBogdanLuks2016HardyStein,
BanuelosKim2019HardySteinNonsymmetric,
BogdanGrzywnyPietruskaPalubaRutkowski2023NonlinearNonlocalDouglas,
Gutowski2023HardySteinPureJump,GutowskiKwasnicki2025BeurlingDenySobolevBregman,
BogdanGutowskiPietruskaPaluba2025PolarizedHardyStein}.  At formula level, the
polarized identity integrates
\[
\mathcal J_p(w,z)=z_1z_2^{\langle p-1\rangle}
-w_1w_2^{\langle p-1\rangle}-w_2^{\langle p-1\rangle}(z_1-w_1)
-(p-1)w_1|w_2|^{p-2}(z_2-w_2)
\]
along \((w,z)=(P_s(f,g)(x),P_s(f,g)(y))\) to represent
\(\int fg^{\langle p-1\rangle}\) for \(p\ge2\).  This fixed power pairing
does not directly specialize to \(\int h_t\log(h_t/g_t)\), and it does not
provide the logarithmic endpoint, the compact-window bounds for \(h_t/g_t\),
or the noncompact Green cutoff passage established below.

\begin{lemma}[Second-moment KL decay to the translated Poisson profile]%
\label{lem:poisson-second-moment-kl-decay}
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on~$\RR$ with mean $\bar{\gamma}$ and
finite centred second moment.  Put
\[
h_t:=P_t*\nu,
\qquad
g_t(x):=P_t(x-\bar{\gamma}).
\]
Then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(t\to\infty).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}$ and $x=\bar{\gamma}+ty$.  Since
$g_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty)=(\pi t)^{-1}(1+y^2)^{-1}$,
\[
u_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty):=\frac{h_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty)}{g_t(\bar{\gamma}+ty)}
=\int_{\RR}\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
For $A>0$ decompose $\nu=\nu_A+\nu_A^c$ according to
$|X_c|\le A$ and $|X_c|>A$.  The elementary kernel bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-moment-kl-kernel-bound}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
\le 2(1+\varepsilon^2)
\qquad(y,\varepsilon\in\RR)
\end{equation}
implies
\[
0\le u_{t,A}^c(y):=\int_{|X_c|>A}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
\le 2\int_{|X_c|>A}\left(1+\frac{X_c^2}{t^2}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
Hence, for $t\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-moment-kl-tail-sup}
\sup_y u_{t,A}^c(y)
\le 2\nu(|X_c|>A)+2\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu .
\end{equation}

The bounded part has total mass $m_A:=\nu(|X_c|\le A)$ and first centred
moment $b_A:=\int_{|X_c|\le A}X_c\dd\nu$.  The exact expansion
\[
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-\varepsilon)^2}
=1+\frac{2y}{1+y^2}\varepsilon
+\frac{(3y^2-1)\varepsilon^2-2y\varepsilon^3}
{(1+y^2)(1+(y-\varepsilon)^2)}
\]
shows that, for each fixed $A$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-truncated-quotient-limit}
u_{t,A}(y):=\int_{|X_c|\le A}
\frac{1+y^2}{1+(y-X_c/t)^2}\dd\nu(\gamma)
=m_A+\frac{2yb_A}{t(1+y^2)}+O_A(t^{-2})
\end{equation}
uniformly in~$y$.  Since
$b_A=-\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c\dd\nu$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
|b_A|\le \left(\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Combining this with
\eqref{eq:second-moment-kl-tail-sup} and
\eqref{eq:bounded-truncated-quotient-limit} yields the needed two-sided
uniform estimate explicitly.  Put
\[
q_A:=\nu(|X_c|>A),\qquad
r_A:=\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu,\qquad
\eta_A(t):=\sup_y |u_{t,A}(y)-m_A|.
\]
Then \(\eta_A(t)\to0\) for each fixed \(A\), and for \(t\ge1\)
\[
-\eta_A(t)-q_A
\le u_t(\bar\gamma+ty)-1
\le \eta_A(t)+q_A+\sup_z u_{t,A}^c(z)
\le \eta_A(t)+3q_A+2r_A
\]
uniformly in \(y\).  Hence
\[
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|u_t-1\|_{L^\infty(\RR)}
\le 3\nu(|X_c|>A)+2\int_{|X_c|>A}X_c^2\dd\nu .
\]
Letting $A\to\infty$ gives $\|u_t-1\|_\infty\to0$.

For all sufficiently large $t$ we therefore have
$|u_t-1|\le1/2$.  With $\delta_t=u_t-1$ and using
$\int g_t\delta_t=\int(h_t-g_t)=0$,
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(h_t\|g_t)
=\int_{\RR}g_t\bigl((1+\delta_t)\log(1+\delta_t)-\delta_t\bigr)\dd x
\le 2\|\delta_t\|_\infty^2,
\]
because $(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\le2s^2$ for $|s|\le1/2$.  The right-hand side
tends to zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Compact-window quotient bounds and Bregman majorant]%
\label{lem:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain}
\label{thm:matched-poisson-quotient-cauchy-bregman-domain}
\label{lem:single-compact-window-bregman-majorant-constants}
\label{lem:bregman-kernel-l1-cutoff-removal}
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), and let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on~\(\RR\)
with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\) and finite centred second moment
\[
\mu_2:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar{\gamma})^2\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
For
\[
h_t=P_t*\nu,\qquad g_t=P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}),\qquad u_t=h_t/g_t,
\qquad t\in[a,b],
\]
put
\[
m:=\frac{1}{2(1+\mu_2/a^2)},\qquad
M:=2+\frac{2\mu_2}{a^2},\qquad
L:=3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr).
\]
Then, for all \(t\in[a,b]\) and \(x,y\in\RR\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML}
m\le u_t(x)\le M,\qquad
|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le L|x-y|.
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\log u_t\) is bounded and Lipschitz uniformly on
\([a,b]\).  Moreover, with
\[
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha\log\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\alpha+\beta,
\qquad
\Psi(z):=\mathbf1_{\{|z|<1\}}+|z|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|z|\ge1\}},
\]
and
\[
C_{a,b,\nu}:=\frac{1}{2m}\max\{L^2,(M-m)^2\},
\]
the one-sided logarithmic Bregman kernel satisfies, for \(x\ne y\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-one-sided-bregman-majorant}
0\le
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\le C_{a,b,\nu}\,g_t(x)\Psi(x-y),
\end{equation}
and the exchanged term satisfies the same estimate with \(x\) and \(y\)
interchanged.  Hence the symmetrized Bregman kernel used in the local
Green-pairing argument below is dominated on
\([a,b]\times\RR^2\) by the integrable majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-sym-bregman-integrable-majorant}
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y),
\end{equation}
whose integral over \([a,b]\times\RR^2\) is \(8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\).  In
particular, if
\[
\mathcal I_\nu(t):=\frac1\pi\iint_{\RR^2}
\frac{g_t(x)\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\]
is understood as the nonnegative Lebesgue integral off the diagonal, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-lemma-one-sided-majorant-integral}
\int_a^b\mathcal I_\nu(t)\dd t
\le \frac{4C_{a,b,\nu}}{\pi}(b-a).
\end{equation}
The one-sided and symmetrized Bregman kernels are in
\(L^1([a,b]\times\RR^2)\); hence every measurable cutoff
\(0\le\chi_n\le1\) with \(\chi_n(t,x,y)\to1\) off the diagonal may be removed
by dominated convergence.  For each fixed \(t>0\) the same cutoff statement
holds on \(\RR^2\), after choosing any compact window containing \(t\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With \(z=(x-\bar{\gamma})/t\), \(X_c=\gamma-\bar{\gamma}\), and
\[
R_\varepsilon(z):=\frac{1+z^2}{1+(z-\varepsilon)^2},
\]
one has
\[
u_t(x)=\int_{\RR}R_{X_c/t}\!\left(\frac{x-\bar{\gamma}}{t}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma).
\]
The two-sided part of \eqref{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML} is
exactly the compact-window Poisson quotient bound
\eqref{eq:prelim-poisson-quotient-two-sided-bound} from
Lemma~\ref{lem:kl-bounded-test-and-poisson-quotient}.  It remains in this
lemma only to add the Lipschitz and Bregman estimates needed for the Green
pairing.
Also
\[
\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)
=\frac{2\varepsilon(1-w^2)-2\varepsilon^2w}{(1+w^2)^2},
\qquad w=z-\varepsilon.
\]
Since
\[
\sup_w\frac{2|1-w^2|}{(1+w^2)^2}\le2,\qquad
\sup_w\frac{2|w|}{(1+w^2)^2}\le1,
\]
we have
\[
|\partial_zR_\varepsilon(z)|\le 2|\varepsilon|+\varepsilon^2
\le 3(1+\varepsilon^2).
\]
After dividing by \(t\) and using \(t\ge a\),
\[
|\partial_xu_t(x)|
\le \frac{3}{t}\int_{\RR}\left(1+\frac{X_c^2}{t^2}\right)\dd\nu(\gamma)
\le 3a^{-1}\bigl(1+\mu_2/a^2\bigr)=L.
\]
This proves the Lipschitz bound in
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-quotient-domain-mML}.  Since
\((\log r)'=1/r\le1/m\) on \([m,M]\), the same bounds also make
\(\log u_t\) uniformly bounded and Lipschitz.

For fixed \(\beta\in[m,M]\), the function
\(\alpha\mapsto \Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\) has first derivative zero at
\(\alpha=\beta\) and second derivative \(1/\alpha\le1/m\) on \([m,M]\).
Taylor's formula therefore gives the compact-range quadratic estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-poisson-compact-quadratic-bregman}
0\le \Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\le \frac{1}{2m}|\alpha-\beta|^2
\qquad(\alpha,\beta\in[m,M]).
\end{equation}
Combining this with the Lipschitz estimate gives
\[
\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le \frac{L^2}{2m}|x-y|^2\qquad(|x-y|<1).
\]
The same quadratic estimate and the range bound
\(|u_t(x)-u_t(y)|\le M-m\) give
\[
\Lambda(u_t(x),u_t(y))
\le \frac{(M-m)^2}{2m}\qquad(|x-y|\ge1).
\]
Dividing by \((x-y)^2\) proves
\eqref{eq:matched-poisson-one-sided-bregman-majorant}.  The exchanged term is
identical after switching \(x\) and \(y\).  Finally
\[
\int_{\RR}\Psi(z)\dd z
=\int_{|z|<1}\dd z+\int_{|z|\ge1}|z|^{-2}\dd z=4,
\]
and \(\int_{\RR}g_t(x)\dd x=1\).  Thus the one-sided majorant integrates over
\([a,b]\times\RR^2\) to \(4C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)\), while adding the exchanged
majorant gives
\[
\int_a^b\iint_{\RR^2}
C_{a,b,\nu}\{g_t(x)+g_t(y)\}\Psi(x-y)\dd x\dd y\dd t
=8C_{a,b,\nu}(b-a)<\infty .
\]
Consequently the one-sided integral estimate is exactly
\eqref{eq:single-lemma-one-sided-majorant-integral}.  The Bregman kernels are
dominated by the displayed \(L^1\) functions, so all stated cutoff removals
follow from dominated convergence; the diagonal is a null set.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cauchy Green formula and constants]%
\label{lem:kl-main-generator-convention-domain}
\label{lem:poisson-cauchy-generator-l1-domain}
\label{lem:bounded-lipschitz-cauchy-cutoff-pairing}
\label{lem:cauchy-normalization-bounded-test-green}
Use the Fourier convention
\[
\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\RR}f(x)e^{-\ii x\xi}\dd x .
\]
Then
\[
\widehat{P_t}(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|},\qquad \partial_tP_t=LP_t,
\]
where
\[
Lf(x)=\frac1\pi\,\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\RR}
\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{(x-y)^2}\dd y .
\]
Let \(0<a<b<\infty\), let \(\mu\) be a finite signed measure on \(\RR\), and
put \(p_t=P_t*\mu\).  For \(R>1\) define
\[
(L_R f)(x):=\frac1\pi\int_{R^{-1}<|z|<R}
\frac{f(x+z)-f(x)}{z^2}\dd z,
\qquad
\Omega_R:=\{(x,y):R^{-1}<|x-y|<R\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poisson-generator-annular-l1-estimate}
\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t\|_1
\le
\frac{\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}{\pi R}
\left(\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1+4\right).
\end{equation}
If \(q_t:\RR\to\RR\) is a possibly time-dependent bounded Lipschitz test
family with no compact-support assumption and with
\[
M_*:=\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\|q_t\|_\infty<\infty,\qquad
L_*:=\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\operatorname{Lip}(q_t)<\infty ,
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-finite}
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)L_Rp_t(x)\dd x
=-\frac1{2\pi}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\end{equation}
and
\begin{align}
&\sup_{\substack{t\in[a,b]\\R>1}}
\iint_{\Omega_R}
\left|
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}
\right|\dd x\dd y \notag\\
&\qquad\le
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\left(2L_*\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_xP_s\|_1+8M_*\right).
\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-total-variation-bound}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-limit}
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)\partial_tp_t(x)\dd x
=-\frac1{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y ,
\end{equation}
and the cutoff error is bounded by
\begin{align}
&\sup_{t\in[a,b]}
\left|
\int_{\RR}q_t(x)\partial_tp_t(x)\dd x
+\frac1{2\pi}\iint_{\Omega_R}
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\dd x\dd y
\right| \notag\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{M_*\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}{\pi R}
\left(\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1+4\right).
\label{eq:bounded-lipschitz-time-dependent-cutoff-error}
\end{align}
Thus the constants are: \(L\) carries the prefactor \(1/\pi\);
each symmetrized Green pairing carries \(-1/(2\pi)\); after the two
logarithmic Bregman pairings are combined and the variables are exchanged,
the final one-sided dissipation carries \(1/\pi\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Fourier transform of the Cauchy kernel is
\(\widehat{P_t}(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|}\), and the displayed principal-value
operator has multiplier \(-|\xi|\).  Hence
\[
\widehat{\partial_tP_t}(\xi)=-|\xi|e^{-t|\xi|}
=\widehat{LP_t}(\xi).
\]
Translation invariance gives
\(L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t=(L_RP_t-\partial_tP_t)*\mu\).  The near-diagonal
omission is controlled by the symmetric Taylor expansion of
\(P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t\), whose odd first-order term integrates to zero, giving
\[
\left\|\frac1\pi\int_{|z|<R^{-1}}
\frac{P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t(\cdot)}{z^2}\dd z\right\|_1
\le \frac{1}{\pi R}\sup_{s\in[a,b]}\|\partial_x^2P_s\|_1 .
\]
The far-tail omission uses \(\|P_t(\cdot+z)-P_t\|_1\le2\), and is at most
\(4/(\pi R)\).  Convolving with \(\mu\) proves
\eqref{eq:poisson-generator-annular-l1-estimate}.

For fixed \(R\), Fubini is legitimate because \(q_t\) is bounded and
\(\int_{R^{-1}<|z|<R}\|p_t(\cdot+z)-p_t\|_1z^{-2}\dd z<\infty\).  Changing
variables in one copy and symmetrizing gives
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-finite}.  The limiting annuli are
controlled by
\[
\iint_{\Omega_R}\left|
\frac{(p_t(x)-p_t(y))(q_t(x)-q_t(y))}{(x-y)^2}\right|\dd x\dd y
\le 2L_*\|\partial_xp_t\|_1+8M_*\|p_t\|_1 ,
\]
using \(\|p_t(\cdot+z)-p_t\|_1\le |z|\|\partial_xp_t\|_1\) for \(|z|<1\)
and \(\le2\|p_t\|_1\) for \(|z|\ge1\).  Since
\(\|p_t\|_1\le\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\) and
\(\|\partial_xp_t\|_1\le\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\|\partial_xP_t\|_1\), this proves
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-total-variation-bound}.  Combining the
finite-annulus identity with
\[
\left|\int_{\RR}q_t(L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t)\dd x\right|
\le \|q_t\|_\infty\|L_Rp_t-\partial_tp_t\|_1
\]
gives \eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-time-dependent-cutoff-error}, and then
\eqref{eq:bounded-lipschitz-annular-green-limit}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Absolutely continuous logarithmic Nemytskii chain rule]%
\label{prop:abstract-cutoff-bregman-kl}
\label{lem:cauchy-kl-cutoff-green-identity}
\label{thm:external-cauchy-kl-hardystein-input}
\label{prop:self-contained-cauchy-kl-hardystein}
\label{lem:poisson-kl-l1-ac-chain-rule}
\label{thm:kl-domain-verification}
\label{lem:poisson-quotient-cutoff-bregman-verification}
\label{lem:kl-tail-chain-rule-cutoff-passage}
\label{prop:kl-tail-cutoff-inputs}
\label{thm:kl-local-doob-bregman-domain-closure}
\label{prop:kl-local-doob-bregman-domain-closure}
Let \(I=[a,b]\) with \(0<a<b<\infty\).  Let \(h_t\) and \(g_t\) be
nonnegative probability densities on \(\RR\), with \(g_t>0\) a.e., such that
\(t\mapsto h_t\) and \(t\mapsto g_t\) are absolutely continuous as
\(L^1(\RR)\)-valued maps.  Assume that there are constants
\(0<m\le M<\infty\) for which
\[
m\le u_t(x):=\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}\le M
\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in\RR,\ t\in I .
\]
Put
\[
H(t):=\int_{\RR}h_t(x)\log\frac{h_t(x)}{g_t(x)}\dd x .
\]
Then \(H\in AC(I)\), and for a.e. \(t\in I\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-moving-quotient-l1-chain-rule}
H'(t)=
\int_{\RR}\log u_t\,\partial_th_t\,\dd x
-\int_{\RR}(u_t-1)\partial_tg_t\,\dd x
\end{equation}
where the derivatives are the \(L^1\)-a.e. time derivatives.  The quotient
\(u_t\) may move with \(t\); no pointwise time derivative of \(u_t\) is used.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
K:=\{(r,s):s>0,\ m\le r/s\le M\}
\]
and \(F(r,s):=r\log(r/s)\) on \(K\).  On this cone
\[
\partial_rF(r,s)=\log(r/s)+1,\qquad
\partial_sF(r,s)=-r/s
\]
are bounded by constants depending only on \(m\) and \(M\).  If
\((r_0,s_0),(r_1,s_1)\in K\), then each point on the line segment between
them stays in \(K\), because
\[
\frac{(1-\theta)r_0+\theta r_1}
{(1-\theta)s_0+\theta s_1}
=
\frac{(1-\theta)s_0(r_0/s_0)+\theta s_1(r_1/s_1)}
{(1-\theta)s_0+\theta s_1}
\in[m,M].
\]
Hence \(F\) is Lipschitz on \(K\) in the sense
\[
|F(r_1,s_1)-F(r_0,s_0)|
\le C_{m,M}(|r_1-r_0|+|s_1-s_0|)
\qquad ((r_j,s_j)\in K).
\]
Therefore \(t\mapsto F(h_t,g_t)\) is an absolutely continuous
\(L^1(\RR)\)-valued map, and \(H(t)=\int F(h_t,g_t)\) is absolutely
continuous.

To identify the derivative, choose a time \(t\) outside the null set where the
\(L^1\)-derivatives \(\partial_th_t\) and \(\partial_tg_t\) fail to exist.
At such a time the Banach-space fundamental theorem gives, in \(L^1\),
\[
\frac{h_{t+\varepsilon}-h_t}{\varepsilon}\to\partial_th_t,\qquad
\frac{g_{t+\varepsilon}-g_t}{\varepsilon}\to\partial_tg_t
\]
along \(\varepsilon\to0\) with \(t+\varepsilon\in I\).  The mean-value formula
on \(K\) gives pointwise
\[
\frac{F(h_{t+\varepsilon},g_{t+\varepsilon})-F(h_t,g_t)}{\varepsilon}
=A_\varepsilon\,\frac{h_{t+\varepsilon}-h_t}{\varepsilon}
+ B_\varepsilon\,\frac{g_{t+\varepsilon}-g_t}{\varepsilon},
\]
where \(A_\varepsilon\) and \(B_\varepsilon\) are convex averages of
\(\partial_rF\) and \(\partial_sF\) along the segment joining
\((h_t,g_t)\) to \((h_{t+\varepsilon},g_{t+\varepsilon})\).  These
coefficients are uniformly bounded, and
\[
A_\varepsilon\to \log u_t+1,\qquad B_\varepsilon\to -u_t
\]
in measure.  Indeed \(h_{t+\varepsilon}\to h_t\) and
\(g_{t+\varepsilon}\to g_t\) in \(L^1\), hence in measure along every
vanishing sequence after taking subsequences; since \(g_t>0\) a.e. and the
quotients remain in \([m,M]\), the corresponding convex-combination quotients
converge to \(u_t\) in measure.  The difference quotients above are uniformly
integrable because they converge in \(L^1\).  Consequently bounded
coefficient convergence in measure, combined with this uniform
integrability, allows passage to the limit in the two integrals.  Thus
\[
H'(t)
=\int_{\RR}(\log u_t+1)\partial_th_t\dd x
-\int_{\RR}u_t\partial_tg_t\dd x
\]
for a.e. \(t\).  Since \(h_t\) and \(g_t\) are probability densities and are
absolutely continuous in \(L^1\),
\(\int\partial_th_t=\int\partial_tg_t=0\) for a.e. \(t\).  This is
\eqref{eq:kl-moving-quotient-l1-chain-rule}.
\end{proof}

Set

\qquad

For \(R>1\) put
