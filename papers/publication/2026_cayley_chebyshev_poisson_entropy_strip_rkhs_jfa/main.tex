\begin{abstract}
For the radial Poisson kernel on \(\RR^d\), a finite centred \(L\)th absolute
moment with \(L\ge\max\{2,d+1\}\) gives a multidimensional entropy jet.  Its
formal quadratic covariance layer is computed separately under finite second
moment and has an explicit positive isotropic/traceless decomposition.  This
finite-covariance identity is not a KL asymptotic by itself; a separate
finite-\(L\) jet corollary identifies it as the actual leading
\(t^{-4}\) KL coefficient when the quotient-remainder hypotheses are present.  The
proof separates the finite quotient jet from the quadratic-layer calculation,
with a large-translation estimate recording the role of the
dimension-dependent moment hypothesis in the full jet.  In
dimension one, the Cayley transform puts the Cauchy law, the Poisson
semigroup, and circle Fourier analysis in one normalization.  For a
fixed input law~\(\nu\), the large-time entropy comparison
\[
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
\]
against the matched translated Cauchy profile becomes a finite
Cayley--Chebyshev coefficient problem.  For every \(N\ge2\), a finite centred
\((2N-2)\)th absolute moment gives the expansion
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N}),
\]
where the coefficients are universal polynomials in the centred moments,
computed by Laurent extraction from Cayley modes.  This is a finite-moment
coefficient-identification theorem for fixed universal polynomials; the
corresponding analytic scope is stated once after the main hierarchy theorem.
For \(N\ge3\), explicit symmetric power-tail examples with infinite top
moment replace the missing \(A_{2N}t^{-2N}\) layer by a tail-dependent
\(t^{-2N}\log t\) term; the \(N=2\) borderline infinite-variance law has the
separate leading scale \(t^{-4}(\log t)^2\).  Thus weaker-tail regimes are
analytic rather than polynomial-evaluation consequences.  The explicit
\(N=4\) layer is evaluated under a finite centred sixth absolute moment, giving the
  \(t^{-4}\), \(t^{-6}\), and \(t^{-8}\) coefficients and the associated
  sixth/eighth-order defect identities; a finite seventh absolute moment
  supplies a quantitative \(O(t^{-9})\) remainder.  The sixth-order coefficient
  defect is nonnegative and controls the \(W_2\) distance to the symmetric
  two-point law at the coefficient level; this coupling itself uses only the
  finite fourth absolute moment needed to identify \(A_6\).  In contrast with the higher
  algebraic coefficient-evaluation levels, the leading one-dimensional
  entropy scale has a sharp analytic tail threshold:
  \(t^4D_{\rm KL}(P_t*\nu\|P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))\) has the finite limit
  \(\mu_2^2/8\) if the centred variance is finite, while it diverges to
  \(+\infty\) if the centred variance is infinite.  The optional
  finite-time post-processing recorded in the appendix is not an additional
  input to these asymptotic theorems: it is a conditional bookkeeping step
  that assumes its coefficient, remainder, threshold, and numerical-error
  bounds have already been supplied independently.
\end{abstract}
