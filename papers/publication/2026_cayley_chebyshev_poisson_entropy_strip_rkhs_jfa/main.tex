\begin{abstract}
For two probability measures with finite \((d+\alpha)\)-th absolute moments,
we prove a relative-entropy dissipation theorem for their simultaneous
symmetric \(\alpha\)-stable heat flows.  On every positive compact time
window the density quotient is bounded above and below and has bounded
logarithmic gradient; the associated logarithmic nonlocal Dirichlet form is
finite, locally integrable, and equals the negative entropy derivative.  An
annular argument closes the two noncompact Green pairings separately, and a
Wasserstein bound closes the endpoint at infinity, giving an exact
tail-integral representation.  The two-solution entropy-production identity
and its one-sided Bregman algebra are classical.  Our contribution is the
stable-continuum measure-data domain and endpoint theorem for that identity.
With a stable moving reference, we provide an exact nonlocal Bregman integral
representation along Johnson's symmetric-stable interpolation, under the
stated finite-moment and finite-initial-entropy domain; no identification
with Johnson's score-pairing integrand is claimed.

Arbitrary-order first unmatched moment relative-entropy asymptotics under
Gaussian smoothing are due to Chen and Niles-Weed
\cite{ChenNilesWeed2022SmoothedWasserstein}, while all-order
stable/fractional heat-kernel density expansions are also prior work
\cite{IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2017,
IshigeKawakamiMichihisa2022RefinedFractional}.  Neither result is claimed as
new.  For the heavy-tailed isotropic strictly \(\alpha\)-stable kernels,
\(0<\alpha<2\), we identify the optimal complete-moment condition that
transfers the density expansion through relative entropy.
If two
laws on \(\RR^d\) match all tensor moments below the integer order \(r\ge1\),
have a nonzero order-\(r\) difference \(\Delta_r\), and have finite
\(p_{r,\alpha,d}\)th absolute moments, where
\[
 p_{r,\alpha,d}=\max\left\{r,
 \frac{2r(d+\alpha)}{d+\alpha+2r}\right\},
\]
then
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\nu\|p_s^{(\alpha,d)}*\eta)
 =\mathcal Q_{\alpha,d,r}(\Delta_r)s^{-2r}+o(s^{-2r}).
\]
The coefficient is an orthogonally invariant positive-definite quadratic
form on symmetric order-\(r\) tensors.  The endpoint moment is sufficient.
When \(d+\alpha>2r\), every exponent
\(\rho\in[r,p_{r,\alpha,d})\) fails uniformly even for pairs with finite
nonzero \(\Delta_r\); when \(d+\alpha\le2r\), the threshold equals \(r\), and
smaller exponents fail only to define \(\Delta_r\) throughout the moment
class.  Thus the contribution is the heavy-tailed critical exponent,
endpoint sufficiency, and uniform sharpness, not the Gaussian quadratic
mechanism itself.

At every order \(r\), finite \(r\)th moments alone also give a law-by-law
decomposition: the universal quadratic tensor term plus a nonnegative
relative \(\Phi\)-energy between two positive proxies that retain each law's
unexpanded tail beyond the smoothing scale and their common lower-order jet.
The mechanism needs neither stability nor regular variation: an abstract
kernel theorem gives a sufficient kernel criterion in terms of bounded
normalized jets and quotient continuity of the top retained derivative;
bounded score automatically supplies finite, linearly growing translate
entropy.  The criterion applies to isotropic stable kernels, hence directly
to the Poisson kernel, and to every multivariate Student kernel, and is
closed under invertible affine images, finite products, and finite positive
mixtures.  It describes a bounded-score/additive proxy branch.  Gaussian
smoothing lies in a different unbounded-score/multiplicative or
exponential-score branch; the paper records this mechanism boundary rather
than claiming Gaussian coverage.
To the best of our knowledge, this explicit positive proxy decomposition,
with vanishing of its KL term as an exact necessary and sufficient condition
for attainment of the universal coefficient, is new; no uniqueness or
canonicity of the proxy is asserted.  The defect is nevertheless unchanged
up to \(o(s^{-2r})\) by separate comparable hard or smooth retention profiles,
by either additive or multiplicative mass normalization, and by common
bounded order-\(r\) proxy gauges.  Among order-\(r\) jet-faithful proxies in
the prescribed retained-tail/common-core ansatz, asymptotic coefficient
rigidity identifies this gauge as the remaining freedom; no classification
of all reasonable proxy conventions is claimed.
For the Cauchy kernel, the Cayley
transform additionally yields the exact equivalence between finite variance
and bounded \(t^4\) relative entropy, an all-order finite Laurent rule for the
subsequent coefficients, and a fully justified nonlocal Bregman tail identity
for the moving matched quotient.
\end{abstract}
