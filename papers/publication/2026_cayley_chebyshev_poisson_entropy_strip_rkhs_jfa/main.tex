\begin{abstract}
The Cayley transform puts the Cauchy law, the Poisson semigroup, and circle
Fourier analysis in one normalization: Haar measure pulls back to
$P_1(x)\,\dd x$, rigidly among normalized orientation-preserving $C^1$
charts.  In this chart the large-time comparison of $P_t*\nu$ with
$P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma)$ becomes a finite Cayley--Chebyshev coefficient
problem at each order.  For every $N\ge2$, a finite centred
$(2N+\eta)$th absolute moment implies
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar\gamma))
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N}),
\]
with explicit fixed-law remainder constants and uniform versions on centred
moment balls.  Odd coefficients vanish by Cayley parity, and the even
coefficients are finite Laurent extractions of universal Cauchy-weighted mode
integrals.  Compact support gives an analytic-convergence regime for the same
formal series.

The order-$t^{-8}$ specialization, proved directly under a finite centred
seventh absolute moment, gives non-negative sixth- and eighth-order defect
coordinates, the symmetric two-point fixed-variance extremizer, strict
eighth-order amplification, and coefficient-level and finite-time $W_2$
stability.  The RKHS component gives the observation-channel counterpart:
secant profiles generate a Poisson strip RKHS whose integer samples determine
the minimal-norm lattice projection, with full recovery exactly on the lattice
subspace.  Doob $\Phi$-entropy, finite phase identities, Hardy--secant calculus,
and the principal shift-invariant criterion supply the auxiliary machinery.
\end{abstract}
