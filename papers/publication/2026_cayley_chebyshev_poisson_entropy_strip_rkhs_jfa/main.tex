\begin{abstract}
For a fixed probability law~\(\nu\) on \(\RR\) with mean \(\bar{\gamma}\), the
Cayley transform reduces the large-scale matched Poisson entropy
\[
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}))
\]
to finite Laurent constant-term algebra.  The one-dimensional coefficient
hierarchy says that if \(\nu\) has finite centred
\((2N-2)\)th absolute moment, then
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}))
=\sum_{m=2}^{N}A_{2m}(\nu)t^{-2m}+o(t^{-2N}),
\]
where the universal polynomials \(A_{2m}\) are obtained from the
Cayley--Chebyshev modes by one constant-term rule.  The same method identifies
the sharp leading threshold:
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^4
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma})\bigr)
=\mu_2^2/8
\]
when the centred variance is finite, and diverges to \(+\infty\) when the
variance is infinite.

In \(\RR^d\), finite \(L\)-moment with \(L\ge d+1\) gives a matched radial
Poisson quotient with an explicit two-region \(L^\infty\cap L^1(\Omega_d)\)
remainder and hence tensor KL coefficients through order \(L+2\); the
\(\mathcal R_{d,L}(t)=o(t^{-L})\) decay of the displayed two-region
functional is equivalent to that finite-moment hypothesis in this range.
Under finite centred second moment, the
moving Cauchy quotient satisfies the exact nonlocal Bregman tail identity
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t*\nu\,\|\,P_t(\cdot-\bar{\gamma}))
=\int_t^\infty \mathcal I_\nu(s)\,\dd s,
\]
after compact-window domain bounds.  Appendices collect related-work
comparisons and auxiliary coefficient-calculus and tail-verification checks;
they are not additional hypotheses for the three main theorems.
\end{abstract}

The main text has three independent outputs.  The one-dimensional Cayley
calculus gives the finite Laurent coefficient hierarchy and the sharp
variance threshold; the multidimensional radial quotient estimates transfer
finite moments into tensor KL coefficients; and the moving-reference Cauchy
calculation gives the nonlocal Bregman tail identity for the matched
quotient.  The appendices that follow provide comparison, normalization, and
coefficient-check details without changing those hypotheses or conclusions.
