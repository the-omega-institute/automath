## Second-extremal classification

The second-largest fiber theorem was checked against the Fibonacci-partition
literature rather than inferred from the absence of such a statement in the
current manuscript.  Crossref returned the title *Integers with a maximal
number of Fibonacci representations* and lead author Petra Kocabova for DOI
`10.1051/ita:2005022`; OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar returned the same title
and authors and the public Numdam full text.  Sections 4--5 of that article
determine only the largest value and its arguments.  Its factorization and
maximizing-tuple results are inputs to the new runner-up proof, not prior
statements of the runner-up value.

The full texts of Weinstein's *Notes on Fibonacci Partitions*,
Chow--Slattery's *On Fibonacci Partitions*, Chow--Jones's *On the Variance of
the Fibonacci Partition Function*, Kempton's *The Dynamics of the Fibonacci
Partition Function*, and Sanna's *A Note on the Power Sums of the Number of
Fibonacci Partitions* were searched for second-largest, second-maximum,
near-maximum, and extremal-rank results.  They treat generator orbits, exact
formulas and averages, variance, local dynamics, or power sums; none gives a
second distinct maximum in a Fibonacci layer or classifies its arguments.

Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, and the official arXiv index were also
searched with combinations of `Fibonacci representation`, `Fibonacci
partition`, `second maximum`, `second largest`, `near maximal`, and
`continuant`.  The nearest result remained the 2005 maximal-value article;
no record located an exact second-level value or argument classification.
No new bibliographic citation was required for the theorem.

## Mesoscopic spectrum

The growing-level limit was checked separately because it is not a formal
restatement of fixed-\(k\) stabilization.  Weinstein's official arXiv record
and full text state stabilization only for a fixed equation \(R(n)=k\) once
the Fibonacci layer index is at least \(2k\), and give the Dirichlet series
for \(\Psi(k)\); they do not take \(k\) to infinity with the layer or derive
a rescaled counting measure.  The official arXiv records and full texts of
Chow--Slattery, Chow--Jones, Kempton, and Sanna were searched for level-set,
small-value, multiplicity-distribution, regular-variation, and mesoscopic
statements.  Their results concern pointwise formulas and first moments,
variance, spatial dynamics and the summatory function, or positive power
sums.  No growing-multiplicity counting law or limiting density was located.
