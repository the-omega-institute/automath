\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Let \(F_0=0,F_1=F_2=1\), and
\(F_{j+2}=F_{j+1}+F_j\).  A binary word
\(\omega\in\{0,1\}^m\) has the raw Fibonacci value
\[
 N_m(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m\omega_jF_{j+1}.
\]
We fold this value modulo \(F_{m+2}\) and represent the resulting visible
value by its legal Zeckendorf word.  If \(X_m\) is the legal visible window,
the fiber multiplicity of \(x\in X_m\) is denoted by \(d_m(x)\).  The central
finite problem is to determine the complete spectrum
\(\{d_m(x):x\in X_m\}\), not only its total mass or an asymptotic moment.

The answer begins with two pointwise identities.  The first uses an affine
permutation modulo \(F_{m+2}\) to identify every fiber with a coefficient of
a finite Fibonacci subset-sum polynomial.  That coefficient is, in turn, a
difference of two values of the classical Fibonacci partition function.  The
second identity lists the fiber spectrum directly as the partition function
on two consecutive standard Fibonacci layers.  These correspondences are
exact at each \(m\), including endpoints and the single possible overflow;
no limiting normalization or transducer is needed.

Once the two-layer identity is established, the extensive theory of the
Fibonacci partition function becomes available.  We keep a strict
distinction between transferred information and finite-window conclusions.
The formulas for the largest partition multiplicities and their maximizing
integers are due to Koc\'abov\'a, Mas\'akov\'a, and Pelantov\'a
\cite{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005}; here they are transported through the
exact interval identity, with all Fibonacci conventions and boundary cases
checked.  Likewise, the all-real moment pressure and its first-order
transition are the golden-ratio Bernoulli-convolution spectrum of
Lau--Ngai, Feng--Olivier, and Feng
\cite{LauNgai1998,FengOlivier2003,Feng2005}.  The pressure is recovered in
the present coordinates because it is needed to formulate the local and
large-deviation questions, but the recovered phase diagram is not one of the
principal claims.

The finite-window results occur below that imported pressure.  At fixed
multiplicity the spectrum stabilizes exactly after a sharp finite layer.
In the frozen range, unnormalized negative moments converge, and at the
critical tilt the Gibbs measure spreads uniformly over generator cost.  The
limiting log multiplicity and normalized cost lie on a coexistence segment.
For a uniform visible state, the pressure corner creates an interval of
nonexposed slopes.  Standard G\"artner--Ellis theory gives the upper bound
and the lower bound at exposed slopes, but not on that interval.  An explicit
orbit map fills the unused part of a target Fibonacci layer while preserving
multiplicity.  It yields the missing local lower bounds and hence a full
large deviation principle.  Marking generator cost leads to a joint rate
function with an affine two-dimensional coexistence face.

\subsection{Exact finite-window correspondences}

Let \(R(n)\) be the number of partitions of \(n\) into distinct members of
\(F_2,F_3,\ldots\), and put \(R(n)=0\) for \(n<0\).  Define
\[
 R^\dagger(n)=R(n)+R(n-1),
 \qquad
 \widetilde d_m(n)=[z^n]\prod_{j=1}^m(1+z^{F_j}).
\]
For \(0\le n<F_{m+2}\), let
\[
 \pi_m(n)\equiv F_{m+1}n+F_{m+1}-1\pmod{F_{m+2}}.
\]

\begin{theoremA}[Pointwise affine correspondence]
For every \(m\ge1\), the map \(\pi_m\) is a permutation of
\(\mathbb Z/F_{m+2}\mathbb Z\), and
\[
 d_m^\#(\pi_m(n))
 =\widetilde d_m(n)
 =R^\dagger(n)-R^\dagger(n-F_{m+1})
 \qquad(0\le n<F_{m+2}).
\]
Here \(d_m^\#\) is the residue-indexed form of the fiber multiplicity.
\end{theoremA}

This is the combined content of
\Cref{thm:affine-transfer,cor:coefficient-spectrum,thm:partition-difference}.
The proof starts with Fourier inversion for the residue fibers, uses the
exact congruence \(F_{m+1}F_j\pmod{F_{m+2}}\), and then proves the
partition-difference identity by a boundary-sensitive decomposition.  The
result is pointwise; equality of moment sums is only a corollary.

The second description uses the standard layers
\[
 \mathcal I_j=[F_{j+1}-1,F_{j+2}-1).
\]

\begin{theoremB}[Two-layer identity]
For every \(m\ge1\),
\[
 \{d_m(x):x\in X_m\}
 =\{R(n):n\in\mathcal I_m\sqcup\mathcal I_{m+1}\}
\]
as multisets.  More explicitly, for \(0\le r<F_{m+2}\),
\[
 d_m^\#(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 R(F_{m+2}+r),&0\le r\le F_{m+1}-2,\\
 R(r),&F_{m+1}-1\le r<F_{m+2}.
 \end{cases}
\]
\end{theoremB}

The proof is \Cref{thm:fiber-partition-interval}.  It also shows why two
layers, rather than one asymptotic layer, are intrinsic to the fold.  As a
contextual consequence, the known extremal classification transfers to the
finite window.  If
\(D_m=\max_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)\), then
\[
 D_0=D_1=1,
 \qquad D_{2k}=F_{k+2},
 \qquad D_{2k+1}=2F_{k+1},
\]
with the ranges and exceptional cases stated in
\Cref{thm:unconditional-extremal-fibers}.  The maximizing residues and the
recurrence \(D_m=D_{m-2}+D_{m-4}\) is a transferred result, not an independent
extremal theorems about the underlying partition function.

\subsection{Local laws and the pressure input}

For a real tilt \(t\), put
\[
 S_t(m)=\sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^t,
 \qquad
 P(t)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log S_t(m).
\]
If \(\tau_\mu(t)\) denotes the \(L^q\)-spectrum of the golden-ratio
Bernoulli convolution in the interval-length normalization, then its level
masses are \(2^{-m}\) times the Fibonacci representation counts and the net
scale is \(\varphi^{-m}\).  Thus the exact exponential-rate dictionary is
\begin{equation}
 P(t)=t\log2-\tau_\mu(t)\log\varphi.
 \label{eq:erdos-pressure-dictionary}
\end{equation}
The existence and phase diagram of \(P\) agree, under this dictionary, with
the published golden-ratio Bernoulli-convolution \(L^q\)-spectrum.  In particular, there is
a unique \(\sigma_0>2\) satisfying
\[
 \frac{\zeta(\sigma_0-1)}{\zeta(\sigma_0)}=2,
\]
and
\[
 P(t)=0\quad(t\le-\sigma_0),
\]
while \(P\) is positive, analytic, and strictly convex for
\(t>-\sigma_0\).  The one-sided slopes at the corner are \(0\) and
\[
 v_c=\frac{\displaystyle
  \sum_{q\ge2}\sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
       q^{-\sigma_0}\log q}
 {\displaystyle
  \sum_{q\ge2}\sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
       c(p/q)q^{-\sigma_0}}>0.
\]
The continued-fraction cost \(c(p/q)\) and its Weinstein renewal role are
defined in \Cref{prop:weighted-generator-renewal}.  The recovery in
\Cref{thm:all-real-pressure} fixes conventions and proves the exact root
equation needed below; it does not claim priority for the spectrum.

Let \(\Psi(k)\) be Weinstein's number of generating orbits at multiplicity
level \(k\), and set
\(C_m(k)=\card{\{x\in X_m:d_m(x)=k\}}\).
The finite-window local statement is exact:
\[
 C_m(1)=2,
 \qquad C_m(k)\le4\Psi(k),
 \qquad C_m(k)=4\Psi(k)\quad(m\ge2k,\ k\ge2).
\]
This is \Cref{thm:local-fiber-spectrum}.  It includes the small levels and
half-open endpoints that are invisible at exponential scale.

For \(s>\sigma_0\), \Cref{thm:spectral-partition-function} gives
\[
 S_{-s}(m)\longrightarrow
 \mathcal Z(s)=\frac4{2-\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)}-2.
\]
The convergence range is exact, and \(\mathcal Z\) has a simple pole at
\(\sigma_0\).  The associated fixed-level Gibbs laws and logarithmic moments
are described in \Cref{thm:critical-gibbs-spectrum}.  These are local and
unnormalized statements that the pressure alone does not supply.

At the endpoint, let
\[
 \mu_C=\sum_{q\ge2}\sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
 c(p/q)q^{-\sigma_0}.
\]
For a state \(x\) of multiplicity greater than two, let \(\ell_m(x)\) be the
cost of the Weinstein generator of its orbit, with the bounded low-level
states assigned cost zero.  Under the Gibbs law
\[
 \widehat Q_m(x)=\frac{d_m(x)^{-\sigma_0}}{S_{-\sigma_0}(m)},
\]
the critical finite-window theorem states
\begin{equation}
 S_{-\sigma_0}(m)\sim\frac{4m}{\mu_C},
 \qquad
 \left(\frac{\log d_m(\widehat{\mathbf X}_m)}m,
       \frac{\ell_m(\widehat{\mathbf X}_m)}m\right)
 \xrightarrow{\mathrm d}(v_cU,U),
 \label{eq:introduction-critical-coexistence}
\end{equation}
where \(U\) is uniform on \([0,1]\); see
\Cref{thm:critical-finite-window-gibbs}.  The factor \(4\) is the exact
two-layer orbit weight away from the active boundary.

\subsection{Large deviations across the corner}

Let \(\mathbf X_m\) be uniform on \(X_m\) and put
\[
 Y_m=\frac1m\log d_m(\mathbf X_m),
 \qquad \varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},
 \qquad \alpha_+=\frac12\log\varphi.
\]

\begin{theoremC}[Full finite-window large deviations]
The sequence \((Y_m)\) satisfies a full large deviation principle with
speed \(m\) and good rate function
\[
 I(\alpha)=\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}
 \{t\alpha-P(t)+\log\varphi\}.
\]
Its effective domain is \([0,\alpha_+]\), and
\[
 I(\alpha)=\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha
 \qquad(0\le\alpha\le v_c).
\]
In particular, the lower bound holds at every nonexposed slope in
\((0,v_c)\).
\end{theoremC}

This is \Cref{thm:full-fiber-ldp}.  The upper bound follows from the limiting
logarithmic moment generating function and compact support.  For an exposed
slope \(v=P'(t)>v_c\), exponential tilting provides a family of source
generators.  To reach a target slope \(\alpha<v\), the orbit map shifts each
source to a larger target layer without changing its multiplicity.  The two
orbit parities fill every sufficiently large target layer, the map is
injective by freeness, and its loss in exponential count is exactly the
scaling factor \(\alpha/v\).  Sending \(t\) down to the corner yields the
affine rate on \([0,v_c]\).  This is the step not provided by a direct
G\"artner--Ellis application.

The cost mark produces a genuinely two-dimensional statement.  On a uniform
point \(\mathbf N_m\) in one standard Fibonacci layer, let
\[
 B_m^R=\frac1m\ell_m^R(\mathbf N_m),
 \qquad
 Y_m^R=\frac1m\log R(\mathbf N_m).
\]

\begin{theoremD}[Joint cost--multiplicity large deviations]
The vectors \((B_m^R,Y_m^R)\) satisfy a full large deviation principle with
speed \(m\).  Their good rate function is
\[
 J(\beta,\alpha)=
 \begin{cases}
 (1-\beta)\log\varphi+\beta I(\alpha/\beta),
  &0<\beta\le1,\ 0\le\alpha\le\beta\alpha_+,\\
 \log\varphi,&(\beta,\alpha)=(0,0),\\
 +\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Its coexistence face is
\[
 J(\beta,\alpha)=\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha
 \qquad(0\le\beta\le1,\ 0\le\alpha\le\beta v_c).
\]
\end{theoremD}

The proof is \Cref{thm:joint-cost-reward-ldp}.  Fixed-cost tilting handles
the strictly convex region.  On the affine face, an exact suffix of any
sufficiently large remaining cost is built from at most two generator
letters and has only logarithmic reward.  Appending it is injective in the
free monoid, so it changes normalized cost without changing the desired
exponential reward count.  This fills the nonexposed two-dimensional region.

\subsection{Contribution boundary and organization}

The pointwise affine correspondence, partition-difference formula,
two-layer identity, exact local stabilization, finite-window critical law,
nonexposed-slope orbit filling, and joint affine rate face are established in
this article.  The pressure phase diagram is imported from the
golden-ratio Bernoulli-convolution literature and translated into the
present normalization.  The extremal values and maximizing integers of the
ordinary Fibonacci partition function are also imported; only their exact
finite-window transfer and the resulting residue list are established here.
The weighted Weinstein renewal identity is a direct consequence of his
published free-generator coordinates, with the layer and endpoint weights
proved in the conventions used here.

A companion manuscript by the same authors, \emph{Brocot condensation and
critical Fibonacci renewal}, proves a total-variation theorem for
denominator-weighted continued fractions and derives a sharp critical cost
tail and fractional renewal correction.  This article does not use the
total-variation theorem or the fractional correction.  Its critical
finite-window law requires only the critical probability normalization,
finite mean, and the elementary lattice renewal theorem, all verified here.

\Cref{sec:preliminaries} defines the finite fold and proves the residue
description.  \Cref{sec:residue-affine} proves the Fourier, affine, and
partition-difference identities.  \Cref{thm:fiber-partition-interval} and
the surrounding section establish the two-layer model and record the
subordinate extremal transfer and pressure conventions.  The local spectrum,
renewal normalization, critical law, and full finite-window LDP follow in
\Cref{sec:local-spectrum,thm:all-real-pressure,thm:full-fiber-ldp}.
The one-layer and joint marked results are collected in
\Cref{sec:standard-thermodynamics}.  Conditional arithmetic and integer
moment consequences are placed in the separately compiled supplement.
