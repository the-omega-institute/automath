\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Let \(F_0=0,F_1=F_2=1\), and \(F_{j+2}=F_{j+1}+F_j\).  A binary word
\(\omega\in\{0,1\}^m\) has raw Fibonacci value
\[
 N_m(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m\omega_jF_{j+1}.
\]
We reduce this value modulo \(F_{m+2}\) and represent the residue by its legal
Zeckendorf word.  If \(X_m\) is the legal visible window, the resulting fold
is \(\Fold_m:\{0,1\}^m\to X_m\), and \(d_m(x)\) denotes the multiplicity of
the fiber over \(x\).  Our finite problem is to determine the spectrum
\(\{d_m(x):x\in X_m\}\), including its residue locations and boundary
behavior.

Two arithmetic models organize the answer.  An affine permutation modulo
\(F_{m+2}\) identifies each fiber with a coefficient of a finite Fibonacci
subset-sum polynomial.  A complementary interval identity identifies the
whole spectrum with the ordinary Fibonacci partition function on two
consecutive layers.  The first description retains the residue of each
fiber; the second exposes the generator orbits used in the local and
probabilistic arguments.

Let \(R(n)\) be the number of partitions of \(n\) into distinct members of
\(F_2,F_3,\ldots\), with \(R(n)=0\) for \(n<0\), and put
\[
 R^\dagger(n)=R(n)+R(n-1),
 \qquad
 \widetilde d_m(n)=[z^n]\prod_{j=1}^m(1+z^{F_j}).
\]
For \(0\le n<F_{m+2}\), define
\[
 \pi_m(n)\equiv F_{m+1}n+F_{m+1}-1\pmod{F_{m+2}}.
\]

\begin{theoremA}[Pointwise affine correspondence]
For every \(m\ge1\), the map \(\pi_m\) is a permutation of
\(\mathbb Z/F_{m+2}\mathbb Z\), and
\[
 d_m^\#(\pi_m(n))
 =\widetilde d_m(n)
 =R^\dagger(n)-R^\dagger(n-F_{m+1})
 \qquad(0\le n<F_{m+2}).
\]
Here \(d_m^\#\) is the residue-indexed fiber multiplicity.
\end{theoremA}

The affine congruence and the boundary-sensitive coefficient decomposition
are proved in
\Cref{thm:affine-transfer,cor:coefficient-spectrum,thm:partition-difference}.

For the second description, write
\[
 \mathcal I_j=[F_{j+1}-1,F_{j+2}-1).
\]

\begin{theoremB}[Two-layer identity]
For every \(m\ge1\),
\[
 \{d_m(x):x\in X_m\}
 =\{R(n):n\in\mathcal I_m\sqcup\mathcal I_{m+1}\}
\]
as multisets.  More explicitly, for \(0\le r<F_{m+2}\),
\[
 d_m^\#(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 R(F_{m+2}+r),&0\le r\le F_{m+1}-2,\\
 R(r),&F_{m+1}-1\le r<F_{m+2}.
 \end{cases}
\]
\end{theoremB}

The proof of \Cref{thm:fiber-partition-interval} treats the possible overflow
and the half-open endpoints directly.  In particular, the two-layer form is
already present at finite \(m\).

The interval identity also transfers the known largest-value classification
of Koc\'abov\'a, Mas\'akov\'a, and Pelantov\'a
\cite{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005}.  If
\(D_m=\max_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)\), then
\[
 D_0=D_1=1,
 \qquad D_{2k}=F_{k+2},
 \qquad D_{2k+1}=2F_{k+1},
\]
with the applicable ranges and maximizing residues given in
\Cref{cor:unconditional-extremal-fibers}.  This classification is used to
identify the right endpoint of the large-deviation domain.

The next distinct extremal level is not part of that transferred
classification.  If
\[
 E_m=\max\{d_m(x):d_m(x)<D_m\},
\]
then \Cref{thm:second-largest-fibers} proves, for \(k\ge5\),
\[
 E_{2k}=4F_{k-1}=F_{k+2}-F_{k-4},\qquad
 E_{2k+1}=5F_{k-1}=2F_{k+1}-F_{k-4}.
\]
Thus \(E_m=E_{m-2}+E_{m-4}\) for \(m\ge14\).  For \(k\ge8\), the
second-largest level occurs at exactly six residues in window \(2k\) and
eight residues in window \(2k+1\); the theorem classifies all of them by
concatenations of source maximizing blocks.

For a real tilt \(t\), set
\[
 S_t(m)=\sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^t,
 \qquad
 P(t)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log S_t(m).
\]
The pressure is the golden-ratio Bernoulli-convolution \(L^q\)-spectrum in
these coordinates.  More precisely, if \(\tau_\mu(t)\) uses interval-length
normalization, then
\begin{equation}
 P(t)=t\log2-\tau_\mu(t)\log\varphi.
 \label{eq:erdos-pressure-dictionary}
\end{equation}
The results of Lau--Ngai, Feng--Olivier, and Feng
\cite{LauNgai1998,FengOlivier2003,Feng2005} give its all-real phase diagram.
There is a unique \(\sigma_0>2\) satisfying
\[
 \frac{\zeta(\sigma_0-1)}{\zeta(\sigma_0)}=2,
\]
the pressure vanishes for \(t\le-\sigma_0\), and it is positive, analytic,
and strictly convex for \(t>-\sigma_0\).  At the corner its left derivative
is zero and its right derivative is a positive number \(v_c\); the explicit
continued-fraction formula is given in \Cref{thm:all-real-pressure}.

The finite-window laws require more than this exponential-rate input.  If
\(\Psi(k)\) denotes Weinstein's number of generating orbits at multiplicity
\(k\), then \Cref{thm:local-fiber-spectrum} proves
\[
 \card{\{x\in X_m:d_m(x)=1\}}=2,
\]
and, for \(k\ge2\),
\[
 \card{\{x\in X_m:d_m(x)=k\}}=4\Psi(k)\qquad(m\ge2k).
\]
The weighted form of the generator decomposition gives the renewal identity
in \Cref{prop:weighted-generator-renewal}.  At the critical tilt, let
\[
 \mu_C=\sum_{q\ge2}\sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
 c(p/q)q^{-\sigma_0},
\]
where \(c(p/q)\) is the generator cost.  For a state of multiplicity greater
than two, let \(\ell_m(x)\) be the cost of its generating orbit, assigning
cost zero to the bounded low-multiplicity states.  Under
\[
 \widehat Q_m(x)=\frac{d_m(x)^{-\sigma_0}}{S_{-\sigma_0}(m)},
\]
\Cref{thm:critical-finite-window-gibbs} gives
\begin{equation}
 S_{-\sigma_0}(m)\sim\frac{4m}{\mu_C},
 \qquad
 \left(\frac{\log d_m(\widehat{\mathbf X}_m)}m,
       \frac{\ell_m(\widehat{\mathbf X}_m)}m\right)
 \xrightarrow{\mathrm d}(v_cU,U),
 \label{eq:introduction-critical-coexistence}
\end{equation}
where \(U\) is uniform on \([0,1]\).  The factor four is the interior
two-layer orbit weight.

The same orbit structure also determines a mesoscopic regime that is not
visible in the pressure.  Put
\[
 \kappa=-\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}
 \right|_{s=\sigma_0}.
\]
If \(K_m\to\infty\) and \(2K_m\le m\), then
\begin{equation}
 \card{\{x\in X_m:d_m(x)\le K_m\}}
 \sim\frac4{\sigma_0\kappa}K_m^{\sigma_0}.
 \label{eq:introduction-mesoscopic-count}
\end{equation}
More generally, \Cref{thm:mesoscopic-fiber-spectrum} gives convergence of
the rescaled counting measures on multiplicities \(d_m(x)/m\le1/2\) to
the measure with density \(4y^{\sigma_0-1}/\kappa\).  Thus fixed-level
stabilization extends to a growing range with an explicit limiting law.
The onset \(m=2k\) in the fixed-level theorem is optimal for every \(k\ge3\),
as proved in \Cref{prop:sharp-stabilization-threshold}.

For the large-deviation statement, let \(\mathbf X_m\) be uniform on \(X_m\)
and put
\[
 Y_m=\frac1m\log d_m(\mathbf X_m),
 \qquad \varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},
 \qquad \alpha_+=\frac12\log\varphi.
\]

\begin{theoremC}[Full finite-window large deviations]
The sequence \((Y_m)\) satisfies a full large deviation principle with
speed \(m\) and good rate function
\[
 I(\alpha)=\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}
 \{t\alpha-P(t)+\log\varphi\}.
\]
Its effective domain is \([0,\alpha_+]\), and
\[
 I(\alpha)=\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha
 \qquad(0\le\alpha\le v_c).
\]
In particular, the lower bound holds at every nonexposed slope in
\((0,v_c)\).
\end{theoremC}

The proof in \Cref{thm:full-fiber-ldp} tilts exposed source states and maps
their generators injectively into larger layers without changing
multiplicity.  Sending the source tilt to the corner fills the entire affine
interval that convex duality alone misses.

The finite-window contributions are the two correspondence theorems,
their boundary-sensitive consequences, local stabilization, the critical
finite-size and coexistence laws, and the orbit-filling lower bounds.  The
pressure phase diagram is used through the cited Bernoulli-convolution
results, and the largest-value formulas through
\cite{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005}; \Cref{prop:weighted-generator-renewal}
derives the required weights from Weinstein's generator coordinates
\cite{Weinstein2016}.  These attributions delimit the claims relative to the
sources located in the literature audit.  The companion manuscript
\emph{Brocot condensation and critical Fibonacci renewal} is used only for
context; none of its total-variation or fractional-renewal results enters
the proofs here.

\Cref{sec:preliminaries,sec:residue-affine} define the fold and prove the
affine and partition-difference formulas.  The interval identity, extremal
transfer, second-extremal classification, and uniform orbit counts follow in
\Cref{sec:extremal-ldp}.
\Cref{sec:local-spectrum} develops the mesoscopic spectrum, weighted renewal,
critical law, and full finite-window LDP.  A final corollary records the corresponding
one-layer normalization, while the separately compiled appendix contains
the convention-heavy extremal audit and auxiliary directions.
