\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The finite-window fold has two exact arithmetic models.  The affine residue
permutation gives a pointwise coefficient-spectrum identity and the
partition-difference formula, while the interval identity gives the complete
fiber multiset as two consecutive layers of the classical Fibonacci
partition function.  Their roles are complementary: the first resolves each
residue, and the second exposes the orbit and renewal structure.

The imported pressure describes the exponential scale but does not settle
the finite-window questions at its corner.  Exact orbit weights yield local
spectral stabilization and the critical linear partition sum.  Under the
critical Gibbs law, normalized generator cost becomes uniform and log
multiplicity follows it along the coexistence segment.  These statements
retain information that disappears after taking only logarithmic moment
growth.

The same distinction is decisive for large deviations.  The pressure corner
creates nonexposed slopes, so a convex-duality upper bound alone cannot prove
a full principle.  The generator-orbit filling construction supplies the
missing one-dimensional lower bounds.  When generator cost is marked, an
exact low-reward suffix fills the remaining cost and produces the affine
coexistence face of the joint rate surface.

The pressure phase diagram and the extremal classification remain
subordinate inputs: the former comes from the golden-ratio
Bernoulli-convolution spectrum, and the latter from the known classification
of ordinary Fibonacci partitions.  What is specific to the finite window is
the exact correspondence, the boundary-sensitive transfer, the local and
critical laws, and the completion of the lower bounds across the freezing
corner.

The companion manuscript \emph{Brocot condensation and critical Fibonacci
renewal} studies the continued-fraction context law that sharpens the
critical generator tail.  Its total-variation theorem is not used here.  In
the other direction, the finite-window identities and large deviations of
this article are not needed for the classical one-layer renewal correction.
This limited interface keeps the two arithmetic mechanisms independently
readable.
