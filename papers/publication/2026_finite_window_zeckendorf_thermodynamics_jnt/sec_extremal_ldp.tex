\section{The interval model and extremal input}
\label{sec:extremal-ldp}

The affine model resolves individual residues.  Extremal and local questions
are better served by a second description in terms of consecutive values of
the ordinary Fibonacci partition function.

\begin{theorem}[Fiber--partition interval identity]
\label{thm:fiber-partition-interval}
Let \(m\ge0\) and \(0\le r<F_{m+2}\) be integers.  Then
\[
 d_m^\#(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 R(F_{m+2}+r),&0\le r\le F_{m+1}-2,\\
 R(r),&F_{m+1}-1\le r<F_{m+2}.
 \end{cases}
\]
An interval with lower endpoint larger than its upper endpoint is empty, so
the statement includes \(m=0,1\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The raw value \(N_m(\omega)\) is a sum of a subset of
\(F_2,\ldots,F_{m+1}\).  By \Cref{prop:single-overflow}, a word over residue
\(r\) has raw value \(r\) or \(F_{m+2}+r\).

If \(0\le r\le F_{m+1}-2\), then
\(F_{m+2}+r\le F_{m+3}-2\).  Every partition counted by
\(R(F_{m+2}+r)\) therefore uses only \(F_2,\ldots,F_{m+2}\).  Deleting
\(F_{m+2}\) bijects the partitions that contain this part with the
\(R(r)\) partitions of \(r\); the remaining partitions are the overflow
words.  Adding the nonoverflow words gives
\[
 d_m^\#(r)=R(r)+R(F_{m+2}+r)-R(r)=R(F_{m+2}+r).
\]
If \(F_{m+1}-1\le r<F_{m+2}\), then
\(F_{m+2}+r\ge F_{m+3}-1\), above the largest raw value
\(F_{m+3}-2\).  No overflow word occurs, and the remaining words are the
\(R(r)\) partitions of \(r\).
\end{proof}

For later use, write
\[
 \chi_m(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 F_{m+2}+V_m(x),&0\le V_m(x)\le F_{m+1}-2,\\
 V_m(x),&F_{m+1}-1\le V_m(x)<F_{m+2}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus \(\chi_m\) is a bijection from \(X_m\) onto the integer points of
\(\mathcal I_m\sqcup\mathcal I_{m+1}\), and
\(d_m(x)=R(\chi_m(x))\).

\begin{corollary}[Largest fibers]
\label{cor:unconditional-extremal-fibers}
\label{thm:unconditional-extremal-fibers}
Let \(D_m=\max_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)\).  Then
\[
 D_0=D_1=1,
 \qquad D_{2k}=F_{k+2}\quad(k\ge0),
\]
and
\[
 D_{2k+1}=2F_{k+1}\quad(k\ge1).
\]
No recurrence hypothesis is assumed.  Apart from finitely many initial
windows \(m\le12\), the maximum occurs at exactly two residues for even
\(m\) and four residues for odd \(m\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 M_\ell=\max_{F_{\ell+1}\le n<F_{\ell+2}}R(n).
\]
After the convention change \(G_j=F_{j+1}\), Theorem~4.7 of
Koc\'abov\'a, Mas\'akov\'a, and Pelantov\'a
\cite{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005} gives
\[
 M_{2a+1}=F_{a+2}\quad(a\ge0),
 \qquad M_{2a+2}=2F_{a+1}\quad(a\ge1),
\]
with \(M_1=M_2=1\).  The first block in
\Cref{thm:fiber-partition-interval} has maximum \(M_{m+1}\); for \(m\ge4\)
the second has maximum at most \(M_m<M_{m+1}\).  Thus
\(D_m=M_{m+1}\), and direct evaluation of the at most eight raw words covers
\(m<4\).  Theorem~5.3 of the same source gives two generic maximizing
arguments in odd source length and four in even source length.  Translating
them by the left endpoint \(F_{m+2}\) gives the stated degeneracies.  Direct
evaluation covers the finite initial range.
\end{proof}

For \(m\ge6\), the displayed formulas immediately give
\(D_m=D_{m-2}+D_{m-4}\).  The cited classification concerns the largest
multiplicity level; no classification of every fixed higher extremal rank is
used here.

We now determine the next distinct level.  For a tuple
\(\mathbf r=(r_1,\ldots,r_\ell)\), where
\(r_1,\ldots,r_{\ell-1}\ge1\) and \(r_\ell\ge0\), put
\[
 |\mathbf r|=\ell+\sum_{i=1}^\ell r_i
\]
and let \(\nu(\mathbf r)\) be the integer whose Zeckendorf word is
\(10^{r_1}\cdots10^{r_\ell}\).  Define
\[
 \mathscr M_j=\{\mathbf r:|\mathbf r|=j,\
 R(\nu(\mathbf r))=M_j\},
 \qquad
 \mathscr A\mathscr B=\{\mathbf a\mathbf b:
 \mathbf a\in\mathscr A,\ \mathbf b\in\mathscr B\},
\]
where juxtaposition denotes tuple concatenation.

\begin{lemma}[Odd-run gap]
\label{lem:odd-run-gap}
Let \(|\mathbf r|=2k+1\), and suppose that every internal coordinate
\(r_1,\ldots,r_{\ell-1}\) is odd.  For \(k\ge5\), either
\[
 R(\nu(\mathbf r))=F_{k+2}
\]
or
\[
 R(\nu(\mathbf r))\le F_{k+2}-F_{k-3}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We use the two-component form of Berstel's matrix formula employed in
\cite[Sec.~2]{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005}.  Write
\((u,v)=(R_0(\nu(\mathbf r)),R(\nu(\mathbf r)))\).  If the final coordinate
is \(2b\), the one-coordinate tuple gives \((u,v)=(b,b+1)\).  Prepending
an odd coordinate \(2a+1\) transforms the pair by
\begin{equation}
 T_a(u,v)=(av+u,(a+1)v+u).
 \label{eq:odd-run-pair-recursion}
\end{equation}
The associated budget is
\[
 k=b+\sum_{i<\ell}(a_i+1).
\]

Let \(\mathcal P_k\) be the set of pairs obtained at budget \(k\), ordered
coordinatewise.  The base pair and the first prepended coordinate give the
exact recursion
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_k=\{(k,k+1)\}\cup
 \bigcup_{0\le a<k}T_a(\mathcal P_{k-a-1}).
 \label{eq:odd-run-pair-set-recursion}
\end{equation}
For \(k\ge5\), put
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_k&=(F_{k+1},F_{k+2}),\\
 A_k&=(F_{k+1}-F_{k-4},F_{k+2}-F_{k-3}),\\
 B_k&=(F_{k+1},F_{k+2}-F_{k-2}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
We prove simultaneously that every pair is dominated by \(C_k\), that the
pairs with \(v=F_{k+2}\) have first coordinate \(F_k\) or \(F_{k+1}\),
and that every pair with \(v<F_{k+2}\) is dominated by \(A_k\) or \(B_k\).
Direct use of
\eqref{eq:odd-run-pair-set-recursion} proves the claim at budgets five and
six.  The same recursion gives \(\mathcal P_j\preceq C_j\) for
\(0\le j\le4\); below budget five only this maximal bound will be used.

Assume it at all smaller budgets.  The map \(T_a\) preserves the
coordinatewise order.  The two maximal images are
\[
 T_0(C_{k-1})=(F_k,F_{k+2}),
 \qquad
 T_1(C_{k-2})=C_k.
\]
The other pair at maximal second coordinate in the source budget, together
with the two nonmaximal envelopes, satisfies
\begin{align*}
 T_0(F_{k-1},F_{k+1})&\preceq B_k,&
 T_0(A_{k-1}),\,T_0(B_{k-1})&\preceq A_k,\\
 T_1(F_{k-2},F_k)&\preceq A_k,&
 T_1(A_{k-2})&=A_k,\qquad
 T_1(B_{k-2})\preceq A_k.
\end{align*}
Each comparison is a direct substitution using
\(F_{j+1}=F_j+F_{j-1}\).  For \(a\ge2\), the coarse bound gives
\[
 T_a(\mathcal P_{k-a-1})
 \preceq T_a(C_{k-a-1})\preceq B_k.
\]
For the last comparison, both coordinates of
\[
 T_a(C_{k-a-1})
 =\bigl(aF_{k-a+1}+F_{k-a},
        (a+1)F_{k-a+1}+F_{k-a}\bigr)
\]
decrease as \(a\) increases from two, and the value at \(a=2\) is \(B_k\).
The base pair \((k,k+1)\) is also dominated by \(B_k\).  This proves the
claim by induction.

The pair \(A_k\) is attained: it occurs at budgets five and six, and
\(T_1(A_{k-2})=A_k\).  Since the second coordinate of \(A_k\) is larger
than that of \(B_k\), the largest nonmaximal value of \(v\) is therefore
\(F_{k+2}-F_{k-3}\).  This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Second-largest fibers]
\label{thm:second-largest-fibers}
For every \(m\ge2\), let
\[
 E_m=\max\{d_m(x):x\in X_m,\ d_m(x)<D_m\}.
\]
For \(k\ge5\),
\begin{align}
 E_{2k}&=4F_{k-1}=F_{k+2}-F_{k-4},
 \label{eq:second-largest-even-window}\\
 E_{2k+1}&=5F_{k-1}=2F_{k+1}-F_{k-4}.
 \label{eq:second-largest-odd-window}
\end{align}
Consequently,
\[
 E_m=E_{m-2}+E_{m-4}\qquad(m\ge14).
\]

For \(k\ge8\), the level \(E_{2k}\) occurs at exactly six fibers, and its
source tuples are the disjoint union
\begin{equation}
 \{\mathbf r(\chi_{2k}(x)):d_{2k}(x)=E_{2k}\}
 =\mathscr M_6\mathscr M_{2k-5}
  \sqcup\mathscr M_{2k-2}\mathscr M_3.
 \label{eq:second-largest-even-source-tuples}
\end{equation}
The level \(E_{2k+1}\) occurs at exactly eight fibers, with source tuples
\begin{equation}
 \{\mathbf r(\chi_{2k+1}(x)):d_{2k+1}(x)=E_{2k+1}\}
 =\mathscr M_7\mathscr M_{2k-5}
  \sqcup\mathscr M_{2k-5}\mathscr M_7.
 \label{eq:second-largest-odd-source-tuples}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathbf r(n)\) denotes the zero-run tuple of the Zeckendorf word of
\(n\).  In particular, the corresponding fiber residue is
\(\nu(\mathbf r)-F_{m+2}\) in every displayed case.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first work in a source layer
\([F_{j+1},F_{j+2})\).  Berstel's factorization, in the notation above, says
that an even internal coordinate gives
\begin{equation}
 R(\nu(r_1,\ldots,r_\ell))
 =R(\nu(r_1,\ldots,r_i))
  R(\nu(r_{i+1},\ldots,r_\ell)).
 \label{eq:runner-up-even-coordinate-factorization}
\end{equation}
For \(k=5,6,7\), direct substitution in Berstel's two-component recursion
\eqref{eq:odd-run-pair-recursion} gives the odd-layer runner-up values
\(12,20,32\) and the even-layer runner-up values \(15,25,40\), respectively.
These are the asserted formulas.  We henceforth assume \(k\ge8\).

Choose the last even internal coordinate.  In a layer of odd length
\(2k+1\), the two factor lengths have the form \(2a\) and \(2b+1\), with
\(a+b=k\).  If \(a=1\), then the exceptional value \(M_2=1\) bounds the
product by \(F_{k+1}<4F_{k-1}\).  Otherwise the known maxima bound the
product by
\[
 2F_aF_{b+2}\le4F_{k-1}.
\]
For \(k\ge5\), equality holds precisely when
\[
 (2a,2b+1)=(6,2k-5)\quad\hbox{or}\quad(2k-2,3).
\]
Indeed, for fixed \(S\), d'Ocagne's identity gives
\begin{equation}
 F_{r+1}F_{S-r-1}-F_rF_{S-r}
 =(-1)^rF_{S-2r-1}.
 \label{eq:fixed-sum-fibonacci-product-step}
\end{equation}
On the half-range \(2\le r\le(S-1)/2\), the successive differences
alternate in sign and decrease in absolute value.  Hence the odd-indexed
local maxima decrease from \(r=3\), and symmetry treats the other half.
Taking \(S=k+2\) proves the displayed bound and its equality cases.  If no
internal coordinate is even, \Cref{lem:odd-run-gap} applies.  Since
\[
 F_{k+2}-F_{k-3}<F_{k+2}-F_{k-4}=4F_{k-1},
\]
the runner-up in the odd source layer is exactly \(4F_{k-1}\), and equality
in \eqref{eq:runner-up-even-coordinate-factorization} requires both factors
to be maximizers.  This proves the first tuple union.

In a layer of even length \(2k+2\), the two factor lengths in
\eqref{eq:runner-up-even-coordinate-factorization} are \(2a+1\) and
\(2b+1\), where \(a+b=k\).  Hence
\[
 R(\nu(\mathbf r))\le F_{a+2}F_{b+2}.
\]
The largest value \(2F_{k+1}\) occurs at \(a=1\) or \(b=1\).  Excluding
those equality cases,
\[
 F_{a+2}F_{b+2}\le5F_{k-1},
\]
with equality precisely at \((a,b)=(3,k-3)\) or \((k-3,3)\).
These assertions follow from \eqref{eq:fixed-sum-fibonacci-product-step}
with \(S=k+4\): the first pair of symmetric peaks is at indices
\(3,S-3\), and after their removal the next pair is at \(5,S-5\).
If one of
the boundary factors at \(a=1\) or \(b=1\) is not maximal, the odd-layer
result already proved bounds the product by
\[
 8F_{k-2}=5F_{k-1}-F_{k-7}<5F_{k-1}.
\]
Finally, if there is no even internal coordinate, the last coordinate is
odd.  Reducing it by one preserves the representation count and gives an
odd-length tuple of cost \(2k+1\), whose value is at most
\(F_{k+2}<5F_{k-1}\).  Thus the even source-layer runner-up is
\(5F_{k-1}\), with precisely the second displayed tuple union.

The explicit maximizing-tuple classification in
\cite[Thm.~5.3]{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005} gives
\[
 |\mathscr M_3|=|\mathscr M_6|=1,\qquad |\mathscr M_7|=2,
\]
and, in the generic ranges used here, two maximizers in odd source length
and four in even source length.  Substitution in the two unions gives six
and eight tuples, respectively; the explicit forms in that theorem also
show that the unions are disjoint.

It remains to pass from one source layer to the fiber window.  The first
block in \Cref{thm:fiber-partition-interval} is the layer of length \(m+1\).
Its runner-up strictly exceeds the maximum of the second block: the two
comparisons reduce to
\[
 4F_{k-1}>2F_k,
 \qquad 5F_{k-1}>F_{k+2}.
\]
Therefore the source runner-up is the fiber runner-up.  The map \(\chi_m\)
gives the stated locations and residue translation.  The identities in
\eqref{eq:second-largest-even-window}--
\eqref{eq:second-largest-odd-window} follow from the Fibonacci recurrence,
as does the recurrence for \(E_m\).
\end{proof}

For real \(t\), define
\[
 S_t(m)=\sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^t.
\]
All fibers are nonempty by \Cref{lem:interval-completeness}, so negative
tilts are well defined.  Set
\[
 \mathcal I_j=[F_{j+1}-1,F_{j+2}-1),
 \qquad
 N_j(k)=\card{\{n\in\mathcal I_j:R(n)=k\}}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Weinstein orbit layers]
\label{lem:weinstein-orbit-layers}
For \(k>2\), every solution of \(R(n)=k\) has a unique form
\[
 [a]\tau^\varepsilon\sigma^\delta(g),
 \qquad g\in G(k),\quad a\ge0,
 \quad\varepsilon,\delta\in\{0,1\},
\]
where \(G(k)\) contains one generator from each of the \(\Psi(k)\) orbits.
Writing \(L(n)\) for the largest Zeckendorf index, the four branches satisfy
\begin{align}
 L([a]n)&=L(n)+2a, \label{eq:L-a-shift}\\
 L(\tau n)&=L(n)\mathbin{\pm}1,
     \quad L(\tau n)\equiv L(n)+1\pmod2, \label{eq:L-tau-shift}\\
 L([a]\tau n)&=L(\tau n)+2a, \label{eq:L-a-tau-shift}\\
 L(\sigma n)&=L(n)+1. \label{eq:L-sigma-shift}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The normal form is Weinstein's Theorem~3.8 \cite{Weinstein2016}.  In his
formulas~(18)--(19), the prefix of \([a]n\) has indices
\(1,3,\ldots,2\theta(n)+2a-3\), while the shifted terminal subpartition
begins at index at least \(2\theta(n)+2a\).  Its largest index is therefore
\(L(n)+2a\).  Formula~(19) shifts that terminal subpartition by \(1\) or
\(-1\), according to parity, proving the second identity and its parity
statement; applying the first identity to \(\tau n\) proves the third.
Weinstein's involution \(\sigma\) adds one to every Zeckendorf index, which
proves the last identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform layer count]
\label{lem:uniform-layer-count}
One has
\[
 N_0(1)=0,\qquad N_j(1)=1\quad(j\ge1).
\]
For every \(j\ge0\) and \(k\ge2\),
\[
 N_j(k)\le2\Psi(k).
\]
Moreover, \(N_j(2)=2\) for \(j\ge4\), while for \(k>2\),
\[
 N_j(k)=2\Psi(k)\qquad(j\ge2k).
\]
The endpoint \(j=2k\) is included.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Weinstein uses \(f_0=f_1=1\), hence \(f_j=F_{j+1}\), and his layer
\([f_j-1,f_{j+1}-1)\) is \(\mathcal I_j\).  His partition function is
\(R\).  Lemma~2.19 of \cite{Weinstein2016}, with the two value-one
endpoints removed, gives
\begin{equation}
 R(n)>1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \bigl(n\in\mathcal I_j\iff L(n)=j\bigr).
 \label{eq:layer-largest-index}
\end{equation}
The value-one solutions are \(f_i-1\), placing one at the left endpoint of
each nonempty layer.

At level two, \(\Psi(2)=1\), and the two families
\[
 f_i-1+f_{i+3},\qquad f_{i+2}-1+f_{i+4}\quad(i\ge0)
\]
place at most one point from each family in a layer and both points in every
layer \(j\ge4\).

Let \(k>2\) and fix \(g\in G(k)\).  By
\Cref{lem:weinstein-orbit-layers}, the two nonsigma branches run through two
opposite-parity progressions and together meet any layer at most once.  Their
sigma images meet layer \(j\) exactly when the corresponding nonsigma
branches meet layer \(j-1\).  Thus an orbit contributes at most two points,
which proves \(N_j(k)\le2\Psi(k)\).

For the endpoint, Weinstein's maximal-generator lemma gives
\(g\le f_{2k}-2<f_{2k}\), and hence
\begin{equation}
 L(g)\le2k-1.
 \label{eq:generator-largest-index-bound}
\end{equation}
The opposite-parity progressions therefore meet every layer \(j\ge2k\), and
they also meet layer \(2k-1\): use the first progression when \(L(g)\) is
odd and the tau progression otherwise.  Consequently the nonsigma branches
contribute once to layer \(j\), and their sigma images contribute once more,
for every \(j\ge2k\).  Distinct generating orbits are disjoint, proving the
equality, including \(j=2k\).
\end{proof}

Let \(\sigma_0>2\) be the unique solution of
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\zeta(\sigma_0-1)}{\zeta(\sigma_0)}=2.
 \label{eq:freezing-exponent}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-layer frozen branch]
\label{prop:negative-temperature-freezing}
For every real \(t\le-\sigma_0\), the pressure exists and
\[
 P(t)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log S_t(m)=0.
\]
Consequently no real-analytic function on \(\mathbb R\) agrees with these
pressure limits and satisfies \(P(0)=\log\varphi\); in particular, no
everywhere finite extension is both real analytic and strictly convex.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(s>2\), let
\[
 A(s)=\sum_{n=2}^\infty\frac{\phi_{\rm E}(n)}{n^s}
 =\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}-1.
\]
Weinstein's free-monoid decomposition gives, in \([0,+\infty]\),
\begin{equation}
 1+\sum_{k=2}^\infty\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^s}
 =\sum_{r=0}^\infty A(s)^r.
 \label{eq:weinstein-level-dirichlet-series}
\end{equation}
The right side is finite exactly for \(s>\sigma_0\).  The interval identity
also gives
\begin{equation}
 S_t(m)=\sum_{n=F_{m+1}-1}^{F_{m+3}-2}R(n)^t.
 \label{eq:real-collision-two-layers}
\end{equation}
If \(t< -\sigma_0\), \Cref{lem:uniform-layer-count} and
\eqref{eq:weinstein-level-dirichlet-series} bound \(S_t(m)\) above and below
by positive constants independent of \(m\), proving the limit.

At \(t=-\sigma_0\), fix \(\varepsilon>0\).  Since only levels
\(k\le D_m\) occur,
\[
 S_{-\sigma_0}(m)
 \le2+4D_m^\varepsilon
 \sum_{k=2}^\infty\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^{\sigma_0+\varepsilon}}.
\]
The series is finite and
\(m^{-1}\log D_m\to\tfrac12\log\varphi\) by
\Cref{cor:unconditional-extremal-fibers}.  Letting
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves the endpoint.  An analytic function that
vanishes on \(( -\infty,-\sigma_0)\) vanishes identically, whereas
\(P(0)=\lim m^{-1}\log\card{X_m}=\log\varphi\), proving the final claims.
\end{proof}

The pressure recovery below identifies this frozen boundary with a corner
whose right derivative is positive.  Convex duality therefore omits the
intermediate slopes, which will be supplied by the orbit-filling construction
in \Cref{thm:full-fiber-ldp}.
