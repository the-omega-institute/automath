\begin{abstract}
We study the multiplicity spectrum of the finite map that folds binary
Fibonacci sums into a legal Zeckendorf window.  An affine permutation of the
residues identifies every fiber with a coefficient of a finite Fibonacci
subset-sum polynomial and gives a partition-difference formula.  A second
identity identifies the complete fiber multiset with the classical
Fibonacci partition function on two consecutive layers; it yields local
spectral stabilization and transfers the known largest values.  A
factorization at even Zeckendorf zero-runs then determines the second-largest
fiber value, all of its locations, and its eventual degeneracy.

Using the known golden-ratio Bernoulli-convolution \(L^q\)-spectrum, we
derive an exact weighted renewal identity in the finite-window coordinates.
At the freezing point the critical partition sum is asymptotically linear,
and the Gibbs law converges to a uniform coexistence law for normalized
generator cost and logarithmic multiplicity.

The pressure corner leaves an interval of slopes for which the
G\"artner--Ellis theorem gives no lower bound.  An injective orbit-filling
map preserves multiplicity while moving exposed source states into larger
Fibonacci layers.  This supplies all nonexposed slopes and proves a full
large deviation principle whose rate function is affine across the
coexistence interval.

At the scale between fixed levels and exponential deviations, the counting
measure of fibers with multiplicity of order \(m\) converges to an explicit
power-law measure.  The fixed-level stabilization threshold is sharp at
every multiplicity.
\end{abstract}
