\section{Extremal convention audit}
\label{sec:extremal-audit}

Put \(G_j=F_{j+1}\), so \(G_0=G_1=1\).  For \(k\ge5\), define
\[
 I_{2k+1}=
 \begin{cases}
 G_{k+1}G_{k-3}+1,&k\text{ even},\\
 G_kG_{k-2}+1,&k\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]
For \(k\ge7\), define
\[
 I_{2k}=
 \begin{cases}
 G_{k+2}G_{k-5}+G_3+1,&k\text{ even},\\
 G_{k+1}G_{k-4}+G_3+1,&k\text{ odd},
 \end{cases}
\]
and
\[
 J_{2k}=
 \begin{cases}
 G_{k+1}G_{k-3}+1,&k\text{ even},\\
 G_kG_{k-2}+1,&k\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Complete maximizing-residue audit]
\label{prop:extremal-residue-audit}
Let \(D_m=\max_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)\).  Then
\[
 D_0=D_1=1,\qquad D_{2k}=F_{k+2}\quad(k\ge0),
\]
and
\[
 D_{2k+1}=2F_{k+1}\quad(k\ge1).
\]
For every \(m\ge0\), a visible word is a maximizer if and only if its residue
belongs to the following set.  If \(m=2k\ge10\), then
\[
 \mathcal M_{2k}=\{I_{2k+1}-1,\ G_{2k}-1-I_{2k+1}\}.
\]
If \(m=2k-1\ge13\), then
\[
 \mathcal M_{2k-1}=\{I_{2k}-1,\ J_{2k}-1,
 G_{2k-1}-1-I_{2k},\ G_{2k-1}-1-J_{2k}\}.
\]
The remaining sets are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
m&D_m&\mathcal M_m\\ \hline
0&1&\{0\}\\
1&1&\{0,1\}\\
2&2&\{0\}\\
3&2&\{0,1,3\}\\
4&3&\{0,3\}\\
5&4&\{3\}\\
6&5&\{3,8\}\\
7&6&\{3,8,11,16\}\\
8&8&\{8,16,24\}\\
9&10&\{16,24,29,37\}\\
11&16&\{37,63,71,79,105\}.
\end{array}
\]
The generic even formula covers \(m=10,12\).  Thus, outside the displayed
initial exceptions, the degeneracy is two in even windows and four in odd
windows.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Koc\'abov\'a, Mas\'akov\'a, and Pelantov\'a use \(G_0=G_1=1\) and parts
\(G_1,G_2,\ldots\) \cite{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005}.  Since
\(G_j=F_{j+1}\), their representation function is \(R\).  Their generic
maximizing arguments are
\[
 G_{2k+1}-1+I_{2k+1},\qquad G_{2k+2}-1-I_{2k+1}
\]
in odd source length \(2k+1\), and
\[
 \begin{split}
 G_{2k}-1+I_{2k},&\quad G_{2k+1}-1-I_{2k},\\
 G_{2k}-1+J_{2k},&\quad G_{2k+1}-1-J_{2k}
 \end{split}
\]
in even source length \(2k\).  Their exceptional source arguments and the
corresponding residues are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{source length}&\text{partition arguments}&\text{fiber residues}\\ \hline
9&63,71,79&8,16,24\\
12&270,296,304,312,338&37,63,71,79,105.
\end{array}
\]
The last column subtracts \(G_9=55\) or \(G_{12}=233\).

The interval identity in the main article shows that the first block of the
fiber list begins at \(F_{m+2}\), and its maximum strictly exceeds that of
the second block for \(m\ge4\).  Subtracting this left endpoint from the
source arguments gives the two generic sets and the exceptional rows above.
Direct enumeration gives the shorter rows, and the source maximum formulas
translate to the displayed values of \(D_m\).
\end{proof}

\section{Source-coordinate audit for the orbit layers}

Weinstein writes \(f_0=f_1=1\), so \(f_j=F_{j+1}\), and his layer
\([f_j-1,f_{j+1}-1)\) is \(\mathcal I_j\).  For \(n\) in his first sheet,
let \(\theta(n)\) and \(Z'(n)\) be the initial-block parameter and terminal
Zeckendorf subpartition in formulas~(17)--(19) of
\cite{Weinstein2016}.  The terminal subpartition is nonempty and has largest
index \(L(n)\).

The defining gap at the start of the tail gives
\begin{equation}
 z(n,\theta(n))\ge2\theta(n).
 \label{eq:appendix-theta-tail-separation}
\end{equation}
For \(\theta(n)=1\), the first index is at least three.  If
\(\theta(n)>1\), the preceding indices rise by two, the first is at least
one, and the gap at \(\theta(n)\) is at least three; hence
\[
 z(n,\theta(n))\ge1+2(\theta(n)-2)+3=2\theta(n).
\]
Formula~(18) is
\[
 [a](n)=f_{2\theta(n)-2+2a}-1
       +\lVert f_{Z'(n)+2a}\rVert.
\]
Using
\[
 f_{2q}-1=f_1+f_3+\cdots+f_{2q-1},
\]
with the empty sum when \(q=0\), and
\eqref{eq:appendix-theta-tail-separation}, this is already the Zeckendorf
decomposition
\[
 Z([a]n)=\langle1,3,\ldots,2\theta(n)+2a-3\rangle
 \sqcup\bigl(Z'(n)+2a\bigr).
\]
The terminal block supplies the largest index, proving
\(L([a]n)=L(n)+2a\).

Formula~(19) gives
\[
 \tau(n)=
 \begin{cases}
 f_{2\theta(n)}-1+\lVert f_{Z'(n)+1}\rVert,
   &z(n,\theta(n))\text{ odd},\\
 f_{2\theta(n)-4}-1+\lVert f_{Z'(n)-1}\rVert,
   &z(n,\theta(n))\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The same prefix expansion and separation show that the shifted terminal
block again supplies the largest index.  Therefore
\[
 L(\tau n)=
 \begin{cases}
 L(n)+1,&z(n,\theta(n))\text{ odd},\\
 L(n)-1,&z(n,\theta(n))\text{ even},
 \end{cases}
\]
and the two nonsigma branches have opposite largest-index parity.  Applying
the first calculation to \(\tau n\) gives
\(L([a]\tau n)=L(\tau n)+2a\).  Finally, Weinstein's involution \(\sigma\)
adds one to every Zeckendorf index, so
\[
 Z(\sigma n)=Z(n)+1,
 \qquad L(\sigma n)=L(n)+1.
\]
These formulas also verify the half-open endpoint convention: the shared
layer endpoint has partition value one and never enters a level \(k\ge2\).

\section{First Knauf layers}

For the matrix convention used in the pressure normalization, put
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 R=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad M_w=w_1\cdots w_{d-1}.
\]
A composition of \(d\) is encoded by a word
\(w\in\{L,R\}^{d-1}\), with a letter \(R\) at each proper partial sum.
For the associated negative continued fraction, the denominator is
\(q=\lVert M_w\rVert_\Sigma\) and the generator cost is \(2d+1\).  The first
four nonempty layers are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
d&\text{words}&q\text{ in word order}&\text{cost}\\ \hline
1&\varnothing&2&3\\
2&L,R&3,\,3&5\\
3&LL,LR,RL,RR&4,\,5,\,5,\,4&7\\
4&\{L,R\}^3&5,\,7,\,8,\,7,\,7,\,8,\,7,\,5&9.
\end{array}
\]
Thus there is no depth-zero fraction in \((0,1)\), and \(1/2\) is the
depth-one empty matrix word.  The table fixes the word length, denominator,
and cost shifts used in the main pressure proposition.

\section{A Tauberian orbit consequence}

Let
\[
 \kappa=-\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}
 \right|_{s=\sigma_0}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Generating-orbit Tauberian law]
\label{thm:psi-tauberian}
As \(x\to\infty\),
\[
 \sum_{k\le x}\Psi(k)\sim\frac{x^{\sigma_0}}{\sigma_0\kappa},
 \qquad
 \sum_{k\le x}\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^{\sigma_0}}\sim\frac1\kappa\log x.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \left(\frac4\kappa+o(1)\right)\log m
 \le S_{-\sigma_0}(m)
 \le\left(\frac{2\log\varphi}{\kappa}+o(1)\right)m.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set
\[
 A(s)=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\phi_{\rm E}(n)}{n^s}
 =\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}-1.
\]
For \(\Re s>\sigma_0\), Weinstein's identity is
\[
 1+\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^s}=\frac1{1-A(s)}.
\]
If \(\tau\ne0\), then
\(|A(\sigma_0+i\tau)|<A(\sigma_0)=1\): equality would force
\(2^{-i\tau}=3^{-i\tau}=1\), contrary to
\(\log2/\log3\notin\mathbb Q\).  The series therefore has only the simple
boundary pole at \(\sigma_0\), of residue \(1/\kappa\).  Wiener--Ikehara
\cite{Korevaar2004} gives the first asymptotic, and partial summation gives
the second.

Local stabilization yields
\[
 S_{-\sigma_0}(m)\ge
 2+4\sum_{2\le k\le m/2}\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^{\sigma_0}},
\]
while the uniform layer bound and \(k\le D_m\) give the same expression with
upper limit \(D_m\) as an upper bound.  Use the second asymptotic and
\(\log D_m\sim\tfrac12m\log\varphi\).
\end{proof}

\section{Finite-prime-support coefficients}

For the boundary weight, put
\[
 K_m(c)=
 \begin{cases}
 4,&c\le m-1,\\
 3,&c=m,\\
 1,&c=m+1,\\
 0,&c\ge m+2.
 \end{cases}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Finite-prime-support coefficient interface]
\label{prop:prime-support-interface}
Fix distinct primes \(\mathcal P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_d\}\).  For
\(\mathbf a\in\mathbb N^d\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\), put
\[
 q_{\mathbf a}=\prod_{i=1}^dp_i^{a_i},
 \qquad
 A_{\mathbf a}(u)=
 \sum_{\substack{1\le h<q_{\mathbf a}\\(h,q_{\mathbf a})=1}}
 u^{c(h/q_{\mathbf a})},
\]
and
\[
 A_{\mathcal P}(\mathbf z,u)=
 \sum_{\mathbf a\ne\mathbf0}A_{\mathbf a}(u)\mathbf z^{\mathbf a}.
\]
If
\[
 U_j(q_{\mathbf n})=\card{\{g:R(g)=q_{\mathbf n},\ L(g)=j\}},
\]
with \(U_0(1)=1\), then
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{\mathbf n\in\mathbb N^d}\sum_{j\ge0}
 U_j(q_{\mathbf n})\mathbf z^{\mathbf n}u^j
 =\frac1{1-A_{\mathcal P}(\mathbf z,u)}.
 \label{eq:prime-support-joint-renewal}
\end{equation}
For \(q_{\mathbf n}>2\),
\begin{equation}
 C_m(q_{\mathbf n})=\sum_{j\ge0}K_m(j)U_j(q_{\mathbf n}).
 \label{eq:prime-support-window-interface}
\end{equation}
After removal of the cost mark,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{\mathbf n\in\mathbb N^d}
 \left(\sum_{j\ge0}U_j(q_{\mathbf n})\right)\mathbf z^{\mathbf n}
 =\frac1{\displaystyle
 2-\prod_{i=1}^d\frac{1-z_i}{1-p_i z_i}}.
 \label{eq:prime-support-unmarked-rational}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under Weinstein's free-monoid parameterization, a letter with denominator
\(q_{\mathbf a}\) contributes exponent vector \(\mathbf a\), cost
\(c(h/q_{\mathbf a})\), and multiplier \(q_{\mathbf a}\).  Unique ordered
factorization proves \eqref{eq:prime-support-joint-renewal}.  The four orbit
branches give \eqref{eq:prime-support-window-interface}.  Finally,
\[
 \sum_{a\ge0}\phi_{\rm E}(p_i^a)z_i^a=\frac{1-z_i}{1-p_i z_i};
\]
multiplication over the primes proves
\eqref{eq:prime-support-unmarked-rational}.
\end{proof}

The active cutoff in \eqref{eq:prime-support-window-interface} remains open.
The unmarked function falls within smooth-point multivariate coefficient
methods such as Pemantle--Wilson \cite{PemantleWilson2002}, but the located
results do not retain the continued-fraction cost with a direction-uniform
error and uniform integrability.  Already for \(\mathcal P=\{2\}\), the
normalized letter law gives mass \(3^{-a}\) to each denominator \(2^a\),
while \(c(1/2^a)=2^{a+1}-1\); hence \(\mathbb EC^2=\infty\) and
\(\mathbb Ee^{\theta C}=\infty\) for \(\theta>0\), although its first moment
is finite.  A sharp cutoff asymptotic therefore requires a lattice stable or
semistable local-renewal theorem uniform under multivariate exponent
conditioning, followed by the nonsmooth weights \(K_m(m)=3\) and
\(K_m(m+1)=1\).  No such theorem is used here.
