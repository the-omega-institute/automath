\begin{theorem}[Multiplicity-free support entropy with arithmetic remainder]
\label{thm:minimal-generator-growth}
For every integer $n\ge3$, put $k=\omega(n)$ and
$L(n)=\log\#\mathcal M_n$. Then $\mathcal M_n$ is nonempty. If $k\ge3$ and
$r=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:private-cover-finite-bounds}
 (2^r-1)^{k-r}
 \le \#\mathcal M_n
 \le \sum_{s=1}^{k}\binom{k}{s}(2R(n))^s2^{s(k-s)}.
\end{equation}
In particular, uniformly along every sequence for which $\omega(n)\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:private-cover-entropy-order}
 \frac{\log2}{4}\,\omega(n)^2-O(1)
 \le L(n)
 \le \frac{\log2}{4}\,\omega(n)^2
      +\omega(n)\log R(n)+O(\omega(n)).
\end{equation}
No exact quadratic coefficient for the full quantity $L(n)$ is claimed.
The coefficient $\log2/4$ is exact only for the multiplicity-free
minimal-cover contribution: the lower construction embeds the unweighted
cover count, and the private-coordinate encoding gives the same quadratic
support term in the upper bound. The display is not an asymptotic formula for
$L(n)$ unless $R(n)=\exp(o(\omega(n)))$; no such unconditional estimate for
$R(n)$ is proved here. In particular, the displayed coefficient is not
claimed to be optimal among all deductions from fibotomic or exact-rank
arithmetic information.

For comparison, the crude support-count estimate gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:finite-minimal-generator-bound}
 \#\mathcal M_n
 \le \sum_{r=0}^{k}\binom{\Omega(F_n)}{r}
 \le n^k.
\end{equation}
Combining only the last inequality with the classical maximal order of
$\omega(n)$ gives the crude corollary
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:crude-maximal-order}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{L(n)\log\log n}{(\log n)^2}\le1.
\end{equation}
This envelope has scale $(\log n)^2/\log\log n$, a factor $\log\log n$
above the witness-cover entropy scale; it is not a consequence of
\eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order}.
At the coarser divisor-lattice scale, the antichain property and Wigert's
theorem give the Sperner-type envelope
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sperner-wigert-envelope}
 L(n)\le\log\tau(F_n)
 \le (\log2\,\log\varphi+o(1))\frac{n}{\log n},
 \qquad \varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2};
\end{equation}
in fact \eqref{eq:crude-maximal-order} is stronger and implies
$L(n)=o(n/\log n)$.

Moreover, as $x\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mean-logarithmic-growth}
 \left(\frac{\log2}{4}+o(1)\right)x(\log\log x)^2
 \le \sum_{3\le n\le x}L(n)
 \le (1+o(1))x\log x\,\log\log x.
\end{equation}

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First, $F_n\in B_n$ for every $n\ge3$.  Indeed,
$F_n\mid F_d$ implies $F_n\le F_d$, and strict increase of the Fibonacci
sequence from index $2$ onward forces $d\ge n$; hence $\alpha(F_n)=n$.
Because $B_n$ is a nonempty finite poset, it has a minimal element, so
$\mathcal M_n\ne\varnothing$ and $L(n)$ is defined.

We first prove the explicit finite private-cover bound in
\eqref{eq:private-cover-finite-bounds}. Fix $k\ge3$, and give
$\mathcal P(n)$ an arbitrary order. Put
\[
 X=\mathcal P(n)\cap\{2,3\},\qquad r=\lfloor k/2\rfloor.
\]
Since $k-\#X\ge k-2\ge r$, choose an $r$-element set
$P\subseteq\mathcal P(n)\setminus X$, and put
$Q=\mathcal P(n)\setminus P$. For every map
\[
 H:Q\longrightarrow 2^P\setminus\{\varnothing\}
\]
and every $i\in P$, define
\[
 T_i=\{i\}\cup\{q\in Q:i\in H(q)\}.
\]
The index $n_{T_i}=\prod_{\ell\in T_i}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n)}$ has a prime
divisor outside $\{2,3\}$, because $i\in P$. Hence $n_{T_i}\ge5$ and
$n_{T_i}\notin\{6,12\}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors} supplies a
prime $\pi_{T_i}$ with $\alpha(\pi_{T_i})=n_{T_i}$; fix one such prime for
each support that occurs.

The supports $T_i$ cover $\mathcal P(n)$ because $H(q)$ is nonempty for every
$q\in Q$. Moreover, $i$ belongs to $T_i$ and to no $T_j$ with $j\ne i$.
Each $\pi_{T_i}$ is a prime atom, so
$E_n(\pi_{T_i})=T_n(\pi_{T_i})=T_i$. Thus
$\{\pi_{T_i}:i\in P\}$ is an $n$-witness cover. Distinct supports have
distinct ranks and therefore distinct underlying primes. If two maps $H$
differ, then their support families differ, since the edge containing the
private coordinate $i$ identifies $T_i$; unique factorization then gives
different minimal generators. There are
$(2^r-1)^{\#Q}=(2^r-1)^{k-r}$ maps $H$, proving the lower bound in
\eqref{eq:private-cover-finite-bounds}.

For the upper bound, let $\Theta$ be an $n$-witness cover with $s$ atoms. For
each $\theta\in\Theta$, choose the least coordinate
\[
 x_\theta\in E_n(\theta)\setminus
 \bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta\setminus\{\theta\}}T_n(\eta).
\]
These private coordinates are distinct; write
$P=\{x_\theta:\theta\in\Theta\}$ and
$Q=\mathcal P(n)\setminus P$. If $x_\theta=x$, then
\[
 T_n(\theta)=\{x\}\cup U_\theta,\qquad U_\theta\subseteq Q,
\]
because every other member of $P$ is private to another atom. By the
prime--ladder alternative in Lemma~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}, either
$E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$ or $E_n(\theta)=\{x\}$. Hence there are at most two
support-pair alternatives, and the definition
\eqref{eq:rank-window-multiplicity} allows at most $R(n)$ atoms for each.
Thus, once $P$ is fixed, the atom indexed by each
$x\in P$ has at most $2R(n)2^{k-s}$ choices. Ignoring coverage and
coprimeness only enlarges the count, so summing over the
$\binom{k}{s}$ possible sets $P$ and over $1\le s\le k$ proves the upper
bound in \eqref{eq:private-cover-finite-bounds}.

Now $r(k-r)=\lfloor k^2/4\rfloor$ and
$(k-r)\log(1-2^{-r})=O(1)$. Hence the logarithm of the lower bound is
$(\log2)k^2/4+O(1)$. For the upper bound, use
$s(k-s)\le k^2/4$, $s\le k$, and
$\sum_s\binom{k}{s}\le2^k$ to obtain
\[
 \log\sum_{s=1}^{k}\binom{k}{s}(2R(n))^s2^{s(k-s)}
 \le\frac{\log2}{4}k^2+k\log R(n)+O(k).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order}.

Let
\[
 \mathcal Q_n=\{p^e:p\mid F_n,\ 1\le e\le\nu_p(F_n)\}.
\]
This set has cardinality $\Omega(F_n)$.  By unique factorization, the map
\[
 m\longmapsto\{p^{\nu_p(m)}:p\mid m\}
\]
is injective from $\mathcal M_n$ into the subsets of $\mathcal Q_n$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound} says that every image has at most
$k=\omega(n)$ members.  This proves the first inequality in
\eqref{eq:finite-minimal-generator-bound}.

Since every prime factor of $F_n$ is at least $2$,
$2^{\Omega(F_n)}\le F_n$.  Also $F_n<2^{n-1}$ for $n\ge3$, by the elementary
induction $F_{j+1}=F_j+F_{j-1}<2^j$.  Hence
$\Omega(F_n)\le n-2$.  A subset of an $N$-element set having at most $k$
members can be encoded injectively by its increasing list padded to length
$k$ with a new symbol; therefore
\[
 \sum_{r=0}^k\binom Nr\le(N+1)^k.
\]
Taking $N=\Omega(F_n)$ gives the second inequality in
\eqref{eq:finite-minimal-generator-bound}.

The classical maximal-order formula for $\omega(n)$
\cite[Theorem~22.11]{HardyWright2008Introduction}
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{\omega(n)\log\log n}{\log n}=1
\]
now combines with $L(n)\le\omega(n)\log n$ to prove
\eqref{eq:crude-maximal-order}. This argument uses only the crude estimate
$\#\mathcal M_n\le n^{\omega(n)}$, not the witness-cover entropy bound. On
the other hand,
$\mathcal M_n$ is an antichain in the divisor lattice of $F_n$, so
\[
 \#\mathcal M_n\le\widthop(\operatorname{Div}(F_n))\le\tau(F_n).
\]
Wigert's maximal-order theorem \cite{Wigert1907Divisors} states that
\[
 \log\tau(N)\le(\log2+o(1))\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}.
\]
Binet's formula gives $\log F_n=n\log\varphi+O(1)$ and
$\log\log F_n=\log n+O(1)$, yielding
\eqref{eq:sperner-wigert-envelope}.  Finally,
$(\log n)^2/\log\log n=o(n/\log n)$, so
\eqref{eq:crude-maximal-order} gives the stated strict improvement.

For the mean upper bound, use the classical mean-value estimate
\cite{HardyRamanujan1917NormalPrimeFactors}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mean-omega-input}
 \sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)=x\log\log x+O(x),
\end{equation}
together with $L(n)\le\omega(n)\log n$.  Uniformly for $n\le x$,
$\log n\le\log x$, and hence
\[
 \sum_{3\le n\le x}L(n)
 \le\log x\sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)
 =(1+o(1))x\log x\,\log\log x.
\]

Tur\'an's second-moment form of the Hardy--Ramanujan theorem
\cite{Turan1934HardyRamanujan} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega-second-moment}
 \sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)^2=(1+o(1))x(\log\log x)^2.
\end{equation}
After enlarging the absolute constant in the lower half of
\eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order}, it holds as
$L(n)\ge(\log2)\omega(n)^2/4-O(1)$ also for $\omega(n)<3$.
Summation and \eqref{eq:omega-second-moment} prove the lower half of
\eqref{eq:mean-logarithmic-growth} and complete the proof.
\end{proof}


\begin{definition}[Sufficient maximum-window hypothesis H1]
\label{hyp:normal-window}
For every $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 \#\{n\le x:\log R(n)>\varepsilon\omega(n)\}=o(x).
\tag{H1}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Sufficient maximum-window hypothesis H2]
\label{hyp:mean-window}
One has
\[
 \sum_{3\le n\le x}\omega(n)\log R(n)
 =o\bigl(x(\log\log x)^2\bigr).
\tag{H2}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional obstruction from Fibonacci prime divisors]
\label{prop:blms-obstructs-mean-window}
Assume Conjecture~5.1 of Bugeaud--Luca--Mignotte--Siksek
\cite{BugeaudLucaMignotteSiksek2005FewPrimeDivisors}, namely that
$\omega(F_n)\gg\log n$ for composite $n$. Then, for every fixed
$\varepsilon$ with $0<\varepsilon<1-\log2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blms-normal-window-obstruction}
 \liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac1x
 \#\{n\le x:\log R(n)>\varepsilon\omega(n)\}
 \ge\frac6{\pi^2},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blms-mean-window-obstruction}
 \liminf_{x\to\infty}
 \frac{\sum_{3\le n\le x}\omega(n)\log R(n)}
 {x(\log\log x)^2}
 \ge\frac6{\pi^2}(1-\log2)>0.
\end{equation}
In particular, under that published conjecture, both sufficient
maximum-window hypotheses \textup{(H1)} and \textup{(H2)} are false.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $n$ be squarefree and put $k=\omega(n)$. The exact-rank partition of the
prime divisors of $F_n$ gives
\[
 \omega(F_n)=\sum_{d\mid n}a(d).
\]
For squarefree $n$, every nonempty support $J\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$ has the
single rank $d=\prod_{\ell\in J}\ell$ in its window. Since $a(1)=0$, the
pigeonhole principle and \eqref{eq:prime-window-versus-R} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:squarefree-blms-pigeonhole}
 R(n)\ge\max_{d\mid n}a(d)
 \ge\frac{\omega(F_n)}{2^k-1}.
\end{equation}
Under the stated conjecture, for every sufficiently large squarefree composite
$n$,
\[
 \log R(n)\ge\log\log n-k\log2+O(1).
\]
The Hardy--Ramanujan theorem has an exceptional set of $o(x)$ integers, so on
all but $o(x)$ squarefree integers one has
$k=(1+o(1))\log\log n$. Consequently
\[
 \omega(n)\log R(n)
 \ge(1-\log2+o(1))(\log\log n)^2
\]
on this set. Squarefree integers have density $6/\pi^2$, and squarefree prime
indices have density zero. Moreover,
\[
 \frac{\log R(n)}{\omega(n)}\ge1-\log2+o(1)
\]
on the same set. This proves
\eqref{eq:blms-normal-window-obstruction}; summation over squarefree composite
indices proves \eqref{eq:blms-mean-window-obstruction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Purely conditional consequences of H1 and H2]
\label{cor:conditional-orders}
Assuming hypothesis \textup{(H1)} in Definition~\ref{hyp:normal-window},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-normal-order}
 L(n)\sim\frac{\log2}{4}(\log\log n)^2
 \quad\text{for almost all }n.
\end{equation}
Assuming hypothesis \textup{(H2)} in Definition~\ref{hyp:mean-window},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-mean-order}
 \sum_{3\le n\le x}L(n)
 \sim\frac{\log2}{4}x(\log\log x)^2.
\end{equation}
These are implications only: neither conclusion is unconditional, and no
positive arithmetic evidence for either sufficient hypothesis is presently
known. Proposition~\ref{prop:blms-obstructs-mean-window} shows that the
published Bugeaud--Luca--Mignotte--Siksek conjecture predicts that both
hypotheses fail.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For the first assertion, intersect the density-one set in
Definition~\ref{hyp:normal-window} with the Hardy--Ramanujan normal-order set.
Then the error $\omega(n)\log R(n)+O(\omega(n))$ in
\eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order} is $o(\omega(n)^2)$. For the second,
sum the upper bound in \eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order}, use
Definition~\ref{hyp:mean-window}, \eqref{eq:omega-second-moment}, and
$\sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)=O(x\log\log x)$, and compare with the unconditional
lower mean bound \eqref{eq:mean-logarithmic-growth}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact residual arithmetic interface]
\label{rem:exact-average-open-interface}
Supplementary Corollary~\ref{supp-cor:theta-refined-connected-entropy} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generator-growth} determine the contribution of the
multiplicity-free minimal-cover model used here: $\log2/4$ is its exact
quadratic coefficient, the fixed-choice rank-pure sector supplies its
theta-refined lower terms, and the private-coordinate upper bound retains the
term $\omega(n)\log R(n)$. Proposition~
\ref{prop:atomic-family-ladder-slots} gives the rank-window description for
every support size:
prime-atom families are finite disjoint unions of the rank
classes $\Pi_\alpha(d)$, while ladder families are singletons. Thus
Hypotheses \textup{(H1)} and \textup{(H2)} in Definitions~
\ref{hyp:normal-window} and \ref{hyp:mean-window} ask precisely for sufficient
upper distributional control of
exact-rank prime multiplicities in those rank windows.

The pointwise estimate \eqref{eq:pointwise-exact-rank-bound} proves only the
consequence obtained by combining the fibotomic size identity with the
divisibility of the exact-rank radical and the congruences
$p\equiv\pm1\pmod d$. Supplementary Remark~
\ref{supp-rem:cumulative-rank-estimates-insufficient} shows that even its
Euler-factor scale does not by itself imply either maximum-window hypothesis. The
manuscript does not prove that these inputs exhaust the information available
from fibotomic arithmetic, or that
\eqref{eq:fibotomic-rank-entropy} is optimal within any information class. Such
an optimality theorem would require, at minimum, a precise axiom class of
admissible information and a realizable extremal model satisfying those
axioms. Neither is supplied here. Whether stronger unconditional dispersion or
concentration consequences follow from fibotomic arithmetic is therefore an
open interface.

The closest distribution theorems do not close this interface.  In the
notation of a nondegenerate Lucas sequence $U$ with nonsquare discriminant
$\Delta$ and squarefree discriminant part $\Delta_0$, Sanna's Theorem~1.1
\cite{Sanna2022RankDivisibility} gives, unconditionally, an asymptotic formula
for
\[
 \#\{p\le x:d\mid\rho_U(p)\}
\]
when $d$ is odd, with $3\nmid d$ if $\Delta_0=-3$, and
$x>\exp(Be^{8\omega(d)}d^8)$.  The condition counted is
$d\mid\rho_U(p)$, not the exact-rank condition $\rho_U(p)=d$; for example,
$3\mid\alpha(17)=9$ although $\alpha(17)\ne3$.  Sanna's Theorem~1.1 on the
index of appearance \cite{Sanna2024IndexAppearance} instead assumes GRH and,
for a fixed positive integer $t$, counts primes having
$\iota_U(p)=t$ uniformly for $x>t^3$. Cera Da Concei\c c\~ao removes the
restrictions in the fixed rank-divisibility density problem and gives
explicit densities for all Lucas sequences and fixed $d$
\cite{CeraDaConceicao2026PrimeDensities}; the condition remains
$d\mid\rho_U(p)$ and still does not isolate a growing exact fiber. In the
Fibonacci case,
$\alpha(p)=d$ and $\iota_F(p)=t$ imply
\[
 p=td+\left(\frac5p\right)=td\pm1.
\]
Taking $x$ on the scale of this candidate prime restricts that theorem to
$t^2\ll d$, and its cumulative fixed-index count still does not isolate the
single rank $d$. Thus none of these results controls the growing exact fibers
$\Pi_\alpha(d)$ required in \eqref{eq:visible-single-rank-interface}.

The primitive-divisor theorems of Carmichael and Bilu--Hanrot--Voutier give
existence in the Fibonacci setting. Granville proves an odd-multiplicity
primitive-factor theorem for a class of second-order Lucas recurrences with
$c\equiv2\pmod4$ and explicitly leaves the odd-$b,c$ case, including the
Fibonacci recurrence $b=c=1$, outside that theorem
\cite{Granville2012PrimitivePrimeFactors}. Thus it is a neighboring
Lucas-sequence result, not a Fibonacci multiplicity input here. Kiss treats
related Lucas-sequence primitive-divisor questions
\cite{Kiss1988PrimitiveDivisors}. Hong proves that, for each fixed positive
integer $\kappa$ and all sufficiently large $n$ with an explicit threshold,
$F_n$ has a primitive divisor at least $(\kappa+1)n-1$
\cite{Hong2025BigPrimitiveDivisors}. These results concern existence, parity of
one primitive factor's multiplicity in the stated Lucas class, or the size of
one Fibonacci primitive divisor; they do not provide the required average
dispersion or critical concentration estimates for Fibonacci exact-rank prime
multiplicities in visible divisor windows. The
Hardy--Ramanujan and
Tur\'an theorems control the additive function $\omega(n)$ but not the prime
factorization of $F_d$ across exact apparition ranks. Likewise, the
Erd\H{o}s--Wintner theorem \cite{ErdosWintner1939Additive} concerns limiting
distributions of additive arithmetic functions, whereas $L(n)$ and $R(n)$
are not additive. No implication from RH or ERH to either rank-window
conjecture is presently known. Accordingly, the normal and mean equivalents
\eqref{eq:conditional-normal-order} and
\eqref{eq:conditional-mean-order} are conditional; the unconditional
statements are \eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order} and
\eqref{eq:mean-logarithmic-growth}, together with the stronger connected lower
bound in Supplementary Corollary~
\ref{supp-cor:theta-refined-connected-entropy}.
None of these estimates implies that the disconnected part of $\mathcal M_n$
is negligible; the strictly stronger blockwise hypothesis needed for that
conclusion is isolated in Supplementary Theorem~
\ref{supp-thm:blockwise-connected-dominance}.
\end{remark}
