Dear Editor,

We resubmit "Minimal preimages of the Fibonacci rank map: squarefree fibers
and weighted covers" to The Fibonacci Quarterly following a reject-and-resubmit
decision.

For each positive integer n, the article studies the exact fiber of the
Fibonacci rank-of-apparition map and its divisibility-minimal elements. Its
main contribution is an exact classification of those minimal elements by
irredundant witness covers, together with the canonical squarefree slice and
its exact weighted cover count. The article also settles two problems posed by
FitzGibbons, Javaheri, Miller, and Verga: whether every fixed-point-order class
of positive order contains infinitely many relatively prime integers, and
whether the basin of every fixed point x > 5 contains infinitely many
relatively prime integers. Both are answered in the stronger prime form as a
direct corollary of classical exact-rank prime existence; no new
prime-existence input is claimed.

The resubmission addresses the two structural grounds for the decision.
First, the affected upper-bound arguments are now independently checkable
within the article. It contains the numbered proposition "Classical
prime-power rank lifting," a numbered lemma establishing the prime-ladder
alternative, and the unique ladder-slot formulas needed by those arguments.
The relevant dependencies now point to numbered statements in the article. To
accommodate these proofs, the article has increased from 27 to 33 pages and
from 24 to 31 theorem, lemma, proposition, and corollary environments.

Second, the priority boundary has been corrected. The article now cites Carl
G. Wagner, "Minimal multiplicative covers of an integer," Discrete Mathematics
24 (1978), 87-94, and T. Hearne and C. Wagner, "Minimal covers of finite
sets," Discrete Mathematics 5 (1973), 247-251. A numbered comparison
proposition states that the prime-atom case is the unordered form of Wagner's
condition and identifies the additional structure as the lowering of a
prime-power rank label rather than deletion of that label. The classical
minimal-cover skeleton is not claimed as new.

The former H1 and H2 conjectural language has also been corrected. H1 and H2
are now called sufficient maximum-window hypotheses. Corollary 6.9 is stated
purely conditionally, records that no positive arithmetic evidence for either
hypothesis is presently known, and states that the published
Bugeaud-Luca-Mignotte-Siksek conjecture predicts that both hypotheses fail.

The submission package includes the 33-page article, the 36-page supplement,
and the verification scripts, tests, archived outputs, literature audit,
checksums, and reproduction instructions listed in the accompanying metadata.
We ask that the supplement and computational materials be made available to
the referee.
