\section{Four-coordinate witness kernels}\label{sec:four-coordinate-kernels}

The general enumeration of irredundant covers of finite sets is classical
\cite{HearneWagner1973MinimalCovers}.  Here the relevant objects carry the
additional Fibonacci essential/full-support decoration.  The classification
below concerns this decorated structure and its arithmetic realization; the
generic cover enumeration and Hall's theorem are used as inputs.

Throughout this section assume
\[
 \mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3,\ell_4\},\qquad
 a_i=\nu_{\ell_i}(n),\qquad V=[4].
\]
For nonempty $J\subseteq V$ and $i\in J$, write
\[
 P_J:=(J,J),\qquad L_{i\mid J}:=(\{i\},J),
 \qquad S_i:=(\{i\},\{i\}).
\]
Thus $P_J$ denotes a prime slot, $L_{i\mid J}$ a proper ladder slot when
$\#J>1$, and $S_i$ the undifferentiated singleton slot, which may be filled by
either a prime or a ladder jump.  Braces and commas in subscripts are
suppressed when no ambiguity can arise.

For arithmetic filling, attach to these symbols the already defined rank-jump
slot sets
\[
 \mathfrak C(P_J)=\{p\in\mathcal A_{J,J}(n):p\text{ is prime}\},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak C(L_{i\mid J})=
 \{p^e\in\mathcal A_{\{i\},J}(n):e>1\},
\]
and
\[
 \mathfrak C(S_i)=\mathcal A_{\{i\},\{i\}}(n).
\]
The exact-rank window, M\"obius count, and unique ladder-candidate formulas
for these sets are those of
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests}; its proof is
coordinatewise and the same formulas are read here with $[3]$ replaced by
$V$.  We use these previously established formulas without claiming a new
arithmetic result.

\begin{theorem}[Complete four-coordinate kernel classification]
\label{thm:four-coordinate-kernel-classification}
Let
\[
 \Delta=\{(I_1,J_1),\ldots,(I_r,J_r)\}
\]
be a collection of nonempty pairs satisfying
\[
 I_j\subseteq J_j\subseteq V,\qquad
 I_j=J_j\ \text{or}\ \#I_j=1,
\]
\[
 \bigcup_{j=1}^rJ_j=V,
 \qquad
 I_j\setminus\bigcup_{h\ne j}J_h\ne\varnothing
 \quad(1\le j\le r).
\]
Up to coordinate permutation and reordering of slots, $\Delta$ belongs to
exactly one of the twenty-four rows in
Tables~\ref{tab:four-coordinate-connected-kernels} and
\ref{tab:four-coordinate-disconnected-kernels}.  The first thirteen rows are
exactly those for which the hypergraph with edges $J_1,\ldots,J_r$ is
connected.  There are $243$ labelled kernels in total, of which $133$ have
connected full-support hypergraph and $110$ do not.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every slot choose a private coordinate
$x_j\in I_j\setminus\bigcup_{h\ne j}J_h$.  These coordinates are distinct,
so $r\le4$.  Suppose first that the $J$-hypergraph is connected.  If $r=1$,
coverage gives $J_1=V$, and the prime--ladder alternative gives $K_1$ or
$K_2$.  If $r=2$, set
\[
 A=J_1\setminus J_2,
 \quad B=J_1\cap J_2,
 \quad C=J_2\setminus J_1.
\]
Private coordinates and connectedness make $A,B,C$ nonempty, and they
partition $V$.  Up to interchanging the two slots, their cardinalities are
$(1,2,1)$ or $(1,1,2)$.  The former gives two three-element edges and the
three prime/ladder patterns $K_3$--$K_5$.  The latter gives edges of sizes two
and three; their sizes distinguish the slots, producing the four patterns
$K_6$--$K_9$.

If $r=3$, write the private coordinates as $x_1,x_2,x_3$ and the remaining
coordinate as $y$.  Private-coordinate exclusion gives
$J_j\subseteq\{x_j,y\}$.  Connectedness excludes $J_j=\{x_j\}$, hence the
full supports form the unique three-leaf star
$J_j=\{x_j,y\}$.  The number of prime slots is then the complete orbit
invariant, giving $K_{10}$--$K_{13}$.  Finally, if $r=4$, the private
coordinates exhaust $V$ and force $J_j=\{x_j\}$ for every $j$, which is
disconnected.  This proves the completeness of the first table.

For a disconnected $J$-hypergraph, its connected components partition $V$.
A one-coordinate component is $S_i$.  A two-coordinate component consists of
one edge and is $P_{ij}$ or $L_{i\mid ij}$.  By the complete
three-coordinate classification, the connected three-coordinate components
are precisely
\[
 P_{123},\quad L_{1\mid123},\quad P_{12}P_{23},\quad
 L_{1\mid12}P_{23},\quad L_{1\mid12}L_{3\mid23}.
\]
The component-size partitions $2+2$, $3+1$, $2+1+1$, and $1+1+1+1$
therefore contribute respectively $3,5,2,1$ orbits, exactly the rows
$D_1$--$D_{11}$.  Component sizes, component types, edge sizes, and the
prime/ladder status after those data are fixed show that no two listed rows
are equivalent.

For each row the labelled orbit size is
$24/\#\operatorname{Stab}_{S_4}(\Delta)$.  The displayed values sum to
\[
 133=1+4+6+12+6+12+12+24+24+4+12+12+4
\]
and
\[
 110=3+12+12+4+12+12+24+12+6+12+1,
\]
respectively.  The preceding exhaustive case split proves that these orbits
contain every labelled kernel, giving the total $243$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Strict separation of the two support notions]
\label{prop:four-coordinate-support-separation}
There exist four-coordinate minimal generators whose positive-support
hypergraph is connected although their full-support hypergraph is not.  More
precisely, for
\[
 n=3^2\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11=3465,
 \qquad
 m=17\cdot61\cdot421\cdot19801=8644661177,
\]
one has $m\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{conn}}$, while the hypergraph with edges
$T_n(\theta)$ for the four rank-jump factors of $m$ is disconnected.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The four primes have exact ranks
\[
 \alpha(17)=9,\qquad \alpha(61)=15,\qquad
 \alpha(421)=21,\qquad \alpha(19801)=33.
\]
Indeed,
\[
 F_9=2\cdot17,\quad F_{15}=2\cdot5\cdot61,\quad
 F_{21}=2\cdot13\cdot421,\quad
 F_{33}=2\cdot89\cdot19801,
\]
and none of the displayed primes divides the Fibonacci number at a proper
divisor of its displayed index.  With coordinates ordered as $(3,5,7,11)$,
their full supports are
\[
 \{1\},\quad\{2\},\quad\{3\},\quad\{4\}.
\]
They cover $V$ irredundantly.  Equivalently, the lcm of the four ranks is
$3465$, whereas deleting them in the displayed order gives the four lcms
\[
 1155,\qquad693,\qquad495,\qquad315,
\]
all strictly smaller than $n$.  The minimal-lcm criterion therefore gives
$m\in\mathcal M_n$.

On the other hand, the positive supports of the four ranks are
\[
 \{1\},\quad\{1,2\},\quad\{1,3\},\quad\{1,4\}.
\]
They form a connected star, so Definition~\ref{article-def:support-hypergraph}
gives $m\in\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{conn}}$.  The four singleton full supports
are disconnected, proving the strict separation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact arithmetic realization in four coordinates]
\label{thm:four-coordinate-arithmetic-realization}
Let $\mathcal O_4$ be the twenty-four orbit representatives in
Tables~\ref{tab:four-coordinate-connected-kernels} and
\ref{tab:four-coordinate-disconnected-kernels}.  Fix
$\rho\in\mathcal O_4$ and a labelled coordinate permutation of $\rho$, and
let $X_\rho$ be its slot set.  Replace each slot $x$ by its finite candidate
set $C_x=\mathfrak C(x)$ defined above.
Then the row has a coprime filling if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:four-hall}
 \#\bigcup_{x\in Y}\nu(C_x)\ge\#Y
 \qquad(Y\subseteq X_\rho),
\end{equation}
where $\nu(C_x)$ is the set of underlying primes of the prime powers in
$C_x$.  The set $\mathcal M_n$ consists exactly of the distinct products
obtained from all such fillings of all labelled rows.

For a candidate rank-jump factor $\theta$, put
\[
 K_n(\theta)=\{i:\nu_{\ell_i}(\alpha(\theta))>0\},
 \qquad
 C_{x,K}=\{\theta\in C_x:K_n(\theta)=K\}.
\]
Then $\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{conn}}$ consists exactly of the products obtained
from profiles $(K_x)_{x\in X_\rho}$ for which the hypergraph
$(V,\{K_x:x\in X_\rho\})$ is connected and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:four-profile-hall}
 \#\bigcup_{x\in Y}\nu(C_{x,K_x})\ge\#Y
 \qquad(Y\subseteq X_\rho).
\end{equation}
For the rows $K_1,\ldots,K_{13}$ the connectivity condition is automatic.
For $D_1,\ldots,D_5$ it is equivalent to intersection of the two positive
supports.  For $D_6,D_7,D_8$ it is equivalent to the singleton component's
positive support meeting the union of the other two.  For $D_9,D_{10}$ the
three positive supports must have connected intersection graph, and for
$D_{11}$ the four positive supports must form a connected hypergraph; in the
last case \eqref{eq:four-profile-hall} comprises all fifteen nonempty slot
subsets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{article-thm:witness-classification} identifies elements of
$\mathcal M_n$ with pairwise coprime jumpic witness covers.  The
prime--ladder alternative identifies the labelled essential/full-support
kernel of any such cover with one of the twenty-four rows in
Theorem~\ref{thm:four-coordinate-kernel-classification}, and the established
rank-jump slot formulas give every candidate for every slot.
For prime powers, pairwise coprimeness is exactly distinctness of underlying
primes.  Hall's theorem \cite[Chapter~2]{LovaszPlummer1986MatchingTheory}
therefore gives \eqref{eq:four-hall}.  Conversely, a Hall filling has the
coverage and private-coordinate properties encoded by its row, hence is a
witness cover and produces an element of $\mathcal M_n$.  Unique
factorization recovers its labelled rank-jump factor set; repetitions caused by
row stabilizers are removed by taking distinct products.

The definition of $\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{conn}}$ uses the positive supports
$K_n(\theta)$, not the full supports $T_n(\theta)$.  After fixing a positive
support profile, the only remaining compatibility condition is again
distinctness of underlying primes, so Hall's theorem gives
\eqref{eq:four-profile-hall}.  Taking the union over precisely the connected
profiles proves the asserted formula.  Since $T_n(\theta)\subseteq
K_n(\theta)$, every profile over a $K$-row is connected.  The stated tests for
the $D$-rows follow directly from their component structures in the second
table.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:four-coordinate-minimal-classification}
The one-, two-, and three-coordinate classifications together with
Theorem~\ref{thm:four-coordinate-arithmetic-realization} give a finite exact
classification of $\mathcal M_n$ and $\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{conn}}$ whenever
$\omega(n)\le4$.  Combined with
Theorem~\ref{thm:mingenerator-connected-factorization}, the same data give the
existing partition normal form for every four-coordinate minimal generator.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Only the case $\omega(n)=4$ is new.  Theorem
\ref{thm:four-coordinate-arithmetic-realization} classifies that case
directly.  The connected-factorization theorem supplies the equivalent
normal form obtained by multiplying the connected factors associated with a
set partition of $\mathcal P(n)$.
\end{proof}
