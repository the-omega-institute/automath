\begin{abstract}
For each positive integer $n$, let $B_n$ denote the set of divisors of the
Fibonacci number $F_n$ whose rank of apparition equals~$n$. We prove that
$B_n$ is an upper set in the divisor lattice of $F_n$ obtained by removing
the covering ideals associated with the maximal proper divisors of~$n$. Our
main result classifies the minimal generators of~$B_n$: every minimal
generator factors uniquely as a pairwise coprime product of atomic
prime-power birth moduli forming a witness cover whose support spans the prime
coordinates of~$n$. This yields the bound $\omega(m)\le\omega(n)$ for every
minimal generator $m$ and a primitive-versus-ladder dichotomy for atomic
factors. We give complete classifications for $\omega(n)\le 3$ and show that
both $B_n$ and its minimal-generator set admit unique factorizations into
connected coordinate blocks, producing partition-lattice formulas for $A(n) =
\#B_n$ and $\#\mathcal{M}_n$. As consequences, nonexceptional birth layers
contain canonical principal intervals, Boolean subposets of rank $\tau(n) -
O(1)$, and satisfy Bell-type lower bounds on odd layers. Numerical data for
$n\le 30$ illustrate these structures.
\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Fibonacci numbers, rank of apparition, divisor

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% ====================================================================

For an integer $q\ge 2$, the \emph{rank of apparition} (or \emph{order of
appearance}) $\alpha(q)$ is the least positive index $n$ such that $q\mid
F_n$, where $(F_n)_{n\ge 0}$ denotes the Fibonacci sequence with $F_0=0$,
$F_1=1$. For each $n\ge 2$, write
\[
D_n:=\operatorname{Div}(F_n),
\qquad
B_n:=\{q\in D_n:\ \alpha(q)=n\},
\qquad
A(n):=\#B_n.
\]
Thus $B_n$ consists of those moduli that first divide a Fibonacci number at
index~$n$. Since $F_2=1$, the boundary layer is empty: throughout the paper
we use the convention $B_2=\mathcal M_2=\varnothing$ and $A(2)=0$.
The counting function $A(n)$ is subsumed by the
$\alpha$-contraction formalism of Stroi\'nski \cite{Stroinski2016AlphaContraction};
see also the classical M\"obius inversions discussed in
\cite{Halton1966DivisibilityFibonacci,FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions}.
In this paper we go beyond enumeration and study $B_n$ as an
ordered subset of the divisor lattice $(D_n,\mid)$, asking how it sits inside
$D_n$, how it is generated, and what combinatorial structure it carries.

Three classical facts underlie the analysis. First, $q\mid F_m$ if and only if
$\alpha(q)\mid m$
\cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm,Vinson1963RankApparition}. Second, the Fibonacci
sequence satisfies strong divisibility: $\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}$.
Third, ranks of apparition respect least common multiples:
$\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))=\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))$. These properties, combined
with the primitive divisor theorem for Fibonacci numbers
\cite{Carmichael1913PrimitiveDivisors,BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas},
are the sole inputs for the main results.

This point of view complements several nearby strands in the literature.
Luca and Pomerance \cite{LucaPomerance2014LocalBehavior} study the local
behavior of $\alpha$ under additive shifts; Trojovsk\'a
\cite{Trojovska2020PeriodicPoints} and FitzGibbons--Javaheri--Miller--Verga
\cite{FitzGibbonsJavaheriMillerVerga2025Dynamics} study periodic points and
iteration dynamics of $\alpha$; Cameron--Counts--Lund--Miller--Piechnik--Wong
\cite{CameronCountsLundMillerPiechnikWong2022Fibotomic} study fibotomic
polynomials and related algebraic factorizations; and Sanna
\cite{Sanna2016LucasSequences} and Marques \cite{Marques2012OrderAppearance}
study the growth and fixed-point behavior of the order-of-appearance map. The
present paper instead fixes a single fiber $B_n$ and studies its divisibility
geometry inside $D_n$. We are not aware of earlier work that formulates these
fibers as upper sets in divisor lattices or that gives the two-support and
three-support classifications proved here.

Our first main result identifies $B_n$ as an upper fiber obtained by sieve.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-upper-fiber}
For every $n\ge2$,
\[
B_n=D_n\setminus\bigcup_{p\mid n}D_{n/p}.
\]
In particular $B_n$ is an upper set in the divisor lattice $D_n$, and
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal M_n}\{q\in D_n:\ m\mid q\}.
\]
This is proved as Theorem~\ref{thm:upper-fiber}.
\end{theorem}

Call a prime power $\theta=p^e$ \emph{atomic for the index $d$} if
$\alpha(\theta)=d$ and $\alpha(p^{e-1})<d$. An \emph{$n$-witness cover} is a
pairwise coprime set of such atomic prime powers whose apparition indices
collectively span the prime factorization of $n$ in a minimal, irredundant
fashion (see Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover} for the precise formulation).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-witness}
For every $n\ge2$, multiplication gives a bijection from $n$-witness covers
$\Theta$ to minimal generators:
\[
\Theta\longmapsto \prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta,
\qquad
\mathcal M_n=
\bigl\{\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta:\Theta\text{ is an $n$-witness cover}
\bigr\}.
\]
Consequently every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ satisfies the sharp support bound
$\omega(m)\le\omega(n)$. The precise cover conditions are given in
Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}, and the result is
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} together with
Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound}.
\end{theorem}

Making this classification effective requires the prime-power apparition
formulas of Fulton--Morris \cite{FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions} and
Lengyel \cite{Lengyel1995OrderFibonacciLucas}. These show that every atomic
factor is either \emph{primitive} (arising from a new prime) or lies on a
\emph{prime-power ladder} (changing exactly one prime coordinate of the
apparition index). This dichotomy
(Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}) enables complete
classifications of $\mathcal M_n$ when $\omega(n)\le 3$, including a sharp
nonemptiness criterion for the three-coordinate families
(Corollary~\ref{cor:two-support-complete-classification} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}).

The same lcm mechanism gives a local-to-global factorization over connected
coordinate blocks.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-connected}
Let $q\in B_n$ and form the support hypergraph on $\mathcal P(n)$ whose
edges are the prime-coordinate supports of the ranks of the prime-power
factors of $q$. If its connected components are $C$, then
\[
q=\prod_C q_C,\qquad q_C\in B_{n_C},
\qquad n_C=\prod_{\ell\in C}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n)} ,
\]
and this factorization is unique. The same statement holds for minimal
generators after replacing $B_{n_C}$ by $\mathcal M_{n_C}$. Consequently
$A(n)$ and $\#\mathcal M_n$ are partition sums over the connected counts.
This is proved in Section~\ref{sec:connected}.
\end{theorem}

As secondary consequences, nonexceptional birth layers contain canonical
principal intervals
(Corollary~\ref{thm:canonical-principal-interval}), Boolean subposets of rank
$\tau(n)-O(1)$ (Corollary~\ref{thm:boolean-skeleton}), and---for odd
$n$---Bell-type lower bounds on $\#\mathcal M_n$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:odd-primitive-cover-realization}).

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:prelim} collects the
background on Fibonacci divisibility and the prime-power apparition formulas.
Section~\ref{sec:upper-fibers} develops the upper-fiber and witness-cover
theory and proves Theorems~\ref{thm:intro-upper-fiber}
and~\ref{thm:intro-witness}. Section~\ref{sec:low-support} treats the
primitive--ladder dichotomy and the low-support classifications.
Section~\ref{sec:connected} records the connected-block factorization
(Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-connected}) and the partition-lattice formulas.
Section~\ref{sec:numerical} presents numerical data, and
Section~\ref{sec:questions} records further questions.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Rank of apparition and Fibonacci divisibility}\label{subsec:fib-div}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rank-basics}
Let $q,u,v\ge2$ and let $a,b,m\ge1$. Then
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $q\mid F_m$ if and only if $\alpha(q)\mid m$.
\item $\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}$.
\item $\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))=\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The strong-divisibility identity in (ii) is classical for the Fibonacci
sequence; see, for example,
\cite{Halton1966DivisibilityFibonacci,Wall1960FibonacciModm}. If
$q\mid F_m$, then $q$ divides both $F_m$ and $F_{\alpha(q)}$, hence divides
$F_{\gcd(m,\alpha(q))}$ by (ii). The minimality of $\alpha(q)$ forces
$\alpha(q)\le \gcd(m,\alpha(q))$, so $\alpha(q)\mid m$. The converse follows
from the divisibility relation $F_r\mid F_s$ whenever $r\mid s$, proving (i).

For (iii), an integer $k$ is divisible by
$\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))$ if and only if $\lcmop(u,v)\mid F_k$. This is equivalent
to $u\mid F_k$ and $v\mid F_k$, which by (i) is equivalent to
$\alpha(u)\mid k$ and $\alpha(v)\mid k$. Thus the positive multiples of
$\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))$ are exactly the positive multiples of
$\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))$, and the two numbers are equal.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Primitive divisors}\label{subsec:primitive}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:primitive-divisors}
For every $n\ge 3$ with $n\notin\{6,12\}$, there exists a prime $p_n$ with
$\alpha(p_n)=n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $n=3,4,5$ one takes $p_3=2$, $p_4=3$, $p_5=5$. For $n\ge 7$, $n\neq 12$,
the primitive divisor theorem
\cite{Carmichael1913PrimitiveDivisors,Yabuta2001PrimitiveDivisorFibonacci,%
BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas}
gives a prime dividing $F_n$ but no earlier Fibonacci number. Since
$\alpha(p_n)\mid n$ and $p_n$ divides no $F_d$ with $d<n$, necessarily
$\alpha(p_n)=n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Counting corollaries}\label{subsec:counting}

The standard M\"obius-inversion formulas for first-appearance counts follow
from Stroi\'nski's $\alpha$-contraction identity
\cite{Stroinski2016AlphaContraction}: for any $f:\NN\to\mathbb{C}$ with
$f(1)=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stroinski}
\sum_{d\mid F_n}f(d)=\sum_{d\mid n}f_\alpha(d),
\qquad
f_\alpha(d):=\sum_{\alpha(m)=d}f(m).
\end{equation}
Taking $f$ to be the indicator of integers at least $2$, of primes, or of
prime powers, and applying M\"obius inversion, one obtains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mobius-birth}
A(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\,\tau(F_d),
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{pr}}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\,\omega(F_d).
\end{equation}
We regard these as known corollaries; the results of
Sections~\ref{sec:upper-fibers}--\ref{sec:connected} are independent of them.

\subsection{Prime-power apparition formulas}\label{subsec:prime-power}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:prime-power-ladders}
Let $p$ be prime, and put $z_p:=\alpha(p)$. For $p\ne2,5$, let
$h_p:=\nu_p(F_{z_p})$. Then the ranks of apparition of prime powers are as
follows:
\[
\alpha(2)=3,\qquad \alpha(4)=6,\qquad
\alpha(2^e)=3\cdot2^{e-2}\quad(e\ge3),
\]
\[
\alpha(5^e)=5^e\quad(e\ge1),
\]
and, for $p\ne2,5$,
\[
\alpha(p^e)=p^{\max\{e-h_p,0\}}\,z_p\quad(e\ge1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
These are the standard prime-power rank formulas for the Fibonacci sequence;
we use them in the form given by Fulton--Morris
\cite{FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions}, Lengyel
\cite{Lengyel1995OrderFibonacciLucas}, and Jarden
\cite[Ch.~III]{Jarden1966RecurringSequences}. The displayed exceptional
2-adic formula follows from the exact valuations
$\nu_2(F_m)=0$ for $m\not\equiv0\pmod3$, $\nu_2(F_m)=1$ for
$m\equiv3\pmod6$, and $\nu_2(F_m)=\nu_2(m)+2$ for
$m\equiv0\pmod6$. The 5-adic identity follows from
$\nu_5(F_m)=\nu_5(m)$ for $5\mid m$. For $p\ne2,5$, the lifting formula says
that once $p$ first appears at $z_p$, each further power of $p$ beyond
$h_p=\nu_p(F_{z_p})$ multiplies the rank by one further factor of $p$, while
the first $h_p$ powers have the same rank $z_p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:atomic-families}
For a prime power $\theta=p^e$ with $e\ge1$, put
$d(\theta):=\alpha(\theta)$ and, for convenience, set
$d^-(\theta):=\alpha(p^{e-1})$, with $\alpha(1)=1$. For $d\ge1$ define
\[
\Atom(d):=\{p^e:\ \alpha(p^e)=d,\ \alpha(p^{e-1})<d\}.
\]
Thus $\Atom(d)$ consists of the prime-power factors that are born exactly at
rank $d$.

If $d\mid n$ and $\theta\in\Atom(d)$, define the full-coordinate support and
essential-coordinate support of $\theta$ relative to $n$ by
\[
T_n(\theta):=\{\ell\in\mathcal P(n):\nu_\ell(d(\theta))=\nu_\ell(n)\},
\]
and
\[
E_n(\theta):=\{\ell\in T_n(\theta):
\nu_\ell(d^-(\theta))<\nu_\ell(n)\}.
\]
Equivalently, $T_n(\theta)$ records the prime coordinates of $n$ supplied at
full exponent by $\theta$, while $E_n(\theta)$ records those full coordinates
lost when $\theta=p^e$ is lowered to $p^{e-1}$. An atom is \emph{primitive} if
$e=1$, and is a \emph{ladder atom} if $e>1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:atomic-classification}
The atomic prime powers are exactly the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $2\in\Atom(3)$, $4\in\Atom(6)$, and
$2^e\in\Atom(3\cdot2^{e-2})$ for every $e\ge4$; the power $8$ is not atomic.
\item $5^e\in\Atom(5^e)$ for every $e\ge1$.
\item If $p\ne2,5$, $z_p=\alpha(p)$, and $h_p=\nu_p(F_{z_p})$, then
$p\in\Atom(z_p)$ and $p^e\in\Atom(p^{e-h_p}z_p)$ for every $e>h_p$; no
power $p^e$ with $1<e\le h_p$ is atomic.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently every atom is either a primitive atom $p\in\Atom(\alpha(p))$ or a
ladder atom $p^e$ with $e>1$, and every ladder atom changes only the
$p$-coordinate of its apparition index when it is lowered by one power of $p$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each assertion is obtained by comparing the value of $\alpha(p^e)$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders} with the value of
$\alpha(p^{e-1})$. For $p=2$, the ranks are
$1,3,6,6,12,24,\dots$ at exponents $0,1,2,3,4,5,\dots$, so exactly
$e=1,2$ and $e\ge4$ give a strict increase. For $p=5$, the rank changes from
$5^{e-1}$ to $5^e$ at every step. For $p\ne2,5$, the ranks are constant and
equal to $z_p$ through exponent $h_p$, and then are multiplied by $p$ at each
subsequent step. This gives precisely the stated atomic powers.

The final assertion follows from the same list. Primitive atoms are exactly
the first powers. In every ladder case the quotient
$d(\theta)/d^-(\theta)$ is a positive power of the same prime $p$ occurring in
the prime-power base, so lowering $\theta$ can remove full support only in the
$p$-coordinate of the rank.
\end{proof}

\section{Birth layers and witness covers}\label{sec:upper-fibers}
%% ====================================================================

\begin{definition}\label{def:birth-layer}
For $n\ge2$ let
\[
D_n:=\operatorname{Div}(F_n),\qquad
B_n:=\{q\in D_n:\alpha(q)=n\},\qquad
A(n):=\#B_n.
\]
The set $B_n$ is partially ordered by divisibility. Its set of minimal
elements is
\[
\mathcal M_n:=\{m\in B_n:\text{ no }q\in B_n\text{ satisfies }q\mid m,\ q<m\},
\]
and $\widthop(B_n)$ denotes the maximum size of an antichain in this poset.
We write $\mathcal P(n)$ for the set of prime divisors of $n$. Because
$F_2=1$, we use the boundary convention
$B_2=\mathcal M_2=\varnothing$ and $A(2)=\widthop(B_2)=0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:upper-fiber}
For every $n\ge 2$,
\[
B_n=D_n\setminus\bigcup_{p\mid n}D_{n/p}.
\]
Moreover $B_n$ is an upper set in $D_n$, and
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal M_n}\{q\in D_n:\ m\mid q\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $q\in B_n$, then $\alpha(q)=n$, so $q\nmid F_{n/p}$ for every prime
$p\mid n$; otherwise $n=\alpha(q)\mid n/p<n$, a contradiction. Conversely, if
$q\in D_n\setminus\bigcup D_{n/p}$, then $d:=\alpha(q)\mid n$. Were $d<n$,
some prime $p$ would divide $n/d$, giving $d\mid n/p$ and $q\mid F_{n/p}$, a
contradiction. Hence $\alpha(q)=n$.

If $q\in B_n$, $q\mid r$, and $r\in D_n$, then $n=\alpha(q)\mid\alpha(r)$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}, while $\alpha(r)\mid n$ because
$r\mid F_n$. Thus $\alpha(r)=n$, proving that $B_n$ is an upper set. Since
$D_n$ is finite, every element of $B_n$ lies above a minimal element of
$B_n$, which gives the displayed decomposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:minimal-generators}
Let $m=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{e_i}$ be a divisor of $F_n$, with distinct primes
$p_i$ and $e_i\ge1$. Put
\[
\lambda_i:=\alpha(p_i^{e_i}),\qquad
\lambda_i^-:=\alpha(p_i^{e_i-1})
\]
where $\alpha(1)=1$. Then $m\in\mathcal M_n$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-criterion}
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_r)=n
\quad\text{and}\quad
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_i^-,
\lambda_{i+1},\dots,\lambda_r)<n
\quad(1\le i\le r).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By repeated use of Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics},
\[
\alpha(m)=\lcmop(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_r).
\]
Thus the first condition in \eqref{eq:lcm-criterion} is equivalent to
$m\in B_n$. For a fixed $i$, the integer $m/p_i$ has the same prime-power
factorization as $m$ except that $p_i^{e_i}$ is replaced by $p_i^{e_i-1}$, so
\[
\alpha(m/p_i)=
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_i^-,
\lambda_{i+1},\dots,\lambda_r).
\]
If all these ranks are $<n$, then lowering any one prime factor removes the
integer from $B_n$. If a proper divisor $s<m$ lay in $B_n$, choose an index
$i$ for which the exponent of $p_i$ in $s$ is smaller than $e_i$. Then
$s\mid m/p_i\mid F_n$, and the upper-set property in
Theorem~\ref{thm:upper-fiber} would force $m/p_i\in B_n$, contradicting the
displayed inequality. Thus no proper divisor of $m$ lies in $B_n$, and $m$ is
minimal.
Conversely, if $m\in\mathcal M_n$, then $m\in B_n$, so the first equality
holds; and if any displayed lowered lcm were still $n$, then $m/p_i\in B_n$
would be a proper divisor of $m$, contradicting minimality.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Witness covers}

\begin{definition}\label{def:witness-cover}
An \emph{$n$-witness cover} is a finite set
$\Theta=\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\}$ of atomic prime powers satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $d(\theta_i)\mid n$ for every $i$;
\item the elements of $\Theta$ are pairwise coprime;
\item the full-coordinate supports cover all prime coordinates of $n$,
\[
\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta}T_n(\theta)=\mathcal P(n);
\]
\item each atom has an essential coordinate not supplied by the other atoms,
\[
E_n(\theta)\nsubseteq\bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta,\ \eta\ne\theta}T_n(\eta)
\qquad(\theta\in\Theta).
\]
\end{enumerate}
The last condition is the irredundance condition; it is deliberately stated
with $E_n$ rather than $T_n$ because witness covers may have overlapping
$T$-sets.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:witness-classification}
For every $n\ge 2$,
$\mathcal M_n=\bigl\{\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta:\
\Theta\text{ is an $n$-witness cover}\bigr\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$, $m=\prod_{i=1}^r\theta_i$ ($\theta_i=p_i^{e_i}$).
Each $\theta_i$ is atomic: if $\alpha(p_i^{e_i-1})=\alpha(p_i^{e_i})$, then
replacing $\theta_i$ by $p_i^{e_i-1}$ preserves the lcm, contradicting
the minimal-generator criterion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators}. Since
$n=\lcmop(d(\theta_i))$, the sets $T_n(\theta_i)$ cover $\mathcal P(n)$. The
condition $\alpha(m/p_i)<n$ forces
$E_n(\theta_i)\nsubseteq\bigcup_{j\neq i}T_n(\theta_j)$; otherwise the lost
coordinates would be supplied by other factors, keeping the lcm equal to $n$.
Thus $\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\}$ is an $n$-witness cover.

Conversely, let $\Theta$ be an $n$-witness cover and
$m=\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta$. By
the lcm property recorded in Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics},
$\alpha(m)=\lcmop(d(\theta):\theta\in\Theta)=n$. For each
$\theta=p^e\in\Theta$, the witness condition
provides $\ell\in E_n(\theta)\setminus\bigcup_{\eta\neq\theta}T_n(\eta)$; at
this coordinate, $\alpha(m/p)<n$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators} then gives $m\in\mathcal M_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:support-bound}
Every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ satisfies $\omega(m)\le\omega(n)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The witness condition provides an injection
$\theta\mapsto\ell(\theta)$ from $\Theta$ into $\mathcal P(n)$: choose
$\ell(\theta)\in E_n(\theta)\setminus\bigcup_{\eta\neq\theta}T_n(\eta)$.
Hence $\omega(m)=\#\Theta\le\omega(n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:n20}
For $n=20$, $F_{20}=6765=3\cdot 5\cdot 11\cdot 41$. The relevant atomic
factors are $3\in\Atom(4)$, $5\in\Atom(5)$, $11\in\Atom(10)$,
$41\in\Atom(20)$. The witness covers are $\{41\}$, $\{3,5\}$, $\{3,11\}$,
yielding $\mathcal M_{20}=\{15,33,41\}$ and $A(20)=11$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}\label{ex:n30}
For $n=30$, $F_{30}=832040=2^3\cdot 5\cdot 11\cdot 31\cdot 61$. The witness
covers are $\{31\}$, $\{4,5\}$, $\{2,11\}$, $\{4,61\}$, $\{11,61\}$, giving
$\mathcal M_{30}=\{20,22,31,244,671\}$ and $A(30)=52$.
With coordinates $(2,3,5)$ for $30=2\cdot3\cdot5$, these covers realize

\subsection*{Substructures of nonexceptional birth layers}

\begin{corollary}\label{thm:canonical-principal-interval}
Let $n\ge3$ and $n\notin\{6,12\}$. Choose a primitive prime
$\pi_n$ with $\alpha(\pi_n)=n$. Then the principal interval
\[
[\pi_n,F_n]:=\{q\in D_n:\ \pi_n\mid q\}
\]
is contained in $B_n$. Consequently $B_n$ contains a canonical copy of the
divisor lattice of $F_n/\pi_n$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The primitive prime $\pi_n$ exists by Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors}.
If $q\in D_n$ and $\pi_n\mid q$, then
$n=\alpha(\pi_n)\mid\alpha(q)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}. Since
$q\mid F_n$, also $\alpha(q)\mid n$. Hence $\alpha(q)=n$ and $q\in B_n$.
Dividing by $\pi_n$ identifies the interval $[\pi_n,F_n]$ with
$\operatorname{Div}(F_n/\pi_n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{thm:boolean-skeleton}
Let $n\ge3$ and $n\notin\{6,12\}$. Put
\[
R_n:=\{d:\ d\mid n,\ 3\le d<n,\ d\notin\{6,12\}\}.
\]
Then $B_n$ contains a Boolean subposet of rank $\#R_n=\tau(n)-O(1)$. In
particular,
\[
\widthop(B_n)\ge \binom{\#R_n}{\lfloor \#R_n/2\rfloor}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose a primitive prime $\pi_n$ with $\alpha(\pi_n)=n$, and for each
$d\in R_n$ choose a primitive prime $\pi_d$ with $\alpha(\pi_d)=d$. These
primes are distinct because their ranks of apparition are distinct, and
$\pi_d\mid F_d\mid F_n$. The map
\[
S\subseteq R_n\longmapsto \pi_n\prod_{d\in S}\pi_d
\]
embeds the Boolean lattice $2^{R_n}$ into the principal interval
$[\pi_n,F_n]\subseteq B_n$ from
Corollary~\ref{thm:canonical-principal-interval}. Sperner's theorem
\cite{Sperner1928Subsets} gives an antichain of size
$\binom{\#R_n}{\lfloor \#R_n/2\rfloor}$ in this Boolean subposet, hence the
same lower bound for $\widthop(B_n)$. Since $R_n$ omits from the divisor set
of $n$ only $1$, possibly $2$, $n$, and possibly $6$ and $12$, its size is
$\tau(n)-O(1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:maximal-order}
For infinitely many nonexceptional $n$,
\[
\widthop(B_n)\ge
\exp\!\left((\log 2+o(1))\,\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $n$ through any sequence for which the divisor function has maximal order
in Wigert's theorem \cite{Wigert1907Divisors}, omitting the finitely many
exceptional values $6$ and $12$. Then
$\tau(n)=\exp((\log2+o(1))\log n/\log\log n)$. By
Corollary~\ref{thm:boolean-skeleton}, $B_n$ contains a Boolean subposet of
rank $\tau(n)-O(1)$, and hence contains an antichain of size at least
\[
\binom{\tau(n)-O(1)}{\lfloor(\tau(n)-O(1))/2\rfloor}.
\]
This is stronger than the stated lower bound along the chosen sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}
Let $\theta\in\Atom(d)$ and assume $d\mid n$. Then exactly one of the
following holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\theta$ is primitive, and $E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$.
\item $\theta$ is a ladder atom. In this case $E_n(\theta)$ is either empty
or a singleton. When it is nonempty, its unique element is the single
coordinate whose exponent is changed by lowering $\theta$ to $\theta/p$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular no Fibonacci atom has $1<\#E_n(\theta)<\#T_n(\theta)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $\theta=p$ is primitive, then $d^-(\theta)=\alpha(1)=1$, so every full
coordinate in $T_n(\theta)$ is lost on lowering and
$E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$.

Now suppose $e>1$. The classification in
Corollary~\ref{cor:atomic-classification} shows that the only strict change
from $d^-(\theta)$ to $d(\theta)$ is multiplication by one prime coordinate:
by $2$ for the atomic powers of $2$ beyond the first, by $5$ for powers of
$5$, and by $p$ for the post-$h_p$ ladder powers of an odd prime
$p\ne5$. Thus lowering a ladder atom can remove full support at only one
coordinate. In the ordinary ladder cases that coordinate is the base prime
$p$. The exceptional atom $4$ has $d(4)=6$ and $d^-(4)=3$, so relative to any
multiple $n$ of $6$ it removes only the $2$-coordinate. Hence
$E_n(\theta)$ is empty if that changed coordinate is not full in $n$, and is
that singleton otherwise.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:extremal-coordinate-separation}
Let $m=\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta\in\mathcal M_n$ be represented by its
witness cover. If $\#\Theta=\omega(n)$, then the witness coordinates are
forced to be disjoint: each atom $\theta\in\Theta$ has a unique coordinate
$\ell(\theta)\in E_n(\theta)$ not lying in any other $T_n(\eta)$, and
$\theta\mapsto\ell(\theta)$ is a bijection from $\Theta$ to $\mathcal P(n)$.
Consequently, when $\omega(n)=2$, every non-atomic minimal generator is a
product of one atom whose full support is the first coordinate and one atom
whose full support is the second coordinate, after relabeling.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $\theta\in\Theta$, the witness-cover condition gives at least one
coordinate
\[
\ell(\theta)\in
E_n(\theta)\setminus\bigcup_{\eta\ne\theta}T_n(\eta).
\]
No two atoms can choose the same coordinate, because
$\ell(\theta)\in T_n(\theta)$ while the displayed condition says that this
coordinate lies in no other $T_n(\eta)$. Hence
$\theta\mapsto\ell(\theta)$ is an injection into $\mathcal P(n)$. If
$\#\Theta=\omega(n)$, it is a bijection.

For $\omega(n)=2$, a non-atomic minimal generator has two factors by the
support bound and the assumption that it is not a single atom. The preceding
paragraph gives one private coordinate for each factor. If one factor had both
coordinates in its full support, then the other factor's private coordinate
would lie in the first factor's $T$-set, impossible. Thus each factor has
exactly one full-support coordinate, as claimed.
\end{proof}

\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Three-coordinate nonemptiness criterion]
\label{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$ and identify subsets of
$[3]$ with the corresponding coordinate sets. For nonempty
$I\subseteq J\subseteq[3]$, put
\[
\mathcal A_{I,J}(n):=
\{\theta\in\bigcup_{d\mid n}\Atom(d):\ E_n(\theta)=I,\ T_n(\theta)=J\}.
\]
Let $\Delta=\{(I_1,J_1),\dots,(I_r,J_r)\}$ be one of the labelled admissible
types among
$\Gamma_1,\Gamma_3,\Gamma_4,\Gamma_5,\Gamma_6,\Gamma_7,\Gamma_8,\Gamma_9$.
Then the labelled type $\Delta$ is realized by an element of
$\mathcal M_n$ if and only if there are pairwise coprime atomic moduli
$\theta_j\in\mathcal A_{I_j,J_j}(n)$ for $1\le j\le r$. In that case
$m=\theta_1\cdots\theta_r$ is the realizing minimal generator, and every
realizing minimal generator is obtained uniquely in this way, up to the order
of factors with identical labelled data.

Equivalently, an unlabelled type $\Gamma_i$ is realized if and only if the
preceding pairwise-coprime selection condition holds after some permutation
of the three coordinates. The excluded type $\Gamma_2$ is never realized.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $m\in\mathcal M_n$ realizes the labelled type
$\Delta$. Write its witness cover as
$\Theta=\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\}$ with the factors ordered so that
$(E_n(\theta_j),T_n(\theta_j))=(I_j,J_j)$. By definition
$\theta_j\in\mathcal A_{I_j,J_j}(n)$, and pairwise coprimeness is part of
Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}.

Conversely, assume that pairwise coprime
$\theta_j\in\mathcal A_{I_j,J_j}(n)$ are given. Because $\Delta$ is an
abstract witness type, the sets $J_j$ cover $[3]$, and for every $j$ one has
$I_j\nsubseteq\bigcup_{h\ne j}J_h$. Translating back to
$T_n$ and $E_n$, the set $\Theta=\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\}$ satisfies the
coverage and irredundance conditions in Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover};
the assumed coprimeness gives the remaining condition. Hence $\Theta$ is an
$n$-witness cover, so Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} gives
$m=\prod_j\theta_j\in\mathcal M_n$ with type $\Delta$.

The factorization of $m$ into prime powers is unique, so any realization
recovers the same atomic factors and hence the same tuple, except for
permuting factors that have identical labelled data. The final assertion
combines this labelled criterion with the coordinate relabeling in
Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-three-types} and the Fibonacci exclusion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:three-coordinate-realization}
If $\omega(n)=3$, every element of $\mathcal M_n$ arises from exactly one
nonempty pairwise-coprime selection in the families
$\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)$ belonging to one of the eight admissible types in
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} together with
Theorems~\ref{thm:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types} and
\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Primitive three-coordinate skeleton]
\label{cor:primitive-three-skeleton}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$ and that
$n_S:=\prod_{i\in S}\ell_i^{\nu_{\ell_i}(n)}$ satisfies
$n_S\ge 3$ and $n_S\notin\{6,12\}$ for every nonempty
$S\subseteq[3]$. Then the four primitive-only three-coordinate
types $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_4$, $\Gamma_7$, and $\Gamma_9$ are realized in
$\mathcal M_n$. More precisely, after choosing primitive primes
$\pi_S$ with $\alpha(\pi_S)=n_S$, the following minimal generators realize
these types:
\[
\pi_{123},\qquad
\pi_{12}\pi_{23},\qquad
\pi_1\pi_{23},\qquad
\pi_1\pi_2\pi_3 .
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The hypothesis and Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors} provide the indicated
primitive primes. A primitive prime for $n_S$ is a primitive atom with
$E_n(\pi_S)=T_n(\pi_S)=S$. The four displayed collections of coordinate
sets are precisely the primitive-only types $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_4$,
$\Gamma_7$, and $\Gamma_9$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-three-types}, and
the chosen primes are pairwise distinct. The
pairwise-coprime selection criterion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} therefore gives the four
minimal generators.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:wss}
The Wall--Sun--Sun condition enters through
the prime-power formula in Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders}: for
$p\neq 2,5$, one has
$\alpha(p^2)=\alpha(p)$ if and only if $\nu_p(F_{\alpha(p)})\ge 2$, which is
precisely the definition of a Wall--Sun--Sun prime
\cite{SunSun1992FibonacciFLT}. No such primes are currently known, and their
nonexistence would imply that every odd prime contributes an immediate ladder
step above the first atomic level.
\end{remark}

\section{Further questions}\label{sec:questions}
%% ====================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Use the three-coordinate nonemptiness criterion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} to study the distribution of
the admissible types, then resolve the connected cores when $\omega(n)=4$. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:four-support-reduction}, only the connected part is
genuinely new.
\item Obtain arithmetic or asymptotic control of the connected counts
$A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n)$ and $M^{\mathrm{conn}}(n)$. The partition-lattice
formulas reduce global invariants to these kernels but do not yet estimate
their frequency or size.
\item Extend the witness-cover formalism to Lucas sequences and, more
generally, to strong divisibility sequences equipped with a
rank-of-apparition map and a primitive-divisor theorem.
\end{enumerate}
