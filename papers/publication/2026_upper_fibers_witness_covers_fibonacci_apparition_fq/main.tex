\begin{abstract}
For each positive integer $n$, let $B_n$ denote the set of divisors of the
Fibonacci number $F_n$ whose rank of apparition under the map $\alpha$ equals
$n$. We describe $B_n$ as an upper set in the divisor lattice of $F_n$ and
classify its minimal elements by irredundant witness covers of the prime
coordinates of $n$. The resulting support budget $\omega(m)\leq\omega(n)$ is
best possible as a uniform bound. We construct a rank-pure exact-rank-prime
sector that embeds every irredundant cover of the prime coordinates except for
at most two explicitly identified exceptional supports, preserving the complete
support hypergraph. If $k=\omega(n)$ and $R(n)$ is the largest number of
atomic prime powers with one fixed essential/full support pair, then, uniformly
as $k\to\infty$,
\[
 \log\#\mathcal M_n^{\rm conn}\geq
 \frac{\log 2}{4}k^2+k\log2-\frac12\log k+O(1),
 \qquad
 \log\#\mathcal M_n
 \leq \frac{\log 2}{4}k^2+k\log R(n)+O(k).
\]
The lower bound has an explicit parity-dependent Jacobi theta constant and is
realized by connected minimal generators. It does not imply that disconnected
generators are negligible in the full arithmetic count. The upper estimate is
not an asymptotic formula without additional control of the unresolved quantity
$R(n)$. We prove that the mean contribution from aggregating several exact-rank
classes in one support window is only $O(x\log\log x)$, reducing the mean
interface to a visible single-rank maximum. We also show that a published
conjecture of Bugeaud--Luca--Mignotte--Siksek would contradict the resulting
mean sparse-window hypothesis, and give a blockwise condition under which
disconnected generators are exponentially negligible. Finally, we classify
the cases $\omega(n)\leq3$ and prove unique connected-block factorizations for
$B_n$ and its minimal elements.
For the remaining single-rank maximum, a fibotomic argument gives the
unconditional pointwise estimate
$a(d)\leq(\log\varphi/2+o(1))\phi_{\rm E}(d)/\log d$, where
$\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ and $\phi_{\rm E}$ is Euler's function, together with
new unconditional upper and lower bounds for its weighted mean contribution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% ====================================================================

For an integer $q\ge 1$, the \emph{rank of apparition} (or \emph{order of
appearance}) $\alpha(q)$ is the least positive index $r$ such that $q\mid
F_r$, where $(F_r)_{r\ge 0}$ denotes the Fibonacci sequence with $F_0=0$,
$F_1=1$; the existence of this least index for every $q$ is recorded in
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-rank}. We use the harmless conventions $\alpha(1)=1$ and
$D_1=\operatorname{Div}(F_1)=\{1\}$ in divisor-lattice formulas. For each
$n\ge 2$, write
\[
D_n:=\operatorname{Div}(F_n),
\qquad
B_n:=\{q\in D_n:\ \alpha(q)=n\},
\qquad
A(n):=\#B_n.
\]
Let $\mathcal P(n)$ be the set of prime divisors of $n$, and let
$\mathcal M_n$ denote the set of minimal elements of $B_n$ under divisibility.
Thus $B_n$ consists of those moduli that first divide a Fibonacci number at
index~$n$. Since $F_2=1$, the boundary layer is empty: throughout the paper
we use the convention $B_2=\mathcal M_2=\varnothing$ and $A(2)=0$.
Throughout, $\log$ is the natural logarithm, $\omega(t)$ is the number of
distinct prime divisors of $t$, and $\Omega(t)$ counts prime divisors with
multiplicity. We write $A_{\rm pr}(n)$ for the number of primes of exact rank
$n$, $\mathsf{Part}(X)$ for the set of set partitions of a finite set $X$,
and $\mathsf B_k$ for the $k$th Bell number. A hypergraph on one vertex is
regarded as connected. These conventions also apply when the notation first
appears in a later section.
The counting function $A(n)$ is subsumed by the
$\alpha$-contraction formalism of Stroi\'nski \cite{Stroinski2016AlphaContraction};
see also the classical M\"obius inversions discussed in
\cite{Halton1966DivisibilityFibonacci,FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions}.
In this paper we go beyond enumeration and study $B_n$ as an
ordered subset of the divisor lattice $(D_n,\mid)$, asking how it sits inside
$D_n$, how it is generated, and what combinatorial structure it carries.

Three classical facts underlie the analysis. First, $q\mid F_m$ if and only if
$\alpha(q)\mid m$
\cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm,Vinson1963RankApparition}. Second, the Fibonacci
sequence satisfies strong divisibility: $\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}$.
Third, ranks of apparition respect least common multiples:
$\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))=\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))$; Renault proves the
corresponding identity in the more general $(a,b)$-Fibonacci setting
\cite{Renault2013PeriodRankOrder}. These properties, combined with the
primitive divisor theorem for Fibonacci numbers
\cite{Carmichael1913PrimitiveDivisors,BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas},
are the sole inputs for the main results.

This point of view complements several nearby strands in the literature.
Luca and Pomerance \cite{LucaPomerance2014LocalBehavior} study the local
behavior of $\alpha$ under additive shifts; Trojovsk\'a
\cite{Trojovska2020PeriodicPoints} and FitzGibbons--Javaheri--Miller--Verga
\cite{FitzGibbonsJavaheriMillerVerga2025Dynamics} study periodic points and
iteration dynamics of $\alpha$; Byer--Dvorachek--Eckard--Harrington--Wise--Wong
\cite{CameronCountsLundMillerPiechnikWong2022Fibotomic} study fibotomic
polynomials and related algebraic factorizations; and Sanna
\cite{Sanna2016LucasSequences} and Marques \cite{Marques2012OrderAppearance}
study the growth and fixed-point behavior of the order-of-appearance map. The
present paper instead fixes a single fiber $B_n$ and studies its divisibility
geometry inside $D_n$. The upper-fiber identity, the coordinatewise minimal-lcm
criterion, the prime--ladder alternative, the private-coordinate injection, and
the coprimality of disjoint coordinate blocks are recorded below as lemmas:
they are formal consequences of the cited classical identities and are not
claimed as independent arithmetic advances. The contributions developed from
these facts are the witness-cover formulation, the exhaustive support-three
classification with its arithmetic realization test, the passage of connected
block factorization to minimal elements, and the support-entropy coefficient
$\log 2/4$. A further contribution is the support-preserving rank-pure
embedding of arbitrary minimal set covers, which transfers the classical theta
asymptotic and a discrete central local limit to an explicit Fibonacci sector.
These lower results isolate, rather than resolve, the arithmetic multiplicity
$R(n)$. Accordingly, we make no unconditional claim of a sharp asymptotic for
$\#\mathcal M_n$ or of the negligibility of its disconnected part. We do,
however, isolate the mean interface further: support-window aggregation costs
only $O(x\log\log x)$ on average, so only a visible single-rank maximum remains.
A fibotomic size estimate and the congruence restrictions on exact-rank primes
then yield a uniform entropy inequality for each exact-rank class and a
pointwise bound of order $\phi_{\rm E}(d)/\log d$. This improves the available
single-rank input but does not settle the mean sparse-window hypothesis.
A conditional dominance theorem also identifies the stronger blockwise control
that would make the disconnected part exponentially negligible. The mean
sparse-window hypothesis is not presented as a prediction: Conjecture~5.1 of
Bugeaud--Luca--Mignotte--Siksek
\cite{BugeaudLucaMignotteSiksek2005FewPrimeDivisors} would imply its failure.

Call a prime power $\theta=p^e$ \emph{atomic for the index $d$} if
$\alpha(\theta)=d$ and $\alpha(p^{e-1})<d$. An \emph{$n$-witness cover} is a
pairwise coprime set of such atomic prime powers whose apparition indices
collectively span the prime factorization of $n$ in a minimal, irredundant
fashion; the full definition is Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}.

\begin{previewtheorem}[Witness-cover theorem]
For every $n\ge2$, multiplication gives a bijection from $n$-witness
covers $\Theta$ to minimal generators:
\[
\mathcal M_n=
\left\{\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta:\ \Theta\text{ is an $n$-witness cover}
\right\}.
\]
Consequently every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ satisfies
$\omega(m)\le\omega(n)$. This support budget is globally sharp in the precise
sense that no smaller bound depending only on $\omega(n)$ is valid; equality
is constructed on every odd layer $n\ge3$. The precise cover conditions are
given in Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}, and the result is
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} together with
Corollaries~\ref{cor:support-bound},~\ref{cor:support-bound-sharp},
and~\ref{cor:support-budget-best-possible}.
\end{previewtheorem}

The witness classification also controls the number of minimal generators,
not merely their form.

\begin{previewtheorem}[Support-entropy theorem]
For $n\ge3$, let $k=\omega(n)$ and let $R(n)$ be the largest atomic-family
multiplicity at one essential/full support pair. If $k\ge3$ and
$r=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$, then
\[
 (2^r-1)^{k-r}\le\#\mathcal M_n
 \le\sum_{s=1}^{k}\binom{k}{s}(2R(n))^s2^{s(k-s)}.
\]
The rank-pure sector gives the stronger connected lower bound
\[
 \log\#\mathcal M_n^{\rm conn}\ge
 \frac{\log2}{4}k^2+k\log2-\frac12\log k+O(1)
\]
uniformly as $k\to\infty$, with an explicit parity-dependent theta constant.
The coefficient $\log2/4$ is exact for the unweighted minimal-cover count
embedded in the fixed-choice rank-pure sector. This assertion is not an
optimality statement for all information obtainable from fibotomic sizes and
exact-rank congruences. These lower bounds do not give an asymptotic formula
for $\log\#\mathcal M_n$ unless $R(n)=\exp(o(\omega(n)))$. Separate
conjectures about $R(n)$ imply conditional normal-order and mean equivalents.
These statements are
Theorems~\ref{thm:rank-pure-universality} and
\ref{thm:minimal-generator-growth}, together with their corollaries.
\end{previewtheorem}

Making this classification effective requires the prime-power apparition
formulas of Fulton--Morris \cite{FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions} and
Lengyel \cite{Lengyel1995OrderFibonacciLucas}. These show that every atomic
factor is either a \emph{prime atom} or lies on a
\emph{prime-power ladder} (changing exactly one prime coordinate of the
apparition index). This dichotomy
(Lemma~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}) enables complete
classifications of $\mathcal M_n$ when $\omega(n)\le 3$. The detailed
ingredients are Corollary~\ref{cor:two-support-complete-classification} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}; the consolidated
classification is Theorem~\ref{thm:low-support-classification-package}. The
corresponding complete description of the full birth layer $B_n$ is obtained by
taking the upper ideals generated by these listed minimal elements; this is
recorded in Corollary~\ref{cor:low-support-birth-layer-package}.

The same lcm mechanism gives a local-to-global factorization over connected
coordinate blocks.

\begin{previewtheorem}[Connected block factorization]
Let $q\in B_n$ and form the support hypergraph on $\mathcal P(n)$ whose
edges are the prime-coordinate supports of the ranks of the prime-power
factors of $q$. If its connected components are $C$, then
\[
q=\prod_C q_C,\qquad q_C\in B_{n_C},
\qquad n_C=\prod_{\ell\in C}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n)} ,
\]
and this factorization is unique. The same statement holds for minimal
generators after replacing $B_{n_C}$ by $\mathcal M_{n_C}$. Consequently
$A(n)$ and $\#\mathcal M_n$ are partition sums over the connected counts.
This is proved in Section~\ref{sec:connected}.
\end{previewtheorem}

As secondary consequences, nonexceptional birth layers contain principal
intervals natural after choosing an exact-rank prime
(Corollary~\ref{cor:canonical-principal-interval}), Boolean subposets of rank
$\tau(n)-O(1)$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:boolean-skeleton}), and---for odd
$n$---realizations of every minimal cover of $\mathcal P(n)$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:odd-rank-pure-universality}).

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:prelim} collects classical
background on Fibonacci divisibility and exact-rank primes.
Section~\ref{sec:upper-fibers} first records the general fiber and witness-cover
structure. Section~\ref{sec:low-support} then introduces the Fibonacci-specific
prime-power arithmetic and the low-support classifications.
Section~\ref{sec:connected} proves the connected-block factorization
(Theorems~\ref{thm:birth-connected-factorization}
and~\ref{thm:mingenerator-connected-factorization}) and the partition-lattice
formulas.
Section~\ref{sec:growth} proves rank-pure universality, the theta-refined
support-entropy bounds, and the discrete local limit, before isolating the
unresolved arithmetic interface. Section~\ref{sec:numerical} presents the
finite verification, and
Section~\ref{sec:questions} records further questions.

\subsection{Rank of apparition and Fibonacci divisibility}\label{subsec:fib-div}

\begin{lemma}[Finite rank of apparition]\label{lem:finite-rank}
For every integer $q\ge1$, the rank of apparition $\alpha(q)$ is finite.  More
precisely, $\alpha(1)=1$, and for every $q\ge2$ there is a positive integer
$r$ such that $q\mid F_r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The case $q=1$ is the stated convention.  Let $q\ge2$.  Work modulo $q$ with
the Fibonacci state vector $(F_m,F_{m+1})$.  The transition
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto (y,x+y)
\]
has inverse $(x,y)\mapsto (y-x,x)$ modulo $q$, so it is a permutation of the
finite set $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^2$.  Therefore the orbit of the initial state $(0,1)$
is periodic and returns to $(0,1)$ after some positive number $r$ of steps.
Thus $(F_r,F_{r+1})\equiv(0,1)\pmod q$, in particular $q\mid F_r$.  The
least such positive $r$ exists by well ordering.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rank-basics}
Let $q,u,v\ge1$ and let $a,b,m\ge1$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $q\mid F_m$ if and only if $\alpha(q)\mid m$.
\item $\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}$.
\item $\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))=\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The divisibility criterion and the strong-divisibility identity are classical;
we use them in the forms proved by Wall \cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm} and
Vinson \cite{Vinson1963RankApparition}. The convention $\alpha(1)=1$ makes
the assertion for $q=1$ immediate.

For the lcm identity, an integer $m$ is divisible by
$\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))$ if and only if $\lcmop(u,v)\mid F_m$, by (i). This is
equivalent to $u\mid F_m$ and $v\mid F_m$, hence to
$\alpha(u)\mid m$ and $\alpha(v)\mid m$, again by (i). Thus the multiples of
$\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))$ are exactly the multiples of
$\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))$, and the two positive integers are equal.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact-rank primes and primitive divisors}\label{subsec:primitive}

A prime $p$ is an \emph{exact-rank prime of rank $d$} if $\alpha(p)=d$;
when it is viewed as an atomic prime power, we also call it a \emph{prime
atom}. We reserve \emph{primitive divisor of $F_d$} for the standard
Carmichael--Bilu--Hanrot--Voutier convention: a prime $p$ dividing $F_d$ is
primitive when
\[
 p\nmid 5F_1F_2\cdots F_{d-1}.
\]
Thus every primitive divisor is an exact-rank prime, but the exact-rank prime
$5$ at rank $5$ is excluded from the primitive-divisor terminology because it
divides the discriminant. This distinction will be maintained throughout.

\begin{theorem}[Classical exact-rank existence]\label{thm:primitive-divisors}
For every $n\ge 3$ with $n\notin\{6,12\}$, there exists a prime $p_n$ with
$\alpha(p_n)=n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $n=3,4,5$ one takes $p_3=2$, $p_4=3$, and $p_5=5$; the last is the
discriminant-related exact-rank exception just described. For $n\ge 7$,
$n\neq 12$, the primitive divisor theorem
\cite{Carmichael1913PrimitiveDivisors,Yabuta2001PrimitiveDivisorFibonacci,%
BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas}
gives a prime dividing $F_n$ but no earlier Fibonacci number. Since
$\alpha(p_n)\mid n$ and $p_n$ divides no $F_d$ with $d<n$, necessarily
$\alpha(p_n)=n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Counting corollaries}\label{subsec:counting}

The standard M\"obius-inversion formulas for first-appearance counts follow
from Stroi\'nski's $\alpha$-contraction identity
\cite{Stroinski2016AlphaContraction}: for any $f:\NN\to\mathbb{C}$ with
$f(1)=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stroinski}
\sum_{d\mid F_n}f(d)=\sum_{d\mid n}f_\alpha(d),
\qquad
f_\alpha(d):=\sum_{\alpha(m)=d}f(m).
\end{equation}
Taking $f$ to be the indicator of integers at least $2$, of primes, or of
prime powers, and applying M\"obius inversion, one obtains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mobius-birth}
A(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\,\tau(F_d),
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{pr}}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\,\omega(F_d).
\end{equation}
We regard these as known corollaries; the results of
Sections~\ref{sec:upper-fibers}--\ref{sec:connected} are independent of them.

\begin{lemma}[Exact-rank prime M\"obius count]\label{lem:primitive-prime-mobius-count}
For $d\ge1$, put
\[
\Pi_\alpha(d):=\{p\text{ prime}:\alpha(p)=d\}.
\]
Then $\Pi_\alpha(d)$ is finite and
\[
\#\Pi_\alpha(d)=\sum_{c\mid d}\mu(d/c)\,\omega(F_c).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each $d\ge1$, the primes with rank dividing $d$ are exactly the prime
divisors of $F_d$: indeed, for a prime $p$, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}
gives $p\mid F_d$ if and only if $\alpha(p)\mid d$.  Hence
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics} partitions the distinct prime divisors of
$F_d$ according to their exact ranks $c\mid d$, including the exceptional
primes $2$ and $5$ with ranks $3$ and $5$ respectively; a prime divisor of
$F_c$ can occur earlier only through the condition $\alpha(p)\mid e$ for the
earlier index $e$.
\[
\omega(F_d)=\sum_{c\mid d}\#\Pi_\alpha(c).
\]
The right-hand side is finite because $F_d$ has only finitely many prime
divisors.  Applying M\"obius inversion on the divisor lattice of $d$ gives the
displayed formula.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Atomic notation}\label{subsec:prime-power}

\begin{definition}\label{def:atomic-families}
For a prime power $\theta=p^e$ with $e\ge1$, put
\[
d(\theta):=\alpha(\theta),\qquad
d^-(\theta):=\alpha(p^{e-1}),
\]
where $\alpha(1)=1$. For $d\ge1$ define
\[
\Atom(d):=\{p^e:\ \alpha(p^e)=d,\ \alpha(p^{e-1})<d\}.
\]
Thus $\Atom(d)$ consists of the prime-power factors that are born exactly at
rank $d$.

If $d(\theta)\mid n$, define the full-coordinate support and the
essential-coordinate support of $\theta$ relative to $n$ by
\[
T_n(\theta):=\{\ell\in\mathcal P(n):\
\nu_\ell(d(\theta))=\nu_\ell(n)\},
\]
and
\[
E_n(\theta):=\{\ell\in T_n(\theta):\
\nu_\ell(d^-(\theta))<\nu_\ell(n)\}.
\]
Equivalently, $T_n(\theta)$ records the prime coordinates of $n$ supplied at
full exponent by $d(\theta)$, while $E_n(\theta)$ records the full coordinates
lost when $\theta=p^e$ is lowered to $p^{e-1}$.

For nonempty $I\subseteq J\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$, put
\[
\mathcal A_{I,J}(n):=
\{\theta\in\bigcup_{d\mid n}\Atom(d):\
E_n(\theta)=I,\ T_n(\theta)=J\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\section{Birth layers and witness covers}\label{sec:upper-fibers}
%% ====================================================================

The first two lemmas in this section use only rank divisibility and the lcm
identity. They are included to fix the structural framework and are not
presented as new Fibonacci identities. The witness-cover theorem packages
their minimality conditions for the subsequent Fibonacci analysis.

\begin{definition}\label{def:birth-layer}
For $n\ge2$ let
\[
D_n:=\operatorname{Div}(F_n),\qquad
B_n:=\{q\in D_n:\ \alpha(q)=n\},\qquad
A(n):=\#B_n.
\]
The set $B_n$ is partially ordered by divisibility. Its set of minimal
elements is
\[
\mathcal M_n:=\{m\in B_n:\ \text{there is no }q\in B_n
\text{ with }q\mid m\text{ and }q<m\},
\]
and
\[
\widthop(B_n):=\max\{\#\mathcal A:\ \mathcal A\subseteq B_n
\text{ is an antichain}\}.
\]
We write $\mathcal P(n)$ for the set of prime divisors of $n$. Since
$F_2=1$, we use the boundary convention
\[
B_2=\mathcal M_2=\varnothing,\qquad A(2)=\widthop(B_2)=0.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Upper-fiber identity]\label{thm:upper-fiber}
For every $n\ge2$,
\[
B_n=D_n\setminus\bigcup_{p\mid n}D_{n/p}.
\]
Moreover $B_n$ is an upper set in $D_n$: if $q\in B_n$, $r\in D_n$, and
$q\mid r$, then $r\in B_n$. Consequently
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal M_n}\{q\in D_n:\ m\mid q\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}, an integer $q$ lies in $D_m$ if and
only if $\alpha(q)\mid m$. Thus $q\in D_n$ lies outside
$\bigcup_{p\mid n}D_{n/p}$ precisely when $\alpha(q)\mid n$ but
$\alpha(q)\nmid n/p$ for every prime $p\mid n$.

If $\alpha(q)=n$, then clearly $\alpha(q)\nmid n/p$ for every $p\mid n$, so
$q$ is outside the union. Conversely, suppose $\alpha(q)\mid n$ and
$\alpha(q)<n$. Then $n/\alpha(q)>1$; choose a prime $p$ dividing
$n/\alpha(q)$. Since $\alpha(q)\mid n/p$, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}
gives $q\in D_{n/p}$, contrary to being outside the union. Hence the
complement is exactly the set of $q\in D_n$ with $\alpha(q)=n$, namely
$B_n$. For $n=2$ this also gives
$B_2=D_2\setminus D_1=\varnothing$ under the boundary convention in
Definition~\ref{def:birth-layer}.

Now let $q\in B_n$, $r\in D_n$, and $q\mid r$. Since $r\in D_n$,
$\alpha(r)\mid n$. Since $q\mid r$, every Fibonacci number divisible by $r$
is divisible by $q$; applying the divisibility criterion to
$F_{\alpha(r)}$ gives $\alpha(q)\mid\alpha(r)$. But $\alpha(q)=n$, so
$n\mid\alpha(r)$ and $\alpha(r)\mid n$, whence $\alpha(r)=n$. Therefore
$r\in B_n$.

Finally $D_n$ is finite, so every element $q\in B_n$ has at least one
minimal element $m$ of $B_n$ below it in the divisibility order. The upper-set
property gives the reverse containment: every element of $D_n$ divisible by
some $m\in\mathcal M_n$ lies again in $B_n$. This proves the displayed
decomposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Minimal-lcm criterion]\label{thm:minimal-generators}
Let $n\ge2$, and let
$m=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{e_i}$ be a divisor of $F_n$, with the $p_i$ distinct
and $e_i\ge1$. Put
\[
\lambda_i:=\alpha(p_i^{e_i}),\qquad
\lambda_i^-:=\alpha(p_i^{e_i-1}),
\]
where $\alpha(1)=1$. Then $m\in\mathcal M_n$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-criterion}
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)=n
\quad\text{and}\quad
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_i^-,
\lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\lambda_r)<n
\quad(1\le i\le r).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because $m\mid F_n$, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics} gives
$\lambda_i\mid n$ for every $i$. Applying the lcm identity in
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics} repeatedly gives
\[
\alpha(m)=\lcmop(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r).
\]
Thus the first condition in \eqref{eq:lcm-criterion} is equivalent to
$m\in B_n$.

Assume first that $m\in\mathcal M_n$. Then $m\in B_n$, so the first lcm is
$n$. For a fixed $i$, the integer $m/p_i$ is a proper divisor of $m$ and is
still a divisor of $F_n$. Minimality of $m$ in $B_n$ implies
$m/p_i\notin B_n$. Since $\alpha(m/p_i)\mid n$, this is equivalent to
$\alpha(m/p_i)<n$. A second application of the lcm identity gives
\[
\alpha(m/p_i)=
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_i^-,
\lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\lambda_r),
\]
which is the lowered prime-power condition in
\eqref{eq:lcm-criterion}.

Conversely, suppose \eqref{eq:lcm-criterion} holds. The first condition gives
$m\in B_n$. Let $q$ be any proper divisor of $m$. Then for at least one
index $i$ the exponent of $p_i$ in $q$ is at most $e_i-1$. Hence
\[
\alpha(q)\mid
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_i^-,
\lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\lambda_r),
\]
again by the lcm identity and by monotonicity under divisibility of prime
powers. The right-hand side is $<n$ by
\eqref{eq:lcm-criterion}. Since $q\mid m\mid F_n$, we also have
$\alpha(q)\mid n$, and therefore $\alpha(q)<n$. Thus no proper divisor of
$m$ lies in $B_n$, so $m$ is minimal in $B_n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Witness covers}

\begin{definition}\label{def:witness-cover}
An \emph{$n$-witness cover} is a finite set
$\Theta=\{\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r\}$ of atomic prime powers satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $d(\theta)\mid n$ for every $\theta\in\Theta$;
\item the elements of $\Theta$ are pairwise coprime;
\item the full-coordinate supports cover all prime coordinates of $n$,
\[
\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta}T_n(\theta)=\mathcal P(n);
\]
\item each atom has an essential coordinate not supplied by the other atoms,
\[
E_n(\theta)\nsubseteq
\bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta,\ \eta\ne\theta}T_n(\eta)
\qquad(\theta\in\Theta).
\]
\end{enumerate}
The last condition is the irredundance condition. It is stated with
$E_n$ rather than $T_n$ because overlapping full-coordinate support is
allowed; what is forbidden is that every coordinate lost by lowering
$\theta$ is already supplied at full exponent by the remaining atoms.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Coordinate test for lowering one atom]
\label{lem:coordinate-lowering-test}
Let $n\ge2$, and let
$\Theta=\{\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r\}$ be a pairwise coprime set of atomic
prime powers with $d(\theta_i)\mid n$ and
\[
\lcmop(d(\theta_1),\ldots,d(\theta_r))=n.
\]
Fix $\theta_i=p_i^{e_i}$, and put
\[
N_i^-:=\lcmop\bigl(d^-(\theta_i),\ d(\theta_j):j\ne i\bigr).
\]
Then $N_i^-=n$ if and only if
\[
E_n(\theta_i)\subseteq\bigcup_{j\ne i}T_n(\theta_j).
\]
Equivalently, lowering $\theta_i$ to $p_i^{e_i-1}$ keeps the apparition lcm
equal to $n$ exactly when every coordinate lost by the lowering is supplied at
full exponent by some other atom. If
$\ell\in E_n(\theta_i)\setminus\bigcup_{j\ne i}T_n(\theta_j)$, then
$\nu_\ell(N_i^-)<\nu_\ell(n)$, so $N_i^-<n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $d^-(\theta_i)\mid d(\theta_i)\mid n$ and $d(\theta_j)\mid n$ for
$j\ne i$, the integer $N_i^-$ divides $n$. Hence $N_i^-=n$ is equivalent to
saying that every prime coordinate $\ell\in\mathcal P(n)$ occurs in $N_i^-$
with valuation $\nu_\ell(n)$.

Assume first that
$E_n(\theta_i)\subseteq\bigcup_{j\ne i}T_n(\theta_j)$, and fix
$\ell\in\mathcal P(n)$. Because the original lcm is $n$, at least one of the
numbers $d(\theta_1),\ldots,d(\theta_r)$ supplies the full
$\ell$-adic valuation of $n$. If such a supplier is some $d(\theta_j)$ with
$j\ne i$, then $\ell\in T_n(\theta_j)$ and the same full valuation appears in
$N_i^-$. The only remaining case is that $\theta_i$ itself supplies the full
valuation and no other atom does, so $\ell\in T_n(\theta_i)$ and
$\ell\notin\bigcup_{j\ne i}T_n(\theta_j)$. The assumed containment then gives
$\ell\notin E_n(\theta_i)$. By the definition of $E_n(\theta_i)$,
$\nu_\ell(d^-(\theta_i))=\nu_\ell(n)$, so the lowered factor still supplies the
full $\ell$-coordinate in $N_i^-$. Thus every coordinate of $n$ is still
present at full valuation, and $N_i^-=n$.

Conversely, suppose that some coordinate
$\ell\in E_n(\theta_i)$ is not in $\bigcup_{j\ne i}T_n(\theta_j)$. The
membership $\ell\in E_n(\theta_i)$ says
$\nu_\ell(d^-(\theta_i))<\nu_\ell(n)$. For every $j\ne i$, the exclusion
$\ell\notin T_n(\theta_j)$ says
$\nu_\ell(d(\theta_j))<\nu_\ell(n)$. Therefore the lcm defining $N_i^-$ also
has $\ell$-adic valuation strictly below $\nu_\ell(n)$. Since $N_i^-\mid n$,
this proves $N_i^-<n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:witness-classification}
For every $n\ge 2$, multiplication gives a bijection
\[
\Theta\longmapsto \prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta
\]
from the set of $n$-witness covers onto $\mathcal M_n$. Its inverse sends
$m\in\mathcal M_n$ to the set
\[
\Theta(m):=\{p^{\nu_p(m)}:p\mid m\}
\]
of prime-power factors occurring in the prime factorization of $m$. In
particular,
\[
\mathcal M_n=\bigl\{\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta:
\Theta\text{ is an $n$-witness cover}\bigr\},
\]
and each element of $\mathcal M_n$ has a unique witness-cover factorization.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$, and write its prime factorization as
$m=\prod_{i=1}^r\theta_i$ with $\theta_i=p_i^{e_i}$ and the $p_i$ distinct.
Each $\theta_i$ is atomic: if $\alpha(p_i^{e_i-1})=\alpha(p_i^{e_i})$, then
replacing $\theta_i$ by $p_i^{e_i-1}$ preserves the lcm, contradicting
the minimal-generator criterion in
Lemma~\ref{thm:minimal-generators}. Since
$n=\lcmop(d(\theta_i))$, the sets $T_n(\theta_i)$ cover $\mathcal P(n)$. The
condition $\alpha(m/p_i)<n$ forces
$E_n(\theta_i)\nsubseteq\bigcup_{j\neq i}T_n(\theta_j)$; otherwise the lost
coordinates would be supplied by other factors, keeping the lcm equal to $n$.
Thus $\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\}$ is an $n$-witness cover.

Conversely, let $\Theta$ be an $n$-witness cover and
$m=\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta$. By
the lcm property recorded in Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics},
$\alpha(m)=\lcmop(d(\theta):\theta\in\Theta)=n$. For each
$\theta=p^e\in\Theta$, the witness condition
provides $\ell\in E_n(\theta)\setminus\bigcup_{\eta\neq\theta}T_n(\eta)$; at
this coordinate, $\alpha(m/p)<n$.
Lemma~\ref{thm:minimal-generators} then gives $m\in\mathcal M_n$.

It remains to record injectivity and the stated inverse.  If two witness covers
$\Theta$ and $\Theta'$ have the same product, then unique factorization in
$\mathbb Z$ gives the same underlying primes with the same exponents in the two
products.  Since the elements of each witness cover are pairwise coprime prime
powers, those prime-power factors are exactly the elements of the cover.  Hence
$\Theta=\Theta'$.  Therefore multiplication is injective, while the first
paragraph shows that the prime-power factor set $\Theta(m)$ of every
$m\in\mathcal M_n$ is an $n$-witness cover and the second paragraph shows that
every $n$-witness cover maps into $\mathcal M_n$.  Thus multiplication is the
claimed bijection, with inverse $m\mapsto\Theta(m)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:support-bound}
Every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ satisfies $\omega(m)\le\omega(n)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The witness condition provides an injection
$\theta\mapsto\ell(\theta)$ from $\Theta$ into $\mathcal P(n)$: choose
$\ell(\theta)\in E_n(\theta)\setminus\bigcup_{\eta\neq\theta}T_n(\eta)$.
Hence $\omega(m)=\#\Theta\le\omega(n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite Fibonacci support grammar]
\label{cor:finite-witness-cover-types}
Fix $k\ge1$. Up to relabeling of the $k$ prime coordinates of $n$, only
finitely many witness-cover support types can occur for minimal generators
$m\in\mathcal M_n$ with $\omega(n)=k$. Put
\[
\mathcal P_k:=\{(I,J):\varnothing\ne I\subseteq J\subseteq[k],\
 I=J\text{ or }|I|=1\},
\qquad
N'_k:=\#\mathcal P_k=(2^k-1)+k(2^{k-1}-1).
\]
Every minimal generator determines a unique labelled support set
\[
\mathsf T(m):=\{(E_n(\theta),T_n(\theta)):\theta\in\Theta(m)\}
 \subseteq\mathcal P_k,
\]
with $1\le|\mathsf T(m)|\le k$, satisfying coverage and irredundance:
\[
\bigcup_{(I,J)\in\mathsf T(m)}J=[k],\qquad
I\nsubseteq\bigcup_{(I',J')\in\mathsf T(m)\setminus\{(I,J)\}}J'.
\]
Consequently the number of possible labelled support types is at most
\[
\sum_{r=1}^k\binom{N'_k}{r}.
\]
The converse is not asserted: arithmetic realization additionally requires
nonempty atom families and representatives with distinct underlying primes.
Unlabelled types are $S_k$-orbits of the labelled support sets. For $k=3$, the
realization layer is the support-three slot test of
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:low-support-classification-package}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification}, every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ is the
product attached to a unique witness cover $\Theta(m)$, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound} gives $\#\Theta(m)\le k$. Irredundance makes
each $E_n(\theta)$ nonempty. After labeling $\mathcal P(n)$ by $[k]$, the
prime--ladder alternative in Lemma~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy} shows
that each atom contributes a pair in $\mathcal P_k$: a prime atom has $I=J$,
whereas a ladder atom with nonempty essential support has $|I|=1$.

Two distinct atoms cannot contribute the same pair $(I,J)$, since then the
essential support of either would be contained in the full support of the
other. Hence $\mathsf T(m)$ is a genuine set and is uniquely determined by
the labelled prime-power factorization. There are $2^k-1$ diagonal pairs and,
for each singleton $I=\{i\}$, there are $2^{k-1}-1$ proper supersets
$J\supsetneq I$, proving the formula for $N'_k$. Coverage and irredundance are
exactly the two displayed conditions. Counting nonempty subsets of
$\mathcal P_k$ of size at most $k$ proves the bound. Passing to unlabelled
types amounts to taking $S_k$-orbits. The atom-family nonemptiness and
coprimeness requirements explain why the converse need not hold.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:support-bound-sharp}
For every odd $n\ge3$ there exists
$m\in\mathcal M_n$ with $\omega(m)=\omega(n)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
n=\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P(n)}\ell^{a_\ell}.
\]
For each $\ell\in\mathcal P(n)$ put $d_\ell=\ell^{a_\ell}$. Since $n$ is
odd and $n\ge3$, every $d_\ell$ is odd and at least $3$; in particular
$d_\ell\notin\{6,12\}$. The primitive divisor theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors}, therefore supplies a prime
$\pi_\ell$ with $\alpha(\pi_\ell)=d_\ell$.

The primes $\pi_\ell$ are distinct, because a single prime has only one rank
of apparition. Each $\pi_\ell$ is a prime atom in
\(\Atom(d_\ell)\), so, relative to $n$,
\[
T_n(\pi_\ell)=E_n(\pi_\ell)=\{\ell\}.
\]
Thus
\[
\Theta_n:=\{\pi_\ell:\ell\in\mathcal P(n)\}
\]
is an $n$-witness cover: the factors are pairwise coprime, their
full-coordinate supports cover $\mathcal P(n)$, and each singleton
essential support is absent from all other full-coordinate supports. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification},
\[
m_n:=\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P(n)}\pi_\ell
\]
belongs to $\mathcal M_n$. Since the factors are distinct primes,
\[
\omega(m_n)=\#\mathcal P(n)=\omega(n).
\]
Hence the upper bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound} cannot be lowered
as a function of $\omega(n)$, even after restricting to odd indices.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Best possible support budget]
\label{cor:support-budget-best-possible}
Let $g:\NN\to\NN$ be any function such that
\[
\omega(m)\le g(\omega(n))
\]
for every $n\ge2$ and every $m\in\mathcal M_n$. Then $g(k)\ge k$ for every
$k\ge1$. Equivalently, Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound} is sharp as a
uniform bound depending only on the number of prime coordinates of $n$, not as
a claim that equality occurs on every layer.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix $k\ge1$ and choose any product $n$ of $k$ distinct odd primes; for
example, take the product of the first $k$ odd primes. Then $n$ is odd,
$n\ge3$, and $\omega(n)=k$. Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound-sharp} gives an
element $m\in\mathcal M_n$ with $\omega(m)=\omega(n)=k$. Applying the assumed
uniform inequality to this pair gives $k=\omega(m)\le g(k)$. Since $k$ was
arbitrary, no uniform support budget smaller than $\omega(n)$ can hold.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:n20}
For $n=20$, $F_{20}=6765=3\cdot 5\cdot 11\cdot 41$. The relevant atomic
factors are $3\in\Atom(4)$, $5\in\Atom(5)$, $11\in\Atom(10)$,
$41\in\Atom(20)$. The witness covers are $\{41\}$, $\{3,5\}$, $\{3,11\}$,
yielding $\mathcal M_{20}=\{15,33,41\}$ and $A(20)=11$.
Indeed, with coordinates $(2,5)$, the ranks $20$, $4,5$, and $4,10$
respectively cover both coordinates and have the singleton essential supports
required by Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}; the value $A(20)=11$ is the
exhaustive divisor/rank count reported in Table~\ref{tab:birth-layer-data-generated}.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Exact support-three realization at $n=30$]
\label{prop:n30-five-type-classification}
Let the prime coordinates of $30$ be labelled by $(2,3,5)$. Then
\[
 \mathcal M_{30}=\{20,22,31,244,671\}.
\]
These five generators realize, respectively, the five unlabelled types
$\Gamma_8,\Gamma_7,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_5,\Gamma_4$.  No generator in
$\mathcal M_{30}$ has type $\Gamma_3$, $\Gamma_6$, or $\Gamma_9$.
Consequently exactly five, not all eight, of the Fibonacci-admissible
three-coordinate types occur at $n=30$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The exact factorization
\[
 F_{30}=832040=2^3\cdot5\cdot11\cdot31\cdot61
\]
reduces the atomic inventory to the prime powers
$2,4,8,5,11,31,61$. Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders} gives the
ranks of $2,4,8$, and $5$. For the remaining primes,
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics} reduces the rank computation to the proper
divisors of $10$, $15$, and $30$: one has
\[
 F_{10}=55,\qquad F_{15}=610,\qquad F_{30}=832040,
\]
and the required nondivisibilities are
\[
\begin{aligned}
11&\nmid F_d &&(d\in\{1,2,5\}),&
61&\nmid F_d &&(d\in\{1,3,5\}),\\
31&\nmid F_d &&(d\in\{1,2,3,5\}),&
31&\nmid F_d &&(d\in\{6,10,15\}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
\theta&2&4&8&5&11&31&61\\ \hline
\alpha(\theta)&3&6&6&5&10&30&15.
\end{array}
\]
Thus $8$ is not atomic, because $\alpha(8)=\alpha(4)$, while the other six
entries are atomic.  Their nonempty slots, with coordinate names used as
subscripts, are exactly
\[
 P_{235}=\{31\},\quad P_{25}=\{11\},\quad P_{35}=\{61\},
 \quad S_2=\varnothing,\quad S_3=\{2\},\quad S_5=\{5\},
 \quad L_{2,23}=\{4\}.
\]
Indeed, a prime atom has essential support equal to its full support, which
gives the three displayed exact-rank prime slots and the singleton atoms $2$
and $5$. The only nonprime atom is $4$; lowering it to $2$ changes its rank
from $6$ to $3$, so its full support is $\{2,3\}$ and its essential support
is $\{2\}$.  This also proves that every slot not displayed above is empty.

Applying the if-and-only-if slot test of
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}, the nonempty row fillings are
precisely
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{type}&\text{cover}&\text{product}\\ \hline
\Gamma_1&\{31\}&31\\
\Gamma_4&\{11,61\}&671\\
\Gamma_5&\{4,61\}&244\\
\Gamma_7&\{2,11\}&22\\
\Gamma_8&\{5,4\}&20.
\end{array}
\]
All selected underlying primes are distinct, so every filling passes the
coprimeness condition.  Conversely, the slot test is exhaustive.  More
explicitly, $\Gamma_3$ requires a full-support ladder slot $L_{i,235}$, and
none exists; $\Gamma_6$ requires two nonempty proper ladder slots with
distinct essential coordinates, whereas $L_{2,23}$ is the only proper ladder
slot; and $\Gamma_9$ requires all three singleton slots, whereas
$S_2=\varnothing$.  Hence those three types are impossible at $n=30$, and
the five products displayed above exhaust $\mathcal M_{30}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Substructures of nonexceptional birth layers}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:canonical-principal-interval}
Let $n\ge3$, $n\notin\{6,12\}$, and choose an exact-rank prime $\pi_n$ with
$\alpha(\pi_n)=n$. Then the principal interval
\[
[\pi_n,F_n]_{D_n}:=\{q\in D_n:\ \pi_n\mid q\}
\]
is contained in $B_n$. In particular $B_n$ contains a divisor-lattice
interval naturally isomorphic to $\operatorname{Div}(F_n/\pi_n)$. This
interval is natural after choosing $\pi_n$; no choice-free canonical interval
is asserted.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors}, there is a prime
$\pi_n$ with $\alpha(\pi_n)=n$. Thus $\pi_n\in B_n$. Since
Lemma~\ref{thm:upper-fiber} says that $B_n$ is an upper set in $D_n$,
every divisor $q$ of $F_n$ with $\pi_n\mid q$ also lies in $B_n$. Division by
$\pi_n$ gives an order-preserving bijection from this interval to
$\operatorname{Div}(F_n/\pi_n)$, with inverse $d\mapsto \pi_n d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:boolean-skeleton}
Let $n\ge3$, $n\notin\{6,12\}$, and put
\[
\mathcal D_n^*:=\{d:\ d\mid n,\ 3\le d<n,\ d\notin\{6,12\}\}.
\]
For each $d\in\mathcal D_n^*$ choose an exact-rank prime
$\pi_d$ with $\alpha(\pi_d)=d$, and choose an exact-rank prime
$\pi_n$ with $\alpha(\pi_n)=n$. Then
\[
S\longmapsto \pi_n\prod_{d\in S}\pi_d
\qquad(S\subseteq\mathcal D_n^*)
\]
embeds the Boolean lattice $2^{\mathcal D_n^*}$ into $B_n$. Hence
$B_n$ contains a Boolean subposet of rank
\[
|\mathcal D_n^*|\ge \tau(n)-5.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The exact-rank primes exist by Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors}. They
are distinct, because distinct ranks of apparition cannot belong to the
same prime. For every $d\mid n$, $\pi_d\mid F_d\mid F_n$, so the displayed
products divide $F_n$. They all also divide a multiple of $\pi_n$ inside
$D_n$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:canonical-principal-interval} puts every such
multiple in $B_n$.

Since the primes $\pi_d$ are distinct, divisibility among the displayed
products is exactly inclusion among the subsets $S$. Thus the map is a
Boolean-lattice embedding. Among the $\tau(n)$ divisors of $n$, the only
divisors excluded from $\mathcal D_n^*$ are contained in
$\{1,2,6,12,n\}$, so $|\mathcal D_n^*|\ge\tau(n)-5$.
\end{proof}

We now introduce the Fibonacci-specific arithmetic input. The structural
results of Section~\ref{sec:upper-fibers} did not use the formulas below.

\begin{proposition}[Classical prime-power rank lifting]
\label{prop:prime-power-ladders}
Let $p$ be prime, and put $z_p:=\alpha(p)$. For $p\ne2,5$, put
$h_p:=\nu_p(F_{z_p})$. Then
\[
\alpha(2)=3,\qquad \alpha(4)=6,\qquad
\alpha(2^e)=3\cdot2^{e-2}\quad(e\ge3),
\]
\[
\alpha(5^e)=5^e\quad(e\ge1),
\]
and, for $p\ne2,5$,
\[
\alpha(p^e)=p^{\max\{e-h_p,0\}}z_p\quad(e\ge1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Wall's rank-divisibility theorem gives
\[
p\mid F_r\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z_p\mid r
\]
\cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm}. For $p\ne2,5$, the classical congruence
\[
F_{p-(5/p)}\equiv0\pmod p
\]
gives
\[
z_p\mid p-\left(\frac5p\right),
\]
so $p\nmid z_p$. Lengyel's valuation formula
\cite[Theorems~1 and~2]{Lengyel1995OrderFibonacciLucas}, in the modern
treatment of Medina--Rowland \cite{MedinaRowland2015PRegularity}, states that
when $z_p\mid r$,
\[
\nu_p(F_r)=\nu_p(F_{z_p})+\nu_p(r).
\]
Writing $r=z_pu$ and using $p\nmid z_p$ yields
\[
\nu_p(F_{z_pu})=h_p+\nu_p(u).
\]
Thus $p^e\mid F_{z_pu}$ if and only if
$\nu_p(u)\ge e-h_p$, and the least admissible $u$ is
$p^{\max\{e-h_p,0\}}$. This proves the formula for $p\ne2,5$ without an
implicit assumption on $z_p$.

For the exceptional primes, the cited formulas specialize to
\[
\nu_5(F_r)=\nu_5(r),
\qquad
\nu_2(F_r)=
\begin{cases}
0, & 3\nmid r,\\
1, & r\equiv3\pmod 6,\\
3, & r\equiv6\pmod {12},\\
\nu_2(r)+2, & 12\mid r.
\end{cases}
\]
The least index divisible by $5^e$ is $5^e$. For $2$, the least indices with
valuation at least $1$, $2$, and $3$ are $3$, $6$, and $6$; for $e\ge4$ the
least admissible multiple of $12$ is $3\cdot2^{e-2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:atomic-classification}
The atomic prime powers are exactly the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $2\in\Atom(3)$, $4\in\Atom(6)$, and
$2^e\in\Atom(3\cdot2^{e-2})$ for every $e\ge4$; the power $8$ is not atomic.
\item $5^e\in\Atom(5^e)$ for every $e\ge1$.
\item If $p\ne2,5$, $z_p=\alpha(p)$, and
$h_p=\nu_p(F_{z_p})$, then $p\in\Atom(z_p)$ and
$p^e\in\Atom(p^{e-h_p}z_p)$ for every $e>h_p$; no power $p^e$ with
$1<e\le h_p$ is atomic.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently every atom is either a prime atom $p\in\Atom(\alpha(p))$ or a
ladder atom $p^e$ with $e>1$. Every ladder atom changes only the $p$-adic
coordinate of its apparition index when it is lowered to $p^{e-1}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders}. For $p=2$, the ranks are
$3,6,6,12,24,\ldots$ on $2,4,8,16,32,\ldots$. Hence $2$ and $4$ are atomic,
$8$ is not, and each $2^e$ with $e\ge4$ is atomic. For $p=5$, every step is
atomic. If $p\ne2,5$, the rank remains $z_p$ for $1\le e\le h_p$ and then
gains one factor of $p$ at each further level. This proves the list. At every
ladder step, $d(\theta)/d^-(\theta)$ is a power of the same prime $p$, which
proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

This section keeps the low-support classification in the main theorem stream
while treating the slot mechanics as proof support. The principal conclusion is
Theorem~\ref{thm:low-support-classification-package}: for support three there
are exactly eight admissible families, and every remaining decision is a finite
slot-filling test.  The arithmetic tests for filling those slots are stated here;
the reader-facing checklist and the worked $n=42$ verification are collected in
Appendix~\ref{app:three-coordinate-checklist}.

\begin{lemma}[Prime--ladder alternative]\label{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}
Let $\theta=p^e\in\Atom(d)$ and suppose $d\mid n$. If $e=1$, then
\[
E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta).
\]
If $e>1$, then $E_n(\theta)$ is either empty or a singleton, and any
coordinate in $E_n(\theta)$ is the prime coordinate $p$. In particular no
Fibonacci atom has
\[
1<\#E_n(\theta)<\#T_n(\theta).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $e=1$, then $d^-(\theta)=\alpha(1)=1$, so every coordinate of
$T_n(\theta)$ is lost after lowering $\theta$; hence
$E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$.

Assume $e>1$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:atomic-classification}, $\theta$ is a
ladder atom. The prime-power formulas in
Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders} show that
$d(\theta)$ and $d^-(\theta)$ differ only in the $p$-adic coordinate of the
apparition index: this is explicit for $p=2$ and $p=5$, and for
$p\ne2,5$ it follows from
$\alpha(p^e)=p^{e-h_p}z_p$ at the atomic ladder levels. Therefore the only
full coordinate that can be lost when
passing from $d(\theta)$ to $d^-(\theta)$ is the coordinate $p$ itself.
Thus $E_n(\theta)$ is empty or $\{p\}$.

The final assertion follows because either $E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$ in
the prime-atom case or $\#E_n(\theta)\le1$ in the ladder case.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Private-coordinate injection]\label{thm:extremal-coordinate-separation}
Let $\Theta$ be an $n$-witness cover. For each
$\theta\in\Theta$ one may choose a coordinate
\[
\ell(\theta)\in
E_n(\theta)\setminus\bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta,\ \eta\ne\theta}T_n(\eta),
\]
and the chosen coordinates are distinct. If
$|\Theta|=\omega(n)$, then after relabeling the elements of $\Theta$ by
the coordinates of $\mathcal P(n)$ one has
\[
T_n(\theta_\ell)=\{\ell\}\qquad(\ell\in\mathcal P(n)).
\]
In particular, when $\omega(n)=2$, every two-element $n$-witness cover has
one atom supported on the first coordinate and one atom supported on the
second.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The existence of $\ell(\theta)$ is exactly the irredundance condition in
Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}. If
$\ell(\theta)=\ell(\eta)$ with $\theta\ne\eta$, then
$\ell(\theta)\in T_n(\eta)$ because
$E_n(\eta)\subseteq T_n(\eta)$, contradicting the choice of
$\ell(\theta)$. Hence the chosen coordinates are distinct.

If $|\Theta|=\omega(n)$, the injection
$\theta\mapsto\ell(\theta)$ is a bijection from $\Theta$ to
$\mathcal P(n)$. The coordinate $\ell(\theta)$ is not in
$T_n(\eta)$ for any $\eta\ne\theta$. Since the $T_n(\eta)$ cover
$\mathcal P(n)$, every coordinate other than $\ell(\theta)$ is the private
coordinate of some other atom and therefore is not in $T_n(\theta)$. Thus
$T_n(\theta)=\{\ell(\theta)\}$ for every $\theta$, which gives the stated
separation after relabeling.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One-coordinate classification]
\label{cor:one-support-complete-classification}
If $\omega(n)=1$, then
\[
\mathcal M_n=\Atom(n).
\]
With the convention of Definition~\ref{def:birth-layer}, the exceptional
case $n=2$ gives $\mathcal M_2=\varnothing=\Atom(2)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$ and let $\Theta$ be the corresponding $n$-witness
cover from Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification}. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound}, $\#\Theta\le\omega(n)=1$. Since the
$T_n$-sets of a witness cover must cover $\mathcal P(n)$, the cover has one
atom, say $\Theta=\{\theta\}$. The coverage condition forces
$T_n(\theta)=\mathcal P(n)$, and because $d(\theta)\mid n$ this is
equivalent to $d(\theta)=n$. Hence $\theta\in\Atom(n)$ and
$m=\theta$.

Conversely, if $\theta\in\Atom(n)$ and $n\ne2$, then
$d(\theta)=n$, so $T_n(\theta)=\mathcal P(n)$. Since
$d^-(\theta)<d(\theta)=n$ and $\mathcal P(n)$ has one element, the unique
prime coordinate of $n$ lies in $E_n(\theta)$. Thus $\{\theta\}$ is an
$n$-witness cover, and Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} gives
$\theta\in\mathcal M_n$. For $n=2$, Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders}
and Corollary~\ref{cor:atomic-classification} show that no prime power has
rank $2$, while Definition~\ref{def:birth-layer} gives
$\mathcal M_2=\varnothing$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Two-coordinate classification]
\label{cor:two-support-complete-classification}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}$ and identify
$\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}$ with $\{1,2\}$ in the notation
$\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)$ from Definition~\ref{def:atomic-families}. Also put
\[
\mathcal P_i(n):=\{\theta\in\mathcal A_{\{i\},\{i\}}(n):
\theta\text{ is a prime atom}\},
\qquad
\mathcal L_i(n):=\{\theta\in\mathcal A_{\{i\},\{i\}}(n):
\theta\text{ is a ladder atom}\}.
\]
Then $\mathcal M_n$ is the disjoint union of the following two families:
\[
\Atom(n)
\]
and
\[
\{\theta_1\theta_2:\ \theta_i\in
\mathcal A_{\{i\},\{i\}}(n)=\mathcal P_i(n)\cup\mathcal L_i(n),\
\gcd(\theta_1,\theta_2)=1\}.
\]
Equivalently, every non-atomic minimal generator is obtained by choosing,
for each coordinate, one prime or ladder atom born exactly on that
coordinate, with the two chosen prime powers coprime.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$, and write its witness cover as $\Theta$. If
$\#\Theta=1$, then its sole atom has rank $n$, so $m\in\Atom(n)$.

Assume $\#\Theta>1$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound},
$\#\Theta\le2$, hence $\#\Theta=2$. The second assertion of
Lemma~\ref{thm:extremal-coordinate-separation} says, after relabeling the
two factors, that one atom has full support $\{1\}$ and the other has full
support $\{2\}$. Since each atom in a witness cover has a nonempty essential
coordinate not supplied by the other atom, the first has
$E_n=T_n=\{1\}$ and the second has $E_n=T_n=\{2\}$. Thus the two atoms lie in
the displayed families, and pairwise coprimeness is part of the witness-cover
definition. Corollary~\ref{cor:atomic-classification} gives the stated
prime-or-ladder decomposition of each one-coordinate family.

Conversely, a single atom in $\Atom(n)$ is an $n$-witness cover by itself,
because lowering it lowers its rank. If
$\theta_i\in\mathcal A_{\{i\},\{i\}}(n)$ are coprime, then their
$T$-sets cover $\{1,2\}$ and each atom has its own essential coordinate.
Therefore $\{\theta_1,\theta_2\}$ is an $n$-witness cover, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} gives
$\theta_1\theta_2\in\mathcal M_n$. The two displayed families are disjoint
because their elements have respectively one and two prime-power factors.
\end{proof}

We now classify the three-support case. The abstract $\Gamma_i$ record only
the pairs $(E_n(\theta),T_n(\theta))$ up to coordinate relabeling. The lemmas
below exclude the one shape incompatible with Fibonacci prime-power arithmetic,
identify finite exact-rank and ladder slots for the remaining shapes, and apply
the distinct-underlying-prime selection criterion.

\begin{lemma}[Abstract three-coordinate skeletons]\label{lem:abstract-three-types}
Let $X=\{1,2,3\}$. Consider finite collections
\[
\Delta=\{(I_1,J_1),\dots,(I_r,J_r)\},\qquad
\varnothing\ne I_a\subseteq J_a\subseteq X,
\]
up to permutation of $X$ and reordering of the pairs, subject to
\[
\bigcup_a J_a=X,\qquad
I_a\nsubseteq\bigcup_{b\ne a}J_b\quad(1\le a\le r).
\]
Then $r\le3$, and every such abstract three-coordinate witness type is
equivalent to exactly one of the following nine labelled types:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\Gamma_1:\ \{(123,123)\},&
\Gamma_2:\ \{(12,123)\},\\
\Gamma_3:\ \{(1,123)\},&
\Gamma_4:\ \{(12,12),(23,23)\},\\
\Gamma_5:\ \{(1,12),(23,23)\},&
\Gamma_6:\ \{(1,12),(3,23)\},\\
\Gamma_7:\ \{(1,1),(23,23)\},&
\Gamma_8:\ \{(1,1),(2,23)\},\\
\Gamma_9:\ \{(1,1),(2,2),(3,3)\}.
\end{array}
\]
Here, for instance, $123$ denotes $\{1,2,3\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each pair choose
$x_a\in I_a\setminus\bigcup_{b\ne a}J_b$. Then $x_a\in J_a$ and the
$x_a$ are distinct, so $r\le\#X=3$.

If $r=1$, then $J_1=X$. Up to relabeling, $I_1$ has size $3$, $2$, or $1$,
giving $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_2$, and $\Gamma_3$.

If $r=2$, neither $J_1$ nor $J_2$ is contained in the other, because each
has a private coordinate outside the other. Thus the unordered pair
$(J_1,J_2)$ is, up to relabeling, either $(12,23)$ or $(1,23)$. In the
overlapping case $(12,23)$, the first private coordinate is $1$ and the
second is $3$. Hence, again up to reversing the two pairs, the possible
essential sets are $(12,23)$, $(1,23)$, or $(1,3)$, giving
$\Gamma_4$, $\Gamma_5$, and $\Gamma_6$. In the disjoint case $(1,23)$, the
first essential set must be $1$, while the second is either $23$ or a
singleton; these are $\Gamma_7$ and $\Gamma_8$.

If $r=3$, the three chosen private coordinates exhaust $X$. Since each
coordinate is absent from the two $J$-sets to which it is not assigned, no
$J_a$ can contain any coordinate other than its own private coordinate.
Thus the only possibility is
$\{(1,1),(2,2),(3,3)\}$, namely $\Gamma_9$.
The cases are mutually exclusive by $r$, by the intersection pattern of the
$J$-sets, and by the sizes of the $I$-sets after those data are fixed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fibonacci three-coordinate types]\label{lem:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types}
Assume $\omega(n)=3$. Every $n$-witness cover has one of the abstract types
\[
\Gamma_1,\Gamma_3,\Gamma_4,\Gamma_5,\Gamma_6,\Gamma_7,\Gamma_8,\Gamma_9,
\]
and no $n$-witness cover has type $\Gamma_2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Theta$ be an $n$-witness cover. The collection of pairs
\[
\{(E_n(\theta),T_n(\theta)):\theta\in\Theta\}
\]
satisfies the abstract hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:abstract-three-types}; coverage and irredundance are exactly
the last two conditions in Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}. Hence its
type is one of $\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_9$.

By Lemma~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}, a Fibonacci atom has either
$E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$, in the prime-atom case, or
$E_n(\theta)$ empty or a singleton, in the ladder case. In a witness cover
$E_n(\theta)$ cannot be empty. Therefore no atom can have
$1<\#E_n(\theta)<\#T_n(\theta)$. The only abstract type in the displayed
list requiring such an atom is $\Gamma_2$, represented by $(12,123)$, so
$\Gamma_2$ is excluded.

This proves the asserted containment in the eight displayed types and the
exclusion of $\Gamma_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Prime and ladder atom families in three coordinates]
\label{lem:three-coordinate-atom-families}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$ and identify subsets of
$[3]=\{1,2,3\}$ with the corresponding coordinate subsets of
$\mathcal P(n)$. For nonempty $J\subseteq[3]$ define the prime-atom family
\[
P_J(n):=\{\theta\in\mathcal A_{J,J}(n):\theta\text{ is a prime}\},
\]
and, for $i\in J$, define the ladder family
\[
L_{i,J}(n):=\{\theta\in\mathcal A_{\{i\},J}(n):
\theta=p^e\text{ with }e>1\}.
\]
Then, for every nonempty $I\subseteq J\subseteq[3]$,
\[
\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)=
\begin{cases}
P_J(n), & I=J,\ \#J>1,\\[1mm]
P_{\{i\}}(n)\cup L_{i,\{i\}}(n), & I=J=\{i\},\\[1mm]
L_{i,J}(n), & I=\{i\}\subsetneq J,\\[1mm]
\varnothing, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\theta\in\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)$. By definition, $\theta$ is an atom with
$E_n(\theta)=I$ and $T_n(\theta)=J$. If $\theta=p$ is a prime atom, then
$d^-(\theta)=1$, so every full coordinate is lost after lowering; hence
$E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$ and $\theta\in P_J(n)$. Conversely, every
$\theta\in P_J(n)$ has $(E_n(\theta),T_n(\theta))=(J,J)$ by definition.

Now suppose $\theta=p^e$ with $e>1$. The prime--ladder alternative
(Lemma~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}) gives that $E_n(\theta)$ is
either empty or a singleton. Since $\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)$ is defined only for
nonempty $I$, any such atom in it has $I=\{i\}$ for some $i\in J$, and it
lies in $L_{i,J}(n)$. Conversely, every member of $L_{i,J}(n)$ is a ladder
atom with $(E_n(\theta),T_n(\theta))=(\{i\},J)$ by definition.

Combining the prime-atom case with the ladder case gives the four displayed
alternatives. If $\#I>1$ and $I\ne J$, the prime-atom case is impossible
because prime atoms have $I=J$, while the ladder case is impossible
because ladder atoms have singleton essential support. Hence the remaining
families are empty.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite slot-selection test]
\label{lem:finite-slot-selection}
Let $X$ be a set of at most three labelled slots.  For each $x\in X$, let
$C_x$ be a finite set of admissible prime powers.  Define
\[
 \nu(C_x):=\{p:\ p\text{ is the underlying prime of some }p^e\in C_x\}.
\]
There are choices $\theta_x\in C_x$ whose underlying primes are pairwise
distinct if and only if, for every $Y\subseteq X$,
\[
\#\bigcup_{y\in Y}\nu(C_y)\ge \#Y .
\]
Consequently, in the three-coordinate classification below, a row is decided
by the finite bipartite graph whose left vertices are the row slots and whose
right vertices are the admissible underlying primes supplied by the exact-rank
rank windows and the explicit ladder candidates.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Hall's marriage theorem to the finite family of underlying-prime sets
$N_x:=\nu(C_x)$, with the slots as the left vertex class
\cite[Chapter~2]{LovaszPlummer1986MatchingTheory}. Its neighborhood condition
is exactly the displayed inequality, and a matching saturating all slots is
equivalent to choosing representatives with distinct underlying primes.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Arithmetic tests for the three-coordinate atom families]
\label{lem:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$, and write
$a_i:=\nu_{\ell_i}(n)$. For nonempty $J\subseteq[3]$ put
\[
\mathcal R_J(n):=\{d\mid n:\ \{k:\nu_{\ell_k}(d)=a_k\}=J\},
\qquad
\mathcal R_J^\circ(n):=\mathcal R_J(n)\setminus\{1,2,6,12\}.
\]
Use the rank classes $\Pi_\alpha(d)$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive-prime-mobius-count}.
Then
\[
P_J(n)=\bigcup_{d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)}\Pi_\alpha(d),
\]
where a prime $p$ is identified with the prime atom $p$. In particular
$P_J(n)\ne\varnothing$ if and only if $\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)\ne\varnothing$.
More generally, after forbidding a finite set $S$ of primes,
$P_J(n)\setminus S\ne\varnothing$ if and only if there exists
$d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ such that
\[
\#\Pi_\alpha(d)>
\#\{p\in S:\alpha(p)=d\},
\qquad
\#\Pi_\alpha(d)=\sum_{c\mid d}\mu(d/c)\,\omega(F_c).
\]

For the ladder families, define a candidate set $\mathcal L_i^*(n)$ as follows.
If $\ell_i\notin\{2,5\}$, write
$z_i:=\alpha(\ell_i)$, $h_i:=\nu_{\ell_i}(F_{z_i})$, and
$s_i:=\nu_{\ell_i}(z_i)$. Then
\[
\mathcal L_i^*(n):=
\begin{cases}
\{4\}, & \ell_i=2,\ a_i=1,\\
\{2^{a_i+2}\}, & \ell_i=2,\ a_i\ge2,\\
\{5^{a_i}\}, & \ell_i=5,\ a_i\ge2,\\
\varnothing, & \ell_i=5,\ a_i=1,\\
\{\ell_i^{h_i+a_i-s_i}\}, &
   \ell_i\notin\{2,5\}\text{ and }a_i>s_i,\\
\varnothing, &
   \ell_i\notin\{2,5\}\text{ and }a_i\le s_i .
\end{cases}
\]
For each $i\in J$,
\[
L_{i,J}(n)=
\{\lambda\in\mathcal L_i^*(n):\alpha(\lambda)\mid n,\ 
\{k:\nu_{\ell_k}(\alpha(\lambda))=a_k\}=J\}.
\]
Thus every ladder family $L_{i,J}(n)$ is empty or consists of the single
displayed prime power.  Hence every labelled three-coordinate slot has an
explicit finite admissible set: a prime slot is the finite disjoint union
of the rank classes $\Pi_\alpha(d)$ with
$d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$, and a ladder slot is decided by the displayed
single candidate.  Pairwise-coprime filling of any row is therefore exactly
the finite slot-selection test of Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-slot-selection}; the
exact-rank prime multiplicities are
$\#\Pi_\alpha(d)=\sum_{c\mid d}\mu(d/c)\omega(F_c)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $p\in P_J(n)$ and put $d=\alpha(p)$. Since $p$ is a prime atom,
$E_n(p)=T_n(p)$, and the condition $T_n(p)=J$ says exactly that
$d\in\mathcal R_J(n)$. No prime has rank $1$ or $2$, because
$F_1=F_2=1$. Also no prime has rank $6$ or $12$: the prime divisors of
$F_6=8$ and $F_{12}=144$ are $2$ and $3$, with
$\alpha(2)=3$ and $\alpha(3)=4$. Hence $d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$.
Conversely, if $d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ and $p\in\Pi_\alpha(d)$, then
$d\mid n$ and the full-coordinate set of $d$ is $J$; since $p$ is a prime atom,
$p\in P_J(n)$. This proves the displayed identity for $P_J(n)$.

The nonemptiness equivalence is precisely where the Fibonacci primitive-divisor
theorem enters. If $P_J(n)$ is nonempty, the identity just proved gives a rank
$d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$. Conversely, if
$\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ is nonempty and $d$ is any element of it, then by
definition $d\mid n$ and $d\notin\{1,2,6,12\}$. The only possible
exact-rank obstructions are exactly $1,2,6,12$: ranks $1$ and $2$ occur for
no prime, ranks $6$ and $12$ occur for no prime as checked above, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors} supplies a prime $p$ with
$\alpha(p)=d$ for every remaining $d\ge3$. Thus
$\Pi_\alpha(d)$ is nonempty for every $d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$, and
$P_J(n)$ is nonempty exactly when $\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ is nonempty. The
version with a forbidden finite set $S$ is the same rank-by-rank argument after
removing the primes of $S$: some rank $d$ remains fillable exactly when the
number of available primes in $\Pi_\alpha(d)$ exceeds the number of forbidden
primes of that rank. The formula for $\#\Pi_\alpha(d)$ is
Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive-prime-mobius-count}.

It remains to identify the ladder families. Let
$\theta=p^e\in L_{i,J}(n)$. By
Lemma~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}, the essential coordinate of a
ladder atom is the prime coordinate $p$ itself; hence $p=\ell_i$. The
prime-power formulas in Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders} now leave
only one possible exponent once the $\ell_i$-adic coordinate of
$\alpha(p^e)$ is required to equal $a_i$. If $\ell_i=2$, the atomic ladder
levels are $4$ of rank $6$ and $2^e$ of rank $3\cdot2^{e-2}$ for $e\ge4$;
therefore the candidate is $4$ when $a_i=1$ and $2^{a_i+2}$ when $a_i\ge2$.
If $\ell_i=5$, the rank is $5^e$, so a nonprime ladder atom with full
$5$-coordinate $a_i$ exists only for $a_i\ge2$, and then it is $5^{a_i}$.
Finally suppose $\ell_i\notin\{2,5\}$. At atomic ladder levels,
\[
\alpha(\ell_i^e)=\ell_i^{e-h_i}z_i\qquad(e>h_i),
\]
so the full-coordinate equation
$\nu_{\ell_i}(\alpha(\ell_i^e))=a_i$ forces
$e=h_i+a_i-s_i$, and this is an atomic ladder exponent precisely when
$a_i>s_i$.

Thus every member of $L_{i,J}(n)$ lies in $\mathcal L_i^*(n)$. The remaining
conditions for membership in $L_{i,J}(n)$ are exactly that its rank divide
$n$ and that its full-coordinate set be $J$. Conversely, if
$\lambda\in\mathcal L_i^*(n)$ satisfies those two displayed conditions, the
same prime-power formulas show that $\lambda$ is an atomic ladder. Lowering
$\lambda$ reduces only the $\ell_i$-coordinate of its rank, so
$E_n(\lambda)=\{i\}$ and $T_n(\lambda)=J$. Hence
$\lambda\in L_{i,J}(n)$.
The displayed exact-rank windows and ladder candidate sets also exhaust the
exceptional cases: $2$ and $5$ are handled by their separate ladder lines,
the ranks $1,2,6,12$ are removed from exact-rank windows, and any
Wall--Sun--Sun plateau for $p\ne2,5$ is already absorbed in the parameter
$h_i=\nu_{\ell_i}(F_{z_i})$ before the single possible exponent
$h_i+a_i-s_i$ is formed.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Three-coordinate slot construction]
\label{def:three-coordinate-slot-construction}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$ and identify the prime
coordinates with $[3]=\{1,2,3\}$.  For every nonempty
$J\subseteq[3]$ let
\[
P_J(n)=\{\theta\in\mathcal A_{J,J}(n):\theta\text{ is a prime}\}
\]
be the exact-rank prime slot. For $i\in J$ let
\[
L_{i,J}(n)=\{\theta\in\mathcal A_{\{i\},J}(n):
\theta=p^e\text{ with }e>1\}
\]
be the ladder slot with essential coordinate $i$ and full support $J$, and put
\[
S_i(n):=P_{\{i\}}(n)\cup L_{i,\{i\}}(n).
\]
The following eight slot families, together with all coordinate permutations,
are called the admissible three-coordinate rows:
\[
\begin{array}{ccl}
\Gamma_1&:& P_{123},\\
\Gamma_3&:& L_{i,123},\\
\Gamma_4&:& P_{ij}P_{jk},\\
\Gamma_5&:& L_{i,ij}P_{jk},\\
\Gamma_6&:& L_{i,ij}L_{k,jk},\\
\Gamma_7&:& S_iP_{jk},\\
\Gamma_8&:& S_iL_{j,jk},\\
\Gamma_9&:& S_iS_jS_k,
\end{array}
\]
where $(i,j,k)$ ranges over ordered triples of distinct coordinates and braces
are suppressed in subscripts.  A \emph{labelled filling} of one of these rows
means choosing one prime power from each displayed slot after the coordinates
$1,2,3$ have been fixed; it is \emph{coprime} if the chosen prime powers have
distinct underlying primes.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Three-coordinate nonemptiness criterion]
\label{lem:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$ and use the slots and rows of
Definition~\ref{def:three-coordinate-slot-construction}. Fix one labelled
coordinate permutation of one of the eight rows
\[
\Gamma_1,
\Gamma_3,
\Gamma_4,
\Gamma_5,
\Gamma_6,
\Gamma_7,
\Gamma_8,
\Gamma_9.
\]
Let $(I_j,J_j)$ be the essential and full coordinate supports attached to the
$j$th slot of the labelled row. The row is realized by an element of
$\mathcal M_n$ if and only if the following arithmetic selection test succeeds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item for every nonsingleton prime slot $I_j=J_j$, $\#J_j>1$, choose a prime
$\theta_j\in\Pi_\alpha(d_j)$ for some
$d_j\in\mathcal R_{J_j}^\circ(n)$;
\item for every proper ladder slot $I_j=\{i\}\subsetneq J_j$, choose the
unique candidate in $L_{i,J_j}(n)$ given by
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests};
\item for every singleton slot $I_j=J_j=\{i\}$, choose either an exact-rank
prime from $\bigcup_{d\in\mathcal R_{\{i\}}^\circ(n)}\Pi_\alpha(d)$ or the
unique ladder candidate in $L_{i,\{i\}}(n)$;
\item the chosen prime powers have distinct underlying primes.
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently, form for each slot its finite admissible set $C_j$: for a
prime slot use
$\bigcup_{d\in\mathcal R_{J_j}^\circ(n)}\Pi_\alpha(d)$, for a proper ladder
slot use $L_{i,J_j}(n)$, and for a singleton slot use
$P_{\{i\}}(n)\cup L_{i,\{i\}}(n)$.  The row succeeds precisely when these
sets satisfy the Hall inequalities of Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-slot-selection},
with underlying-prime multiplicities in each exact-rank window given by
$\#\Pi_\alpha(d)=\sum_{c\mid d}\mu(d/c)\omega(F_c)$.

In that case $m=\theta_1\cdots\theta_r$ is the realizing minimal generator.
Its labelled atomic factor set
\[
 \{(\theta,E_n(\theta),T_n(\theta)):\theta\text{ is a maximal prime-power
 factor of }m\}
\]
is unique. The associated unlabelled shape is unique only as the $S_3$-orbit
of the labelled support set $\mathsf T(m)$.

Equivalently, the rows in
Definition~\ref{def:three-coordinate-slot-construction} are unlabelled
representatives modulo coordinate relabeling: an unlabelled type $\Gamma_i$ is
realized if and only if the preceding labelled arithmetic selection test
succeeds for one of its coordinate permutations. The excluded type $\Gamma_2$
is never realized.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $m\in\mathcal M_n$ realizes the fixed labelled row. Write its witness cover as
$\Theta=\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\}$ with the factors ordered so that
$(E_n(\theta_j),T_n(\theta_j))=(I_j,J_j)$. By definition
$\theta_j\in\mathcal A_{I_j,J_j}(n)$, and pairwise coprimeness is part of
Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}. Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-atom-families}
places each such factor in the corresponding prime or ladder family, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests} translates those
families into the displayed rank-window and ladder-candidate conditions. Thus
the arithmetic selection test succeeds.

Conversely, assume that the arithmetic selection test succeeds. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests}, each selected
prime power lies in the required prime or ladder family, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-atom-families} gives
$\theta_j\in\mathcal A_{I_j,J_j}(n)$. Distinct underlying primes are exactly
pairwise coprimeness for prime powers. Because the fixed row is an abstract
witness type, the sets $J_j$ cover $[3]$, and for every $j$ one has
$I_j\nsubseteq\bigcup_{h\ne j}J_h$. Translating back to $T_n$ and $E_n$, the
set $\Theta=\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\}$ satisfies the coverage and
irredundance conditions in Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}; coprimeness
gives the remaining condition. Hence $\Theta$ is an $n$-witness cover, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} gives
$m=\prod_j\theta_j\in\mathcal M_n$ with the prescribed row type.

The factorization of $m$ into maximal prime powers is unique, so it recovers
the displayed labelled atomic factor set uniquely. A raw enumeration over all
coordinate permutations may nevertheless repeat a labelled row or a product
when a $\Gamma_i$ has a nontrivial stabilizer. Accordingly, unlabelled shapes
are identified only at the level of $S_3$-orbits, and all enumerated products
are deduplicated. The final exclusion of $\Gamma_2$ is
Lemma~\ref{lem:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Three-coordinate realization]\label{lem:three-coordinate-realization}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$. After the labelled coordinate
supports $E_n,T_n\subseteq\{1,2,3\}$ are fixed, every element of
$\mathcal M_n$ arises from exactly one nonempty arithmetic selection in the
rank windows $\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ and the explicit ladder candidates
$\mathcal L_i^*(n)$ belonging to one fixed labelled coordinate permutation of
one of the eight admissible types in
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}; the selected prime powers
must have distinct underlying primes. The unlabelled $\Gamma$-rows are only
representatives modulo coordinate relabeling.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} together with
Lemmas~\ref{lem:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types} and
\ref{lem:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}.
\end{proof}

is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:three-coordinate-checklist}; it is used below
only to justify that the displayed table is an effective closed classification.

\begin{theorem}[Complete low-support classification: eight support-three families]
\label{thm:low-support-classification-package}

\item If $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}$, then $\mathcal M_n$ is the
 disjoint union of $\Atom(n)$ and the products
 $\theta_1\theta_2$ with

\begin{corollary}[Support-three obstruction certificates]
\label{cor:support-three-obstruction-certificates}
Assume $\omega(n)=3$ and let a proposed support-three minimal-generator shape be
specified by an irredundant finite list of atomic slots whose full-support sets
cover the labelled coordinates $\{1,2,3\}$.  Exactly one of the following
alternatives occurs.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item The shape is the excluded skeleton $\Gamma_2$, equivalently it requires an
atom with $1<\#E_n(\theta)<\#T_n(\theta)=3$; then no Fibonacci minimal
generator has that shape.
\item After relabeling it is one of the eight rows in
Table~\ref{tab:three-coordinate-classification}.  The row contributes exactly
the products obtained from the finite candidate sets
$\mathfrak C(P_J)$, $\mathfrak C(L_{i,J})$, and $\mathfrak C(S_i)$ by choosing
one prime power in each row slot with distinct underlying primes.
\item The row is one of the eight admissible skeletons but the finite
distinct-prime Hall inequalities of Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-slot-selection} fail;
then the row contributes no element of $\mathcal M_n$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus every failure in support three has a certificate of one of two kinds: the
single structural Fibonacci obstruction $\Gamma_2$, or a finite Hall deficiency
inside an otherwise admissible slot row.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:abstract-three-types}, every irredundant three-coordinate
witness skeleton is one of $\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_9$. The prime--ladder
dichotomy, Lemma~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}, excludes precisely the
case in which an atom has a proper nonsingleton essential set inside a larger
full-support set; among the nine skeletons this is exactly $\Gamma_2$, proving
(a).

For every remaining skeleton, Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-atom-families}
translates each abstract slot into one of the prime slots $P_J(n)$, ladder
slots $L_{i,J}(n)$, or singleton slots $S_i(n)$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests}
identifies these as the finite candidate sets displayed in
Theorem~\ref{thm:low-support-classification-package}.  A selected row therefore
forms a witness cover exactly when its chosen prime powers are pairwise
coprime, and for prime powers this is exactly distinctness of underlying primes.
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-slot-selection} is precisely the Hall criterion for such a
selection.  If the criterion holds, Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification}
turns the selected witness cover into the corresponding element of
$\mathcal M_n$; if it fails, no coprime filling of that row exists.  These are
(b) and (c), and the alternatives exhaust all support-three possibilities.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Low-support birth layers and counting]
\label{cor:low-support-normal-form}
\label{cor:low-support-birth-layer-package}
\label{prop:low-support-cardinality-formula}
Let $n\ge2$ with $\omega(n)\le3$, and let $\mathcal G_n$ be the finite
minimal-generator set described in
Theorem~\ref{thm:low-support-classification-package}.  Then $\mathcal G_n$ is
the canonical antichain of minimal elements of $B_n$, and for every $q\in D_n$,
\[
q\in B_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{there exists }g\in\mathcal G_n\text{ with }g\mid q.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{g\in\mathcal G_n}\{q\in D_n:\ g\mid q\},
\]
where the union is taken inside the divisor lattice $D_n$ and is not asserted
to be disjoint.  The birth-layer cardinality is the closed finite formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:low-support-cardinality-formula}
A(n)=
\sum_{\varnothing\ne\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal G_n}
(-1)^{|\mathcal S|+1}
\tau\left(\frac{F_n}{\lcmop(g:g\in\mathcal S)}\right),
\end{equation}
with the convention that the empty sum is $0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:low-support-classification-package} gives
$\mathcal G_n=\mathcal M_n$ for $\omega(n)\le3$, including the boundary
convention $\mathcal G_2=\varnothing=\mathcal M_2$. Minimal elements of an
upper set are pairwise incomparable, so $\mathcal G_n$ is an antichain in
$D_n$.  The upper-fiber identity, Lemma~\ref{thm:upper-fiber}, gives for every
$n\ge2$ the principal-upper-ideal decomposition
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal M_n}\{q\in D_n:m\mid q\}.
\]
Replacing $\mathcal M_n$ by $\mathcal G_n$ gives the displayed union and the
membership criterion.  The minimal elements of this union are exactly the
generators in $\mathcal G_n$, because no two elements of $\mathcal G_n$ are
comparable.

It remains to count the union.  If $\mathcal G_n=\varnothing$, then
$B_n=\varnothing$, so $A(n)=0$ and the empty-sum convention gives
\eqref{eq:low-support-cardinality-formula}.  Otherwise, for $g\in\mathcal G_n$
put $U_g:=\{q\in D_n:g\mid q\}$.  For a nonempty subset
$\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal G_n$, a divisor $q$ of $F_n$ lies in
$\bigcap_{g\in\mathcal S}U_g$ if and only if
$\lcmop(g:g\in\mathcal S)$ divides $q$.  Since every $g\in\mathcal G_n$ divides
$F_n$, this least common multiple also divides $F_n$, and division by it gives a
bijection
\[
\bigcap_{g\in\mathcal S}U_g
\longleftrightarrow
\operatorname{Div}\left(F_n/\lcmop(g:g\in\mathcal S)\right).
\]
Hence
\[
\#\bigcap_{g\in\mathcal S}U_g=
\tau\left(\frac{F_n}{\lcmop(g:g\in\mathcal S)}\right).
\]
Ordinary finite inclusion--exclusion applied to
$B_n=\bigcup_{g\in\mathcal G_n}U_g$ proves
\eqref{eq:low-support-cardinality-formula}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Prime-only three-coordinate skeleton]
\label{cor:primitive-three-skeleton}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$ and that
$n_S:=\prod_{i\in S}\ell_i^{\nu_{\ell_i}(n)}$ satisfies
$n_S\ge 3$ and $n_S\notin\{6,12\}$ for every nonempty
$S\subseteq[3]$. Then the four prime-only three-coordinate
types $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_4$, $\Gamma_7$, and $\Gamma_9$ are realized in
$\mathcal M_n$. More precisely, after choosing exact-rank primes
$\pi_S$ with $\alpha(\pi_S)=n_S$, the following minimal generators realize
these types:
\[
\pi_{123},\qquad
\pi_{12}\pi_{23},\qquad
\pi_1\pi_{23},\qquad
\pi_1\pi_2\pi_3 .
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The hypothesis and Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors} provide the indicated
exact-rank primes. An exact-rank prime for $n_S$ is a prime atom with
$E_n(\pi_S)=T_n(\pi_S)=S$. The four displayed collections of coordinate
sets are precisely the prime-only types $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_4$,
$\Gamma_7$, and $\Gamma_9$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:abstract-three-types}, and
the chosen primes are pairwise distinct. The
pairwise-coprime selection criterion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} therefore gives the four
minimal generators.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:wss}
The only Wall--Sun--Sun input needed here is local.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders}, for $p\neq 2,5$ one has
$\alpha(p^2)=\alpha(p)$ if and only if $\nu_p(F_{\alpha(p)})\ge 2$, the
Wall--Sun--Sun condition for $p$ \cite{SunSun1992FibonacciFLT}.  Thus the
condition merely determines whether the first odd-prime ladder step is delayed;
we do not use any distributional assertion about such primes.
\end{remark}

\section{Connected block factorizations}\label{sec:connected}
%% ====================================================================

The hypergraph decomposition below is a generic consequence of strong
divisibility and disjoint coordinate supports. Its role here is structural:
the substantive endpoint for the present fibers is the induced factorization
of minimal elements and the associated partition formulas.

Throughout this section, write
$n_C:=\prod_{\ell\in C}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n)}$ for nonempty
$C\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:support-hypergraph}
Let $q=\prod_i p_i^{e_i}\in D_n$, and put
$\lambda_i:=\alpha(p_i^{e_i})$. The \emph{support hypergraph} $H_n(q)$ has
vertex set $\mathcal P(n)$ and one edge
\[
\operatorname{supp}_n(\lambda_i):=
\{\ell\in\mathcal P(n):\nu_\ell(\lambda_i)>0\}
\]
for each nonempty such support. We call $q$ \emph{connected} if
$H_n(q)$ is connected on its vertex set. Let
\[
B_n^{\mathrm{conn}}:=\{q\in B_n:\ H_n(q)\text{ is connected}\},\qquad
A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n):=\#B_n^{\mathrm{conn}},
\]
and
\[
\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{conn}}:=
\{m\in\mathcal M_n:\ H_n(m)\text{ is connected}\},\qquad
M^{\mathrm{conn}}(n):=\#\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{conn}}.
\]
For a connected component $C$ of $H_n(q)$, define $q_C$ to be the product of
those prime-power factors $p_i^{e_i}$ whose support edge lies in $C$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Disjoint-block coprimality]\label{thm:coprime-embedding}
Let $\Pi$ be a set partition of $\mathcal P(n)$. For each block
$C\in\Pi$, choose $q_C\in D_{n_C}$. Then the integers $q_C$ are pairwise
coprime. If in addition $q_C\in B_{n_C}$ for every $C$, then
\[
q:=\prod_{C\in\Pi}q_C
\]
lies in $B_n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $C\ne C'$, then $\gcd(n_C,n_{C'})=1$, so
\[
\gcd(F_{n_C},F_{n_{C'}})=F_{\gcd(n_C,n_{C'})}=F_1=1
\]
by Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}. Since $q_C\mid F_{n_C}$ and
$q_{C'}\mid F_{n_{C'}}$, the chosen factors are pairwise coprime.
Equivalently, any common prime divisor $p$ would satisfy
$\alpha(p)\mid n_C$ and $\alpha(p)\mid n_{C'}$, hence
$\alpha(p)\mid\gcd(n_C,n_{C'})=1$; this is impossible for a prime, and in
particular excludes the shared special prime $5$ and any shared exact-rank prime
across disjoint coordinate blocks.

When $q_C\in B_{n_C}$ for every $C$, we have $\alpha(q_C)=n_C$. Repeated use
of the lcm formula in Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics} gives
\[
\alpha\!\left(\prod_{C\in\Pi}q_C\right)
=\lcmop(n_C:\ C\in\Pi)=\prod_{C\in\Pi}n_C=n.
\]
Also $q_C\mid F_{n_C}\mid F_n$, so the pairwise coprime product divides
$F_n$. Hence $q\in B_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:birth-connected-factorization}
Every $q\in B_n$ has a unique factorization
\[
q=\prod_{C\in\pi(q)}q_C
\]
indexed by the connected components $\pi(q)$ of $H_n(q)$, where
$q_C\in B_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$ for every component $C$. Conversely, if
$\Pi$ is a set partition of $\mathcal P(n)$ and
$q_C\in B_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$ for every $C\in\Pi$, then
$\prod_C q_C\in B_n$ and its connected components are exactly the blocks of
$\Pi$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition-formula}
A(n)=\sum_{\Pi\in\mathsf{Part}(\mathcal P(n))}
\prod_{C\in\Pi}A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n_C),
\end{equation}
with the convention that an empty factor contributes zero whenever
$B_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}=\varnothing$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $q=\prod_i p_i^{e_i}\in B_n$ and let $C$ be a component of $H_n(q)$.
Every prime-power factor contributing to $q_C$ has rank support contained in
$C$, so $\alpha(q_C)$ divides $n_C$. Conversely, fix $\ell\in C$. Since
$\alpha(q)=n$ is the lcm of the ranks $\alpha(p_i^{e_i})$, some factor of
$q$ has $\ell$-adic rank exponent equal to $\nu_\ell(n)$. Its support edge
contains $\ell$, and therefore belongs to the component $C$. Thus the same
factor occurs in $q_C$, and the $\ell$-adic exponent of $\alpha(q_C)$ is
$\nu_\ell(n)$. This holds for every $\ell\in C$, while no coordinate outside
$C$ occurs in $\alpha(q_C)$; hence $\alpha(q_C)=n_C$ and
$q_C\in B_{n_C}$. The component of $H_{n_C}(q_C)$ on the vertex set $C$ is
connected by construction, so $q_C\in B_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$.

Uniqueness follows from unique prime-power factorization of $q$ and the
unique connected-component partition of a hypergraph. The converse is exactly
Lemma~\ref{thm:coprime-embedding}; because each $q_C$ is connected on its
own block and no edge of $H_n(\prod_C q_C)$ meets two different blocks, the
components are precisely the blocks of $\Pi$.

Counting the converse construction over all set partitions gives
\eqref{eq:partition-formula}, and uniqueness shows that no element is counted
twice.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mingenerator-connected-factorization}
Every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ has a unique factorization
\[
m=\prod_{C\in\pi(m)}m_C
\]
over the connected components of $H_n(m)$, with
$m_C\in\mathcal M_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$ for every $C$. Conversely, if
$\Pi$ is a set partition of $\mathcal P(n)$ and
$m_C\in\mathcal M_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$ for every $C\in\Pi$, then
$\prod_C m_C\in\mathcal M_n$ and has component partition $\Pi$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimal-partition-formula}
\#\mathcal M_n=
\sum_{\Pi\in\mathsf{Part}(\mathcal P(n))}
\prod_{C\in\Pi}M^{\mathrm{conn}}(n_C).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$. Theorem~\ref{thm:birth-connected-factorization}
gives $m_C\in B_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$ for each component. If some
$s_C\in B_{n_C}$ satisfied $s_C\mid m_C$ and $s_C<m_C$, then
\[
s_C\prod_{D\ne C}m_D
\]
would divide $m$. The factors are pairwise coprime by
Lemma~\ref{thm:coprime-embedding}, and the displayed product lies in
$B_n$ by the same theorem. This contradicts the minimality of $m$ in $B_n$.
Therefore $m_C\in\mathcal M_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$.

Conversely, let $m=\prod_{C\in\Pi}m_C$ with
$m_C\in\mathcal M_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$. Lemma~\ref{thm:coprime-embedding}
puts $m$ in $B_n$. If $s\mid m$ and $s\in B_n$, write
$s=\prod_C s_C$ with $s_C\mid m_C$; pairwise coprimeness of the block
factors makes this decomposition unique. Since
\[
n=\alpha(s)=\lcmop(\alpha(s_C):C\in\Pi)
\]
and $\alpha(s_C)$ divides $n_C$, the disjointness of the coordinate sets
forces $\alpha(s_C)=n_C$ for every $C$. Thus $s_C\in B_{n_C}$ for every
$C$, and minimality of $m_C$ gives $s_C=m_C$. Hence $s=m$, proving
$m\in\mathcal M_n$. The component statement and uniqueness are the same as
in Theorem~\ref{thm:birth-connected-factorization}. Counting unique
decompositions yields \eqref{eq:minimal-partition-formula}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Connected inversion]\label{cor:connected-inversion}
The connected birth counts are determined from the total counts by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:connected-inversion}
A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n)=
\sum_{\Pi\in\mathsf{Part}(\mathcal P(n))}
(-1)^{|\Pi|-1}(|\Pi|-1)!
\prod_{C\in\Pi}A(n_C).
\end{equation}
The same inversion holds for minimal generators:
\[
M^{\mathrm{conn}}(n)=
\sum_{\Pi\in\mathsf{Part}(\mathcal P(n))}
(-1)^{|\Pi|-1}(|\Pi|-1)!
\prod_{C\in\Pi}\#\mathcal M_{n_C}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:partition-formula} is the ordinary exponential formula on
the finite set $\mathcal P(n)$ with block weight
$C\mapsto A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n_C)$. M\"obius inversion in the partition lattice
\cite[Section~3.7]{Stanley2012EnumerativeCombinatoricsI}
gives
\[
\mu_{\mathsf{Part}}(\Pi,\hat1)=(-1)^{|\Pi|-1}(|\Pi|-1)!,
\]
where $\hat1$ is the partition containing all coordinates in one block.
Applying the same partition-lattice inversion to
\eqref{eq:minimal-partition-formula} gives the minimal-generator formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:four-support-reduction}
If $\omega(n)=4$, every element of $B_n$ and every element of
$\mathcal M_n$ is obtained uniquely by choosing connected blocks whose sizes
form one of the set-partition types
\[
4,\qquad 3+1,\qquad 2+2,\qquad 2+1+1,\qquad 1+1+1+1.
\]
Consequently, after the one-, two-, and three-coordinate classifications are
known, the only data not reduced by this corollary are the connected
four-coordinate sets $B_n^{\mathrm{conn}}$ and
$\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{conn}}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is Theorems~\ref{thm:birth-connected-factorization} and
\ref{thm:mingenerator-connected-factorization} specialized to the set
$\mathcal P(n)$ of size four. Every disconnected partition has all blocks of
size at most three, so its factors are controlled by the previously proved
one-, two-, and three-coordinate classifications. The only partition with a
block of size four is the single-block partition, which is precisely the
connected four-coordinate case.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Symbolic support patterns through the four-coordinate reduction]
\label{ex:symbolic-support-patterns}
Identify the prime-coordinate set with $[k]$ and write
$\mathcal A_{I,J}$ for $\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)$. The following table gives a
compact reading of the witness-cover rules in small support. Each displayed
product is to be retained exactly when the indicated slots are nonempty and the

\[
\begin{array}{ccl}
\toprule

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:odd-primitive-cover-realization}
Let $n\ge3$ be odd. For each nonempty $S\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$ put
\[
n_S=\prod_{\ell\in S}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n)}.
\]
Choose an exact-rank prime $\pi_S$ with $\alpha(\pi_S)=n_S$, whose existence is
guaranteed by Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors}. Then every set partition
$\Pi$ of $\mathcal P(n)$ gives a minimal generator
\[
m_\Pi:=\prod_{C\in\Pi}\pi_C\in\mathcal M_n.
\]
Thus
\[
\#\mathcal M_n\ge \mathsf B_{\omega(n)},
\]
and, since the minimal generators form an antichain in the divisor lattice,
\[
\widthop(B_n)\ge \#\mathcal M_n\ge \mathsf B_{\omega(n)},
\]
where $\mathsf B_k$ is the $k$th Bell number.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $n$ is odd and $n\ge3$, every nonempty $n_S$ is odd and at least $3$;
therefore $n_S\notin\{6,12\}$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors}
supplies the required exact-rank prime $\pi_S$. For each block $C$, the prime
$\pi_C$ is a prime atom of rank $n_C$ and
therefore belongs to $\mathcal M_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$: its support
hypergraph has the single edge $C$. Distinct blocks have coprime Fibonacci
ambient factors by Lemma~\ref{thm:coprime-embedding}, so the primes
$\pi_C$ are pairwise coprime. Theorem~\ref{thm:mingenerator-connected-factorization}
then gives $m_\Pi\in\mathcal M_n$.

The connected-component partition of $m_\Pi$ is exactly $\Pi$, so different
set partitions give different minimal generators. There are
$\mathsf B_{\omega(n)}$ set partitions of $\mathcal P(n)$.
Finally, no two distinct elements of $\mathcal M_n$ are comparable under
divisibility: if $m,m'\in\mathcal M_n$ and $m\mid m'$ with $m\ne m'$, then
$m'$ is not minimal in the upper set $B_n$. Hence $\mathcal M_n$ is an
antichain in $B_n$, so $\widthop(B_n)\ge\#\mathcal M_n$.
\end{proof}

\section{Support entropy and the unresolved arithmetic multiplicity}
\label{sec:growth}

Write $\Omega(N)=\sum_p\nu_p(N)$ for the number of prime factors of $N$
counted with multiplicity. Set $R(2)=1$, consistently with
$\mathcal M_2=\varnothing$. For $n\ge3$ define the maximal atomic rank-window
multiplicity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-window-multiplicity}
 R(n):=\max\!\left\{1,
 \max_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq J\subseteq\mathcal P(n)}
       \#\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)\right\}.
\end{equation}
By the prime--ladder alternative, the nonsingleton families counted here are the
exact-rank windows $P_J(n)$, whereas every ladder family contributes at most
one atom. Thus $R(n)$ records the maximal atomic-family multiplicity retained
by the counting bounds below. It is an exact parameter for those bounds, but
it is not asserted to be an information-theoretically complete invariant of
the arithmetic outside the support combinatorics.

We first remove the contribution caused solely by collecting several exact
rank classes in one support window. For $d\mid n$, write
\[
 T_n(d):=\{\ell\in\mathcal P(n):
 \nu_\ell(d)=\nu_\ell(n)\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Mean rank-window deaggregation]
\label{thm:mean-rank-window-deaggregation}
Put
\[
 a(d):=\#\Pi_\alpha(d),\qquad
 \mathcal D^+(n):=\{d\mid n:T_n(d)\ne\varnothing\},
\]
and
\[
 A^*(n):=\max\left\{1,\max_{d\in\mathcal D^+(n)}a(d)\right\}.
\]
Then, as $x\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-window-deaggregation}
 \sum_{3\le n\le x}\omega(n)\log R(n)
 =\sum_{3\le n\le x}\omega(n)\log A^*(n)
 +O(x\log\log x).
\end{equation}
Consequently the mean sparse-window hypothesis \textup{(H2)} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:visible-single-rank-interface}
 \sum_{3\le n\le x}\omega(n)
 \log\max\left\{1,
 \max_{\substack{d\mid n\\T_n(d)\ne\varnothing}}
 \#\Pi_\alpha(d)\right\}
 =o\bigl(x(\log\log x)^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For nonempty $J\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$, let
\[
 W_J(n):=\{d\mid n:T_n(d)=J\}.
\]
If $n=\prod_\ell\ell^{e_\ell}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support-window-size}
 \#W_J(n)=\prod_{\ell\notin J}e_\ell.
\end{equation}
The prime atoms in the family with essential and full supports $(J,J)$ form
the disjoint union
\[
 \coprod_{d\in W_J(n)}\Pi_\alpha(d),
\]
because a prime has a unique rank of apparition. By
Lemma~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}, every remaining effective atomic
family is a ladder family with singleton essential support. Once that
essential coordinate $\ell$ is fixed, the underlying prime is $\ell$, and
the full-exponent equation in Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders}
determines at most one ladder exponent. Therefore, with
\[
 P(n):=\max_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\mathcal P(n)}
       \sum_{d\in W_J(n)}a(d),
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-window-versus-R}
 P(n)\le R(n)\le P(n)+1.
\end{equation}
Every $d\in\mathcal D^+(n)$ belongs to one of the windows above. Hence
\[
 A^*(n)\le R(n)
 \le1+A^*(n)\max_J\#W_J(n)
 \le1+A^*(n)\prod_{\ell^{e_\ell}\parallel n}e_\ell.
\]
Writing
\[
 h(n):=\sum_{\ell^{e_\ell}\parallel n}\log e_\ell,
\]
and using $A^*(n)\ge1$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pointwise-window-deaggregation}
 0\le\log R(n)-\log A^*(n)\le\log2+h(n).
\end{equation}
For each exponent $e\ge2$, $\log e\le\sum_{2\le j\le e}\log j$, so
\[
 h(n)\le\sum_{\substack{p^j\mid n\\j\ge2}}\log j.
\]
The classical mean estimate \eqref{eq:mean-omega-input}, used uniformly with
$\log\log(3y)$ in place of $\log\log y$ for small $y$, yields
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)h(n)
 &\le\sum_{\substack{p^j\le x\\j\ge2}}\log j
       \sum_{m\le x/p^j}\omega(p^jm)\\
 &\le\sum_{\substack{p^j\le x\\j\ge2}}\log j
       \sum_{m\le x/p^j}(1+\omega(m))\\
 &\ll x\log\log x\sum_p\sum_{j\ge2}\frac{\log j}{p^j}
 \ll x\log\log x.
\end{align*}
The double series converges absolutely. Summing
\eqref{eq:pointwise-window-deaggregation} and using
$\sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)\ll x\log\log x$ proves
\eqref{eq:mean-window-deaggregation}; the stated equivalence follows because
$x\log\log x=o(x(\log\log x)^2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Single-rank sufficient and necessary interfaces]
\label{cor:single-rank-mean-interfaces}
A sufficient condition for \textup{(H2)} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-rank-sufficient-condition}
 \sum_{d\le x}
 \frac{(\omega(d)+\log\log x)\log\max\{1,a(d)\}}{d}
 =o\bigl((\log\log x)^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
Conversely, \textup{(H2)} implies the diagonal necessary condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-rank-necessary-condition}
 \sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)\log\max\{1,a(n)\}
 =o\bigl(x(\log\log x)^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since
\[
 \log A^*(n)\le\sum_{d\mid n}\log\max\{1,a(d)\},
\]
the substitution $n=dm$ and $\omega(dm)\le\omega(d)+\omega(m)$ gives
\[
 \sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)\log A^*(n)
 \ll x\sum_{d\le x}
 \frac{(\omega(d)+\log\log x)\log\max\{1,a(d)\}}{d}.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:single-rank-sufficient-condition} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:mean-rank-window-deaggregation} imply \textup{(H2)}. For the
necessary condition, $n\in\mathcal D^+(n)$, and hence
$R(n)\ge A^*(n)\ge\max\{1,a(n)\}$; summation proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fibotomic rank entropy]
\label{thm:fibotomic-rank-entropy}
Let $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, let $\phi_{\rm E}$ denote Euler's totient
function, and, for $d\geq3$, put
\[
 \mathfrak F_d:=\prod_{r\mid d}F_r^{\mu(d/r)}.
\]
For $k\geq0$, set $m=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ and
\[
 \mathcal H_d(k):=
 k\log\frac{2d}{3}+2\log(m!)+(k-2m)\log(m+1).
\]
There is an absolute constant $C_F$ such that, for every $d\geq3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibotomic-rank-entropy}
 \mathcal H_d(a(d))\leq \phi_{\rm E}(d)\log\varphi+C_F.
\end{equation}
More generally, every finitely supported family of nonnegative real numbers
$(\lambda_d)_{d\geq3}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-fibotomic-rank-entropy}
 \sum_{d\geq3}\lambda_d\mathcal H_d(a(d))
 \leq (\log\varphi)\sum_{d\geq3}\lambda_d\phi_{\rm E}(d)
       +C_F\sum_{d\geq3}\lambda_d.
\end{equation}
Uniformly as $d\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pointwise-exact-rank-bound}
 a(d)\leq
 \left(\frac{\log\varphi}{2}+o(1)\right)
 \frac{\phi_{\rm E}(d)}{\log d}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, uniformly as $n\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:visible-exact-rank-bound}
 A^*(n)\leq
 \left(\frac{\log\varphi}{2}+o(1)\right)
 \frac{\phi_{\rm E}(n)}{\log n}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Psi_d(x)$ be the $d$th fibotomic polynomial. The fibotomic
factorization of Byer--Dvorachek--Eckard--Harrington--Wise--Wong
\cite{CameronCountsLundMillerPiechnikWong2022Fibotomic} gives
\[
 F_d(x)=\prod_{r\mid d}\Psi_r(x),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak F_d=\Psi_d(1)\in\mathbb N.
\]
This published factorization is used as an input and is not asserted as a
result of the present paper.
Binet's formula, in the form
\[
 F_r=\frac{\varphi^r}{\sqrt5}
 \bigl(1-(-\varphi^{-2})^r\bigr),
\]
and the identities
$\sum_{r\mid d}\mu(d/r)=0$ and
$\sum_{r\mid d}r\mu(d/r)=\phi_{\rm E}(d)$ show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibotomic-binet-error}
 \log\mathfrak F_d=\phi_{\rm E}(d)\log\varphi+E_d,
 \qquad
 |E_d|\leq
 C_F:=\sum_{j\geq1}
 \left|\log\bigl(1-(-\varphi^{-2})^j\bigr)\right|.
\end{equation}
The series defining $C_F$ converges geometrically.

If $p\in\Pi_\alpha(d)$, then $p\mid F_d$ but $p\nmid F_r$ for every
proper divisor $r$ of $d$. Since
$F_d=\prod_{r\mid d}\mathfrak F_r$ and each factor is an integer, $p$ must
divide $\mathfrak F_d$. Distinct exact-rank primes are coprime, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-rank-radical-divides-fibotomic}
 \prod_{p\in\Pi_\alpha(d)}p\mid\mathfrak F_d.
\end{equation}
For $p\notin\{2,5\}$, the classical rank congruence gives
\[
 d=\alpha(p)\mid p-\left(\frac5p\right),
\]
see \cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm,Vinson1963RankApparition}. Thus
$p\equiv\pm1\pmod d$. The exceptional ranks $d=3,5$ have respectively
the single exact-rank primes $2,5$ and satisfy the estimate directly. For
every other $d$, write the exact-rank primes in increasing order as
$p_1<\cdots<p_k$. There are at most two candidates in each pair of residue
classes $jd\pm1$, whence
\[
 p_i\geq d\left\lceil\frac i2\right\rceil-1
 \geq\frac{2d}{3}\left\lceil\frac i2\right\rceil.
\]
It follows that, with $m=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-congruence-product-bound}
 \prod_{i=1}^kp_i\geq
 \left(\frac{2d}{3}\right)^k(m!)^2(m+1)^{k-2m}.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:fibotomic-binet-error}--
\eqref{eq:rank-congruence-product-bound} and taking logarithms proves
\eqref{eq:fibotomic-rank-entropy}. Multiplication by $\lambda_d\geq0$ and
finite summation proves \eqref{eq:weighted-fibotomic-rank-entropy}.

Stirling's lower bound applied in \eqref{eq:fibotomic-rank-entropy} gives,
with an absolute implied constant,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-entropy-coarse}
 a(d)\bigl(\log d+\log\max\{1,a(d)\}-O(1)\bigr)
 \leq \phi_{\rm E}(d)\log\varphi+O(1).
\end{equation}
If $a(d)\leq\phi_{\rm E}(d)/(\log d)^2$, then
\eqref{eq:pointwise-exact-rank-bound} follows immediately. Otherwise the
standard uniform lower bound
$\phi_{\rm E}(d)\gg d/\log\log(3d)$
\cite{HardyWright2008Introduction} implies
\[
 \log a(d)=\log d+O(\log\log d)=\log d+o(\log d).
\]
Substitution in \eqref{eq:rank-entropy-coarse} yields
$(2+o(1))a(d)\log d\leq\phi_{\rm E}(d)\log\varphi$, proving
\eqref{eq:pointwise-exact-rank-bound} uniformly.

For \eqref{eq:visible-exact-rank-bound}, the case $A^*(n)=1$ follows from
$\phi_{\rm E}(n)/\log n\to\infty$. Otherwise choose
$d_n\in\mathcal D^+(n)$ with $A^*(n)=a(d_n)$. The preceding estimate has
the coarse consequence $a(d)\ll d/\log(2d)$. If
$d_n\leq n/(\log n)^3$, the lower bound for $\phi_{\rm E}(n)$ gives
\[
 a(d_n)=o\left(\frac{\phi_{\rm E}(n)}{\log n}\right).
\]
If $d_n>n/(\log n)^3$, then
$\log d_n=(1+o(1))\log n$ and, since $d_n\mid n$,
$\phi_{\rm E}(d_n)\leq\phi_{\rm E}(n)$. Applying
\eqref{eq:pointwise-exact-rank-bound} at $d_n$ completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bounds for the visible single-rank contribution]
\label{cor:visible-rank-sum-bounds}
Put
\[
 S(x):=\sum_{3\leq n\leq x}\omega(n)\log A^*(n).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:visible-rank-sum-upper}
 S(x)\leq
 \sum_{3\leq n\leq x}\omega(n)\log\phi_{\rm E}(n)
 -x(\log\log x)^2+O(x\log\log x),
\end{equation}
whereas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:visible-rank-sum-lower}
 S(x)\geq
 \left(\frac{2\log2}{25}+o(1)\right)x\log\log x.
\end{equation}
Neither estimate settles \eqref{eq:visible-single-rank-interface}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Taking logarithms in \eqref{eq:visible-exact-rank-bound}, uniformly for
large $n$, gives
\[
 \log A^*(n)\leq
 \log\phi_{\rm E}(n)-\log\log n
 +\log\frac{\log\varphi}{2}+o(1).
\]
In addition to \eqref{eq:mean-omega-input}, partial summation gives
\[
 \sum_{3\leq n\leq x}\omega(n)\log\log n
 =x(\log\log x)^2+O(x\log\log x).
\]
For example, on $\sqrt x<n\leq x$ one may replace $\log\log n$ by
$\log\log x$ with $O(1)$ error, while the complementary range contributes
$o(x)$. Summation proves \eqref{eq:visible-rank-sum-upper}.

For the lower bound, Jarden proved that $L_{5p}$ has at least two distinct
primitive prime divisors for every prime $p>5$
\cite{Jarden1968LucasTwoPrimitive}; this published theorem is used as an
input and is not asserted as a result of the present paper. If $q$ is one of
them, then
$q\mid L_{5p}$ and
\[
 F_{10p}=F_{5p}L_{5p},\qquad \gcd(F_{5p},L_{5p})\mid2.
\]
Here $q$ is odd, so the gcd identity gives $q\nmid F_{5p}$. Thus
$\alpha(q)=2r$ for some $r\mid5p$. If $r<5p$, then
$F_{2r}=F_rL_r$ and $q\nmid F_r$, so $q\mid L_r$, contrary to the
primitivity of $q$ for $L_{5p}$.
Consequently $\alpha(q)=10p$, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jarden-exact-rank-consequence}
 a(10p)\geq2\qquad(p>5\ \hbox{prime}).
\end{equation}

Let $n=10m$, where $5\nmid m$ and $m$ has a prime factor $p>5$. Then
$d=10p$ divides $n$ and
$\nu_5(d)=\nu_5(n)=1$, so $T_n(d)\ne\varnothing$. By
\eqref{eq:jarden-exact-rank-consequence}, $A^*(n)\geq2$. The excluded
integers $m$ having no prime factor greater than $5$ are of the form
$2^a3^b$ and contribute only $O((\log x)^3)$ after weighting by $\omega$.
Writing $y=x/10$ and interchanging two finite sums gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{m\leq y\\5\nmid m}}\omega(m)
 =\sum_{\substack{q\leq y\\q\ \mathrm{prime},\ q\ne5}}
 \left(\left\lfloor\frac yq\right\rfloor
 -\left\lfloor\frac y{5q}\right\rfloor\right)
 =\frac45y\log\log y+O(y).
\]
Since $\omega(10m)=\omega(m)+O(1)$, the total $\omega$-weight of the
constructed integers is
$(2/25)x\log\log x+O(x)$. Multiplication by $\log2$ proves
\eqref{eq:visible-rank-sum-lower}.
\end{proof}


For a finite set $V$, let $\operatorname{MinCov}(V)$ denote the set of
minimal, or irredundant, covers of $V$ by nonempty subsets:
\[
 \operatorname{MinCov}(V):=
 \left\{\mathcal C\subseteq 2^V\setminus\{\varnothing\}:
 \bigcup_{S\in\mathcal C}S=V,\quad
 S\nsubseteq\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal C\setminus\{S\}}T
 \ \text{for every }S\in\mathcal C\right\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Rank-pure universality]\label{thm:rank-pure-universality}
Let $n\ge3$, put $V=\mathcal P(n)$, and, for every nonempty $S\subseteq V$,
define
\[
 n_S:=\prod_{\ell\in S}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n)},\qquad
 a_n(S):=\#\Pi_\alpha(n_S),
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal E_n:=\{S\subseteq V:S\ne\varnothing,\ n_S\in\{2,6,12\}\}.
\]
Then $\#\mathcal E_n\le2$, and every member of $\mathcal E_n$ has cardinality
at most two. Define the rank-pure prime sector
\[
 \mathcal R_n^{\rm rp}:=
 \left\{
 \prod_{S\in\mathcal C}p_S:
 \begin{array}{l}
  \mathcal C\in\operatorname{MinCov}(V),\quad
  \mathcal C\cap\mathcal E_n=\varnothing,\\
  p_S\in\Pi_\alpha(n_S)\quad(S\in\mathcal C)
 \end{array}
 \right\}.
\]
Then $\mathcal R_n^{\rm rp}\subseteq\mathcal M_n$, the displayed representation is
unique, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-pure-weighted-sector}
 \#\mathcal R_n^{\rm rp}=
 \sum_{\substack{\mathcal C\in\operatorname{MinCov}(V)\\
                   \mathcal C\cap\mathcal E_n=\varnothing}}
 \prod_{S\in\mathcal C}a_n(S),
 \qquad
 a_n(S)=\sum_{d\mid n_S}\mu(n_S/d)\,\omega(F_d).
\end{equation}
Moreover, the support hypergraph of the product attached to $\mathcal C$ has
edge set exactly $\mathcal C$. In particular, connected minimal covers map to
$\mathcal M_n^{\rm conn}$, with their complete hypergraph structure preserved.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The map $S\mapsto n_S$ is injective. If $n_S=2$, then
$S=\{2\}$ and $\nu_2(n)=1$. If $n_S=6$, then
$S=\{2,3\}$ and $\nu_2(n)=\nu_3(n)=1$; if $n_S=12$, then
$S=\{2,3\}$, $\nu_2(n)=2$, and $\nu_3(n)=1$. Thus the ranks $6$ and $12$
cannot both occur, while rank $2$ can coexist only with rank $6$. This proves
the assertions about $\mathcal E_n$.

Fix $\mathcal C\in\operatorname{MinCov}(V)$ avoiding $\mathcal E_n$. For
every $S\in\mathcal C$, the integer $n_S$ is at least $3$ and is not $6$ or
$12$. Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors} therefore gives a prime
$p_S\in\Pi_\alpha(n_S)$. Distinct supports have distinct ranks, so the primes
$p_S$ are distinct. Since $p_S$ is a prime atom and $d^-(p_S)=1$,
\[
 E_n(p_S)=T_n(p_S)=S.
\]
Consequently the coverage and private-coordinate conditions defining
$\operatorname{MinCov}(V)$ are exactly the witness-cover conditions for
$\{p_S:S\in\mathcal C\}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} places
$\prod_{S\in\mathcal C}p_S$ in $\mathcal M_n$.

Unique factorization recovers every selected prime from the product; its rank
$\alpha(p_S)=n_S$ then recovers $S$. Hence both the cover and all prime choices
are recovered, proving injectivity and the weighted count in
\eqref{eq:rank-pure-weighted-sector}. The formula for $a_n(S)$ is
Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive-prime-mobius-count}. Finally,
\[
 \operatorname{supp}_n(\alpha(p_S))=
 \operatorname{supp}_n(n_S)=S,
\]
so Definition~\ref{def:support-hypergraph} gives precisely the edge set
$\mathcal C$ and therefore preserves connected components.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Odd-layer cover universality]
\label{cor:odd-rank-pure-universality}
Let $n\ge3$ be odd and put $k=\omega(n)$. Then $\mathcal E_n=\varnothing$.
After fixing one prime $\pi_S\in\Pi_\alpha(n_S)$ for every nonempty
$S\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$, the map
\[
 \mathcal C\longmapsto\prod_{S\in\mathcal C}\pi_S
\]
embeds $\operatorname{MinCov}(\mathcal P(n))$ into $\mathcal M_n$ and
preserves the support hypergraph. Hence, if $C_k$ denotes the number of minimal
covers of a labelled $k$-set, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-minimal-cover-bound}
 \#\mathcal M_n\ge C_k,
 \qquad
 \widthop(B_n)\ge C_k.
\end{equation}
Through Royle's correspondence
\cite{Royle2000SetCoversSplitGraphs}, every isomorphism type of split graph on
$k$ vertices consequently has a rank-pure Fibonacci realization on the prime
coordinates of $n$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every nonempty $n_S$ is odd and at least $3$, so no support is exceptional.
Theorem~\ref{thm:rank-pure-universality} gives the embedding and support
preservation. Minimal generators form an antichain, which proves the width
bound. Royle's theorem is a correspondence between isomorphism types of split
graphs and minimal covers; choosing a labelling of a representative cover and
then applying the embedding gives the final assertion.
\end{proof}

We next fix the classical enumeration input, without claiming it as a result of
this paper. Let $C_{k,s}$ count the minimal covers of a labelled $k$-set having
$s$ members, put $C_k=\sum_s C_{k,s}$, and put
\[
 b_k:=\sum_{s=0}^k\binom{k}{s}2^{s(k-s)}.
\]
Hearne and Wagner \cite{HearneWagner1973MinimalCovers} proved
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hearne-wagner}
 C_{k,s}=\sum_{j=s}^k\binom{k}{j}
 \genfrac{\{}{\}}{0pt}{}{j}{s}
 (2^s-s-1)^{k-j}.
\end{equation}
The central-sum asymptotic occurring in the labelled split-graph enumeration of
Bender--Richmond--Wormald \cite{BenderRichmondWormald1985ChordalSplit}, in the
form recorded also by Troyka \cite[Section~3]{Troyka2019SplitGraphs}, gives,
with
\[
 \vartheta_0:=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}2^{-r^2},\qquad
 \vartheta_1:=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}2^{-(r+1/2)^2},\qquad
 \varepsilon\equiv k\pmod2,
\]
the published asymptotic
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimal-cover-theta}
 C_k\sim b_k\sim
 \vartheta_\varepsilon\binom{k}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}2^{k^2/4}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $b_{k-1}/b_k=O(2^{-k/2})$.

\begin{lemma}[Connected minimal-cover concentration]
\label{lem:connected-minimal-cover-concentration}
If $C_k^{\rm conn}$ denotes the number of connected minimal covers, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:connected-minimal-cover-asymptotic}
 C_k-C_k^{\rm conn}=O(kC_{k-1})=o(C_k).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Connected components of a minimal cover are themselves minimal covers, so the
labelled exponential formula gives
\[
 C_k=\sum_{j=1}^k\binom{k-1}{j-1}C_j^{\rm conn}C_{k-j}.
\]
The terms $j=1$ and $j=k-1$ contribute $O(kC_{k-1})$. Choosing the least
private element in each cover member gives $C_j\le b_j\le2^j2^{j^2/4}$.
For the remaining terms, use this bound and the central lower bound for
$C_{k-1}$ furnished by the $s=\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor$ term of
\eqref{eq:hearne-wagner}. After division by $kC_{k-1}$, the term indexed by
$j$ is
\[
 \ll \binom{k}{j}
 2^{-(j-1)(k-j-1)/2}\qquad(2\le j\le k-2).
\]
The sum of these bounds is bounded (and in fact tends to zero), by symmetry
and separation into $2\le j\le k/2$ and its reflection. This proves the first
estimate in \eqref{eq:connected-minimal-cover-asymptotic}; the second equality
follows from \eqref{eq:minimal-cover-theta}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Theta-refined connected support entropy]
\label{cor:theta-refined-connected-entropy}
Uniformly along every sequence of integers for which $k=\omega(n)\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-connected-lower-bound}
 \#\mathcal M_n^{\rm conn}\ge
 (1-o(1))\,
 \vartheta_\varepsilon\binom{k}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}2^{k^2/4},
 \qquad \varepsilon\equiv k\pmod2.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-connected-log-lower-bound}
 \log\#\mathcal M_n^{\rm conn}\ge
 \frac{\log2}{4}k^2+k\log2-\frac12\log k
 +\log\vartheta_\varepsilon+\frac12\log\frac2\pi+o(1).
\end{equation}
The same lower bounds hold with $\mathcal M_n$ in place of
$\mathcal M_n^{\rm conn}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix an exceptional support $S_0\in\mathcal E_n$. If a minimal cover contains
$S_0$, choose a canonical private vertex $x\in S_0$ of that member. Every
other member avoids $x$ and is recovered from its canonical private vertex and
an arbitrary subset of the remaining nonprivate vertices. The number of such
encodings is at most $b_{k-1}$ for each choice of $x$. Therefore the number of
minimal covers meeting $\mathcal E_n$ is at most
\[
 \sum_{S_0\in\mathcal E_n}|S_0|b_{k-1}\le4b_{k-1}=o(C_k).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:connected-minimal-cover-concentration} also shows that only
$o(C_k)$ minimal covers are disconnected. Thus $(1-o(1))C_k$ covers are simultaneously
connected and disjoint from $\mathcal E_n$. Theorem
\ref{thm:rank-pure-universality} embeds them in
$\mathcal M_n^{\rm conn}$, proving \eqref{eq:theta-connected-lower-bound}.
Taking logarithms and using
\[
 \log\binom{k}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}
 =k\log2-\frac12\log k+\frac12\log\frac2\pi+o(1)
\]
gives \eqref{eq:theta-connected-log-lower-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Discrete local limit in a fixed-prime rank-pure sector]
\label{cor:rank-pure-local-limit}
Let $(n_j)$ be any sequence with $k_j=\omega(n_j)\to\infty$. For every
$j$ and every nonexceptional nonempty support
$S\subseteq\mathcal P(n_j)$, fix one prime
$\pi_{j,S}\in\Pi_\alpha((n_j)_S)$. Let $\mathcal Q_{n_j}^{\rm rp}$ consist of the
products $\prod_{S\in\mathcal C}\pi_{j,S}$ over all
$\mathcal C\in\operatorname{MinCov}(\mathcal P(n_j))$ disjoint from
$\mathcal E_{n_j}$, and choose $m$ uniformly from $\mathcal Q_{n_j}^{\rm rp}$. Put
$W_j=\omega(m)$. Along the subsequence $k_j=2q_j$, for every fixed
$d\in\mathbb Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:even-rank-pure-local-limit}
 \Pr(W_j=q_j+d)\longrightarrow\frac{2^{-d^2}}{\vartheta_0}.
\end{equation}
Along the subsequence $k_j=2q_j+1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-rank-pure-local-limit}
 \Pr(W_j=q_j+d)\longrightarrow
 \frac{2^{-(d-1/2)^2}}{\vartheta_1}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $W_j=k_j/2+O_{\mathbb P}(1)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Unique factorization and distinct support ranks show that $W_j$ is the number
of members of the corresponding cover. By the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:theta-refined-connected-entropy}, deleting covers meeting
$\mathcal E_{n_j}$ changes the uniform distribution in total variation by
$o(1)$. For $s=\lfloor k/2\rfloor+d$ with fixed $d$, the $j=s$ term of
\eqref{eq:hearne-wagner} and the canonical-private-element encoding give
\[
 \binom{k}{s}(2^s-s-1)^{k-s}
 \le C_{k,s}\le \binom{k}{s}2^{s(k-s)},
 \qquad
 C_{k,s}\sim\binom{k}{s}2^{s(k-s)}.
\]
Dividing this by \eqref{eq:minimal-cover-theta} gives
\eqref{eq:even-rank-pure-local-limit} and
\eqref{eq:odd-rank-pure-local-limit}. The quadratic limiting tails are
summable, which is equivalent to tightness on the integer scale.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:connected-lower-not-dominance}
Corollary~\ref{cor:theta-refined-connected-entropy} proves that connected
minimal generators alone realize the theta-refined lower bound supplied by the
unweighted minimal-cover count in the fixed-choice rank-pure sector, including
its linear and logarithmic corrections. It does not compare the total connected
and disconnected parts of $\mathcal M_n$, nor does it assert that this sector
exhausts the consequences of fibotomic information. Such a comparison would
require upper control of the arithmetic multiplicities in $R(n)$ and is not
presently available.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Combinatorial support entropy]
\label{thm:minimal-generator-growth}
For every integer $n\ge3$, put $k=\omega(n)$ and
$L(n)=\log\#\mathcal M_n$. Then $\mathcal M_n$ is nonempty. If $k\ge3$ and
$r=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:private-cover-finite-bounds}
 (2^r-1)^{k-r}
 \le \#\mathcal M_n
 \le \sum_{s=1}^{k}\binom{k}{s}(2R(n))^s2^{s(k-s)}.
\end{equation}
In particular, uniformly along every sequence for which $\omega(n)\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:private-cover-entropy-order}
 \frac{\log2}{4}\,\omega(n)^2-O(1)
 \le L(n)
 \le \frac{\log2}{4}\,\omega(n)^2
      +\omega(n)\log R(n)+O(\omega(n)).
\end{equation}
The coefficient $\log2/4$ is the exact quadratic coefficient of the
multiplicity-free minimal-cover contribution: the lower construction embeds
the unweighted cover count, and the private-coordinate encoding gives the same
quadratic support term in the upper bound. This is not an asymptotic formula
for $L(n)$ unless $R(n)=\exp(o(\omega(n)))$; no such unconditional estimate
for $R(n)$ is proved here. In particular, the displayed coefficient is not
claimed to be optimal among all deductions from fibotomic or exact-rank
arithmetic information.

For comparison, the crude support-count estimate gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:finite-minimal-generator-bound}
 \#\mathcal M_n
 \le \sum_{r=0}^{k}\binom{\Omega(F_n)}{r}
 \le n^k.
\end{equation}
Combining only the last inequality with the classical maximal order of
$\omega(n)$ gives the crude corollary
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:crude-maximal-order}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{L(n)\log\log n}{(\log n)^2}\le1.
\end{equation}
This envelope has scale $(\log n)^2/\log\log n$, a factor $\log\log n$
above the witness-cover entropy scale; it is not a consequence of
\eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order}.
At the coarser divisor-lattice scale, the antichain property and Wigert's
theorem give the Sperner-type envelope
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sperner-wigert-envelope}
 L(n)\le\log\tau(F_n)
 \le (\log2\,\log\varphi+o(1))\frac{n}{\log n},
 \qquad \varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2};
\end{equation}
in fact \eqref{eq:crude-maximal-order} is stronger and implies
$L(n)=o(n/\log n)$.

Moreover, as $x\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mean-logarithmic-growth}
 \left(\frac{\log2}{4}+o(1)\right)x(\log\log x)^2
 \le \sum_{3\le n\le x}L(n)
 \le (1+o(1))x\log x\,\log\log x.
\end{equation}

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First, $F_n\in B_n$ for every $n\ge3$.  Indeed,
$F_n\mid F_d$ implies $F_n\le F_d$, and strict increase of the Fibonacci
sequence from index $2$ onward forces $d\ge n$; hence $\alpha(F_n)=n$.
Because $B_n$ is a nonempty finite poset, it has a minimal element, so
$\mathcal M_n\ne\varnothing$ and $L(n)$ is defined.

We first prove the explicit finite private-cover bound in
\eqref{eq:private-cover-finite-bounds}. Fix $k\ge3$, and give
$\mathcal P(n)$ an arbitrary order. Put
\[
 X=\mathcal P(n)\cap\{2,3\},\qquad r=\lfloor k/2\rfloor.
\]
Since $k-\#X\ge k-2\ge r$, choose an $r$-element set
$P\subseteq\mathcal P(n)\setminus X$, and put
$Q=\mathcal P(n)\setminus P$. For every map
\[
 H:Q\longrightarrow 2^P\setminus\{\varnothing\}
\]
and every $i\in P$, define
\[
 T_i=\{i\}\cup\{q\in Q:i\in H(q)\}.
\]
The index $n_{T_i}=\prod_{\ell\in T_i}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n)}$ has a prime
divisor outside $\{2,3\}$, because $i\in P$. Hence $n_{T_i}\ge5$ and
$n_{T_i}\notin\{6,12\}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors} supplies a
prime $\pi_{T_i}$ with $\alpha(\pi_{T_i})=n_{T_i}$; fix one such prime for
each support that occurs.

The supports $T_i$ cover $\mathcal P(n)$ because $H(q)$ is nonempty for every
$q\in Q$. Moreover, $i$ belongs to $T_i$ and to no $T_j$ with $j\ne i$.
Each $\pi_{T_i}$ is a prime atom, so
$E_n(\pi_{T_i})=T_n(\pi_{T_i})=T_i$. Thus
$\{\pi_{T_i}:i\in P\}$ is an $n$-witness cover. Distinct supports have
distinct ranks and therefore distinct underlying primes. If two maps $H$
differ, then their support families differ, since the edge containing the
private coordinate $i$ identifies $T_i$; unique factorization then gives
different minimal generators. There are
$(2^r-1)^{\#Q}=(2^r-1)^{k-r}$ maps $H$, proving the lower bound in
\eqref{eq:private-cover-finite-bounds}.

For the upper bound, let $\Theta$ be an $n$-witness cover with $s$ atoms. For
each $\theta\in\Theta$, choose the least coordinate
\[
 x_\theta\in E_n(\theta)\setminus
 \bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta\setminus\{\theta\}}T_n(\eta).
\]
These private coordinates are distinct; write
$P=\{x_\theta:\theta\in\Theta\}$ and
$Q=\mathcal P(n)\setminus P$. If $x_\theta=x$, then
\[
 T_n(\theta)=\{x\}\cup U_\theta,\qquad U_\theta\subseteq Q,
\]
because every other member of $P$ is private to another atom. By
Lemma~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}, either
$E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$ or $E_n(\theta)=\{x\}$. Hence there are at most two
support-pair alternatives, and the definition
\eqref{eq:rank-window-multiplicity} allows at most $R(n)$ atoms for each.
Thus, once $P$ is fixed, the atom indexed by each
$x\in P$ has at most $2R(n)2^{k-s}$ choices. Ignoring coverage and
coprimeness only enlarges the count, so summing over the
$\binom{k}{s}$ possible sets $P$ and over $1\le s\le k$ proves the upper
bound in \eqref{eq:private-cover-finite-bounds}.

Now $r(k-r)=\lfloor k^2/4\rfloor$ and
$(k-r)\log(1-2^{-r})=O(1)$. Hence the logarithm of the lower bound is
$(\log2)k^2/4+O(1)$. For the upper bound, use
$s(k-s)\le k^2/4$, $s\le k$, and
$\sum_s\binom{k}{s}\le2^k$ to obtain
\[
 \log\sum_{s=1}^{k}\binom{k}{s}(2R(n))^s2^{s(k-s)}
 \le\frac{\log2}{4}k^2+k\log R(n)+O(k).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order}.

Let
\[
 \mathcal Q_n=\{p^e:p\mid F_n,\ 1\le e\le\nu_p(F_n)\}.
\]
This set has cardinality $\Omega(F_n)$.  By unique factorization, the map
\[
 m\longmapsto\{p^{\nu_p(m)}:p\mid m\}
\]
is injective from $\mathcal M_n$ into the subsets of $\mathcal Q_n$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound} says that every image has at most
$k=\omega(n)$ members.  This proves the first inequality in
\eqref{eq:finite-minimal-generator-bound}.

Since every prime factor of $F_n$ is at least $2$,
$2^{\Omega(F_n)}\le F_n$.  Also $F_n<2^{n-1}$ for $n\ge3$, by the elementary
induction $F_{j+1}=F_j+F_{j-1}<2^j$.  Hence
$\Omega(F_n)\le n-2$.  A subset of an $N$-element set having at most $k$
members can be encoded injectively by its increasing list padded to length
$k$ with a new symbol; therefore
\[
 \sum_{r=0}^k\binom Nr\le(N+1)^k.
\]
Taking $N=\Omega(F_n)$ gives the second inequality in
\eqref{eq:finite-minimal-generator-bound}.

The classical maximal-order formula for $\omega(n)$
\cite[Theorem~22.11]{HardyWright2008Introduction}
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{\omega(n)\log\log n}{\log n}=1
\]
now combines with $L(n)\le\omega(n)\log n$ to prove
\eqref{eq:crude-maximal-order}. This argument uses only the crude estimate
$\#\mathcal M_n\le n^{\omega(n)}$, not the witness-cover entropy bound. On
the other hand,
$\mathcal M_n$ is an antichain in the divisor lattice of $F_n$, so
\[
 \#\mathcal M_n\le\widthop(\operatorname{Div}(F_n))\le\tau(F_n).
\]
Wigert's maximal-order theorem \cite{Wigert1907Divisors} states that
\[
 \log\tau(N)\le(\log2+o(1))\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}.
\]
Binet's formula gives $\log F_n=n\log\varphi+O(1)$ and
$\log\log F_n=\log n+O(1)$, yielding
\eqref{eq:sperner-wigert-envelope}.  Finally,
$(\log n)^2/\log\log n=o(n/\log n)$, so
\eqref{eq:crude-maximal-order} gives the stated strict improvement.

For the mean upper bound, use the classical mean-value estimate
\cite{HardyRamanujan1917NormalPrimeFactors}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mean-omega-input}
 \sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)=x\log\log x+O(x),
\end{equation}
together with $L(n)\le\omega(n)\log n$.  Uniformly for $n\le x$,
$\log n\le\log x$, and hence
\[
 \sum_{3\le n\le x}L(n)
 \le\log x\sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)
 =(1+o(1))x\log x\,\log\log x.
\]

Tur\'an's second-moment form of the Hardy--Ramanujan theorem
\cite{Turan1934HardyRamanujan} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega-second-moment}
 \sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)^2=(1+o(1))x(\log\log x)^2.
\end{equation}
After enlarging the absolute constant in the lower half of
\eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order}, it holds as
$L(n)\ge(\log2)\omega(n)^2/4-O(1)$ also for $\omega(n)<3$.
Summation and \eqref{eq:omega-second-moment} prove the lower half of
\eqref{eq:mean-logarithmic-growth} and complete the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Known baseline for the arithmetic interface]
\label{lem:R-baseline}
For every $n\ge3$,
\[
 R(n)\le 1+\omega(F_n)=O(n),
 \qquad
 \log R(n)\le\log n+O(1).
\]
Moreover, Stroi\'nski's cumulative estimate gives
\[
 \limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log x}{x^2}
 \#\{p:\alpha(p)\le x\}
 \le \frac{3\log\varphi}{2\pi^2}.
\]
Neither estimate implies the conjectures below.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every prime atom in a family $\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)$ divides $F_n$, and a fixed
ladder family contains at most one atom. Thus a family contains at most all
distinct prime divisors of $F_n$ together with one ladder atom, proving
$R(n)\le1+\omega(F_n)$. Since
$2^{\omega(F_n)}\le\operatorname{rad}(F_n)\le F_n$ and
$\log F_n=n\log\varphi+O(1)$, this gives $R(n)=O(n)$ and the logarithmic
bound. The cumulative estimate is Stroi\'nski's Theorem~7
\cite{Stroinski2016AlphaContraction}.

Quantitatively, the normal-order conjecture below asks for
$\log R(n)=o(\log\log n)$ for almost all $n$, whereas the elementary estimate
gives only $O(\log n)$. The cumulative estimate controls all primes of rank at
most $x$ together and likewise gives at best a logarithmic bound of order
$\log n$ for a single window. For the mean conjecture, the elementary bound
and the mean order of $\omega$ give only
$O(x\log x\log\log x)$, not $o(x(\log\log x)^2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Cumulative rank estimates do not imply \textup{(H2)}]
\label{rem:cumulative-rank-estimates-insufficient}
This nonimplication can be made quantitative without asserting that the model
below is realized by Fibonacci ranks. Fix $0<c<\log\varphi/2$ and put
\[
 a_0(d):=\max\left\{1,
 \left\lfloor\frac{c\phi_{\rm E}(d)}{\log(2d)}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\]
Elementary integral comparison gives
\[
 \sum_{d\le y}a_0(d)\ll\frac{y^2}{\log y},\qquad
 a_0(d)\ll1+\frac{\phi_{\rm E}(d)}{\log(2d)},\qquad
 \sum_{d\le y}a_0(d)\log d\ll y^2.
\]
Thus this formal multiplicity sequence has the cumulative, pointwise, and
logarithmic-mass scales supplied by the presently known estimates, including
Theorem~\ref{thm:fibotomic-rank-entropy}. It is not asserted to arise from
Fibonacci exact-rank primes, and therefore it neither proves sharpness of the
fibotomic inequality nor supplies a realizable extremal model for it. On the
other hand, the standard
lower bound for Euler's function gives
\[
 \log a_0(n)=\log n-\log\log n+O(\log\log\log(3n))
\]
for large $n$.
Partial summation in \eqref{eq:mean-omega-input} therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)\log a_0(n)
 \sim x\log x\log\log x,
\]
which is larger than the scale in \textup{(H2)}. Hence cumulative exact-rank
counts alone cannot establish the required visible-divisor maximum estimate.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Unconditional lower growth]
\label{cor:unconditional-lower-growth}
For almost all $n$,
\[
 \log\#\mathcal M_n\ge
 \left(\frac{\log2}{4}+o(1)\right)(\log\log n)^2.
\]
If $N_y:=\prod_{p\le y}p$, then
\[
 \log\#\mathcal M_{N_y}\ge
 \left(\frac{\log2}{4}+o(1)\right)
 \frac{(\log N_y)^2}{(\log\log N_y)^2}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The Hardy--Ramanujan normal-order theorem states that
$\omega(n)=(1+o(1))\log\log n$ for almost all $n$
\cite{HardyRamanujan1917NormalPrimeFactors,%
Tenenbaum2015AnalyticProbabilistic}. Substitution in the lower half of
\eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order} proves the first assertion. The prime
number theorem gives
$\omega(N_y)=\pi(y)\sim y/\log y\sim\log N_y/\log\log N_y$; substituting this
in the same lower bound proves the second assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[Normal rank-window sparsity]\label{hyp:normal-window}
For every $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 \#\{n\le x:\log R(n)>\varepsilon\omega(n)\}=o(x).
\tag{H1}
\]
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Mean sparse-window hypothesis]\label{hyp:mean-window}
One has
\[
 \sum_{3\le n\le x}\omega(n)\log R(n)
 =o\bigl(x(\log\log x)^2\bigr).
\tag{H2}
\]
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional obstruction from Fibonacci prime divisors]
\label{prop:blms-obstructs-mean-window}
Assume Conjecture~5.1 of Bugeaud--Luca--Mignotte--Siksek
\cite{BugeaudLucaMignotteSiksek2005FewPrimeDivisors}, namely that
$\omega(F_n)\gg\log n$ for composite $n$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blms-mean-window-obstruction}
 \liminf_{x\to\infty}
 \frac{\sum_{3\le n\le x}\omega(n)\log R(n)}
 {x(\log\log x)^2}
 \ge\frac6{\pi^2}(1-\log2)>0.
\end{equation}
In particular, under that published conjecture, the mean sparse-window
hypothesis \textup{(H2)} is false.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $n$ be squarefree and put $k=\omega(n)$. The exact-rank partition of the
prime divisors of $F_n$ gives
\[
 \omega(F_n)=\sum_{d\mid n}a(d).
\]
For squarefree $n$, every nonempty support $J\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$ has the
single rank $d=\prod_{\ell\in J}\ell$ in its window. Since $a(1)=0$, the
pigeonhole principle and \eqref{eq:prime-window-versus-R} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:squarefree-blms-pigeonhole}
 R(n)\ge\max_{d\mid n}a(d)
 \ge\frac{\omega(F_n)}{2^k-1}.
\end{equation}
Under the stated conjecture, for every sufficiently large squarefree composite
$n$,
\[
 \log R(n)\ge\log\log n-k\log2+O(1).
\]
The Hardy--Ramanujan theorem has an exceptional set of $o(x)$ integers, so on
all but $o(x)$ squarefree integers one has
$k=(1+o(1))\log\log n$. Consequently
\[
 \omega(n)\log R(n)
 \ge(1-\log2+o(1))(\log\log n)^2
\]
on this set. Squarefree integers have density $6/\pi^2$, and squarefree prime
indices have density zero. Summation over squarefree composite indices proves
\eqref{eq:blms-mean-window-obstruction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conditional normal and mean orders]
\label{cor:conditional-orders}
Assuming Conjecture~\ref{hyp:normal-window},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-normal-order}
 L(n)\sim\frac{\log2}{4}(\log\log n)^2
 \quad\text{for almost all }n.
\end{equation}
Assuming Conjecture~\ref{hyp:mean-window},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-mean-order}
 \sum_{3\le n\le x}L(n)
 \sim\frac{\log2}{4}x(\log\log x)^2.
\end{equation}
Neither conclusion is unconditional.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For the first assertion, intersect the density-one set in
Conjecture~\ref{hyp:normal-window} with the Hardy--Ramanujan normal-order set.
Then the error $\omega(n)\log R(n)+O(\omega(n))$ in
\eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order} is $o(\omega(n)^2)$. For the second,
sum the upper bound in \eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order}, use
Conjecture~\ref{hyp:mean-window}, \eqref{eq:omega-second-moment}, and
$\sum_{n\le x}\omega(n)=O(x\log\log x)$, and compare with the unconditional
lower mean bound \eqref{eq:mean-logarithmic-growth}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact residual arithmetic interface]
\label{rem:exact-average-open-interface}
Corollary~\ref{cor:theta-refined-connected-entropy} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generator-growth} determine the contribution of the
multiplicity-free minimal-cover model used here: $\log2/4$ is its exact
quadratic coefficient, the fixed-choice rank-pure sector supplies its
theta-refined lower terms, and the private-coordinate upper bound retains the
term $\omega(n)\log R(n)$. The rank partition used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests} applies verbatim to every
support size: prime-atom families are finite disjoint unions of the rank
classes $\Pi_\alpha(d)$, while ladder families are singletons. Thus
Conjectures~\ref{hyp:normal-window} and
\ref{hyp:mean-window} ask precisely for upper distributional control of
exact-rank prime multiplicities in those rank windows.

The pointwise estimate \eqref{eq:pointwise-exact-rank-bound} proves only the
consequence obtained by combining the fibotomic size identity with the
divisibility of the exact-rank radical and the congruences
$p\equiv\pm1\pmod d$. Remark
\ref{rem:cumulative-rank-estimates-insufficient} shows that even its
Euler-factor scale does not by itself imply either rank-window conjecture. The
manuscript does not prove that these inputs exhaust the information available
from fibotomic arithmetic, or that
\eqref{eq:fibotomic-rank-entropy} is optimal within any information class. Such
an optimality theorem would require, at minimum, a precise axiom class of
admissible information and a realizable extremal model satisfying those
axioms. Neither is supplied here. Whether stronger unconditional dispersion or
concentration consequences follow from fibotomic arithmetic is therefore an
open interface.

The closest distribution theorems do not close this interface.  In the
notation of a nondegenerate Lucas sequence $U$ with nonsquare discriminant
$\Delta$ and squarefree discriminant part $\Delta_0$, Sanna's Theorem~1.1
\cite{Sanna2022RankDivisibility} gives, unconditionally, an asymptotic formula
for
\[
 \#\{p\le x:d\mid\rho_U(p)\}
\]
when $d$ is odd, with $3\nmid d$ if $\Delta_0=-3$, and
$x>\exp(Be^{8\omega(d)}d^8)$.  The condition counted is
$d\mid\rho_U(p)$, not the exact-rank condition $\rho_U(p)=d$; for example,
$3\mid\alpha(17)=9$ although $\alpha(17)\ne3$.  Sanna's Theorem~1.1 on the
index of appearance \cite{Sanna2024IndexAppearance} instead assumes GRH and,
for a fixed positive integer $t$, counts primes having
$\iota_U(p)=t$ uniformly for $x>t^3$.  In the Fibonacci case,
$\alpha(p)=d$ and $\iota_F(p)=t$ imply
\[
 p=td+\left(\frac5p\right)=td\pm1.
\]
Taking $x$ on the scale of this candidate prime restricts that theorem to
$t^2\ll d$, and its cumulative fixed-index count still does not isolate the
single rank $d$.  Thus neither result controls the growing exact fibers
$\Pi_\alpha(d)$ required in \eqref{eq:visible-single-rank-interface}.

The primitive-divisor theorems of Carmichael and Bilu--Hanrot--Voutier give
existence in the Fibonacci setting. Granville proves an odd-multiplicity
primitive-factor theorem for a class of second-order Lucas recurrences with
$c\equiv2\pmod4$ and explicitly leaves the odd-$b,c$ case, including the
Fibonacci recurrence $b=c=1$, outside that theorem
\cite{Granville2012PrimitivePrimeFactors}. Thus it is a neighboring
Lucas-sequence result, not a Fibonacci multiplicity input here. Kiss treats
related Lucas-sequence primitive-divisor questions
\cite{Kiss1988PrimitiveDivisors}. Hong proves that, for each fixed positive
integer $\kappa$ and all sufficiently large $n$ with an explicit threshold,
$F_n$ has a primitive divisor at least $(\kappa+1)n-1$
\cite{Hong2025BigPrimitiveDivisors}. These results concern existence, parity of
one primitive factor's multiplicity in the stated Lucas class, or the size of
one Fibonacci primitive divisor; they do not provide the required average
dispersion or critical concentration estimates for Fibonacci exact-rank prime
multiplicities in visible divisor windows. The
Hardy--Ramanujan and
Tur\'an theorems control the additive function $\omega(n)$ but not the prime
factorization of $F_d$ across exact apparition ranks. Likewise, the
Erd\H{o}s--Wintner theorem \cite{ErdosWintner1939Additive} concerns limiting
distributions of additive arithmetic functions, whereas $L(n)$ and $R(n)$
are not additive. No implication from RH or ERH to either rank-window
conjecture is presently known. Accordingly, the normal and mean equivalents
\eqref{eq:conditional-normal-order} and
\eqref{eq:conditional-mean-order} are conditional; the unconditional
statements are \eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order} and
\eqref{eq:mean-logarithmic-growth}, together with the stronger connected lower
bound \eqref{eq:theta-connected-log-lower-bound}. None of these estimates
implies that the disconnected part of $\mathcal M_n$ is negligible; the
strictly stronger blockwise hypothesis needed for that conclusion is isolated
in Theorem~\ref{thm:blockwise-connected-dominance} below.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Refined private-coordinate upper bound]
\label{lem:refined-private-coordinate-upper-bound}
For $m\ge2$, put $s=\omega(m)$ and $r=R(m)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refined-private-coordinate-upper-bound}
 \#\mathcal M_m\le U_s(r),\qquad
 U_s(r):=\sum_{t=1}^s\binom st
             \bigl(r2^{s-t}+1\bigr)^t.
\end{equation}
There is an absolute constant $K$ such that, for all $s,r\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refined-upper-versus-covers}
 U_s(r)\le Kr^sC_s.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The case $m=2$ is immediate from $\mathcal M_2=\varnothing$ and $R(2)=1$.
Suppose $m\ge3$, and let a witness cover have $t$ atoms. Choose the canonical
private coordinate of each atom as in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generator-growth}; let $P$ be the resulting
$t$-set and $Q$ its complement. For a fixed $x\in P$, a prime atom has full
support $\{x\}\cup U$ for some $U\subseteq Q$, giving at most
$r2^{s-t}$ choices. A ladder atom with essential coordinate $x$ must be a
power of the index prime $x$, and the full-exponent equation in
Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders} determines at most one such atom
over all its possible full supports. Thus the atom assigned to $x$ has at most
$r2^{s-t}+1$ choices. Summing over $P$ and $t$ proves
\eqref{eq:refined-private-coordinate-upper-bound}; ignoring coverage and
coprimeness only enlarges the count.

For $r\ge1$,
\[
 U_s(r)\le r^sU_s(1).
\]
If $t\le3s/4$, then, uniformly in this range,
\[
 (2^{s-t}+1)^t
 =2^{t(s-t)}(1+2^{-(s-t)})^t
 =(1+o(1))2^{t(s-t)}.
\]
The sum over $t>3s/4$ is
$2^{-s^2/16+O(s)}$ times a central term of
$b_s=\sum_t\binom st2^{t(s-t)}$. Hence $U_s(1)\sim b_s$. The published
asymptotic \eqref{eq:minimal-cover-theta} gives $C_s\sim b_s$; enlarging a
constant to cover the finitely many small values of $s$ proves
\eqref{eq:refined-upper-versus-covers}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Blockwise criterion for connected dominance]
\label{thm:blockwise-connected-dominance}
Let $(n_j)$ be a sequence, put $V_j=\mathcal P(n_j)$ and
$k_j=\omega(n_j)\to\infty$, and for nonempty $C\subseteq V_j$ put
\[
 (n_j)_C:=\prod_{\ell\in C}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n_j)}.
\]
For a nontrivial partition $\Pi\in\mathsf{Part}(V_j)$ define
\[
 \Delta(\Pi):=\sum_{\substack{C,D\in\Pi\\C<D}}|C||D|
 =\frac{k_j^2-\sum_{C\in\Pi}|C|^2}{2}.
\]
Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockwise-window-condition}
 \varepsilon_j:=
 \max_{\substack{\Pi\in\mathsf{Part}(V_j)\\|\Pi|\ge2}}
 \frac{\sum_{C\in\Pi}|C|\log R((n_j)_C)}{\Delta(\Pi)}
 \longrightarrow0\qquad\text{\rm(BW)}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:connected-dominance-conclusion}
 \frac{\#(\mathcal M_{n_j}\setminus\mathcal M_{n_j}^{\rm conn})}
 {\#\mathcal M_{n_j}^{\rm conn}}
 \le\exp\left[-\left(\frac{\log2}{2}-o(1)\right)k_j\right]
 =o(1).
\end{equation}
A sufficient condition for \eqref{eq:blockwise-window-condition} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockwise-window-sufficient-condition}
 \max_{\varnothing\ne C\subsetneq V_j}
 \frac{\log R((n_j)_C)}{k_j-|C|}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad\text{\rm(BW$'$)}.
\end{equation}
In particular, \eqref{eq:connected-dominance-conclusion} holds if
$R((n_j)_C)=1$ for every nonempty proper block $C$ and all sufficiently
large $j$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The unique connected-block factorization in
Theorem~\ref{thm:mingenerator-connected-factorization} gives
\[
 \#(\mathcal M_{n_j}\setminus\mathcal M_{n_j}^{\rm conn})
 =\sum_{\substack{\Pi\in\mathsf{Part}(V_j)\\|\Pi|\ge2}}
   \prod_{C\in\Pi}M^{\rm conn}((n_j)_C).
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:refined-private-coordinate-upper-bound} to each block.
The theta asymptotic \eqref{eq:minimal-cover-theta} gives uniform two-sided
bounds
\[
 C_s\asymp(s+1)^{-1/2}2^{s^2/4+s}.
\]
Consequently there is an absolute constant $K_1$ such that every partition
$\Pi$ with block sizes $s_C=|C|$ contributes, after division by $C_{k_j}$,
at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition-contribution-bound}
 K_1^{|\Pi|}\sqrt{k_j}\,
 \exp\left\{-\frac{\log2}{2}\Delta(\Pi)
 +\sum_{C\in\Pi}|C|\log R((n_j)_C)\right\}.
\end{equation}
Under \textup{(BW)}, this is
\[
 K_1^{|\Pi|}\sqrt{k_j}\,
 \exp\left\{-\left(\frac{\log2}{2}-o(1)\right)
 \Delta(\Pi)\right\},
\]
where the $o(1)$ is uniform over all nontrivial partitions.

If every block has size at most $k_j/2$, then
$\Delta(\Pi)\ge k_j^2/4$. There are at most $k_j^{k_j}$ set partitions,
so the total of these terms is $\exp(-\Omega(k_j^2))$. Otherwise there is a
unique largest block of size $k_j-q>k_j/2$. Then
$\Delta(\Pi)\ge q(k_j-q)$, and the remaining $q$ vertices can be selected and
partitioned in at most
\[
 \binom{k_j}{q}\mathsf B_q\le(ek_j)^q
\]
ways. Summing \eqref{eq:partition-contribution-bound} over
$1\le q<k_j/2$ therefore gives
\[
 \exp\left[-\left(\frac{\log2}{2}-o(1)\right)k_j\right]C_{k_j}.
\]
Corollary~\ref{cor:theta-refined-connected-entropy} gives
$M^{\rm conn}(n_j)\ge(1-o(1))C_{k_j}$, proving
\eqref{eq:connected-dominance-conclusion}.

Finally, if the maximum in \textup{(BW$'$)} is $\eta_j$, then every block of
a nontrivial partition is proper and
\[
 \sum_{C\in\Pi}|C|\log R((n_j)_C)
 \le\eta_j\sum_{C\in\Pi}|C|(k_j-|C|)
 =2\eta_j\Delta(\Pi).
\]
Thus \textup{(BW$'$)} implies \textup{(BW)}, and the last assertion is the
case $\eta_j=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite divisor/rank verification with supplied factorization]
\label{prop:effective-birth-layer-computation}
Let $n\ge2$ and suppose the integer factorization of $F_n$ is known. Then
$B_n$, $A(n)$, and $\mathcal M_n$ are recovered exactly by the following finite
divisor/rank test.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Enumerate the divisor set $D_n=\operatorname{Div}(F_n)$ from the
 factorization of $F_n$.
\item Enumerate the divisor set $\operatorname{Div}(n)$. For each
 $q\in D_n$, compute
 \[
 \alpha_n(q):=\min\{d\mid n:q\mid F_d\}.
 \]
\item Set $B_n=\{q\in D_n:q\ge2,\ \alpha_n(q)=n\}$ and
 $A(n)=\#B_n$.
\item Sort $B_n$ by divisibility-compatible order, for instance by size, and
 retain exactly those $m\in B_n$ for which no previously retained element
 divides $m$; the retained set is $\mathcal M_n$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since every $q\in D_n$ divides $F_n$, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}
shows that $q\mid F_d$ for some $d$ if and only if $\alpha(q)\mid d$. In
particular $q\mid F_n$ implies $\alpha(q)\mid n$, so the least positive index
at which $q$ appears is exactly
\[
\alpha(q)=\min\{d\mid n:q\mid F_d\}=\alpha_n(q).
\]
Hence the test in step (c) gives precisely the definition
$B_n=\{q\in D_n:\alpha(q)=n\}$, with the harmless exclusion of $q=1$ already
consistent with the convention $\alpha(1)=1$ and $n\ge2$. Counting this set
gives $A(n)$.

It remains to justify step (d). The set $B_n$ is finite. If its elements are
processed in increasing size, then every proper divisor of the current element
has already been processed. We claim inductively that every discarded element
has a retained minimal divisor. The claim is vacuous before the first element.
If the current element is discarded, it has a previously retained divisor by
the algorithm, so the claim holds for it; if it is retained, it is its own
retained divisor. Thus every earlier divisor, retained or discarded, has a
retained divisor below it. Consequently the current element is retained if and
only if no smaller element of $B_n$ divides it, which is exactly minimality in
the divisibility poset. The retained set is therefore $\mathcal M_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Reproducibility scope]
The preceding proposition is used only to document the finite verification
interface for the table, not as a complexity theorem.  After the factorization
of $F_n$ has been supplied, the displayed loops involve only the finite divisor
sets of $F_n$ and $n$, ordinary divisibility tests $q\mid F_d$, and divisor
comparisons inside $B_n$.  The manuscript makes no bit-complexity assertion and
does not include the cost of factoring $F_n$ in the numerical evidence.
\end{remark}

\section{Further questions}\label{sec:questions}
%% ====================================================================

The preceding sections complete the divisor-lattice theorem chain for this
article.  We record only two boundary markers; neither is used as evidence for
the results proved above.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Classify connected support kernels of size at least four. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:four-support-reduction}, the one-, two-, and
three-coordinate classifications already determine every disconnected
four-coordinate pattern. Corollary~\ref{cor:theta-refined-connected-entropy}
shows that connected kernels already realize the theta-refined lower bound of
the fixed-choice rank-pure minimal-cover sector, so a full connected
high-support classification is not a lower-order residual problem within that
sector. It would be a separate project.
\item Prove or disprove either rank-window sparsity conjecture. For the mean
hypothesis, Theorem~\ref{thm:mean-rank-window-deaggregation} reduces the
problem to the visible single-rank maximum
\eqref{eq:visible-single-rank-interface}; Proposition
\ref{prop:blms-obstructs-mean-window} also shows that a published conjecture
predicts failure rather than sparsity. As explained in
Remark~\ref{rem:exact-average-open-interface}, the remaining question is an
upper distribution problem for exact-rank prime multiplicities.
The paper derives conditional normal and mean orders, but does not
claim that either conjecture follows from presently known primitive-divisor
theorems, RH, or ERH.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Reader's checklist for the three-coordinate table]
\label{prop:three-coordinate-table-checklist}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,
\ell_2,\ell_3\}$.  The following finite procedure returns exactly
$\mathcal M_n$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For every nonempty $J\subseteq[3]$, compute the exact-rank window
$\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ and the rank classes
$\Pi_\alpha(d)$ for $d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive-prime-mobius-count}. This gives every prime
slot $P_J(n)$.
\item For each coordinate $i$, compute the single possible ladder candidate
$\mathcal L_i^*(n)$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests}, then retain it in
$L_{i,J}(n)$ exactly for those $J$ for which its rank divides $n$ and has
full-coordinate set $J$.
\item For each coordinate permutation of each of the eight rows in
Table~\ref{tab:three-coordinate-classification}, replace the displayed slots
by the finite sets found in (i) and (ii), with
$S_i(n)=P_{\{i\}}(n)\cup L_{i,\{i\}}(n)$.
\item Keep precisely those row fillings whose selected prime powers have
distinct underlying primes, equivalently those satisfying the Hall
inequalities in Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-slot-selection}; multiply the selected
prime powers and insert the product into a set, thereby removing repetitions
caused by stabilizers and coordinate permutations.
\end{enumerate}
The products retained in (iv) are exactly the minimal generators listed in
Theorem~\ref{thm:low-support-classification-package}.  In particular, the
table is a closed finite classification for fixed $n$, not an interface to an
additional infinite search.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Step (i) is precisely the prime-slot identity in
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests}; the M\"obius formula
there is Lemma~\ref{lem:primitive-prime-mobius-count}.  Step (ii) is the
ladder-slot identity in the same proposition, which says that every
$L_{i,J}(n)$ is empty or consists of the displayed single candidate.  Step
(iii) is only the coordinate-labelling convention of
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}.  By that theorem, after the
slots of a labelled row are replaced by their finite admissible sets, a row
realizes a minimal generator if and only if the slot-selection test in step
(iv) succeeds; Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-slot-selection} is exactly the stated
Hall criterion for distinct underlying primes.  The one-factor rows
$\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_3$ are the atomic generators with full
three-coordinate support.  Conversely, every minimal generator with
three-coordinate support has, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-realization}, one fixed labelled
coordinate permutation of one of the eight row types and therefore appears in
this procedure. The labelled atomic factor set is unique, whereas the
unlabelled row is an $S_3$-orbit; the set insertion in step (iv) prevents the
raw permuted enumeration from asserting stronger uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Applying the checklist at $n=42$]
\label{ex:n42-three-coordinate-checklist}
Let $n=42=2\cdot3\cdot7$ and label the coordinates by
$(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)=(2,3,7)$.  The prime factors of
\[
F_{42}=2^3\cdot13\cdot29\cdot211\cdot421
\]
have ranks
\[
\alpha(2)=3,
\alpha(4)=6,
\alpha(13)=7,
\alpha(29)=14,
\alpha(421)=21,
\alpha(211)=42.
\]
Thus $\Atom(42)=\{211\}$. The exact-rank windows used below are
$P_{12}(42)=\varnothing$, $P_{13}(42)=\{29\}$,
$P_{23}(42)=\{421\}$, and $P_{123}(42)=\{211\}$.  Singleton slots are
$S_1(42)=\varnothing$, $S_2(42)=\{2\}$, and $S_3(42)=\{13\}$.  The proper
ladder slots relevant here are
$L_{1,12}(42)=\{4\}$, $L_{1,123}(42)=\varnothing$,
$L_{2,J}(42)=\varnothing$, and $L_{3,J}(42)=\varnothing$.

The table then gives the following non-atomic generators.  The row
$\Gamma_4$ with the permuted slots $P_{13}$ and $P_{23}$ gives
$29\cdot421=12209$.  The row $\Gamma_5$ gives
$4\cdot421=1684$ from $L_{1,12}$ and $P_{23}$, while the analogous
coordinate permutation with an exact-rank $P_{13}$ partner is not a row of type
$\Gamma_5$ for this ladder orientation.  The row $\Gamma_7$ gives
$2\cdot29=58$ from $S_2$ and $P_{13}$ after relabeling.  The row $\Gamma_8$
gives $13\cdot4=52$ from $S_3$ and the permuted ladder slot $L_{1,12}$.
All other rows fail because one of the required slots is empty.  Hence
\[
\mathcal M_{42}=\{52,58,211,1684,12209\}.
\]
The example illustrates the theorem's reading rule: every success or failure
is decided by a finite slot list and a coprimeness check.
\end{example}
