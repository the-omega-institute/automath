\begin{abstract}
For each positive integer $n$, let $B_n$ denote the set of divisors of the
Fibonacci number $F_n$ whose rank of apparition equals~$n$. We prove that
$B_n$ is an upper set in the divisor lattice of $F_n$ obtained by removing
the covering ideals associated with the maximal proper divisors of~$n$. Our
main result classifies the minimal generators of~$B_n$: every minimal
generator factors uniquely as a pairwise coprime product of atomic
prime-power birth moduli forming a witness cover whose support spans the prime
coordinates of~$n$. This yields the sharp bound $\omega(m)\le\omega(n)$ for
every minimal generator $m$, with equality for every odd $n\ge3$, and a
primitive-versus-ladder dichotomy for atomic factors. We give complete
classifications for $\omega(n)\le 3$ and show that
both $B_n$ and its minimal-generator set admit unique factorizations into
connected coordinate blocks, producing partition-lattice formulas for $A(n) =
\#B_n$ and $\#\mathcal{M}_n$. As consequences, nonexceptional birth layers
contain canonical principal intervals, Boolean subposets of rank $\tau(n) -
O(1)$, and satisfy Bell-type lower bounds on odd layers. Numerical data for
$n\le 30$ illustrate these structures.
\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Fibonacci numbers, rank of apparition, divisor
lattices, upper sets, witness covers, atomic decompositions.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% ====================================================================

For an integer $q\ge 2$, the \emph{rank of apparition} (or \emph{order of
appearance}) $\alpha(q)$ is the least positive index $n$ such that $q\mid
F_n$, where $(F_n)_{n\ge 0}$ denotes the Fibonacci sequence with $F_0=0$,
$F_1=1$. We use the harmless conventions $\alpha(1)=1$ and
$D_1=\operatorname{Div}(F_1)=\{1\}$ in divisor-lattice formulas. For each
$n\ge 2$, write
\[
D_n:=\operatorname{Div}(F_n),
\qquad
B_n:=\{q\in D_n:\ \alpha(q)=n\},
\qquad
A(n):=\#B_n.
\]
Let $\mathcal P(n)$ be the set of prime divisors of $n$, and let
$\mathcal M_n$ denote the set of minimal elements of $B_n$ under divisibility.
Thus $B_n$ consists of those moduli that first divide a Fibonacci number at
index~$n$. Since $F_2=1$, the boundary layer is empty: throughout the paper
we use the convention $B_2=\mathcal M_2=\varnothing$ and $A(2)=0$.
The counting function $A(n)$ is subsumed by the
$\alpha$-contraction formalism of Stroi\'nski \cite{Stroinski2016AlphaContraction};
see also the classical M\"obius inversions discussed in
\cite{Halton1966DivisibilityFibonacci,FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions}.
In this paper we go beyond enumeration and study $B_n$ as an
ordered subset of the divisor lattice $(D_n,\mid)$, asking how it sits inside
$D_n$, how it is generated, and what combinatorial structure it carries.

Three classical facts underlie the analysis. First, $q\mid F_m$ if and only if
$\alpha(q)\mid m$
\cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm,Vinson1963RankApparition}. Second, the Fibonacci
sequence satisfies strong divisibility: $\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}$.
Third, ranks of apparition respect least common multiples:
$\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))=\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))$. These properties, combined
with the primitive divisor theorem for Fibonacci numbers
\cite{Carmichael1913PrimitiveDivisors,BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas},
are the sole inputs for the main results.

This point of view complements several nearby strands in the literature.
Luca and Pomerance \cite{LucaPomerance2014LocalBehavior} study the local
behavior of $\alpha$ under additive shifts; Trojovsk\'a
\cite{Trojovska2020PeriodicPoints} and FitzGibbons--Javaheri--Miller--Verga
\cite{FitzGibbonsJavaheriMillerVerga2025Dynamics} study periodic points and
iteration dynamics of $\alpha$; Cameron--Counts--Lund--Miller--Piechnik--Wong
\cite{CameronCountsLundMillerPiechnikWong2022Fibotomic} study fibotomic
polynomials and related algebraic factorizations; and Sanna
\cite{Sanna2016LucasSequences} and Marques \cite{Marques2012OrderAppearance}
study the growth and fixed-point behavior of the order-of-appearance map. The
present paper instead fixes a single fiber $B_n$ and studies its divisibility
geometry inside $D_n$. The contribution of this paper is the upper-fiber and
witness-cover description of these fibers, together with the two-support and
three-support classifications that follow from that description.

Our first main result identifies $B_n$ as an upper fiber obtained by sieve.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-upper-fiber}
For every $n\ge2$,
\[
B_n=D_n\setminus\bigcup_{p\mid n}D_{n/p}.
\]
In particular, $B_n$ is an upper set in the divisor lattice $D_n$, and
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal M_n}\{q\in D_n:\ m\mid q\}.
\]
This is proved as Theorem~\ref{thm:upper-fiber}.
\end{theorem}

Call a prime power $\theta=p^e$ \emph{atomic for the index $d$} if
$\alpha(\theta)=d$ and $\alpha(p^{e-1})<d$. An \emph{$n$-witness cover} is a
pairwise coprime set of such atomic prime powers whose apparition indices
collectively span the prime factorization of $n$ in a minimal, irredundant
fashion; the full definition is Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-witness}
For every $n\ge2$, multiplication gives a bijection from $n$-witness
covers $\Theta$ to minimal generators:
\[
\mathcal M_n=
\left\{\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta:\ \Theta\text{ is an $n$-witness cover}
\right\}.
\]
Consequently every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ satisfies
$\omega(m)\le\omega(n)$, and this bound is attained for every odd $n\ge3$. The
precise cover conditions are given in Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover},
and the result is Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} together with
Corollaries~\ref{cor:support-bound} and~\ref{cor:support-bound-sharp}.
\end{theorem}

Making this classification effective requires the prime-power apparition
formulas of Fulton--Morris \cite{FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions} and
Lengyel \cite{Lengyel1995OrderFibonacciLucas}. These show that every atomic
factor is either \emph{primitive} (arising from a new prime) or lies on a
\emph{prime-power ladder} (changing exactly one prime coordinate of the
apparition index). This dichotomy
(Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}) enables complete
classifications of $\mathcal M_n$ when $\omega(n)\le 3$. The detailed
ingredients are Corollary~\ref{cor:two-support-complete-classification} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}; the consolidated
classification is Theorem~\ref{thm:low-support-classification-package}. The
corresponding complete description of the full birth layer $B_n$ is obtained by
taking the upper ideals generated by these listed minimal elements; this is
recorded in Corollary~\ref{cor:low-support-birth-layer-package}.

The same lcm mechanism gives a local-to-global factorization over connected
coordinate blocks.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-connected}
Let $q\in B_n$ and form the support hypergraph on $\mathcal P(n)$ whose
edges are the prime-coordinate supports of the ranks of the prime-power
factors of $q$. If its connected components are $C$, then
\[
q=\prod_C q_C,\qquad q_C\in B_{n_C},
\qquad n_C=\prod_{\ell\in C}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n)} ,
\]
and this factorization is unique. The same statement holds for minimal
generators after replacing $B_{n_C}$ by $\mathcal M_{n_C}$. Consequently
$A(n)$ and $\#\mathcal M_n$ are partition sums over the connected counts.
This is proved in Section~\ref{sec:connected}.
\end{theorem}

As secondary consequences, nonexceptional birth layers contain canonical
principal intervals
(Corollary~\ref{cor:canonical-principal-interval}), Boolean subposets of rank
$\tau(n)-O(1)$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:boolean-skeleton}), and---for odd
$n$---Bell-type lower bounds on $\#\mathcal M_n$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:odd-primitive-cover-realization}).

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:prelim} collects the
background on Fibonacci divisibility and the prime-power apparition formulas.
Section~\ref{sec:upper-fibers} proves the upper-fiber and witness-cover
theory used later. Section~\ref{sec:low-support} treats the
primitive--ladder dichotomy and the low-support classifications.
Section~\ref{sec:connected} records the connected-block factorization
(Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-connected}) and the partition-lattice formulas.
Section~\ref{sec:numerical} presents numerical data, and
Section~\ref{sec:questions} records further questions.

\subsection{Rank of apparition and Fibonacci divisibility}\label{subsec:fib-div}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rank-basics}
Let $q,u,v\ge1$ and let $a,b,m\ge1$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $q\mid F_m$ if and only if $\alpha(q)\mid m$.
\item $\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}$.
\item $\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))=\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The divisibility criterion and the strong-divisibility identity are classical;
we use them in the forms proved by Wall \cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm} and
Vinson \cite{Vinson1963RankApparition}. The convention $\alpha(1)=1$ makes
the assertion for $q=1$ immediate.

For the lcm identity, an integer $m$ is divisible by
$\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))$ if and only if $\lcmop(u,v)\mid F_m$, by (i). This is
equivalent to $u\mid F_m$ and $v\mid F_m$, hence to
$\alpha(u)\mid m$ and $\alpha(v)\mid m$, again by (i). Thus the multiples of
$\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))$ are exactly the multiples of
$\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))$, and the two positive integers are equal.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Primitive divisors}\label{subsec:primitive}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:primitive-divisors}
For every $n\ge 3$ with $n\notin\{6,12\}$, there exists a prime $p_n$ with
$\alpha(p_n)=n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $n=3,4,5$ one takes $p_3=2$, $p_4=3$, $p_5=5$. For $n\ge 7$, $n\neq 12$,
the primitive divisor theorem
\cite{Carmichael1913PrimitiveDivisors,Yabuta2001PrimitiveDivisorFibonacci,%
BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas}
gives a prime dividing $F_n$ but no earlier Fibonacci number. Since
$\alpha(p_n)\mid n$ and $p_n$ divides no $F_d$ with $d<n$, necessarily
$\alpha(p_n)=n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Counting corollaries}\label{subsec:counting}

The standard M\"obius-inversion formulas for first-appearance counts follow
from Stroi\'nski's $\alpha$-contraction identity
\cite{Stroinski2016AlphaContraction}: for any $f:\NN\to\mathbb{C}$ with
$f(1)=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stroinski}
\sum_{d\mid F_n}f(d)=\sum_{d\mid n}f_\alpha(d),
\qquad
f_\alpha(d):=\sum_{\alpha(m)=d}f(m).
\end{equation}
Taking $f$ to be the indicator of integers at least $2$, of primes, or of
prime powers, and applying M\"obius inversion, one obtains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mobius-birth}
A(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\,\tau(F_d),
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{pr}}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\,\omega(F_d).
\end{equation}
We regard these as known corollaries; the results of
Sections~\ref{sec:upper-fibers}--\ref{sec:connected} are independent of them.

\subsection{Prime-power apparition formulas}\label{subsec:prime-power}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:prime-power-ladders}
Let $p$ be prime, and put $z_p:=\alpha(p)$. For $p\ne2,5$, put
$h_p:=\nu_p(F_{z_p})$. Then the ranks of apparition of prime powers are as
follows:
\[
\alpha(2)=3,\qquad \alpha(4)=6,\qquad
\alpha(2^e)=3\cdot2^{e-2}\quad(e\ge3),
\]
\[
\alpha(5^e)=5^e\quad(e\ge1),
\]
and, for $p\ne2,5$,
\[
\alpha(p^e)=p^{\max\{e-h_p,0\}}\,z_p\quad(e\ge1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
These are the standard prime-power rank formulas for the Fibonacci sequence.
Wall proves the rank-divisibility and lifting framework for Fibonacci
periods and ranks \cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm}, and Lengyel gives the
explicit $p$-adic order formula for Fibonacci and Lucas numbers
\cite[Theorems~1 and~2]{Lengyel1995OrderFibonacciLucas}. In the notation
above, those formulas say that for $p\ne2,5$, once $p^e$ first appears at
rank $z_p$, the rank remains $z_p$ up to exponent $h_p=\nu_p(F_{z_p})$ and
then is multiplied by one further factor of $p$ at each additional
$p$-adic level. This gives
$\alpha(p^e)=p^{\max\{e-h_p,0\}}z_p$.

The same cited formulas contain the exceptional Fibonacci primes $2$ and
$5$. Since $F_3=2$, $F_6=8$, and $\nu_2(F_6)=3$, one obtains
$\alpha(2)=3$, $\alpha(4)=\alpha(8)=6$, and thereafter each additional
power of $2$ doubles the rank, giving
$\alpha(2^e)=3\cdot2^{e-2}$ for $e\ge3$. Since $F_5=5$ and the $5$-adic
lifting is linear at the rank $5$, one obtains $\alpha(5^e)=5^e$ for all
$e\ge1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:atomic-families}
For a prime power $\theta=p^e$ with $e\ge1$, put
\[
d(\theta):=\alpha(\theta),\qquad
d^-(\theta):=\alpha(p^{e-1}),
\]
where $\alpha(1)=1$. For $d\ge1$ define
\[
\Atom(d):=\{p^e:\ \alpha(p^e)=d,\ \alpha(p^{e-1})<d\}.
\]
Thus $\Atom(d)$ consists of the prime-power factors that are born exactly at
rank $d$.

If $d(\theta)\mid n$, define the full-coordinate support and the
essential-coordinate support of $\theta$ relative to $n$ by
\[
T_n(\theta):=\{\ell\in\mathcal P(n):\
\nu_\ell(d(\theta))=\nu_\ell(n)\},
\]
and
\[
E_n(\theta):=\{\ell\in T_n(\theta):\
\nu_\ell(d^-(\theta))<\nu_\ell(n)\}.
\]
Equivalently, $T_n(\theta)$ records the prime coordinates of $n$ supplied at
full exponent by $d(\theta)$, while $E_n(\theta)$ records the full coordinates
lost when $\theta=p^e$ is lowered to $p^{e-1}$.

For nonempty $I\subseteq J\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$, put
\[
\mathcal A_{I,J}(n):=
\{\theta\in\bigcup_{d\mid n}\Atom(d):\
E_n(\theta)=I,\ T_n(\theta)=J\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:atomic-classification}
The atomic prime powers are exactly the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $2\in\Atom(3)$, $4\in\Atom(6)$, and
$2^e\in\Atom(3\cdot2^{e-2})$ for every $e\ge4$; the power $8$ is not atomic.
\item $5^e\in\Atom(5^e)$ for every $e\ge1$.
\item If $p\ne2,5$, $z_p=\alpha(p)$, and
$h_p=\nu_p(F_{z_p})$, then $p\in\Atom(z_p)$ and
$p^e\in\Atom(p^{e-h_p}z_p)$ for every $e>h_p$; no power $p^e$ with
$1<e\le h_p$ is atomic.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently every atom is either a primitive atom $p\in\Atom(\alpha(p))$ or
a ladder atom $p^e$ with $e>1$. Every ladder atom changes only the $p$-adic
coordinate of its apparition index when it is lowered to $p^{e-1}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders}. For $p=2$, the ranks are
$3,6,6,12,24,\ldots$ on $2,4,8,16,32,\ldots$. Hence $2$ and $4$ are atomic,
$8$ is not, and each $2^e$ with $e\ge4$ is atomic because lowering the
exponent divides the rank by $2$. For $p=5$, the ranks are $5^e$, so every
step is atomic.

Let $p\ne2,5$. The formula
$\alpha(p^e)=p^{\max\{e-h_p,0\}}z_p$ gives $\alpha(p)=z_p$, so $p$ is
atomic. For $1<e\le h_p$ the rank is still $z_p$, so $p^e$ is not atomic.
For $e>h_p$, lowering from $p^e$ to $p^{e-1}$ changes the rank from
$p^{e-h_p}z_p$ to $p^{e-1-h_p}z_p$, so $p^e$ is atomic.

The final assertion follows from the same list. A nonprimitive atom has
$e>1$ and is one of the displayed ladder steps; in each case the quotient
$d(\theta)/d^-(\theta)$ is a power of the same prime $p$, so lowering
$\theta$ can change only the $p$-adic coordinate of the apparition index.
\end{proof}

\section{Birth layers and witness covers}\label{sec:upper-fibers}
%% ====================================================================

\begin{definition}\label{def:birth-layer}
For $n\ge2$ let
\[
D_n:=\operatorname{Div}(F_n),\qquad
B_n:=\{q\in D_n:\ \alpha(q)=n\},\qquad
A(n):=\#B_n.
\]
The set $B_n$ is partially ordered by divisibility. Its set of minimal
elements is
\[
\mathcal M_n:=\{m\in B_n:\ \text{there is no }q\in B_n
\text{ with }q\mid m\text{ and }q<m\},
\]
and
\[
\widthop(B_n):=\max\{\#\mathcal A:\ \mathcal A\subseteq B_n
\text{ is an antichain}\}.
\]
We write $\mathcal P(n)$ for the set of prime divisors of $n$. Since
$F_2=1$, we use the boundary convention
\[
B_2=\mathcal M_2=\varnothing,\qquad A(2)=\widthop(B_2)=0.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:upper-fiber}
For every $n\ge2$,
\[
B_n=D_n\setminus\bigcup_{p\mid n}D_{n/p}.
\]
Moreover $B_n$ is an upper set in $D_n$: if $q\in B_n$, $r\in D_n$, and
$q\mid r$, then $r\in B_n$. Consequently
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal M_n}\{q\in D_n:\ m\mid q\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}, an integer $q$ lies in $D_m$ if and
only if $\alpha(q)\mid m$. Thus $q\in D_n$ lies outside
$\bigcup_{p\mid n}D_{n/p}$ precisely when $\alpha(q)\mid n$ but
$\alpha(q)\nmid n/p$ for every prime $p\mid n$.

If $\alpha(q)=n$, then clearly $\alpha(q)\nmid n/p$ for every $p\mid n$, so
$q$ is outside the union. Conversely, suppose $\alpha(q)\mid n$ and
$\alpha(q)<n$. Then $n/\alpha(q)>1$; choose a prime $p$ dividing
$n/\alpha(q)$. Since $\alpha(q)\mid n/p$, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}
gives $q\in D_{n/p}$, contrary to being outside the union. Hence the
complement is exactly the set of $q\in D_n$ with $\alpha(q)=n$, namely
$B_n$. For $n=2$ this also gives
$B_2=D_2\setminus D_1=\varnothing$ under the boundary convention in
Definition~\ref{def:birth-layer}.

Now let $q\in B_n$, $r\in D_n$, and $q\mid r$. Since $r\in D_n$,
$\alpha(r)\mid n$. Since $q\mid r$, every Fibonacci number divisible by $r$
is divisible by $q$; applying the divisibility criterion to
$F_{\alpha(r)}$ gives $\alpha(q)\mid\alpha(r)$. But $\alpha(q)=n$, so
$n\mid\alpha(r)$ and $\alpha(r)\mid n$, whence $\alpha(r)=n$. Therefore
$r\in B_n$.

Finally $D_n$ is finite, so every element $q\in B_n$ has at least one
minimal element $m$ of $B_n$ below it in the divisibility order. The upper-set
property gives the reverse containment: every element of $D_n$ divisible by
some $m\in\mathcal M_n$ lies again in $B_n$. This proves the displayed
decomposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:minimal-generators}
Let $n\ge2$, and let
$m=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{e_i}$ be a divisor of $F_n$, with the $p_i$ distinct
and $e_i\ge1$. Put
\[
\lambda_i:=\alpha(p_i^{e_i}),\qquad
\lambda_i^-:=\alpha(p_i^{e_i-1}),
\]
where $\alpha(1)=1$. Then $m\in\mathcal M_n$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-criterion}
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)=n
\quad\text{and}\quad
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_i^-,
\lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\lambda_r)<n
\quad(1\le i\le r).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $m\mid F_n$, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics} gives
$\lambda_i\mid n$ for every $i$. Applying the lcm identity in
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics} repeatedly gives
\[
\alpha(m)=\lcmop(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r).
\]
Thus the first condition in \eqref{eq:lcm-criterion} is equivalent to
$m\in B_n$.

Assume first that $m\in\mathcal M_n$. Then $m\in B_n$, so the first lcm is
$n$. For a fixed $i$, the integer $m/p_i$ is a proper divisor of $m$ and is
still a divisor of $F_n$. Minimality of $m$ in $B_n$ implies
$m/p_i\notin B_n$. Since $\alpha(m/p_i)\mid n$, this is equivalent to
$\alpha(m/p_i)<n$. A second application of the lcm identity gives
\[
\alpha(m/p_i)=
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_i^-,
\lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\lambda_r),
\]
which is the lowered prime-power condition in
\eqref{eq:lcm-criterion}.

Conversely, suppose \eqref{eq:lcm-criterion} holds. The first condition gives
$m\in B_n$. Let $q$ be any proper divisor of $m$. Then for at least one
index $i$ the exponent of $p_i$ in $q$ is at most $e_i-1$. Hence
\[
\alpha(q)\mid
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_i^-,
\lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\lambda_r),
\]
again by the lcm identity and by monotonicity under divisibility of prime
powers. The right-hand side is $<n$ by
\eqref{eq:lcm-criterion}. Since $q\mid m\mid F_n$, we also have
$\alpha(q)\mid n$, and therefore $\alpha(q)<n$. Thus no proper divisor of
$m$ lies in $B_n$, so $m$ is minimal in $B_n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Witness covers}

\begin{definition}\label{def:witness-cover}
An \emph{$n$-witness cover} is a finite set
$\Theta=\{\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r\}$ of atomic prime powers satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $d(\theta)\mid n$ for every $\theta\in\Theta$;
\item the elements of $\Theta$ are pairwise coprime;
\item the full-coordinate supports cover all prime coordinates of $n$,
\[
\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta}T_n(\theta)=\mathcal P(n);
\]
\item each atom has an essential coordinate not supplied by the other atoms,
\[
E_n(\theta)\nsubseteq
\bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta,\ \eta\ne\theta}T_n(\eta)
\qquad(\theta\in\Theta).
\]
\end{enumerate}
The last condition is the irredundance condition. It is stated with
$E_n$ rather than $T_n$ because overlapping full-coordinate support is
allowed; what is forbidden is that every coordinate lost by lowering
$\theta$ is already supplied at full exponent by the remaining atoms.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:witness-classification}
For every $n\ge 2$,
$\mathcal M_n=\bigl\{\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta:\
\Theta\text{ is an $n$-witness cover}\bigr\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$, $m=\prod_{i=1}^r\theta_i$ ($\theta_i=p_i^{e_i}$).
Each $\theta_i$ is atomic: if $\alpha(p_i^{e_i-1})=\alpha(p_i^{e_i})$, then
replacing $\theta_i$ by $p_i^{e_i-1}$ preserves the lcm, contradicting
the minimal-generator criterion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators}. Since
$n=\lcmop(d(\theta_i))$, the sets $T_n(\theta_i)$ cover $\mathcal P(n)$. The
condition $\alpha(m/p_i)<n$ forces
$E_n(\theta_i)\nsubseteq\bigcup_{j\neq i}T_n(\theta_j)$; otherwise the lost
coordinates would be supplied by other factors, keeping the lcm equal to $n$.
Thus $\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\}$ is an $n$-witness cover.

Conversely, let $\Theta$ be an $n$-witness cover and
$m=\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta$. By
the lcm property recorded in Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics},
$\alpha(m)=\lcmop(d(\theta):\theta\in\Theta)=n$. For each
$\theta=p^e\in\Theta$, the witness condition
provides $\ell\in E_n(\theta)\setminus\bigcup_{\eta\neq\theta}T_n(\eta)$; at
this coordinate, $\alpha(m/p)<n$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators} then gives $m\in\mathcal M_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:support-bound}
Every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ satisfies $\omega(m)\le\omega(n)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The witness condition provides an injection
$\theta\mapsto\ell(\theta)$ from $\Theta$ into $\mathcal P(n)$: choose
$\ell(\theta)\in E_n(\theta)\setminus\bigcup_{\eta\neq\theta}T_n(\eta)$.
Hence $\omega(m)=\#\Theta\le\omega(n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:support-bound-sharp}
The support bound in Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound} is optimal. More
precisely, if $n\ge3$ is odd, then there exists
$m\in\mathcal M_n$ with $\omega(m)=\omega(n)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
n=\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P(n)}\ell^{a_\ell}.
\]
For each $\ell\in\mathcal P(n)$ put $d_\ell=\ell^{a_\ell}$. Since $n$ is
odd and $n\ge3$, every $d_\ell$ is odd and at least $3$; in particular
$d_\ell\notin\{6,12\}$. The primitive divisor theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors}, therefore supplies a prime
$\pi_\ell$ with $\alpha(\pi_\ell)=d_\ell$.

The primes $\pi_\ell$ are distinct, because a single prime has only one rank
of apparition. Each $\pi_\ell$ is a primitive atom in
\(\Atom(d_\ell)\), so, relative to $n$,
\[
T_n(\pi_\ell)=E_n(\pi_\ell)=\{\ell\}.
\]
Thus
\[
\Theta_n:=\{\pi_\ell:\ell\in\mathcal P(n)\}
\]
is an $n$-witness cover: the factors are pairwise coprime, their
full-coordinate supports cover $\mathcal P(n)$, and each singleton
essential support is absent from all other full-coordinate supports. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification},
\[
m_n:=\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P(n)}\pi_\ell
\]
belongs to $\mathcal M_n$. Since the factors are distinct primes,
\[
\omega(m_n)=\#\mathcal P(n)=\omega(n).
\]
Hence the upper bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound} cannot be lowered
as a function of $\omega(n)$, even after restricting to odd indices.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:n20}
For $n=20$, $F_{20}=6765=3\cdot 5\cdot 11\cdot 41$. The relevant atomic
factors are $3\in\Atom(4)$, $5\in\Atom(5)$, $11\in\Atom(10)$,
$41\in\Atom(20)$. The witness covers are $\{41\}$, $\{3,5\}$, $\{3,11\}$,
yielding $\mathcal M_{20}=\{15,33,41\}$ and $A(20)=11$.
Indeed, with coordinates $(2,5)$, the ranks $20$, $4,5$, and $4,10$
respectively cover both coordinates and have the singleton essential supports
required by Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}; the value $A(20)=11$ is the
exhaustive divisor/rank count reported in Table~\ref{tab:birth-layer-data}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}\label{ex:n30}
For $n=30$, $F_{30}=832040=2^3\cdot 5\cdot 11\cdot 31\cdot 61$. The witness
covers are $\{31\}$, $\{4,5\}$, $\{2,11\}$, $\{4,61\}$, $\{11,61\}$, giving
$\mathcal M_{30}=\{20,22,31,244,671\}$ and $A(30)=52$.
With coordinates $(2,3,5)$ for $30=2\cdot3\cdot5$, these covers realize
respectively the types $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_8$, $\Gamma_7$, $\Gamma_5$, and
$\Gamma_4$.
Here the rank supports are, respectively,
$\alpha(31)=30$, $(\alpha(4),\alpha(5))=(6,5)$,
$(\alpha(2),\alpha(11))=(3,10)$, $(\alpha(4),\alpha(61))=(6,15)$, and
$(\alpha(11),\alpha(61))=(10,15)$, so each listed cover has the stated
coordinate support and irredundant essential coordinate; $A(30)=52$ is the
exhaustive divisor/rank count.
\end{example}

\subsection*{Substructures of nonexceptional birth layers}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:canonical-principal-interval}
Let $n\ge3$, $n\notin\{6,12\}$, and let $\pi_n$ be any primitive prime
divisor of $F_n$. Then the principal interval
\[
[\pi_n,F_n]_{D_n}:=\{q\in D_n:\ \pi_n\mid q\}
\]
is contained in $B_n$. In particular $B_n$ contains a divisor-lattice
interval naturally isomorphic to $\operatorname{Div}(F_n/\pi_n)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors}, there is a prime
$\pi_n$ with $\alpha(\pi_n)=n$. Thus $\pi_n\in B_n$. Since
Theorem~\ref{thm:upper-fiber} says that $B_n$ is an upper set in $D_n$,
every divisor $q$ of $F_n$ with $\pi_n\mid q$ also lies in $B_n$. Division by
$\pi_n$ gives an order-preserving bijection from this interval to
$\operatorname{Div}(F_n/\pi_n)$, with inverse $d\mapsto \pi_n d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:boolean-skeleton}
Let $n\ge3$, $n\notin\{6,12\}$, and put
\[
\mathcal D_n^*:=\{d:\ d\mid n,\ 3\le d<n,\ d\notin\{6,12\}\}.
\]
For each $d\in\mathcal D_n^*$ choose a primitive prime
$\pi_d$ with $\alpha(\pi_d)=d$, and choose a primitive prime
$\pi_n$ with $\alpha(\pi_n)=n$. Then
\[
S\longmapsto \pi_n\prod_{d\in S}\pi_d
\qquad(S\subseteq\mathcal D_n^*)
\]
embeds the Boolean lattice $2^{\mathcal D_n^*}$ into $B_n$. Hence
$B_n$ contains a Boolean subposet of rank
\[
|\mathcal D_n^*|\ge \tau(n)-5.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The primitive primes exist by Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors}. They
are distinct, because distinct ranks of apparition cannot belong to the
same prime. For every $d\mid n$, $\pi_d\mid F_d\mid F_n$, so the displayed
products divide $F_n$. They all also divide a multiple of $\pi_n$ inside
$D_n$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:canonical-principal-interval} puts every such
multiple in $B_n$.

Since the primes $\pi_d$ are distinct, divisibility among the displayed
products is exactly inclusion among the subsets $S$. Thus the map is a
Boolean-lattice embedding. Among the $\tau(n)$ divisors of $n$, the only
divisors excluded from $\mathcal D_n^*$ are contained in
$\{1,2,6,12,n\}$, so $|\mathcal D_n^*|\ge\tau(n)-5$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:maximal-order}
For every $n\ge3$ with $n\notin\{6,12\}$,
\[
\widthop(B_n)\ge
\binom{|\mathcal D_n^*|}{\lfloor |\mathcal D_n^*|/2\rfloor}.
\]
Consequently there are infinitely many $n$ for which
\[
\log\log \widthop(B_n)
\ge (\log 2+o(1))\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:boolean-skeleton} embeds a Boolean lattice of rank
$k=|\mathcal D_n^*|$ into $B_n$. By Sperner's theorem
\cite{Sperner1928Subsets}, the largest antichain in this Boolean lattice
has size $\binom{k}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}$, giving the first inequality.

Wigert's maximal-order theorem for the divisor function
\cite{Wigert1907Divisors} gives infinitely many integers $n$ with
\[
\log \tau(n)=(\log 2+o(1))\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}.
\]
Discarding the finitely many exceptional indices $6$ and $12$ leaves an
infinite subsequence with the same asymptotic relation. Since
$k\ge\tau(n)-5$ and
$\binom{k}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\ge 2^k/(k+1)$, we have
\[
\log \widthop(B_n)\ge k\log 2-\log(k+1).
\]
Taking another logarithm along the Wigert sequence gives the displayed
lower bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}
Let $\theta=p^e\in\Atom(d)$ and suppose $d\mid n$. If $e=1$, then
\[
E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta).
\]
If $e>1$, then $E_n(\theta)$ is either empty or a singleton, and any
coordinate in $E_n(\theta)$ is the prime coordinate $p$. In particular no
Fibonacci atom has
\[
1<\#E_n(\theta)<\#T_n(\theta).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $e=1$, then $d^-(\theta)=\alpha(1)=1$, so every coordinate of
$T_n(\theta)$ is lost after lowering $\theta$; hence
$E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$.

Assume $e>1$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:atomic-classification}, $\theta$ is a
ladder atom. The prime-power formulas in
Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders} show that
$d(\theta)$ and $d^-(\theta)$ differ only in the $p$-adic coordinate of the
apparition index: this is explicit for $p=2$ and $p=5$, and for
$p\ne2,5$ it follows from
$\alpha(p^e)=p^{e-h_p}z_p$ at the atomic ladder levels. Therefore the only
full coordinate that can be lost when
passing from $d(\theta)$ to $d^-(\theta)$ is the coordinate $p$ itself.
Thus $E_n(\theta)$ is empty or $\{p\}$.

The final assertion follows because either $E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$ in
the primitive case or $\#E_n(\theta)\le1$ in the ladder case.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:extremal-coordinate-separation}
Let $\Theta$ be an $n$-witness cover. For each
$\theta\in\Theta$ one may choose a coordinate
\[
\ell(\theta)\in
E_n(\theta)\setminus\bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta,\ \eta\ne\theta}T_n(\eta),
\]
and the chosen coordinates are distinct. If
$|\Theta|=\omega(n)$, then after relabeling the elements of $\Theta$ by
the coordinates of $\mathcal P(n)$ one has
\[
T_n(\theta_\ell)=\{\ell\}\qquad(\ell\in\mathcal P(n)).
\]
In particular, when $\omega(n)=2$, every two-element $n$-witness cover has
one atom supported on the first coordinate and one atom supported on the
second.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The existence of $\ell(\theta)$ is exactly the irredundance condition in
Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}. If
$\ell(\theta)=\ell(\eta)$ with $\theta\ne\eta$, then
$\ell(\theta)\in T_n(\eta)$ because
$E_n(\eta)\subseteq T_n(\eta)$, contradicting the choice of
$\ell(\theta)$. Hence the chosen coordinates are distinct.

If $|\Theta|=\omega(n)$, the injection
$\theta\mapsto\ell(\theta)$ is a bijection from $\Theta$ to
$\mathcal P(n)$. The coordinate $\ell(\theta)$ is not in
$T_n(\eta)$ for any $\eta\ne\theta$. Since the $T_n(\eta)$ cover
$\mathcal P(n)$, every coordinate other than $\ell(\theta)$ is the private
coordinate of some other atom and therefore is not in $T_n(\theta)$. Thus
$T_n(\theta)=\{\ell(\theta)\}$ for every $\theta$, which gives the stated
separation after relabeling.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One-coordinate classification]
\label{cor:one-support-complete-classification}
If $\omega(n)=1$, then
\[
\mathcal M_n=\Atom(n).
\]
With the convention of Definition~\ref{def:birth-layer}, the exceptional
case $n=2$ gives $\mathcal M_2=\varnothing=\Atom(2)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$ and let $\Theta$ be the corresponding $n$-witness
cover from Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification}. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound}, $\#\Theta\le\omega(n)=1$. Since the
$T_n$-sets of a witness cover must cover $\mathcal P(n)$, the cover has one
atom, say $\Theta=\{\theta\}$. The coverage condition forces
$T_n(\theta)=\mathcal P(n)$, and because $d(\theta)\mid n$ this is
equivalent to $d(\theta)=n$. Hence $\theta\in\Atom(n)$ and
$m=\theta$.

Conversely, if $\theta\in\Atom(n)$ and $n\ne2$, then
$d(\theta)=n$, so $T_n(\theta)=\mathcal P(n)$. Since
$d^-(\theta)<d(\theta)=n$ and $\mathcal P(n)$ has one element, the unique
prime coordinate of $n$ lies in $E_n(\theta)$. Thus $\{\theta\}$ is an
$n$-witness cover, and Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} gives
$\theta\in\mathcal M_n$. For $n=2$, Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders}
and Corollary~\ref{cor:atomic-classification} show that no prime power has
rank $2$, while Definition~\ref{def:birth-layer} gives
$\mathcal M_2=\varnothing$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Two-coordinate classification]
\label{cor:two-support-complete-classification}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}$ and identify
$\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}$ with $\{1,2\}$ in the notation
$\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)$ from Definition~\ref{def:atomic-families}. Also put
\[
\mathcal P_i(n):=\{\theta\in\mathcal A_{\{i\},\{i\}}(n):
\theta\text{ is primitive}\},
\qquad
\mathcal L_i(n):=\{\theta\in\mathcal A_{\{i\},\{i\}}(n):
\theta\text{ is a ladder atom}\}.
\]
Then $\mathcal M_n$ is the disjoint union of the following two families:
\[
\Atom(n)
\]
and
\[
\{\theta_1\theta_2:\ \theta_i\in
\mathcal A_{\{i\},\{i\}}(n)=\mathcal P_i(n)\cup\mathcal L_i(n),\
\gcd(\theta_1,\theta_2)=1\}.
\]
Equivalently, every non-atomic minimal generator is obtained by choosing,
for each coordinate, one primitive or ladder atom born exactly on that
coordinate, with the two chosen prime powers coprime.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$, and write its witness cover as $\Theta$. If
$\#\Theta=1$, then its sole atom has rank $n$, so $m\in\Atom(n)$.

Assume $\#\Theta>1$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:support-bound},
$\#\Theta\le2$, hence $\#\Theta=2$. The second assertion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:extremal-coordinate-separation} says, after relabeling the
two factors, that one atom has full support $\{1\}$ and the other has full
support $\{2\}$. Since each atom in a witness cover has a nonempty essential
coordinate not supplied by the other atom, the first has
$E_n=T_n=\{1\}$ and the second has $E_n=T_n=\{2\}$. Thus the two atoms lie in
the displayed families, and pairwise coprimeness is part of the witness-cover
definition. Corollary~\ref{cor:atomic-classification} gives the stated
primitive-or-ladder decomposition of each one-coordinate family.

Conversely, a single atom in $\Atom(n)$ is an $n$-witness cover by itself,
because lowering it lowers its rank. If
$\theta_i\in\mathcal A_{\{i\},\{i\}}(n)$ are coprime, then their
$T$-sets cover $\{1,2\}$ and each atom has its own essential coordinate.
Therefore $\{\theta_1,\theta_2\}$ is an $n$-witness cover, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} gives
$\theta_1\theta_2\in\mathcal M_n$. The two displayed families are disjoint
because their elements have respectively one and two prime-power factors.
\end{proof}

We now classify the three-support case. The statements below should be read
as a short pipeline rather than as four independent enumerations. First,
Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-three-types} forgets Fibonacci arithmetic and lists
the possible abstract witness shapes on three labelled prime coordinates.
Second, Theorem~\ref{thm:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types} restores the
primitive--ladder dichotomy and removes the one abstract shape that a
Fibonacci atom cannot have. Third,
Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests} translates each
remaining slot into explicit arithmetic data: primitive windows
$\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ and, for ladder slots, the single possible
prime-power candidate left by the rank formulas. Finally,
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} gives the actual
nonemptiness test. Thus the nine $\Gamma$-types are only the abstract menu;
realization for a fixed $n$ is decided later by choosing primes in the
allowed windows and checking coprimeness.

The transition from abstract type to arithmetic slot is as follows. If a slot
has $I=J$, it is primitive, except that singleton slots may also be filled by
a ladder atom with the same one-coordinate support. A nonsingleton primitive
slot may be filled only by a prime whose rank lies in
$\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$; the ranks $1,2,6,12$ are removed because no
primitive Fibonacci prime has those ranks. If
$I=\{i\}\subsetneq J$, the slot is a proper ladder slot, and the
prime-power formulas leave at most one candidate $\mathcal L_i^*(n)$ before
the final full-support test for $J$. A pair with
$1<\#I<\#J$ has no Fibonacci realization: it is neither primitive nor a
one-coordinate ladder. This explains the exclusion of
$\Gamma_2=\{(12,123)\}$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:abstract-three-types}
Let $X=\{1,2,3\}$. Consider finite collections
\[
\Delta=\{(I_1,J_1),\dots,(I_r,J_r)\},\qquad
\varnothing\ne I_a\subseteq J_a\subseteq X,
\]
up to permutation of $X$ and reordering of the pairs, subject to
\[
\bigcup_a J_a=X,\qquad
I_a\nsubseteq\bigcup_{b\ne a}J_b\quad(1\le a\le r).
\]
Then $r\le3$, and every such abstract three-coordinate witness type is
equivalent to exactly one of the following nine labelled types:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\Gamma_1:\ \{(123,123)\},&
\Gamma_2:\ \{(12,123)\},\\
\Gamma_3:\ \{(1,123)\},&
\Gamma_4:\ \{(12,12),(23,23)\},\\
\Gamma_5:\ \{(1,12),(23,23)\},&
\Gamma_6:\ \{(1,12),(3,23)\},\\
\Gamma_7:\ \{(1,1),(23,23)\},&
\Gamma_8:\ \{(1,1),(2,23)\},\\
\Gamma_9:\ \{(1,1),(2,2),(3,3)\}.
\end{array}
\]
Here, for instance, $123$ denotes $\{1,2,3\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each pair choose
$x_a\in I_a\setminus\bigcup_{b\ne a}J_b$. Then $x_a\in J_a$ and the
$x_a$ are distinct, so $r\le\#X=3$.

If $r=1$, then $J_1=X$. Up to relabeling, $I_1$ has size $3$, $2$, or $1$,
giving $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_2$, and $\Gamma_3$.

If $r=2$, neither $J_1$ nor $J_2$ is contained in the other, because each
has a private coordinate outside the other. Thus the unordered pair
$(J_1,J_2)$ is, up to relabeling, either $(12,23)$ or $(1,23)$. In the
overlapping case $(12,23)$, the first private coordinate is $1$ and the
second is $3$. Hence, again up to reversing the two pairs, the possible
essential sets are $(12,23)$, $(1,23)$, or $(1,3)$, giving
$\Gamma_4$, $\Gamma_5$, and $\Gamma_6$. In the disjoint case $(1,23)$, the
first essential set must be $1$, while the second is either $23$ or a
singleton; these are $\Gamma_7$ and $\Gamma_8$.

If $r=3$, the three chosen private coordinates exhaust $X$. Since each
coordinate is absent from the two $J$-sets to which it is not assigned, no
$J_a$ can contain any coordinate other than its own private coordinate.
Thus the only possibility is
$\{(1,1),(2,2),(3,3)\}$, namely $\Gamma_9$.
The cases are mutually exclusive by $r$, by the intersection pattern of the
$J$-sets, and by the sizes of the $I$-sets after those data are fixed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types}
Assume $\omega(n)=3$. Every $n$-witness cover has one of the abstract types
\[
\Gamma_1,\Gamma_3,\Gamma_4,\Gamma_5,\Gamma_6,\Gamma_7,\Gamma_8,\Gamma_9,
\]
and no $n$-witness cover has type $\Gamma_2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Theta$ be an $n$-witness cover. The collection of pairs
\[
\{(E_n(\theta),T_n(\theta)):\theta\in\Theta\}
\]
satisfies the abstract hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-three-types}; coverage and irredundance are exactly
the last two conditions in Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}. Hence its
type is one of $\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_9$.

By Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}, a Fibonacci atom has either
$E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$, in the primitive case, or
$E_n(\theta)$ empty or a singleton, in the ladder case. In a witness cover
$E_n(\theta)$ cannot be empty. Therefore no atom can have
$1<\#E_n(\theta)<\#T_n(\theta)$. The only abstract type in the displayed
list requiring such an atom is $\Gamma_2$, represented by $(12,123)$, so
$\Gamma_2$ is excluded.

This proves the asserted containment in the eight displayed types and the
exclusion of $\Gamma_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Primitive and ladder atom families in three coordinates]
\label{prop:three-coordinate-atom-families}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$ and identify subsets of
$[3]=\{1,2,3\}$ with the corresponding coordinate subsets of
$\mathcal P(n)$. For nonempty $J\subseteq[3]$ define the primitive family
\[
P_J(n):=\{\theta\in\mathcal A_{J,J}(n):\theta\text{ is a prime}\},
\]
and, for $i\in J$, define the ladder family
\[
L_{i,J}(n):=\{\theta\in\mathcal A_{\{i\},J}(n):
\theta=p^e\text{ with }e>1\}.
\]
Then, for every nonempty $I\subseteq J\subseteq[3]$,
\[
\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)=
\begin{cases}
P_J(n), & I=J,\ \#J>1,\\[1mm]
P_{\{i\}}(n)\cup L_{i,\{i\}}(n), & I=J=\{i\},\\[1mm]
L_{i,J}(n), & I=\{i\}\subsetneq J,\\[1mm]
\varnothing, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\theta\in\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)$. By definition, $\theta$ is an atom with
$E_n(\theta)=I$ and $T_n(\theta)=J$. If $\theta=p$ is primitive, then
$d^-(\theta)=1$, so every full coordinate is lost after lowering; hence
$E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$ and $\theta\in P_J(n)$. Conversely, every
$\theta\in P_J(n)$ has $(E_n(\theta),T_n(\theta))=(J,J)$ by definition.

Now suppose $\theta=p^e$ with $e>1$. The primitive--ladder dichotomy
(Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}) gives that $E_n(\theta)$ is
either empty or a singleton. Since $\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)$ is defined only for
nonempty $I$, any such atom in it has $I=\{i\}$ for some $i\in J$, and it
lies in $L_{i,J}(n)$. Conversely, every member of $L_{i,J}(n)$ is a ladder
atom with $(E_n(\theta),T_n(\theta))=(\{i\},J)$ by definition.

Combining the primitive case with the ladder case gives the four displayed
alternatives. If $\#I>1$ and $I\ne J$, the primitive case is impossible
because primitive atoms have $I=J$, while the ladder case is impossible
because ladder atoms have singleton essential support. Hence the remaining
families are empty.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Arithmetic tests for the three-coordinate atom families]
\label{prop:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$, and write
$a_i:=\nu_{\ell_i}(n)$. For nonempty $J\subseteq[3]$ put
\[
\mathcal R_J(n):=\{d\mid n:\ \{k:\nu_{\ell_k}(d)=a_k\}=J\},
\qquad
\mathcal R_J^\circ(n):=\mathcal R_J(n)\setminus\{1,2,6,12\}.
\]
Also put
\[
\Pi_\alpha(d):=\{p\text{ prime}:\alpha(p)=d\}.
\]
Then
\[
P_J(n)=\bigcup_{d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)}\Pi_\alpha(d),
\]
where a prime $p$ is identified with the primitive atom $p$. In particular
$P_J(n)\ne\varnothing$ if and only if $\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)\ne\varnothing$.
More generally, after forbidding a finite set $S$ of primes,
$P_J(n)\setminus S\ne\varnothing$ if and only if there exists
$d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ such that
\[
\#\Pi_\alpha(d)>
\#\{p\in S:\alpha(p)=d\},
\qquad
\#\Pi_\alpha(d)=\sum_{c\mid d}\mu(d/c)\,\omega(F_c).
\]

For the ladder families, define a candidate set $\mathcal L_i^*(n)$ as follows.
If $\ell_i\notin\{2,5\}$, write
$z_i:=\alpha(\ell_i)$, $h_i:=\nu_{\ell_i}(F_{z_i})$, and
$s_i:=\nu_{\ell_i}(z_i)$. Then
\[
\mathcal L_i^*(n):=
\begin{cases}
\{4\}, & \ell_i=2,\ a_i=1,\\
\{2^{a_i+2}\}, & \ell_i=2,\ a_i\ge2,\\
\{5^{a_i}\}, & \ell_i=5,\ a_i\ge2,\\
\varnothing, & \ell_i=5,\ a_i=1,\\
\{\ell_i^{h_i+a_i-s_i}\}, &
   \ell_i\notin\{2,5\}\text{ and }a_i>s_i,\\
\varnothing, &
   \ell_i\notin\{2,5\}\text{ and }a_i\le s_i .
\end{cases}
\]
For each $i\in J$,
\[
L_{i,J}(n)=
\{\lambda\in\mathcal L_i^*(n):\alpha(\lambda)\mid n,\ 
\{k:\nu_{\ell_k}(\alpha(\lambda))=a_k\}=J\}.
\]
Thus every ladder family $L_{i,J}(n)$ is empty or consists of the single
displayed prime power.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $p\in P_J(n)$ and put $d=\alpha(p)$. Since $p$ is a primitive atom,
$E_n(p)=T_n(p)$, and the condition $T_n(p)=J$ says exactly that
$d\in\mathcal R_J(n)$. No prime has rank $1$ or $2$, because
$F_1=F_2=1$. Also no prime has rank $6$ or $12$: the prime divisors of
$F_6=8$ and $F_{12}=144$ are $2$ and $3$, with
$\alpha(2)=3$ and $\alpha(3)=4$. Hence $d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$.
Conversely, if $d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ and $p\in\Pi_\alpha(d)$, then
$d\mid n$ and the full-coordinate set of $d$ is $J$; since $p$ is primitive,
$p\in P_J(n)$. This proves the displayed identity for $P_J(n)$.

The primitive divisor theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors}, supplies
at least one prime of rank $d$ for every
$d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$. Therefore $P_J(n)$ is nonempty exactly when
$\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ is nonempty. The version with a forbidden finite set
$S$ is the same statement rank by rank. The formula for $\#\Pi_\alpha(d)$ is
the prime case of the M\"obius inversion in \eqref{eq:mobius-birth}.

It remains to identify the ladder families. Let
$\theta=p^e\in L_{i,J}(n)$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}, the essential coordinate of a
ladder atom is the prime coordinate $p$ itself; hence $p=\ell_i$. The
prime-power formulas in Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders} now leave
only one possible exponent once the $\ell_i$-adic coordinate of
$\alpha(p^e)$ is required to equal $a_i$. If $\ell_i=2$, the atomic ladder
levels are $4$ of rank $6$ and $2^e$ of rank $3\cdot2^{e-2}$ for $e\ge4$;
therefore the candidate is $4$ when $a_i=1$ and $2^{a_i+2}$ when $a_i\ge2$.
If $\ell_i=5$, the rank is $5^e$, so a nonprimitive ladder with full
$5$-coordinate $a_i$ exists only for $a_i\ge2$, and then it is $5^{a_i}$.
Finally suppose $\ell_i\notin\{2,5\}$. At atomic ladder levels,
\[
\alpha(\ell_i^e)=\ell_i^{e-h_i}z_i\qquad(e>h_i),
\]
so the full-coordinate equation
$\nu_{\ell_i}(\alpha(\ell_i^e))=a_i$ forces
$e=h_i+a_i-s_i$, and this is an atomic ladder exponent precisely when
$a_i>s_i$.

Thus every member of $L_{i,J}(n)$ lies in $\mathcal L_i^*(n)$. The remaining
conditions for membership in $L_{i,J}(n)$ are exactly that its rank divide
$n$ and that its full-coordinate set be $J$. Conversely, if
$\lambda\in\mathcal L_i^*(n)$ satisfies those two displayed conditions, the
same prime-power formulas show that $\lambda$ is an atomic ladder. Lowering
$\lambda$ reduces only the $\ell_i$-coordinate of its rank, so
$E_n(\lambda)=\{i\}$ and $T_n(\lambda)=J$. Hence
$\lambda\in L_{i,J}(n)$.
The displayed primitive windows and ladder candidate sets also exhaust the
exceptional cases: $2$ and $5$ are handled by their separate ladder lines,
the ranks $1,2,6,12$ are removed from primitive windows, and any
Wall--Sun--Sun plateau for $p\ne2,5$ is already absorbed in the parameter
$h_i=\nu_{\ell_i}(F_{z_i})$ before the single possible exponent
$h_i+a_i-s_i$ is formed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Three-coordinate nonemptiness criterion]
\label{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$ and use the atom families
$\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)$ from
Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-atom-families}.
Let $\Delta=\{(I_1,J_1),\dots,(I_r,J_r)\}$ be one of the labelled admissible
types among
$\Gamma_1,\Gamma_3,\Gamma_4,\Gamma_5,\Gamma_6,\Gamma_7,\Gamma_8,\Gamma_9$.
Here labelled means that the subsets $I_j,J_j\subseteq\{1,2,3\}$ are tied to
the fixed coordinate supports $E_n$ and $T_n$ for the ordered prime set
$\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$.
Then the labelled type $\Delta$ is realized by an element of
$\mathcal M_n$ if and only if the following arithmetic selection test
succeeds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item for every nonsingleton primitive slot $I_j=J_j$, $\#J_j>1$, choose a prime
$\theta_j\in\Pi_\alpha(d_j)$ for some
$d_j\in\mathcal R_{J_j}^\circ(n)$;
\item for every proper ladder slot $I_j=\{i\}\subsetneq J_j$, choose the
unique candidate in $L_{i,J_j}(n)$ given by
Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests};
\item for every singleton slot $I_j=J_j=\{i\}$, choose either a primitive
prime from $\bigcup_{d\in\mathcal R_{\{i\}}^\circ(n)}\Pi_\alpha(d)$ or the
unique ladder candidate in $L_{i,\{i\}}(n)$;
\item the chosen prime powers have distinct underlying primes.
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently, after the possible ladder candidates are fixed, the primitive
choices are a finite matching problem in the explicit rank windows
$\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$, with vertex multiplicities
$\#\Pi_\alpha(d)=\sum_{c\mid d}\mu(d/c)\omega(F_c)$ and with any already used
ladder prime deleted from the matching pool.

In that case $m=\theta_1\cdots\theta_r$ is the realizing minimal generator,
and every realization with these fixed labelled coordinate supports is
obtained uniquely in this way, up to the order of factors with identical
labelled data.

Equivalently, the displayed rows $\Gamma_i$ are unlabelled representatives
modulo coordinate relabeling: an unlabelled type $\Gamma_i$ is realized if and
only if the preceding labelled arithmetic selection test succeeds for one of
its coordinate permutations. The excluded type $\Gamma_2$ is never realized.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $m\in\mathcal M_n$ realizes the labelled type
$\Delta$. Write its witness cover as
$\Theta=\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\}$ with the factors ordered so that
$(E_n(\theta_j),T_n(\theta_j))=(I_j,J_j)$. By definition
$\theta_j\in\mathcal A_{I_j,J_j}(n)$, and pairwise coprimeness is part of
Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}. Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-atom-families}
places each such factor in the corresponding primitive or ladder family, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests} translates those
families into the displayed rank-window and ladder-candidate conditions. Thus
the arithmetic selection test succeeds.

Conversely, assume that the arithmetic selection test succeeds. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests}, each selected
prime power lies in the required primitive or ladder family, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-atom-families} gives
$\theta_j\in\mathcal A_{I_j,J_j}(n)$. Distinct underlying primes are exactly
pairwise coprimeness for prime powers. Because $\Delta$ is an abstract
witness type, the sets $J_j$ cover $[3]$, and for every $j$ one has
$I_j\nsubseteq\bigcup_{h\ne j}J_h$. Translating back to $T_n$ and $E_n$, the
set $\Theta=\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\}$ satisfies the coverage and
irredundance conditions in Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}; coprimeness
gives the remaining condition. Hence $\Theta$ is an $n$-witness cover, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} gives
$m=\prod_j\theta_j\in\mathcal M_n$ with type $\Delta$.

 The factorization of $m$ into prime powers is unique. Therefore, after the
labelled supports $I_j,J_j\subseteq\{1,2,3\}$ are fixed, any realization
recovers the same atomic factors and hence the same tuple, except for
permuting factors that have identical labelled data. For unlabelled rows, one
first chooses a coordinate relabeling and then applies this fixed-labelled
criterion. The final exclusion of $\Gamma_2$ is
Theorem~\ref{thm:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:three-coordinate-realization}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$. After the labelled coordinate
supports $E_n,T_n\subseteq\{1,2,3\}$ are fixed, every element of
$\mathcal M_n$ arises from exactly one nonempty arithmetic selection in the
rank windows $\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ and the explicit ladder candidates
$\mathcal L_i^*(n)$ belonging to one fixed labelled coordinate permutation of
one of the eight admissible types in
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}; the selected prime powers
must have distinct underlying primes. The unlabelled $\Gamma$-rows are only
representatives modulo coordinate relabeling.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} together with
Theorems~\ref{thm:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types} and
\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete low-support classification package]
\label{thm:low-support-classification-package}
Let $n\ge2$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $\omega(n)=1$, then $\mathcal M_n=\Atom(n)$.
\item If $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}$, then $\mathcal M_n$ is the
 disjoint union of the atomic family $\Atom(n)$ and the products
 $\theta_1\theta_2$, where
 $\theta_i\in\mathcal A_{\{i\},\{i\}}(n)$ and
 $\gcd(\theta_1,\theta_2)=1$.
\item If $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$, then every
 $m\in\mathcal M_n$ is obtained uniquely after fixing the labelled coordinate
 supports $E_n,T_n\subseteq\{1,2,3\}$, up to relabeling equal slots, from one
 coordinate permutation of one of the eight rows in
 Table~\ref{tab:three-coordinate-classification}. The displayed rows are
 representatives modulo coordinate relabeling: all coordinate permutations of
 every displayed row are part of the classification. In the table,
 $S_i(n):=P_{\{i\}}(n)\cup L_{i,\{i\}}(n)$; a product of selected slots is
 admitted exactly when the selected prime powers have distinct underlying
 primes.
\end{enumerate}

In every primitive slot $P_J(n)$, availability is decided by the finite rank

\#\Pi_\alpha(d)=\sum_{c\mid d}\mu(d/c)\omega(F_c).
\]
In every ladder slot $L_{i,J}(n)$, availability is decided by the single
candidate set $\mathcal L_i^*(n)$ and the full-support test
\[
L_{i,J}(n)=\{\lambda\in\mathcal L_i^*(n):\alpha(\lambda)\mid n,
\ \{k:\nu_{\ell_k}(\alpha(\lambda))=\nu_{\ell_k}(n)\}=J\}.
\]
No minimal generator has the excluded type $\Gamma_2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The one-coordinate assertion is Corollary~\ref{cor:one-support-complete-classification}.
The two-coordinate assertion is Corollary~\ref{cor:two-support-complete-classification}.
Assume now that $\omega(n)=3$. Theorem~\ref{thm:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types}
reduces every witness cover to the eight listed abstract types and excludes
$\Gamma_2$. Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-atom-families} translates
an abstract slot $(I,J)$ into exactly one of the displayed primitive, ladder,
or singleton families: nonsingleton slots with $I=J$ are $P_J(n)$, proper
ladder slots $I=\{i\}\subsetneq J$ are $L_{i,J}(n)$, and singleton slots are
$S_i(n)=P_{\{i\}}(n)\cup L_{i,\{i\}}(n)$. Substituting these translations into
the eight admissible abstract types of Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-three-types}
gives precisely the rows of Table~\ref{tab:three-coordinate-classification}.

It remains only to identify the tests in the last column. For primitive slots,
Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests} gives
$P_J(n)=\bigcup_{d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)}\Pi_\alpha(d)$ and, after deleting
previously used underlying primes, the stated finite multiplicity criterion.
For ladder slots, the same proposition gives the displayed singleton-candidate
formula for $L_{i,J}(n)$. Pairwise coprimeness of the chosen prime powers is
exactly the distinct-underlying-prime condition because all slots are filled by
prime powers. Hence the table's row test is the arithmetic selection test of
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}. That theorem also gives the
converse construction and uniqueness, up to reordering slots with identical
labelled data, while Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} identifies the
resulting products with the elements of $\mathcal M_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Complete low-support birth layers]
\label{cor:low-support-birth-layer-package}
Let $n\ge2$ and suppose $\omega(n)\le3$. Define $\mathcal G_n$ by the
corresponding list in Theorem~\ref{thm:low-support-classification-package}:
for $\omega(n)=1$ take $\mathcal G_n=\Atom(n)$; for
$\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}$ take the union of $\Atom(n)$ with the
two-coordinate products displayed in Theorem~\ref{thm:low-support-classification-package};
and for $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$ take all products admitted by
the eight rows of Table~\ref{tab:three-coordinate-classification}, after
permuting the coordinates and imposing the stated arithmetic realization and
coprimeness tests. Then the full birth layer is
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{g\in\mathcal G_n}\{q\in D_n:\ g\mid q\}.
\]
The union is taken inside the divisor lattice $D_n$; it is not asserted to be
disjoint, and overlaps occur exactly when a divisor of $F_n$ is divisible by
more than one listed generator.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:low-support-classification-package} identifies the displayed
set $\mathcal G_n$ with $\mathcal M_n$ in all cases $\omega(n)
\le3$, including the boundary convention $\mathcal M_2=\varnothing=\Atom(2)$.
The upper-fiber theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:upper-fiber}, gives for every
$n\ge2$ the principal-upper-ideal decomposition
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal M_n}\{q\in D_n:\ m\mid q\}.
\]
Substituting $\mathcal G_n=\mathcal M_n$ gives the displayed formula. Since the
same divisor $q\in D_n$ may be divisible by two distinct minimal generators,
the formula is a set-theoretic union of upper ideals in $D_n$, not a disjoint
union.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Primitive three-coordinate skeleton]
\label{cor:primitive-three-skeleton}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}$ and that
$n_S:=\prod_{i\in S}\ell_i^{\nu_{\ell_i}(n)}$ satisfies
$n_S\ge 3$ and $n_S\notin\{6,12\}$ for every nonempty
$S\subseteq[3]$. Then the four primitive-only three-coordinate
types $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_4$, $\Gamma_7$, and $\Gamma_9$ are realized in
$\mathcal M_n$. More precisely, after choosing primitive primes
$\pi_S$ with $\alpha(\pi_S)=n_S$, the following minimal generators realize
these types:
\[
\pi_{123},\qquad
\pi_{12}\pi_{23},\qquad
\pi_1\pi_{23},\qquad
\pi_1\pi_2\pi_3 .
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The hypothesis and Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors} provide the indicated
primitive primes. A primitive prime for $n_S$ is a primitive atom with
$E_n(\pi_S)=T_n(\pi_S)=S$. The four displayed collections of coordinate
sets are precisely the primitive-only types $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_4$,
$\Gamma_7$, and $\Gamma_9$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-three-types}, and
the chosen primes are pairwise distinct. The
pairwise-coprime selection criterion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} therefore gives the four
minimal generators.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:wss}
The Wall--Sun--Sun condition enters through
the prime-power formula in Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders}: for
$p\neq 2,5$, one has
$\alpha(p^2)=\alpha(p)$ if and only if $\nu_p(F_{\alpha(p)})\ge 2$, which is
precisely the definition of a Wall--Sun--Sun prime
\cite{SunSun1992FibonacciFLT}. No such primes are currently known, and their
nonexistence would imply that every odd prime contributes an immediate ladder
step above the first atomic level.
\end{remark}

\section{Connected block factorizations}\label{sec:connected}
%% ====================================================================

Throughout this section, write
$n_C:=\prod_{\ell\in C}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n)}$ for nonempty
$C\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:support-hypergraph}
Let $q=\prod_i p_i^{e_i}\in D_n$, and put
$\lambda_i:=\alpha(p_i^{e_i})$. The \emph{support hypergraph} $H_n(q)$ has
vertex set $\mathcal P(n)$ and one edge
\[
\operatorname{supp}_n(\lambda_i):=
\{\ell\in\mathcal P(n):\nu_\ell(\lambda_i)>0\}
\]
for each nonempty such support. We call $q$ \emph{connected} if
$H_n(q)$ is connected on its vertex set. Let
\[
B_n^{\mathrm{conn}}:=\{q\in B_n:\ H_n(q)\text{ is connected}\},\qquad
A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n):=\#B_n^{\mathrm{conn}},
\]
and
\[
\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{conn}}:=
\{m\in\mathcal M_n:\ H_n(m)\text{ is connected}\},\qquad
M^{\mathrm{conn}}(n):=\#\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{conn}}.
\]
For a connected component $C$ of $H_n(q)$, define $q_C$ to be the product of
those prime-power factors $p_i^{e_i}$ whose support edge lies in $C$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:coprime-embedding}
Let $\Pi$ be a set partition of $\mathcal P(n)$. For each block
$C\in\Pi$, choose $q_C\in D_{n_C}$. Then the integers $q_C$ are pairwise
coprime. If in addition $q_C\in B_{n_C}$ for every $C$, then
\[
q:=\prod_{C\in\Pi}q_C
\]
lies in $B_n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $C\ne C'$, then $\gcd(n_C,n_{C'})=1$, so
\[
\gcd(F_{n_C},F_{n_{C'}})=F_{\gcd(n_C,n_{C'})}=F_1=1
\]
by Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}. Since $q_C\mid F_{n_C}$ and
$q_{C'}\mid F_{n_{C'}}$, the chosen factors are pairwise coprime.

When $q_C\in B_{n_C}$ for every $C$, we have $\alpha(q_C)=n_C$. Repeated use
of the lcm formula in Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics} gives
\[
\alpha\!\left(\prod_{C\in\Pi}q_C\right)
=\lcmop(n_C:\ C\in\Pi)=\prod_{C\in\Pi}n_C=n.
\]
Also $q_C\mid F_{n_C}\mid F_n$, so the pairwise coprime product divides
$F_n$. Hence $q\in B_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:birth-connected-factorization}
Every $q\in B_n$ has a unique factorization
\[
q=\prod_{C\in\pi(q)}q_C
\]
indexed by the connected components $\pi(q)$ of $H_n(q)$, where
$q_C\in B_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$ for every component $C$. Conversely, if
$\Pi$ is a set partition of $\mathcal P(n)$ and
$q_C\in B_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$ for every $C\in\Pi$, then
$\prod_C q_C\in B_n$ and its connected components are exactly the blocks of
$\Pi$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition-formula}
A(n)=\sum_{\Pi\in\mathsf{Part}(\mathcal P(n))}
\prod_{C\in\Pi}A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n_C),
\end{equation}
with the convention that an empty factor contributes zero whenever
$B_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}=\varnothing$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $q=\prod_i p_i^{e_i}\in B_n$ and let $C$ be a component of $H_n(q)$.
Every prime-power factor contributing to $q_C$ has rank support contained in
$C$, so $\alpha(q_C)$ divides $n_C$. Conversely, fix $\ell\in C$. Since
$\alpha(q)=n$ is the lcm of the ranks $\alpha(p_i^{e_i})$, some factor of
$q$ has $\ell$-adic rank exponent equal to $\nu_\ell(n)$. Its support edge
contains $\ell$, and therefore belongs to the component $C$. Thus the same
factor occurs in $q_C$, and the $\ell$-adic exponent of $\alpha(q_C)$ is
$\nu_\ell(n)$. This holds for every $\ell\in C$, while no coordinate outside
$C$ occurs in $\alpha(q_C)$; hence $\alpha(q_C)=n_C$ and
$q_C\in B_{n_C}$. The component of $H_{n_C}(q_C)$ on the vertex set $C$ is
connected by construction, so $q_C\in B_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$.

Uniqueness follows from unique prime-power factorization of $q$ and the
unique connected-component partition of a hypergraph. The converse is exactly
Theorem~\ref{thm:coprime-embedding}; because each $q_C$ is connected on its
own block and no edge of $H_n(\prod_C q_C)$ meets two different blocks, the
components are precisely the blocks of $\Pi$.

Counting the converse construction over all set partitions gives
\eqref{eq:partition-formula}, and uniqueness shows that no element is counted
twice.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mingenerator-connected-factorization}
Every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ has a unique factorization
\[
m=\prod_{C\in\pi(m)}m_C
\]
over the connected components of $H_n(m)$, with
$m_C\in\mathcal M_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$ for every $C$. Conversely, if
$\Pi$ is a set partition of $\mathcal P(n)$ and
$m_C\in\mathcal M_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$ for every $C\in\Pi$, then
$\prod_C m_C\in\mathcal M_n$ and has component partition $\Pi$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimal-partition-formula}
\#\mathcal M_n=
\sum_{\Pi\in\mathsf{Part}(\mathcal P(n))}
\prod_{C\in\Pi}M^{\mathrm{conn}}(n_C).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$. Theorem~\ref{thm:birth-connected-factorization}
gives $m_C\in B_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$ for each component. If some
$s_C\in B_{n_C}$ satisfied $s_C\mid m_C$ and $s_C<m_C$, then
\[
s_C\prod_{D\ne C}m_D
\]
would divide $m$. The factors are pairwise coprime by
Theorem~\ref{thm:coprime-embedding}, and the displayed product lies in
$B_n$ by the same theorem. This contradicts the minimality of $m$ in $B_n$.
Therefore $m_C\in\mathcal M_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$.

Conversely, let $m=\prod_{C\in\Pi}m_C$ with
$m_C\in\mathcal M_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:coprime-embedding}
puts $m$ in $B_n$. If $s\mid m$ and $s\in B_n$, write
$s=\prod_C s_C$ with $s_C\mid m_C$; pairwise coprimeness of the block
factors makes this decomposition unique. Since
\[
n=\alpha(s)=\lcmop(\alpha(s_C):C\in\Pi)
\]
and $\alpha(s_C)$ divides $n_C$, the disjointness of the coordinate sets
forces $\alpha(s_C)=n_C$ for every $C$. Thus $s_C\in B_{n_C}$ for every
$C$, and minimality of $m_C$ gives $s_C=m_C$. Hence $s=m$, proving
$m\in\mathcal M_n$. The component statement and uniqueness are the same as
in Theorem~\ref{thm:birth-connected-factorization}. Counting unique
decompositions yields \eqref{eq:minimal-partition-formula}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Connected inversion]\label{cor:connected-inversion}
The connected birth counts are determined from the total counts by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:connected-inversion}
A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n)=
\sum_{\Pi\in\mathsf{Part}(\mathcal P(n))}
(-1)^{|\Pi|-1}(|\Pi|-1)!
\prod_{C\in\Pi}A(n_C).
\end{equation}
The same inversion holds for minimal generators:
\[
M^{\mathrm{conn}}(n)=
\sum_{\Pi\in\mathsf{Part}(\mathcal P(n))}
(-1)^{|\Pi|-1}(|\Pi|-1)!
\prod_{C\in\Pi}\#\mathcal M_{n_C}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:partition-formula} is the ordinary exponential formula on
the finite set $\mathcal P(n)$ with block weight
$C\mapsto A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n_C)$. M\"obius inversion in the partition lattice
gives
\[
\mu_{\mathsf{Part}}(\Pi,\hat1)=(-1)^{|\Pi|-1}(|\Pi|-1)!,
\]
where $\hat1$ is the partition containing all coordinates in one block.
Applying the same partition-lattice inversion to
\eqref{eq:minimal-partition-formula} gives the minimal-generator formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:four-support-reduction}
If $\omega(n)=4$, every element of $B_n$ and every element of
$\mathcal M_n$ is obtained uniquely by choosing connected blocks whose sizes
form one of the set-partition types
\[
4,\qquad 3+1,\qquad 2+2,\qquad 2+1+1,\qquad 1+1+1+1.
\]
Consequently, after the one-, two-, and three-coordinate classifications are
known, the only new four-coordinate data are the connected four-coordinate
sets $B_n^{\mathrm{conn}}$ and $\mathcal M_n^{\mathrm{conn}}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is Theorems~\ref{thm:birth-connected-factorization} and
\ref{thm:mingenerator-connected-factorization} specialized to the set
$\mathcal P(n)$ of size four. Every disconnected partition has all blocks of
size at most three, so its factors are controlled by the previously proved
one-, two-, and three-coordinate classifications. The only partition with a
block of size four is the single-block partition, which is precisely the
connected four-coordinate case.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:odd-primitive-cover-realization}
Let $n\ge3$ be odd. Assume that for every nonempty
$S\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$ the index
$n_S=\prod_{\ell\in S}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n)}$ is not equal to $1$ and admits a
primitive prime $\pi_S$ with $\alpha(\pi_S)=n_S$; in particular this holds
whenever no $n_S$ is $6$ or $12$. Then every set partition
$\Pi$ of $\mathcal P(n)$ gives a minimal generator
\[
m_\Pi:=\prod_{C\in\Pi}\pi_C\in\mathcal M_n.
\]
Thus
\[
\#\mathcal M_n\ge \mathsf B_{\omega(n)},
\]
where $\mathsf B_k$ is the $k$th Bell number.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For each block $C$, the prime $\pi_C$ is a primitive atom of rank $n_C$ and
therefore belongs to $\mathcal M_{n_C}^{\mathrm{conn}}$: its support
hypergraph has the single edge $C$. Distinct blocks have coprime Fibonacci
ambient factors by Theorem~\ref{thm:coprime-embedding}, so the primes
$\pi_C$ are pairwise coprime. Theorem~\ref{thm:mingenerator-connected-factorization}
then gives $m_\Pi\in\mathcal M_n$.

The connected-component partition of $m_\Pi$ is exactly $\Pi$, so different
set partitions give different minimal generators. There are
$\mathsf B_{\omega(n)}$ set partitions of $\mathcal P(n)$.
For odd $n$, every nonempty $n_S$ is odd and hence cannot be $6$ or $12$;
when $n_S\ge3$, Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors} supplies the required
primitive prime.
\end{proof}

\section{Numerical evidence}\label{sec:numerical}
%% ====================================================================

Table~\ref{tab:birth-layer-data} displays the birth layer cardinality $A(n)$
and the number of minimal generators $\#\mathcal M_n$ for $2\le n\le 30$,
computed by exhaustive enumeration. Several features visible in the data are
consistent with the structural results of the preceding sections.
First, $\#\mathcal M_n=1$ whenever $\omega(n)=1$ and
$n\notin\{19,25,27\}$; the exceptions arise from composite Fibonacci numbers
with multiple atomic families at the same prime-power index.
Second, the jump at $n=12$ ($A(12)=10$, $\#\mathcal M_{12}=3$) and at $n=24$
($A(24)=55$, $\#\mathcal M_{24}=4$) reflects the interaction of the
exceptional prime $2$ with the non-primitive layers at indices $6$ and $12$.
Third, the first nonempty three-coordinate layer occurs at $n=30$ and has
$\#\mathcal M_{30}=5$; the displayed witness covers realize exactly the
types $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_4$, $\Gamma_5$, $\Gamma_7$, and $\Gamma_8$.
The remaining admissible shapes $\Gamma_3$, $\Gamma_6$, and $\Gamma_9$ are
family shapes governed by the nonemptiness criterion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}, not consequences of the
$n=30$ data.

The table is exhaustively verified for $2\le n\le30$ by the reproducibility
files included with the source package,
\[
\text{\texttt{scripts/compute\_birth\_layer\_table.py}},\qquad
\text{\texttt{generated/tab\_birth\_layer\_data.tex}} .
\]
The following proposition records the finite divisor/rank interface used for
that computation.

\begin{proposition}[Effective divisor/rank computation]
\label{prop:effective-birth-layer-computation}
Let $n\ge2$ and suppose the integer factorization of $F_n$ is known. Then
$B_n$, $A(n)$, and $\mathcal M_n$ are computable by the following finite
algorithm.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Enumerate the divisor set $D_n=\operatorname{Div}(F_n)$ from the
 factorization of $F_n$.
\item Enumerate the divisor set $\operatorname{Div}(n)$. For each
 $q\in D_n$, compute
 \[
 \alpha_n(q):=\min\{d\mid n:q\mid F_d\}.
 \]
\item Set $B_n=\{q\in D_n:q\ge2,\ \alpha_n(q)=n\}$ and
 $A(n)=\#B_n$.
\item Sort $B_n$ by divisibility-compatible order, for instance by size, and
 retain exactly those $m\in B_n$ for which no previously retained element
 divides $m$; the retained set is $\mathcal M_n$.
\end{enumerate}
The number of divisibility tests $q\mid F_d$ is at most
$\tau(F_n)\tau(n)$, and the minimal-generator extraction requires at most
$\tau(F_n)^2$ divisor comparisons. Thus, apart from the integer factorization
of $F_n$, the computation uses polynomially many arithmetic operations in
$\tau(F_n)$ and $\tau(n)$; with naive Fibonacci evaluation it is bounded by
$O(n\tau(F_n)\tau(n)+\tau(F_n)^2)$ integer divisibility/comparison steps.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since every $q\in D_n$ divides $F_n$, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}
shows that $q\mid F_d$ for some $d$ if and only if $\alpha(q)\mid d$. In
particular $q\mid F_n$ implies $\alpha(q)\mid n$, so the least positive index
at which $q$ appears is exactly
\[
\alpha(q)=\min\{d\mid n:q\mid F_d\}=\alpha_n(q).
\]
Hence the test in step (c) gives precisely the definition
$B_n=\{q\in D_n:\alpha(q)=n\}$, with the harmless exclusion of $q=1$ already
consistent with the convention $\alpha(1)=1$ and $n\ge2$. Counting this set
gives $A(n)$.

It remains to justify step (d). The set $B_n$ is finite. If its elements are
processed in increasing size, then every proper divisor of the current element
has already been processed. Therefore an element is retained exactly when no
smaller element of $B_n$ divides it, which is the definition of being minimal
in the divisibility poset. This retained set is $\mathcal M_n$.

The bounds are immediate from the loops. There are $\tau(F_n)$ choices of
$q$ and $\tau(n)$ possible indices $d$ in the rank test. Comparing each pair
of elements of $B_n$ gives the crude upper bound $\tau(F_n)^2$ for the
minimality step. Computing each $F_d$ by the elementary recurrence for
$d\le n$ gives the stated naive evaluation bound; faster doubling methods can
replace this without changing the finite divisor/rank interface.
\end{proof}

The script implements Proposition~\ref{prop:effective-birth-layer-computation}:
for each $2\le n\le30$ it computes $F_n$ exactly, enumerates all divisors of
$F_n$, determines $\alpha(q)$ by testing the divisors of $n$, forms
$B_n=\{q\mid F_n:\alpha(q)=n\}$, and extracts $\mathcal M_n$ as the minimal
divisibility elements. The generated table source contains the same numerical
columns as Table~\ref{tab:birth-layer-data}, including the displayed
minimal-generator sets. The comments following the table, such as the
assignment of the $n=30$ covers to the types $\Gamma_i$, are derived from the
theorems above rather than from additional computation.

\section{Further questions}\label{sec:questions}
%% ====================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Use the three-coordinate nonemptiness criterion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} to study the distribution of
the admissible types, then resolve the connected cores when $\omega(n)=4$. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:four-support-reduction}, only the connected part is
genuinely new.
\item Obtain arithmetic or asymptotic control of the connected counts
$A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n)$ and $M^{\mathrm{conn}}(n)$. The partition-lattice
formulas reduce global invariants to these kernels but do not yet estimate
their frequency or size.
\item Extend the witness-cover formalism to Lucas sequences and, more
generally, to strong divisibility sequences equipped with a
rank-of-apparition map and a primitive-divisor theorem.
\end{enumerate}
