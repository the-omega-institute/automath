\section{Finite verification for the three-coordinate table}
\label{app:three-coordinate-checklist}

\begin{proposition}[Reader's checklist for the three-coordinate table]
\label{prop:three-coordinate-table-checklist}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,
\ell_2,\ell_3\}$.  The following finite procedure returns exactly
$\mathcal M_n$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For every nonempty $J\subseteq[3]$, compute the exact-rank window
$\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ and the rank classes
$\Pi_\alpha(d)$ for $d\in\mathcal R_J^\circ(n)$ by
Lemma~\ref{article-lem:primitive-prime-mobius-count}. This gives every prime
slot $P_J(n)$.
\item For each coordinate $i$, compute the single possible ladder candidate
$\mathcal L_i^*(n)$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests}, then retain it in
$L_{i,J}(n)$ exactly for those $J$ for which its rank divides $n$ and has
full-coordinate set $J$.
\item For each coordinate permutation of each of the eight rows in
Table~\ref{tab:three-coordinate-classification}, replace the displayed slots
by the finite sets found in (i) and (ii), with
$S_i(n)=P_{\{i\}}(n)\cup L_{i,\{i\}}(n)$.
\item Keep precisely those row fillings whose selected prime powers have
distinct underlying primes, equivalently those satisfying the Hall
inequalities in Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-slot-selection}; multiply the selected
prime powers and insert the product into a set, thereby removing repetitions
caused by stabilizers and coordinate permutations.
\end{enumerate}
The products retained in (iv) are exactly the minimal generators listed in
Theorem~\ref{thm:low-support-classification-package}.  In particular, the
table is a closed finite classification for fixed $n$, not an interface to an
additional infinite search.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Step (i) is precisely the prime-slot identity in
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-arithmetic-tests}; the M\"obius formula
there is Lemma~\ref{article-lem:primitive-prime-mobius-count}.  Step (ii) is the
ladder-slot identity in the same proposition, which says that every
$L_{i,J}(n)$ is empty or consists of the displayed single candidate.  Step
(iii) is only the coordinate-labelling convention of
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}.  By that theorem, after the
slots of a labelled row are replaced by their finite admissible sets, a row
realizes a minimal generator if and only if the slot-selection test in step
(iv) succeeds; Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-slot-selection} is exactly the stated
Hall criterion for distinct underlying primes.  The one-factor rows
$\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_3$ are the atomic generators with full
three-coordinate support.  Conversely, every minimal generator with
three-coordinate support has, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:three-coordinate-realization}, one fixed labelled
coordinate permutation of one of the eight row types and therefore appears in
this procedure. The labelled atomic factor set is unique, whereas the
unlabelled row is an $S_3$-orbit; the set insertion in step (iv) prevents the
raw permuted enumeration from asserting stronger uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Applying the checklist at $n=42$]
\label{ex:n42-three-coordinate-checklist}
Let $n=42=2\cdot3\cdot7$ and label the coordinates by
$(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)=(2,3,7)$.  The prime factors of
\[
F_{42}=2^3\cdot13\cdot29\cdot211\cdot421
\]
have ranks
\[
\alpha(2)=3,
\alpha(4)=6,
\alpha(13)=7,
\alpha(29)=14,
\alpha(421)=21,
\alpha(211)=42.
\]
Thus $\Atom(42)=\{211\}$. The exact-rank windows used below are
$P_{12}(42)=\varnothing$, $P_{13}(42)=\{29\}$,
$P_{23}(42)=\{421\}$, and $P_{123}(42)=\{211\}$.  Singleton slots are
$S_1(42)=\varnothing$, $S_2(42)=\{2\}$, and $S_3(42)=\{13\}$.  The proper
ladder slots relevant here are
$L_{1,12}(42)=\{4\}$, $L_{1,123}(42)=\varnothing$,
$L_{2,J}(42)=\varnothing$, and $L_{3,J}(42)=\varnothing$.

The table then gives the following non-atomic generators.  The row
$\Gamma_4$ with the permuted slots $P_{13}$ and $P_{23}$ gives
$29\cdot421=12209$.  The row $\Gamma_5$ gives
$4\cdot421=1684$ from $L_{1,12}$ and $P_{23}$, while the analogous
coordinate permutation with an exact-rank $P_{13}$ partner is not a row of type
$\Gamma_5$ for this ladder orientation.  The row $\Gamma_7$ gives
$2\cdot29=58$ from $S_2$ and $P_{13}$ after relabeling.  The row $\Gamma_8$
gives $13\cdot4=52$ from $S_3$ and the permuted ladder slot $L_{1,12}$.
All other rows fail because one of the required slots is empty.  Hence
\[
\mathcal M_{42}=\{52,58,211,1684,12209\}.
\]
The example illustrates the theorem's reading rule: every success or failure
is decided by a finite slot list and a coprimeness check.
\end{example}
