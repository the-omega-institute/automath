\section{Quantitative bounds}\label{sec:growth}

The parameter $R(n)$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generator-growth} is the
largest number of rank jumps with one fixed essential/full support pair.
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-jump-family-ladder-slots} shows that a ladder
family has at most one member.  Thus the residual multiplicity in $R(n)$
comes from the exact-rank prime windows
\[
 P_J(n):=\coprod_{\substack{d\mid n\\T_n(d)=J}}\Pi_\alpha(d),
 \qquad \varnothing\ne J\subseteq\mathcal P(n).
\]

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generator-growth}]
First, $F_n\in B_n$ for $n\geq3$.  Indeed, $F_n\mid F_d$ implies
$F_n\leq F_d$, and strict increase from index $2$ onward forces $d\geq n$.
Hence $B_n$ is a nonempty finite poset and has a minimal element.

For the lower bound, fix $k\geq3$, set $r=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$, and choose
an $r$-element set
\[
 P\subseteq\mathcal P(n)\setminus\{2,3\};
\]
this is possible because $k-2\geq r$.  Put
$Q=\mathcal P(n)\setminus P$.  For every map
\[
 H:Q\longrightarrow2^P\setminus\{\varnothing\}
\]
and every $i\in P$, define
\[
 T_i:=\{i\}\cup\{q\in Q:i\in H(q)\}.
\]
The index $n_{T_i}$ contains the prime $i\notin\{2,3\}$ and therefore is
not $6$ or $12$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors} supplies a prime
$\pi_{T_i}$ of exact rank $n_{T_i}$.  The sets $T_i$ cover
$\mathcal P(n)$, and $i$ is private to $T_i$, so
$\{\pi_{T_i}:i\in P\}$ is an $n$-witness cover.  Distinct maps $H$ give
distinct support families and hence distinct products.  There are
$(2^r-1)^{k-r}$ such maps.

For the upper bound, let $\Theta$ be an $n$-witness cover with $s$ factors.
For each $\theta\in\Theta$, choose a private coordinate
\[
 x_\theta\in E_n(\theta)\setminus
 \bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta\setminus\{\theta\}}T_n(\eta).
\]
These coordinates are distinct.  Write
$P=\{x_\theta:\theta\in\Theta\}$ and
$Q=\mathcal P(n)\setminus P$.  If $x_\theta=x$, then
\[
 T_n(\theta)=\{x\}\cup U_\theta,\qquad U_\theta\subseteq Q.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}, either
$E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$ or $E_n(\theta)=\{x\}$.  For each of these two
support-pair choices there are at most $R(n)$ possible factors.  Once $P$ is
fixed, each $x\in P$ therefore has at most
$2R(n)2^{k-s}$ choices.  Dropping the coverage and coprimeness restrictions
only increases the count.  Summing over the $\binom{k}{s}$ choices of $P$
and $1\leq s\leq k$ proves \eqref{eq:private-cover-finite-bounds}.

Finally, $r(k-r)=\lfloor k^2/4\rfloor$ and
$(k-r)\log(1-2^{-r})=O(1)$.  The logarithm of the lower bound is therefore
$(\log2)k^2/4+O(1)$.  For the upper bound, use
$s(k-s)\leq k^2/4$, $s\leq k$, and
$\sum_s\binom{k}{s}\leq2^k$.  This gives
\[
 \log\sum_{s=1}^k\binom{k}{s}(2R(n))^s2^{s(k-s)}
 \leq\frac{\log2}{4}k^2+k\log R(n)+O(k),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:private-cover-entropy-order}.
\end{proof}

The quadratic term comes entirely from the support covers.  An almost-all
equivalent for $\log\#\mathcal M_n$ would additionally require distributional
control of the exact-rank multiplicities inside the prime windows, together
with an upper bound for the ladder and other non-rank-pure witness covers.
Present cumulative primitive-divisor estimates do not supply that control.
