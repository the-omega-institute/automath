\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% ====================================================================

For an integer $q\ge 1$, the \emph{rank of apparition} (or \emph{order of
appearance}) $\alpha(q)$ is the least positive index $r$ such that $q\mid
F_r$, where $(F_r)_{r\ge 0}$ denotes the Fibonacci sequence with $F_0=0$,
$F_1=1$; the existence of this least index for every $q$ is recorded in
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-rank}. We use the harmless conventions $\alpha(1)=1$ and
$D_1=\operatorname{Div}(F_1)=\{1\}$ in divisor-lattice formulas. For each
$n\ge 2$, write
\[
D_n:=\operatorname{Div}(F_n),
\qquad
B_n:=\{q\in D_n:\ \alpha(q)=n\},
\qquad
A(n):=\#B_n.
\]
Let $\mathcal P(n)$ be the set of prime divisors of $n$, and let
$\mathcal M_n$ denote the set of minimal elements of $B_n$ under divisibility.
Thus $B_n$ consists of those moduli that first divide a Fibonacci number at
index~$n$. Since $F_2=1$, the boundary layer is empty: throughout the paper
we use the convention $B_2=\mathcal M_2=\varnothing$ and $A(2)=0$.
Throughout, $\log$ is the natural logarithm, $\omega(t)$ is the number of
distinct prime divisors of $t$, and $\Omega(t)$ counts prime divisors with
multiplicity. We write $A_{\rm pr}(n)$ for the number of primes of exact rank
$n$, $\mathsf{Part}(X)$ for the set of set partitions of a finite set $X$,
and $\mathsf B_k$ for the $k$th Bell number. A hypergraph on one vertex is
regarded as connected. These conventions also apply when the notation first
appears in a later section.
The counting function $A(n)$ is subsumed by the
$\alpha$-contraction formalism of Stroi\'nski \cite{Stroinski2016AlphaContraction};
see also the classical M\"obius inversions discussed in
\cite{Halton1966DivisibilityFibonacci,FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions}.
In this paper we go beyond enumeration and study $B_n$ as an
ordered subset of the divisor lattice $(D_n,\mid)$, asking how it sits inside
$D_n$, how it is generated, and what combinatorial structure it carries.

Three classical facts underlie the analysis. First, $q\mid F_m$ if and only if
$\alpha(q)\mid m$
\cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm,Vinson1963RankApparition}. Second, the Fibonacci
sequence satisfies strong divisibility: $\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}$.
Third, ranks of apparition respect least common multiples:
$\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))=\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))$; Renault proves the
corresponding identity in the more general $(a,b)$-Fibonacci setting
\cite{Renault2013PeriodRankOrder}. These properties, combined with the
primitive divisor theorem for Fibonacci numbers
\cite{Carmichael1913PrimitiveDivisors,BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas},
are the sole inputs for the main results.

This point of view complements several nearby strands in the literature.
Luca and Pomerance \cite{LucaPomerance2014LocalBehavior} study the local
behavior of $\alpha$ under additive shifts; Trojovsk\'a
\cite{Trojovska2020PeriodicPoints} and FitzGibbons--Javaheri--Miller--Verga
\cite{FitzGibbonsJavaheriMillerVerga2025Dynamics} study periodic points and
iteration dynamics of $\alpha$; Byer--Dvorachek--Eckard--Harrington--Wise--Wong
\cite{CameronCountsLundMillerPiechnikWong2022Fibotomic} study fibotomic
polynomials and related algebraic factorizations; and Sanna
\cite{Sanna2016LucasSequences} and Marques \cite{Marques2012OrderAppearance}
study the growth and fixed-point behavior of the order-of-appearance map. The
present paper instead fixes a single fiber $B_n$ and studies its divisibility
geometry inside $D_n$. The upper-fiber identity, the coordinatewise minimal-lcm
criterion, the prime--ladder alternative, the private-coordinate injection, and
the coprimality of disjoint coordinate blocks are recorded below as lemmas:
they are formal consequences of the cited classical identities and are not
claimed as independent arithmetic advances. The contributions developed from
these facts are the witness-cover formulation, the exhaustive support-three
classification with its arithmetic realization test, the passage of connected
block factorization to minimal elements, and the support-entropy coefficient
$\log 2/4$. A further contribution is the support-preserving rank-pure
embedding of arbitrary minimal set covers, which transfers the classical theta
asymptotic and a discrete central local limit to an explicit Fibonacci sector.
These lower results isolate, rather than resolve, the arithmetic multiplicity
$R(n)$. Accordingly, we make no unconditional claim of a sharp asymptotic for
$\#\mathcal M_n$ or of the negligibility of its disconnected part. We do,
however, isolate the mean interface further: support-window aggregation costs
only $O(x\log\log x)$ on average, so only a visible single-rank maximum remains.
A fibotomic size estimate and the congruence restrictions on exact-rank primes
then yield a uniform entropy inequality for each exact-rank class and a
pointwise bound of order $\phi_{\rm E}(d)/\log d$. This improves the available
single-rank input but does not settle the mean sparse-window hypothesis.
A conditional dominance theorem also identifies the stronger blockwise control
that would make the disconnected part exponentially negligible. The mean
sparse-window hypothesis is not presented as a prediction: Conjecture~5.1 of
Bugeaud--Luca--Mignotte--Siksek
\cite{BugeaudLucaMignotteSiksek2005FewPrimeDivisors} would imply its failure.

Call a prime power $\theta=p^e$ \emph{atomic for the index $d$} if
$\alpha(\theta)=d$ and $\alpha(p^{e-1})<d$. An \emph{$n$-witness cover} is a
pairwise coprime set of such atomic prime powers whose apparition indices
collectively span the prime factorization of $n$ in a minimal, irredundant
fashion; the full definition is Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}.

\begin{previewtheorem}[Witness-cover theorem]
For every $n\ge2$, multiplication gives a bijection from $n$-witness
covers $\Theta$ to minimal generators:
\[
\mathcal M_n=
\left\{\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta:\ \Theta\text{ is an $n$-witness cover}
\right\}.
\]
Consequently every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ satisfies
$\omega(m)\le\omega(n)$. This support budget is globally sharp in the precise
sense that no smaller bound depending only on $\omega(n)$ is valid; equality
is constructed on every odd layer $n\ge3$. The precise cover conditions are
given in Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}, and the result is
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} together with
Corollaries~\ref{cor:support-bound},~\ref{cor:support-bound-sharp},
and the \suppref{Corollary ``Best possible support budget''}.
\end{previewtheorem}

The witness classification also controls the number of minimal generators,
not merely their form.

\begin{previewtheorem}[Support-entropy theorem]
For $n\ge3$, let $k=\omega(n)$ and let $R(n)$ be the largest atomic-family
multiplicity at one essential/full support pair. If $k\ge3$ and
$r=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$, then
\[
 (2^r-1)^{k-r}\le\#\mathcal M_n
 \le\sum_{s=1}^{k}\binom{k}{s}(2R(n))^s2^{s(k-s)}.
\]
The rank-pure sector gives the stronger connected lower bound
\[
 \log\#\mathcal M_n^{\rm conn}\ge
 \frac{\log2}{4}k^2+k\log2-\frac12\log k+O(1)
\]
uniformly as $k\to\infty$, with an explicit parity-dependent theta constant.
The coefficient $\log2/4$ is exact for the unweighted minimal-cover count
embedded in the fixed-choice rank-pure sector. This assertion is not an
optimality statement for all information obtainable from fibotomic sizes and
exact-rank congruences. These lower bounds do not give an asymptotic formula
for $\log\#\mathcal M_n$ unless $R(n)=\exp(o(\omega(n)))$. Separate
conjectures about $R(n)$ imply conditional normal-order and mean equivalents.
These statements are
Theorems~\ref{thm:rank-pure-universality} and
\ref{thm:minimal-generator-growth}, together with their corollaries.
\end{previewtheorem}

The prime-power apparition formulas of Fulton--Morris
\cite{FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions} and Lengyel
\cite{Lengyel1995OrderFibonacciLucas} make the classification effective:
every atom is a prime atom or lies on a prime-power ladder changing one index
coordinate. The complete cases $\omega(n)\le3$, including the eight
support-three families, their finite slot test, and the induced description of
$B_n$, are proved in the \suppref{section ``Low-support classifications''}.

The same lcm mechanism gives a local-to-global factorization over connected
coordinate blocks.

\begin{previewtheorem}[Connected block factorization]
Let $q\in B_n$ and form the support hypergraph on $\mathcal P(n)$ whose
edges are the prime-coordinate supports of the ranks of the prime-power
factors of $q$. If its connected components are $C$, then
\[
q=\prod_C q_C,\qquad q_C\in B_{n_C},
\qquad n_C=\prod_{\ell\in C}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n)} ,
\]
and this factorization is unique. The same statement holds for minimal
generators after replacing $B_{n_C}$ by $\mathcal M_{n_C}$. Consequently
$A(n)$ and $\#\mathcal M_n$ are partition sums over the connected counts.
This is proved in Section~\ref{sec:connected}.
\end{previewtheorem}

As secondary consequences, nonexceptional birth layers contain principal
intervals natural after choosing an exact-rank prime and Boolean subposets of
rank $\tau(n)-O(1)$; both are proved in the \suppref{section
``Supplementary witness-cover consequences''}. For odd $n$, every minimal
cover of $\mathcal P(n)$ is realized by
Corollary~\ref{cor:odd-rank-pure-universality}.

Section~\ref{sec:prelim} records the classical background;
Section~\ref{sec:upper-fibers} develops witness covers;
Section~\ref{sec:connected} proves both connected-block factorizations; and
Section~\ref{sec:growth} proves rank-window deaggregation, fibotomic and
rank-pure bounds, and the entropy theorem before isolating the unresolved
arithmetic interface. Section~\ref{sec:questions} records its two boundary
questions. The \suppref{section ``Low-support classifications''} proves the
complete cases $\omega(n)\le3$; the supplementary sections on rank-pure
asymptotics and finite numerical evidence prove the theta/local-limit
consequences and document the exhaustive tables and case checks.
