\section{Further questions}\label{sec:questions}
%% ====================================================================

The theorem attempt leaves a two-sided arithmetic interface.  First,
determine the normal order of the weighted excess $\mathcal W(n)$ in
\eqref{eq:weighted-cover-excess}.  In particular, prove or disprove
\[
 \log\mathcal W(n)=o((\log\log n)^2)
\]
for almost all odd $n$.  By
Corollary~\ref{cor:odd-weighted-cover-obstruction}, this is necessary for the
target asymptotic and concerns only the explicit exact-rank-prime sector.
On squarefree indices Corollary~\ref{cor:canonical-squarefree-slice} identifies
this sector canonically as the minimal squarefree part of $\alpha^{-1}(n)$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp-cumulative-data-obstruction} also shows exactly
why a bound for the cumulative quantity $\omega(F_n)$ alone cannot settle the
question: at fixed total exact-rank mass, full-support concentration attains
the minimum possible weighted cover sum. Thus resolving a conjecture about
$\omega(F_n)$ is not a substitute for locating that mass among exact ranks.

Second, bound the complementary witness covers containing ladder atoms or
prime atoms from sub-full rank windows. On squarefree indices this is exactly
the nonsquarefree part of $\mathcal M_n$. The example
$169\in\mathcal M_{91}\setminus\mathcal R_{91}^{\rm rp}$ shows that this
sector cannot be discarded even on odd squarefree layers.  A complete
almost-all upper bound must show that the combined contribution of this
complement and the weighted rank-pure excess is
$\exp(o((\log\log n)^2))$ relative to the unweighted cover scale.

Conjectures~\ref{hyp:normal-window} and \ref{hyp:mean-window} are sufficient
maximum-window formulations for both tasks.  Theorem
\ref{thm:mean-rank-window-deaggregation} reduces the mean version to the
visible single-rank maximum \eqref{eq:visible-single-rank-interface}, while
Proposition~\ref{prop:blms-obstructs-mean-window} shows that a published
conjecture would force that particular mean formulation to fail.  None of the
cited primitive-divisor theorems, RH, or ERH currently supplies the required
growing exact-rank distribution estimate.
