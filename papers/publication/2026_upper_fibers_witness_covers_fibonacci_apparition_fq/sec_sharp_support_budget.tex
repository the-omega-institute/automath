\begin{theorem}[Exact support spectra and the extremal slice]
\label{thm:exact-support-spectra}
Let $n\ge3$, write
\[
 n=\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P(n)}\ell^{a_\ell},
 \qquad k=\omega(n),
\]
and put
\[
 \Sigma(n):=\{\omega(m):m\in\mathcal M_n\},\qquad
 \Sigma^{\rm conn}(n):=\{\omega(m):m\in\mathcal M_n^{\rm conn}\}.
\]
For each $\ell\in\mathcal P(n)$, define the singleton diagonal rank-jump family
\[
 \mathcal A_\ell(n):=
 \mathcal A_{\{\ell\},\{\ell\}}(n).
\]
Then multiplication gives a bijection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extremal-support-product-bijection}
 \prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P(n)}\mathcal A_\ell(n)
 \longrightarrow
 \{m\in\mathcal M_n:\omega(m)=k\},
 \qquad (\theta_\ell)_\ell\longmapsto
 \prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P(n)}\theta_\ell.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extremal-support-product-count}
 \#\{m\in\mathcal M_n:\omega(m)=k\}
 =\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P(n)}\#\mathcal A_\ell(n).
\end{equation}
The complete support spectrum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-total-support-spectrum}
 \Sigma(n)=
 \begin{cases}
  \{1,\ldots,k-1\},&n\text{ is even and squarefree},\\
  \{1,\ldots,k\},&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For the connected spectrum, $\Sigma^{\rm conn}(n)=\{1\}$ when $k=1$.
If $k\ge2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-connected-support-spectrum}
 \Sigma^{\rm conn}(n)=
 \begin{cases}
  \{1,\ldots,k-1\},
   &\begin{array}{l}
     n\text{ is squarefree, or }n=12s\text{ with }s\text{ squarefree}\
     \text{and }\gcd(s,6)=1,
    \end{array}\\[2mm]
  \{1,\ldots,k\},&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first classify the extremal slice. Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$ have
$\omega(m)=k$, and let $\Theta(m)$ be its witness cover. For every
$\theta\in\Theta(m)$, choose a private coordinate
\[
 \ell(\theta)\in E_n(\theta)\setminus
 \bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta(m),\ \eta\ne\theta}T_n(\eta).
\]
The chosen coordinates are distinct, since a coordinate chosen for two rank-jump factors
would lie in the full support of each and hence would not be private to either.
There are $k$ rank-jump factors and $k$ coordinates, so $\theta\mapsto\ell(\theta)$ is a
bijection. If $q\ne\ell(\theta)$, then $q$ is private to another rank-jump factor and
therefore cannot lie in $T_n(\theta)$. Hence
\[
 T_n(\theta)=E_n(\theta)=\{\ell(\theta)\}.
\]
Thus the inverse of \eqref{eq:extremal-support-product-bijection} sends $m$
to its uniquely labelled rank-jump factors in the families $\mathcal A_\ell(n)$.

Conversely, choose $\theta_\ell\in\mathcal A_\ell(n)$ for every
$\ell\in\mathcal P(n)$.
These rank-jump factors are pairwise coprime. Indeed, if
$\theta_\ell=p^e$ and $\theta_q=p^f$ had the same underlying prime, then
$e\ne f$ because the same rank-jump factor cannot lie in two differently labelled
families. Say $e<f$. Then $p^e\mid p^{f-1}$ would give
\[
 d(\theta_\ell)\mid d^-(\theta_q).
\]
Since $\ell$ occurs to its full $n$-adic exponent in $d(\theta_\ell)$, it
would also occur fully in $d(\theta_q)$, contrary to
$T_n(\theta_q)=\{q\}$ and $q\ne\ell$. The singleton full supports cover
$\mathcal P(n)$, and the singleton essential supports are private. Hence the
chosen rank-jump factors form an $n$-witness cover. The witness-cover theorem proves that
their product belongs to $\mathcal M_n$ and has $k$ distinct prime factors.
Uniqueness follows from unique factorization. This proves the bijection and
\eqref{eq:extremal-support-product-count}.

We next decide when the factors on the right of
\eqref{eq:extremal-support-product-count} are nonzero. If $\ell$ is odd, or
if $\ell=2$ and $a_2\ge2$, then the exact-rank existence theorem supplies a
prime of rank $\ell^{a_\ell}$; this is an element of
$\mathcal A_\ell(n)$. Suppose now that $a_2=1$. If $n$ is squarefree and
$\theta\in\mathcal A_2(n)$, then $d(\theta)\mid n$ cannot contain an odd
coordinate: every such coordinate would occur fully and would belong to
$T_n(\theta)$. Thus $d(\theta)=2$, which is impossible because $F_2=1$ and
$\Atom(2)=\varnothing$. Therefore the extremal slice is empty when $n$ is
even and squarefree.

If instead $a_2=1$ and $q^2\mid n$ for some odd prime $q$, take a rank-jump factor of
rank $2q$. For $q=3$, the rank-jump factor $4\in\Atom(6)$ has, relative to $n$,
$E_n(4)=T_n(4)=\{2\}$. For $q\ge5$, the rank $2q$ is nonexceptional, so an
exact-rank prime exists and again has essential and full support $\{2\}$,
because the $q$-coordinate is sub-full in $n$. Thus
$\mathcal A_2(n)\ne\varnothing$. All other diagonal families were already
seen to be nonempty, proving that the support value $k$ occurs exactly in the
second case of \eqref{eq:exact-total-support-spectrum}.

It remains to realize every smaller support size. Assume $k\ge2$ and
$1\le r\le k-1$. Choose a partition $\Pi$ of $\mathcal P(n)$ into $r$
nonempty blocks, with the additional condition that $\{2\}$ is not a block
when $a_2=1$. Such a partition exists because $r<k$. For $C\in\Pi$, put
$n_C=\prod_{\ell\in C}\ell^{a_\ell}$ and choose
$\theta_C\in\Atom(n_C)$. This is possible by exact-rank prime existence
unless $n_C\in\{6,12\}$; in those two cases use respectively
$4\in\Atom(6)$ and $16\in\Atom(12)$. The forbidden rank $2$ was excluded by
the choice of $\Pi$. Strong divisibility makes the rank-jump factors belonging to
disjoint blocks pairwise coprime. Their full supports are the blocks $C$, and
each has a nonempty essential support because lowering it decreases its rank.
They therefore form an $n$-witness cover. Its product has exactly $r$ prime
factors, completing the proof of \eqref{eq:exact-total-support-spectrum}.

The same idea gives all connected support sizes below $k$. Choose a hub
$h\in\mathcal P(n)$, taking $h=3$ whenever
$\{2,3\}\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$ and $n_{\{2,3\}}\in\{6,12\}$, and partition
$\mathcal P(n)\setminus\{h\}$ into $r$ nonempty sets $P_1,\ldots,P_r$.
Use one rank-jump factor of exact rank $n_{\{h\}\cup P_i}$ for each $i$. The only
exceptional support that can occur is $\{2,3\}$; use $4$ at rank $6$ or $16$
at rank $12$. In both cases its essential support contains the private
coordinate $2\in P_i$. Every nonexceptional rank-jump factor is a prime jump and has any
coordinate of the nonempty set $P_i$ as a private coordinate. The rank-jump factors are
pairwise coprime: their ranks are distinct, and the exceptional rank-jump factor has
underlying prime $2$, whose rank $3$ is not one of the other multi-coordinate
ranks. The resulting witness cover is connected because all its rank-support
edges contain $h$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:connected-spectrum-lower-interval}
 \{1,\ldots,k-1\}\subseteq\Sigma^{\rm conn}(n)\qquad(k\ge2).
\end{equation}

We finally decide whether the endpoint $k$ can be connected. In an extremal
witness cover, write $\theta_\ell$ for the rank-jump factor with
$T_n(\theta_\ell)=\{\ell\}$ and let
$S_\ell=\operatorname{supp}_n(d(\theta_\ell))$ be its positive-coordinate
rank support. If $q\in S_\ell\setminus\{\ell\}$, then
\[
 1\le\nu_q(d(\theta_\ell))<a_q,
\]
so necessarily $a_q\ge2$. If $n$ is squarefree, every $S_\ell$ is therefore
the singleton $\{\ell\}$, and the rank-support hypergraph is disconnected
when $k\ge2$.

There is one further obstruction. Let $n=12s$ with $s$ squarefree and
$\gcd(s,6)=1$. The only coordinate of exponent at least two is $2$. Hence an
edge $S_3$ larger than $\{3\}$ would force $d(\theta_3)=6$. But
$\Atom(6)=\{4\}$, and lowering $4$ gives rank $3$; relative to this $n$, the
$3$-coordinate is not lost. Thus a rank-jump factor of rank $6$ with
$T_n(\theta_3)=\{3\}$ has empty essential support and cannot occur in a
witness cover. Consequently $S_3=\{3\}$. Since $a_3=1$, the coordinate $3$
cannot occur sub-fully in any other $S_\ell$, so it is isolated and the
extremal generator is disconnected.

Conversely, suppose that $n$ is nonsquarefree and is not of the preceding
form. Choose a coordinate $h$ with $a_h\ge2$. If possible take $h\ne2$. For
each $\ell\ne h$, choose a rank-jump factor of rank
\[
 d_\ell=\ell^{a_\ell}h,
\]
and choose an exact-rank prime of rank $h^{a_h}$ for the $h$-rank-jump factor. The ranks
$d_\ell$ are nonexceptional except possibly when $h=3$ and $\ell=2$; use
$4$ when $d_2=6$ and $16$ when $d_2=12$. These exceptional rank-jump factors have
$E_n=T_n=\{2\}$ because the $3$-coordinate is sub-full. If the only possible
hub is $h=2$, then either $3\nmid n$ or $a_2\ge3$. In the latter case, use
$9\in\Atom(12)$ for the $3$-rank-jump factor; relative to $n$ it has
$E_n(9)=T_n(9)=\{3\}$. Use exact-rank primes of rank
$\ell^{a_\ell}2$ for the remaining nonhub coordinates. In every case we have
chosen one rank-jump factor from each $\mathcal A_\ell(n)$, so the extremal-slice
bijection makes their product a minimal generator. Its rank-support edges are
$\{h\}$ and the pairs $\{h,\ell\}$, hence it is connected. Together with
\eqref{eq:connected-spectrum-lower-interval}, this proves
\eqref{eq:exact-connected-support-spectrum}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:support-bound-sharp}
For $n\ge3$, there exists $m\in\mathcal M_n$ with
$\omega(m)=\omega(n)$ if and only if $n$ is not even and squarefree. In
particular, equality occurs on every odd layer.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is the endpoint assertion in \eqref{eq:exact-total-support-spectrum}.
\end{proof}
