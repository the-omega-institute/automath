\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Let \(U=(U_n)_{n\ge0}\) be a sequence of place values and let a word
\(x_0\cdots x_{m-1}\) be read least-significant digit first, with value
\[
  \val_U(x_0\cdots x_{m-1})=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}x_jU_j.
\]
This paper studies an arithmetic restriction imposed by finite-fan-out
multiple context-free syntax: how few rational primes can occur among the
values of an infinite multiple context-free sublanguage of a canonical
numeration language? The answer is governed by a local recurrence action.
It applies beyond ordinary bases and remains informative when the trailing
coefficient of the recurrence is not a unit.

The sequence \(U\) is assumed to be strictly increasing and eventually to
satisfy an integral linear recurrence with nonzero trailing coefficient. A
finite nonnegative digit alphabet and a canonical language \(\mathcal R_U\)
give one representation of each positive integer. No ordering of values by
canonical length is assumed. Regularity of \(\mathcal R_U\) is needed only
for the effective one-system statement.

\subsection{Main results}

The four principal results distinguish local information extracted from
one pumped orbit from global information supplied by a finite cover of a
slender language.

\begin{maintheorem}[A: synchronized orbit and prime support]
Let \((U,A,\mathcal R_U)\) be a canonical eventually recurrent numeration
system with trailing recurrence coefficient \(a_0\), and let
\(L\subseteq\mathcal R_U\) be an infinite \(k\)-MCFL. There is a fixed
synchronized family
\[
  W(t)=a u_1v_1^tw_1s_1^tu_2\cdots
       u_kv_k^tw_ks_k^tu_{k+1}\in L \qquad(t\ge0)
\]
of positive total pumped length whose values \(N(t)=\val_U(W(t))\) are
pairwise distinct. For every \(q\ge2\) with \(\gcd(q,a_0)=1\), some
\(H(q)\ge1\) satisfies
\[
  N(t+H(q))\equiv N(t)\pmod q \qquad(t\ge0).
\]
If \(S\) is the set of prime divisors of \(a_0\), then every such \(L\)
has either unbounded prime support outside \(S\) or unbounded \(p\)-adic
valuation for some \(p\in S\). Consequently the prime and
bounded-\(\Omega\) representation languages are MCF-immune.
\end{maintheorem}

The synchronized family is chosen before the modulus. The full orbit
statement is \Cref{thm:mcfl-synchronized-congruence-orbit}; its topological
and prime-support consequences are
\Cref{thm:mcfl-local-adic-fingerprint,thm:nonunit-mcfl-escape-dichotomy}.

Let \(\mu_U\) be the minimal tail recurrence polynomial and let
\(S_{\mathrm r}\) be the prime divisors of its constant term.

\begin{maintheorem}[B: geometric rays and weak-Perron systems]
The following are equivalent: some infinite finite-fan-out MCFL in
\(\mathcal R_U\) has bounded prime support outside \(S_{\mathrm r}\);
\(\mathcal R_U\) contains a synchronized family with values \(cb^t\) for
integers \(c\ge1\) and \(b\ge2\); and such a family is itself an infinite
linear MCFL of finite fan-out. If \(U\) is a standard greedy linear
numeration basis and \(\mu_U\) is the minimal polynomial of a weak Perron
number \(\beta>1\), these conditions are also equivalent to each of
\[
  \beta^m=B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2},\qquad
  \mu_U(X)\mid X^m-B,
\]
for some \(m\ge1\), and to the eventual identity
\(U_{n+m}=BU_n\).
\end{maintheorem}

The general equivalence is
\Cref{thm:bounded-outside-support-geometric-ray}. The weak-Perron
specialization is
\Cref{cor:linear-perron-bounded-support-classification}.
The alternating-radix sequences
\(U_{2t}=B^t\), \(U_{2t+1}=pB^t\), with \(B=pq\), show that the positive
case can have the nonintegral weak Perron root \(\sqrt B\).

For \(X\subseteq\mathbb N_{\ge1}\), write
\(\operatorname{Rep}_U(X)=\{z\in\mathcal R_U:\val_U(z)\in X\}\).

\begin{maintheorem}[C: sparse automatic sets in an independent base]
Let \(k,\ell\ge2\) be multiplicatively independent. If
\(X\subseteq\mathbb N_{\ge1}\) is infinite and sparse \(k\)-automatic, then
\(\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(X)\) is MCF-immune. Equivalently, every MCFL
contained in this language is finite.
\end{maintheorem}

This is \Cref{thm:sparse-automatic-independent-base-mcf-immune}. It lifts
the sparse--sparse automatic intersection theorem to an immunity statement:
an infinite MCFL would contain a synchronized family, and independently
starring its pumped blocks places that family in a sparse regular language
in base \(\ell\).

\begin{maintheorem}[D: slender context-free Cobham theorem]
Let \(U\) and \(V\) be strictly increasing standard greedy linear
numeration sequences whose minimal tail recurrence polynomials are the
minimal polynomials of weak Perron numbers \(\alpha>1\) and \(\beta>1\).
If \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are multiplicatively independent and both
\(\operatorname{Rep}_U(X)\) and \(\operatorname{Rep}_V(X)\) are slender
context-free languages, then \(X\) is finite.
\end{maintheorem}

This is \Cref{thm:slender-cfl-cobham}. With complete recurrence and
paired-loop data, \Cref{thm:effective-slender-cfl-cardinality} gives a
computable upper bound for \(\lvert X\rvert\).

\subsection{Proof architecture}

The recurrence first becomes a finite-dimensional affine action. After a
prefix beyond the recurrence transient, every digit acts by an integral
matrix, and every block matrix is invertible modulo integers coprime to the
trailing coefficient. The weak pumping lemma of
Seki--Matsumura--Fujii--Kasami supplies one family in which finitely many
fixed blocks are raised to a common exponent. A common order of their
matrices in a finite general linear group gives the returns in Main
Theorem A; positive pumped length and canonical uniqueness give
distinctness.

These local returns place the orbit densely in the relevant congruence
neighbourhoods. Broughan's adic classifications then yield the
scattered-set obstruction. For nonunit recurrences, an intrinsic
Cantor--Bendixson calculation after deleting the exceptional primes gives
the alternative between additional prime divisors and increasing exceptional
valuations. In the unit case, the same returns also produce quotient
congruences of arbitrary depth and a divisibility tree.

For Main Theorem B, a bounded-support orbit is restricted to an arithmetic
subsequence supported on a fixed finite set of primes. Its values form a
pairwise distinct positive linear recurrence. Evertse's theorem on prime
ideals in quotients excludes two or more characteristic roots after the
root-of-unity classes are separated; Schur's theorem excludes a
nonconstant polynomial factor. A further arithmetic subsequence therefore
has values \(cb^t\). The converse is supplied by an explicit linear MCFG
for every synchronized ray.

In a weak-Perron greedy system, peripheral conjugates become equal after a
common power. Residue-class asymptotics and the greedy length interval
squeeze the geometric ratio to \(b=\beta^D\), where \(D\) is the total
pumped length. This proves the algebraic classification in Main Theorem B
without assuming that the full greedy language is regular.

For Main Theorem C, the synchronized family extracted from an infinite
MCFL is placed inside the regular language obtained by allowing its pumped
blocks to vary independently. This envelope has polynomial growth and hence
represents a sparse automatic set. Albayrak--Bell's sparse--sparse
intersection theorem then excludes the family in a multiplicatively
independent base.

Slenderness provides global rather than local control: a classical
classification covers an entire slender context-free language by finitely
many paired loops. The affine action
turns each loop, after finitely many residue restrictions, into recurrence
sequences with one positive dominant root. Mignotte's theorem makes the
common-value set of a loop from the \(U\)-cover and a loop from the
\(V\)-cover finite, proving Main Theorem D.

\subsection{Relation to prior work}

The fixed-base context-free prime obstruction is due to Hartmanis--Shank
and Sch\"utzenberger \cite{HartmanisShank1968,Schutzenberger1968}. Our
language-theoretic input is the weak pumping lemma for MCFLs
\cite[Lemma~3.2]{SekiEtAl1991}, which supplies one synchronized family.
The stronger pumping property fails for general MCFLs
\cite{KanazawaKobeleMichaelisSalvatiYoshinaka2014}. The substitution lemma
of Duncan--Elder--Frenkel--Lyu controls every sufficiently marked long word
but allows switchable tuples rather than one fixed simultaneous-power
orbit \cite[Theorem~4.5]{DuncanElderFrenkelLiu2026}; that distinction is
why the older weak lemma gives the matrix powers used here.

Evertse's quotient theorem is the arithmetic input for the geometric-ray
step \cite[Theorem~3]{Evertse1984}; later \(S\)-part estimates concern the
size of the supported part of recurrence terms
\cite{BugeaudEvertse2017}. Broughan supplies the ambient adic
classifications \cite{Broughan2003}. Frougny's normalization theorem and
the Bertrand and substitution literature provide the regular canonical
interfaces for Pisot numeration
\cite{Frougny1992,BruyereHansel1997,Fabre1995,FrougnySolomyak1992}.
Charlier--Kreczman obtain the residue-class quotient limits underlying the
weak-Perron length squeeze \cite[Proposition~10 and Remark~12]{CharlierKreczman2025};
the prime-support and MCFL classification is the additional interface
proved here.

Albayrak--Bell prove that sparse automatic sets in multiplicatively
independent bases have finite intersection
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{AlbayrakBell2023}. Main Theorem C uses this result to
replace the second sparse automatic language by an arbitrary finite-fan-out
multiple context-free sublanguage and concludes MCF-immunity.

For the weak-Perron two-system result, the paired-loop classification is
due to
Latteux--Thierrin and Ilie
\cite{LatteuxThierrin1983,Ilie1994,IlieRozenbergSalomaa2000}, and the
common-value input is Mignotte's theorem
\cite{Mignotte1978Intersection}. Albayrak--Bell's quantitative
Cobham--Semenov theorem contains the regular integer-base specialization
and gives a stronger explicit automaton bound there
\cite[Theorem~4.1]{AlbayrakBell2023}. Regular systems generated by Perron
substitutions and regular abstract-numeration cases lie in existing Cobham theory
\cite[Theorem~1]{Durand2011CobhamSubstitutions}%
\cite[Theorem~49]{CharlierLeroyRigo2013CobhamANS}. The contribution of
Main Theorem D is the passage from genuinely nonregular slender
context-free representation languages to recurrence sequences in
weak-Perron positional systems.

\subsection{Organization}

\Cref{sec:context-free-pisot} develops local rigidity from one synchronized
orbit: the Zeckendorf model, affine recurrence action, prime-support
obstructions, geometric-ray characterization, and weak-Perron
classification. \Cref{sec:slender-cobham} first derives MCF-immunity in an
independent base from sparse automatic intersection, then develops global
rigidity from a finite paired-loop cover and proves the weak-Perron
two-system theorem and its effective cardinality refinement. Both branches
pass from structured word families to arithmetic rigidity.
