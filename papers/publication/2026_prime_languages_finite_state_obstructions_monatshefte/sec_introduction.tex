\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Finite-state symbolic models impose rigid constraints on counting
functions. In a deterministic automaton, the number of accepted words
of length \(m\) is governed by powers of a finite matrix. In a sofic
shift, the number of admissible words of a given length is likewise
controlled by a finite-state presentation. These mechanisms force
periodic density laws or exponential-polynomial growth, and that is
often enough to rule out arithmetic languages whose size is governed by
the prime number theorem.

This note is centered on the resulting quantitative obstruction rather
than on a new contribution to density theory. Once the classical density
dichotomy for regular languages is written with explicit residue classes
and constants, it yields fixed-DFA approximation bounds for prime slices.
The main addition is an effective, uniform version of this conversion:
from the DFA transition matrix and explicit prime-counting bounds one
computes the residue alternatives, thresholds, and constants in the
base-\(b\) prime-slice obstruction. The same finite-budget mechanism then
gives the Zeckendorf analogues for trim strongly connected
presentations. The density dichotomies themselves, the Zeckendorf growth
law, and the analytic observations are used only as background in the
specialized forms needed for those corollaries. The eventual-sofic
exclusion for prime Zeckendorf slices is a short consequence of
regularity plus non-regularity, not a separate asymptotic theorem.

Throughout, we work with trim deterministic presentations whose
underlying directed graph is strongly connected and has Perron root
\(>1\); numeration automata with multiple strongly connected components
do occur \cite{CharlierRampersadRigoWaxweiler2010}, so this hypothesis
is part of the setup rather than a claim of full generality.

Throughout, \(f(m)\asymp g(m)\) means that \(c_1g(m)\le f(m)\le c_2g(m)\)
for some positive constants \(c_1,c_2\) and all sufficiently large
\(m\). We use the standard prime-counting convention
\(\pi(x)=\#\{p\le x:p\text{ prime}\}\). Thus an integer half-open
interval \([X,Y)\) contains exactly \(\pi(Y-1)-\pi(X-1)\) primes; in
asymptotic estimates below this may equivalently be written
\(\pi(Y)-\pi(X)+O(1)\). We write \(F_0=0\), \(F_1=1\) for the Fibonacci numbers,
\(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), and for a Zeckendorf word
\(w=x_1\cdots x_m\) we set
\[
  \val(w):=\sum_{k=1}^m x_kF_{k+1}.
\]

\subsection{Main results}

We summarize the background inputs and the quantitative corollaries
needed later; the precise residue-class refinements and proofs appear
in Sections~\ref{sec:automata}--\ref{sec:zeckendorf}.

We begin with the classical density dichotomy for deterministic
automata, recorded in \Cref{thm:dfa-density-dichotomy} because its
constants feed directly into the prime approximation bounds. In the
binary case, if
\[
  a_m(\mathcal A)
  :=\#\{w\in\{0,1\}^m:\mathcal A\text{ accepts }w\},
\]
then
\[
  \frac{a_m(\mathcal A)}{2^m}=c_{m\bmod D_A}+O(\theta^m)
\]
for some automaton period \(D_A\), non-negative residue constants
\(c_0,\dots,c_{D_A-1}\), and \(\theta\in(0,1)\). Thus the normalized
count either decays exponentially on every residue class, or is
bounded below by a positive constant on at least one residue class. In
particular, the intermediate scale
\(a_m(\mathcal A)/2^m\asymp m^{-\alpha}\) is excluded.

When this is applied to the binary prime language
\(\mathcal P_m^{(2)}\), whose size is \(\asymp 2^m/m\) by the prime
number theorem \cite{HardyWright2008}, the full base-\(b\) results
\Cref{cor:dfa-prime-symmetric-diff,cor:dfa-prime-recall-precision}
give the following binary consequences. For every fixed deterministic
finite automaton there is \(c>0\) such that
\[
  \abs{(L(\mathcal A)\cap\{0,1\}^m)\triangle\mathcal P_m^{(2)}}
  \ge c\,\frac{2^m}{m}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\), and positive-density languages have
\(\gg 2^m\) symmetric difference along an arithmetic progression. For
the corresponding recall and precision ratios, every fixed DFA has
either exponentially decaying recall or \(O(1/m)\) precision along an
infinite arithmetic progression. These introductory statements are
summaries of the later corollaries, not separate theorem content.

\begin{example}[MSD-first base-\(5\) convention]\label{ex:msd-base}
  With digits read from the most significant position first, the word
  \(w=2031\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}^4\) encodes the integer
  \(2\cdot 5^3+0\cdot 5^2+3\cdot 5+1=266\). The leading digit must be
  non-zero when counting prime slices of fixed length: although the
  word \(0031\) has the same numerical value, it is excluded from
  \(\mathcal P^{(5)}_4\) because it lies outside
  \(\{1,\dots,4\}\Sigma_5^{3}\). This is the convention used throughout
  \Cref{sec:automata}.
\end{example}

For the Zeckendorf setting we use the background growth law proved in
the specialized form needed in
\Cref{prop:zeckendorf-regular-powerlaw}. It applies to regular
sublanguages of
\[
  \mathcal Z
  :=\{x_1\cdots x_m\in\{0,1\}^\ast:
      x_ix_{i+1}\neq 11,\ x_m=1\}.
\]
For such an \(L\), after deleting states that are not reachable from
the initial state or cannot reach an accepting state, let \(\rho\) be
the maximal spectral radius of a strongly connected component in a DFA
recognizing \(L\), and define
\[
  N_L(T):=\#\{w\in L:\val(w)\le T\},
\]
where \(\val(w)\) is the Zeckendorf value of \(w\). The growth law says
\[
  N_L(T)=T^{\alpha+o(1)},
  \qquad
  \alpha=
  \begin{cases}
    0, & \rho\le 1,\\[0.3em]
    \dfrac{\log \rho}{\log \varphi}, & \rho>1.
  \end{cases}
  \qquad (T\to\infty).
\]
\Cref{sec:zeckendorf} combines this background statement with
prime-counting estimates to deduce the quantitative Zeckendorf
corollaries. The non-regularity and eventual-sofic exclusions then
follow immediately. In the endpoint case \(\rho\le1\), the displayed
formula is used only in the form \(N_L(T)=T^{o(1)}\).

\begin{example}[LSD-first Zeckendorf convention]\label{ex:lsd-zeck}
  Consider the integer \(23=F_8+F_3\). Its Zeckendorf expansion uses
  digits \(x_k\in\{0,1\}\) with \(x_k\) multiplying \(F_{k+1}\) and the
  least significant digit listed first. The admissible word
  \(w=0100001\) has length \(7\), satisfies the no-\(11\) rule, and
  evaluates to
  \(x_1F_2+x_2F_3+\cdots+x_7F_8=0+1\cdot 2+\cdots+1\cdot 21=23\).
  The terminal \(1\) is the most significant digit, consistent with the
  trim deterministic presentation used in \Cref{sec:zeckendorf}.
\end{example}

The final remarks are only contextual. They recall the classical
P\'olya--Carlson--Estermann natural-boundary obstruction for
prime-supported Euler products and the elementary \(2\pi i\)-periodicity
of scalar determinant models after \(z=e^{-s}\); these statements are
not used in the finite-budget proofs.

\subsection{Relation to prior work}\label{subsec:prior-work}

We give a theorem-by-theorem account of what is known, what is a
specialized reproof, and what is new.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\Cref{thm:dfa-density-dichotomy} (density dichotomy):
  classical.} The eventual periodicity of normalized word counts for a
  deterministic automaton is a direct Perron--Frobenius consequence and
  is standard in the density literature for regular languages; see
  \cite{Berstel1973,SalomaaSoittola1978,BodirskyGaertnerVonOertzenSchwinghammer2004,Bell2020UpperDensity,Koga2019Density,BerstelReutenauer2011}.
  We record it only in the residue-class form needed for the later
  prime estimates.

  \item \textbf{\Cref{lem:trim-density} and
  \Cref{prop:zeckendorf-regular-powerlaw}: classical.} These results are standard residue-class regroupings of
  the periodic Perron--Frobenius theorem for trim strongly connected
  presentations and of the known Zeckendorf growth law; see, e.g.,
  \cite{SalomaaSoittola1978,LecomteRigo2001,CharlierRampersad2011}.

  \item \textbf{\Cref{thm:parry-density} (trim strongly connected
  presentations): background formulation.} This is a specialized
  relative-density formulation of the conditional-density literature
  \cite{HanselPerrin1989,Kozik2005Conditional,Koga2019Density,Montoya2025Relative,BertheGouletPerrin2025}.
  Its role is to isolate the trim, strongly connected case needed for
  the later prime obstructions.

  \item \textbf{Effective quantitative prime-slice obstructions.} The
  introductory binary corollaries above summarize the later statements
  \Cref{prop:dfa-quantitative-obstruction,cor:dfa-prime-symmetric-diff,cor:dfa-prime-recall-precision}.
  The effective theorem \Cref{thm:effective-fixed-dfa-prime-obstruction}
  makes the fixed-base constants and thresholds computable from a
  compressed Markov-chain certificate for the transition matrix of the
  given DFA. This is an effective packaging of the classical density
  dichotomy with explicit prime-counting input, not a new density
  theorem. Together with
  \Cref{cor:relative-finite-budget-obstruction}, this gives a uniform
  obstruction to approximating the base-\(b\) prime slices by a single
  fixed DFA, both for symmetric difference and for simultaneous
  recall/precision. The Zeckendorf analogues
  \Cref{cor:zeck-symmetric-diff,cor:zeck-recall-precision} follow by the
  same mechanism.

  \item \textbf{Sofic-slice exclusion
  (\Cref{rem:zeckendorf-primes-not-sofic}).} Once
  \Cref{cor:zeckendorf-prime-language-not-regular} is known, the
  regularity of sofic block languages immediately rules out eventual
  sofic realization of the prime Zeckendorf slices. We record this only
  because it fits the overall narrative.

  \item \textbf{Analytic obstructions: classical background.}
  \Cref{thm:euler-product-natural-boundary} is the usual
  P\'olya--Carlson natural-boundary mechanism for Euler products.
  \Cref{prop:finite-zeta-imaginary-periodicity} is the corresponding
  periodicity obstruction for determinant models after \(z=e^{-s}\). Both are
  classical
  \cite{Polya1923,Carlson1921,Estermann1928,ArtinMazur1965,BowenLanford1970}.
\end{itemize}
\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Relation to density and approximation literature.} For ordinary
automatic sets, asymptotic density questions go back at least to
Berstel \cite{Berstel1973}; the standard global sources include
B\"uchi's automata-theoretic decidability work \cite{Buchi1960} and
Allouche--Shallit's monograph on automatic sequences
\cite{AlloucheShallit2003}. Matrix and algorithmic treatments appear
in
\cite{BodirskyGaertnerVonOertzenSchwinghammer2004,Bell2020UpperDensity},
and Koga's paper \cite{Koga2019Density} revisits Sin'ya's density-zero
criterion \cite{Sinya2021}. The rational-measure viewpoint of Hansel
and Perrin \cite{HanselPerrin1989} interprets regular languages via
probability measures on shifts of finite type. Kozik's MFCS 2005 paper
\cite{Kozik2005Conditional} formulates conditional densities for
deterministic presentations in the trim strongly connected setting,
Montoya \cite{Montoya2025Relative} recasts these questions in terms of
relative densities, and Berth\'e--Goulet-Ouellet--Perrin
\cite{BertheGouletPerrin2025} establish a broad density theorem under
shift-invariant measures. Accordingly,
\Cref{thm:dfa-density-dichotomy,prop:zeckendorf-regular-powerlaw,thm:parry-density}
should be read here as background statements in a form suited to the
prime-slice applications. Cobham's theorem
\cite{Cobham1969} is the foundational reference for base-dependent
automatic recognizability of sets of integers. Regular approximation of
non-regular languages is a separate line of work; see, for example,
Eisman and Ravikumar \cite{EismanRavikumar2005} and the asymptotic
approximation framework of Sin'ya \cite{Sinya2021}. Polynomial and
sparse growth phenomena for regular languages are part of the classical
transfer-matrix picture \cite{Berstel1973,SalomaaSoittola1978}, and
numeration systems on regular languages are treated in
\cite{LecomteRigo2001,Frougny1992,BruyereHansel1997,CharlierRampersad2011,Rigo2014Vol1,Rigo2014Vol2}.
The present contribution is narrower: it packages the fixed-DFA
prime-slice obstruction with computable residue-class constants and
explicit thresholds, then applies the same finite-budget argument in the
Zeckendorf setting.


\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Summary.} \Cref{thm:dfa-density-dichotomy} together with
\Cref{lem:trim-density} and \Cref{prop:zeckendorf-regular-powerlaw}
record the classical density statements needed for deterministic
presentations. \Cref{thm:parry-density} packages the conditional-density
framework for precisely those trim, strongly connected presentations
with Perron root \(\lambda>1\) that drive our applications; we do not
claim any results for presentations with multiple strongly connected
components, consistent with the counterexamples in
\cite{CharlierRampersadRigoWaxweiler2010}. The paper's effective
contribution is the finite-budget obstruction mechanism with
quantitative fixed-DFA recall/precision and symmetric-difference
instances
(\Cref{cor:relative-finite-budget-obstruction,prop:dfa-quantitative-obstruction,cor:dfa-prime-symmetric-diff,cor:dfa-prime-recall-precision}),
their Zeckendorf companions
(\Cref{cor:zeck-symmetric-diff,cor:zeck-recall-precision}) and the
slice-rigidity route to prime Zeckendorf non-regularity
(\Cref{prop:zeckendorf-regular-slice-rigidity,cor:zeckendorf-prime-language-not-regular}).
The eventual-sofic exclusion is recorded only as the brief consequence
\Cref{rem:zeckendorf-primes-not-sofic}, while
\Cref{thm:euler-product-natural-boundary,prop:finite-zeta-imaginary-periodicity}
remain classical background.

\medskip
Finite-automata non-recognizability of the base-\(b\) prime language
goes back to Minsky and Papert \cite{MinskyPapert1966}.
Hartmanis--Shank and Sch\"utzenberger proved stronger related results
for broader language classes and acceptable sets
\cite{HartmanisShank1968,Schutzenberger1968}. The interaction between primes and digital
constraints has a much deeper literature. Mauduit and Rivat
\cite{MauduitRivat2010}, Drmota--Mauduit--Rivat
\cite{DrmotaMauduitRivat2009}, and M\"ullner \cite{Muellner2017} prove
analytic equidistribution results for digit functions and automatic
sequences evaluated at primes. Maynard's theorem on primes with missing
digits \cite{Maynard2019}, Bourgain's work on primes with prescribed
binary digits
\cite{Bourgain2013PrescribedDigits,Bourgain2015PrescribedDigitsII},
Swaenepoel's extension to a positive proportion of preassigned digits
in arbitrary bases \cite{Swaenepoel2020PreassignedDigits}, and work on
reversed-prime or digit-reversal questions such as Bhowmik--Suzuki
\cite{BhowmikSuzuki2023} address the complementary problem of locating
primes inside large digit-restricted sets. These analytic results allow
the digit constraints to vary with the length and prove prime
asymptotics or positive proportions inside those varying families. The
present paper does neither: the automaton is fixed once and for all,
and the obstruction is the elementary mismatch between eventual
periodic finite-state densities and the finite prime budget
\(\asymp\lambda^m/m\). For Zeckendorf and more general Parry numeration
systems, the regularity of admissible expansions is standard; see
\cite{Frougny1992,BruyereHansel1997,Rigo2014Vol1,Rigo2014Vol2}. Our treatment uses only this
regular syntax together with the transfer-matrix viewpoint on word
counts, familiar from symbolic dynamics and analytic combinatorics
\cite{LindMarcus1995,Kitchens1998,FlajoletSedgewick2009}.

Recent Monatshefte work illustrates the surrounding symbolic-dynamical
landscape: Bustos-Gajardo--Kellendonk--Yassawi use finite semigroup
methods for factors of substitution shifts
\cite{BustosKellendonkYassawi2025}, and Charlier--Cisternino study
soficity phenomena in Cantor real-base expansions
\cite{CharlierCisternino2021}. These are complementary to the present
finite-budget obstruction; they do not supply, and are not used as, a
prime-slice density theorem.

\subsection{Organisation}

\Cref{sec:automata} proves the density dichotomy for deterministic
automata and derives the fixed-base prime corollaries.
\Cref{sec:zeckendorf} records the background growth law for regular
Zeckendorf sublanguages, deduces the corresponding quantitative prime
corollaries, proves the slice-level rigidity that yields prime
Zeckendorf non-regularity, and then notes the easy sofic exclusion.
\Cref{sec:analytic} collects final background analytic comparators
concerning Euler products and scalar determinant models. Later sections
cite the statements recorded above directly, so their proofs and
corollaries need not restate the results in full.
