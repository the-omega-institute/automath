\begin{abstract}
Let \(U\) be a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers that is
eventually linearly recurrent, and suppose that a finite digit language
gives unique representations strictly ordered by length. Combining the
weak pumping lemma for multiple context-free languages with the affine
recurrence action, we show that every infinite multiple context-free
sublanguage contains one strictly increasing synchronized orbit returning
modulo every integer coprime to the recurrence's trailing coefficient.
Applying Broughan's adic classifications and a deleted-prime intrinsic
Cantor--Bendixson calculation yields scattered-set obstructions, prime and
bounded-\(\Omega\) MCF-immunity, and, in the unit case, quotient chains of
arbitrary congruence depth and an induced countably branching divisibility
tree.

For the intrinsic minimal tail recurrence we prove an inverse theorem:
bounded prime support away from its constant term occurs on an infinite
MCFL if and only if the canonical language contains a synchronized ray
whose values are \(cb^t\) with integers \(c\ge1\), \(b\ge2\). The
arithmetic rigidity step is deduced explicitly from Evertse's theorem on
prime ideals in quotients of nondegenerate recurrences. On the promised
class of valid presentations, with a DFA and a supplied or effectively
certified minimal tail recurrence, positive instances are semidecidable;
the missing uniform witness bound for a general decision procedure is isolated. For
standard greedy linear numeration bases with Perron recurrence the problem
is decidable and is positive exactly when the dominant Perron number is an
integer. Thus the Pisot hypothesis can be replaced by the weaker
strict-dominance hypothesis.
\end{abstract}
