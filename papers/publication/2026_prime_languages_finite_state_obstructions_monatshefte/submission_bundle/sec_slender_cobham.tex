\section{A slender-context-free two-system theorem}
\label{sec:slender-cobham}

This section has a different architecture from the preceding one-system
MCFL argument. It uses a global finite cover of each full representation
language, turns every member of that cover into finitely many recurrence
sequences, and invokes finiteness of common values. In particular, none of
the finite-group return, deleted-prime, fixed-support quotient,
geometric-subsequence, or divisibility-tree arguments enters the proof.

We record the two external inputs with their hypotheses. A language \(L\)
is \emph{slender} when
\(\sup_n\#(L\cap A^n)<\infty\). In the terminology of
Ilie--Rozenberg--Salomaa, this is \(0\)-poly-slender, and a \(1\)-Dyck loop
is a language
\[
  \{u v^n w x^n y:n\ge0\}.
\]
Their Theorem~7 states that a context-free language is \(0\)-poly-slender
if and only if it is a finite union of \(1\)-Dyck loops
\cite[Theorem~7]{IlieRozenbergSalomaa2000}. The characterization was first
proved, in the equivalent terminology of iterative or semidiscrete
context-free languages, by Latteux--Thierrin
\cite{LatteuxThierrin1983}, and independently by Ilie
\cite{Ilie1994}.

Mignotte's common-value theorem concerns two rational-integer linear
recurrence sequences. In its dominant-root form, each sequence has an
expansion
\[
  A_n=a\lambda^n+\sum_{i=2}^s P_i(n)\lambda_i^n,
  \qquad
  B_m=b\kappa^m+\sum_{j=2}^t Q_j(m)\kappa_j^m,
\]
where \(a,b\ne0\), the characteristic roots and coefficients are
algebraic, \(\lambda,\kappa>1\), and
\(
  \abs{\lambda_i}<\lambda,
  \abs{\kappa_j}<\kappa
\)
for all remaining characteristic roots. If \(\lambda\) and \(\kappa\)
are multiplicatively independent, then \(A_n=B_m\) has only finitely many
pairs of nonnegative integer solutions; the proof is effective
\cite{Mignotte1978Intersection}. We use exactly this finiteness conclusion.

The next lemma supplies the hypothesis that is not a consequence merely of
the root-growth squeeze.

\begin{lemma}[Dominant-root transfer for a paired greedy loop]
  \label{lem:paired-loop-dominant-root-transfer}
  Let \(U=(U_n)_{n\ge0}\) be a strictly increasing standard greedy linear
  numeration sequence. Suppose that its least eventual recurrence
  polynomial is the minimal polynomial \(\mu\) of a weak Perron number
  \(\alpha>1\). If
  \[
    z_n=u v^n w x^n y\in\mathcal R_U\qquad(n\ge0)
  \]
  and \(D=\abs v+\abs x>0\), then there is \(h\ge1\) such that, for every
  \(0\le r<h\), the integer sequence
  \[
    Z_r(t)=\val_U(z_{ht+r})\qquad(t\ge0)
  \]
  is eventually a linear recurrence whose least eventual characteristic
  roots have the unique dominating root
  \[
    \alpha^{hD}.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We audit the actual affine matrices. Let \(C\) be the companion matrix of
  \(\mu\). For a block \(b=b_1\cdots b_\ell\), multiplication of the digit
  matrices in \Cref{lem:affine-recurrence-block-action} gives
  \begin{equation}
    M_b=
    \begin{pmatrix}1&q_b\\0&C^\ell\end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:paired-loop-affine-block-form}
  \end{equation}
  for a row \(q_b\), with \(M_\varepsilon=I\). Because \(\mu\) is
  irreducible over characteristic zero, it is separable, so \(C\) is
  diagonalizable over a splitting field. Hence \(C^\ell\) is
  diagonalizable even when distinct conjugates acquire the same
  \(\ell\)-th power. The eigenvalues of \(M_b\) are \(1\) and
  \(\gamma^\ell\), where \(\gamma\) runs over the conjugates of \(\alpha\).
  If \(b\ne\varepsilon\), every peripheral eigenvalue has modulus
  \(\alpha^\ell>1\), so it cannot collide with the affine eigenvalue \(1\).
  Equation \eqref{eq:paired-loop-affine-block-form} then shows that its
  peripheral generalized eigenspace is semisimple. Thus the affine row
  creates neither a peripheral eigenvalue nor a peripheral Jordan block.

  Discarding finitely many initial parameters puts the reading position
  beyond the recurrence transient. If \(v\ne\varepsilon\), the represented
  value is then a fixed scalar functional of
  \[
    M_x^n A M_v^n
  \]
  for one fixed matrix \(A\); if \(v=\varepsilon\), it is a fixed scalar
  functional of \(M_x^n A\). This follows directly by reading the five
  fixed blocks in LSD-first order; fixed initial powers used to cross the
  transient are absorbed into \(A\). Vectorization in the first case gives
  the actual powering matrix
  \[
    K=M_v^{\mathsf T}\otimes M_x,
  \]
  with the evident one-factor interpretation when one pumped block is
  empty. Its spectral radius is \(\alpha^D\). Its peripheral eigenvalues
  are exactly the products
  \(
    \gamma_1^{\abs v}\gamma_2^{\abs x}
  \)
  obtained from peripheral conjugates in the nonempty factors, and their
  generalized eigenspaces are semisimple by the preceding paragraph and
  the tensor-product Jordan formula.

  Choose \(h\) from the weak-Perron power characterization, so that
  \[
    \abs\gamma=\alpha\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \gamma^h=\alpha^h
  \]
  for every conjugate \(\gamma\). Every peripheral eigenvalue of \(K^h\) is then
  the same positive number
  \(
    \Lambda=\alpha^{hD}
  \), and the \(\Lambda\)-generalized eigenspace is still semisimple.
  All other eigenvalues of \(K^h\) have modulus strictly below
  \(\Lambda\). Cayley--Hamilton applied to the displayed scalar functional
  therefore gives, for each residue \(r\),
  \begin{equation}
    Z_r(t)=c_r\Lambda^t+
      \sum_j P_{r,j}(t)\xi_{r,j}^t,
    \qquad \abs{\xi_{r,j}}<\Lambda.
    \label{eq:paired-loop-spectral-expansion}
  \end{equation}
  This proves at matrix level that peripheral collisions coalesce to one
  algebraic root and that no polynomial factor in \(t\) can accompany it.

  It remains to audit cancellation by the actual value functional. The
  word \(z_{ht+r}\) has length \(L_r+hDt\) for a fixed \(L_r\). Greedy
  validity gives
  \[
    U_{L_r+hDt-1}\le Z_r(t)<U_{L_r+hDt}.
  \]
  The positive residue asymptotics
  \eqref{eq:weak-perron-residue-asymptotic}--
  \eqref{eq:weak-perron-positive-residues}, applied to these two fixed
  index residues for the chosen period, show
  \[
    0<\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{Z_r(t)}{\Lambda^t}
      \le
      \limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{Z_r(t)}{\Lambda^t}<\infty.
  \]
  In \eqref{eq:paired-loop-spectral-expansion} every lower-modulus term,
  including all its possible Jordan polynomials, is \(o(\Lambda^t)\).
  Consequently the spectral-projection coefficient \(c_r\) of the actual
  affine value functional is nonzero (indeed positive). Total peripheral
  cancellation is therefore impossible, and \(\Lambda\) survives as the
  unique dominating root of the least scalar recurrence.
\end{proof}

For \(X\subseteq\NN_{\ge1}\), write
\[
  \operatorname{Rep}_U(X)
    =\{z\in\mathcal R_U:\val_U(z)\in X\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Slender-context-free Cobham theorem]
  \label{thm:slender-cfl-cobham}
  Let \(U\) and \(V\) be strictly increasing standard greedy linear
  numeration sequences whose least eventual recurrence polynomials are the
  minimal polynomials of weak Perron numbers \(\alpha>1\) and \(\beta>1\),
  respectively. Suppose that \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are multiplicatively
  independent. If, for one set \(X\subseteq\NN_{\ge1}\), both full
  representation languages
  \[
    \operatorname{Rep}_U(X)
    \quad\hbox{and}\quad
    \operatorname{Rep}_V(X)
  \]
  are slender context-free languages, then \(X\) is finite.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Suppose that \(X\) is infinite. The paired-loop classification gives
  finite covers
  \[
    \begin{aligned}
      \operatorname{Rep}_U(X)
        &=\bigcup_{i=1}^a
          \{u_i v_i^n w_i x_i^n y_i:n\ge0\},\\
      \operatorname{Rep}_V(X)
        &=\bigcup_{j=1}^b
          \{u'_j(v'_j)^m w'_j(x'_j)^m y'_j:m\ge0\}.
    \end{aligned}
  \]
  Loops with zero total pumped length are singletons and may be removed;
  they cover only finitely many values. Apply
  \Cref{lem:paired-loop-dominant-root-transfer} to every remaining loop.
  After the finitely many residue restrictions, \(X\) is covered in each
  system by finitely many integer recurrence sequences having respective
  unique dominating roots
  \[
    \alpha^{h_i(\abs{v_i}+\abs{x_i})}
    \quad\hbox{and}\quad
    \beta^{k_j(\abs{v'_j}+\abs{x'_j})}.
  \]
  Infinitely many elements of \(X\) must lie in the intersection of the
  images of one sequence from the first finite family and one from the
  second. Their dominating roots are multiplicatively independent, since
  they are positive powers of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\). Mignotte's theorem
  says that the equality of their terms has only finitely many index pairs,
  a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Priority and scope]
  \label{rem:slender-cobham-priority}
  Three existing Cobham statements delimit the result. The natural published
  benchmark for the regular integer-base case is the quantitative
  Cobham--Semenov theorem of Albayrak--Bell. For multiplicatively independent
  integers \(k,\ell\ge2\), they prove that the intersection of a sparse
  \(k\)-automatic subset of \(\mathbb N^d\) and a sparse \(\ell\)-automatic
  subset of \(\mathbb N^d\) is finite, with an explicit bound in terms of the
  bases, dimension, and minimal automata
  \cite[Theorems~1.1 and 4.1]{AlbayrakBell2023}. Taking the two sets equal gives
  simultaneous sparse automatic finiteness. A slender regular integer-base
  representation language is sparse, so this contains the regular
  integer-base specialization of the theorem above. More broadly, their
  \(d\)-dimensional statement covers sparse automatic tuple sets whose regular
  representation languages may have polynomial rather than bounded word
  growth; in particular it covers the regular poly-slender integer-base
  shadow. Their theorem is strictly stronger on the overlap and broader in
  this regular direction, while the present theorem supplies no quantitative
  bound.

  Second, Durand proves that a sequence which is simultaneously
  \(\alpha\)- and \(\beta\)-substitutive for multiplicatively independent
  Perron numbers is ultimately periodic
  \cite[Theorem~1]{Durand2011CobhamSubstitutions}. Third, the abstract-
  numeration theorem of Charlier--Leroy--Rigo starts with infinite regular
  languages ordered genealogically, requires both representation languages
  of the set to be regular, and concludes that simultaneous recognition in
  two independent systems gives a finite union of arithmetic progressions
  \cite[Definitions~33, 34, and 47 and Theorem~49]%
  {CharlierLeroyRigo2013CobhamANS}. In its exponential case, independence
  means multiplicative independence of the dominant language-growth
  eigenvalues. Thus the regular Perron-substitutive and regular abstract-
  numeration shadows are already part of existing Cobham theory.

  Those recognition hypotheses fail at the full scope of the theorem above.
  A slender context-free representation language may be genuinely
  nonregular, so it need not furnish either a finite automaton recognizing
  the full representation language or a substitutive characteristic
  sequence. The dimension in Albayrak--Bell does not remove this condition:
  a coordinate or product encoding enters their theorem only when its padded
  synchronous tuple language is itself sparse regular, and its coordinates
  still use ordinary integer-base valuation. It therefore does not absorb a
  genuinely nonregular paired-loop language or replace the weak-Perron affine
  value map used here. Nor need a weak-Perron positional system supply the
  regular canonical, genealogical-rank interface required by an abstract
  numeration system.
  The role of \Cref{lem:paired-loop-dominant-root-transfer} is precisely the
  nonregular weak-Perron bridge: it sends each word-theoretic paired loop to
  residue subsequences with a unique positive dominant root, while resolving
  peripheral collisions, affine and tensor-product Jordan factors, and
  cancellation by the value functional. Only after that transfer can
  Mignotte's common-value theorem be applied.

  Accordingly, the regular integer-base and regular abstract-numeration
  shadows belong to existing Cobham theory. What is new here is the passage
  from regular recognition to full slender context-free representation
  languages, together with the dominant-root transfer for paired loops in
  weak-Perron positional systems. This claim does not extend to nonregular
  poly-slender representation languages: finite unions of higher Dyck loops
  would require a genuinely multivariate argument, which is not asserted
  here.
\end{remark}
