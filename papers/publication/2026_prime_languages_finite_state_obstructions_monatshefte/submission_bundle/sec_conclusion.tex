\section{Conclusion and open interface}\label{sec:conclusion}

The affine recurrence action turns one weak MCFL pumping family into a
single injective numerical orbit with simultaneous local returns. Positive
pumped length and uniqueness of canonical representation supply
distinctness, while finite-group return times supply congruences. Applying
Broughan's adic classifications to these orbits,
together with the deleted-prime intrinsic derivative calculation, gives the
scattered-set obstruction, the nonunit escape dichotomy, and prime and
bounded-\(\Omega\) MCF-immunity.

For the minimal tail recurrence, the residual bounded-support phenomenon
has an exact structural form. It occurs on an infinite finite-fan-out MCFL
if and only if the canonical language contains a synchronized ray with
values \(cb^t\). Evertse's quotient theorem is used with an explicit
prime-ideal norm bound to reduce a fixed-support recurrence to one
characteristic root; Schur's theorem removes its polynomial factor. The
ratio \(b\) uses only primes dividing the minimal tail constant. In a
standard greedy linear numeration basis with weak-Perron recurrence, residue
asymptotics and length thresholds show that such a ray exists exactly when
some positive power of the weak Perron number is an integer. Alternating
radices give an infinite family with nonintegral root \(\sqrt B\) and a
regular ray of values \(B^t\).

On the promised class of valid effectively presented regular canonical
systems, with the minimal tail recurrence and threshold supplied or
certified, positive promised instances can be enumerated and certified. The unresolved general step is not
candidate verification but witness compression: no computable bound is
known for the fan-out and word lengths of a smallest geometric synchronized
scheme. DFA path compression does not preserve the associated affine
recurrence matrices. Conversely, no reduction proving undecidability is
known that also preserves unique canonical representation, the minimal tail
recurrence, and the special digit-matrix form.
Accordingly the general decision problem remains open, while the
weak-Perron subclass has the exact algebraic classification above.

The nearest broader global problem is the degeneracy/Cobham-extension
problem for context-free sequences discussed by Caucal and Le Gonidec
\cite{CaucalLeGonidec2014}; this paper does not settle it. The weak pumping
mechanism itself still supplies only one ray inside one infinite MCFL, and
two representations of the same set may supply unrelated rays with no
common values. It neither controls every sufficiently long word nor the
whole canonical language. The slender-context-free theorem proved here
escapes that ceiling only because the Latteux--Thierrin--Ilie
classification replaces a single pumped orbit by a finite paired-loop cover
of each full representation language. Mignotte's theorem then makes every
pair of loop images have finite intersection. Retaining the individual
recurrence shifts, spectral data, and effective Mignotte thresholds gives the
cardinality bound in \Cref{thm:effective-slender-cfl-cardinality}, but not a
closed bound in compact grammar-size parameters. This is a different global
argument, not a strengthening of the weak MCFL pumping lemma.
