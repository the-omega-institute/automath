\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Let \(U=(U_n)_{n\ge0}\) be a sequence of place values and let a word
\(x_0\cdots x_{m-1}\) be read least-significant digit first, with value
\[
  \val_U(x_0\cdots x_{m-1})=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}x_jU_j.
\]
This paper studies an arithmetic restriction imposed by finite-fan-out
multiple context-free syntax: how few rational primes can occur among the
values of an infinite multiple context-free sublanguage of a canonical
numeration language? The answer is governed by a local recurrence action.
It applies beyond ordinary bases and remains informative when the trailing
coefficient of the recurrence is not a unit.

The ambient assumptions are deliberately explicit. The sequence \(U\) is
strictly increasing and eventually satisfies an integral linear recurrence
with nonzero trailing coefficient. A finite nonnegative digit alphabet and
a canonical language \(\mathcal R_U\) give exactly one representation of
each positive integer. No ordering of values by canonical length is assumed.
Regularity of \(\mathcal R_U\) is not needed for the structural theorems; it
is imposed only for the effective statement.

\subsection{Main results}

The recurrence gives an affine matrix \(D_\varepsilon\) for each digit.
After a fixed prefix beyond the transient, concatenation is matrix
multiplication, and every block matrix is invertible modulo each integer
coprime to the trailing coefficient. The weak pumping lemma of
Seki--Matsumura--Fujii--Kasami then supplies a single synchronized family
\[
  W(t)=a u_1v_1^tw_1s_1^tu_2\cdots
       u_kv_k^tw_ks_k^tu_{k+1}\qquad(t\ge0)
\]
inside every infinite \(k\)-MCFL. Positive pumped length makes the words
distinct, and uniqueness of canonical representations makes
\(N(t)=\val_U(W(t))\) pairwise distinct. For every admissible
modulus \(q\), a common return time for the pumped block matrices gives
\[
  N(t+H(q))\equiv N(t)\pmod q\qquad(t\ge0).
\]
This is \Cref{thm:mcfl-synchronized-congruence-orbit}. Importantly, the
factorization is fixed before \(q\) is chosen.

The same orbit has two topological consequences. Broughan's standard adic
topologies, completions, and ambient prime-factor strata identify a
countable no-isolated-point subspace with \(\mathbb Q\) and its profinite
closure with the Cantor set. Our step is to prove that every synchronized
MCFL orbit satisfies that hypothesis,
yielding a scattered-set obstruction
(\Cref{thm:mcfl-local-adic-fingerprint}). The deleted-prime intrinsic
Cantor--Bendixson height of a set with at most \(K\) primes outside the
exceptional support and bounded exceptional valuations is \(K+1\)
(\Cref{prop:local-prime-factor-cb-rank}). These facts give the nonunit
escape dichotomy \Cref{thm:nonunit-mcfl-escape-dichotomy}: on every
infinite MCFL, either the number of prime divisors outside the trailing
coefficient is unbounded, or one valuation at a prime dividing that
coefficient is unbounded. Prime and bounded-\(\Omega\) representation
languages are therefore MCF-immune without a unit hypothesis.

The trailing coefficient of a nonminimal recurrence witness is not an
invariant. \Cref{prop:recurrence-witness-separation} exhibits ordinary
base \(p\) and the Zeckendorf sequence with inflated recurrence witnesses
having the same exceptional prime \(p\), but opposite bounded-support
behavior. We therefore pass to the minimal tail recurrence
\[
  \mu_U(X)=X^{d_{\mathrm r}}-c_{d_{\mathrm r}-1}X^{d_{\mathrm r}-1}
    -\cdots-c_1X-c_0
\]
and write \(S_{\mathrm r}=\{p:p\mid c_0\}\).

The central inverse theorem is the geometric-ray characterization
\Cref{thm:bounded-outside-support-geometric-ray}. The following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item some infinite finite-fan-out MCFL \(L\subseteq\mathcal R_U\)
    has bounded prime support outside \(S_{\mathrm r}\);
  \item \(\mathcal R_U\) contains a synchronized family \(W(t)\) of the
    displayed form with \(\val_U(W(t))=cb^t\) for integers \(c\ge1\),
    \(b\ge2\);
  \item such a family itself forms an infinite linear MCFL of finite
    fan-out.
\end{enumerate}
The difficult direction first uses local return times to pass to a
subsequence supported on a fixed finite set of primes. That subsequence is
a pairwise distinct positive linear recurrence. We then separate root-of-unity
classes of characteristic roots. Evertse's theorem says uniformly that the
largest prime-ideal norm in a quotient of two terms of a nondegenerate
recurrence with at least two characteristic roots tends to infinity
\cite[Theorem~3]{Evertse1984}. Since quotients of the fixed-support
subsequence have prime ideals only above a fixed finite set of rational
primes, Evertse excludes two or more roots. Schur's theorem excludes a
nonconstant polynomial factor in the remaining one-root expression. Some
arithmetic subsequence is consequently exactly \(cb^t\); no claim is made
that the original recurrence is itself geometric. The full
prime-ideal norm bound and all quantifiers from Evertse's input are given
in \Cref{lem:evertse-quotient-support-input}; this recurrence theorem is
used as a classical input, not claimed as new.

Every geometric ratio is locally constrained:
\[
  \operatorname{rad}(b)\mid\operatorname{rad}(|c_0|)
\]
by \Cref{lem:geometric-ratio-tail-support}. If the digit alphabet,
recurrence data, minimal tail recurrence, and a DFA for \(\mathcal R_U\)
are effective, a proposed synchronized scheme can be checked using the
finite transition monoid and an exact recurrence identity. On the promised
class of valid presentations, with a supplied or certified minimal tail
recurrence and threshold, enumerating all schemes proves the \(\Sigma^0_1\)
upper bound for positive promised instances in
\Cref{cor:bounded-outside-support-semidecidable}.

This does not decide negative instances. A general decision procedure
would require a computable complete bound on fan-out and block lengths, or
an equivalent finite negative certificate. DFA loop compression alone does
not preserve the affine recurrence matrix and therefore does not preserve
the exact geometric identity. \Cref{rem:general-decision-interface}
records this failed step precisely. No undecidability claim is made: known
undecidability for unrestricted matrix equations does not preserve the
companion-form digit action and the canonical conditions used here.

There are two resolved regimes. When the trailing coefficient is a unit,
the synchronized orbit admits quotient congruences of arbitrary prescribed
depth and an induced copy of the countably branching divisibility tree with
pairwise coprime, arbitrarily rough edge quotients
(\Cref{thm:unit-mcfl-deep-congruence-chain,thm:unit-mcfl-divisibility-tree}).
For standard greedy linear numeration bases with weak-Perron recurrence,
length thresholds complete the bounded-support classification: the
geometric-ray properties above hold exactly when some positive power of the
weak Perron number is an integer
(\Cref{cor:linear-perron-bounded-support-classification}). Hence the
strict-Perron subclass recovers the integral-root boundary, while
imprimitive examples such as alternating-radix systems with
\(\beta=\sqrt B\) give genuinely nonintegral positive cases. The
residue-growth mechanism used in this specialization is not new:
Charlier--Kreczman obtain residue-class quotient limits for regular
positional systems and record the corresponding converse for eventually
increasing linear recurrences whose dominating eigenvalues have a common
power \cite[Proposition~10 and Remark~12]{CharlierKreczman2025}. Our
contribution here is the prime-support/MCFL interface and its exact
geometric-ray and algebraic classification, not that quotient-limit
mechanism.

A separate two-system result begins from a different language-theoretic
input. The Latteux--Thierrin--Ilie classification gives a finite paired-loop
cover of an entire slender context-free language. Combining that global
cover with the affine block action, the weak-Perron residue asymptotics, and
Mignotte's common-value theorem for recurrence sequences yields
\Cref{thm:slender-cfl-cobham}. Thus, for two multiplicatively independent
weak-Perron linear numeration systems, the representations of one fixed set
cannot both be slender context-free unless the set is finite. The natural
published benchmark for the regular integer-base case is Albayrak--Bell's
\(d\)-dimensional quantitative Cobham--Semenov theorem for sparse automatic
sets \cite[Theorems~1.1 and 4.1]{AlbayrakBell2023}. It contains the regular
integer-base specialization, covers regular sparse (in particular regular
poly-slender) tuple languages more broadly, and supplies an explicit
automaton-data bound. Regular Perron-substitutive and regular abstract-
numeration shadows likewise belong to existing Cobham theory
\cite[Theorem~1]{Durand2011CobhamSubstitutions}%
\cite[Theorem~49]{CharlierLeroyRigo2013CobhamANS}. The new part here is the
passage from regular recognition to full slender context-free representation
languages, together with the dominant-root transfer for paired loops in
weak-Perron positional systems. This is not an extension of the one-orbit
pumping theorem: it does not use finite-group returns, deleted-prime
topology, fixed-support quotient rigidity, geometric subsequences, or the
divisibility tree. From complete effective recurrence data and constructive
paired-loop covers, \Cref{thm:effective-slender-cfl-cardinality} additionally
computes a cardinality bound. It does not extract an Albayrak--Bell-style
closed formula in compact grammar-size parameters.

\subsection{Relation to prior work}\label{subsec:prior-work}

The fixed-base context-free prime obstruction is classical, due to
Hartmanis--Shank and Sch\"utzenberger
\cite{HartmanisShank1968,Schutzenberger1968}. Our language-theoretic input
is exactly Seki--Matsumura--Fujii--Kasami's weak pumping lemma
\cite[Lemma~3.2]{SekiEtAl1991}: an infinite \(k\)-MCFL contains one family
with \(2k\) fixed factors pumped by the same exponent. It says nothing
about every sufficiently long word, and the corresponding strong pumping
property fails for general MCFLs
\cite{KanazawaKobeleMichaelisSalvatiYoshinaka2014}.

Duncan--Elder--Frenkel--Lyu's newer substitution lemma
\cite[Theorem~4.5]{DuncanElderFrenkelLiu2026} instead constrains every word
with sufficiently many marked positions, but permits a switchable-tuple
alternative to bounded pumping. Its substitutions are not a single
one-parameter family of simultaneous powers of fixed blocks. They therefore
do not give the fixed affine matrix powers, one return time for all
parameters, or all-modulus return used here; nor do they replace the paired
Ogden pump in the Zeckendorf argument. No immunity conclusion of this paper
follows directly from that lemma. We use the older weak lemma because it
provides exactly one fixed synchronized orbit. Placing that orbit beyond the
recurrence transient, proving its finite-group returns, and deriving the
prime-support consequences are the contributions here.

Evertse's theorem on sums of \(S\)-units and linear recurrences is the
nearest arithmetic input \cite{Evertse1984}. It supplies exactly the
unbounded prime-ideal support in term quotients needed for the geometric
rigidity step; it does not state the synchronized-language characterization.
Later \(S\)-part estimates for recurrence terms, such as
Bugeaud--Evertse \cite{BugeaudEvertse2017}, likewise study the size of the
part supported on a fixed finite prime set, not synchronized numeration
languages. The recurrence rigidity lemma below packages these classical
arithmetic inputs for our language interface rather than claiming a separate
new \(S\)-unit theorem. Broughan's Theorem~2.4, Corollary~3.1, and
Theorem~4.3 \cite{Broughan2003} provide respectively the
\(\mathbb Q\)-space classification, Cantor completion, and full-adic
prime-factor strata. These ambient adic spaces and their classifications
are standard. Our packaging consists of the MCFL-orbit application, the
deleted-prime sets and their intrinsic derivative, and the resulting
``local prime-factor rank.'' Proposition~2.10 is a local refinement inside
that standard ambient space, not a new classification of it.
Frougny's
normalization theorem and the Bertrand and substitution literature provide
the regular canonical interfaces for Pisot numeration
\cite{Frougny1992,BruyereHansel1997,Fabre1995,FrougnySolomyak1992}.

Charlier--Kreczman remove the dominant-root restriction from the regularity
problem for positional numeration systems \cite{CharlierKreczman2025}.
They associate a periodic alternate real base to the residue classes of the
place-value sequence and give a full characterization of regularity through
the graph of the greedy and quasi-greedy expansions of (1) in the shifted
alternate bases. Their procedure is a semidecision in general, and becomes a
decision procedure when the associated alternate base is known to be Parry.
Their Proposition~10 proves that regularity of the whole greedy language
forces residue-class consecutive-quotient limits. More directly relevant
here, Remark~12 states that, for an arbitrary linear recurrence, equality of
the (p)-th powers of its dominating eigenvalues implies convergence of
\(U_n/U_{n-p}\) to that common power when \((\lvert U_n\rvert)\) is
eventually increasing. Under the irreducible weak-Perron hypotheses below,
separability gives equal peripheral multiplicities, weak-Perron periodicity
gives the common power, and strict increase supplies their last hypothesis.
Thus their work already contains the root-growth mechanism needed for our
length squeeze, in essentially equivalent quotient-limit form. We retain a
self-contained coefficient-level proof because it identifies every residue
coefficient and verifies positivity under exactly our hypotheses. Their
regularity characterization neither assumes nor concludes anything about
MCFL sublanguages, bounded prime support, synchronized geometric schemes, or
the five equivalent conditions proved here; conversely, our theorem does not
characterize regularity of the whole greedy language.

The exact relation with Skolem, Positivity, Ultimate Positivity, orbit
hitting, and unrestricted matrix equations is stated once, at the effective
boundary in \Cref{rem:general-decision-interface}; none supplies the missing
finite witness bound for the constrained companion-form problem here.

The Supplementary Information (Online Resource 1) retains the finite-state results: the
density dichotomy and effective fixed-DFA prime-slice bounds in ordinary
bases, the corresponding Zeckendorf growth and regular-language results,
and the classical analytic comparisons. It compares its fixed-DFA error
bounds explicitly with Shen's qualitative obstruction and Dubbe's growing
state-complexity bound. These independent results are not used in the proof
of the recurrent MCFL theorem chain.

\subsection{Organization}

\Cref{sec:context-free-pisot} begins with the context-free Zeckendorf
motivation, develops the recurrent block action and synchronized MCFL orbit,
proves the local-adic and prime-support consequences, establishes the
geometric-ray characterization and its effective boundary, and concludes
with the unit and weak-Perron specializations. \Cref{sec:slender-cobham}
uses the global paired-loop classification to prove the separate two-system
finiteness theorem, gives its effective cardinality refinement from complete
presentations, and locates its nonregular contribution relative to existing
Cobham results. \Cref{sec:conclusion} summarizes the proved scope and the
remaining decision interface.
