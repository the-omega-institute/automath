\section{Deterministic Automata and Base-\texorpdfstring{$b$}{b} Primes}
\label{sec:automata}

Throughout this section, fix an integer \(b\ge 2\) and write
\(\Sigma_b:=\{0,1,\dots,b-1\}\).

\begin{definition}\label{def:dfa}
A complete deterministic finite automaton over \(\Sigma_b\) is a tuple
\(\mathcal A=(Q,\Sigma_b,\delta,q_0,F)\), where \(Q\) is a finite state
set, \(q_0\in Q\) is the initial state, \(F\subseteq Q\) is the set of
accepting states, and
\[
  \delta:Q\times\Sigma_b\to Q
\]
is a total transition map.
\end{definition}

Completeness is only a convenient normalization here: adjoining a sink
state completes any DFA without changing its language. The substantive
restriction throughout is determinism.

For \(m\ge 0\), write
\[
  a_m(\mathcal A)
  :=\#\{w\in\Sigma_b^m:\mathcal A\text{ accepts }w\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Density dichotomy for DFA languages]
  \label{thm:dfa-density-dichotomy}
  There exist an integer \(D_A\ge 1\), non-negative constants
  \(c_0,\dots,c_{D_A-1}\), and \(\theta\in(0,1)\) such that
  \[
    \frac{a_m(\mathcal A)}{b^m}=c_{m\bmod D_A}+O(\theta^m)
    \qquad (m\to\infty).
  \]
  Consequently exactly one of the following alternatives holds:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item There exists \(\eta\in(0,1)\) such that the normalized
    density satisfies
    \[
      \frac{a_m(\mathcal A)}{b^m}=O(\eta^m);
    \]
    equivalently, \(a_m(\mathcal A)=O((b\eta)^m)\).
    \item There exists a residue class \(r\bmod D_A\) and a constant
    \(c>0\) such that
    \[
      \frac{a_{r+kD_A}(\mathcal A)}{b^{r+kD_A}}\ge c
      \qquad\text{for all sufficiently large }k.
    \]
  \end{enumerate}
  In particular, no DFA language can satisfy
  \[
    a_m(\mathcal A)/b^m\asymp m^{-\alpha}
  \]
  for any \(\alpha>0\). More generally, there do not exist
  \(K_1,K_2>0\) and an infinite set \(M\subseteq\NN\) such that
  \[
    \frac{K_1}{m}\le \frac{a_m(\mathcal A)}{b^m}\le \frac{K_2}{m}
    \qquad(m\in M).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  For each digit \(d\in\Sigma_b\), let \(T_d\in\{0,1\}^{Q\times Q}\) be
  the transition matrix for \(d\), and let
  \[
    B:=\sum_{d\in\Sigma_b}T_d.
  \]
  Since the automaton is complete and deterministic, every row sum of
  \(B\) is equal to \(b\). If \(u\) is the indicator vector of the
  initial state and \(v\) is the indicator of the accepting set, then
  \[
    a_m(\mathcal A)=u^{\mathsf T}B^m v.
  \]
  Set \(P:=B/b\). Then \(P\) is a stochastic matrix on the finite set
  \(Q\). After permuting states, put \(P\) in canonical Markov-chain
  form
  \[
    P=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      T & R_1 & \cdots & R_s\\
      0 & C_1 & 0 & 0\\
      \vdots & 0 & \ddots & 0\\
      0 & 0 & 0 & C_s
    \end{pmatrix},
  \]
  where \(T\) is the transient block, \(\rho(T)<1\), and each \(C_i\)
  is an irreducible stochastic recurrent class. For \(m\ge1\),
  \[
    P^m=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      T^m &
      \sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}T^\ell R_1C_1^{m-1-\ell} &
      \cdots &
      \sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}T^\ell R_sC_s^{m-1-\ell}\\
      0 & C_1^m & 0 & 0\\
      \vdots & 0 & \ddots & 0\\
      0 & 0 & 0 & C_s^m
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  Thus the transient-to-recurrent blocks need not be exponentially
  small: their absorption convolutions generally converge to non-zero
  periodic limits.

  Let \(d_i\) be the period of \(C_i\), and put
  \(D_A:=\operatorname{lcm}(d_1,\dots,d_s)\). By the periodic
  Perron--Frobenius theorem for the recurrent blocks, each sequence
  \(C_i^{kD_A+r}\) converges exponentially fast as \(k\to\infty\), for
  each fixed \(r\). Since \(T^\ell\) decays exponentially, the displayed
  convolution also has an exponentially fast limit on every residue
  class. Hence the entrywise limits
  \[
    S_r:=\lim_{k\to\infty}P^{kD_A+r}
    \qquad(0\le r<D_A)
  \]
  exist; the matrices \(S_r\) include both the recurrent-class limits
  and the absorption terms from transient states into those recurrent
  classes. This theorem uses these as qualitative Markov-chain limits;
  the certified computable version, with rational tail constants and
  verifiable proof objects, is isolated in
  \Cref{lem:effective-spectral-constants,thm:effective-fixed-dfa-prime-obstruction}.
  Consequently there are \(C>0\) and \(\theta\in(0,1)\) such
  that
  \[
    P^m=S_{m\bmod D_A}+E_m,
    \qquad
    \|E_m\|\le C\theta^m.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    u^{\mathsf T}P^m v=u^{\mathsf T}S_{m\bmod D_A}v+O(\theta^m).
  \]
  For \(0\le r<D_A\), define
  \[
    c_r:=\lim_{k\to\infty}u^{\mathsf T}P^{kD_A+r}v=u^{\mathsf T}S_r v.
  \]
  The limit exists by the preceding expansion, and \(c_r\ge 0\) because
  every term \(u^{\mathsf T}P^{kD_A+r}v\) is non-negative. Thus
  \[
    \frac{a_m(\mathcal A)}{b^m}
    =u^{\mathsf T}P^m v
    =c_{m\bmod D_A}+O(\theta^m),
  \]
  as claimed.

  If every \(c_r\) vanishes, then
  \(a_m(\mathcal A)/b^m=O(\theta^m)\), which is alternative
  \textup{(i)}. Otherwise some \(c_r\) is strictly positive, and along
  the corresponding residue class the density has a positive lower
  bound, which is alternative \textup{(ii)}.

  The exclusion of intermediate polynomial density follows by the same
  residue-class argument. Suppose first that
  \(a_m(\mathcal A)/b^m\asymp m^{-\alpha}\) for some \(\alpha>0\).
  If all \(c_r\) vanish, then \(a_m(\mathcal A)/b^m=O(\theta^m)\), which
  is eventually smaller than every positive multiple of \(m^{-\alpha}\).
  If some \(c_r>0\), then along that residue class
  \(a_m(\mathcal A)/b^m\ge c_r/2\) for all sufficiently large \(m\),
  which is incompatible with an upper bound \(O(m^{-\alpha})\).
  Finally, suppose that for some \(K_1,K_2>0\) the two-sided
  finite-budget bound \(K_1/m\le a_m(\mathcal A)/b^m\le K_2/m\) holds
  on an infinite set \(M\). Passing to an infinite subset in one residue
  class \(r\bmod D_A\), the case \(c_r=0\) contradicts the lower bound
  \(K_1/m\), while the case \(c_r>0\) contradicts the upper bound
  \(K_2/m\). This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

For later use we spell out the elementary effective content of the
preceding proof. Assume that the entries of the DFA transition matrices
are given explicitly. Put \(P=B/b\). The strongly connected components
of the directed graph of \(P\), the recurrent classes, their periods
\(d_i\), and hence \(D_A=\operatorname{lcm}(d_i)\), are obtained by the
standard linear-time graph algorithms. For each residue class \(r\bmod
D_A\), the limit
\[
  c_r=\lim_{k\to\infty}u^{\mathsf T}P^{kD_A+r}v
\]
is the value of the finite Markov chain on that residue class; it can be
computed by solving the finite linear systems for the stationary
distributions on the recurrent classes and the absorption probabilities
from transient states. Equivalently, it is obtained as the exact
limit of the rational matrices \(P^{kD_A+r}\). Thus the residue
alternatives \(c_r=0\) and \(c_r>0\) are decidable by exact rational
linear algebra when the transition table is integral.

This density dichotomy is classical; compare
\cite{Berstel1973,BodirskyGaertnerVonOertzenSchwinghammer2004,Bell2020UpperDensity,Koga2019Density}.
It is not the new ingredient of the paper; we keep the short proof
because the residue-class constants are the input certified in
\Cref{thm:effective-fixed-dfa-prime-obstruction} and then used in the
prime corollaries below.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit prime-counting input]
  \label{lem:explicit-pnt-input}
  The following numerical constants are admissible:
  \[
    X_0=355991,\qquad C_{\pi,1}=2,\qquad C_{\pi,2}=2.
  \]
  For every \(x\ge X_0\),
  \[
    \left|\pi(x)-\frac{x}{\log x}\right|
      \le C_{\pi,1}\frac{x}{(\log x)^2},
    \qquad
    \pi(x)\le C_{\pi,2}\frac{x}{\log x}.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Rosser--Schoenfeld prove \(\pi(x)>x/\log x\) for \(x\ge17\)
  \cite{RosserSchoenfeld1962}. Dusart's published explicit upper
  estimate \cite[Theorem~1.10]{Dusart2018} gives
  \[
    \pi(x)\le
      \frac{x}{\log x}\left(1+\frac{1}{\log x}
        +\frac{2.51}{(\log x)^2}\right)
    \qquad (x\ge355991).
  \]
  These are the exact published inputs used here. Since
  \(\log 355991>12\), for \(x\ge355991\) we have
  \[
    0\le \pi(x)-\frac{x}{\log x}
    \le \frac{x}{(\log x)^2}
       +2.51\,\frac{x}{(\log x)^3}
    \le 2\,\frac{x}{(\log x)^2},
  \]
  and also
  \[
    \pi(x)\le
      \frac{x}{\log x}\left(1+\frac{1}{\log x}
        +\frac{2.51}{(\log x)^2}\right)
      \le 2\,\frac{x}{\log x}.
  \]
\end{proof}

We shall use the following interval-count convention in both prime-slice
estimates. For \(X<Y\) with \(X\ge X_0\),
\[
  \#\{p\text{ prime}:X\le p<Y\}
  =\pi(Y)-\pi(X)+\varepsilon_{X,Y},
  \qquad |\varepsilon_{X,Y}|\le2.
\]
Thus all half-open endpoint changes in the slice estimates are accounted
for by one absolute \(O(1)\) term.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:dfa-density-sharpness}
  Both alternatives occur already for elementary regular languages. The
  language \(\Sigma_b^\ast 0\) of words ending in \(0\) has density
  \(1/b\) in every positive length, so it lies in
  alternative~\textup{(ii)}. By contrast, the language \(0^\ast 1\)
  satisfies \(\#(0^\ast 1\cap\Sigma_b^m)=1\) for every \(m\ge 1\), so
  its normalized density is \(b^{-m}\). It therefore lies in the
  normalized exponential-sparse alternative~\textup{(i)} even though
  the raw count is constant rather than decreasing.
\end{remark}

For base-\(b\) expansions we use the standard most-significant-digit
first convention: a word \(d_1\cdots d_m\in\Sigma_b^m\) represents the
integer \(\sum_{j=1}^m d_j b^{m-j}\). For each \(m\ge 1\), let
\(\mathcal P_m^{(b)}\) be the set of base-\(b\) words of length \(m\)
whose first digit is nonzero and whose value is prime. Let
\(p_m^{(b)}:=\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(b)}}\). By the prime number theorem
\cite{HardyWright2008},
\[
  p_m^{(b)}
  =\pi(b^m-1)-\pi(b^{m-1}-1)
  \asymp \frac{b^m}{m}.
\]
The condition that the first digit be nonzero excludes leading zeros.
One may equivalently compare a DFA language against
\(\{1,\dots,b-1\}\Sigma_b^{m-1}\); in particular, the scale
\(\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(b)}}\asymp b^m/m\) is unchanged because every
prime in \([b^{m-1},b^m)\) already has base-\(b\) length \(m\).

\begin{lemma}[Prime counts in base-\(b\) slices]
  \label{lem:base-prime-count}
  Let \(X_0,C_{\pi,1},C_{\pi,2}\) be constants satisfying
  \Cref{lem:explicit-pnt-input}. There are explicit constants
  \(A_b,B_b>0\) and an explicit integer \(M_b\) such that, for all
  \(m\ge M_b\),
  \[
    A_b\,\frac{b^m}{m}
    \le \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(b)}}
    \le B_b\,\frac{b^m}{m}.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Put \(L_b:=\log b\). The threshold used below is obtained by the
  following finite algorithm. Start with
  \(M\ge2\) satisfying \(b^{M-1}\ge X_0\). Increase \(M\) until
  \(1-m/(b(m-1))\ge(1-1/b)/2\) for all \(m\ge M\), which is certified
  from the identity
  \(1-m/(b(m-1))=(b-1)/b-1/(b(m-1))\). Then increase \(M\) until
  \[
    C_{\pi,1}\left(\frac{1}{mL_b^2}
      +\frac{m}{b(m-1)^2L_b^2}\right)
      +\frac{2m}{b^m}
    \le
    \frac{1}{2L_b}
      \left(1-\frac{m}{b(m-1)}\right).
  \]
  To make the last step reproducible, note that the two rational
  functions \(1/m\) and \(m/(m-1)^2\) are decreasing for \(m\ge2\), and
  \(m/b^m\) is decreasing for \(m\ge1/\log b\). The right-hand side is
  increasing in \(m\). Thus after
  \(M_{\mathrm{mon}}:=\max\{2,\lceil1/\log b\rceil+1\}\), it is enough
  to test the displayed inequality at the candidate value \(M\); the
  finitely many smaller \(m\) between the initial lower bound and
  \(M_{\mathrm{mon}}\) are checked directly. Let \(M_b\) be the first
  candidate accepted by this procedure. This produces a reproducible
  value of \(M_b\), not an unspecified asymptotic threshold.
  The leading-digit convention gives the exact half-open interval count
  \[
    \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(b)}}=\pi(b^m-1)-\pi(b^{m-1}-1).
  \]
  Applying the interval-count convention after
  \Cref{lem:explicit-pnt-input}, the endpoint correction is the visible
  inequality
  \[
    \left|
      \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(b)}}-
      \bigl(\pi(b^m)-\pi(b^{m-1})\bigr)
    \right|\le 2,
  \]
  valid for every \(m\ge1\). Equivalently, this is
  \[
    \pi(b^m)-\pi(b^{m-1})+O(1),
  \]
  with the displayed absolute constant. For \(m\ge M_b\), the explicit
  estimate in \Cref{lem:explicit-pnt-input} gives
  \[
    \begin{aligned}
    \pi(b^m)-\pi(b^{m-1})
    \ge
    &\frac{b^m}{m\log b}
      -\frac{b^{m-1}}{(m-1)\log b}\\
    &{}-C_{\pi,1}\frac{b^m}{m^2(\log b)^2}
      -C_{\pi,1}\frac{b^{m-1}}{(m-1)^2(\log b)^2}.
    \end{aligned}
  \]
  Since
  \[
    \frac{b^m}{m\log b}
    -\frac{b^{m-1}}{(m-1)\log b}
    =
    \frac{b^m}{m\log b}
    \left(1-\frac{m}{b(m-1)}\right),
  \]
  the choice of \(M_b\), together with the endpoint \(O(1)\), gives the
  explicit lower bound
  \[
    \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(b)}}
    \ge
    \frac{1}{2\log b}
    \left(1-\frac{m}{b(m-1)}\right)\frac{b^m}{m}.
  \]
  By construction,
  \(1-m/(b(m-1))\ge (1-1/b)/2\), so one may take
  \[
    A_b:=\frac{1-1/b}{4\log b}.
  \]
  The upper bound follows from
  \[
    \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(b)}}\le\pi(b^m)
      \le C_{\pi,2}\frac{b^m}{m\log b},
  \]
  so \(B_b:=C_{\pi,2}/\log b\) is admissible for all \(m\ge M_b\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Certified spectral decay for a fixed residue class]
  \label{lem:effective-spectral-constants}
  Let \(P\) be a rational stochastic matrix. Suppose that the recurrent
  classes and their cyclic decompositions have been computed, and let
  \(D_A\) be a common multiple of the recurrent periods. For each
  residue class \(r\bmod D_A\) there is an algorithm which outputs
  rational numbers \(0<\tau_r<\theta_r<1\), \(K_r>0\), and an integer
  \(N_r\) such that
  \[
    \|P^m-S_r\|_\infty\le K_r\theta_r^m
    \qquad(m\equiv r\bmod D_A,\ m\ge N_r),
  \]
  where \(S_r=\lim_{k\to\infty}P^{kD_A+r}\). The output includes a
  finite certificate consisting of the strict inequalities
  \[
    \rho(T)<\tau_r,\qquad
    \rho(Q_{j,t}-\Pi_{j,t})<\tau_r^{d_j},
  \]
  where \(T=P_{TT}\), \(Q_{j,t}=P^{d_j}|_{R_{j,t}}\) is the primitive
  return chain on the cyclic class \(R_{j,t}\), and
  \(\Pi_{j,t}=\mathbf 1\pi_{j,t}\), plus rational resolvent bounds. The
  proof object also records, for each residue, an integer polynomial
  degree \(J_r\), a monotonicity threshold for the inequality
  \(C_rm^{J_r}\tau_r^m\le K_r\theta_r^m\), and the finite list of exact
  rational matrix inequalities checked below that threshold. We assert
  computability of these data; no polynomial bit-complexity or
  output-sensitive complexity bound is claimed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Put \(P\) in canonical Markov-chain block form. The decaying blocks
  are the transient block \(T=P_{TT}\) and, for each recurrent cyclic
  class \(R_{j,t}\), the return-error block
  \(E_{j,t}:=Q_{j,t}-\Pi_{j,t}\). The block \(T\) has spectral radius
  \(<1\), and each \(E_{j,t}\) has spectral radius \(<1\) because
  \(Q_{j,t}\) is primitive stochastic.

  Compute the characteristic polynomials of these rational blocks and
  isolate their complex roots as algebraic numbers. If the finite list
  of roots is empty, take \(\tau_r=1/3\) and \(\theta_r=1/2\). Otherwise
  choose a rational \(\tau_r\in(0,1)\) such that all eigenvalues of
  \(T\) have modulus \(<\tau_r\) and all eigenvalues of every
  \(E_{j,t}\) have modulus \(<\tau_r^{d_j}\); then choose any rational
  \(\theta_r\) with \(\tau_r<\theta_r<1\). Each strict comparison is
  certified by rational isolating rectangles whose squared moduli lie
  below the corresponding rational threshold. This gives the
  spectral-radius part of the certificate, including the case in which
  there are no non-peripheral roots to control.

  It remains to turn spectral separation into a bound on powers without
  invoking a Jordan basis. Let \(A\) be any one of the rational blocks
  and let \(\eta\in(0,1)\) be the corresponding block rate
  (\(\eta=\tau_r\) for \(T\), and
  \(\eta=\tau_r^{d_j}\) for \(E_{j,t}\)). Choose a rational
  \(\gamma\) with \(\rho(A)<\gamma<\eta\). From the root isolating
  rectangles we compute a rational \(\delta>0\) such that
  \[
    |\det(zI-A)|\ge\delta\qquad(|z|=\gamma).
  \]
  Indeed, if \(R_\xi\) is the isolating rectangle for a root \(\xi\),
  exact rational interval arithmetic gives a positive rational lower
  bound \(\Delta_\xi\) for
  \(\inf\{|z-w|:|z|=\gamma,\ w\in R_\xi\}\); positivity follows from
  \(\rho(A)<\gamma\) and from the disjoint certified rectangles. The
  characteristic polynomial is monic, so the
  product \(\prod_\xi\Delta_\xi\) is a valid determinant lower bound,
  with one factor for each root counted with algebraic multiplicity. The
  adjugate entries of
  \(zI-A\) are polynomials in \(z\), so their absolute values on the
  same circle have an elementary rational upper bound \(U\) obtained
  from the coefficients of \(A\) and the identity \(|z|=\gamma\).
  Cauchy's formula
  for the matrix resolvent gives
  \[
    A^m=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=\gamma}z^m(zI-A)^{-1}\,dz,
    \qquad
    \|A^m\|_\infty\le L_A\gamma^m\le L_A\eta^m,
  \]
  where \(L_A\) is a rational number computed from \(U,\delta,\gamma\),
  and the dimension of \(A\). The data
  \((\gamma,\delta,U,L_A)\) are the resolvent certificate for \(A\).

  We now spell out the finite block normal form used by the verifier.
  In the canonical order \(T,R_1,\ldots,R_s\), and for
  \(m\equiv r\bmod D_A\), the only non-zero blocks of \(P^m-S_r\) are
  of the following types.
  \[
    T^m,\qquad C_j^m-C_{j,r}^{\infty},
  \]
  on the diagonal, and
  \[
    \sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}
      T^\ell P_{T,R_j}\bigl(C_j^{m-1-\ell}-C_{j,r-\ell-1}^{\infty}\bigr)
      -
    \sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}
      T^\ell P_{T,R_j}C_{j,r-\ell-1}^{\infty}
  \]
  in the transient-to-\(R_j\) block. Here \(C_{j,a}^{\infty}\) is the
  rational limit of \(C_j^n\) along \(n\equiv a\bmod d_j\); explicitly
  its rows supported on \(R_{j,t}\) are equal to
  \(\pi_{j,t+a}\) on \(R_{j,t+a}\) and zero elsewhere. For each
  \(a\bmod d_j\) let
  \(G_{j,u}:=P_{R_{j,u},R_{j,u+1}}\) and
  \(W_{j,t,a}:=G_{j,t}G_{j,t+1}\cdots G_{j,t+a-1}\), with
  \(W_{j,t,0}\) the identity on \(R_{j,t}\). The certificate expands
  \(C_j^{d_jh+a}-C_{j,a}^{\infty}\), for \(h\ge1\), as the finite
  rational sum
  \[
    \sum_{t=0}^{d_j-1}
      M_{j,t,a}(h),
  \]
  where \(M_{j,t,a}(h)\) is the full \(R_j\times R_j\) matrix whose only
  non-zero block is the block from rows \(R_{j,t}\) to columns
  \(R_{j,t+a}\), equal to \(W_{j,t,a}E_{j,t+a}^{h}\). The case \(h=0\)
  is one of the finitely many initial powers checked directly. The
  verifier checks the identity for \(0\le a<d_j\) by multiplying the
  listed rational cyclic blocks and using
  \(Q_{j,t+a}^{h}-\Pi_{j,t+a}=E_{j,t+a}^{h}\) for \(h\ge1\). Thus every
  summand is a listed rational connector product containing either
  \(T^n\), one \(E_{j,t}^n\), or one convolution of such powers.

  The resolvent bounds above dominate a listed direct product
  \(X A^n Y\) by
  \(\|X\|_\infty L_A\|Y\|_\infty\eta^n\). A listed finite convolution is
  first split by writing \(\ell=u+D_Aq\). If
  \(m-1-u=d_jH_{m,u}+a\), with \(0\le a<d_j\), then its recurrent
  return-error exponent is \(H_{m,u}-L_jq\), where
  \(L_j=D_A/d_j\). Thus a residue summand has the form
  \[
    \sum_q X_uT^{D_Aq}Y_uE_{j,t_u}^{H_{m,u}-L_jq}Z_u,
  \]
  with the terms having non-positive recurrent exponent placed in the
  finite direct-check list. For the remaining terms the product of the
  two exponential bounds, with the finite \(T^u\) factor already included
  in the rational connector \(X_u\), is
  \[
    \tau_r^{D_Aq}\tau_r^{d_jH_{m,u}-D_Aq}
      =\tau_r^{m-1-u-a},
  \]
  so the \(q\)-dependence cancels. Since there are at most \(m\) terms
  and only finitely many residues \(u,a\), exact rational norm bounds
  give a listed inequality \(C\,m\tau_r^m\) after absorbing
  \(\tau_r^{-1-u-a}\) into \(C\). The infinite tail above is bounded by the
  listed geometric estimate
  \[
    \sum_{\ell=m}^{\infty}
      \|T^\ell\|_\infty\|P_{T,R_j}\|_\infty
      \|C_{j,r-\ell-1}^{\infty}\|_\infty
    \le
    \frac{L_T\|P_{T,R_j}\|_\infty}{1-\tau_r}\tau_r^m .
  \]
  Adding the finitely many rational coefficients gives explicit
  \(C_r>0\) and \(J_r\le1\) with
  \[
    \|P^m-S_r\|_\infty\le C_rm^{J_r}\tau_r^m
    \qquad(m\equiv r\bmod D_A),
  \]
  after increasing \(C_r\) to absorb the finitely many omitted small
  exponents and the finite factors
  \(\max_{0\le u<D_A,\ 0\le a<d_j}\tau_r^{-1-u-a}\). This is the only
  polynomial loss retained in the certificate: the block and convolution
  estimates are first made at the strict rate \(\tau_r\), and the final
  advertised rate \(\theta_r\) is introduced only after choosing
  \(\theta_r\in(\tau_r,1)\). Since \(\tau_r/\theta_r<1\), the rational
  sequence \(m^{J_r}(\tau_r/\theta_r)^m\) is eventually decreasing and
  tends to \(0\). A finite rational search therefore produces \(K_r\)
  and \(N_r\) such that
  \[
    C_rm^{J_r}\tau_r^m\le K_r\theta_r^m
    \qquad(m\ge N_r).
  \]
  The certificate records both rates, namely the block rate \(\tau_r\)
  and the final output rate \(\theta_r\), together with
  \(C_r,J_r,K_r,N_r\) and the explicit monotone-tail threshold. The
  finitely many powers in the
  residue class below that threshold are checked directly by rational
  matrix powering and are included as exact inequalities; if necessary,
  \(K_r\) is enlarged before the certificate is printed. Thus the
  certificate verifies the displayed inequality for all
  \(m\equiv r\bmod D_A\) with \(m\ge N_r\).

  Thus the verifier never appeals to an unnamed finite-chain formula.
  Its normal form for each entry of \(P^m-S_r\) is the explicit finite
  list consisting of the direct transient powers \(T^m\), the recurrent
  return-error terms \(W_{j,t,a}E_{j,t+a}^h\), the residue-grouped
  convolutions just displayed, and the geometric resolvent tails
  \((I-T^{D_A})^{-1}T^u\). Each listed matrix is rational once the
  stationary vectors and cyclic projections have been solved over
  \(\QQ\), and each inequality is reduced to rational norm bounds plus
  the certified root-isolation comparisons above.
\end{proof}
