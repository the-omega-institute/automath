\section{Global rigidity from paired-loop covers}
\label{sec:slender-cobham}

The preceding argument extracts one synchronized orbit from an arbitrary
infinite MCFL. Slender context-free languages provide more: their entire
language is covered by finitely many paired loops. Combining that global
cover with the same affine recurrence evaluation yields a two-system
rigidity theorem.

We record the two external inputs with their hypotheses. A language \(L\)
is \emph{slender} when
\(\sup_n\#(L\cap A^n)<\infty\). In the terminology of
Ilie--Rozenberg--Salomaa, this is \(0\)-poly-slender, and a \(1\)-Dyck loop
is a language
\[
  \{u v^n w x^n y:n\ge0\}.
\]
Their Theorem~7 states that a context-free language is \(0\)-poly-slender
if and only if it is a finite union of \(1\)-Dyck loops
\cite[Theorem~7]{IlieRozenbergSalomaa2000}. The characterization was first
proved, in the equivalent terminology of iterative or semidiscrete
context-free languages, by Latteux--Thierrin
\cite{LatteuxThierrin1983}, and independently by Ilie
\cite{Ilie1994}.

Mignotte's common-value theorem concerns two rational-integer linear
recurrence sequences. We use the following precise dominant-root form. The
input consists of ordinary recurrences indexed from (0), together with
expansions
\[
  A_n=a\lambda^n+\sum_{i=2}^s P_i(n)\lambda_i^n,
  \qquad
  B_m=b\kappa^m+\sum_{j=2}^t Q_j(m)\kappa_j^m,
\]
where \(a,b\ne0\), the characteristic roots and coefficients are
algebraic, \(\lambda,\kappa>1\), and
\(
  \abs{\lambda_i}<\lambda,
  \abs{\kappa_j}<\kappa
\)
for all remaining characteristic roots. If \(\lambda\) and \(\kappa\)
are multiplicatively independent, Mignotte's proof effectively produces an
integer \(M(A,B)\) such that
\begin{equation}
  A_n=B_m,\quad n\ge M(A,B)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  a\lambda^n=b\kappa^m.
  \label{eq:mignotte-effective-threshold}
\end{equation}
The same assertion with the sequences interchanged produces \(M(B,A)\).
In particular \(A_n=B_m\) has only finitely many nonnegative integer
solutions \cite{Mignotte1978Intersection}; Bennett--Pint\'er give an
accessible published restatement of the integral-recurrence,
unique-dominant-root hypotheses \cite{BennettPinter2015}. The qualitative
theorem below uses only finiteness. The effective refinement retains the two
thresholds in \eqref{eq:mignotte-effective-threshold}.

The next lemma supplies the hypothesis that is not a consequence merely of
the root-growth squeeze.

\begin{lemma}[Dominant-root transfer for a paired greedy loop]
  \label{lem:paired-loop-dominant-root-transfer}
  Let \(U=(U_n)_{n\ge0}\) be a strictly increasing standard greedy linear
  numeration sequence. Suppose that its minimal tail recurrence
  polynomial is the minimal polynomial \(\mu\) of a weak Perron number
  \(\alpha>1\). If
  \[
    z_n=u v^n w x^n y\in\mathcal R_U\qquad(n\ge0)
  \]
  and \(D=\abs v+\abs x>0\), then there is \(h\ge1\) such that, for every
  \(0\le r<h\), the integer sequence
  \[
    Z_r(t)=\val_U(z_{ht+r})\qquad(t\ge0)
  \]
  has an eventual linear recurrence whose minimal tail recurrence has the
  unique dominating characteristic root
  \[
    \alpha^{hD}.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  We compute the affine matrices. Let \(C\) be the companion matrix of
  \(\mu\). For a block \(b=b_1\cdots b_\ell\), multiplication of the digit
  matrices in \Cref{lem:affine-recurrence-block-action} gives
  \begin{equation}
    M_b=
    \begin{pmatrix}1&q_b\\0&C^\ell\end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:paired-loop-affine-block-form}
  \end{equation}
  for a row \(q_b\), with \(M_\varepsilon=I\). Because \(\mu\) is
  irreducible over characteristic zero, it is separable, so \(C\) is
  diagonalizable over a splitting field. Hence \(C^\ell\) is
  diagonalizable even when distinct conjugates acquire the same
  \(\ell\)-th power. The eigenvalues of \(M_b\) are \(1\) and
  \(\gamma^\ell\), where \(\gamma\) runs over the conjugates of \(\alpha\).
  If \(b\ne\varepsilon\), every peripheral eigenvalue has modulus
  \(\alpha^\ell>1\), so it cannot collide with the affine eigenvalue \(1\).
  Equation \eqref{eq:paired-loop-affine-block-form} then shows that its
  peripheral generalized eigenspace is semisimple. Thus the affine row
  creates neither a peripheral eigenvalue nor a peripheral Jordan block.

  Discarding finitely many initial parameters puts the reading position
  beyond the recurrence transient. If \(v\ne\varepsilon\), the represented
  value is then a fixed scalar functional of
  \[
    M_x^n A M_v^n
  \]
  for one fixed matrix \(A\); if \(v=\varepsilon\), it is a fixed scalar
  functional of \(M_x^n A\). This follows directly by reading the five
  fixed blocks in LSD-first order; fixed initial powers used to cross the
  transient are absorbed into \(A\). Vectorization in the first case gives
  the actual powering matrix
  \[
    K=M_v^{\mathsf T}\otimes M_x,
  \]
  with the evident one-factor interpretation when one pumped block is
  empty. Its spectral radius is \(\alpha^D\). Its peripheral eigenvalues
  are exactly the products
  \(
    \gamma_1^{\abs v}\gamma_2^{\abs x}
  \)
  obtained from peripheral conjugates in the nonempty factors, and their
  generalized eigenspaces are semisimple by the preceding paragraph and
  the tensor-product Jordan formula.

  Choose \(h\) from the weak-Perron power characterization, so that
  \[
    \abs\gamma=\alpha\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \gamma^h=\alpha^h
  \]
  for every conjugate \(\gamma\). Every peripheral eigenvalue of \(K^h\) is then
  the same positive number
  \(
    \Lambda=\alpha^{hD}
  \), and the \(\Lambda\)-generalized eigenspace is still semisimple.
  All other eigenvalues of \(K^h\) have modulus strictly below
  \(\Lambda\). Cayley--Hamilton applied to the displayed scalar functional
  therefore gives, for each residue \(r\),
  \begin{equation}
    Z_r(t)=c_r\Lambda^t+
      \sum_j P_{r,j}(t)\xi_{r,j}^t,
    \qquad \abs{\xi_{r,j}}<\Lambda.
    \label{eq:paired-loop-spectral-expansion}
  \end{equation}
  This proves at matrix level that peripheral collisions coalesce to one
  algebraic root and that no polynomial factor in \(t\) can accompany it.

  It remains to rule out cancellation by the value functional. The
  word \(z_{ht+r}\) has length \(L_r+hDt\) for a fixed \(L_r\). Greedy
  validity gives
  \[
    U_{L_r+hDt-1}\le Z_r(t)<U_{L_r+hDt}.
  \]
  The positive residue asymptotics
  \eqref{eq:weak-perron-residue-asymptotic}--
  \eqref{eq:weak-perron-positive-residues}, applied to these two fixed
  index residues for the chosen period, show
  \[
    0<\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{Z_r(t)}{\Lambda^t}
      \le
      \limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{Z_r(t)}{\Lambda^t}<\infty.
  \]
  In \eqref{eq:paired-loop-spectral-expansion} every lower-modulus term,
  including all its possible Jordan polynomials, is \(o(\Lambda^t)\).
  Consequently the spectral-projection coefficient \(c_r\) of the actual
  affine value functional is nonzero (indeed positive). Total peripheral
  cancellation is therefore impossible, and \(\Lambda\) survives as the
  unique dominating root of the least scalar recurrence.
\end{proof}

For \(X\subseteq\NN_{\ge1}\), write
\[
  \operatorname{Rep}_U(X)
    =\{z\in\mathcal R_U:\val_U(z)\in X\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Slender-context-free Cobham theorem]
  \label{thm:slender-cfl-cobham}
  Let \(U\) and \(V\) be strictly increasing standard greedy linear
  numeration sequences whose minimal tail recurrence polynomials are the
  minimal polynomials of weak Perron numbers \(\alpha>1\) and \(\beta>1\),
  respectively. Suppose that \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are multiplicatively
  independent. If, for one set \(X\subseteq\NN_{\ge1}\), both full
  representation languages
  \[
    \operatorname{Rep}_U(X)
    \quad\hbox{and}\quad
    \operatorname{Rep}_V(X)
  \]
  are slender context-free languages, then \(X\) is finite.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Suppose that \(X\) is infinite. The paired-loop classification gives
  finite covers
  \[
    \begin{aligned}
      \operatorname{Rep}_U(X)
        &=\bigcup_{i=1}^a
          \{u_i v_i^n w_i x_i^n y_i:n\ge0\},\\
      \operatorname{Rep}_V(X)
        &=\bigcup_{j=1}^b
          \{u'_j(v'_j)^m w'_j(x'_j)^m y'_j:m\ge0\}.
    \end{aligned}
  \]
  Loops with zero total pumped length are singletons and may be removed;
  they cover only finitely many values. Apply
  \Cref{lem:paired-loop-dominant-root-transfer} to every remaining loop.
  After the finitely many residue restrictions, \(X\) is covered in each
  system by finitely many integer recurrence sequences having respective
  unique dominating roots
  \[
    \alpha^{h_i(\abs{v_i}+\abs{x_i})}
    \quad\hbox{and}\quad
    \beta^{k_j(\abs{v'_j}+\abs{x'_j})}.
  \]
  Infinitely many elements of \(X\) must lie in the intersection of the
  images of one sequence from the first finite family and one from the
  second. Their dominating roots are multiplicatively independent, since
  they are positive powers of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\). Mignotte's theorem
  says that the equality of their terms has only finitely many index pairs,
  a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Effective refinement}

We now retain the finite data suppressed in the preceding proof. This is an
effectivity statement from complete presentations, not a uniform estimate in
a few size parameters.

\begin{definition}[Effective two-system presentation]
  \label{def:effective-two-system-presentation}
  An \emph{effective two-system presentation} consists of effective
  presentations of \(U\) and \(V\), in the sense defined before
  \Cref{cor:bounded-outside-support-semidecidable}, subject to the
  hypotheses of \Cref{thm:slender-cfl-cobham}, together with:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item isolating data and a certificate that each minimal tail recurrence
      polynomial is the minimal polynomial of the relevant weak Perron root;
    \item for each full representation language, either a context-free
      grammar that generates that slender language or a finite cover by words
      \(
        u v^n w x^n y
      \)
      with \(n\ge0\). In the grammar case, the constructive form of the
      slender-language theorem computes such a cover from the grammar
      \cite{LatteuxThierrin1983,Ilie1994}; the resulting paired-loop form is
      the one recorded in \cite[Theorem~7]{IlieRozenbergSalomaa2000}.
  \end{enumerate}
  The grammars or covers represent exactly \(\operatorname{Rep}_U(X)\) and
  \(\operatorname{Rep}_V(X)\). Algebraic numbers are given by minimal
  polynomials and isolating data, so algebraic arithmetic, modulus
  comparison, and root-of-unity tests are effective.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Effective dominant-root transfer]
  \label{lem:effective-paired-loop-transfer}
  From the effective recurrence data for \(U\) and one nonconstant valid
  paired loop
  \[
    z_n=u v^n w x^n y,\qquad D=\abs v+\abs x>0,
  \]
  one can compute an integer \(h\ge1\) and, for every \(0\le r<h\), the
  following finite package:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
    \item an individual shift \(s_r\ge0\);
    \item integer recurrence coefficients and enough integer initial values
      to define, from index \(0\), the sequence
      \[
        A_r(t)=\val_U(z_{h(t+s_r)+r})\qquad(t\ge0);
      \]
    \item its unique positive dominant root
      \(\Lambda=\alpha^{hD}\), its algebraic dominant coefficient
      \(c_r>0\), and computable algebraic numbers
      \(C_r>0\) and \(1\le\rho_r<\Lambda\) such that
      \begin{equation}
        \abs{A_r(t)-c_r\Lambda^t}\le C_r\rho_r^t
        \qquad(t\ge0).
        \label{eq:effective-loop-error}
      \end{equation}
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  All constructions occur in a computable number field containing the roots
  of the supplied minimal polynomial. Compute its conjugates on the circle
  of radius \(\alpha\), test the quotients of peripheral conjugates for being
  roots of unity, and take for \(h\) a common multiple of their orders. This
  is the effective version of the power choice in
  \Cref{lem:paired-loop-dominant-root-transfer}.

  Handle the supplied recurrence threshold for each loop. If
  \(v\ne\varepsilon\), compute how many initial copies of \(v\) are
  needed for the remaining repeated action to start in the stationary
  recurrence range and absorb those copies into the fixed prefix. If
  \(v=\varepsilon\), then \(x\ne\varepsilon\), and do the same with the
  first copies of \(x\). For each residue \(r\), increase the parameter to
  the least resulting value congruent to \(r\pmod h\); this gives the
  recorded, loop- and residue-dependent shift \(s_r\). Every excluded
  parameter is thereby explicit and finite.

  Form the integral affine block matrices from
  \eqref{eq:paired-loop-affine-block-form} and the actual powering matrix
  \(M_v^{\mathsf T}\otimes M_x\), with the one-factor version when a pumped
  block is empty. Applying Cayley--Hamilton to the scalar value functional
  gives integer recurrence coefficients and a computable order; direct
  evaluation gives that many initial values. Exact spectral decomposition
  of its \(h\)-th power gives the coefficient \(c_r\) and all remaining
  algebraic roots and Jordan polynomials. The proof of
  \Cref{lem:paired-loop-dominant-root-transfer} shows that \(c_r\ne0\): the
  greedy length squeeze uses the supplied recurrence threshold and the two
  fixed index residues belonging to this particular loop. Since the values
  are positive, the coefficient is positive.

  Finally compute a real algebraic \(\rho_r\) strictly between the largest
  modulus of a remaining root and \(\Lambda\). The coefficients and degrees
  of the finitely many Jordan polynomials are explicit. Bounding
  \(t^e q^t\) for each \(q<1\), and enlarging the constant to cover the
  finitely many small indices, effectively produces \(C_r\) in
  \eqref{eq:effective-loop-error}. Thus the output is an ordinary recurrence
  beginning at \(0\), not an eventual recurrence with an unrecorded prefix.
\end{proof}

For the next statement, apply the lemma to all nonconstant loops. Index the
resulting recurrence sequences by finite sets \(P\) and \(Q\): write
\[
  A_p(t)=c_p\Lambda_p^t+O(\rho_p^t),\qquad
  B_q(m)=d_qK_q^m+O(\sigma_q^m),
  \quad p\in P,\ q\in Q,
\]
with every coefficient and error bound supplied by the effective packages.
Let \(E_U\) and \(E_V\) be the finite sets of values represented by all
zero-pump loops and by every parameter discarded in forming these packages,
and put \(E=E_U\cup E_V\). These sets are computed by direct evaluation.

\begin{theorem}[Effective cardinality bound]
  \label{thm:effective-slender-cfl-cardinality}
  From an effective two-system presentation for \(X\), one can compute an
  upper bound for \(\abs X\). More precisely, set
  \[
    M_{pq}=M(A_p,B_q),\qquad N_{pq}=M(B_q,A_p).
  \]
  Then the integer
  \begin{equation}
    \mathcal B=\abs E+
      \sum_{p\in P}\sum_{q\in Q}(M_{pq}N_{pq}+1)
    \label{eq:effective-global-cardinality-bound}
  \end{equation}
  is computable from the presentation and satisfies \(\abs X\le\mathcal B\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Fix \(p\in P\) and \(q\in Q\). Their dominant roots are positive powers
  of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), respectively, and hence are
  multiplicatively independent. The effective Mignotte thresholds in
  \eqref{eq:mignotte-effective-threshold}, applied in both orders, imply that
  every solution of \(A_p(t)=B_q(m)\) outside
  \[
    0\le t<M_{pq},\qquad 0\le m<N_{pq}
  \]
  satisfies
  \begin{equation}
    c_p\Lambda_p^t=d_qK_q^m.
    \label{eq:effective-leading-term-equality}
  \end{equation}
  Equation \eqref{eq:effective-leading-term-equality} has at most one
  nonnegative integer solution: two distinct solutions would give
  \(
    \Lambda_p^{t_1-t_2}=K_q^{m_1-m_2}
  \), contrary to multiplicative independence. The finite rectangle contains
  at most \(M_{pq}N_{pq}\) pairs. Therefore
  \[
    \#\{(t,m):A_p(t)=B_q(m)\}\le M_{pq}N_{pq}+1.
  \]

  If \(x\in X\setminus E\), its unique \(U\)-representation lies in the
  retained tail of some \(U\)-loop and its unique \(V\)-representation lies
  in the retained tail of some \(V\)-loop. Thus \(x=A_p(t)=B_q(m)\) for some
  \(p,q,t,m\). Summing the pair bounds and adding \(E\) gives
  \eqref{eq:effective-global-cardinality-bound}. Repeated values or overlap
  among loops are only overcounted.
\end{proof}

The input to \(\mathcal B\) is the full recurrence and loop data: it includes
the individual recurrence thresholds and initial values, the actual fixed
and pumped words, the affine and tensor matrices, the algebraic dominant
coefficients, spectral gaps, and Mignotte's effective constants. We do not
extract a closed expression in grammar size, recurrence heights, or block
lengths. In particular, no uniformity is being inferred from the finitely
many prefixes: each discarded prefix and each shift \(s_r\) is computed and
entered in \(E\).

\begin{remark}[Cobham comparison]
  \label{rem:slender-cobham-priority}
  In regular integer bases, Albayrak--Bell's quantitative Cobham--Semenov
  theorem contains the slender specialization above and extends to sparse
  automatic tuple sets of polynomial growth
  \cite[Theorems~1.1 and 4.1]{AlbayrakBell2023}. Their result is stronger on
  this overlap: its bound is an explicit expression in the bases, dimension,
  and minimal-automaton sizes, whereas \Cref{thm:effective-slender-cfl-cardinality}
  computes a bound from complete recurrence and paired-loop data.

  For a nonregular slender context-free language, the paired-loop cover
  produces recurrence subsequences with one positive dominant root in a
  weak-Perron system. The regular
  Perron-substitutive and genealogically ordered abstract-numeration cases
  already belong to the theories of Durand and Charlier--Leroy--Rigo
  \cite[Theorem~1]{Durand2011CobhamSubstitutions}%
  \cite[Theorem~49]{CharlierLeroyRigo2013CobhamANS}. Genuinely nonregular
  poly-slender languages would require a multivariate extension of the
  paired-loop argument and lie outside \Cref{thm:slender-cfl-cobham}.
\end{remark}
