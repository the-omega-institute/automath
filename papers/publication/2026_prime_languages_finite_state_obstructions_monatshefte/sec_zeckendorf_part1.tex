\section{Context: Zeckendorf Growth}
\label{sec:zeckendorf}

This section specialises the density dichotomy to Zeckendorf words. We
record only the minimal classical facts needed later---in particular the
Zeckendorf growth law---and then combine them with prime-counting
estimates to obtain the quantitative obstructions promised in
\suppref{Section~1}.

Let
\[
  \mathcal Z
  :=\{x_1\cdots x_m\in\{0,1\}^\ast:
      x_ix_{i+1}\neq 11,\ x_m=1\}
\]
be the language of canonical Zeckendorf words. We write these words
least-significant digit first: for \(w=x_1\cdots x_m\in\mathcal Z\),
\[
  \val(w):=\sum_{k=1}^m x_kF_{k+1},
\]
where \(F_0=0\), \(F_1=1\), \(F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n\), and
\(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). Thus \(x_m=1\) marks the most significant
nonzero digit. The map \(w\mapsto\val(w)\) is a bijection from
\(\mathcal Z\) onto \(\NN_{\ge 1}\) \cite{Zeckendorf1972}.
Consequently \(\mathcal Z\cap\{0,1\}^{\le m}\) parametrises the
interval \([1,F_{m+2})\), so words of length exactly \(m\) parametrise
\([F_{m+1},F_{m+2})\). Equivalently,
\(\abs{\mathcal Z_m}=F_m\) for every \(m\ge 1\).
We write
\[
  \mathcal Z_m:=\{w\in\mathcal Z:\abs{w}=m\}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}
  :=\{w\in\mathcal Z_m:\val(w)\text{ is prime}\},
\]
and set
\[
  \mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}
  :=\bigcup_{m\ge 1}\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Length growth for regular Zeckendorf sublanguages]
  \label{lem:zeckendorf-length-growth}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) be regular. Choose a DFA recognizing
  \(L\), delete states that are not reachable from the initial state or
  cannot reach an accepting state, and let \(\rho\) be the maximal
  spectral radius of a strongly connected component of the resulting
  automaton. Then \(\rho\le\varphi\). Moreover,
  \[
    \#\{w\in L:\abs w\le m\}=
    \begin{cases}
      \rho^{m+o(m)}, & \rho>1,\\
      \exp(o(m)), & \rho\le1.
    \end{cases}
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Since \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\), the number of words of length at most
  \(m\) in \(L\) is bounded above by
  \[
    \#(\mathcal Z\cap\{0,1\}^{\le m})=F_{m+2}-1=\varphi^{m+o(m)}.
  \]
  For any trimmed strongly connected component \(C\) of spectral radius
  \(\rho_C\), choose entry and exit paths through the trimmed automaton.
  Periodic Perron--Frobenius theory gives a residue class of lengths on
  which the number of paths that traverse \(C\) is
  \(\rho_C^{n+o(n)}\). These paths yield distinct accepted words after
  adding the fixed entry and exit words. The preceding ambient upper
  bound therefore forces \(\rho_C\le\varphi\) for every component, and
  hence \(\rho\le\varphi\).

  Put the trimmed adjacency matrix in block upper-triangular form by
  strongly connected components. The upper bound
  \(O(m^N\rho^m)\), with \(N\) depending only on the number of blocks,
  follows from the usual path decomposition through this acyclic block
  graph and standard finite-dimensional matrix-power estimates for the
  diagonal blocks. If
  \(\rho>1\), choose a reachable and co-reachable component of spectral
  radius \(\rho\); the same periodic Perron--Frobenius lower bound along
  one admissible residue class gives \(\rho^{m+o(m)}\) words of length at
  most \(m\). If \(\rho\le1\), every non-negative integral irreducible
  recurrent block has spectral radius at most \(1\), hence is either
  nilpotent in the acyclic part or a permutation block on a directed
  cycle. A path can pass through only finitely many such blocks, so the
  total number of accepted words of length at most \(m\) is polynomial in
  \(m\), and therefore \(\exp(o(m))\). This proves the displayed
  estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Growth law for regular Zeckendorf languages]
  \label{prop:zeckendorf-regular-powerlaw}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) be regular. Choose a DFA recognizing
  \(L\), delete states that are not reachable from the initial state or
  cannot reach an accepting state, and let \(\rho\) be the maximal
  spectral radius of a strongly connected component of the resulting
  automaton. Define
  \[
    N_L(T):=\#\{w\in L:\val(w)\le T\}.
  \]
  If \(L=\varnothing\), then \(N_L(T)=0\) for all \(T\). Otherwise
  \[
    N_L(T)=T^{\alpha+o(1)},
    \qquad
    \alpha=
    \begin{cases}
      0, & \rho\le 1,\\[0.3em]
      \dfrac{\log \rho}{\log \varphi}, & \rho>1.
    \end{cases}
    \qquad (T\to\infty).
  \]
  In the endpoint case \(\rho\le1\) this assertion means only
  \(N_L(T)=T^{o(1)}\); no sharper polynomial asymptotic is asserted.
  This transfer-matrix statement uses the LSD-first Zeckendorf convention
  fixed at the start of the section.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We use \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-length-growth}. The general
  \(S\)-recognizable-set theorem of
  \cite[Theorem~7]{CharlierRampersad2011} gives the same conclusion. In
  the present system the ambient count satisfies
  \[
    \#(\mathcal Z\cap\{0,1\}^{\le m})
    =F_{m+2}-1
    =\varphi^{m+o(m)},
  \]
  and, conversely, \(F_{m+2}=\varphi^{m+O(1)}\). Hence
  \(\val(w)\le T\) is equivalent, up to an \(O(1)\) error in length, to
  \(\abs w\le (\log T)/(\log\varphi)\).

  By \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-length-growth},
  the number of accepted words of length at most \(m\) is
  \(\rho^{m+o(m)}\) if \(\rho>1\), and \(\exp(o(m))\) if \(\rho\le1\).
  Substituting \(m=(\log T)/(\log\varphi)+O(1)\) gives exactly
  \(N_L(T)=T^{\alpha+o(1)}\), with \(\alpha=0\) for \(\rho\le1\) and
  \(\alpha=\log\rho/\log\varphi\) for \(\rho>1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:zeckendorf-examples}
  The endpoint cases and the borderline case \(\rho=1\) already occur
  for simple regular sublanguages.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item For \(L=\mathcal Z\), one has \(N_L(T)=\lfloor T\rfloor\),
    \(\rho=\varphi\), and \(\alpha=1\).
    \item If \(L\) is finite, then \(N_L(T)\) is eventually constant,
    so \(\alpha=0\).
    \item For \(L=0^\ast1\subseteq\mathcal Z\), one has \(a_m=1\) for
    every \(m\), hence \(\rho=1\) and
    \(N_L(T)\asymp \log T=T^{o(1)}\).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  The same transfer-matrix argument applies more generally to Parry
  numeration systems whose admissible digit language is regular. In that
  setting the regular syntax of admissible expansions replaces
  \(\mathcal Z\), and the dominant exponential growth constant of the
  numeration system replaces \(\varphi\); see
  \cite{Frougny1992,BruyereHansel1997,LecomteRigo2001,Rigo2014Vol1,Rigo2014Vol2}.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Prime counts in Zeckendorf slices]
  \label{lem:zeck-prime-count}
  Take the explicit prime-counting constants
  \(X_0,C_{\pi,1},C_{\pi,2}\) from \suppref{Lemma~1.3}. There are constants
  \(A_{\mathrm Z},B_{\mathrm Z}>0\) and an explicit integer \(M_{\mathrm Z}\)
  such that for all \(m\ge M_{\mathrm Z}\),
  \[
    A_{\mathrm Z}\,\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
    \le \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    \le B_{\mathrm Z}\,\frac{\varphi^m}{m}.
  \]
  Equivalently, for all sufficiently large \(m\), the prime count in
  the exact Zeckendorf slice \([F_{m+1},F_{m+2})\) is between
  \(c_1F_m/m\) and \(c_2F_m/m\) for suitable constants
  \(c_1,c_2>0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Binet's formula gives
  \[
    F_n=\frac{\varphi^n-(-\varphi)^{-n}}{\sqrt5}.
  \]
  For every \(n\ge2\),
  \[
    \frac{\varphi^n-1}{\sqrt5}\le F_n\le
    \frac{\varphi^n+1}{\sqrt5}\le \varphi^n.
  \]
  Hence, for \(j=1,2\) and \(m\ge1\),
  \[
    c_F\varphi^m\le F_{m+j}\le C_F\varphi^m,\qquad
    c_F:=\frac{\varphi^2-1}{\sqrt5\varphi},\quad
    C_F:=\varphi^2.
  \]
  Define
  \[
    c_{\log}:=\frac12\log\varphi,\qquad C_{\log}:=3\log\varphi,
  \]
  and let \(M_{\log}\) be produced by the following finite calculation.
  Increase \(M\ge2\) until, for all \(m\ge M\) and \(j=1,2\),
  \[
    c_{\log}m\le \log F_{m+j}\le C_{\log}m.
  \]
  This is reproducible from the preceding Binet bounds: the two
  displayed inequalities are guaranteed once the linear inequalities
  \(\log c_F+m\log\varphi\ge c_{\log}m\) and
  \(\log C_F+m\log\varphi\le C_{\log}m\) hold, so it is enough to test
  the resulting explicit lower bounds for \(m\).

  Choose \(M_R\ge M_{\log}\) by the following explicit Binet
  majorization. No monotonicity input is needed for the exact sequence
  \[
    R_m
    :=
    \frac{F_{m+1}}{F_{m+2}}\,
    \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+1}}.
  \]
  Write
  \[
    F_n=\frac{\varphi^n}{\sqrt5}(1-\varepsilon_n),
    \qquad
    \varepsilon_n:=(-1)^n\varphi^{-2n}.
  \]
  For \(m\ge1\),
  \[
    \frac{F_{m+1}}{F_{m+2}}
    =
    \varphi^{-1}
    \frac{1-\varepsilon_{m+1}}{1-\varepsilon_{m+2}}
    \le
    \varphi^{-1}
    \frac{1+\varphi^{-2m-2}}{1-\varphi^{-2m-4}},
  \]
  and, once \(\varphi^{-2m-2}\le1/2\),
  \[
    \log F_{m+1}
    \ge
    (m+1)\log\varphi-\tfrac12\log5-2\varphi^{-2m-2}.
  \]
  Also \(F_{m+2}/F_{m+1}\le3\) for \(m\ge1\), so
  \[
    \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+1}}
    =
    1+\frac{\log(F_{m+2}/F_{m+1})}{\log F_{m+1}}
    \le
    1+
    \frac{\log3}
    {(m+1)\log\varphi-\tfrac12\log5-2\varphi^{-2m-2}},
  \]
  whenever the denominator is positive. The two error functions
  \(\varphi^{-2m-2}\) and
  \(((m+1)\log\varphi-\tfrac12\log5-2\varphi^{-2m-2})^{-1}\) are bounded
  above, for all \(m\ge M_{\log}\), by explicit decreasing rational
  majorants obtained from rational lower and upper intervals for
  \(\varphi\) and \(\log\varphi\). Consequently the product of the last
  two displayed upper bounds is itself bounded by a decreasing explicit
  function tending to \(\varphi^{-1}\). A finite rational search on this
  majorant, using rational intervals for \(\varphi,\log\varphi,\log3\),
  produces \(M_R\) such that for every \(m\ge M_R\)
  \[
    \frac{F_{m+1}}{F_{m+2}}
      \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+1}}
    \le\frac{1+\varphi^{-1}}2 .
  \]
  The certificate therefore verifies a single explicit Binet upper bound
  that is valid for all \(m\ge M_R\), rather than a monotone-tail
  assertion for \(R_m\) itself.

  Choose \(M_0\ge M_R\) by increasing \(M\) until \(F_{m+1}\ge X_0\)
  and
  \[
    \frac12\le
    \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{m\log\varphi},
    \frac{\log F_{m+1}}{m\log\varphi}
    \le2,
  \]
  for every \(m\ge M_0\). The logarithmic ratio conditions are checked
  from the same linear Binet bounds, while the last displayed
  product inequality is already certified by the choice of \(M_R\). Put
  \[
    \Delta_m:=
    1-\frac{F_{m+1}}{F_{m+2}}
      \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+1}}.
  \]
  Then \(\Delta_m\ge(1-\varphi^{-1})/2\) for \(m\ge M_0\). Enlarge
  \(M_0\) to \(M_{\mathrm Z}\) so that, for all \(m\ge M_{\mathrm Z}\),
  \[
    C_{\pi,1}\left(
      \frac{F_{m+2}}{(\log F_{m+2})^2}
      +\frac{F_{m+1}}{(\log F_{m+1})^2}\right)+2
    \le
    \frac12\,\frac{F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+2}}\Delta_m .
  \]
  This last enlargement is also finite and reproducible. Using the
  certified bounds \(c_F\varphi^m\le F_{m+j}\le C_F\varphi^m\) and
  \(c_{\log}m\le\log F_{m+j}\le C_{\log}m\), the left-hand side is
  bounded above by an explicit multiple of \(\varphi^m/m^2\) plus \(2\),
  while the right-hand side is bounded below by an explicit multiple of
  \(\varphi^m/m\). Once that symbolic majorization verifies the tail,
  the finite prefix is checked by exact Fibonacci integers and real
  interval arithmetic for the logarithms. This is the certificate for
  \(M_{\mathrm Z}\).
  Every word in \(\mathcal Z_m\) represents an integer in
  \([F_{m+1},F_{m+2})\), so
  \[
    \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    =\pi(F_{m+2}-1)-\pi(F_{m+1}-1)
  \]
  and the interval-count convention following
  \suppref{Lemma~1.3} gives the endpoint correction
  \[
    \left|
      \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}-
      \bigl(\pi(F_{m+2})-\pi(F_{m+1})\bigr)
    \right|\le 2.
  \]
  Here \(\pi(x)=\#\{p\le x:p\text{ prime}\}\). For
  \(m\ge M_{\mathrm Z}\), \suppref{Lemma~1.3} gives
  \[
    \begin{aligned}
    \pi(F_{m+2})-\pi(F_{m+1})
    \ge
    &\frac{F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+2}}
      -\frac{F_{m+1}}{\log F_{m+1}}\\
    &{}-C_{\pi,1}\frac{F_{m+2}}{(\log F_{m+2})^2}
      -C_{\pi,1}\frac{F_{m+1}}{(\log F_{m+1})^2}.
    \end{aligned}
  \]
  By the definition of \(M_{\mathrm Z}\), using the displayed endpoint
  inequality,
  \[
    \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    \ge \frac12\,\frac{F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+2}}\Delta_m .
  \]
  The explicit Binet constants above and
  \(\Delta_m\ge(1-\varphi^{-1})/2\) by construction show that one may
  take
  \[
    A_{\mathrm Z}:=\frac{c_F(1-\varphi^{-1})}{8C_{\log}}.
  \]
  For the upper bound,
  \[
    \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}\le \pi(F_{m+2})
      \le C_{\pi,2}\frac{F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+2}}
      \le \frac{C_{\pi,2}C_F}{c_{\log}}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}.
  \]
  Thus \(B_{\mathrm Z}:=C_{\pi,2}C_F/c_{\log}\) is admissible for every
  \(m\ge M_{\mathrm Z}\).
\end{proof}

All automata in this section read Zeckendorf words in the displayed
LSD-first order.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical Zeckendorf presentation]
  \label{lem:zeckendorf-canonical-presentation}
  The language \(\mathcal Z\) is accepted by the two-state trim
  strongly connected deterministic presentation \(\mathcal N_{\mathrm Z}\)
  with state set \(\{0,1\}\), initial state \(0\), accepting state
  \(\{1\}\), and transitions
  \[
    0\xrightarrow{0}0,\qquad
    0\xrightarrow{1}1,\qquad
    1\xrightarrow{0}0.
  \]
  Its adjacency matrix is
  \[
    M_{\mathrm Z}=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      1&1\\
      1&0
    \end{pmatrix},
  \]
  its Perron root is \(\varphi\), and its graph period is \(1\). Hence
  \(\mathcal N_{\mathrm Z}\) satisfies the hypotheses of
  \suppref{Theorem~1.18} with \(\lambda=\varphi\), and
  \(z_m=\abs{\mathcal Z_m}=F_m\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  A path in the displayed graph records exactly a binary word with no
  adjacent \(1\)'s: after reading a \(1\) the only available next digit
  is \(0\), while from state \(0\) both digits are available. The
  accepting state is \(1\), so the accepted words are precisely those
  no-\(11\) words whose terminal digit is \(1\), namely
  \(\mathcal Z\). The state \(0\) is initial and lies on the loop
  labelled \(0\), while \(0\xrightarrow{1}1\xrightarrow{0}0\); therefore
  both states are reachable from the initial state and can reach the
  accepting state, and the underlying directed graph is strongly
  connected. The loop at \(0\) gives period \(1\).

  The characteristic polynomial of \(M_{\mathrm Z}\) is
  \(t^2-t-1\). Its spectral radius is the positive root
  \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2>1\). Finally the accepted length-\(m\) words
  are the canonical Zeckendorf words of length \(m\), whose number is
  \(F_m\) by the standard Fibonacci recurrence with initial values
  \(F_1=F_2=1\).
\end{proof}
