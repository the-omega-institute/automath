\section{Synchronized pumping and recurrent numeration}
\label{sec:context-free-pisot}

We now pass from finite automata to pushdown automata. Recall that an
infinite language is \emph{CF-immune} if it contains no infinite
context-free sublanguage. Here a context-free language means one accepted
by an arbitrary nondeterministic pushdown automaton, equivalently generated
by a context-free grammar. We impose no determinism, unambiguity,
real-time, or growth hypothesis on that automaton. The classical
fixed-base theorem is due independently to Hartmanis--Shank and
Sch\"utzenberger
\cite{HartmanisShank1968,Schutzenberger1968}. The point below is that
the square-modulus block action survives the two simultaneous pumps
supplied by Ogden's lemma \cite{Ogden1968}; the interchange lemma of
Ogden--Ross--Winklmann \cite{OgdenRossWinklmann1985} is a stronger
related tool, but is not needed for the proof.

Write
\[
  \mathcal Z=\{x_1\cdots x_m\in\{0,1\}^\ast:
      x_ix_{i+1}\ne11,\ x_m=1\}
\]
and \(\val(x_1\cdots x_m)=\sum_{j=1}^m x_jF_{j+1}\).
For \(n\ge2\), let \(\omega(n)\) and \(\Omega(n)\) denote respectively
the number of distinct prime divisors and the number counted with
multiplicity; put \(\omega(1)=\Omega(1)=0\). The required Zeckendorf
block action is recorded in \suppref{Lemma~2.5}.

\begin{lemma}[Paired Zeckendorf pumping congruence]
  \label{lem:zeckendorf-paired-pumping-congruence}
  Let \(u,v,x,y,z\in\{0,1\}^\ast\), and put
  \[
    N(k):=\val(uv^kxy^kz)\qquad(k\ge0).
  \]
  Suppose that \(\abs v+\abs y>0\). If \(q\ge2\), and \(h\) is a
  common multiple of the orders modulo \(q\) of the nonempty-block
  matrices among \(M_v,M_y\) (with \(M_\varepsilon=I\)), then
  \[
    N(k+h)\equiv N(k)\pmod q
    \qquad(k\ge0).
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  With \(v_0=(0,1,1)^t\), concatenation and
  \suppref{Lemma~2.5} gives
  \[
    N(k)=e_1^tM_zM_y^kM_xM_v^kM_uv_0.
  \]
  Every nonempty block matrix is invertible modulo \(q\). By the
  definition of \(h\), both \(M_v^h\equiv I\pmod q\) and
  \(M_y^h\equiv I\pmod q\), where the same identities are automatic for
  an empty block. Substitution in the displayed product proves the
  congruence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Context-free coprime-quotient Zeckendorf pumping]
  \label{thm:context-free-coprime-quotient-zeckendorf-pumping}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) be context-free. There is an integer
  \(P_L\ge1\) such that every \(w\in L\) with
  \(\abs w\ge P_L\) and \(\val(w)\ge2\) begins a sequence
  \(w_0=w,w_1,w_2,\ldots\) in \(L\) whose lengths are strictly
  increasing and whose values \(N_i=\val(w_i)\) satisfy
  \[
    N_{i+1}=N_iQ_i,\qquad Q_i>1,\qquad
    Q_i\equiv1\pmod {N_i},\qquad \gcd(Q_i,N_i)=1.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Apply Ogden's lemma with every position distinguished. It supplies a
  constant \(P_L\) such that each \(w\in L\) of length at least \(P_L\)
  has a factorisation
  \[
    w=uvxyz,\qquad \abs v+\abs y>0,\qquad
    uv^kxy^kz\in L\quad(k\ge0).
  \]
  The additional marked-position bound in Ogden's lemma is not needed
  here. Starting with \(w_i\in L\), choose such a factorisation and
  let \(h_i\) be a common multiple of the orders of \(M_v\) and \(M_y\)
  in
  \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\ZZ/N_i^2\ZZ)\), omitting an empty block. These
  orders exist by
  \suppref{Lemma~2.5}. Define
  \[
    w_{i+1}:=uv^{\,1+h_i}xy^{\,1+h_i}z.
  \]
  By \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-paired-pumping-congruence},
  \(N_{i+1}\equiv N_i\pmod {N_i^2}\). Hence
  \(N_{i+1}=N_i(1+t_iN_i)\) for an integer \(t_i\).

  Since \(h_i\ge1\) and \(\abs v+\abs y>0\), the new word is longer.
  Both words belong to \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\), and canonical
  Zeckendorf intervals are strictly ordered by length; consequently
  \(N_{i+1}>N_i\), so \(t_i\ge1\). Taking
  \(Q_i=1+t_iN_i\) gives all four assertions. The new word still has
  length at least \(P_L\) and value at least \(2\), so the construction
  iterates.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Zeckendorf CF-immunity package]
  \label{cor:zeckendorf-context-free-immunity-package}
  Every infinite context-free \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) satisfies
  \[
    \sup_{w\in L}\omega(\val(w))=\infty.
  \]
  Consequently, for each \(K\ge1\), the bounded-distinct-prime language
  \(\mathcal B_K\), the bounded-almost-prime language \(\mathcal A_K\),
  and every infinite finite-prime-support language \(\mathcal Z_S\)
  are CF-immune. In particular, the Zeckendorf prime language
  \(\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\) is CF-immune.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Choose a sufficiently long word in \(L\) and apply
  \Cref{thm:context-free-coprime-quotient-zeckendorf-pumping}. Since
  \(N_j\mid N_i\) for \(j\le i\), while every \(Q_i>1\) is coprime to
  \(N_i\), a prime divisor of \(Q_i\) divides no earlier value. Thus
  \(\omega(N_i)\ge\omega(N_0)+i\). The immunity conclusions follow
  exactly as in the regular cases: \(\omega\le K\) on \(\mathcal B_K\),
  \(\mathcal A_K\subseteq\mathcal B_K\), and
  \(\omega\le\abs S\) on \(\mathcal Z_S\). Finally prime values have
  \(\omega=1\), while Euclid's theorem makes
  \(\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\) infinite.
\end{proof}

We next isolate the precise base-independent interface. This is
deliberately narrower than every object called a Pisot or abstract
numeration system in the literature.

\begin{definition}[Canonical eventually recurrent numeration]
  \label{def:canonical-eventually-recurrent-numeration}
  A canonical eventually recurrent numeration system consists of:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(U\arabic*)}]
    \item a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers
    \(U=(U_n)_{n\ge0}\) and a finite digit alphabet
    \(A\subseteq\NN\) containing \(0\);
    \item an LSD-first language \(\mathcal R_U\subseteq A^\ast\), with
    \[
      \val_U(x_0\cdots x_{m-1})
        :=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}x_jU_j,
    \]
    for which \(\val_U:\mathcal R_U\to\NN_{\ge1}\) is a bijection;
    \item strict length ordering: if \(r,s\in\mathcal R_U\) and
    \(\abs r<\abs s\), then \(\val_U(r)<\val_U(s)\);
    \item integers \(J\ge0,d\ge1,a_0,\ldots,a_{d-1}\), with
    \(a_0\ne0\), such that
    \[
      U_{n+d}=a_0U_n+a_1U_{n+1}+\cdots+a_{d-1}U_{n+d-1}
      \qquad(n\ge J).
    \]
  \end{enumerate}
  Relative to this recurrence witness, it is a \emph{unit system} if
  \(\abs{a_0}=1\). No regularity of \(\mathcal R_U\) is included in
  this definition.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Affine recurrence block action]
  \label{lem:affine-recurrence-block-action}
  Under \textup{(U4)}, let \(C\) be the \(d\times d\) companion matrix
  with superdiagonal entries \(1\) and last row
  \((a_0,\ldots,a_{d-1})\). For \(\varepsilon\in A\), put
  \[
    D_\varepsilon=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      1&\varepsilon e_1^t\\
      0&C
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  Once \(r\ge J\), appending \(\varepsilon\) sends
  \[
    (N_r,U_r,\ldots,U_{r+d-1})^t
    \quad\hbox{to}\quad
    D_\varepsilon(N_r,U_r,\ldots,U_{r+d-1})^t.
  \]
  Moreover
  \(\det D_\varepsilon=(-1)^{d-1}a_0\). Hence every block action is
  invertible modulo \(q\) whenever \(\gcd(q,a_0)=1\), and modulo every
  \(q\ge2\) in a unit system. Here and below, for a block
  \(b=b_1\cdots b_\ell\), we write
  \(M_b=D_{b_\ell}\cdots D_{b_1}\), with \(M_\varepsilon=I\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The first coordinate becomes \(N_r+\varepsilon U_r\); the remaining
  coordinates shift once and the final one is supplied by \textup{(U4)}.
  These are exactly the displayed matrix equations. The determinant
  is \(\det C=(-1)^{d-1}a_0\). Multiplicativity of determinants gives
  the assertion for blocks.
\end{proof}

The coefficient \(a_0\) belongs to the displayed recurrence witness,
not intrinsically to \((U,A,\mathcal R_U)\); see
\Cref{prop:recurrence-witness-separation} for the resulting strict
separation.

We now use the standard fan-out convention: a multiple context-free
language (MCFL) is a \(k\)-MCFL for some finite \(k\ge1\). An infinite
language is \emph{MCF-immune} if it contains no infinite MCFL
sublanguage. The weak pumping lemma of Seki--Matsumura--Fujii--Kasami
states that an infinite \(k\)-MCFL contains one word that can be pumped
synchronously at \(2k\) factors \cite[Lemma~3.2]{SekiEtAl1991}. The
lemma makes no assertion about every sufficiently long word; that
stronger form fails for general MCFLs
\cite{KanazawaKobeleMichaelisSalvatiYoshinaka2014}.

\begin{theorem}[Synchronized local-congruence orbit]
  \label{thm:mcfl-synchronized-congruence-orbit}
  Let \((U,A,\mathcal R_U)\) satisfy \textup{(U1)--(U4)}, and let
  \(L\subseteq\mathcal R_U\) be an infinite \(k\)-MCFL. There are words
  \[
    \begin{gathered}
      a,u_1,\ldots,u_{k+1},v_1,\ldots,v_k\in A^\ast,\\
      w_1,\ldots,w_k,s_1,\ldots,s_k\in A^\ast
    \end{gathered}
  \]
  such that
  \[
    W(t)=a u_1v_1^tw_1s_1^tu_2\cdots
          u_kv_k^tw_ks_k^tu_{k+1}\in L
          \qquad(t\ge0),
  \]
  \[
    \sum_{j=1}^k(\abs{v_j}+\abs{s_j})>0,
  \]
  and \(N(t):=\val_U(W(t))\) is strictly increasing. Moreover, for
  every \(q\ge2\) with \(\gcd(q,a_0)=1\), there is \(H(q)\ge1\) such
  that
  \[
    N(t+H(q))\equiv N(t)\pmod q
    \qquad\text{for every }t\ge0.
  \]
  The factorization, and hence the orbit \(W(t)\), is independent of
  \(q\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Delete the finitely many words of length less than \(J\). Since the
  alphabet is finite, some \(a\in A^J\) is the prefix of infinitely many
  remaining words. MCFLs are closed, without increasing fan-out, under
  left quotient by a fixed word \cite[Section~3]{SekiEtAl1991}; hence
  \[
    L_a=\{x\in A^\ast:ax\in L\}
  \]
  is an infinite \(k\)-MCFL. The weak pumping lemma supplies
  \(u_j,v_j,w_j,s_j\) with the displayed synchronized pumping property
  and with at least one \(v_j\) or \(s_j\) nonempty.

  All digit actions after \(a\) are in the stationary range of
  \textup{(U4)}. Fix an allowed modulus \(q\). By
  \Cref{lem:affine-recurrence-block-action}, every nonempty matrix among
  \[
    M_{v_1},M_{s_1},\ldots,M_{v_k},M_{s_k}
  \]
  lies in the finite group
  \(\operatorname{GL}_{d+1}(\ZZ/q\ZZ)\). Let \(H(q)\) be a common
  multiple of their orders; empty blocks contribute the identity.
  Replacing \(t\) by \(t+H(q)\) in the matrix product for \(W(t)\)
  therefore leaves every powered block unchanged modulo \(q\), proving
  the congruence simultaneously for all \(t\ge0\).

  Finally,
  \[
    \abs{W(t+1)}-\abs{W(t)}
      =\sum_{j=1}^k(\abs{v_j}+\abs{s_j})>0.
  \]
  Both words are canonical, so \textup{(U3)} gives
  \(N(t+1)>N(t)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Explicit grammar for a synchronized ray]
  \label{lem:synchronized-ray-linear-mcfg}
  Fix \(k\ge1\), a word \(a\), and words
  \(u_1,\ldots,u_{k+1},v_1,\ldots,v_k,w_1,\ldots,w_k,
  s_1,\ldots,s_k\) over a finite alphabet. If
  \[
    \sum_{j=1}^k(\abs{v_j}+\abs{s_j})>0,
  \]
  then
  \[
    \left\{a u_1v_1^tw_1s_1^tu_2\cdots
      u_kv_k^tw_ks_k^tu_{k+1}:t\ge0\right\}
  \]
  is an infinite linear \(2k\)-MCFL.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Take a nonterminal \(X\) of fan-out \(2k\) and a start symbol \(T\) of
  fan-out one. The productions are
  \[
    X(\varepsilon,\ldots,\varepsilon)\leftarrow,
  \]
  \[
    X(v_1x_1,s_1x_2,\ldots,v_kx_{2k-1},s_kx_{2k})
      \leftarrow X(x_1,\ldots,x_{2k}),
  \]
  and
  \[
    T(a u_1x_1w_1x_2u_2\cdots
      u_kx_{2k-1}w_kx_{2k}u_{k+1})
      \leftarrow X(x_1,\ldots,x_{2k}).
  \]
  After \(t\) recursive applications, the tuple derived from \(X\) is
  \((v_1^t,s_1^t,\ldots,v_k^t,s_k^t)\), so the generated language is
  exactly the displayed ray. Every variable occurs exactly once in each
  production. The positive total pumped length makes the words at
  distinct parameters have distinct lengths.
\end{proof}

Put \(S=\{p:p\text{ is prime and }p\mid a_0\}\). On
\(\NN_{\ge1}\), let \(\tau_S\) be the topology generated by the basis
\[
  \{(n+q\ZZ)\cap\NN_{\ge1}:n\ge1,\ q\ge1,\ \gcd(q,a_0)=1\}.
\]
Its completion is
\[
  \widehat{\ZZ}_{S^c}
    :=\varprojlim_{\gcd(q,a_0)=1}\ZZ/q\ZZ
    \cong\prod_{p\notin S}\ZZ_p.
\]
These are the adic topologies and completions studied by Broughan
\cite{Broughan2003}.

\begin{theorem}[MCFL local-adic topological fingerprint]
  \label{thm:mcfl-local-adic-fingerprint}
  For the orbit \(Y=\{N(t):t\ge0\}\) supplied by
  \Cref{thm:mcfl-synchronized-congruence-orbit}, the following hold.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item The subspace \(Y\subseteq(\NN_{\ge1},\tau_S)\) has no isolated
    points and is homeomorphic to \(\mathbb Q\).
    \item Its closure in \(\widehat{\ZZ}_{S^c}\) is homeomorphic to the
    Cantor set.
    \item If \(B\subseteq\NN_{\ge1}\) is scattered in \(\tau_S\), then
    \[
      \operatorname{Rep}_U(B)
        :=\{w\in\mathcal R_U:\val_U(w)\in B\}
    \]
    is MCF-immune whenever it is infinite.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Let \(N(t)\in Y\), and let its basic neighborhood be specified modulo
  an allowed \(q\). If \(q=1\), take any later orbit point. If \(q\ge2\),
  \Cref{thm:mcfl-synchronized-congruence-orbit} gives
  \(N(t+H(q))\equiv N(t)\pmod q\), and strict increase makes these points
  distinct. Thus \(Y\) has no isolated point.

  The allowed congruences separate distinct positive integers: for
  \(m\ne n\), choose a prime outside the finite set \(S\) that does not
  divide \(m-n\). Consequently \(\tau_S\) is Hausdorff and metrizable;
  it also has a countable basis. Hence \(Y\) is a countable metrizable
  space without isolated points. The Sierpi\'nski characterization used
  in \cite[Theorem~2.4]{Broughan2003} yields \(Y\cong\mathbb Q\).

  The profinite ring \(\widehat{\ZZ}_{S^c}\) is compact, metrizable, and
  zero-dimensional. Its closed subspace \(\overline Y\) has no isolated
  point: an isolated point of the closure would, by density of \(Y\),
  belong to and be isolated in \(Y\). Thus \(\overline Y\) is a nonempty
  compact perfect zero-dimensional metrizable space, and the classical
  Cantor-space characterization gives the second assertion; compare
  \cite[Corollary~3.1]{Broughan2003}.

  For the last assertion, an infinite MCFL sublanguage of
  \(\operatorname{Rep}_U(B)\) would, by the first assertion, provide a
  nonempty subspace \(Y\subseteq B\) without isolated points. This
  contradicts scatteredness, which is hereditary to subspaces.
\end{proof}

For a prime \(p\), write \(v_p(n)\) for the exponent of \(p\) in \(n\),
and let \(\omega_{S^c}(n)=\#\{p\notin S:p\mid n\}\).
For \(K\ge0\) and \(E=(E_p)_{p\in S}\in\NN_0^S\), define
\[
  X_{K,E}=\{n\ge1:\omega_{S^c}(n)\le K,
               \ v_p(n)\le E_p\ (p\in S)\}.
\]
For \(X\subseteq\NN_{\ge1}\), let \(D_SX\) be the set of points of \(X\)
that are not isolated in its \(\tau_S\)-subspace topology. Thus \(D_S\)
is the intrinsic Cantor--Bendixson derivative.

\begin{proposition}[Exact local prime-factor rank]
  \label{prop:local-prime-factor-cb-rank}
  For every \(K\ge1\),
  \[
    D_SX_{K,E}=X_{K-1,E}.
  \]
  Consequently
  \[
    D_S^{\,j}X_{K,E}=X_{K-j,E}\quad(0\le j\le K),
    \qquad D_S^{\,K+1}X_{K,E}=\varnothing,
  \]
  and the Cantor--Bendixson height of \(X_{K,E}\) is exactly \(K+1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Suppose first that \(n\in X_{K-1,E}\), and let \(q\ge1\) be coprime
  to \(a_0\). Dirichlet's theorem supplies a prime
  \(r\equiv1\pmod q\) outside the finite set \(S\cup\{p:p\mid n\}\).
  Then \(nr\ne n\), \(nr\equiv n\pmod q\), the \(S\)-adic valuations
  are unchanged, and
  \[
    \omega_{S^c}(nr)=\omega_{S^c}(n)+1\le K.
  \]
  Hence \(n\in D_SX_{K,E}\).

  Conversely, let \(n\in X_{K,E}\) have
  \(\omega_{S^c}(n)=K\), and write
  \[
    n=c\prod_{i=1}^K\ell_i^{e_i},
    \qquad c=\prod_{p\in S}p^{f_p},\quad 0\le f_p\le E_p,
  \]
  with distinct \(\ell_i\notin S\). Put
  \(q_0=\prod_i\ell_i^{e_i+1}\). If \(m\in X_{K,E}\) and
  \(m\equiv n\pmod {q_0}\), then
  \(v_{\ell_i}(m)=e_i\) for every \(i\). The budget of \(K\) primes
  outside \(S\) leaves no further such prime, so \(m\) belongs to the
  finite set
  \[
    F=\left\{
      \left(\prod_{p\in S}p^{g_p}\right)
      \prod_{i=1}^K\ell_i^{e_i}:
      0\le g_p\le E_p
    \right\}.
  \]
  Choose a prime \(r\notin S\cup\{\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_K\}\) dividing
  none of the finitely many nonzero differences \(m-n\), \(m\in F\).
  Then \(q=q_0r\) is coprime to \(a_0\), and the \(q\)-neighborhood of
  \(n\) meets \(X_{K,E}\) only at \(n\). Thus precisely the points with
  fewer than \(K\) outside primes survive one derivative. Iteration
  proves the formula. Finally \(X_{0,E}\) is a nonempty finite set (it
  contains \(1\)), so the height is exactly \(K+1\).
\end{proof}

Broughan's related full-adic result counts prime factors with
multiplicity and identifies closures of the corresponding strata
\cite[Theorem~4.3]{Broughan2003}. The preceding proposition instead
uses distinct prime support, deletes the finite set \(S\), imposes
bounded \(S\)-adic valuations, and computes the intrinsic derivative
rank.

\begin{theorem}[Nonunit escape dichotomy and MCF-immunity]
  \label{thm:nonunit-mcfl-escape-dichotomy}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal R_U\) be an infinite MCFL. Then either
  \[
    \sup_{w\in L}\omega_{S^c}(\val_U(w))=\infty,
  \]
  or there is a prime \(p\mid a_0\) such that
  \[
    \sup_{w\in L}v_p(\val_U(w))=\infty.
  \]
  Consequently the following languages are MCF-immune whenever they are
  infinite:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item the prime representation language \(\mathcal P_U\);
    \item every language on which \(\Omega(\val_U(w))\) is bounded;
    \item every finite-prime-support language on which all
    \(v_p(\val_U(w))\), \(p\mid a_0\), are bounded.
  \end{enumerate}
  In a unit system, every bounded-\(\omega(\val_U(w))\) language is
  MCF-immune.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  If neither alternative held, finiteness of \(S\) would give
  \(K\ge0\) and \(E\in\NN_0^S\) such that
  \[
    \{\val_U(w):w\in L\}\subseteq X_{K,E}.
  \]
  By \Cref{prop:local-prime-factor-cb-rank}, \(X_{K,E}\) is scattered.
  This contradicts
  \Cref{thm:mcfl-local-adic-fingerprint}. Prime values satisfy
  \(\omega_{S^c}\le1\) and \(v_p\le1\); bounded \(\Omega\) bounds both
  alternatives; and finite prime support bounds \(\omega_{S^c}\), with
  the remaining valuations bounded by hypothesis. If \(S=\varnothing\),
  the second alternative is absent, giving the unit-system assertion.
\end{proof}

The second alternative is necessary. In ordinary base \(b\), where
\(a_0=b\), the regular LSD-first language
\(\{0^n1:n\ge0\}\) represents \(b^n\). Its distinct prime support is
fixed, while \(v_p(b^n)\) is unbounded for every \(p\mid b\).

\begin{proposition}[Recurrence-witness inflation and strict separation]
  \label{prop:recurrence-witness-separation}
  If a tail has minimal recurrence polynomial \(\mu_U\), then for every
  prime \(p\) it also has a recurrence witness with characteristic
  polynomial \((X-p)\mu_U(X)\).  When \(\abs{\mu_U(0)}=1\), the trailing
  coefficient of this witness has absolute value \(p\).

  Moreover, there are two canonical eventually recurrent
  numeration systems with regular representation languages and recurrence
  witnesses whose trailing-coefficient prime set is \(\{p\}\), but with
  opposite bounded-outside-prime escape behavior:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item ordinary base \(p\) contains the infinite regular language
    \(\{0^n1:n\ge0\}\), whose values are \(p^n\);
    \item the Zeckendorf system, with \(F_0=0,F_1=1\) and
    \(F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n\), equipped with the order-three recurrence
    witness
    \[
      F_{n+3}=-pF_n+(1-p)F_{n+1}+(p+1)F_{n+2},
    \]
    contains no infinite MCFL \(L\) for which
    \[
      \sup_{w\in L}\omega_{\{p\}^c}(\val(w))<\infty.
    \]
  \end{enumerate}
  Thus the prime divisors of an arbitrarily selected trailing coefficient
  do not determine the escape behavior of the numeration system.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The polynomial identity \(\mu_U(C)z_J=0\) remains valid after
  multiplication by \(X-p\).  In the companion convention, the trailing
  recurrence coefficient is the negative of the constant term of the
  characteristic polynomial, proving the first assertion.

  The base-\(p\) assertion is the preceding example.  For the Zeckendorf
  sequence, multiplication of its minimal polynomial by \(X-p\) gives
  \[
    (X-p)(X^2-X-1)
      =X^3-(p+1)X^2+(p-1)X+p,
  \]
  which is exactly the displayed recurrence and has trailing coefficient
  \(-p\).  This changes neither the sequence nor its regular canonical
  representation language.  On the other hand, its minimal recurrence
  has unit trailing coefficient.  If the displayed supremum were bounded
  by \(K\), then
  \(\omega(\val(w))\le K+1\) on \(L\), contrary to the unit-recurrence
  conclusion of
  \Cref{thm:nonunit-mcfl-escape-dichotomy}.  This proves the separation.
\end{proof}

Let
\[
  \mu_U(X)=X^{d_{\mathrm r}}-c_{d_{\mathrm r}-1}X^{d_{\mathrm r}-1}
    -\cdots-c_1X-c_0
\]
be the minimal recurrence polynomial of a tail of \(U\). Since it
divides the characteristic polynomial of a recurrence allowed in
\textup{(U4)}, its constant term is nonzero. Enlarge the recurrence
threshold to \(J_{\mathrm r}\) so that this minimal recurrence holds for
every \(n\ge J_{\mathrm r}\), and put
\[
  S_{\mathrm r}=\{p:p\text{ is prime and }p\mid c_0\}.
\]

\begin{definition}[Geometric synchronized scheme]
  \label{def:geometric-synchronized-scheme}
  A geometric synchronized scheme for \(U\) consists of
  \(a\in A^{J_{\mathrm r}}\), an integer \(k\ge1\), integers
  \(c\ge1,b\ge2\), and words
  \[
    u_1,\ldots,u_{k+1},\quad
    v_1,\ldots,v_k,\quad w_1,\ldots,w_k,\quad
    s_1,\ldots,s_k\in A^\ast,
  \]
  such that
  \[
    W(t)=a u_1v_1^tw_1s_1^tu_2\cdots
      u_kv_k^tw_ks_k^tu_{k+1}\in\mathcal R_U
      \qquad(t\ge0),
  \]
  \[
    \sum_{j=1}^k(\abs{v_j}+\abs{s_j})>0,
    \qquad \val_U(W(t))=cb^t\quad(t\ge0).
  \]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Arithmetic input of Evertse]
  \label{lem:evertse-quotient-support-input}
  Let \(K\) be a number field. For \(x\in K^\times\), let \(P_K(x)\)
  be the largest norm of a finite prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\) for which
  \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(x)\ne0\), with \(P_K(x)=1\) when
  \(x\) is a unit. If \(Z=(Z(n))_{n\ge0}\) is a nondegenerate linear
  recurrence over \(K\) with at least two characteristic roots, then
  \[
    \lim_{\substack{r\to\infty\\ r>s\ge0,\ Z(s)\ne0}}
      P_K\!\left(\frac{Z(r)}{Z(s)}\right)=\infty.
  \]
  In particular, a rational-integer recurrence with no zero terms, all
  supported on one finite set of rational primes, cannot be both
  nondegenerate and have at least two characteristic roots.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The displayed uniform limit is exactly
  \cite[Theorem~3]{Evertse1984}. For the consequence, let \(S\) be the
  finite rational-prime support and choose a number field \(K\) containing
  the characteristic roots and coefficients. If \(Z(r),Z(s)\ne0\), the
  principal fractional ideal generated by \(Z(r)/Z(s)\) is supported
  only at prime ideals above primes in \(S\). Every prime ideal
  \(\mathfrak p\) above \(p\in S\) has
  \[
    \operatorname N\mathfrak p=p^{f(\mathfrak p/p)}
      \le p^{[K:\mathbb Q]}.
  \]
  Consequently
  \[
    P_K\!\left(\frac{Z(r)}{Z(s)}\right)
      \le \max\bigl(\{p^{[K:\mathbb Q]}:p\in S\}\cup\{1\}\bigr)
  \]
  for every \(r,s\) with nonzero denominator. This fixed bound
  contradicts Evertse's limit when there are at least two characteristic
  roots.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Increasing \(S\)-unit recurrences]
  \label{lem:s-unit-increasing-recurrence-rigidity}
  Let \(Y=(Y(n))_{n\ge0}\) be a strictly increasing sequence of positive
  rational integers satisfying a linear recurrence. If all prime
  divisors of all \(Y(n)\) belong to one finite set, then there are
  integers \(r\ge0,h\ge1,c\ge1\), and \(b\ge2\) such that
  \[
    Y(r+hn)=cb^n\qquad(n\ge0).
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Write the standard exponential-polynomial expansion, after discarding
  the contribution of the zero characteristic root and a finite initial
  segment, as
  \[
    Y(n)=\sum_{i=1}^m f_i(n)\alpha_i^n,
  \]
  where the \(\alpha_i\) are distinct nonzero algebraic numbers and the
  nonzero \(f_i\) are algebraic polynomials. Partition the roots by
  \(\alpha_i\sim\alpha_j\) when \(\alpha_i/\alpha_j\) is a root of
  unity. Choose \(h_0\ge1\) divisible by the orders of all such
  quotients. For each \(j\in\{0,\ldots,h_0-1\}\), substitution of
  \(n=j+h_0t\) and collection within the equivalence classes gives
  \[
    Y(j+h_0t)=\sum_C g_{C,j}(t)\beta_C^t,
    \qquad \beta_C=\alpha_i^{h_0}\quad(i\in C).
  \]
  Delete the identically zero summands. Distinct remaining \(\beta_C\)
  have quotient that is not a root of unity. Thus every residue
  subsequence is a nonzero nondegenerate recurrence; it is nonzero
  because all of its terms are positive.

  Every term of every residue subsequence is a nonzero rational integer
  supported on the prescribed finite set. If a residue subsequence had
  at least two characteristic roots, the consequence in
  \Cref{lem:evertse-quotient-support-input} would contradict the fixed
  support of all its term quotients. Hence every residue subsequence has
  exactly one characteristic root and therefore has the form
  \[
    Z(n)=f(n)\lambda^n
  \]
  with one characteristic root.

  Choose one residue subsequence and shift its index if necessary so
  that the displayed expansion holds from \(n=0\). The Hankel matrix of
  \(Z\) has rational entries and finite rank, so a minimal linear
  dependence among its shift columns has coefficients in
  \(\mathbb Q\). Thus the minimal annihilating polynomial of \(Z\) lies
  in \(\mathbb Q[X]\). Over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) it is a power of
  \(X-\lambda\). Galois invariance therefore gives
  \(\lambda\in\mathbb Q\). Now \(f(n)=Z(n)/\lambda^n\in\mathbb Q\)
  for every \(n\), and interpolation from \(\deg(f)+1\) integer
  arguments gives \(f\in\mathbb Q[X]\). If \(f\) were nonconstant, clear its
  denominators to obtain a nonconstant \(g\in\mathbb Z[X]\). The
  identity \(Z(n)=f(n)\lambda^n\) would confine every prime divisor of
  every nonzero \(g(n)\) to a fixed finite set. Indeed, if
  \(\lambda=a/b\) is in lowest terms and \(g=Df\), then
  \[
    g(n)a^n=Db^nZ(n).
  \]
  Thus that fixed set is the union of the prescribed prime set and the
  prime divisors of \(Dab\). This contradicts Schur's theorem that a
  nonconstant integer polynomial has values divisible by infinitely
  many primes; see, for example, \cite{HardyWright2008}. Hence \(f\) is
  constant.

  We have therefore \(Z(n)=c\lambda^n\), where
  \(c=Z(0)\) is a positive integer. If \(\lambda=a/b\) in lowest terms,
  integrality for every \(n\) forces \(b^n\mid c\) for every \(n\), so
  \(b=1\). Positivity and strict increase then force
  \(\lambda\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2}\). Translating the chosen residue and
  index shift back to \(Y\) supplies the asserted \(r\) and \(h\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Geometric-ray characterization]
  \label{thm:bounded-outside-support-geometric-ray}
  For a canonical eventually recurrent numeration system satisfying
  \textup{(U1)--(U4)}, the following are equivalent.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item There are a finite \(k\) and an infinite \(k\)-MCFL
    \(L\subseteq\mathcal R_U\) such that
    \[
      \sup_{w\in L}\omega_{S_{\mathrm r}^c}(\val_U(w))<\infty.
    \]
    \item The system admits a geometric synchronized scheme in the
    sense of \Cref{def:geometric-synchronized-scheme}.
    \item The language \(\mathcal R_U\) contains an infinite linear MCFL
    \(L_0=\{W(t):t\ge0\}\) of finite fan-out for which there are
    \(c\ge1,b\ge2\) satisfying
    \[
      \val_U(W(t))=cb^t\qquad(t\ge0).
    \]
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Suppose first that \textup{(i)} holds. Use the minimal tail recurrence
  as the witness in
  \Cref{thm:mcfl-synchronized-congruence-orbit}, and write its resulting
  ray and strictly increasing value sequence as \(W(t)\) and \(N(t)\).
  Let
  \[
    K_0=\max_{t\ge0}\omega_{S_{\mathrm r}^c}(N(t)),
  \]
  and choose \(t_0\) attaining the maximum. Let \(T\) be the set of
  primes outside \(S_{\mathrm r}\) that divide \(N(t_0)\), and put
  \(q=\prod_{\ell\in T}\ell\). If \(T\ne\varnothing\), take the return
  time \(H=H(q)\) from
  \Cref{thm:mcfl-synchronized-congruence-orbit}; if \(T=\varnothing\),
  put \(H=1\). Every prime in \(T\) divides every \(N(t_0+nH)\). A prime
  divisor outside \(S_{\mathrm r}\cup T\) would therefore give at least
  \(K_0+1\) distinct prime divisors outside \(S_{\mathrm r}\), contrary
  to the definition of \(K_0\). Thus all terms of
  \[
    Y(n)=N(t_0+nH)
  \]
  are supported on the fixed finite set \(S_{\mathrm r}\cup T\).

  The entries of every matrix power \(M^n\) satisfy a linear recurrence
  by Cayley--Hamilton. The expression for \(N(t)\) is a finite sum of
  finite products of such entries, and linear recurrence sequences are
  closed under sums and termwise products. Hence \(Y\) is a strictly
  increasing positive integer recurrence. By
  \Cref{lem:s-unit-increasing-recurrence-rigidity}, there are
  \(r\ge0,h\ge1,c\ge1,b\ge2\) such that
  \(N(t_\ast+h_\ast n)=cb^n\), where
  \(t_\ast=t_0+Hr\) and \(h_\ast=Hh\).

  In the synchronized factorization, replace
  \[
    u_j\ \text{by}\ u_jv_j^{t_\ast},\qquad
    v_j\ \text{by}\ v_j^{h_\ast},\qquad
    w_j\ \text{by}\ w_js_j^{t_\ast},\qquad
    s_j\ \text{by}\ s_j^{h_\ast}.
  \]
  This absorbs the fixed powers and parametrizes exactly the subray
  \(W(t_\ast+h_\ast n)\). Its total pumped length remains positive, so
  it is a geometric synchronized scheme. This proves
  \textup{(i)}\(\Rightarrow\)\textup{(ii)}.

  The implication \textup{(ii)}\(\Rightarrow\)\textup{(iii)} is
  \Cref{lem:synchronized-ray-linear-mcfg}. Finally, under
  \textup{(iii)}, the language \(L_0\) is an infinite MCFL and
  \[
    \omega_{S_{\mathrm r}^c}(cb^t)\le\omega(cb)
    \qquad(t\ge0),
  \]
  proving \textup{(i)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Prime support of the geometric ratio]
  \label{lem:geometric-ratio-tail-support}
  In every geometric synchronized scheme
  \(\val_U(W(t))=cb^t\), each prime divisor of \(b\) divides the
  constant coefficient \(c_0\) of the minimal tail recurrence.  Equivalently,
  \[
    \operatorname{rad}(b)\mid\operatorname{rad}(\abs{c_0}).
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Suppose that a prime \(p\mid b\) does not divide \(c_0\), put
  \(e=v_p(c)\), and take the allowed modulus \(q=p^{e+1}\).  Every
  nonempty block matrix among the \(M_{v_j}\) and \(M_{s_j}\) is invertible
  modulo \(q\) by \Cref{lem:affine-recurrence-block-action}.  A common
  multiple \(H\ge1\) of their orders gives, by the matrix product defining
  the scheme exactly as in the proof of
  \Cref{thm:mcfl-synchronized-congruence-orbit},
  \[
    \val_U(W(t+H))\equiv\val_U(W(t))\pmod q
    \qquad(t\ge0).
  \]
  At \(t=0\) this says
  \[
    cb^H\equiv c\pmod {p^{e+1}}.
  \]
  But \(p\mid b\) implies \(p\nmid b^H-1\), so
  \(v_p(c(b^H-1))=e\), contradicting the displayed congruence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Positive semidecidability]
  \label{cor:bounded-outside-support-semidecidable}
  Suppose the numeration system is given effectively by its finite
  digit alphabet, recurrence and initial data, and a DFA recognizing
  \(\mathcal R_U\), with the minimal tail recurrence and a valid
  threshold \(J_{\mathrm r}\) effectively computable from these data.
  Then the property in
  \Cref{thm:bounded-outside-support-geometric-ray}\textup{(i)} is
  recursively enumerable, equivalently it has a \(\Sigma^0_1\) upper
  bound.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Enumerate \(k\ge1\), \(a\in A^{J_{\mathrm r}}\), and all finite tuples
  of words occurring in
  \Cref{def:geometric-synchronized-scheme}, rejecting tuples of zero
  total pumped length. For a fixed tuple, whether the DFA accepts
  \(W(t)\) for every \(t\ge0\) is decidable: the tuple of powers of the
  finitely many pumped-block transformations is eventually periodic in
  the finite transition monoid, and its preperiod and period are found
  by exhaustive iteration.

  The affine matrices construct an exact linear recurrence for
  \[
    N(t)=\val_U(W(t)).
  \]
  Kronecker products implement the termwise
  products arising from the several synchronized block powers. For a
  tuple accepted at every parameter, compute \(c=N(0)\). The only
  possible integer ratio is \(b=N(1)/N(0)\). Check that
  \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2}\), and then decide whether the recurrence
  \(N(t+1)-bN(t)\) is identically zero by checking an initial segment of
  length equal to an explicitly constructed recurrence order. Thus each
  candidate scheme is decidable, and the enumeration halts exactly on
  positive instances by
  \Cref{thm:bounded-outside-support-geometric-ray}. No claim of a
  decision procedure for negative instances is made.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact algorithmic boundary]
  \label{rem:general-decision-interface}
  The preceding corollary does not yield a decision procedure. To turn
  its enumeration into one, it would suffice to compute from the input
  DFA and minimal tail recurrence a bound \(B\) such that every positive
  instance has a geometric synchronized scheme with fan-out and total
  constant and pumped-block length at most \(B\). No such compression
  follows from DFA pumping: replacing a long loop by a shorter word with
  the same DFA endpoints need not preserve its affine recurrence matrix,
  and can therefore destroy the identity
  \(\val_U(W(t+1))=b\val_U(W(t))\).

  The neighboring decision theory does not close this step. Orbit and
  zero problems for linear recurrences already contain the open Skolem
  problem \cite{OuaknineWorrell2012,TarasovVyalyi2011}, while unrestricted
  multiplicative matrix equations include undecidable families
  \cite{BellEtAl2008MatrixEquations}. Neither observation is a reduction
  for the companion-form digit matrices here, and conditions
  \textup{(U1)--(U3)} must also be preserved. Thus general decidability,
  undecidability, and any hardness classification remain open; the proved
  effective statement is exactly the positive semidecision above. The
  Perron-recurrence subclass of greedy linear numeration bases is resolved
  separately below.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrarily deep quotient congruences]
  \label{thm:unit-mcfl-deep-congruence-chain}
  Suppose \(\abs{a_0}=1\), and let \(L\subseteq\mathcal R_U\) be an
  infinite MCFL. For arbitrary sequences \(M_i\ge1\) and \(r_i\ge1\),
  \(L\) contains a strictly increasing sequence \(N_i\) such that
  \[
    N_{i+1}=N_iQ_i,\qquad Q_i>1,\qquad
    Q_i\equiv1\pmod {M_iN_i^{r_i}}.
  \]
  If \(M_i\) is divisible by every prime at most \(B_i\), then every
  prime divisor of \(Q_i\) exceeds \(B_i\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Take the fixed orbit \(N(t)\) from
  \Cref{thm:mcfl-synchronized-congruence-orbit}, discarding finitely many
  initial parameters so that \(N(t)\ge2\). Having selected
  \(N_i=N(t_i)\), apply the synchronized congruence with
  \[
    q_i=M_iN_i^{r_i+1}.
  \]
  Set \(t_{i+1}=t_i+H(q_i)\). Strict increase and the congruence give
  \[
    N_{i+1}=N_i+c_iM_iN_i^{r_i+1}
           =N_i(1+c_iM_iN_i^{r_i})
  \]
  for an integer \(c_i\ge1\). This proves the first assertion. Under
  the last hypothesis, \(Q_i\equiv1\pmod p\) for every prime
  \(p\le B_i\), so none of those primes divides \(Q_i\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Induced full divisibility tree]
  \label{thm:unit-mcfl-divisibility-tree}
  Under the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:unit-mcfl-deep-congruence-chain}, there is an injection
  \[
    \Phi:\NN^{<\omega}\longrightarrow L
  \]
  such that
  \[
    \val_U(\Phi(\sigma))\mid\val_U(\Phi(\tau))
      \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sigma\preceq\tau,
  \]
  where \(\preceq\) denotes the prefix order. The quotients attached to
  distinct edges can be chosen pairwise coprime. Moreover, given an
  arbitrary prescribed threshold for each edge, every prime divisor of
  its quotient can be required to exceed that threshold.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Use the fixed strictly increasing orbit from
  \Cref{thm:mcfl-synchronized-congruence-orbit}. Enumerate the edges of
  \(\NN^{<\omega}\) so that each parent occurs before its children. Map
  the root to any orbit word of value \(R_0\ge2\). Suppose finitely many
  edges have been assigned, and consider a new edge from a parent of
  value \(P\). Let \(R\) be divisible by \(R_0\), by every previously
  assigned edge quotient, and by every prime not exceeding the prescribed
  threshold for the new edge. Apply the synchronized orbit congruence
  modulo \(P^2R\) at the parameter of the parent. A sufficiently large
  positive multiple of the return time gives an unused orbit word of
  value \(P'>P\) with
  \[
    P'\equiv P\pmod {P^2R}.
  \]
  Hence \(P'=PQ\), where \(Q>1\) and \(Q\equiv1\pmod {PR}\). In
  particular, \(Q\) is coprime to the root and to every earlier edge
  quotient, and no prime up to the prescribed threshold divides \(Q\).

  Recursion defines \(\Phi\) on every node. The value at a node is the
  root value times the product of the edge quotients along its path.
  Since the root and all distinct edge quotients are pairwise coprime,
  a node value divides another exactly when its path-edge set is an
  initial segment of the other path. This is precisely the prefix
  relation and also proves injectivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Prime immunity for recurrent numeration systems]
  \label{thm:recurrent-numeration-prime-cf-immunity}
  Let \((U,A,\mathcal R_U)\) satisfy \textup{(U1)--(U4)}, and put
  \[
    \mathcal P_U
      :=\{w\in\mathcal R_U:\val_U(w)\text{ is prime}\}.
  \]
  Then \(\mathcal P_U\) is MCF-immune, and therefore CF-immune,
  REG-immune, and not recognizable by a finite automaton over \(A\).

  If, in addition, \(\abs{a_0}=1\), then every infinite multiple
  context-free
  \(L\subseteq\mathcal R_U\) has unbounded
  \(\omega(\val_U(w))\). More precisely, \(L\) contains a
  coprime-quotient chain \(N_{i+1}=N_iQ_i\) with
  \(Q_i>1\) and \(Q_i\equiv1\pmod {N_i}\). Thus the bounded-\(\omega\),
  bounded-\(\Omega\), and finite-prime-support languages of a unit
  system are MCF-immune whenever they are infinite. Without the unit
  hypothesis, every bounded-\(\Omega\) language is still MCF-immune.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The MCF-immunity assertions follow from
  \Cref{thm:nonunit-mcfl-escape-dichotomy}, including its
  bounded-\(\Omega\) conclusion. In a unit system \(S\) is empty, so
  that theorem gives unbounded \(\omega\). Taking
  \(M_i=r_i=1\) in
  \Cref{thm:unit-mcfl-deep-congruence-chain} gives the stated chain.
  Regular languages are context-free and context-free languages are
  MCFLs. Finally, \textup{(U2)} and Euclid's theorem make
  \(\mathcal P_U\) infinite.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Linear numeration bases with Perron recurrence]
  \label{cor:linear-perron-prime-cf-immunity}
  Let \(U=(U_n)_{n\ge0}\) be a standard greedy linear numeration basis in
  the following precise sense: \(U_0=1\), the \(U_n\) are strictly
  increasing integers with bounded quotients, \(\mathcal R_U\) is the LSD-first
  reversal of the usual greedy representation language, and \(U\)
  has minimal tail recurrence polynomial equal to the minimal polynomial
  of a Perron number \(\beta\). Thus
  \(\beta>1\) is a real algebraic integer and every other conjugate
  \(\beta'\) satisfies \(\abs{\beta'}<\beta\); compare
  \cite{LindMarcus1995,Rigo2014Vol2}. Then its prime
  representation language is MCF-immune, hence CF-immune, REG-immune and
  nonrecognizable. The same statements hold in the usual MSD-first
  convention.

  If the dominant Perron number is a unit and the chosen recurrence has
  trailing coefficient \(a_0=\pm1\), then the full coprime-quotient and
  bounded-prime-support MCF-immunity conclusions of
  \Cref{thm:recurrent-numeration-prime-cf-immunity} hold.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Greedy expansion gives a unique representation of every positive
  integer, and its length-\(m\) words represent values between successive
  place-value thresholds; thus \textup{(U1)--(U3)} hold. The defining
  Perron recurrence gives \textup{(U4)}. Apply
  \Cref{thm:recurrent-numeration-prime-cf-immunity}. The immunity proof
  uses only \textup{(U1)--(U4)} and does not assume that the full greedy
  language is regular. In the Pisot subclass, Frougny's finite
  normalization theorem supplies a finite transducer, and the associated
  canonical syntax and Bertrand/substitution descriptions are standard
  \cite{Frougny1992,BruyereHansel1997,Fabre1995,FrougnySolomyak1992}.
  Finally, multiple context-free, context-free, and
  regular languages are closed under reversal, so changing between the LSD-first proof
  convention and the usual MSD-first convention preserves every
  conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Pisot and non-Pisot Perron systems]
  \label{ex:pisot-unit-nonunit}
  The Fibonacci recurrence has characteristic polynomial
  \(X^2-X-1\), the Pell recurrence has \(X^2-2X-1\), and the
  Tribonacci recurrence has \(X^3-X^2-X-1\). Their trailing recurrence
  coefficient is \(a_0=1\), so the iterated conclusion applies. In
  ordinary base \(b\), \(U_n=b^n\) and \(a_0=b\); prime MCF-immunity
  follows, recovering the classical conclusion, but the unit iteration
  is not being asserted. A genuinely nonintegral, nonunit Pisot example
  is the dominant root \(1+\sqrt3\) of \(X^2-2X-2\): the recurrence
  \(U_{n+2}=2U_{n+1}+2U_n\) has \(a_0=2\), and the action is singular
  modulo \(2\).  Nevertheless,
  \Cref{cor:linear-perron-bounded-support-classification} shows that every
  infinite MCFL in this nonintegral system acquires unbounded prime support
  away from \(2\); singularity of the ambient action does not produce a
  valuation-only escape language.

  The polynomial \(X^2-5X+5\) gives a genuinely non-Pisot instance. Its
  dominant root \(\beta=(5+\sqrt5)/2\) is Perron, while its other conjugate
  \((5-\sqrt5)/2\) is greater than one. The integer sequence
  \[
    U_0=1,\qquad U_1=4,\qquad
    U_{n+2}=5U_{n+1}-5U_n
  \]
  begins \(1,4,15,55,200,\ldots\) and is strictly increasing. Indeed,
  \[
    U_{n+1}>3U_n>0
  \]
  holds at \(n=0\), and if it holds at \(n\), then
  \[
    U_{n+2}=5(U_{n+1}-U_n)
      >\frac{10}{3}U_{n+1}>3U_{n+1}.
  \]
  Moreover, if \(3<U_{n+1}/U_n\le4\), then
  \[
    \frac{U_{n+2}}{U_{n+1}}
      =5-\frac{5U_n}{U_{n+1}}
      \le 5-\frac54<4,
  \]
  while the lower bound was just proved; starting from \(U_1/U_0=4\)
  therefore gives \(3<U_{n+1}/U_n\le4\) for every \(n\). Thus the quotients
  are bounded, so its greedy language is the greedy language of a linear
  numeration basis with Perron recurrence in the stated sense. The
  classification below implies that every infinite MCFL in this standard
  greedy language has unbounded prime support away from \(5\).
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}[Bounded support for Perron-recurrence numeration]
  \label{cor:linear-perron-bounded-support-classification}
  Let \(U\) be a greedy linear numeration basis in the precise sense of
  \Cref{cor:linear-perron-prime-cf-immunity}, let \(\beta\) be its dominant
  Perron number, and let \(S_{\mathrm r}\) be the prime divisors of the
  constant coefficient of its minimal tail recurrence.  The following are
  equivalent:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item there is an infinite MCFL \(L\subseteq\mathcal R_U\) such that
    \[
      \sup_{w\in L}\omega_{S_{\mathrm r}^c}(\val_U(w))<\infty;
    \]
    \item \(U\) admits a geometric synchronized scheme;
    \item \(\beta\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2}\).
  \end{enumerate}
  Consequently, if \(\beta\) is nonintegral, every infinite MCFL
  \(L\subseteq\mathcal R_U\) has unbounded prime support outside
  \(S_{\mathrm r}\).  For effective input as in
  \Cref{cor:bounded-outside-support-semidecidable}, the three equivalent
  properties are decidable in this Perron-recurrence class: the answer is positive
  exactly when the supplied minimal polynomial has degree one.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The equivalence of \textup{(i)} and \textup{(ii)} is
  \Cref{thm:bounded-outside-support-geometric-ray}.  Suppose that
  \textup{(ii)} holds, write
  \[
    \val_U(W(t))=cb^t,
    \qquad \abs{W(t)}=L+Dt,
  \]
  where \(D>0\) is the total pumped length.  In a greedy \(U\)-system, a
  canonical word of length \(m\) represents a value in the half-open interval
  \([U_{m-1},U_m)\).  Hence
  \[
    U_{L+Dt-1}\le cb^t<U_{L+Dt}.
  \]
  The standard solution of the recurrence defined by the minimal polynomial
  of the Perron number gives
  \[
    \lim_{n\to\infty}U_n^{1/n}=\beta.
  \]
  Indeed, the coefficient of the dominant root is nonzero because the
  displayed polynomial is the minimal tail recurrence, while every other
  conjugate has modulus strictly less than \(\beta\). Taking
  \(t\)-th roots in the preceding inequalities therefore yields
  \(b=\beta^D\).

  If \(\beta\) had degree greater than one, any other conjugate
  \(\beta'\) would satisfy \((\beta')^D=b\), and hence
  \(\abs{\beta'}=b^{1/D}=\beta\), contrary to the defining Perron
  inequality.  Thus \(\beta\) has degree one; being an algebraic integer
  greater than one, it belongs to \(\mathbb Z_{\ge2}\).  This proves
  \textup{(ii)}\(\Rightarrow\)\textup{(iii)}.

  Conversely, suppose \(\beta=B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2}\).  Its minimal
  polynomial is \(X-B\), so, after the minimal recurrence threshold
  \(J_{\mathrm r}\), one has \(U_{n+1}=BU_n\).  The greedy representation
  of \(U_n\), in the LSD-first convention, is \(0^n1\).  Taking
  \[
    a=0^{J_{\mathrm r}},\quad k=1,\quad
    u_1=w_1=s_1=\varepsilon,\quad v_1=0,\quad u_2=1
  \]
  gives canonical words \(W(t)=0^{J_{\mathrm r}+t}1\) and
  \[
    \val_U(W(t))=U_{J_{\mathrm r}}B^t.
  \]
  This is a geometric synchronized scheme, proving
  \textup{(iii)}\(\Rightarrow\)\textup{(ii)}.  The universal assertion
  for nonintegral \(\beta\) is the negation of \textup{(i)}.  Finally, a
  Perron minimal polynomial has degree one exactly when its root is an
  integer, which gives the stated terminating decision procedure.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact open interfaces]
  \label{rem:pisot-open-interfaces}
  The terminology for linear numeration systems with Perron or Pisot
  recurrence is not uniform.
  \Cref{cor:linear-perron-prime-cf-immunity} covers the standard
  positional linear \(U\)-systems just specified, including integer
  bases, Zeckendorf/Fibonacci, Pell, higher-order Pisot recurrences, and
  non-Pisot Perron examples such as \(X^2-5X+5\).
  It does not assert the theorem for arbitrary real-base
  \(\beta\)-shift representations, non-positional abstract numeration
  systems, redundant noninjective digit systems, or systems lacking an
  eventual integral recurrence and strict length ordering. Extending
  the argument to any of those classes requires a stationary finite
  block action that is invertible modulo represented primes.

  For a fixed nonunit recurrence witness, the ambient action can be
  singular modulo primes dividing \(a_0\), so the unrestricted unit-system
  divisibility tree need not follow. The witness-relative surviving
  statement is \Cref{thm:nonunit-mcfl-escape-dichotomy}: every infinite
  MCFL must acquire unbounded prime support outside that finite set or
  unbounded valuation at one of its primes. The invariant restricted
  action and the failure of arbitrary recurrence witnesses to classify
  escape behavior are distinguished in
  \Cref{prop:recurrence-witness-separation}.
  Within greedy linear numeration bases with Perron recurrence, the residual
  alternative is classified
  by \Cref{cor:linear-perron-bounded-support-classification}: it occurs exactly
  for an integral dominant root.  The ordinary-base example preceding
  \Cref{thm:unit-mcfl-deep-congruence-chain} shows that the valuation
  alternative cannot be removed.
\end{remark}
