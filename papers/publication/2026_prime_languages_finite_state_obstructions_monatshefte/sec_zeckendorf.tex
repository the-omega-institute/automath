\section{Context: Zeckendorf Growth}
\label{sec:zeckendorf}

This section specialises the density dichotomy to Zeckendorf words. We
record only the minimal classical facts needed later---in particular the
Zeckendorf growth law---and then combine them with prime-counting
estimates to obtain the quantitative obstructions promised in
\Cref{sec:automata}.

Let
\[
  \mathcal Z
  :=\{x_1\cdots x_m\in\{0,1\}^\ast:
      x_ix_{i+1}\neq 11,\ x_m=1\}
\]
be the language of canonical Zeckendorf words. We write these words
least-significant digit first: for \(w=x_1\cdots x_m\in\mathcal Z\),
\[
  \val(w):=\sum_{k=1}^m x_kF_{k+1},
\]
where \(F_0=0\), \(F_1=1\), \(F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n\), and
\(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). Thus \(x_m=1\) marks the most significant
nonzero digit. The map \(w\mapsto\val(w)\) is a bijection from
\(\mathcal Z\) onto \(\NN_{\ge 1}\) \cite{Zeckendorf1972}.
Consequently \(\mathcal Z\cap\{0,1\}^{\le m}\) parametrises the
interval \([1,F_{m+2})\), so words of length exactly \(m\) parametrise
\([F_{m+1},F_{m+2})\). Equivalently,
\(\abs{\mathcal Z_m}=F_m\) for every \(m\ge 1\).
We write
\[
  \mathcal Z_m:=\{w\in\mathcal Z:\abs{w}=m\}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}
  :=\{w\in\mathcal Z_m:\val(w)\text{ is prime}\},
\]
and set
\[
  \mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}
  :=\bigcup_{m\ge 1}\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Length growth for regular Zeckendorf sublanguages]
  \label{lem:zeckendorf-length-growth}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) be regular. Choose a DFA recognizing
  \(L\), delete states that are not reachable from the initial state or
  cannot reach an accepting state, and let \(\rho\) be the maximal
  spectral radius of a strongly connected component of the resulting
  automaton. Then \(\rho\le\varphi\). Moreover,
  \[
    \#\{w\in L:\abs w\le m\}=
    \begin{cases}
      \rho^{m+o(m)}, & \rho>1,\\
      \exp(o(m)), & \rho\le1.
    \end{cases}
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Since \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\), the number of words of length at most
  \(m\) in \(L\) is bounded above by
  \[
    \#(\mathcal Z\cap\{0,1\}^{\le m})=F_{m+2}-1=\varphi^{m+o(m)}.
  \]
  For any trimmed strongly connected component \(C\) of spectral radius
  \(\rho_C\), choose entry and exit paths through the trimmed automaton.
  Periodic Perron--Frobenius theory gives a residue class of lengths on
  which the number of paths that traverse \(C\) is
  \(\rho_C^{n+o(n)}\). These paths yield distinct accepted words after
  adding the fixed entry and exit words. The preceding ambient upper
  bound therefore forces \(\rho_C\le\varphi\) for every component, and
  hence \(\rho\le\varphi\).

  Put the trimmed adjacency matrix in block upper-triangular form by
  strongly connected components. The upper bound
  \(O(m^N\rho^m)\), with \(N\) depending only on the number of blocks,
  follows from the usual path decomposition through this acyclic block
  graph and the Jordan-form estimates for the diagonal blocks. If
  \(\rho>1\), choose a reachable and co-reachable component of spectral
  radius \(\rho\); the same periodic Perron--Frobenius lower bound along
  one admissible residue class gives \(\rho^{m+o(m)}\) words of length at
  most \(m\). If \(\rho\le1\), every non-negative integral irreducible
  recurrent block has spectral radius at most \(1\), hence is either
  nilpotent in the acyclic part or a permutation block on a directed
  cycle. A path can pass through only finitely many such blocks, so the
  total number of accepted words of length at most \(m\) is polynomial in
  \(m\), and therefore \(\exp(o(m))\). This proves the displayed
  estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Growth law for regular Zeckendorf languages]
  \label{prop:zeckendorf-regular-powerlaw}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) be regular. Choose a DFA recognizing
  \(L\), delete states that are not reachable from the initial state or
  cannot reach an accepting state, and let \(\rho\) be the maximal
  spectral radius of a strongly connected component of the resulting
  automaton. Define
  \[
    N_L(T):=\#\{w\in L:\val(w)\le T\}.
  \]
  If \(L=\varnothing\), then \(N_L(T)=0\) for all \(T\). Otherwise
  \[
    N_L(T)=T^{\alpha+o(1)},
    \qquad
    \alpha=
    \begin{cases}
      0, & \rho\le 1,\\[0.3em]
      \dfrac{\log \rho}{\log \varphi}, & \rho>1.
    \end{cases}
    \qquad (T\to\infty).
  \]
  In the endpoint case \(\rho\le1\) this assertion means only
  \(N_L(T)=T^{o(1)}\); no sharper polynomial asymptotic is asserted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  This is the Zeckendorf specialization of the general growth theory for
  \(S\)-recognizable sets \cite[Theorem~7]{CharlierRampersad2011}; see
  also \cite{LecomteRigo2001}. In the present setting the estimate can
  be read directly from
  \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-length-growth}, as follows. In the present
  numeration system the ambient counting function satisfies
  \[
    \#(\mathcal Z\cap\{0,1\}^{\le m})
    =F_{m+2}-1
    =\varphi^{m+o(m)},
  \]
  and, conversely, \(F_{m+2}=\varphi^{m+O(1)}\). Hence
  \(\val(w)\le T\) is equivalent, up to an \(O(1)\) error in length, to
  \(\abs w\le (\log T)/(\log\varphi)\).

  By \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-length-growth},
  the number of accepted words of length at most \(m\) is
  \(\rho^{m+o(m)}\) if \(\rho>1\), and \(\exp(o(m))\) if \(\rho\le1\).
  Substituting \(m=(\log T)/(\log\varphi)+O(1)\) gives exactly
  \(N_L(T)=T^{\alpha+o(1)}\), with \(\alpha=0\) for \(\rho\le1\) and
  \(\alpha=\log\rho/\log\varphi\) for \(\rho>1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:zeckendorf-examples}
  The endpoint cases and the borderline case \(\rho=1\) already occur
  for simple regular sublanguages.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item For \(L=\mathcal Z\), one has \(N_L(T)=\lfloor T\rfloor\),
    \(\rho=\varphi\), and \(\alpha=1\).
    \item If \(L\) is finite, then \(N_L(T)\) is eventually constant,
    so \(\alpha=0\).
    \item For \(L=0^\ast1\subseteq\mathcal Z\), one has \(a_m=1\) for
    every \(m\), hence \(\rho=1\) and
    \(N_L(T)\asymp \log T=T^{o(1)}\).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  The same transfer-matrix argument applies more generally to Parry
  numeration systems whose admissible digit language is regular. In that
  setting the regular syntax of admissible expansions replaces
  \(\mathcal Z\), and the dominant exponential growth constant of the
  numeration system replaces \(\varphi\); see
  \cite{Frougny1992,BruyereHansel1997,LecomteRigo2001,Rigo2014Vol1,Rigo2014Vol2}.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Prime counts in Zeckendorf slices]
  \label{lem:zeck-prime-count}
  Let \(X_0,K_1,K_2\) be constants satisfying
  \Cref{lem:explicit-pnt-input}. There are explicit constants
  \(A_{\mathrm Z},B_{\mathrm Z}>0\) and an explicit integer \(M_{\mathrm Z}\)
  such that for all \(m\ge M_{\mathrm Z}\),
  \[
    A_{\mathrm Z}\,\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
    \le \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    \le B_{\mathrm Z}\,\frac{\varphi^m}{m}.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Choose \(M_0\) such that \(F_{m+1}\ge X_0\) and
  \[
    \frac12\le
    \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{m\log\varphi},
    \frac{\log F_{m+1}}{m\log\varphi}
    \le2,
    \qquad
    \frac{F_{m+1}}{F_{m+2}}
      \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+1}}
    \le\frac{1+\varphi^{-1}}2
  \]
  for every \(m\ge M_0\). Such an \(M_0\) is found effectively from
  Binet's formula. Put
  \[
    \Delta_m:=
    1-\frac{F_{m+1}}{F_{m+2}}
      \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+1}}.
  \]
  Then \(\Delta_m\ge(1-\varphi^{-1})/2\) for \(m\ge M_0\). Enlarge
  \(M_0\) to \(M_{\mathrm Z}\) so that, for all \(m\ge M_{\mathrm Z}\),
  \[
    K_1\left(
      \frac{F_{m+2}}{(\log F_{m+2})^2}
      +\frac{F_{m+1}}{(\log F_{m+1})^2}\right)+2
    \le
    \frac12\,\frac{F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+2}}\Delta_m .
  \]
  This is again an effective finite search, since the left-hand side is
  \(O(\varphi^m/m^2)\) while the right-hand side is
  \(\gg\varphi^m/m\).
  Every word in \(\mathcal Z_m\) represents an integer in
  \([F_{m+1},F_{m+2})\), so
  \[
    \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    =\pi(F_{m+2}-1)-\pi(F_{m+1}-1)
    =\pi(F_{m+2})-\pi(F_{m+1})+O(1),
  \]
  with \(\pi(x)=\#\{p\le x:p\text{ prime}\}\). For
  \(m\ge M_{\mathrm Z}\), \Cref{lem:explicit-pnt-input} gives
  \[
    \pi(F_{m+2})-\pi(F_{m+1})
    \ge
    \frac{F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+2}}-\frac{F_{m+1}}{\log F_{m+1}}
    -K_1\frac{F_{m+2}}{(\log F_{m+2})^2}
    -K_1\frac{F_{m+1}}{(\log F_{m+1})^2}.
  \]
  By the definition of \(M_{\mathrm Z}\), after absorbing the endpoint
  \(O(1)\),
  \[
    \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    \ge \frac12\,\frac{F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+2}}\Delta_m .
  \]
  Binet's formula gives
  \(F_{m+j}=\varphi^{m+j}/\sqrt5+O(\varphi^{-m})\) for \(j=1,2\), and
  hence
  \[
    C_1\varphi^m\le F_{m+j}\le C_2\varphi^m,\qquad
    C_3m\le \log F_{m+j}\le C_4m
  \]
  for all sufficiently large \(m\), with suitable positive constants
  \(C_1,\dots,C_4\). Also
  \(\Delta_m\ge(1-\varphi^{-1})/2\) by construction. Therefore one may
  take, after enlarging \(M_{\mathrm Z}\) if needed,
  \[
    A_{\mathrm Z}:=\frac{C_1(1-\varphi^{-1})}{8C_4}.
  \]
  For the upper bound,
  \[
    \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}\le \pi(F_{m+2})
      \le K_2\frac{F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+2}}
      \le \frac{K_2C_2}{C_3}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}.
  \]
  Thus \(B_{\mathrm Z}:=K_2C_2/C_3\) is admissible for every
  \(m\ge M_{\mathrm Z}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Canonical Zeckendorf presentation]
  \label{lem:zeckendorf-canonical-presentation}
  The language \(\mathcal Z\) is accepted by the two-state trim
  strongly connected deterministic presentation \(\mathcal N_{\mathrm Z}\)
  with state set \(\{0,1\}\), initial state \(0\), accepting state
  \(\{1\}\), and transitions
  \[
    0\xrightarrow{0}0,\qquad
    0\xrightarrow{1}1,\qquad
    1\xrightarrow{0}0.
  \]
  Its adjacency matrix is
  \[
    M_{\mathrm Z}=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      1&1\\
      1&0
    \end{pmatrix},
  \]
  its Perron root is \(\varphi\), and its graph period is \(1\). Hence
  \(\mathcal N_{\mathrm Z}\) satisfies the hypotheses of
  \Cref{thm:parry-density} with \(\lambda=\varphi\), and
  \(z_m=\abs{\mathcal Z_m}=F_m\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  A path in the displayed graph records exactly a binary word with no
  adjacent \(1\)'s: after reading a \(1\) the only available next digit
  is \(0\), while from state \(0\) both digits are available. The
  accepting state is \(1\), so the accepted words are precisely those
  no-\(11\) words whose terminal digit is \(1\), namely
  \(\mathcal Z\). The state \(0\) is initial and lies on the loop
  labelled \(0\), while \(0\xrightarrow{1}1\xrightarrow{0}0\); therefore
  both states are reachable from the initial state and can reach the
  accepting state, and the underlying directed graph is strongly
  connected. The loop at \(0\) gives period \(1\).

  The characteristic polynomial of \(M_{\mathrm Z}\) is
  \(t^2-t-1\). Its spectral radius is the positive root
  \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2>1\). Finally the accepted length-\(m\) words
  are the canonical Zeckendorf words of length \(m\), whose number is
  \(F_m\) by the standard Fibonacci recurrence with initial values
  \(F_1=F_2=1\).
\end{proof}

For a deterministic automaton \(\mathcal A\) over the Zeckendorf digit
alphabet, write
\[
  a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)
  :=\abs{L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal Z_m}.
\]

\begin{corollary}[Zeckendorf symmetric-difference obstruction]
  \label{cor:zeck-symmetric-diff}
  Let \(\mathcal A\) be a deterministic automaton over the Zeckendorf
  alphabet. Then there exists \(c_{\mathcal A,\varphi}>0\) such that
  \[
    \abs{(L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal Z_m)
         \triangle\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    \ge c_{\mathcal A,\varphi}\,\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
  \]
  for all sufficiently large \(m\). If
  \(a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)\) lies in the positive-density
  alternative of \Cref{thm:parry-density}, then along the corresponding
  arithmetic progression of lengths one has the stronger bound
  \(\abs{(L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal Z_m)\triangle\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
  \gg \varphi^m\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Apply \Cref{cor:relative-finite-budget-obstruction} with
  \[
    \mathcal N=\mathcal N_{\mathrm Z},\qquad
    \lambda=\varphi,\qquad
    z_m=F_m,\qquad
    T_m=\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}.
  \]
  The presentation hypotheses and \(z_m=F_m\) are supplied by
  \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-canonical-presentation}; the finite-budget
  hypothesis
  \[
    A_{\mathrm Z}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
    \le \abs{T_m}
    \le B_{\mathrm Z}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
  \]
  is exactly \Cref{lem:zeck-prime-count}. The corollary therefore gives
  the all-large-\(m\) symmetric-difference bound. Its positive-density
  residue-class clause gives the stronger \(\Omega(\varphi^m)\) bound
  on every residue class where the limiting relative density of
  \(L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal Z_m\) inside \(\mathcal Z_m\) is positive.
\end{proof}

Define recall and precision for Zeckendorf slices by
\[
  \mathrm{Rec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}
  :=\frac{\abs{L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
          {\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{Prec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}
  :=\frac{\abs{L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
          {a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)}
\]
whenever both \(\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}>0\) and
\(a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)>0\).

\begin{corollary}[No simultaneous recall and precision in Zeckendorf]
  \label{cor:zeck-recall-precision}
  Let \(\mathcal A\) be a deterministic automaton over the Zeckendorf
  alphabet. Let \(p\), the constants \(c_0,\dots,c_{p-1}\), and
  \(\theta\) be supplied by
  \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-canonical-presentation,thm:parry-density}, so that
  \[
    \frac{a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)}{F_m}
    =c_{m\bmod p}+O(\theta^m)
  \]
  for all sufficiently large \(m\). Let \(B_{\mathrm Z}\) be the upper
  estimate constant from \Cref{lem:zeck-prime-count}. For each residue
  class \(r\bmod p\), exactly one of the following alternatives holds:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
    \item If \(c_r=0\), then
    \(\mathrm{Rec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\) decays exponentially along
    \(m\equiv r\pmod p\).
    \item If \(c_r>0\), then there is a constant
    \(C_{r,\varphi}>0\), for instance
    \(C_{r,\varphi}=2C_\varphi B_{\mathrm Z}/c_r\) with
    \(F_m\ge C_\varphi^{-1}\varphi^m\) for all large \(m\), such that
    \[
      \mathrm{Prec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\le \frac{C_{r,\varphi}}{m}
    \]
    for all sufficiently large \(m\equiv r\pmod p\).
  \end{enumerate}
  Consequently every deterministic automaton satisfies one of the
  following explicitly global alternatives:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item \(\mathrm{Rec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\) decays exponentially along
    every residue class of lengths.
    \item There exists at least one residue class \(r\bmod p\) with
    \(c_r>0\) such that
    \(\mathrm{Prec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\le C_{\mathcal A,\varphi}/m\) for
    all sufficiently large \(m\equiv r\pmod p\) for which precision is
    defined.
  \end{enumerate}
  Precision is left undefined on lengths with
  \(a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)=0\); the statement makes no uniform
  precision assertion on residue classes other than those covered by
  the per-residue alternative.
  Consequently no fixed DFA has both recall and precision bounded below
  on infinitely many Zeckendorf lengths.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Fix \(A_{\mathrm Z},B_{\mathrm Z}>0\) as in
  \Cref{lem:zeck-prime-count}; thus
  \[
    A_{\mathrm Z}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
    \le \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    \le B_{\mathrm Z}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
  \]
  for all sufficiently large \(m\). Also fix constants
  \(K_1,K_2>0\) such that
  \(K_1\varphi^m\le F_m\le K_2\varphi^m\) for all large \(m\).

  Treat each residue class \(r\bmod p\) separately. If \(c_r=0\), then
  along \(m\equiv r\pmod p\),
  \(a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)=O(\theta^mF_m)\). Hence
  \[
    \mathrm{Rec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}
    \le
    \frac{a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)}
         {\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
    \ll m\theta^m.
  \]
  Since \(m\theta^m=O(\theta_r^m)\) for some \(\theta_r\in(0,1)\),
  recall decays exponentially on this residue class.

  If \(c_r>0\), then for all sufficiently large \(m\equiv r\pmod p\),
  \[
    a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)\ge \frac{c_r}{2}F_m
    \ge \frac{c_rK_1}{2}\varphi^m.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    \mathrm{Prec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}
    \le
    \frac{\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
         {a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)}
    \le
    \frac{2B_{\mathrm Z}}{c_rK_1}\cdot\frac{1}{m}.
  \]
  This proves the stated per-residue alternative, with
  \(C_{r,\varphi}=2B_{\mathrm Z}/(c_rK_1)\).

  If all \(c_r\) vanish, the first alternative holds on every residue
  class, hence globally. Otherwise choose a residue class with
  \(c_r>0\); the second alternative holds on defined lengths in that
  arithmetic progression, and one may take
  \(C_{\mathcal A,\varphi}=C_{r,\varphi}\).

  Finally suppose that both recall and precision were bounded below by
  some \(\delta>0\) on infinitely many Zeckendorf lengths. By the
  pigeonhole principle, infinitely many of those lengths lie in one
  residue class \(r\bmod p\). The case \(c_r=0\) contradicts the
  exponential recall decay above. The case \(c_r>0\) contradicts the
  bound \(\mathrm{Prec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\le C_{r,\varphi}/m\) along that
  same residue class.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Regular Zeckendorf slices exclude the prime scale]
  \label{prop:zeckendorf-regular-slice-rigidity}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) be a regular language, and put
  \[
    \ell_m:=\abs{L\cap\mathcal Z_m}.
  \]
  Then there are an integer \(p\ge1\), non-negative constants
  \(c_0,\dots,c_{p-1}\), and \(\theta\in(0,1)\) such that
  \[
    \frac{\ell_m}{F_m}=c_{m\bmod p}+O(\theta^m).
  \]
  Consequently, on each residue class modulo \(p\), the ratio
  \[
    \frac{\ell_m}{\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
  \]
  either tends to \(0\) exponentially up to the harmless polynomial
  factor \(m\), or tends to \(+\infty\) at least linearly in \(m\).
  In particular no regular sublanguage of \(\mathcal Z\) has
  \(\ell_m=\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}\) for all sufficiently
  large \(m\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Let \(\mathcal A\) be a deterministic automaton recognizing \(L\).
  Since \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\), one has
  \[
    \ell_m=\abs{L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal Z_m}
          =a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A).
  \]
  By \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-canonical-presentation}, the canonical
  Zeckendorf presentation is trim, deterministic, strongly connected,
  has Perron root \(\varphi\), and satisfies
  \(z_m=\abs{\mathcal Z_m}=F_m\). Applying
  \Cref{thm:parry-density} to this presentation and to
  \(\mathcal A\) gives the displayed expansion.

  Fix a residue class \(r\bmod p\). By \Cref{lem:zeck-prime-count}
  there are constants \(A_{\mathrm Z},B_{\mathrm Z}>0\) such that
  \[
    A_{\mathrm Z}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
    \le\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    \le B_{\mathrm Z}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
  \]
  for all sufficiently large \(m\). Also fix \(K_1,K_2>0\) such that
  \(K_1\varphi^m\le F_m\le K_2\varphi^m\) for all large \(m\).

  If \(c_r=0\), then along \(m\equiv r\pmod p\) we have
  \[
    \ell_m=O(\theta^mF_m)=O(\theta^m\varphi^m).
  \]
  Hence
  \[
    \frac{\ell_m}{\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
    =O(m\theta^m),
  \]
  which tends to \(0\) exponentially after increasing \(\theta\) within
  \((0,1)\).

  If \(c_r>0\), then for all sufficiently large
  \(m\equiv r\pmod p\),
  \[
    \ell_m\ge\frac{c_r}{2}F_m
        \ge\frac{c_rK_1}{2}\varphi^m.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    \frac{\ell_m}{\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
    \ge
    \frac{c_rK_1}{2B_{\mathrm Z}}\,m,
  \]
  and the ratio tends to \(+\infty\) at least linearly on this residue
  class.

  Every residue class falls into exactly one of these two cases. Thus
  equality
  \(\ell_m=\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}\) cannot hold for all
  sufficiently large \(m\), since any fixed residue class contains
  arbitrarily large lengths and on that class the ratio cannot remain
  equal to \(1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Prime Zeckendorf language is not regular]
  \label{cor:zeckendorf-prime-language-not-regular}
  Let
  \[
    \mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}
    :=\{\mathrm{Zeck}(p)\in\mathcal Z:p\text{ prime}\},
  \]
  where \(\mathrm{Zeck}(n)\) denotes the canonical Zeckendorf
  representation of \(n\). Then \(\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\) is not a
  regular language.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Suppose that \(\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\) were regular. Applying
  \Cref{prop:zeckendorf-regular-slice-rigidity} to
  \(L=\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\) gives a period \(p\) such that on every
  residue class modulo \(p\) the ratio
  \[
    \frac{\abs{\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\cap\mathcal Z_m}}
         {\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
  \]
  either tends to \(0\) or tends to \(+\infty\). But by definition
  \(\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\cap\mathcal Z_m=\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\)
  for every \(m\). The displayed ratio is therefore identically \(1\),
  a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sofic corollary]
  \label{rem:zeckendorf-primes-not-sofic}
  The preceding non-regularity statement excludes eventual sofic
  realization of the prime Zeckendorf length slices by the following
  short argument.
  Let \(\mathcal B_m(X)\) denote the admissible words of length
  \(m\) in a sofic shift \(X\). If
  \(\mathcal B_m(X)=\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\) for all \(m\ge N\),
  then
  \[
    \mathcal B(X)\triangle\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\subseteq \{0,1\}^{<N},
  \]
  a finite set. Since \(\mathcal B(X)\) is regular
  (\cite[Theorem~3.2.1]{LindMarcus1995}; see also
  \cite[Chapter~4]{PerrinPin2004}), this would make
  \(\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\) a finite symmetric difference away from a
  regular language, hence regular itself, contradicting
  \Cref{cor:zeckendorf-prime-language-not-regular}.
\end{remark}
