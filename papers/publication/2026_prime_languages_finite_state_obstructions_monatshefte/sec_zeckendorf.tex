\section{Context: Zeckendorf Growth}
\label{sec:zeckendorf}

This section specialises the density dichotomy to Zeckendorf words. We
record only the minimal classical facts needed later---in particular the
Zeckendorf growth law---and then combine them with prime-counting
estimates to obtain the quantitative obstructions promised in
\suppref{Section~1}.

Let
\[
  \mathcal Z
  :=\{x_1\cdots x_m\in\{0,1\}^\ast:
      x_ix_{i+1}\neq 11,\ x_m=1\}
\]
be the language of canonical Zeckendorf words. We write these words
least-significant digit first: for \(w=x_1\cdots x_m\in\mathcal Z\),
\[
  \val(w):=\sum_{k=1}^m x_kF_{k+1},
\]
where \(F_0=0\), \(F_1=1\), \(F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n\), and
\(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). Thus \(x_m=1\) marks the most significant
nonzero digit. The map \(w\mapsto\val(w)\) is a bijection from
\(\mathcal Z\) onto \(\NN_{\ge 1}\) \cite{Zeckendorf1972}.
Consequently \(\mathcal Z\cap\{0,1\}^{\le m}\) parametrises the
interval \([1,F_{m+2})\), so words of length exactly \(m\) parametrise
\([F_{m+1},F_{m+2})\). Equivalently,
\(\abs{\mathcal Z_m}=F_m\) for every \(m\ge 1\).
We write
\[
  \mathcal Z_m:=\{w\in\mathcal Z:\abs{w}=m\}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}
  :=\{w\in\mathcal Z_m:\val(w)\text{ is prime}\},
\]
and set
\[
  \mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}
  :=\bigcup_{m\ge 1}\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Length growth for regular Zeckendorf sublanguages]
  \label{lem:zeckendorf-length-growth}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) be regular. Choose a DFA recognizing
  \(L\), delete states that are not reachable from the initial state or
  cannot reach an accepting state, and let \(\rho\) be the maximal
  spectral radius of a strongly connected component of the resulting
  automaton. Then \(\rho\le\varphi\). Moreover,
  \[
    \#\{w\in L:\abs w\le m\}=
    \begin{cases}
      \rho^{m+o(m)}, & \rho>1,\\
      \exp(o(m)), & \rho\le1.
    \end{cases}
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Since \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\), the number of words of length at most
  \(m\) in \(L\) is bounded above by
  \[
    \#(\mathcal Z\cap\{0,1\}^{\le m})=F_{m+2}-1=\varphi^{m+o(m)}.
  \]
  For any trimmed strongly connected component \(C\) of spectral radius
  \(\rho_C\), choose entry and exit paths through the trimmed automaton.
  Periodic Perron--Frobenius theory gives a residue class of lengths on
  which the number of paths that traverse \(C\) is
  \(\rho_C^{n+o(n)}\). These paths yield distinct accepted words after
  adding the fixed entry and exit words. The preceding ambient upper
  bound therefore forces \(\rho_C\le\varphi\) for every component, and
  hence \(\rho\le\varphi\).

  Put the trimmed adjacency matrix in block upper-triangular form by
  strongly connected components. The upper bound
  \(O(m^N\rho^m)\), with \(N\) depending only on the number of blocks,
  follows from the usual path decomposition through this acyclic block
  graph and standard finite-dimensional matrix-power estimates for the
  diagonal blocks. If
  \(\rho>1\), choose a reachable and co-reachable component of spectral
  radius \(\rho\); the same periodic Perron--Frobenius lower bound along
  one admissible residue class gives \(\rho^{m+o(m)}\) words of length at
  most \(m\). If \(\rho\le1\), every non-negative integral irreducible
  recurrent block has spectral radius at most \(1\), hence is either
  nilpotent in the acyclic part or a permutation block on a directed
  cycle. A path can pass through only finitely many such blocks, so the
  total number of accepted words of length at most \(m\) is polynomial in
  \(m\), and therefore \(\exp(o(m))\). This proves the displayed
  estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Growth law for regular Zeckendorf languages]
  \label{prop:zeckendorf-regular-powerlaw}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) be regular. Choose a DFA recognizing
  \(L\), delete states that are not reachable from the initial state or
  cannot reach an accepting state, and let \(\rho\) be the maximal
  spectral radius of a strongly connected component of the resulting
  automaton. Define
  \[
    N_L(T):=\#\{w\in L:\val(w)\le T\}.
  \]
  If \(L=\varnothing\), then \(N_L(T)=0\) for all \(T\). Otherwise
  \[
    N_L(T)=T^{\alpha+o(1)},
    \qquad
    \alpha=
    \begin{cases}
      0, & \rho\le 1,\\[0.3em]
      \dfrac{\log \rho}{\log \varphi}, & \rho>1.
    \end{cases}
    \qquad (T\to\infty).
  \]
  In the endpoint case \(\rho\le1\) this assertion means only
  \(N_L(T)=T^{o(1)}\); no sharper polynomial asymptotic is asserted.
  This is a transfer-matrix restatement for the LSD-first Zeckendorf
  convention fixed at the start of the section, not a new
  recognizability theorem for abstract numeration systems.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We use \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-length-growth}. The general
  \(S\)-recognizable-set theorem of
  \cite[Theorem~7]{CharlierRampersad2011} gives the same conclusion. In
  the present system the ambient count satisfies
  \[
    \#(\mathcal Z\cap\{0,1\}^{\le m})
    =F_{m+2}-1
    =\varphi^{m+o(m)},
  \]
  and, conversely, \(F_{m+2}=\varphi^{m+O(1)}\). Hence
  \(\val(w)\le T\) is equivalent, up to an \(O(1)\) error in length, to
  \(\abs w\le (\log T)/(\log\varphi)\).

  By \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-length-growth},
  the number of accepted words of length at most \(m\) is
  \(\rho^{m+o(m)}\) if \(\rho>1\), and \(\exp(o(m))\) if \(\rho\le1\).
  Substituting \(m=(\log T)/(\log\varphi)+O(1)\) gives exactly
  \(N_L(T)=T^{\alpha+o(1)}\), with \(\alpha=0\) for \(\rho\le1\) and
  \(\alpha=\log\rho/\log\varphi\) for \(\rho>1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:zeckendorf-examples}
  The endpoint cases and the borderline case \(\rho=1\) already occur
  for simple regular sublanguages.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item For \(L=\mathcal Z\), one has \(N_L(T)=\lfloor T\rfloor\),
    \(\rho=\varphi\), and \(\alpha=1\).
    \item If \(L\) is finite, then \(N_L(T)\) is eventually constant,
    so \(\alpha=0\).
    \item For \(L=0^\ast1\subseteq\mathcal Z\), one has \(a_m=1\) for
    every \(m\), hence \(\rho=1\) and
    \(N_L(T)\asymp \log T=T^{o(1)}\).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  The same transfer-matrix argument applies more generally to Parry
  numeration systems whose admissible digit language is regular. In that
  setting the regular syntax of admissible expansions replaces
  \(\mathcal Z\), and the dominant exponential growth constant of the
  numeration system replaces \(\varphi\); see
  \cite{Frougny1992,BruyereHansel1997,LecomteRigo2001,Rigo2014Vol1,Rigo2014Vol2}.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Prime counts in Zeckendorf slices]
  \label{lem:zeck-prime-count}
  Let \(X_0,C_{\pi,1},C_{\pi,2}\) be constants satisfying
  \suppref{Lemma~1.3}. There are explicit constants
  \(A_{\mathrm Z},B_{\mathrm Z}>0\) and an explicit integer \(M_{\mathrm Z}\)
  such that for all \(m\ge M_{\mathrm Z}\),
  \[
    A_{\mathrm Z}\,\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
    \le \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    \le B_{\mathrm Z}\,\frac{\varphi^m}{m}.
  \]
  Equivalently, for all sufficiently large \(m\), the prime count in
  the exact Zeckendorf slice \([F_{m+1},F_{m+2})\) is between
  \(c_1F_m/m\) and \(c_2F_m/m\) for suitable constants
  \(c_1,c_2>0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Binet's formula gives
  \[
    F_n=\frac{\varphi^n-(-\varphi)^{-n}}{\sqrt5}.
  \]
  For every \(n\ge2\),
  \[
    \frac{\varphi^n-1}{\sqrt5}\le F_n\le
    \frac{\varphi^n+1}{\sqrt5}\le \varphi^n.
  \]
  Hence, for \(j=1,2\) and \(m\ge1\),
  \[
    c_F\varphi^m\le F_{m+j}\le C_F\varphi^m,\qquad
    c_F:=\frac{\varphi^2-1}{\sqrt5\varphi},\quad
    C_F:=\varphi^2.
  \]
  Define
  \[
    c_{\log}:=\frac12\log\varphi,\qquad C_{\log}:=3\log\varphi,
  \]
  and let \(M_{\log}\) be produced by the following finite calculation.
  Increase \(M\ge2\) until, for all \(m\ge M\) and \(j=1,2\),
  \[
    c_{\log}m\le \log F_{m+j}\le C_{\log}m.
  \]
  This is reproducible from the preceding Binet bounds: the two
  displayed inequalities are guaranteed once the linear inequalities
  \(\log c_F+m\log\varphi\ge c_{\log}m\) and
  \(\log C_F+m\log\varphi\le C_{\log}m\) hold, so it is enough to test
  the resulting explicit lower bounds for \(m\).

  Choose \(M_R\ge M_{\log}\) by the following explicit Binet
  majorization. No monotonicity input is needed for the exact sequence
  \[
    R_m
    :=
    \frac{F_{m+1}}{F_{m+2}}\,
    \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+1}}.
  \]
  Write
  \[
    F_n=\frac{\varphi^n}{\sqrt5}(1-\varepsilon_n),
    \qquad
    \varepsilon_n:=(-1)^n\varphi^{-2n}.
  \]
  For \(m\ge1\),
  \[
    \frac{F_{m+1}}{F_{m+2}}
    =
    \varphi^{-1}
    \frac{1-\varepsilon_{m+1}}{1-\varepsilon_{m+2}}
    \le
    \varphi^{-1}
    \frac{1+\varphi^{-2m-2}}{1-\varphi^{-2m-4}},
  \]
  and, once \(\varphi^{-2m-2}\le1/2\),
  \[
    \log F_{m+1}
    \ge
    (m+1)\log\varphi-\tfrac12\log5-2\varphi^{-2m-2}.
  \]
  Also \(F_{m+2}/F_{m+1}\le3\) for \(m\ge1\), so
  \[
    \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+1}}
    =
    1+\frac{\log(F_{m+2}/F_{m+1})}{\log F_{m+1}}
    \le
    1+
    \frac{\log3}
    {(m+1)\log\varphi-\tfrac12\log5-2\varphi^{-2m-2}},
  \]
  whenever the denominator is positive. The two error functions
  \(\varphi^{-2m-2}\) and
  \(((m+1)\log\varphi-\tfrac12\log5-2\varphi^{-2m-2})^{-1}\) are bounded
  above, for all \(m\ge M_{\log}\), by explicit decreasing rational
  majorants obtained from rational lower and upper intervals for
  \(\varphi\) and \(\log\varphi\). Consequently the product of the last
  two displayed upper bounds is itself bounded by a decreasing explicit
  function tending to \(\varphi^{-1}\). A finite rational search on this
  majorant, using rational intervals for \(\varphi,\log\varphi,\log3\),
  produces \(M_R\) such that for every \(m\ge M_R\)
  \[
    \frac{F_{m+1}}{F_{m+2}}
      \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+1}}
    \le\frac{1+\varphi^{-1}}2 .
  \]
  The certificate therefore verifies a single explicit Binet upper bound
  that is valid for all \(m\ge M_R\), rather than a monotone-tail
  assertion for \(R_m\) itself.

  Choose \(M_0\ge M_R\) by increasing \(M\) until \(F_{m+1}\ge X_0\)
  and
  \[
    \frac12\le
    \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{m\log\varphi},
    \frac{\log F_{m+1}}{m\log\varphi}
    \le2,
  \]
  for every \(m\ge M_0\). The logarithmic ratio conditions are checked
  from the same linear Binet bounds, while the last displayed
  product inequality is already certified by the choice of \(M_R\). Put
  \[
    \Delta_m:=
    1-\frac{F_{m+1}}{F_{m+2}}
      \frac{\log F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+1}}.
  \]
  Then \(\Delta_m\ge(1-\varphi^{-1})/2\) for \(m\ge M_0\). Enlarge
  \(M_0\) to \(M_{\mathrm Z}\) so that, for all \(m\ge M_{\mathrm Z}\),
  \[
    C_{\pi,1}\left(
      \frac{F_{m+2}}{(\log F_{m+2})^2}
      +\frac{F_{m+1}}{(\log F_{m+1})^2}\right)+2
    \le
    \frac12\,\frac{F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+2}}\Delta_m .
  \]
  This last enlargement is also finite and reproducible. Using the
  certified bounds \(c_F\varphi^m\le F_{m+j}\le C_F\varphi^m\) and
  \(c_{\log}m\le\log F_{m+j}\le C_{\log}m\), the left-hand side is
  bounded above by an explicit multiple of \(\varphi^m/m^2\) plus \(2\),
  while the right-hand side is bounded below by an explicit multiple of
  \(\varphi^m/m\). Once that symbolic majorization verifies the tail,
  the finite prefix is checked by exact Fibonacci integers and real
  interval arithmetic for the logarithms. This is the certificate for
  \(M_{\mathrm Z}\).
  Every word in \(\mathcal Z_m\) represents an integer in
  \([F_{m+1},F_{m+2})\), so
  \[
    \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    =\pi(F_{m+2}-1)-\pi(F_{m+1}-1)
  \]
  and the interval-count convention following
  \suppref{Lemma~1.3} gives the endpoint correction
  \[
    \left|
      \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}-
      \bigl(\pi(F_{m+2})-\pi(F_{m+1})\bigr)
    \right|\le 2.
  \]
  Here \(\pi(x)=\#\{p\le x:p\text{ prime}\}\). For
  \(m\ge M_{\mathrm Z}\), \suppref{Lemma~1.3} gives
  \[
    \begin{aligned}
    \pi(F_{m+2})-\pi(F_{m+1})
    \ge
    &\frac{F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+2}}
      -\frac{F_{m+1}}{\log F_{m+1}}\\
    &{}-C_{\pi,1}\frac{F_{m+2}}{(\log F_{m+2})^2}
      -C_{\pi,1}\frac{F_{m+1}}{(\log F_{m+1})^2}.
    \end{aligned}
  \]
  By the definition of \(M_{\mathrm Z}\), using the displayed endpoint
  inequality,
  \[
    \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    \ge \frac12\,\frac{F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+2}}\Delta_m .
  \]
  The explicit Binet constants above and
  \(\Delta_m\ge(1-\varphi^{-1})/2\) by construction show that one may
  take
  \[
    A_{\mathrm Z}:=\frac{c_F(1-\varphi^{-1})}{8C_{\log}}.
  \]
  For the upper bound,
  \[
    \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}\le \pi(F_{m+2})
      \le C_{\pi,2}\frac{F_{m+2}}{\log F_{m+2}}
      \le \frac{C_{\pi,2}C_F}{c_{\log}}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}.
  \]
  Thus \(B_{\mathrm Z}:=C_{\pi,2}C_F/c_{\log}\) is admissible for every
  \(m\ge M_{\mathrm Z}\).
\end{proof}

All automata in this section read Zeckendorf words in the displayed
LSD-first order.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical Zeckendorf presentation]
  \label{lem:zeckendorf-canonical-presentation}
  The language \(\mathcal Z\) is accepted by the two-state trim
  strongly connected deterministic presentation \(\mathcal N_{\mathrm Z}\)
  with state set \(\{0,1\}\), initial state \(0\), accepting state
  \(\{1\}\), and transitions
  \[
    0\xrightarrow{0}0,\qquad
    0\xrightarrow{1}1,\qquad
    1\xrightarrow{0}0.
  \]
  Its adjacency matrix is
  \[
    M_{\mathrm Z}=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      1&1\\
      1&0
    \end{pmatrix},
  \]
  its Perron root is \(\varphi\), and its graph period is \(1\). Hence
  \(\mathcal N_{\mathrm Z}\) satisfies the hypotheses of
  \suppref{Theorem~1.15} with \(\lambda=\varphi\), and
  \(z_m=\abs{\mathcal Z_m}=F_m\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  A path in the displayed graph records exactly a binary word with no
  adjacent \(1\)'s: after reading a \(1\) the only available next digit
  is \(0\), while from state \(0\) both digits are available. The
  accepting state is \(1\), so the accepted words are precisely those
  no-\(11\) words whose terminal digit is \(1\), namely
  \(\mathcal Z\). The state \(0\) is initial and lies on the loop
  labelled \(0\), while \(0\xrightarrow{1}1\xrightarrow{0}0\); therefore
  both states are reachable from the initial state and can reach the
  accepting state, and the underlying directed graph is strongly
  connected. The loop at \(0\) gives period \(1\).

  The characteristic polynomial of \(M_{\mathrm Z}\) is
  \(t^2-t-1\). Its spectral radius is the positive root
  \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2>1\). Finally the accepted length-\(m\) words
  are the canonical Zeckendorf words of length \(m\), whose number is
  \(F_m\) by the standard Fibonacci recurrence with initial values
  \(F_1=F_2=1\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Regular immunity of the Zeckendorf prime language}
\label{subsec:zeckendorf-regular-immunity}

The preceding slice estimates use the prime number theorem. The next
argument is different: it is a PNT-free pumping obstruction in the
spirit of Sch\"utzenberger's finite-state obstruction for base
representations, but adapted to the LSD-first Zeckendorf evaluation.

\begin{lemma}[Unimodular Zeckendorf block action]
  \label{lem:zeckendorf-unimodular-block-action}
  After reading a prefix of length \(r\) in LSD-first order, put
  \[
    V_r:=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      N_r\\ F_{r+1}\\ F_{r+2}
    \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    N_r=\sum_{j=1}^r x_jF_{j+1}.
  \]
  The initial vector is \(V_0=(0,F_1,F_2)^t=(0,1,1)^t\). Reading one
  further digit \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\) sends \(V_r\) to
  \(D_\varepsilon V_r\), where
  \[
    D_\varepsilon:=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      1&0&\varepsilon\\
      0&0&1\\
      0&1&1
    \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    \det D_\varepsilon=-1.
  \]
  Hence, for a word \(y=y_1\cdots y_\ell\), the block action
  \[
    M_y:=D_{y_\ell}\cdots D_{y_1}
  \]
  has determinant \((-1)^\ell\). In particular, if \(\ell>0\), then
  the reduction of \(M_y\) belongs to
  \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\ZZ/q\ZZ)\) for every \(q\ge2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  If the next digit is \(\varepsilon\), then it occupies position
  \(r+1\) and contributes \(\varepsilon F_{r+2}\) to the value. Thus
  \[
    N_{r+1}=N_r+\varepsilon F_{r+2},\qquad
    F_{r+2}=F_{r+2},\qquad
    F_{r+3}=F_{r+1}+F_{r+2}.
  \]
  These three identities are exactly
  \(V_{r+1}=D_\varepsilon V_r\). Expanding the determinant along the
  first row gives
  \[
    \det D_\varepsilon
    =\det\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
     +\varepsilon\det\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
    =-1.
  \]
  Multiplicativity of the determinant gives
  \(\det M_y=(-1)^\ell\). Since \(\pm1\) is a unit modulo every
  \(q\ge2\), the reduction of \(M_y\) is invertible over
  \(\ZZ/q\ZZ\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Zeckendorf pumping congruence]
  \label{lem:zeckendorf-pumping-congruence}
  Let \(x,y,z\in\{0,1\}^\ast\) with \(\abs y>0\), and put
  \[
    N(k):=\val(xy^kz)
  \]
  whenever the displayed word is read in LSD-first order. If \(q\ge2\)
  and \(h\) is the order of \(M_y\) in
  \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\ZZ/q\ZZ)\), then
  \[
    N(k+h)\equiv N(k)\pmod q
  \]
  for every \(k\ge0\). In particular, if \(p=N(k)\) is prime, then
  \(N(k+h)\) is divisible by \(p\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Let \(v_0=(0,1,1)^t\). By
  \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-unimodular-block-action}, concatenation of
  LSD-first blocks gives
  \[
    N(k)=
    e_1^t\,M_zM_y^kM_xv_0,
  \]
  where \(e_1=(1,0,0)^t\). Since \(M_y^h\equiv I\pmod q\), we have
  \(M_y^{k+h}\equiv M_y^k\pmod q\). Multiplying on the left by
  \(M_z\), on the right by \(M_xv_0\), and taking the first coordinate
  gives the congruence for \(N(k+h)\) and \(N(k)\). If \(q=p=N(k)\) is
  prime, this congruence says \(N(k+h)\equiv0\pmod p\).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Prime-factor counts and divisibility antichains]
  \label{def:Omega-total-prime-factor-count}
  For \(n\ge2\), write
  \(n=\prod_p p^{a_p}\). We put
  \[
    \omega(n):=\#\{p:a_p>0\},
    \qquad
    \Omega(n):=\sum_p a_p,
  \]
  where \(\omega(n)\) is the number of distinct rational prime
  divisors of \(n\), and \(\Omega(n)\) is the total number of prime
  factors of \(n\), counted with multiplicity. We also put
  \(\omega(1)=\Omega(1)=0\). Then
  \[
    \omega(n)\le \Omega(n)
  \]
  for every \(n\ge1\). A set
  \(S\subseteq\NN_{\ge1}\) is a divisibility antichain if no two
  distinct elements \(a,b\in S\) satisfy \(a\mid b\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Coprime-quotient Zeckendorf pumping]
  \label{thm:coprime-quotient-zeckendorf-pumping}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) be recognized by an \(n\)-state
  deterministic finite automaton. If \(w\in L\), \(\abs w\ge n\), and
  \(\val(w)\ge2\), then there are accepted words
  \[
    w_0=w,\ w_1,\ w_2,\ldots
  \]
  with strictly increasing Zeckendorf lengths and values
  \(N_i:=\val(w_i)\) such that, for every \(i\ge0\),
  \[
    N_{i+1}=N_iQ_i,\qquad Q_i>1,\qquad Q_i\equiv1\pmod {N_i}.
  \]
  In particular \(\gcd(Q_i,N_i)=1\) for every \(i\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Let \(\mathcal A\) be an \(n\)-state DFA recognizing \(L\). Since
  \(\mathcal A\) accepts \(w\) and \(\abs w\ge n\), the usual pumping
  argument gives a factorization \(w=xyz\) with \(\abs y>0\) and
  \(xy^kz\in L\) for every \(k\ge0\). Put
  \[
    k_0=1,\qquad w_i=xy^{k_i}z,\qquad
    N_i=\val(w_i).
  \]
  Thus \(w_0=w\) and \(N_0=\val(w)\ge2\). Suppose \(k_i\) has been
  defined and \(N_i\ge2\). Let
  \[
    h_i=
    \operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{GL}_3(\ZZ/N_i^2\ZZ)}(M_y)
  \]
  be the order of the nonempty middle-block matrix modulo \(N_i^2\),
  which is defined by
  \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-unimodular-block-action}. Set
  \[
    k_{i+1}=k_i+h_i .
  \]
  Applying \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-pumping-congruence} with
  \(q=N_i^2\) gives
  \[
    N_{i+1}
      =\val(xy^{k_{i+1}}z)
      \equiv \val(xy^{k_i}z)
      =N_i
      \pmod {N_i^2}.
  \]
  Hence there is an integer \(t_i\) with
  \[
    N_{i+1}=N_i+N_i^2t_i=N_i(1+N_it_i).
  \]
  Since \(h_i\ge1\) and \(\abs y>0\), we have \(k_{i+1}>k_i\), and
  the length of \(xy^{k_{i+1}}z\) is strictly larger than the length
  of \(xy^{k_i}z\). Both words lie in \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\), hence
  are canonical Zeckendorf words. The canonical words of length \(m\)
  represent exactly the interval \([F_{m+1},F_{m+2})\), and these
  intervals are strictly ordered by length. Therefore
  \(N_{i+1}>N_i\). It follows from the displayed formula that
  \(t_i\ge1\).

  Put \(Q_i=1+N_it_i\). Then \(N_{i+1}=N_iQ_i\),
  \(Q_i>1\), and \(Q_i\equiv1\pmod {N_i}\). The last congruence gives
  \(\gcd(Q_i,N_i)=1\). The strict growth also gives
  \(N_{i+1}\ge N_i\ge2\), so the recursive construction continues
  indefinitely. This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Zeckendorf divisibility-chain pumping]
  \label{thm:zeckendorf-divisibility-chain-pumping}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) be recognized by an \(n\)-state
  deterministic finite automaton. If \(w\in L\), \(\abs w\ge n\), and
  \(\val(w)\ge2\), then there are words
  \[
    w_0=w,\ w_1,\ w_2,\ldots
  \]
  in \(L\) such that the Zeckendorf lengths \(\abs{w_i}\) are strictly
  increasing and
  \[
    \val(w_i)\ \text{strictly divides}\ \val(w_{i+1})
  \]
  for every \(i\ge0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  This is the immediate divisibility part of
  \Cref{thm:coprime-quotient-zeckendorf-pumping}: there
  \(N_{i+1}=N_iQ_i\) with \(Q_i>1\), so \(N_i\) strictly divides
  \(N_{i+1}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[New rational primes in every infinite regular Zeckendorf language]
  \label{cor:new-rational-primes-in-every-infinite-regular-zeckendorf-language}
  If \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) is infinite and regular, then \(L\)
  contains accepted words \(w_0,w_1,\ldots\) such that, with
  \(N_i=\val(w_i)\), the set of rational prime divisors of \(N_i\)
  strictly increases at every step. More precisely, in the chain of
  \Cref{thm:coprime-quotient-zeckendorf-pumping}, every quotient
  \(Q_i=N_{i+1}/N_i\) has a prime divisor that divides no earlier
  \(N_j\), \(0\le j\le i\). Hence infinitely many distinct rational
  primes divide values occurring in \(L\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Let an \(n\)-state DFA recognize \(L\). Since \(L\) is infinite, it
  contains words of arbitrarily large length; all but at most one have
  value at least \(2\). Choose \(w\in L\) with \(\abs w\ge n\) and
  \(\val(w)\ge2\), and apply
  \Cref{thm:coprime-quotient-zeckendorf-pumping}. For each \(i\),
  \(Q_i>1\), so choose a prime \(p_i\mid Q_i\). Since
  \(Q_i\equiv1\pmod {N_i}\), we have \(\gcd(Q_i,N_i)=1\). Moreover
  \(N_j\mid N_i\) for every \(j\le i\), because the values form a
  divisibility chain. Thus \(p_i\) divides none of
  \(N_0,\ldots,N_i\). But \(p_i\mid N_{i+1}=N_iQ_i\), so the prime
  divisor set strictly increases from \(N_i\) to \(N_{i+1}\). Iterating
  gives infinitely many distinct rational prime divisors among the
  accepted values.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unbounded distinct prime divisors]
  \label{cor:unbounded-distinct-prime-divisors}
  If \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) is infinite and regular, then
  \[
    \sup_{w\in L}\omega(\val(w))=\infty .
  \]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  In the sequence supplied by
  \Cref{cor:new-rational-primes-in-every-infinite-regular-zeckendorf-language},
  each step introduces at least one rational prime divisor not present
  earlier. Hence
  \[
    \omega(\val(w_i))\ge \omega(\val(w_0))+i
  \]
  for every \(i\), and the displayed supremum is infinite.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unbounded prime-factor count in infinite regular Zeckendorf languages]
  \label{cor:unbounded-prime-factor-count-in-infinite-regular-zeckendorf-languages}
  If \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) is infinite and regular, then
  \[
    \sup_{w\in L}\Omega(\val(w))=\infty .
  \]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  This follows from
  \Cref{cor:unbounded-distinct-prime-divisors} and the inequality
  \(\omega(n)\le\Omega(n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Divisibility-antichain obstruction]
  \label{cor:divisibility-antichain-obstruction}
  If \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) is regular and \(\val(L)\) is a
  divisibility antichain, then \(L\) is finite.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  If \(L\) were infinite, then it would contain an accepted word
  \(w\) of length at least the number of states in a DFA for \(L\) and
  with \(\val(w)\ge2\). The
  divisibility-chain theorem would produce \(w_0,w_1\in L\) with
  \(\val(w_0)\) strictly dividing \(\val(w_1)\), contradicting the
  antichain hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bounded-distinct-prime Zeckendorf languages are REG-immune]
  \label{cor:bounded-distinct-prime-zeckendorf-languages-reg-immune}
  For every fixed \(K\ge1\), the language
  \[
    \mathcal B_K
      :=\{w\in\mathcal Z:\omega(\val(w))\le K\}
  \]
  is infinite and contains no infinite regular sublanguage.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The language \(\mathcal B_K\) is infinite because it contains the
  Zeckendorf representations of all primes. If \(L\) were an infinite
  regular sublanguage of \(\mathcal B_K\), then
  \(\omega(\val(w))\le K\) for every \(w\in L\), contradicting
  \Cref{cor:unbounded-distinct-prime-divisors}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-prime-support Zeckendorf languages are REG-immune]
  \label{cor:finite-prime-support-zeckendorf-languages-reg-immune}
  Let \(S\) be a finite set of rational primes and put
  \[
    \mathcal Z_S
      :=\{w\in\mathcal Z:
          \text{every prime divisor of }\val(w)\text{ lies in }S\}.
  \]
  Then \(\mathcal Z_S\) contains no infinite regular sublanguage. If
  \(S\neq\emptyset\), then \(\mathcal Z_S\) is infinite, and therefore
  it is REG-immune.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Every value represented in \(\mathcal Z_S\) has at most \(\abs S\)
  distinct prime divisors. Thus any infinite regular sublanguage of
  \(\mathcal Z_S\) would contradict
  \Cref{cor:unbounded-distinct-prime-divisors}. If \(S\neq\emptyset\),
  choose \(p\in S\). For every \(a\ge0\), the integer \(p^a\) has all
  its prime divisors in \(S\), and its canonical Zeckendorf
  representation belongs to \(\mathcal Z_S\). These values are
  distinct, so \(\mathcal Z_S\) is infinite.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bounded almost-prime Zeckendorf languages are REG-immune]
  \label{cor:bounded-almost-prime-zeckendorf-languages-reg-immune}
  For every fixed \(K\ge1\), the language
  \[
    \mathcal A_K
      :=\{w\in\mathcal Z:\Omega(\val(w))\le K\}
  \]
  is infinite and contains no infinite regular sublanguage.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The language \(\mathcal A_K\) is infinite because it contains the
  Zeckendorf representations of all primes. Since
  \(\omega(n)\le\Omega(n)\), we have
  \(\mathcal A_K\subseteq\mathcal B_K\). Hence an infinite regular
  sublanguage of \(\mathcal A_K\) would be an infinite regular
  sublanguage of \(\mathcal B_K\), contradicting
  \Cref{cor:bounded-distinct-prime-zeckendorf-languages-reg-immune}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Zeckendorf-Sch\"utzenberger regular immunity]
  \label{thm:zeckendorf-schutzenberger-regular-immunity}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) be regular. If
  \[
    \val(L):=\{\val(w):w\in L\}
  \]
  is contained in the set of prime numbers, then \(L\) is finite. More
  quantitatively, if an \(n\)-state deterministic finite automaton
  recognizes such an \(L\), then no word of length at least \(n\) is
  accepted.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Since prime values have exactly one distinct prime divisor, the
  finiteness assertion follows from
  \Cref{cor:bounded-distinct-prime-zeckendorf-languages-reg-immune}
  with \(K=1\). For the quantitative statement, suppose an \(n\)-state
  DFA accepting such an \(L\) accepted a word \(w\) with \(\abs w\ge n\).
  Then \(\val(w)\) is prime, hence at least \(2\). By
  \Cref{thm:coprime-quotient-zeckendorf-pumping}, \(L\) would contain
  accepted values \(N_0\) and \(N_1=N_0Q_0\) with \(Q_0>1\), so
  \(N_1\) is composite. This contradicts the assumption that
  \(\val(L)\) is contained in the primes.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Infinite regular Zeckendorf languages contain composites]
  \label{cor:regular-zeckendorf-languages-contain-composites}
  Every infinite regular language \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) contains
  infinitely many words whose Zeckendorf values are composite.
  Equivalently, no fixed DFA can generate infinitely many canonical
  Zeckendorf primes without also generating infinitely many composite
  values.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  By
  \Cref{cor:unbounded-distinct-prime-divisors},
  the set of values \(\omega(\val(w))\) is unbounded on \(L\). In
  particular, infinitely many accepted words have
  \(\omega(\val(w))\ge2\), and their values are composite.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The Zeckendorf prime language is REG-immune]
  \label{cor:zeckendorf-prime-language-reg-immune}
  The language
  \[
    \mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}
    =\{w\in\mathcal Z:\val(w)\text{ is prime}\}
  \]
  is REG-immune: it is infinite and contains no infinite regular
  sublanguage.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Euclid's theorem and the bijection \(w\mapsto\val(w)\) from
  \(\mathcal Z\) to \(\NN_{\ge1}\) show that
  \(\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\) is infinite. If it contained an infinite
  regular sublanguage \(L\), then every value in \(L\) would have
  exactly one distinct prime divisor, contradicting
  \Cref{cor:bounded-distinct-prime-zeckendorf-languages-reg-immune}
  with \(K=1\).
\end{proof}

For a deterministic automaton \(\mathcal A\) over the Zeckendorf digit
alphabet, write
\[
  a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)
  :=\abs{L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal Z_m}.
\]

\begin{corollary}[Zeckendorf symmetric-difference obstruction]
  \label{cor:zeck-symmetric-diff}
  All automata in this statement read the Zeckendorf words LSD-first, as
  in the definition of \(\mathcal Z\) above.
  Let \(\mathcal A\) be a deterministic automaton over the Zeckendorf
  alphabet. Then there exists \(c_{\mathcal A,\varphi}>0\) such that
  \[
    \abs{(L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal Z_m)
         \triangle\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    \ge c_{\mathcal A,\varphi}\,\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
  \]
  for all sufficiently large \(m\). If
  \(a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)\) lies in the positive-density
  alternative of \suppref{Theorem~1.15}, then along the corresponding
  arithmetic progression of lengths one has the stronger bound
  \(\abs{(L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal Z_m)\triangle\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
  \gg \varphi^m\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Apply \suppref{Corollary~1.16} with
  \[
    \mathcal N=\mathcal N_{\mathrm Z},\qquad
    \lambda=\varphi,\qquad
    z_m=F_m,\qquad
    T_m=\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}.
  \]
  The presentation hypotheses and \(z_m=F_m\) are supplied by
  \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-canonical-presentation}; the finite-budget
  hypothesis
  \[
    A_{\mathrm Z}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
    \le \abs{T_m}
    \le B_{\mathrm Z}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
  \]
  is \Cref{lem:zeck-prime-count}. The corollary gives the all-large-\(m\)
  symmetric-difference bound. Its positive-density residue-class clause
  gives the stronger \(\Omega(\varphi^m)\) bound on every residue class
  where the limiting relative density of
  \(L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal Z_m\) inside \(\mathcal Z_m\) is positive.
\end{proof}

Define recall and precision for Zeckendorf slices by
\[
  \mathrm{Rec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}
  :=\frac{\abs{L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
          {\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{Prec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}
  :=\frac{\abs{L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
          {a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)}
\]
whenever both \(\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}>0\) and
\(a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)>0\), i.e. on lengths where the
denominator is nonzero.

\begin{corollary}[No simultaneous recall and precision in Zeckendorf]
  \label{cor:zeck-recall-precision}
  Let \(\mathcal A\) be a deterministic automaton over the Zeckendorf
  alphabet. Let \(D_Z\), the constants \(c_0,\dots,c_{D_Z-1}\), and
  \(\theta\) be supplied by
  \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-canonical-presentation} and \suppref{Theorem~1.15}, so that
  \[
    \frac{a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)}{F_m}
    =c_{m\bmod D_Z}+O(\theta^m)
  \]
  for all sufficiently large \(m\). Let \(B_{\mathrm Z}\) be the upper
  estimate constant from \Cref{lem:zeck-prime-count}. For each residue
  class \(r\bmod D_Z\), exactly one of the following alternatives holds:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
    \item If \(c_r=0\), then
    \(\mathrm{Rec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\) decays exponentially along
    \(m\equiv r\pmod {D_Z}\).
    \item If \(c_r>0\), then there is a constant
    \(C_{r,\varphi}>0\), for instance
    \(C_{r,\varphi}=2C_\varphi B_{\mathrm Z}/c_r\) with
    \(F_m\ge C_\varphi^{-1}\varphi^m\) for all large \(m\), such that
    \[
      \mathrm{Prec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\le \frac{C_{r,\varphi}}{m}
    \]
    for all sufficiently large \(m\equiv r\pmod {D_Z}\).
  \end{enumerate}
  Consequently every deterministic automaton satisfies one of the
  following statements:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
    \item \(\mathrm{Rec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\) decays exponentially along
    every residue class of lengths.
    \item There exists at least one residue class \(r\bmod D_Z\) with
    \(c_r>0\) such that
    \(\mathrm{Prec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\le C_{\mathcal A,\varphi}/m\) for
    all sufficiently large \(m\equiv r\pmod {D_Z}\) for which precision is
    defined.
  \end{enumerate}
  Precision is left undefined on lengths with
  \(a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)=0\); the statement makes no uniform
  precision assertion on residue classes other than those covered by
  the per-residue alternative.
  Consequently no fixed DFA has both recall and precision bounded below
  on infinitely many Zeckendorf lengths.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Fix \(A_{\mathrm Z},B_{\mathrm Z}>0\) as in
  \Cref{lem:zeck-prime-count}; thus
  \[
    A_{\mathrm Z}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
    \le \abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    \le B_{\mathrm Z}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
  \]
  for all sufficiently large \(m\). Also fix constants
  \(c_F',C_F'>0\) such that
  \(c_F'\varphi^m\le F_m\le C_F'\varphi^m\) for all large \(m\).

  Treat each residue class \(r\bmod D_Z\) separately. If \(c_r=0\), then
  along \(m\equiv r\pmod {D_Z}\),
  \(a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)=O(\theta^mF_m)\). Hence
  \[
    \mathrm{Rec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}
    \le
    \frac{a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)}
         {\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
    \ll m\theta^m.
  \]
  Since \(m\theta^m=O(\theta_r^m)\) for some \(\theta_r\in(0,1)\),
  recall decays exponentially on this residue class.

  If \(c_r>0\), then for all sufficiently large \(m\equiv r\pmod {D_Z}\),
  \[
    a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)\ge \frac{c_r}{2}F_m
    \ge \frac{c_rc_F'}{2}\varphi^m.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    \mathrm{Prec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}
    \le
    \frac{\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
         {a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A)}
    \le
    \frac{2B_{\mathrm Z}}{c_rc_F'}\cdot\frac{1}{m}.
  \]
  This proves the stated per-residue alternative, with
  \(C_{r,\varphi}=2B_{\mathrm Z}/(c_rc_F')\).

  If all \(c_r\) vanish, the first alternative holds on every residue
  class, hence globally. Otherwise choose a residue class with
  \(c_r>0\); the second alternative holds on defined lengths in that
  arithmetic progression, and one may take
  \(C_{\mathcal A,\varphi}=C_{r,\varphi}\).

  Finally suppose that both recall and precision were bounded below by
  some \(\delta>0\) on infinitely many Zeckendorf lengths. By the
  pigeonhole principle, infinitely many of those lengths lie in one
  residue class \(r\bmod D_Z\). The case \(c_r=0\) contradicts the
  exponential recall decay above. The case \(c_r>0\) contradicts the
  bound \(\mathrm{Prec}_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\le C_{r,\varphi}/m\) along that
  same residue class.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Regular Zeckendorf slices exclude the prime scale]
  \label{prop:zeckendorf-regular-slice-rigidity}
  Let \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\) be a regular language, and put
  \[
    \ell_m:=\abs{L\cap\mathcal Z_m}.
  \]
  Then there are an integer \(D_Z\ge1\), non-negative constants
  \(c_0,\dots,c_{D_Z-1}\), and \(\theta\in(0,1)\) such that
  \[
    \frac{\ell_m}{F_m}=c_{m\bmod D_Z}+O(\theta^m).
  \]
  Consequently, on each residue class modulo \(D_Z\), the ratio
  \[
    \frac{\ell_m}{\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
  \]
  either tends to \(0\) exponentially up to the harmless polynomial
  factor \(m\), or tends to \(+\infty\) at least linearly in \(m\).
  In particular no regular sublanguage of \(\mathcal Z\) has
  \(\ell_m=\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}\) for all sufficiently
  large \(m\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Let \(\mathcal A\) be a deterministic automaton recognizing \(L\).
  Since \(L\subseteq\mathcal Z\), one has
  \[
    \ell_m=\abs{L(\mathcal A)\cap\mathcal Z_m}
          =a_m^{(\mathrm Z)}(\mathcal A).
  \]
  By \Cref{lem:zeckendorf-canonical-presentation}, the canonical
  Zeckendorf presentation is trim, deterministic, strongly connected,
  has Perron root \(\varphi\), and satisfies
  \(z_m=\abs{\mathcal Z_m}=F_m\). Applying
  the background relative-density theorem
  \suppref{Theorem~1.15} to this presentation and to
  \(\mathcal A\) gives the displayed expansion; its proof is the
  substochastic product expansion of
  \suppref{Lemma~1.14} combined with the positive
  periodic denominator constants from \suppref{Lemma~1.13}.

  Fix a residue class \(r\bmod D_Z\). By \Cref{lem:zeck-prime-count}
  there are constants \(A_{\mathrm Z},B_{\mathrm Z}>0\) such that
  \[
    A_{\mathrm Z}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
    \le\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}
    \le B_{\mathrm Z}\frac{\varphi^m}{m}
  \]
  for all sufficiently large \(m\). Also fix \(c_F'',C_F''>0\) such that
  \(c_F''\varphi^m\le F_m\le C_F''\varphi^m\) for all large \(m\).

  If \(c_r=0\), then along \(m\equiv r\pmod {D_Z}\) we have
  \[
    \ell_m=O(\theta^mF_m)=O(\theta^m\varphi^m).
  \]
  Hence
  \[
    \frac{\ell_m}{\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
    =O(m\theta^m),
  \]
  which tends to \(0\) exponentially after increasing \(\theta\) within
  \((0,1)\).

  If \(c_r>0\), then for all sufficiently large
  \(m\equiv r\pmod {D_Z}\),
  \[
    \ell_m\ge\frac{c_r}{2}F_m
        \ge\frac{c_rc_F''}{2}\varphi^m.
  \]
  Therefore
  \[
    \frac{\ell_m}{\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}}
    \ge
    \frac{c_rc_F''}{2B_{\mathrm Z}}\,m,
  \]
  and the ratio tends to \(+\infty\) at least linearly on this residue
  class.

  Every residue class falls into exactly one of these two cases. Thus
  equality
  \(\ell_m=\abs{\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}}\) cannot hold for all
  sufficiently large \(m\), since any fixed residue class contains
  arbitrarily large lengths and on that class the ratio cannot remain
  equal to \(1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Prime Zeckendorf language is not regular]
  \label{cor:zeckendorf-prime-language-not-regular}
  The prime Zeckendorf language \(\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\) is not
  regular.
\end{corollary}

This corollary is now an immediate consequence of the stronger
PNT-free regular-immunity theorem. The preceding
\Cref{prop:zeckendorf-regular-slice-rigidity} remains useful as a
separate slice-scale obstruction: it explains why the exact
prime-slice cardinalities cannot be reproduced by a regular
sublanguage on all large lengths. General results relating
\(S\)-recognizability, \(S\)-automaticity, and morphic predicates in
abstract numeration systems include Maes--Rigo \cite{MaesRigo2002} and
the standard references cited in \Cref{subsec:prior-work}.

\begin{proof}
  This follows at once from
  \Cref{cor:zeckendorf-prime-language-reg-immune}, since an infinite
  regular language is an infinite regular sublanguage of itself.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sofic corollary]
  \label{rem:zeckendorf-primes-not-sofic}
  The preceding non-regularity statement excludes eventual sofic
  realization of the prime Zeckendorf length slices by the following
  short argument.
  Let \(\mathcal B_m(X)\) denote the admissible words of length
  \(m\) in a sofic shift \(X\). If
  \(\mathcal B_m(X)=\mathcal P_m^{(\mathrm Z)}\) for all \(m\ge N\),
  then
  \[
    \mathcal B(X)\triangle\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\subseteq \{0,1\}^{<N},
  \]
  a finite set. Since \(\mathcal B(X)\) is regular
  (\cite[Theorem~3.2.1]{LindMarcus1995}; see also
  \cite[Chapter~4]{PerrinPin2004}), this would make
  \(\mathcal P_{\mathcal Z}\) a finite symmetric difference away from a
  regular language, hence regular itself, contradicting
  \Cref{cor:zeckendorf-prime-language-not-regular}.
\end{remark}
