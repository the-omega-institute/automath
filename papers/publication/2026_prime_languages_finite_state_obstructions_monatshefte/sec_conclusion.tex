\section{Further remarks}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper proves a finite-budget obstruction for fixed finite-state
models facing prime-scale slices. The effective fixed-DFA theorem makes
the base-\(b\) obstruction computable from the transition matrix: the
residue alternatives, thresholds, and constants come from finite
Markov-chain linear algebra plus explicit prime-counting bounds. The
multicomponent presentation theorem keeps the residue-wise polynomial
factors on \(\lambda\)-active classes of arbitrary trim deterministic
presentations and compares those exponents against targets of size
\(\asymp\lambda^m/m\).

Together these results show that fixed-DFA approximation of prime
slices fails quantitatively: recall and precision cannot stay
simultaneously bounded below, and the symmetric difference from the
prime slice is always at least \(c_{\mathcal A,b}b^m/m\) in the ordinary
base-\(b\) setting and at least
\(c_{\mathcal A,\varphi}\varphi^m/m\) in the Zeckendorf setting. The
  Zeckendorf section also proves a sharper PNT-free language-theoretic
  obstruction. The unimodular action modulo \(N_i^2\) works for the two
  simultaneous pumps supplied by Ogden's lemma and gives the stated
  context-free coprime-quotient chain.

The same calculation depends only on a stationary affine companion
action. Under unique canonical representation, strict length ordering,
and an eventual integral recurrence with nonzero trailing coefficient,
the weak MCFL pumping lemma supplies one increasing orbit that is
periodic modulo every integer coprime to the trailing coefficient. Its
deleted-congruence subspace is a copy of \(\mathbb Q\), and its closure
in the corresponding profinite completion is a Cantor set. The exact
Cantor--Bendixson rank of bounded local prime-factor layers gives the
recurrence-witness-relative nonunit dichotomy: every infinite MCFL has
unbounded prime support away from the trailing coefficient or unbounded
valuation at one of its prime divisors. Thus prime and bounded-\(\Omega\) languages are
MCF-immune for all systems in the recurrent interface.

The selected trailing coefficient need not be intrinsic. In particular,
inflating the Fibonacci recurrence by \(X-p\) produces the
same selected bad-prime set as base \(p\) without producing its escape
language.

For the intrinsic minimal tail recurrence, the residual escape question
has an exact structural answer. An infinite MCFL with bounded prime
support away from the minimal constant term exists if and only if the
canonical language contains a synchronized pumping ray whose numerical
values are \(cb^t\) with integers \(c\ge1\) and \(b\ge2\). Such a ray has
an explicit finite-fan-out linear MCFG. With a DFA for the canonical
syntax, candidate rays can be enumerated and checked by finite-monoid
periodicity and exact recurrence identities, giving positive
semidecidability. The ratio of every certified geometric ray is supported on
the minimal tail constant. For standard linear Pisot \(U\)-systems, greedy
length thresholds complete the classification: such a ray, and hence such an
MCFL, exists exactly when the dominant Pisot number is an integer. Thus this
subclass is decidable by inspecting the degree of the minimal polynomial.
Termination on negative instances for general regular recurrent
presentations remains open.

In a unit recurrence, the same fixed orbit realizes quotient
congruences of arbitrary prescribed depth. A recursive use of these
congruences embeds the full countably branching tree as an induced
divisibility suborder, with pairwise coprime edge quotients whose least
prime factors exceed arbitrary edge-wise thresholds. Standard linear
Pisot \(U\)-systems satisfy the recurrent interface; Frougny
normalization supplies their finite-state canonical syntax. In the
nonunit case, ordinary base-\(b\) powers show that the valuation escape
alternative is necessary, whereas every nonintegral Pisot system forces
unbounded support away from the norm primes. The final analytic appendix is only
contextual comparison.

Several natural questions remain outside the scope of this paper. The
density and finite-budget results use determinism essentially;
nondeterministic, weighted, or
bounded-ambiguity variants would require new hypotheses because
cancellation or ambiguity can change the counting problem. Broader
arithmetic targets other than primes are likewise future-work
directions rather than theorem claims here. Arbitrary real-base
\(\beta\)-shifts, redundant representations, and non-positional
abstract numeration systems also remain outside the theorem unless they
can be equipped with the stationary invertible block action isolated in
\Cref{rem:pisot-open-interfaces}.
