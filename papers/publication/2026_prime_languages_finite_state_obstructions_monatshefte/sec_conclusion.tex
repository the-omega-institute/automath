\section{Further remarks}\label{sec:conclusion}

This short note derives quantitative prime-slice corollaries from
classical density inputs rather than new density theory. The
background input is the periodic density law for regular languages and
its deterministic trim strongly connected relative-density analogue. The
finite-budget obstruction mechanism shows that these facts force
fixed-DFA approximation of prime slices to fail quantitatively: recall
and precision cannot stay simultaneously bounded below, and the
symmetric difference from the prime slice is always at least
\(c_{\mathcal A,\bullet}\,\lambda^m/m\), where \(\lambda=b\) in the
ordinary base-\(b\) setting and \(\lambda=\varphi\) in the Zeckendorf
setting. The Zeckendorf section records the corresponding non-regularity
statement and the eventual-sofic corollary. The final analytic section is
kept as background comparison: it records classical natural-boundary
and periodicity obstructions for Euler products and finite determinant
models, without supplying an additional main theorem chain.

Several natural questions remain outside the scope of this note. The
density dichotomy uses completeness only as a normalization and uses
determinism essentially; nondeterministic, weighted, or
bounded-ambiguity variants would require new hypotheses because
cancellation or ambiguity can change the counting problem. Broader
Parry systems and arithmetic targets other than primes are likewise
future-work directions rather than theorem claims here.
