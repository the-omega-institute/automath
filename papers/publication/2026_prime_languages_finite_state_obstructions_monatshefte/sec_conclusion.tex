\section{Further remarks}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper proves a finite-budget obstruction for fixed finite-state
models facing prime-scale slices. The effective fixed-DFA theorem makes
the base-\(b\) obstruction computable from the transition matrix: the
residue alternatives, thresholds, and constants come from finite
Markov-chain linear algebra plus explicit prime-counting bounds. The
multicomponent presentation theorem keeps the residue-wise polynomial
factors on \(\lambda\)-active classes of arbitrary trim deterministic
presentations and compares those exponents against targets of size
\(\asymp\lambda^m/m\).

Together these results show that fixed-DFA approximation of prime
slices fails quantitatively: recall and precision cannot stay
simultaneously bounded below, and the symmetric difference from the
prime slice is always at least \(c_{\mathcal A,b}b^m/m\) in the ordinary
base-\(b\) setting and at least
\(c_{\mathcal A,\varphi}\varphi^m/m\) in the Zeckendorf setting. The
Zeckendorf section also proves a sharper PNT-free language-theoretic
obstruction. The unimodular action modulo \(N_i^2\) works not only for
one regular pump but for the two simultaneous pumps supplied by Ogden's
lemma. It produces an infinite chain \(N_{i+1}=N_iQ_i\) with
\(Q_i\equiv1\pmod {N_i}\) inside every sufficiently long context-free
Zeckendorf sublanguage. Hence every infinite context-free Zeckendorf
sublanguage has unbounded distinct rational prime divisors, and the
bounded-distinct-prime, bounded almost-prime, finite-prime-support, and
prime Zeckendorf languages are CF-immune.

The same calculation depends only on a stationary affine companion
action. Under unique canonical representation, strict length ordering,
and an eventual integral recurrence with nonzero trailing coefficient,
the prime language is CF-immune and thus nonrecognizable. Standard
linear Pisot \(U\)-systems satisfy this interface; Frougny
normalization supplies their finite-state canonical syntax. Unit
recurrences also support the full iterated coprime-quotient conclusion.
For nonunit recurrences, prime immunity remains valid but the
bounded-prime-support iteration is left open because the action can
become singular modulo a later composite value. The final analytic
appendix is only contextual comparison.

Several natural questions remain outside the scope of this paper. The
density and finite-budget results use determinism essentially;
nondeterministic, weighted, or
bounded-ambiguity variants would require new hypotheses because
cancellation or ambiguity can change the counting problem. Broader
arithmetic targets other than primes are likewise future-work
directions rather than theorem claims here. Arbitrary real-base
\(\beta\)-shifts, redundant representations, and non-positional
abstract numeration systems also remain outside the theorem unless they
can be equipped with the stationary invertible block action isolated in
\Cref{rem:pisot-open-interfaces}.
