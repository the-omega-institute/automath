\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The affine recurrence action converts one synchronized pumping family into
an injective numerical orbit with simultaneous local returns. Adic topology
turns those returns into scatteredness and prime-support obstructions, while
Evertse's quotient theorem and Schur's theorem identify the remaining
bounded-support possibility as a geometric ray \(cb^t\). In standard
weak-Perron greedy systems, the length squeeze classifies that possibility
algebraically: it occurs if and only if a positive power of the weak Perron
root is an integer.

In ordinary bases, the synchronized orbit has a separate global consequence.
Independently starring its pumped blocks produces a sparse regular envelope.
The sparse automatic intersection theorem in multiplicatively independent
bases then implies that every infinite sparse automatic set has an MCF-immune
representation language in the other base.

For slender context-free languages, a finite paired-loop cover supplies
global control of every represented value. The same affine evaluation sends
each loop to finitely many recurrence sequences, and dominant-root transfer
followed by Mignotte's theorem gives the two-system Cobham finiteness result.
Complete recurrence and loop data yield an effective cardinality bound. For
the one-system geometric-ray problem, candidate verification is effective,
but no uniform bound is known for the fan-out and block lengths of a smallest
witness; this is the missing step toward a general decision procedure.
