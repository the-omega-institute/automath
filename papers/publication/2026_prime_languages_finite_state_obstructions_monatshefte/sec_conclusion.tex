\section{Further remarks}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper derives quantitative prime-slice corollaries from classical
density inputs rather than new density theory. The background input is
the periodic density law for regular languages and its deterministic
trim strongly connected relative-density analogue. The effective
fixed-DFA theorem makes the base-\(b\) obstruction computable from the
transition matrix: the residue alternatives, thresholds, and constants
come from finite Markov-chain linear algebra plus explicit
prime-counting bounds. The resulting finite-budget obstruction shows
that fixed-DFA approximation of prime slices fails quantitatively:
recall and precision cannot stay simultaneously bounded below, and the
symmetric difference from the prime slice is always at least
\(c_{\mathcal A,b}b^m/m\) in the ordinary base-\(b\) setting and at
least \(c_{\mathcal A,\varphi}\varphi^m/m\) in the Zeckendorf setting.
The Zeckendorf section records the corresponding non-regularity
statement and the eventual-sofic corollary. The final analytic remarks
are background comparison only: they record classical natural-boundary
and periodicity obstructions for Euler products and scalar determinant
models, without supplying an additional main theorem chain.

Several natural questions remain outside the scope of this note. The
density dichotomy uses completeness only as a normalization and uses
determinism essentially; nondeterministic, weighted, or
bounded-ambiguity variants would require new hypotheses because
cancellation or ambiguity can change the counting problem. Broader
Parry systems and arithmetic targets other than primes are likewise
future-work directions rather than theorem claims here.
