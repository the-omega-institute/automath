\section{Conclusion and open interface}\label{sec:conclusion}

The affine recurrence action turns one weak MCFL pumping family into a
single numerical orbit with simultaneous local returns. Strict ordering of
canonical lengths supplies monotonicity, and finite-group return times
supply congruences. Applying Broughan's adic classifications to these orbits,
together with the deleted-prime intrinsic derivative calculation, gives the
scattered-set obstruction, the nonunit escape dichotomy, and prime and
bounded-\(\Omega\) MCF-immunity.

For the minimal tail recurrence, the residual bounded-support phenomenon
has an exact structural form. It occurs on an infinite finite-fan-out MCFL
if and only if the canonical language contains a synchronized ray with
values \(cb^t\). Evertse's quotient theorem is used with an explicit
prime-ideal norm bound to reduce a fixed-support recurrence to one
characteristic root; Schur's theorem removes its polynomial factor. The
ratio \(b\) uses only primes dividing the minimal tail constant. In a
standard greedy linear numeration basis with Perron recurrence, length
thresholds then show that such a ray exists exactly for an integral dominant
root.

On the promised class of valid effectively presented regular canonical
systems, with the minimal tail recurrence and threshold supplied or
certified, positive promised instances can be enumerated and certified. The unresolved general step is not
candidate verification but witness compression: no computable bound is
known for the fan-out and word lengths of a smallest geometric synchronized
scheme. DFA path compression does not preserve the associated affine
recurrence matrices. Conversely, no reduction proving undecidability is
known that also preserves unique canonical representation, strict length
ordering, the minimal tail recurrence, and the special digit-matrix form.
Accordingly the general decision problem remains open, while the
Perron-recurrence subclass of greedy linear numeration bases is decidable.
This removes the Pisot hypothesis: strict dominance of the Perron root over
its conjugates is exactly what the greedy length argument uses.

regular Zeckendorf results. Keeping those comparisons outside the article
leaves the main theorem chain centered on synchronized recurrence orbits
and their arithmetic consequences.
