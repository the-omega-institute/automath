\section{Further remarks}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper proves a finite-budget obstruction for fixed finite-state
models facing prime-scale slices. The effective fixed-DFA theorem makes
the base-\(b\) obstruction computable from the transition matrix: the
residue alternatives, thresholds, and constants come from finite
Markov-chain linear algebra plus explicit prime-counting bounds. The
multicomponent presentation theorem keeps the residue-wise polynomial
factors for arbitrary trim deterministic presentations and compares
those exponents against targets of size \(\asymp\lambda^m/m\). Together
these results show that fixed-DFA approximation of prime slices fails
quantitatively: recall and precision cannot stay simultaneously bounded
below, and the symmetric difference from the prime slice is always at
least \(c_{\mathcal A,b}b^m/m\) in the ordinary base-\(b\) setting and
at least \(c_{\mathcal A,\varphi}\varphi^m/m\) in the Zeckendorf
setting. The Zeckendorf section records the corresponding
non-regularity statement and the eventual-sofic corollary. The final
analytic appendix is only contextual comparison.

Several natural questions remain outside the scope of this note. The
density and finite-budget results use determinism essentially;
nondeterministic, weighted, or
bounded-ambiguity variants would require new hypotheses because
cancellation or ambiguity can change the counting problem. Broader
arithmetic targets other than primes are likewise future-work
directions rather than theorem claims here.
