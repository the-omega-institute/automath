\begin{abstract}
Classical density theory for regular languages gives quantitative
finite-budget obstructions to approximating prime slices by one fixed
automaton. We retain those base-\(b\) and Zeckendorf bounds, including
the lower scales \(b^m/m\) and \(\varphi^m/m\), and strengthen the
PNT-free language theorem. The weak pumping lemma for multiple
context-free languages, combined with an affine recurrence block
action, produces one strictly increasing numerical orbit that is
simultaneously periodic modulo every integer coprime to the recurrence's
trailing coefficient. In the corresponding deleted-congruence topology
this orbit is homeomorphic to \(\mathbb Q\), and its profinite closure is
a Cantor set.

The argument is then made base-independent. For a canonical positional
system with strictly length-ordered representations and an eventual
integral recurrence having nonzero trailing coefficient, every infinite
multiple context-free sublanguage must have unbounded prime support
away from that coefficient or unbounded valuation at one of its prime
divisors. Hence prime and bounded-\(\Omega\) representation languages
are MCF-immune, including for nonunit recurrences. In the unit case the
same fixed orbit contains quotient chains of arbitrarily prescribed
congruence depth and an induced copy of the countably branching
divisibility tree with pairwise coprime, arbitrarily rough edge
quotients. More precisely, bounded prime support outside the minimal
  tail recurrence occurs on an infinite MCFL if and only if the canonical
  language contains a synchronized ray whose values form an exact integer
  geometric progression. For regular canonical syntax this property is
  recursively enumerable. In every such ray the ratio is supported on the
  minimal tail constant. For standard linear Pisot \(U\)-systems the property
  is fully decidable: it holds exactly for an integral dominant root. Thus
  every infinite MCFL in a nonintegral Pisot system has unbounded prime
  support even after the norm primes are deleted. Arbitrary real-base
\(\beta\)-shifts and non-positional abstract numeration systems remain
explicit open interfaces.
\end{abstract}
