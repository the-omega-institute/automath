\begin{abstract}
We study prime-valued slices of regular positional languages through the
transition matrices of a fixed deterministic finite automaton. In ordinary
integer bases, spectral decomposition gives a density dichotomy and
effective bounds for prime slices with the automaton held fixed. The same
matrix framework yields word-growth asymptotics needed for finite-budget
estimates. For Zeckendorf representations, a unimodular block action and
the canonical Fibonacci intervals give corresponding regular-language
growth and prime-support obstructions. We compare these fixed-automaton
estimates with qualitative nonrecognizability results and with bounds that
allow state complexity to grow, and record the analytic prime estimates
used in the comparison.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}

A regular language turns positional counting into a finite-dimensional
matrix problem. Powers of its transition matrix separate transient states,
periodic recurrent components, and the spectral terms that control word
counts in arithmetic length classes. Coupling those counts with prime
estimates gives quantitative information for a fixed automaton rather than
only a recognition obstruction.

The first part develops this transition-matrix analysis for ordinary bases
and proves the resulting density and prime-slice alternatives. The second
part treats Zeckendorf representations, where Fibonacci length intervals
and a unimodular digit action replace constant-base scaling. The final
appendix collects the analytic comparisons used by both branches. All
constants are attached to the displayed automaton and recurrence data.
