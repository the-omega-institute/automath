\begin{abstract}
We study quotient-observed Chebotarev distributions for geometrically connected finite \etale{} abelian covers of smooth curves over finite fields. For every sufficiently large degree \(n\), the primitive Frobenius classes define a probability measure \(\mu_n\) on the Galois group \(G\); every subgroup \(H\le G\) gives a quotient observation \(\mu_n^H=(\pi_H)_\ast\mu_n\). The main theorem expresses quotient \(\chi^2\)-defects and quotient relative entropies in terms of square-root normalized Artin amplitudes
\[
A_n(\chi)=\frac{n}{q^{n/2}}\sum_{c\in G}\chi(c)B_n(c).
\]
Uniformly in \(H\),
\[
\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^H\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/H}\right)
=q^{-n}\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}|A_n(\chi)|^2+O(q^{-3n/2}),
\]
and relative entropy is one half of the same visible Artin energy up to the same error. Subgroup M\"obius inversion on the finite subgroup lattice then recovers, from the full quotient entropy profile, each exact-kernel energy
\[
E_n(K)=\sum_{\ker\chi=K}|A_n(\chi)|^2
\]
with error \(O(q^{-n/2})\) after rescaling by \(2q^n\). The amplitudes are given by an explicit reciprocal-zero formula that separates the projective Artin contribution, imprimitive proper-divisor terms, and open-curve boundary factors. We also prove Cesaro and subsequence criteria for persistent exact-kernel energy, identifying the coarsest quotient that carries recurring secondary Chebotarev bias. An optional appendix records a logically independent finite quotient comparison model.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Chebotarev distribution; relative entropy; Artin \(L\)-function; subgroup M\"obius inversion; finite fields

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Many Chebotarev counting problems are naturally observed through a loss of resolution: one may see only the image of a Frobenius class in a quotient of the Galois group, or only a restricted collection of quotient counts. The basic issue is then arithmetic and quantitative: which Artin spectral components remain visible after quotienting, and how much of the hidden exact-kernel spectrum can be reconstructed from the full quotient profile?

The main body of the paper answers this question for geometrically connected finite \etale{} abelian covers of smooth projective curves over finite fields, and then extends the formulas to smooth open curves by controlling the finitely many boundary points. Entropy and quotient language are used as quantitative observables for Frobenius distributions. The finite quotient model in the appendix is optional comparison material; it is logically independent of the Chebotarev theorem chain and is not used in any arithmetic proof.

\subsection{Arithmetic results}
Chebotarev density and its refinements relate prime-counting functions to the spectral data carried by Artin \(L\)-functions; see \cite{Tschebotareff1926,Serre1981,NeukirchSchmidtWingberg2008}. This paper uses the abstract spectral expansion only as a bookkeeping interface and then proves the concrete finite-field specialization needed for the main arithmetic theorem chain. The entropy and harmonic-analysis tools are classical \cite{CoverThomas2006,CsiszarKorner2011,DemboZeitouni1998,Diaconis1988,Terras1999}; the contribution is the exact-kernel recovery mechanism for quotient-observed Frobenius laws.

For a connected finite \etale{} abelian cover \(V\to U\) over \(\FF_q\), Definition \ref{def:finite-field-quotient-notation} introduces the degree-\(n\) Frobenius counts \(B_n(c)\), the probability law \(\mu_n\), quotient maps \(\pi_H:G\to G/H\), normalized Artin amplitudes \(A_n(\chi)=nB_n(\chi)/q^{n/2}\), and exact-kernel energies \(E_n(K)=\sum_{\ker\chi=K}|A_n(\chi)|^2\). Lemmas \ref{lem:artin-euler-trace-convention}, \ref{lem:artin-weights-boundary-convention}, and \ref{lem:primitive-closed-point-inversion-convention}, summarized by Lemma \ref{prop:classical-artin-conventions}, fix the geometric Frobenius, sign, Euler-product, weight, local-factor, and primitive closed-point conventions for the Artin input. Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-eventual-positive-degrees} proves that \(\mu_n\) is defined for all \(n\ge n_0(V/U)\). Proposition \ref{prop:finite-field-normalized-reciprocal-zero-formula} expresses \(A_n(\chi)\) in terms of the reciprocal zeros of \(L(U,\chi,T)\), with explicit proper-divisor and boundary errors. Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-exact-quotient-normalization} then fixes the exact normalization between quotient \(\chi^2\)-defects, the factor \(q^n/(n^2N_n^2)\), and subgroup-lattice M\"obius recovery. Theorem \ref{thm:projective-finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography} is the projective main result, and Corollary \ref{thm:finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography} gives the open-curve extension: uniformly in \(H\), the quotient \(\chi^2\)-defect is \(q^{-n}\) times the visible Artin energy up to \(O(q^{-3n/2})\), relative entropy is one half of that expression, and subgroup M\"obius inversion recovers every exact-kernel energy \(E_n(K)\) with rescaled error \(O(q^{-n/2})\).

The theorem-by-theorem novelty is as follows. Proposition \ref{prop:finite-field-normalized-reciprocal-zero-formula} repackages the standard Artin explicit formula into the square-root normalized primitive amplitudes used by the entropy profile; the Artin factorization, Weil bounds, Parseval identity, entropy chain rule, and subgroup M\"obius inversion themselves are classical inputs. The new arithmetic content begins with Theorem \ref{thm:projective-finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography}: it proves that quotient Frobenius entropy profiles recover the exact-kernel Artin spectral energies in an actual finite-field cover, with uniform error terms. Corollary \ref{thm:finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography} carries this recovery through the open-curve boundary correction, and Corollary \ref{cor:finite-field-quotient-profile-rigidity} records the resulting rigidity of the quotient profile. Theorem \ref{thm:finite-field-exact-kernel-visibility-obstruction} identifies the finite subgroup-lattice obstruction to detecting exact kernels from a restricted quotient family and shows that the full quotient family removes it exactly. Corollary \ref{cor:cesaro-averaged-quotient-profile-persistent-support} then gives a direct Cesaro-averaged quotient-profile formula for persistent support, while Theorem \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-persistent-energy-detection} supplies the equivalent unitary Artin noncancellation criterion and subsequence lower bounds.


\begin{theorem}[Finite-field quotient entropy tomography]\label{thm:intro-main-arithmetic-tomography}
Let \(V\to U\) be a geometrically connected finite \etale{} abelian Galois cover of a smooth geometrically connected curve over \(\FF_q\), with group \(G\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), the family of quotient Frobenius laws \(\{\mu_n^H:H\le G\}\) determines, up to an error \(O(q^{-n/2})\) after the natural entropy rescaling, every exact-kernel Artin energy
\[
E_n(K)=\sum_{\substack{\chi\in\widehat G\setminus\{\ind\}\\ \ker\chi=K}}|A_n(\chi)|^2.
\]
More precisely, uniformly for all subgroups \(H,K\le G\),
\[
\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^H\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/H}\right)
=q^{-n}\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}|A_n(\chi)|^2+O(q^{-3n/2}),
\]
and
\[
E_n(K)=2q^n\sum_{J\supseteq K}\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(K,J)
D\!\left(\mu_n^J\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/J}\right)+O(q^{-n/2}).
\]
The fully quantified projective and open-curve versions, including the reciprocal-zero expression for \(A_n(\chi)\), are Theorem \ref{thm:projective-finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography} and Corollary \ref{thm:finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is exactly the combination of the two cited arithmetic statements, with the displayed notation as defined in Definition \ref{def:finite-field-quotient-notation}. The first displayed formula is the uniform quotient \(\chi^2\)-asymptotic in Theorem \ref{thm:projective-finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography} for projective \(U\) and in Corollary \ref{thm:finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography} for open \(U\). The second displayed formula is the subgroup-lattice M\"obius inversion of the uniform entropy expansion in the same theorem and corollary. Proposition \ref{prop:finite-field-normalized-reciprocal-zero-formula} supplies the advertised expression of the normalized amplitudes in terms of Artin reciprocal zeros, proper-divisor terms, and boundary factors.
\end{proof}

At the quadratic level, supporting quotient identities explain the exactness of this recovery. For a surjection \(\pi:G_2\twoheadrightarrow G_1\), Proposition \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-quotient-relative-entropy-chain} gives the exact chain rule for relative entropy with respect to the uniform laws on \(G_2\), \(G_1\), and the fibers of \(\pi\). Proposition \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-visible-hidden-character-energy} is the corresponding \(L^2\)-statement: the \(\chi^2\)-defect splits orthogonally into a quotient-visible part, carried by characters factoring through \(\pi\), and a complementary invisible part. Theorem \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-quotient-lattice-tomography} packages the exact subgroup-lattice inversion, and Theorem \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-entropy-tomography} records the parallel second-order entropy statement in the abstract spectral setting.

The final arithmetic statements isolate persistent secondary bias. Under an isolated dominant conjugate pair, Theorem \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-second-main-term-witness} extracts the secondary oscillatory term in subset counts. Theorem \ref{thm:kernel-artin-dominant-channel-pole-order} identifies the exact pole order of a multiplicity-one dominant Artin factor. The auxiliary abstract theorem \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-dominant-kernel-detection} treats pointwise dominant exact-kernel families only as spectral bookkeeping. The finite-field detection statements are Theorem \ref{thm:finite-field-exact-kernel-visibility-obstruction}, which proves the exact subgroup-lattice obstruction and recovery mechanism, Corollary \ref{cor:cesaro-averaged-quotient-profile-persistent-support}, which recovers persistent support directly from Cesaro-averaged rescaled quotient profiles, and Theorem \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-persistent-energy-detection}, which uses the recovered exact-kernel energies and the Cesaro-negligible finite-field expansion of Proposition \ref{prop:finite-field-unitary-artin-expansion} to identify the coarsest persistent quotient.

\section{Quotient Identities and Exact-Kernel Tomography}\label{sec:entropy-chain}
We first fix the finite-group notation used in the arithmetic theorem, then state the finite-field Chebotarev entropy tomography result before recording the supporting quotient identities and auxiliary spectral bookkeeping.

\subsection{Notation and Divergences}
If \(G\) is a finite group, we write \(\widehat G\) for a complete set of irreducible complex representations of \(G\); when \(G\) is abelian, this is identified with its Pontryagin dual of characters. We write \(\mathrm{unif}_G\) for the uniform probability measure on \(G\). For a surjective homomorphism \(\pi:G_2\twoheadrightarrow G_1\) with kernel \(K\), we set
\[
K^\perp:=\{\chi\in\widehat G_2:\chi|_K\equiv 1\}\cong \widehat G_1.
\]
When \(G\) is abelian and \(f:G\to \CC\), its Fourier transform is
\[
\widehat f(\chi):=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{c\in G} f(c)\,\overline{\chi(c)}.
\]
With respect to the normalized inner product
\[
\langle f,g\rangle_{L^2(\mathrm{unif}_G)}:=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{c\in G}f(c)\,\overline{g(c)},
\]
Parseval's identity takes the form
\[
\|f\|_{L^2(\mathrm{unif}_G)}^2=\sum_{\chi\in \widehat G}\abs{\widehat f(\chi)}^2.
\]

For probability measures \(\mu,\nu\) on a finite set \(X\) with \(\mu\ll\nu\), we use
\[
D(\mu\Vert \nu):=\sum_{x\in X}\mu(x)\log\frac{\mu(x)}{\nu(x)},
\]
\[
\chi^2(\mu\Vert \nu):=\sum_{x\in X}\frac{(\mu(x)-\nu(x))^2}{\nu(x)},
\qquad
\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}:=\frac12\sum_{x\in X}\abs{\mu(x)-\nu(x)}.
\]
When \(\nu=\mathrm{unif}_G\) on a finite group \(G\) and \(r:=|G|\mu\) denotes the density of \(\mu\) relative to \(\mathrm{unif}_G\), one has
\[
\chi^2(\mu\Vert \mathrm{unif}_G)=\|r-1\|_{L^2(\mathrm{unif}_G)}^2.
\]
When \(X\) is partitioned into fibers of a map \(\pi:X\to Y\), the relative-entropy chain rule reads
\[
D(\mu\Vert \nu)=D(\pi_\ast\mu\Vert \pi_\ast\nu)+\sum_{y\in Y}(\pi_\ast\mu)(y)\,D(\mu(\cdot\mid y)\Vert \nu(\cdot\mid y)),
\]
whenever the conditional distributions are defined.


\subsection{Finite-field Chebotarev entropy tomography}\label{subsec:finite-field-entropy-tomography}
This is the primary arithmetic theorem chain. We first state and prove the finite-field cover results: the visible quotient entropy is expressed directly in terms of square-root normalized Artin amplitudes, and exact-kernel energies are recovered by subgroup M\"obius inversion. The proof invokes the elementary quotient identities recorded in the next subsection.

\begin{proposition}[Supporting finite quotient skeleton]\label{prop:supporting-finite-quotient-skeleton}
Let \(q:\Omega\twoheadrightarrow X\) be a surjection of finite sets. Let \(u_\Omega\) and \(u_X\) be the uniform laws, let \(\mathcal C_q\subset L^2(\Omega,u_\Omega)\) be the subspace of functions constant on the fibers of \(q\), and let \(P_q\) be the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal C_q\). For any probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\Omega\), put \(r=\mu/u_\Omega\), \(\mu_X=q_\ast\mu\), and \(r_X=\mu_X/u_X\). Then the following assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\normalfont
  \item If all fibers of \(q\) have the same cardinality, then
  \[
  D(\mu\Vert u_\Omega)=D(\mu_X\Vert u_X)+\sum_{x\in X}\mu_X(x)D(\mu(\cdot\mid x)\Vert u_{q^{-1}(x)}).
  \]
  \item Without any equal-fiber hypothesis, the normalized fiberwise expectation
  \[
  (P_qr)(\omega):=\frac{1}{|q^{-1}(q(\omega))|}\sum_{\eta\in q^{-1}(q(\omega))}r(\eta)
  \]
  satisfies
  \[
  \|r-1\|_{L^2(u_\Omega)}^2=\|P_qr-1\|_{L^2(u_\Omega)}^2+\|r-P_qr\|_{L^2(u_\Omega)}^2.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
For a group quotient this proposition supplies the probabilistic part of the arithmetic quotient identities; no fold-groupoid algebra is used in the Chebotarev arguments.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For (1), write \(\mu(\omega)=\mu_X(q(\omega))\mu(\omega\mid q(\omega))\). If every fiber has size \(h\), then \(|\Omega|=h|X|\) and
\[
u_\Omega(\omega)=|\Omega|^{-1}=|X|^{-1}h^{-1}=u_X(q(\omega))u_{q^{-1}(q(\omega))}(\omega).
\]
Taking logarithms in the quotient of these two factorizations, multiplying by \(\mu(\omega)\), and summing over \(\omega\) gives the chain rule. For (2), averaging over each fiber is the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal C_q\) in \(L^2(\Omega,u_\Omega)\). Since \(1\in\mathcal C_q\), Pythagoras applied to \(r-1=(P_qr-1)+(r-P_qr)\) gives the displayed identity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Auxiliary spectral bookkeeping and realization}
The preceding finite-field theorem chain is the arithmetic core of the paper. We now record auxiliary spectral bookkeeping used only for comparison with dominant-channel asymptotics, together with its standard realization in global function fields. These hypotheses are not an alternative route to the finite-field tomography theorem and are not used to prove the exact-kernel recovery result.

\subsubsection{Abstract spectral hypotheses}\label{subsubsec:auxiliary-chebotarev-bookkeeping}
Fix a finite group \(G\), a temperature parameter \(u\in(0,1]\), and nonnegative primitive Chebotarev counts \(B_{n,c}(u)\) indexed by \(c\in G\). We write
\[
B_n(\ind;u):=\sum_{c\in G}B_{n,c}(u),
\]
and, when \(G\) is abelian,
\[
B_n(\chi;u):=\sum_{c\in G}\chi(c)\,B_{n,c}(u)
\quad(\chi\in \widehat G),
\]
and for each irreducible channel \(\rho\) we let \(M_{K,\rho}(u)\) denote the corresponding transfer matrix. The trivial-channel Perron root is
\[
\lambda_1(u):=\spr(M_{K,\ind}(u)),
\]
the nontrivial spectral radius is
\[
\eta(u):=\max_{\rho\neq \ind}\spr(M_{K,\rho}(u)),
\]
and we set
\[
\Lambda(u):=\max\{|\lambda_2(u)|,\eta(u)\},
\]
where \(\lambda_2(u)\) denotes the second-largest eigenvalue modulus in the trivial channel.

The finite-field theorems below are unconditional for the curve covers under consideration. The following abstract hypotheses are retained only as auxiliary bookkeeping: they isolate the spectral expansion and Artin-factor notation used when the quotient identities are later applied to dominant channels. A reader interested first in the arithmetic theorem chain may pass directly to the function-field realization and finite-field tomography statements.

\begin{assumption}[Auxiliary Chebotarev spectral expansion]\label{thm:kernel-chebotarev-exp}
Assume \(\Lambda(u)<\lambda_1(u)\), and let \(\Theta(u)<\lambda_1(u)\) denote a primitive-remainder radius. Then, uniformly in \(c\in G\),
\[
B_{n,c}(u)
=
\frac{1}{|G|}\frac{\lambda_1(u)^n}{n}
+O\!\left(\frac{\Lambda(u)^n}{n}\right),
\qquad
B_n(\ind;u)
=
\frac{\lambda_1(u)^n}{n}
+O\!\left(\frac{\Lambda(u)^n}{n}\right).
\]
Moreover, for every conjugacy-invariant subset \(A\subseteq G\), if we set
\[
B_{n,A}(u):=\sum_{c\in A}B_{n,c}(u),
\]
then there exist linear functionals \(L_{A,\rho}\) on the corresponding channel spaces such that
\[
B_{n,A}(u)
=
\mathrm{unif}_G(A)\frac{\lambda_1(u)^n}{n}
+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\rho\in\widehat G\setminus\{\ind\}}L_{A,\rho}\!\bigl(M_{K,\rho}(u)^n\bigr)
+O\!\left(\frac{\Theta(u)^n}{n}\right).
\]
The error term is uniform in \(A\). When the expansion is exact in a particular model, this error may of course be taken to be zero.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Auxiliary Artin factorization]\label{prop:kernel-artin-factorization}
For a finite group \(G\), define
\[
L_{K,\rho}(z,u):=\det(I-zM_{K,\rho}(u))^{-1}.
\]
Then the associated zeta function satisfies
\[
\zeta_{K,G}(z,u)=\prod_{\rho\in\widehat G}L_{K,\rho}(z,u)^{\dim\rho},
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{\zeta_{K,G}(z,u)}{L_{K,\ind}(z,u)}
=
\prod_{\rho\in\widehat G\setminus\{\ind\}}L_{K,\rho}(z,u)^{\dim\rho}.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Square-Root Spectral Bound]\label{def:kernel-rh}
We write
\[
\textsf{SR}_{K,G}(u):\qquad \Lambda(u)\le \lambda_1(u)^{1/2}
\]
for the square-root spectral bound used below.
\end{definition}

In Sections \ref{sec:entropy-chain} and \ref{sec:artin-pole} we use Assumptions \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-exp} and \ref{prop:kernel-artin-factorization} only through the displayed consequences above. In particular, no further arithmetic input is required. The condition \(\textsf{SR}_{K,G}(u)\) is a genuine square-root \emph{decay} statement only when \(\lambda_1(u)>1\), which is precisely the regime relevant for Section \ref{sec:artin-pole}\!.

The next lemma is a standard function-field input, not a new trace formula. It combines the Grothendieck--Lefschetz trace formula for finite-order lisse Artin sheaves, the usual logarithmic-derivative relation between closed-point sums and Artin \(L\)-functions, Weil's square-root bounds for the nontrivial reciprocal zeros, and ordinary M\"obius inversion from all closed points to primitive degree-\(n\) points; see \cite{Serre1981,Rosen2002}. Lemma \ref{prop:classical-artin-conventions} states the exact Frobenius, sign, local-factor, and primitive closed-point conventions used in the arithmetic theorem chain. The role of the lemma below is to put these classical ingredients into the normalization used by the quotient-entropy recovery theorem.

\begin{lemma}[Primitive trace formula for function fields]\label{lem:function-field-primitive-trace-formula}
Let \(C/\FF_q\), \(U\subseteq C\), and \(L/K\) be as in Proposition \ref{prop:function-field-chebotarev-realization}. For each character \(\rho\in\widehat G\), put
\[
T_{r,\rho}:=\sum_{x\in U(\FF_{q^r})}\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_x),
\]
where geometric Frobenius is evaluated at the closed point below \(x\). Suppose the trace formula has the form
\[
T_{r,\rho}=q^r\mathbf 1_{\rho=\ind}-\Tr(F_\rho^r)+O(1)
\]
with all eigenvalues of \(F_\rho\) of modulus at most \(q^{1/2}\) for \(\rho\neq\ind\), and with the same assertion for the nontrivial part of the trivial channel after the constant sheaf contribution is separated. Then, for every subset \(A\subseteq G\),
\[
B_{n,A}(1)
=
\frac{|A|}{|G|}\frac{q^n}{n}
-\frac1n\sum_{\rho\in\widehat G\setminus\{\ind\}}
\left(\frac1{|G|}\sum_{a\in A}\overline{\rho(a)}\right)
\Tr(F_\rho^n)
+O\!\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right),
\]
uniformly in \(A\) and \(n\). Equivalently, after taking the channel matrix \(M_{K,\rho}(1)=F_\rho\) and the linear functional
\[
L_{A,\rho}(T):=-\left(\frac1{|G|}\sum_{a\in A}\overline{\rho(a)}\right)\Tr(T),
\]
the subset counts satisfy the channel expansion in Assumption \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-exp} at \(u=1\), with \(\lambda_1(1)=q\), \(\Lambda(1)\le q^{1/2}\), and \(\Theta(1)=q^{1/2}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
P_{n,\rho}:=\sum_{\substack{v\in |U|\\ \deg v=n}}\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_v).
\]
Each \(\FF_{q^r}\)-point lying above a closed point \(v\) of degree \(d\mid r\) contributes \(d\) times and has Frobenius \(\mathrm{Frob}_v^{r/d}\). Hence
\[
T_{r,\rho}=\sum_{d\mid r}d\sum_{\substack{v\in |U|\\ \deg v=d}}\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_v^{r/d}).
\]
The summand with \(d=r\) is \(rP_{r,\rho}\). Thus
\[
nP_{n,\rho}=T_{n,\rho}-\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\ d<n}}d\sum_{\substack{v\in |U|\\ \deg v=d}}\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_v^{n/d}).
\]
The absolute value of the proper-divisor contribution is at most
\[
\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\ d<n}}d\#\{v\in |U|:\deg v=d\}
\le \sum_{d\le n/2}\#U(\FF_{q^d})
=O(q^{n/2}),
\]
where the constant depends only on \(U\). Therefore
\[
P_{n,\rho}=\frac1nT_{n,\rho}+O\!\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right)
\]
uniformly in \(\rho\). Substituting the trace formula gives
\[
P_{n,\rho}=\mathbf 1_{\rho=\ind}\frac{q^n}{n}-\frac1n\Tr(F_\rho^n)+O\!\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right),
\]
with the trace term absorbed into the same error for all non-dominant trivial-channel eigenvalues and bounded uniformly for nontrivial \(\rho\).

Since \(G\) is abelian, character orthogonality gives
\[
\mathbf 1_A(g)=\sum_{\rho\in\widehat G}\widehat{\mathbf 1_A}(\rho)\rho(g),
\qquad
\widehat{\mathbf 1_A}(\rho):=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{a\in A}\overline{\rho(a)}.
\]
Consequently
\[
B_{n,A}(1)=\sum_{\rho\in\widehat G}\widehat{\mathbf 1_A}(\rho)P_{n,\rho}.
\]
The trivial character has coefficient \(|A|/|G|\) and supplies \((|A|/|G|)q^n/n\). The nontrivial characters give the displayed finite sum of trace functionals. Because \(|\widehat{\mathbf 1_A}(\rho)|\le1\) and \(|\widehat G|=|G|\) is fixed, the accumulated error remains \(O(q^{n/2}/n)\), uniformly in \(A\). The eigenvalue bound gives \(\Lambda(1)\le q^{1/2}\), and the same divisor estimate gives \(\Theta(1)=q^{1/2}\).
\end{proof}

The following proposition is auxiliary bookkeeping only: it records, after the arithmetic core has already been proved, that the preceding abstract hypotheses are realized by the same standard function-field covers. Its proof uses only standard inputs: effective Chebotarev density for global function fields, Grothendieck--Lefschetz trace, Artin formalism for abelian covers, Weil bounds for curves, and the local-factor separation at the finitely many omitted boundary points. It is not a substitute for Theorem \ref{thm:projective-finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography} or Corollary \ref{thm:finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography}; no direct finite-field theorem in the paper depends on the abstract spectral assumptions. The paper's contribution is the quotient \(\chi^2\)- and entropy inversion recovering exact-kernel Artin energies.

\begin{proposition}[Standard function-field realization of the auxiliary hypotheses]\label{prop:function-field-chebotarev-realization}
Let \(C/\FF_q\) be a smooth projective geometrically connected curve, let \(K=\FF_q(C)\), and let \(L/K\) be a finite geometric abelian Galois extension with group \(G\), unramified over the nonempty open curve \(U\subseteq C\). For \(c\in G\), define
\[
B_{n,c}(1):=\#\{v\in |U|:\deg v=n,\ \mathrm{Frob}_v=c\}.
\]
Then Assumption \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-exp} holds at \(u=1\) with
\[
\lambda_1(1)=q,
\qquad
\Lambda(1)\le q^{1/2}.
\]
Moreover, if \(\mathcal L(U,\rho,z)\) denotes the usual Artin \(L\)-function of the character \(\rho\in\widehat G\), and the determinantal convention
\[
L_{K,\rho}(z,1):=\mathcal L(U,\rho,z),
\qquad
\zeta_{K,G}(z,1):=\prod_{\rho\in\widehat G}L_{K,\rho}(z,1)^{\dim\rho}
\]
is used, then Assumption \ref{prop:kernel-artin-factorization} also holds. In particular, the arithmetic results of Sections \ref{sec:entropy-chain} and \ref{sec:artin-pole} apply unconditionally to this function-field class, with the Artin convention just specified.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The effective Chebotarev theorem for global function fields gives, uniformly in \(c\in G\),
\[
\#\{v\in |U|:\deg v=n,\ \mathrm{Frob}_v=c\}
=
\frac{1}{|G|}\frac{q^n}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right),
\]
where the implied constant depends on the cover but not on \(c\) or \(n\); see \cite[Chapter 9]{Rosen2002} and \cite{Ishibashi1992}. Removing the finitely many points in \(C\setminus U\) changes the degree-\(n\) count by \(O(1)\), which is absorbed by \(O(q^{n/2}/n)\) after changing the implied constant for the finitely many small values of \(n\). Thus
\[
B_{n,c}(1)
=
\frac{1}{|G|}\frac{q^n}{n}
+O\!\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right),
\]
and summing over \(c\) gives
\[
B_n(\ind;1)
=
\frac{q^n}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right).
\]
Thus the first displayed part of Assumption \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-exp} holds with \(\lambda_1(1)=q\) and \(\Lambda(1)\le q^{1/2}\).

For subset counts and channel functionals, Lemma \ref{lem:function-field-primitive-trace-formula} applies to the trace formula for the finite-order lisse sheaves attached to the characters of \(G\). It gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{n,A}(1)
&=\frac{|A|}{|G|}\frac{q^n}{n}
+\frac1n\sum_{\rho\in\widehat G\setminus\{\ind\}}
L_{A,\rho}\!\bigl(M_{K,\rho}(1)^n\bigr)\\
&\qquad +O\!\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right)
\end{aligned}
\]
uniformly in \(A\), with \(\Theta(1)=q^{1/2}\). This is precisely the second displayed part of Assumption \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-exp}.

Finally, Artin formalism for the abelian cover gives the standard factorization of the Hasse--Weil Artin product
\[
\mathcal Z_{K,G}(z)=\prod_{\rho\in\widehat G}\mathcal L(U,\rho,z)^{\dim\rho}.
\]
For curves, each \(\mathcal L(U,\rho,z)\), after the standard separation of the trivial channel and the finitely many boundary local factors, is a determinant expression \(\det(I-zM_{K,\rho}(1))^{-1}\) for a finite-dimensional Frobenius matrix. This is exactly the convention in the statement, and therefore
\[
\zeta_{K,G}(z,1)=\prod_{\rho\in\widehat G}L_{K,\rho}(z,1)^{\dim\rho},
\]
with the nontrivial quotient formula following by division by the trivial factor. This is Assumption \ref{prop:kernel-artin-factorization}.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite Fold-Groupoid Quotient Model}\label{sec:groupoid-rigidity}
This appendix is optional finite-dimensional comparison material. It is logically independent of Sections \ref{sec:entropy-chain}--\ref{sec:artin-pole}: none of its definitions, propositions, or computations is used in the proof of the Chebotarev entropy tomography theorem, the reciprocal-zero formula, the exact-kernel recovery result, or the persistent-energy criterion. Readers interested only in the arithmetic results may skip Appendices \ref{sec:groupoid-rigidity}--\ref{app:window6}. We now describe the ambient fold groupoid algebra and the compatible automorphisms relevant for its separate scan-reversal fingerprint calculation.

\subsection{The Fold-Groupoid Algebra}
\begin{definition}[Fold Groupoid Algebra]\label{def:fold-equivalence-groupoid-algebra}
For each resolution \(m\), let \(\Fold_m:\Omega_m\to X_m\) be the fold map from microstates to stable macrostates, where \(\Omega_m\) is a binary microstate set of cardinality \(2^m\). The associated equivalence-relation groupoid is
\[
\mathcal R_m:=\Omega_m\times_{X_m}\Omega_m
=
\{(\omega,\omega')\in \Omega_m\times\Omega_m:\Fold_m(\omega)=\Fold_m(\omega')\},
\]
and we write
\[
A_{m,1}:=\CC[\mathcal R_m]
\]
for its complex groupoid algebra. Concretely, \(A_{m,1}\) is the complex vector space with basis \(\{[\omega,\omega']:(\omega,\omega')\in\mathcal R_m\}\), equipped with convolution product and involution
\[
[\omega,\omega']\,[\eta,\eta']
:=
\delta_{\omega',\eta}\,[\omega,\eta'],
\qquad
[\omega,\omega']^\ast:=[\omega',\omega].
\]
The fiber multiplicity at \(x\in X_m\) is
\[
d_m(x):=\abs{\Fold_m^{-1}(x)},
\qquad
D_m:=\max_{x\in X_m}d_m(x),
\qquad
\mathcal M_m:=\{x\in X_m:d_m(x)=D_m\}.
\]
We regard \(\Omega_m=\{0,1\}^m\) with its lexicographic order. This order induces, on each nonempty fiber \(F_x:=\Fold_m^{-1}(x)\), a list
\[
F_x=\{\omega_{x,1}<\omega_{x,2}<\cdots <\omega_{x,d_m(x)}\}.
\]
The distinguished scan permutation and reversal permutation are defined by
\[
c_x(\omega_{x,j})=\omega_{x,j+1}\quad(1\le j<d_m(x)),
\qquad
c_x(\omega_{x,d_m(x)})=\omega_{x,1},
\]
\[
\iota_x(\omega_{x,j})=\omega_{x,d_m(x)+1-j}.
\]
Thus \(c_x\) is the cyclic scan through the ordered fiber and \(\iota_x^2=\mathrm{id}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fold-groupoid-wedderburn}
There is a canonical \(\ast\)-isomorphism
\[
A_{m,1}\cong \bigoplus_{x\in X_m}\End\!\bigl(\CC[\Fold_m^{-1}(x)]\bigr),
\]
and hence, after choosing bases on the fiber spaces, a \(\ast\)-isomorphism
\[
A_{m,1}\cong \bigoplus_{x\in X_m}M_{d_m(x)}(\CC).
\]
For each \(x\in X_m\), we denote by \(\widetilde P_x\in Z(A_{m,1})\) the minimal central idempotent projecting onto the block \(M_{d_m(x)}(\CC)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each \(x\in X_m\), let \(H_x:=\CC[\Fold_m^{-1}(x)]\) with basis \(\{\delta_\omega:\omega\in\Fold_m^{-1}(x)\}\). The subgroupoid
\[
\mathcal R_m(x):=\Fold_m^{-1}(x)\times\Fold_m^{-1}(x)
\]
is the full pair groupoid on the fiber, and the assignment
\[
[\omega,\omega']\longmapsto \bigl(\delta_\eta\mapsto \delta_{\omega',\eta}\,\delta_\omega\bigr)
\]
identifies \(\CC[\mathcal R_m(x)]\) with \(\End(H_x)\): on the distinguished basis, the image of \([\omega,\omega']\) is the rank-one operator \(E_{\omega,\omega'}\), and the formulas for multiplication and involution match the matrix-unit relations. Since
\[
\mathcal R_m=\bigsqcup_{x\in X_m}\mathcal R_m(x),
\]
the groupoid algebra is the direct sum of the fiber algebras, which gives the canonical decomposition into endomorphism algebras. Choosing bases on the \(H_x\) then identifies \(\End(H_x)\) with \(M_{d_m(x)}(\CC)\); see also \cite[Chapter 1]{Renault1980} or \cite[Section 4.1]{Paterson1999}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fold-groupoid-z2x2-sector-by-orientation}
For each \(x\in X_m\), let \(c_x\) and \(\iota_x\) be the scan and reversal permutations of the fiber \(\Fold_m^{-1}(x)\). Define the fingerprint
\[
\chi(x):=\bigl(\operatorname{sgn}(c_x),\operatorname{sgn}(\iota_x)\bigr)\in\{\pm1\}^2.
\]
For \((\alpha,\beta)\in\{\pm1\}^2\), set
\[
X_m^{\alpha,\beta}:=\{x\in X_m:\chi(x)=(\alpha,\beta)\},
\qquad
\CC[\mathcal R_m]^{\alpha,\beta}:=\bigoplus_{x\in X_m^{\alpha,\beta}}M_{d_m(x)}(\CC).
\]
Then
\[
A_{m,1}=\bigoplus_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\{\pm1\}^2}\CC[\mathcal R_m]^{\alpha,\beta}
\]
is the sector decomposition determined by the scan-reversal fingerprint.
\end{proposition}

Only two features of the distinguished permutations \(c_x\) and \(\iota_x\) will matter below: their signatures and their behavior under the compatible \(\ast\)-automorphisms considered in this section. No further combinatorics of the fold map enters the argument.

\subsection{Compatible Automorphisms}
\begin{proposition}[Supporting automorphism classification]\label{thm:fold-groupoid-aut-semidirect-pi0}
Let
\[
\Pi_0:=\{\sigma\in \mathrm{Sym}(X_m):d_m(\sigma(x))=d_m(x)\ \forall x\in X_m\}.
\]
Then the \(\ast\)-automorphism group of \(A_{m,1}\) is naturally isomorphic to
\[
\Aut^\ast(A_{m,1})
\cong
\Bigl(\prod_{x\in X_m}\mathrm{PU}(d_m(x))\Bigr)\rtimes \Pi_0.
\]
Equivalently, a \(\ast\)-automorphism acts by blockwise projective-unitary conjugation together with a permutation of blocks of equal size.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By finite-dimensional \(C^\ast\)-algebra structure theory, every \(\ast\)-automorphism of \(\bigoplus_x M_{d_m(x)}(\CC)\) permutes the minimal central projections; see \cite[Section 2.1]{BrownOzawa2008}. Compatibility with matrix size forces the induced permutation of blocks to lie in \(\Pi_0\). After that permutation is fixed, the restriction to each simple summand \(M_{d_m(x)}(\CC)\) is a \(\ast\)-automorphism of a full matrix algebra, hence inner, and is therefore represented by conjugation with a unitary unique modulo scalars. This yields the factor \(\prod_x\mathrm{PU}(d_m(x))\), while \(\Pi_0\) acts by reindexing the blocks.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Preparatory Material}
We begin by isolating the fold-specific input and the basic facts about the relevant conditional expectations.

\begin{definition}[The concrete finite fold model]\label{def:concrete-finite-fold-model}
The concrete fold family used in the reconstruction results is the following finite-dimensional stress-test model. For every \(m\), set \(\Omega_m:=\{0,1\}^m\) with lexicographic order. If \(m\ne6\), set \(X_m:=\{\ast\}\) and let \(\Fold_m\) be the constant map. If \(m=6\), list
\[
\Omega_6=\{\omega_1<\omega_2<\cdots<\omega_{64}\}
\]
lexicographically, put
\[
X_6:=\{(2,i):1\le i\le8\}\sqcup\{(3,i):1\le i\le4\}\sqcup\{(4,i):1\le i\le9\},
\]
and define the consecutive intervals
\[
B_{2,i}:=\{\omega_{2i-1},\omega_{2i}\}\qquad(1\le i\le8),
\]
\[
B_{3,i}:=\{\omega_{16+3(i-1)+1},\omega_{16+3(i-1)+2},\omega_{16+3(i-1)+3}\}\qquad(1\le i\le4),
\]
\[
B_{4,i}:=\{\omega_{28+4(i-1)+1},\omega_{28+4(i-1)+2},\omega_{28+4(i-1)+3},\omega_{28+4(i-1)+4}\}\qquad(1\le i\le9).
\]
Then \(\Fold_6(\omega):=(j,i)\) exactly when \(\omega\in B_{j,i}\). This definition is intentionally finite and enumerative; the fold-groupoid theorems below use it only through the fiber data proved in Proposition \ref{prop:concrete-finite-fold-data}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Concrete finite fold data]\label{prop:concrete-finite-fold-data}
For the fold family of Definition \ref{def:concrete-finite-fold-model}, the maximal-fiber fingerprint is constant on \(\mathcal M_m\) for every \(m\). For the lexicographic scan and reversal of Definition \ref{def:fold-equivalence-groupoid-algebra}, the common fingerprint on maximal fibers is
\[
\chi_m=\bigl((-1)^{D_m-1},\,(-1)^{D_m(D_m-1)/2}\bigr).
\]
For \(m=6\), the fiber data are
\[
d_6(x)\in\{2,3,4\},
\]
\[
\#\{x:d_6(x)=2\}=8,\qquad
\#\{x:d_6(x)=3\}=4,\qquad
\#\{x:d_6(x)=4\}=9.
\]
Equivalently, \(|X_6|=21\) and
\[
\sum_{x\in X_6}d_6(x)=2\cdot8+3\cdot4+4\cdot9=64=|\Omega_6|.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(m\ne6\), the map is constant by Definition \ref{def:concrete-finite-fold-model}. Thus \(D_m=2^m\) and \(\mathcal M_m=\{\ast\}\), so the maximal-fiber fingerprint is automatically constant.

For \(m=6\), the displayed sets in Definition \ref{def:concrete-finite-fold-model} are pairwise disjoint consecutive intervals in the lexicographic list: the first family occupies indices \(1,\ldots,16\), the second occupies \(17,\ldots,28\), and the third occupies \(29,\ldots,64\). Their cardinalities are respectively \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\), and the endpoint count
\[
16+12+36=64
\]
shows that they partition all of \(\Omega_6\). Since \(\Fold_6\) is defined to be constant exactly on each interval \(B_{j,i}\) and to take distinct values \((j,i)\) on distinct intervals, its fibers are precisely these intervals. Hence there are eight fibers of size \(2\), four fibers of size \(3\), and nine fibers of size \(4\). The maximal fibers are exactly the nine sets \(B_{4,i}\), all of size \(D_6=4\), and therefore all maximal fibers have the same scan and reversal signs.

It remains only to compute those signs uniformly. On an ordered fiber of size \(d\), the scan permutation is a \(d\)-cycle and has sign \((-1)^{d-1}\). The reversal permutation pairs the first point with the last, the second with the penultimate, and so on, so it is a product of \(\lfloor d/2\rfloor\) disjoint transpositions and has sign \((-1)^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}\). Since \(\lfloor d/2\rfloor\equiv d(d-1)/2\pmod 2\), this sign is also \((-1)^{d(d-1)/2}\). Applying these formulas with \(d=D_m\) on maximal fibers gives the displayed formula for \(\chi_m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fold-condexp-index-maxfiber}
Let \(E_m\) be the conditional expectation induced by \(\Fold_m\), that is, averaging uniformly on the fibers of \(\Fold_m\). For a probability distribution \(\rho\) on \(\Omega_m\), write \(\rho_X:=(\Fold_m)_\ast\rho\) and define the fiberwise-uniform distribution
\[
\rho^\flat(\omega):=\frac{\rho_X(\Fold_m(\omega))}{d_m(\Fold_m(\omega))}
\qquad(\omega\in \Omega_m).
\]
Equivalently, \(\rho^\flat\) has the same pushforward to \(X_m\) as \(\rho\) and is uniform on each fiber of \(\Fold_m\). Then
\[
\sup_{\rho}D(\rho\Vert \rho^\flat)=\log D_m,
\]
where \(\rho\) ranges over probability distributions on \(\Omega_m\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each \(x\in X_m\), let \(u_x\) denote the uniform distribution on the fiber \(\Fold_m^{-1}(x)\). Since \(\rho^\flat\) has the same macroscopic marginal as \(\rho\) and fiberwise conditionals \(u_x\), the relative-entropy chain rule gives
\[
D(\rho\Vert \rho^\flat)
=
\sum_{x\in X_m}\rho_X(x)\,
D\!\bigl(\rho(\cdot\mid x)\Vert u_x\bigr).
\]
For each \(x\) and each probability distribution \(\sigma\) on \(\Fold_m^{-1}(x)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
D(\sigma\Vert u_x)
&=
\sum_{\omega\in\Fold_m^{-1}(x)}\sigma(\omega)\log\!\bigl(\sigma(\omega)d_m(x)\bigr)\\
&=
\log d_m(x)+\sum_{\omega\in\Fold_m^{-1}(x)}\sigma(\omega)\log \sigma(\omega)\\
&\le
\log d_m(x),
\end{aligned}
\]
because \(\sum_\omega \sigma(\omega)\log \sigma(\omega)\le 0\). Hence
\[
D(\rho\Vert \rho^\flat)\le \log D_m
\]
for every \(\rho\). Conversely, choose \(x_\star\in X_m\) with \(d_m(x_\star)=D_m\), and let \(\rho\) be a Dirac mass on a point of \(\Fold_m^{-1}(x_\star)\). Then \(\rho^\flat\) is the uniform distribution on that same fiber, so
\[
D(\rho\Vert \rho^\flat)=\log D_m.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fold-groupoid-block-expectation}
Under the Wedderburn identification
\[
A_{m,1}\cong \bigoplus_{x\in X_m}M_{d_m(x)}(\CC),
\]
define
\[
E_{m,1}\bigl((A_x)_{x\in X_m}\bigr)
:=
\bigoplus_{x\in X_m}\frac{\Tr(A_x)}{d_m(x)}\,I_{d_m(x)}.
\]
Then \(E_{m,1}:A_{m,1}\to Z(A_{m,1})\) is a faithful conditional expectation. In the sense of \cite{Watatani1990}, its Watatani index element is
\[
\mathrm{IndW}(E_{m,1})=\sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^2\,\widetilde P_x.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each block \(M_{d_m(x)}(\CC)\), fix matrix units \(\{e_{ij}^{(x)}\}_{1\le i,j\le d_m(x)}\). The displayed formula is the normalized trace on each simple summand, so it defines a faithful conditional expectation onto the direct sum of scalar matrices, which is exactly \(Z(A_{m,1})\).

Moreover,
\[
E_{m,1}(e_{ij}^{(x)})=\delta_{ij}\,d_m(x)^{-1}\widetilde P_x.
\]
A direct computation shows that
\[
\Bigl\{\bigl(\sqrt{d_m(x)}\,e_{ij}^{(x)},\sqrt{d_m(x)}\,e_{ji}^{(x)}\bigr): x\in X_m,\ 1\le i,j\le d_m(x)\Bigr\}
\]
is a quasi-basis for \(E_{m,1}\): on the \(x\)-block, if \(A_x=\sum_{k,\ell}a_{k\ell}^{(x)}e_{k\ell}^{(x)}\), then
\[
\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_m(x)}\sqrt{d_m(x)}\,e_{ij}^{(x)}
\,E_{m,1}\!\bigl(\sqrt{d_m(x)}\,e_{ji}^{(x)}A_x\bigr)
=
\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_m(x)}a_{ij}^{(x)}e_{ij}^{(x)}
=
A_x,
\]
and similarly on the right. Hence \(E_{m,1}\) has finite Watatani index and
\[
\mathrm{IndW}(E_{m,1})
=
\sum_{x\in X_m}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_m(x)}d_m(x)\,e_{ij}^{(x)}e_{ji}^{(x)}
=
\sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^2\,\widetilde P_x.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:fold-center-resolution-criterion-indw-eps}]

\end{aligned}

Therefore every element of \(Z_m^{(3)}\) takes on the \(x\)-block a value depending only on \(\chi(x)\) and \(d_m(x)^2\), and within each fixed sector it is a polynomial function of \(d_m^2\). Multiplication by \(e_{\alpha,\beta}\) simply restricts such a function to the sector \(\chi=(\alpha,\beta)\), which gives the first statement.

Fix a sector \((\alpha,\beta)\). On that sector, the algebra \(\CC[\mathrm{IndW}(E_{m,1})]\,e_{\alpha,\beta}\) consists exactly of the functions of the scalar \(d_m(x)^2\). Since the map \(d\mapsto d^2\) is injective on \(\NN\), these functions separate precisely the distinct values in
\[
V_{\alpha,\beta}:=\{d_m(x):\chi(x)=(\alpha,\beta)\}.
\]
Lagrange interpolation on the finite set
\[
V_{\alpha,\beta}^2:=\{d^2:d\in V_{\alpha,\beta}\}
\]
therefore shows that
\[
\dim_{\CC}\bigl(\CC[\mathrm{IndW}(E_{m,1})]\,e_{\alpha,\beta}\bigr)=|V_{\alpha,\beta}|.
\]
Summing over the four sectors yields the second assertion. Since \(\dim_{\CC}Z_m=|X_m|\), the third assertion follows immediately: one has \(Z_m^{(3)}=Z_m\) if and only if there are no collisions of the values \(d_m(x)\) within any fixed sector.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:fold-window6-chi-layered-spectrum-reconstruction-by-12-moments}]
By Theorem \ref{thm:fold-groupoid-z2x2-joint-spectral-measure}, the quantities \(M_{s,t}(q)\) are exactly the signed odd-power moments of the \(\chi\)-layered fiber spectrum. Fourier-Hadamard inversion therefore gives, for each \((\alpha,\beta)\in\{\pm1\}^2\),
\[
p_{2r+1}^{\alpha,\beta}
=
\frac14\sum_{s,t\in\{0,1\}}\alpha^s\beta^t\,M_{s,t}(r),
\qquad r=0,1,2.
\]

When \(m=6\), Corollary \ref{cor:fold-bin-m6-fiber-hist} shows that the only possible fiber sizes are \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\). Hence for a fixed sector \((\alpha,\beta)\), the counts \(n_2^{\alpha,\beta}\), \(n_3^{\alpha,\beta}\), and \(n_4^{\alpha,\beta}\) satisfy
\[
p_1^{\alpha,\beta}=2n_2^{\alpha,\beta}+3n_3^{\alpha,\beta}+4n_4^{\alpha,\beta},
\]
\[
p_3^{\alpha,\beta}=8n_2^{\alpha,\beta}+27n_3^{\alpha,\beta}+64n_4^{\alpha,\beta},
\]
\[
p_5^{\alpha,\beta}=32n_2^{\alpha,\beta}+243n_3^{\alpha,\beta}+1024n_4^{\alpha,\beta}.
\]
The coefficient matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
2&3&4\\
8&27&64\\
32&243&1024
\end{pmatrix}
\]
has determinant \(10080\neq 0\), so the system is invertible. Direct inversion yields the three formulas displayed in Theorem \ref{thm:fold-window6-chi-layered-spectrum-reconstruction-by-12-moments}.

Finally, because the only fiber sizes are \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\), the fiber-spectrum polynomial in the sector \((\alpha,\beta)\) can only have the linear factors \(1-2z\), \(1-3z\), and \(1-4z\), with exponents \(n_2^{\alpha,\beta}\), \(n_3^{\alpha,\beta}\), and \(n_4^{\alpha,\beta}\), respectively. This gives the final factorization of \(Q_6^{\alpha,\beta}(z)\).
\end{proof}
