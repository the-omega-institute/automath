\begin{definition}[Finite-field quotient notation]\label{def:finite-field-quotient-notation}
Let \(U/\FF_q\) be a smooth geometrically connected curve and let \(V\to U\) be a geometrically connected finite \etale{} abelian Galois cover with group \(G\). For \(c\in G\) put
\[
B_n(c):=\#\{v\in |U|:\deg v=n,\ \mathrm{Frob}_v=c\},
\qquad
N_n:=\sum_{c\in G}B_n(c),
\]
and, for all \(n\) with \(N_n>0\), define the primitive Frobenius-label law
\[
\mu_n(c):=\frac{B_n(c)}{N_n}\qquad(c\in G).
\]
By Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-eventual-positive-degrees} below, there is an integer \(n_0(U)\) such that \(N_n>0\) for every \(n\ge n_0(U)\). All later assertions involving \(\mu_n\) are made only in this range.
For a subgroup \(H\le G\) write
\[
\pi_H:G\twoheadrightarrow G/H,
\qquad
\mu_n^H:=(\pi_H)_\ast\mu_n.
\]
For \(\chi\in\widehat G\) define the twisted degree-\(n\) prime sum and its square-root normalization by
\[
B_n(\chi):=\sum_{c\in G}\chi(c)B_n(c),
\qquad
A_n(\chi):=\frac{nB_n(\chi)}{q^{n/2}}.
\]
Finally, for \(K\le G\), define the exact-kernel Artin energy
\[
E_n(K):=
\sum_{\substack{\chi\in\widehat G\setminus\{\ind\}\\ \ker\chi=K}}
|A_n(\chi)|^2,
\]
and denote by \(\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(K,J)\) the M\"obius function of the finite subgroup lattice of \(G\), ordered by inclusion.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Eventual positivity of primitive degrees]\label{lem:finite-field-eventual-positive-degrees}
Let \(U/\FF_q\) be a smooth geometrically connected curve. With
\[
N_n=\#\{v\in |U|:\deg v=n\},
\]
there is an integer \(n_0(U)\) such that \(N_n>0\) for every \(n\ge n_0(U)\). More precisely,
\[
N_n=\frac{q^n}{n}+O_U\!\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The closed-point identity
\[
\#U(\FF_{q^r})=\sum_{d\mid r}dN_d
\]
and M\"obius inversion give
\[
nN_n=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\#U(\FF_{q^{n/d}}),
\]
where \(\mu\) is the ordinary integer M\"obius function. The Weil bound for the smooth projective compactification of \(U\), together with the finitely many removed boundary points, gives
\[
\#U(\FF_{q^m})=q^m+O_U(q^{m/2})+O_U(1).
\]
The term \(d=1\) is therefore \(q^n+O_U(q^{n/2})\), while the sum of all terms with \(d>1\) is \(O_U(q^{n/2})\). Hence \(nN_n=q^n+O_U(q^{n/2})\), which proves the asymptotic. For all sufficiently large \(n\), the main term dominates the error, so \(N_n>0\).
\end{proof}

The next proposition is the explicit-formula input in the normalization used by
the entropy theorem.  It separates three sources that occur at different
scales: the square-root normalized reciprocal-zero term of the Artin factor on
the compact curve, the imprimitive contribution from proper divisors of \(n\),
and the finite boundary correction caused by replacing a projective curve by an
open curve.

\begin{proposition}[Classical Artin conventions for finite-field covers]\label{prop:classical-artin-conventions}
Let \(j:U\hookrightarrow C\) be a smooth geometrically connected curve over
\(\FF_q\) inside its smooth projective compactification, and let
\(V\to U\) be a geometrically connected finite \etale{} abelian Galois cover
with group \(G\).  We use geometric Frobenius: for a closed point
\(v\in |U|\), \(\mathrm{Frob}_v\in G\) is the inverse of the arithmetic
Frobenius acting on the geometric fibre, and for an
\(\FF_{q^r}\)-point above \(v\) with \(d=\deg v\mid r\) the trace contribution
is \(\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_v^{r/d})\).  For a character
\(\chi\in\widehat G\), the open Artin factor is normalized by
\[
L(U,\chi,T)
=
\prod_{v\in |U|}
\left(1-\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_v)T^{\deg v}\right)^{-1}
=
\prod_j(1-\alpha_{\chi,j}T).
\]
With this sign convention,
\[
\sum_{d\mid r}d\sum_{\substack{v\in |U|\\ \deg v=d}}
\chi\!\left(\mathrm{Frob}_v^{r/d}\right)
=
-\sum_j\alpha_{\chi,j}^{\,r}
\]
for every nontrivial \(\chi\) after the trivial constant-sheaf factor has been
separated.  For \(\chi\ne\ind\), every nontrivial compact reciprocal zero has
absolute value at most \(q^{1/2}\).  If \(S=C\setminus U\), then replacing the
open factor by the compact middle-extension factor on \(C\) changes the
degree-\(r\) logarithmic-derivative coefficient by a sum of local boundary
terms supported at \(S\); each boundary local factor has degree at most
\(\deg s\), and all its reciprocal roots have absolute value at most \(1\).
Finally, primitive degree-\(n\) closed points are always counted with exact
degree \(n\); hence the passage from all \(\FF_{q^r}\)-points to primitive
closed points is the ordinary divisor relation above, or equivalently its
M\"obius inversion on divisors.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
These are the standard Artin \(L\)-function conventions for finite-order
lisse sheaves on curves over finite fields.  The Euler product is the usual
one for geometric Frobenius.  Taking \(T\,d/dT\) of the product gives
\[
T\frac{L'(U,\chi,T)}{L(U,\chi,T)}
=
\sum_{r\ge1}
\left(
\sum_{d\mid r}d\sum_{\substack{v\in |U|\\ \deg v=d}}
\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_v^{r/d})
\right)T^r,
\]
where the exponent \(r/d\) records the number of times geometric Frobenius at
\(v\) acts on an \(\FF_{q^r}\)-point lying over \(v\).  If
\(L(U,\chi,T)=\prod_j(1-\alpha_{\chi,j}T)\), then the same logarithmic
derivative equals \(-\sum_{r\ge1}(\sum_j\alpha_{\chi,j}^{r})T^r\), proving the
displayed sign.  The Grothendieck--Lefschetz trace formula and Deligne--Weil
purity for finite-order sheaves on curves give the reciprocal-zero bound on
the compact middle-extension factor; in the rank-one Artin setting used here
this is the classical function-field Artin formalism recorded, for example, in
Rosen's treatment of Artin factors and Serre's Chebotarev formalism
\cite{Rosen2002,Serre1981}.  Passing from \(C\) to \(U\) removes only the
finitely many Euler factors at points of \(S\).  At a boundary point \(s\), the
factor is the characteristic polynomial of geometric Frobenius on the
inertia-invariant stalk.  Its degree is at most the degree of the closed point
when written over \(\FF_q\), and finite monodromy makes its reciprocal roots
roots of unity or zero.  The closed-point degree convention follows from the
identity that an exact degree-\(d\) closed point contributes precisely \(d\)
geometric points to \(U(\FF_{q^r})\) when \(d\mid r\), and contributes none
otherwise.  M\"obius inversion on the divisor poset is therefore the primitive
closed-point inversion used throughout the paper.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Normalized reciprocal-zero formula and scale separation]\label{prop:finite-field-normalized-reciprocal-zero-formula}
Assume the notation of Definition \ref{def:finite-field-quotient-notation}. Let \(\chi\in\widehat G\setminus\{\ind\}\). After the standard separation of the trivial channel, write the nontrivial Artin factor attached to \(\chi\) as
\[
L(U,\chi,T)=\prod_{j=1}^{m_\chi}(1-\alpha_{\chi,j}T)
\]
for the open curve \(U\). Then
\[
A_n(\chi)
=
-q^{-n/2}\sum_{j=1}^{m_\chi}\alpha_{\chi,j}^{\,n}
+
R_n(\chi),
\]
where
\[
R_n(\chi):=
-q^{-n/2}
\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\ d<n}}
 d\sum_{\substack{v\in |U|\\ \deg v=d}}
 \chi\!\left(\mathrm{Frob}_v^{n/d}\right).
\]
The term \(-q^{-n/2}\sum_j\alpha_{\chi,j}^{\,n}\) is the normalized Artin-zero contribution. The term \(R_n(\chi)\) is exactly the imprimitive proper-divisor contribution and satisfies \(R_n(\chi)=O_U(1)\), uniformly in \(\chi\) and \(n\). If \(U=C\) is projective, there is no boundary contribution. For a nonprojective open \(U\subset C\), comparison with the middle-extension factor on \(C\) changes the logarithmic-derivative coefficient only through the finitely many boundary local factors; after division by \(q^{n/2}\), this boundary correction is \(O_U(q^{-n/2})\). Thus the formula distinguishes the Artin reciprocal-zero term, the proper-divisor imprimitive term at the \(A_n\)-scale, and the exponentially small open-curve boundary/local-factor term.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The logarithmic derivative of the Artin Euler product gives, for every \(r\ge1\),
\[
\sum_{d\mid r}d\sum_{\substack{v\in |U|\\ \deg v=d}}
\chi\!\left(\mathrm{Frob}_v^{r/d}\right)
=
-\sum_{j=1}^{m_\chi}\alpha_{\chi,j}^{\,r}.
\]
Taking \(r=n\) and isolating the term \(d=n\) gives
\[
nB_n(\chi)
=
-\sum_{j=1}^{m_\chi}\alpha_{\chi,j}^{\,n}
-
\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\ d<n}}
 d\sum_{\substack{v\in |U|\\ \deg v=d}}
 \chi\!\left(\mathrm{Frob}_v^{n/d}\right).
\]
Division by \(q^{n/2}\) is the displayed formula for the open-curve Artin factor. Since \(|\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_v^{n/d})|=1\), the proper-divisor sum is bounded in absolute value by
\[
\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\d<n}}d\#\{v\in |U|:\deg v=d\}
\le
\sum_{d\le n/2}\#U(\FF_{q^d})
=O_U(q^{n/2}),
\]
which proves the uniform bound for \(R_n(\chi)\). If \(U\subset C\) is obtained by removing a finite set \(S\), the compact middle-extension factor and the open Artin factor differ by the local factors at \(S\). Each boundary closed point of degree dividing \(n\) contributes at most its degree to the logarithmic-derivative coefficient. Hence the total boundary correction in the degree-\(n\) coefficient is bounded by \(\sum_{s\in S}\deg(s)=O_U(1)\), and after division by \(q^{n/2}\) it is \(O_U(q^{-n/2})\). In the projective case \(S=\emptyset\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform boundary Euler-factor comparison]\label{lem:finite-field-boundary-euler-factor-comparison}
Let \(j:U\hookrightarrow C\) be a smooth curve inside its smooth projective compactification, let \(S=C\setminus U\), and let \(V\to U\) be a finite \etale{} abelian Galois cover with group \(G\). For each \(\chi\in\widehat G\), compare the Artin \(L\)-function on \(U\) with the compactly supported middle-extension factor on \(C\) in the usual function-field Artin formalism. Then their ratio is a finite product of local boundary factors
\[
L(U,\chi,T)=L(C,j_\ast\chi,T)
\prod_{s\in S}P_{s,\chi}(T)^{-1},
\]
where each \(P_{s,\chi}(T)\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(\deg s\) and every reciprocal root has absolute value at most \(1\). Consequently, if \(\Delta_{\partial,n}(\chi)\) denotes the difference between the degree-\(n\) logarithmic-derivative coefficients of the two sides before square-root normalization, then
\[
|\Delta_{\partial,n}(\chi)|
\le \sum_{s\in S}\deg(s)
\]
for all \(n\ge1\), uniformly in \(\chi\in\widehat G\). Equivalently, the induced change in the normalized Artin amplitude is
\[
\left|q^{-n/2}\Delta_{\partial,n}(\chi)\right|
\le \left(\sum_{s\in S}\deg(s)\right)q^{-n/2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a lisse rank-one Artin sheaf attached to \(\chi\), the Euler product for \(L(U,\chi,T)\) is the product over closed points of \(U\), while the middle-extension factor on \(C\) is obtained by adjoining the finitely many local factors at points of \(S\); this is the standard Artin \(L\)-function formalism over function fields, as in Rosen's account of function-field zeta and Artin factors \cite{Rosen2002} or Serre's Chebotarev formalism \cite{Serre1981}. At a boundary point \(s\), the local factor is the characteristic polynomial of geometric Frobenius on the inertia-invariant stalk. The sheaf has rank one before taking invariants, so this local factor has degree at most one over the residue field of \(s\), equivalently at most \(\deg s\) when written as a polynomial in \(T\) over \(\FF_q\). Its eigenvalues are roots of unity or zero because the cover is finite \etale{} and the monodromy group is finite, so every reciprocal root contributing to \(-T(P'_{s,\chi}/P_{s,\chi})\) has absolute value at most \(1\). The coefficient of \(T^n\) in the logarithmic derivative of a degree-\(d\) polynomial all of whose reciprocal roots have modulus at most \(1\) is bounded by \(d\). Summing over the finitely many boundary points gives the displayed uniform bound, and division by \(q^{n/2}\) gives the normalized estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Unitary Artin expansion with complete imprimitive decomposition]\label{prop:finite-field-unitary-artin-expansion}
Assume the notation of Definition \ref{def:finite-field-quotient-notation}. Let \(\chi\in\widehat G\setminus\{\ind\}\) have order \(m_\chi\). For each \(n\), put
\[
\mathcal T_\chi(n):=
\{e:e\mid n,\ e\ge2,\ \mu(e)\ne0,\ \chi^e=\ind\}.
\]
Then
\[
A_n(\chi)
=
-\sum_{\substack{1\le j\le m_\chi\\ |\alpha_{\chi,j}|=q^{1/2}}}
\left(\frac{\alpha_{\chi,j}}{q^{1/2}}\right)^n
+\sum_{e\in\mathcal T_\chi(n)}
\mu(e)q^{-n/2}\#U(\FF_{q^{n/e}})
+e_n^{\mathrm{imp}}(\chi),
\]
where
\[
\frac1N\sum_{n\le N}|e_n^{\mathrm{imp}}(\chi)|^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
The displayed finite sum is the complete trivial-channel imprimitive contribution. Its term with \(e=2\), present exactly for quadratic \(\chi\) and even \(n\), equals
\[
-\mathbf 1_{2\mid n}+O_U(q^{-n/4})+O_U(q^{-n/2})
= -\frac{1+(-1)^n}{2}+O_U(q^{-n/4}),
\]
and therefore contributes a unitary leading part. Every term in the same sum with \(e>2\) is exponentially decaying, uniformly after summing over \(e\mid n\). Equivalently, there are finitely many coefficients \(b_{\chi,\zeta}\in\CC\), indexed by unit complex numbers \(\zeta\), and an error term \(e_n(\chi)\) such that
\[
A_n(\chi)=\sum_{\zeta}b_{\chi,\zeta}\zeta^n+e_n(\chi),
\qquad
\frac1N\sum_{n\le N}|e_n(\chi)|^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
The unitary part consists exactly of the reciprocal-zero terms with \(|\alpha_{\chi,j}|=q^{1/2}\) and, when \(\chi\) is quadratic, the imprimitive trivial-channel term \(-(1+(-1)^n)/2\). All remaining proper-divisor, higher-order trivial-channel, nontrivial imprimitive, and boundary contributions are Cesaro-square negligible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
P_n(\psi):=n\sum_{\substack{v\in |U|\\ \deg v=n}}\psi(\mathrm{Frob}_v)
\]
for any character \(\psi\), including the trivial one, and
\[
S_r(\psi):=\sum_{d\mid r}P_d(\psi^{r/d}).
\]
The logarithmic derivative identity used in Proposition \ref{prop:finite-field-normalized-reciprocal-zero-formula} says that, for nontrivial \(\psi\),
\[
S_r(\psi)=-\sum_j\alpha_{\psi,j}^{\,r},
\]
while for the trivial character \(S_r(\ind)=\#U(\FF_{q^r})\). The divisor relation
\[
S_n(\chi)=\sum_{e\mid n}P_{n/e}(\chi^e)
\]
has the M\"obius inverse
\[
P_n(\chi)=\sum_{e\mid n}\mu(e)S_{n/e}(\chi^e).
\]
Indeed, after expanding the right side, the coefficient of \(P_{n/k}(\chi^k)\) is \(\sum_{e\mid k}\mu(e)\), which is \(1\) for \(k=1\) and \(0\) otherwise. Dividing by \(q^{n/2}\) gives an exact expression for \(A_n(\chi)\).

The term \(e=1\) contributes
\[
-q^{-n/2}\sum_j\alpha_{\chi,j}^{\,n}.
\]
The summands with \(|\alpha_{\chi,j}|=q^{1/2}\) are exactly the displayed unitary exponentials; those with \(|\alpha_{\chi,j}|<q^{1/2}\) have Cesaro mean square zero. If \(e\ge2\) and \(\chi^e\ne\ind\), the Weil bound \(|\alpha_{\chi^e,j}|\le q^{1/2}\) gives
\[
q^{-n/2}|S_{n/e}(\chi^e)|=O(q^{-n(e-1)/(2e)})
\]
for each fixed divisor \(e\), and the sum over all such divisors has Cesaro mean square zero. If \(\chi^e=\ind\), then
\[
q^{-n/2}S_{n/e}(\ind)=q^{-n/2}\#U(\FF_{q^{n/e}})=q^{-n(1/2-1/e)}+O_U(q^{-n(1/2-1/(2e))})+O_U(q^{-n/2}).
\]
The condition \(\chi^e=\ind\) is exactly the condition that \(e\) belongs to \(\mathcal T_\chi(n)\), after discarding nonsquarefree values because \(\mu(e)=0\). Thus the displayed sum over \(\mathcal T_\chi(n)\) is the full trivial-channel imprimitive contribution. The estimate above is Cesaro-square negligible for \(e>2\). Indeed \(e>2\) gives \(1/2-1/e\ge1/6\), and the number of divisors of \(n\) is at most \(n\), so the total contribution of all such terms is \(O_U(nq^{-n/6})\). For \(e=2\), which occurs exactly when \(\chi\) is quadratic and \(2\mid n\), the contribution is
\[
\mu(2)q^{-n/2}\#U(\FF_{q^{n/2}})=-\mathbf 1_{2\mid n}+O_U(q^{-n/4})+O_U(q^{-n/2}),
\]
and the indicator is the unitary exponential combination \((1+(-1)^n)/2\). Moving the exponentially decaying part of the quadratic term, the terms with \(e>2\), the nontrivial imprimitive terms with \(\chi^e\ne\ind\), and the subunit reciprocal-zero terms into the error gives Cesaro mean square zero. Finally, the boundary term for nonprojective \(U\) is \(O_U(q^{-n/2})\) by Proposition \ref{prop:finite-field-normalized-reciprocal-zero-formula}, hence has Cesaro mean square zero. Combining these estimates gives the asserted decomposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact quotient normalization]\label{lem:finite-field-exact-quotient-normalization}
Assume the notation of Definition \ref{def:finite-field-quotient-notation}, and let \(n\) be such that \(N_n>0\). For every subgroup \(H\le G\), put \(H^\perp:=\{\chi\in\widehat G:\chi|_H\equiv1\}\). Then
\[
\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^H\,\middle\|\,\mathrm{unif}_{G/H}\right)
=
\frac{q^n}{n^2N_n^2}
\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}|A_n(\chi)|^2.
\]
Consequently, for every \(K\le G\),
\[
\sum_{J\supseteq K}\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(K,J)
\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^J\,\middle\|\,\mathrm{unif}_{G/J}\right)
=
\frac{q^n}{n^2N_n^2}\,E_n(K).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition \ref{thm:kernel-chebotarev-visible-hidden-character-energy} to the quotient map \(\pi_H:G\twoheadrightarrow G/H\), with the degree-\(n\) finite-field counts \(B_n(c)\). Its Fourier formula gives
\[
\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^H\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/H}\right)
=
\frac{1}{N_n^2}
\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}|B_n(\chi)|^2,
\]
because the trivial-channel count is \(B_n(\ind)=\sum_{c\in G}B_n(c)=N_n\). By definition \(A_n(\chi)=nB_n(\chi)/q^{n/2}\), so \(|B_n(\chi)|^2=q^n|A_n(\chi)|^2/n^2\), proving the first formula.

For the second formula, substitute the first formula and interchange the finite sums:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{J\supseteq K}\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(K,J)
\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^J\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/J}\right)\\
&\qquad=
\frac{q^n}{n^2N_n^2}
\sum_{J\supseteq K}\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(K,J)
\sum_{\substack{L\supseteq J}}
\sum_{\substack{\chi\in\widehat G\setminus\{\ind\}\\ \ker\chi=L}}
|A_n(\chi)|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
For a fixed exact kernel \(L\), the coefficient of its inner energy is
\[
\sum_{K\subseteq J\subseteq L}\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(K,J),
\]
which is \(1\) if \(L=K\) and \(0\) otherwise by the defining inversion identity for the M\"obius function on the finite subgroup lattice. The remaining contribution is therefore exactly \((q^n/(n^2N_n^2))E_n(K)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform visible-energy bound and normalization replacement]\label{lem:finite-field-uniform-energy-normalization}
Assume the notation of Definition \ref{def:finite-field-quotient-notation}. Then the normalized nontrivial Artin amplitudes satisfy
\[
\max_{\chi\in\widehat G\setminus\{\ind\}} |A_n(\chi)|=O_{U,V}(1)
\]
uniformly in \(n\). Consequently, uniformly for all subgroups \(H,K\le G\),
\[
\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}|A_n(\chi)|^2=O_{U,V}(1),
\qquad
E_n(K)=O_{U,V}(1).
\]
For every \(n\) with \(N_n>0\), the exact coefficient in Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-exact-quotient-normalization} may therefore be replaced by \(q^{-n}\) at the theorem scale:
\[
\frac{q^n}{n^2N_n^2}
\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}|A_n(\chi)|^2
=
q^{-n}
\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}|A_n(\chi)|^2
+O_{U,V}(q^{-3n/2}),
\]
and
\[
\frac{q^n}{n^2N_n^2}E_n(K)
=
q^{-n}E_n(K)+O_{U,V}(q^{-3n/2}).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition \ref{prop:finite-field-normalized-reciprocal-zero-formula} and the Riemann hypothesis for curves over finite fields, every reciprocal zero occurring after the trivial factors have been separated has absolute value at most \(q^{1/2}\). The number of reciprocal zeros is fixed for each of the finitely many nontrivial characters of \(G\), and the proper-divisor and boundary terms in Proposition \ref{prop:finite-field-normalized-reciprocal-zero-formula} are uniformly bounded. Thus \(A_n(\chi)=O_{U,V}(1)\), uniformly in \(n\) and in \(\chi\ne\ind\). The two energy bounds follow because \(G\), and hence \(\widehat G\) and the subgroup lattice, is finite.

Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-eventual-positive-degrees} gives
\[
\varepsilon_n:=\frac{nN_n}{q^n}-1=O_U(q^{-n/2}).
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{q^n}{n^2N_n^2}=q^{-n}(1+\varepsilon_n)^{-2}=q^{-n}+O_U(q^{-3n/2}).
\]
Multiplying this coefficient estimate by either of the uniformly bounded finite energy sums proves the two displayed replacement formulas.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Admissible effective cutoff for the entropy expansion]\label{lem:finite-field-admissible-cutoff}
Assume the notation of Definition \ref{def:finite-field-quotient-notation}. Fix constants \(C_U\ge1\) and \(M_{V/U}\ge1\) such that, for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\left|\frac{nN_n}{q^n}-1\right|\le C_Uq^{-n/2}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\max_{\chi\ne\ind}|A_n(\chi)|\le M_{V/U}.
\]
Such constants are effective for a fixed cover: \(C_U\) is obtained from the Weil bound for the smooth compactification and the finite boundary set, and \(M_{V/U}\) is obtained from the reciprocal-zero formula of Proposition \ref{prop:finite-field-normalized-reciprocal-zero-formula}, the Weil bound for Artin reciprocal zeros, and the fixed finite character group. Put \(g=|G|\), and choose
\[
n_0(V/U)
\ge
\left\lceil
\frac{2}{\log q}
\log\!\bigl(\max\{2C_U,4(g-1)M_{V/U},1\}\bigr)
\right\rceil .
\]
Then for every \(n\ge n_0(V/U)\) one has \(N_n>0\), and for every subgroup \(H\le G\) and every class \(\bar c\in G/H\), writing
\[
|G/H|\mu_n^H(\bar c)=1+\delta_n^H(\bar c),
\]
one has \(|\delta_n^H(\bar c)|\le1/2\). Hence the entropy Taylor expansion used in Theorem \ref{thm:projective-finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography} is valid uniformly over all quotients for every \(n\ge n_0(V/U)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first displayed inequality and the definition of \(n_0(V/U)\) give \(|nN_n/q^n-1|\le1/2\), hence \(N_n>0\) and
\[
\frac{q^{n/2}}{nN_n}
=q^{-n/2}\left(\frac{nN_n}{q^n}\right)^{-1}
\le 2q^{-n/2}.
\]
Let \(Q=G/H\). Fourier inversion for the count vector on \(Q\) gives
\[
|Q|\mu_n^H(\bar c)-1
=\frac1{N_n}
\sum_{\psi\in\widehat Q\setminus\{\ind\}}
B_n(\psi\circ\pi_H)\overline{\psi(\bar c)}.
\]
Since \(|B_n(\psi\circ\pi_H)|=q^{n/2}|A_n(\psi\circ\pi_H)|/n\), the amplitude bound gives
\[
|\delta_n^H(\bar c)|
\le (|Q|-1)M_{V/U}\frac{q^{n/2}}{nN_n}
\le 2(g-1)M_{V/U}q^{-n/2}
\le\frac12.
\]
This is precisely the uniform smallness condition needed for
\((1+x)\log(1+x)=x+x^2/2+O(|x|^3)\) with an absolute remainder constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Projective finite-field Chebotarev entropy tomography]\label{thm:projective-finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography}
Let \(V\to C\) be a geometrically connected finite \etale{} abelian Galois cover of a smooth projective geometrically connected curve over \(\FF_q\), with group \(G\). Use the notation of Definition \ref{def:finite-field-quotient-notation} with \(U=C\), and choose \(n_0(V/C)\) by Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-admissible-cutoff}. For every \(n\ge n_0(V/C)\), the law \(\mu_n\) is defined. The quantities \(A_n(\chi)\) are bounded uniformly in \(n\ge n_0(V/C)\) and \(\chi\ne\ind\), and, uniformly for all subgroups \(H\le G\),
\[
\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^H\,\middle\|\,\mathrm{unif}_{G/H}\right)
=
q^{-n}\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}|A_n(\chi)|^2
+O(q^{-3n/2}),
\]
where \(H^\perp:=\{\chi\in\widehat G:\chi|_H\equiv1\}\). Moreover
\[
D\!\left(\mu_n^H\,\middle\|\,\mathrm{unif}_{G/H}\right)
=
\frac12 q^{-n}\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}|A_n(\chi)|^2
+O(q^{-3n/2})
\]
uniformly in \(H\). Consequently, for every \(K\le G\),
\[
E_n(K)
=
2q^n\sum_{J\supseteq K}\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(K,J)
D\!\left(\mu_n^J\,\middle\|\,\mathrm{unif}_{G/J}\right)
+O(q^{-n/2}),
\]
uniformly in \(K\). At the quadratic level the exact identity is
\[
\sum_{J\supseteq K}\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(K,J)
\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^J\,\middle\|\,\mathrm{unif}_{G/J}\right)
=
\frac{q^n}{n^2N_n^2}\,E_n(K).
\]
Equivalently, since \(N_n=q^n/n+O(q^{n/2}/n)\), the left-hand side is
\[
q^{-n}E_n(K)+O(q^{-3n/2}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-uniform-energy-normalization} gives the uniform boundedness of the amplitudes and of every visible energy sum. The prime number theorem for the trivial channel gives
\[
N_n=\frac{q^n}{n}+O\!\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right),
\qquad
\varepsilon_n:=\frac{nN_n}{q^n}-1=O(q^{-n/2}).
\]
For a fixed quotient \(Q_H=G/H\), Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-exact-quotient-normalization} gives the exact identity
\[
\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^H\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{Q_H}\right)
=
q^{-n}(1+\varepsilon_n)^{-2}
\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}|A_n(\chi)|^2.
\]
Equivalently, Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-uniform-energy-normalization} replaces the exact coefficient \(q^n/(n^2N_n^2)\) by \(q^{-n}\) with total error \(O(q^{-3n/2})\), proving the asserted \(\chi^2\)-asymptotic.

It remains to pass from \(\chi^2\) to relative entropy uniformly over the subgroup lattice. Put
\[
r_n^H(\bar c):=|Q_H|\mu_n^H(\bar c)=1+\delta_n^H(\bar c).
\]
Fourier inversion on \(Q_H\), the exact normalization above, the boundedness of the amplitudes, and the finiteness of \(G\) give
\[
\max_{H\le G}\max_{\bar c\in Q_H}|\delta_n^H(\bar c)|=O(q^{-n/2}).
\]
For \(|x|\le1/2\),
\[
(1+x)\log(1+x)=x+\frac{x^2}{2}+O(|x|^3).
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\) this expansion may be applied to every \(\delta_n^H(\bar c)\). Also
\[
|Q_H|^{-1}\sum_{\bar c}\delta_n^H(\bar c)=0.
\]
Hence
\[
D(\mu_n^H\|\mathrm{unif}_{Q_H})
=
\frac12\frac1{|Q_H|}\sum_{\bar c\in Q_H}|\delta_n^H(\bar c)|^2
+O(q^{-3n/2})
=
\frac12\chi^2(\mu_n^H\|\mathrm{unif}_{Q_H})+O(q^{-3n/2}),
\]
uniformly in \(H\). This proves the relative-entropy expansion.

For the inversion formula, Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-exact-quotient-normalization} gives the displayed exact \(\chi^2\)-identity, while Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-uniform-energy-normalization} gives its stated \(q^{-n}\)-asymptotic. Substituting the entropy expansion into the same finite M\"obius transform and multiplying by \(2q^n\) gives the relative-entropy recovery formula, because the subgroup lattice is finite and \(2q^nO(q^{-3n/2})=O(q^{-n/2})\).
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Open-curve Chebotarev entropy tomography]\label{thm:finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography}
Let \(V\to U\) be a geometrically connected finite \etale{} abelian Galois cover of a smooth geometrically connected curve over \(\FF_q\), with group \(G\). Use the notation of Definition \ref{def:finite-field-quotient-notation}, and choose an integer \(n_0(V/U)\) large enough that Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-eventual-positive-degrees} gives \(N_n>0\) and the entropy Taylor expansion in the proof of Theorem \ref{thm:projective-finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography} is valid for every \(n\ge n_0(V/U)\). Then, for every \(n\ge n_0(V/U)\), the law \(\mu_n\) is defined and all conclusions of Theorem \ref{thm:projective-finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography} hold with \(U\) in place of \(C\). In particular, uniformly in \(H\le G\) and \(K\le G\),
\[
\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^H\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/H}\right)
=q^{-n}\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}|A_n(\chi)|^2+O(q^{-3n/2}),
\]
\[
D\!\left(\mu_n^H\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/H}\right)
=\frac12q^{-n}\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}|A_n(\chi)|^2+O(q^{-3n/2}),
\]
and
\[
E_n(K)=2q^n\sum_{J\supseteq K}\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(K,J)
D\!\left(\mu_n^J\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/J}\right)+O(q^{-n/2}).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem \ref{thm:projective-finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography} uses only the normalized reciprocal-zero formula, the Weil bound for the reciprocal zeros, the prime number theorem for the trivial channel, finite Fourier inversion, and subgroup-lattice M\"obius inversion. Proposition \ref{prop:finite-field-normalized-reciprocal-zero-formula} gives the same normalized formula for a smooth open curve, with the omitted boundary contributing \(O_U(q^{-n/2})\), and Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-eventual-positive-degrees} gives \(N_n=q^n/n+O_U(q^{n/2}/n)\) and eventual positivity.

More explicitly, write \(\Delta_\partial A_n(\chi)\) for the normalized boundary contribution to the nontrivial amplitude. Uniformly in \(\chi\ne\ind\), Proposition \ref{prop:finite-field-normalized-reciprocal-zero-formula} and Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-uniform-energy-normalization} give
\[
\Delta_\partial A_n(\chi)=O_U(q^{-n/2}),
\qquad
\sum_{\chi\in H^\perp\setminus\{\ind\}}
\left(|A_n(\chi)+\Delta_\partial A_n(\chi)|^2-|A_n(\chi)|^2\right)=O_{U,V}(q^{-n/2}),
\]
because the set of characters is finite and the amplitudes are uniformly bounded. After multiplication by the exact coefficient \(q^n/(n^2N_n^2)=q^{-n}+O_U(q^{-3n/2})\), the induced boundary change in every quotient \(\chi^2\) is \(O_{U,V}(q^{-3n/2})\). The entropy Taylor estimate in the theorem then gives
\[
\Delta_\partial\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^H\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/H}\right)=O_{U,V}(q^{-3n/2}),
\qquad
2q^n\Delta_\partial D\!\left(\mu_n^H\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/H}\right)=O_{U,V}(q^{-n/2}),
\]
uniformly in \(H\le G\). Finite subgroup-lattice summation preserves the same \(O(q^{-n/2})\) scale in the M\"obius inversion formula. Thus the open-curve boundary is already inside the uniform error terms, and the displayed \(\chi^2\), entropy, and inversion formulas hold for all \(n\ge n_0(V/U)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-field quotient-profile rigidity]\label{cor:finite-field-quotient-profile-rigidity}
Let \(V\to U\) be as in Corollary \ref{thm:finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography}. Fix \(n\ge n_0(V/U)\). The full quotient \(\chi^2\)-profile
\[
H\longmapsto
\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^H\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/H}\right)
\]
determines every exact-kernel energy \(E_n(K)\) exactly by
\[
E_n(K)=\frac{n^2N_n^2}{q^n}
\sum_{J\supseteq K}\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(K,J)
\chi^2\!\left(\mu_n^J\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/J}\right).
\]
The full relative-entropy profile determines the same energies with the uniform asymptotic formula
\[
E_n(K)=2q^n\sum_{J\supseteq K}\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(K,J)
D\!\left(\mu_n^J\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/J}\right)+O(q^{-n/2}).
\]
Consequently, if two geometrically connected finite \etale{} abelian covers over the same \(U/\FF_q\) have the same Galois group \(G\) and the same quotient \(\chi^2\)-profile in degree \(n\), then their exact-kernel energy functions \(K\mapsto E_n(K)\) are identical. If their quotient relative-entropy profiles agree in degree \(n\), then the two exact-kernel energy functions differ by \(O(q^{-n/2})\), uniformly in \(K\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The exact identity is the \(\chi^2\)-identity in Theorem \ref{thm:projective-finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography}, carried to open curves by Corollary \ref{thm:finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography}, solved for \(E_n(K)\). The relative-entropy formula is exactly the entropy inversion formula in Corollary \ref{thm:finite-field-chebotarev-entropy-tomography}. If two covers have the same quotient \(\chi^2\)-profile, the right-hand side of the exact formula is the same for each \(K\). If they have the same relative-entropy profile, subtracting the two displayed asymptotic formulas gives a difference bounded by the sum of the two uniform \(O(q^{-n/2})\) errors; since the subgroup lattice is finite and depends only on \(G\), this remains \(O(q^{-n/2})\) uniformly in \(K\).
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Worked quadratic Kummer cover of the torus]\label{ex:cyclic-kummer-quotient-tomography}
Assume that \(q\) is odd, and consider the connected Kummer cover
\[
V=\Spec \FF_q[z,z^{-1}]\longrightarrow U=\mathbb{G}_{m,\FF_q},
\qquad t=z^2.
\]
It is finite \etale{} over \(U\) with Galois group \(G=\{1,\sigma\}\cong\ZZ/2\ZZ\). Let \(\chi\) be the nontrivial character. For a closed point \(v\in|\mathbb G_m|\) of degree \(n\), represented by a monic irreducible polynomial \(f(t)\in\FF_q[t]\) with \(f\ne t\), the Frobenius class is determined by the quadratic residue symbol
\[
\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_v)=\left(\frac{t}{f}\right)=
\eta_q\!\left((-1)^nf(0)\right),
\]
where \(\eta_q\) is the quadratic character of \(\FF_q^\ast\). Equivalently, if \(a\) is a root of \(f\) in \(\FF_{q^n}\) and \(\eta_n\) is the quadratic character of \(\FF_{q^n}^{\ast}\), then \(\chi(\mathrm{Frob}_v)=\eta_n(a)\), because \(\eta_n(a)=\eta_q(N_{\FF_{q^n}/\FF_q}(a))=\eta_q((-1)^nf(0))\). Hence the degree-\(n\) twisted primitive count is the quadratic-character sum over elements of exact degree \(n\):
\[
nB_n(\chi)=
\sum_{\substack{a\in\FF_{q^n}^{\ast}\\ [\FF_q(a):\FF_q]=n}}
\eta_n(a).
\]
For \(e\mid n\), the restriction of \(\eta_n\) to \(\FF_{q^{n/e}}^{\ast}\) is \(\eta_{n/e}\) if \(e\) is odd and is the trivial character if \(e\) is even. M\"obius inversion over the subfields of \(\FF_{q^n}\) therefore gives the closed formula
\[
nB_n(\chi)
=\sum_{\substack{e\mid n\\ e\text{ even}}}
\mu(e)(q^{n/e}-1).
\]
Thus
\[
A_n(\chi)=q^{-n/2}
\sum_{\substack{e\mid n\\ e\text{ even}}}
\mu(e)(q^{n/e}-1).
\]
In particular,
\[
A_n(\chi)=0\quad(n\text{ odd}),
\]
while, writing \(n=2m\),
\[
A_{2m}(\chi)
=q^{-m}\sum_{e\mid m}
\mu(2e)(q^{m/e}-1)
=-1+O(q^{-m/2}).
\]
For example, the first even degrees give
\[
A_2(\chi)=-1+q^{-1},
\qquad
A_4(\chi)=-1+q^{-2},
\qquad
A_6(\chi)=-1+q^{-2}.
\]
The corresponding degree-\(n\) Frobenius counts are
\[
B_n(1)=\frac{N_n+B_n(\chi)}2,
\qquad
B_n(\sigma)=\frac{N_n-B_n(\chi)}2,
\qquad
nN_n=\sum_{e\mid n}\mu(e)(q^{n/e}-1).
\]
Thus
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
n& nB_n(\chi)& B_n(1)&B_n(\sigma)\\ \hline
1&0&(q-1)/2&(q-1)/2\\
2&-(q-1)&(q-1)^2/4&(q^2-1)/4\\
3&0&(q^3-q)/6&(q^3-q)/6\\
4&-(q^2-1)&(q^2-1)^2/8&(q^4-1)/8
\end{array}
\]
The subgroup lattice is \(\{1\}\subset G\), and the only exact nontrivial kernel is \(\{1\}\), so
\[
E_n(\{1\})=|A_n(\chi)|^2,
\qquad E_n(G)=0.
\]
The full quotient law is
\[
\mu_n(1)=\frac12\left(1+\frac{B_n(\chi)}{N_n}\right),
\qquad
\mu_n(\sigma)=\frac12\left(1-\frac{B_n(\chi)}{N_n}\right),
\]
and, with \(x_n:=B_n(\chi)/N_n\), its exact quotient entropy and exact quadratic profile are
\[
D(\mu_n\|\mathrm{unif}_G)
=\frac12(1+x_n)\log(1+x_n)
+\frac12(1-x_n)\log(1-x_n),
\]
\[
\chi^2(\mu_n\|\mathrm{unif}_G)
=\left(\frac{B_n(\chi)}{N_n}\right)^2
=\frac{q^n}{n^2N_n^2}|A_n(\chi)|^2.
\]
The quotient by \(G\) is a point and has zero entropy. Since \(\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(\{1\},\{1\})=1\) and \(\mu_{\mathrm{sub}}(\{1\},G)=-1\), the tomography formula recovers the actual degree-\(n\) energy as
\[
E_n(\{1\})
=\frac{n^2N_n^2}{q^n}\chi^2(\mu_n\|\mathrm{unif}_G)
=|A_n(\chi)|^2,
\]
and the entropy version gives
\[
E_n(\{1\})=2q^nD(\mu_n\|\mathrm{unif}_G)+O(q^{-n/2}).
\]
Thus this cover displays the complete arithmetic data: the Frobenius labels are split by quadratic norms, the nontrivial Artin amplitude is given by the closed formula above, and the single nontrivial quotient profile recovers the exact-kernel energy degree by degree.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Checked biquadratic Kummer quotient lattice]\label{prop:checked-biquadratic-kummer-quotient-lattice}
Assume \(q\equiv1\pmod4\), and put
\[
U=\mathbb A^1_{\FF_q}\setminus\{0,1\},
\qquad
V=\Spec \FF_q[t,z,w,(t(t-1))^{-1}]/(z^2-t,\ w^2-(t-1)).
\]
Then \(V\to U\) is a geometrically connected finite \etale{} Galois cover with group
\(G\cong(\ZZ/2\ZZ)^2\).  Let \(H_1,H_2,H_3\) be the three order-two
subgroups, and let \(\chi_1,\chi_2,\chi_3\) be the corresponding nontrivial
characters, represented respectively by the quadratic functions
\(t\), \(t-1\), and \(t(t-1)\).  Define
\[
M_n:=nN_n=\sum_{e\mid n}\mu(e)(q^{n/e}-2),
\qquad
T_n:=\sum_{\substack{e\mid n\\ e\text{ even}}}\mu(e)(q^{n/e}-2)
-\sum_{\substack{e\mid n\\ e\text{ odd}}}\mu(e).
\]
Then, for every \(i=1,2,3\),
\[
nB_n(\chi_i)=T_n,
\qquad
A_n(\chi_i)=q^{-n/2}T_n,
\qquad
E_n(H_i)=q^{-n}T_n^2,
\]
and \(E_n(\{1\})=E_n(G)=0\).  If \(g_0\) is the identity element of \(G\),
then the primitive Frobenius counts are
\[
nB_n(g_0)=\frac{M_n+3T_n}{4},
\qquad
nB_n(g)=\frac{M_n-T_n}{4}\quad(g\ne g_0).
\]
For the first degrees one obtains
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
n&M_n&T_n&\bigl(nB_n(g_0),\ nB_n(g\ne g_0)\bigr)\\ \hline
1&q-2&-1&\bigl((q-5)/4,\ (q-1)/4\bigr)\\
2&q^2-q&-(q-1)&\bigl((q-1)(q-3)/4,\ (q^2-1)/4\bigr)\\
3&q^3-q&0&\bigl((q^3-q)/4,\ (q^3-q)/4\bigr)\\
4&q^4-q^2&-(q^2-1)&\bigl((q^2-1)(q^2-3)/4,\ (q^4-1)/4\bigr).
\end{array}
\]
Writing \(y_n:=T_n/M_n\), each index-two quotient has the exact law
\[
(\pi_{H_i})_\ast\mu_n=\left(\frac{1+y_n}{2},\frac{1-y_n}{2}\right),
\]
and hence
\[
\chi^2\!\left((\pi_{H_i})_\ast\mu_n\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/H_i}\right)=y_n^2,
\]
\[
D\!\left((\pi_{H_i})_\ast\mu_n\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/H_i}\right)
=\frac12(1+y_n)\log(1+y_n)+\frac12(1-y_n)\log(1-y_n).
\]
The full quotient law is
\[
\mu_n(g_0)=\frac{1+3y_n}{4},
\qquad
\mu_n(g)=\frac{1-y_n}{4}\quad(g\ne g_0),
\]
so
\[
\chi^2(\mu_n\|\mathrm{unif}_G)=3y_n^2,
\]
\[
D(\mu_n\|\mathrm{unif}_G)
=\frac{1+3y_n}{4}\log(1+3y_n)
+3\frac{1-y_n}{4}\log(1-y_n).
\]
Consequently the quotient profile recovers the exact-kernel energies degree by degree:
\[
E_n(H_i)=\frac{M_n^2}{q^n}
\chi^2\!\left((\pi_{H_i})_\ast\mu_n\middle\|\mathrm{unif}_{G/H_i}\right)
=q^{-n}T_n^2,
\]
while the full quotient records their sum,
\[
\frac{M_n^2}{q^n}\chi^2(\mu_n\|\mathrm{unif}_G)
=\sum_{i=1}^3E_n(H_i)=3q^{-n}T_n^2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cover is finite and \etale{} because the zeros of \(t\) and \(t-1\), and the
pole at infinity, are outside \(U\).  Over \(\overline{\FF}_q(t)\), the classes
of \(t\) and \(t-1\) are independent modulo squares: the valuation at \(t=0\)
is odd for \(t\) and even for \(t-1\), while the valuation at \(t=1\) is even
for \(t\) and odd for \(t-1\).  Hence the geometric Galois group is still
\((\ZZ/2\ZZ)^2\), proving geometric connectedness and identifying the three
nontrivial quadratic characters.

The formula for \(M_n\) is ordinary M\"obius inversion applied to
\(\#U(\FF_{q^m})=q^m-2\).  Let \(\eta_m\) be the quadratic character on
\(\FF_{q^m}^{\ast}\).  Since \(q\equiv1\pmod4\), \(-1\) is a square in every
\(\FF_{q^m}\).  Therefore
\[
\sum_{a\in \FF_{q^m}\setminus\{0,1\}}\eta_m(a)=-1,
\qquad
\sum_{a\in \FF_{q^m}\setminus\{0,1\}}\eta_m(a-1)=-1,
\]
and the standard quadratic-polynomial sum gives
\[
\sum_{a\in \FF_{q^m}\setminus\{0,1\}}\eta_m(a(a-1))=-1.
\]
For \(e\mid n\), the restriction of \(\eta_n\) to
\(\FF_{q^{n/e}}^{\ast}\) is \(\eta_{n/e}\) when \(e\) is odd and is the trivial
character when \(e\) is even.  Thus the all-subfield contribution over
\(\FF_{q^{n/e}}\) is \(-1\) for odd \(e\) and \(q^{n/e}-2\) for even \(e\), for
all three characters.  M\"obius inversion over subfields gives the displayed
formula \(nB_n(\chi_i)=T_n\), and division by \(q^{n/2}\) gives
\(A_n(\chi_i)=q^{-n/2}T_n\).

Fourier inversion on the elementary abelian group gives, for each \(g\in G\),
\[
nB_n(g)=\frac14\left(M_n+T_n\sum_{i=1}^3\chi_i(g)\right).
\]
The sum of the three nontrivial characters is \(3\) at the identity and \(-1\)
at every nonidentity element, proving the count formula and the table.  The
only nontrivial characters have kernels \(H_1,H_2,H_3\), so
\(E_n(H_i)=|A_n(\chi_i)|^2=q^{-n}T_n^2\) and the exact-kernel energies for
\(\{1\}\) and \(G\) are zero.  The quotient laws follow by summing the displayed
counts over cosets of each \(H_i\) and by dividing by \(N_n=M_n/n\).  The
displayed \(\chi^2\) and relative-entropy formulas are direct substitutions in
the definitions against the uniform laws on two and four points.  Finally,
because \(M_n=nN_n\), the two recovery identities are precisely the exact
quotient normalization of Lemma \ref{lem:finite-field-exact-quotient-normalization}
specialized to this cover.
\end{proof}
