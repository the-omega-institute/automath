\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Let \(B(u)=B_0+uB_1\) be a finite output-labelled weighted adjacency
matrix, and write \(\Delta(z,u)=\det(I-zB(u))\). The structural part of
the paper applies to primitive kernels whose output-zero and output-one
edges are exchanged by an involution. For such kernels
\Cref{lem:self-dual-compression} gives the functional equation
\(\Delta(z,u)=\Delta(uz,u^{-1})\) and a completed determinant
\(H(w,s)\in\ZZ[s][w]\) after the change of variables
\[
  u=r^2,\qquad s=r+r^{-1},\qquad w=zr.
\]
The main result, \Cref{thm:self-dual-endpoint-selection}, is a reusable
endpoint-asymptotic theorem for this class. It is stated for an
arbitrary primitive finite kernel satisfying the self-duality above,
with hypotheses expressed only in terms of the unitary spectral gap,
the completed determinant \(H(w,s)\), and an explicit root-separation
constant. Under those hypotheses the theorem proves that the
large-\(m\) real cyclotomic factors
\[
  R_m(w)=\prod_{\alpha\in A_m}H(w,\alpha)
        =
  \Res_s\bigl(\Phi_m^+(s),H(w,s)\bigr)
\]
select the endpoint conjugates \(\pm 2\cos(\pi/m)\), where \(R_m\) is
defined by the root product and \(A_m\) is the set of real primitive
\(2m\)-th cyclotomic conjugates. It turns any
Taylor jet of the endpoint branch at \(s=2\) into the corresponding
asymptotic expansion of the twisted Perron roots. The ordered resultant
equality is part of the statement: with the standard Sylvester
convention, placing \(\Phi_m^+\) in the first slot gives the conjugate
product without an additional sign, while the reversed order introduces
the sign \((-1)^{(\deg_s H)\deg\Phi_m^+}\).

The paper has two contributions. The first is kernel-independent
structural theory for self-dual finite kernels, stated before the
example and used as theorem-level input throughout the application. It
has two reusable parts. \Cref{thm:sign-quotient-plane-genus} gives a
general normal-form quotient theorem: whenever a completed determinant
satisfies the self-dual parity conditions, the sign quotient is obtained
by the invariants \(x=w^2\) and \(y=sw\), and a smooth plane model
immediately gives the quotient genus. \Cref{thm:self-dual-endpoint-selection}
is a kernel-independent endpoint-selection and asymptotic-transfer
theorem: once the spectral gap, endpoint isolation, and root-separation
hypotheses are verified for a completed determinant \(H(w,s)\), the
large-\(m\) cyclotomic asymptotics follow formally from the endpoint
Taylor jet. These statements do not mention the ten-state kernel and
require no certificate data from that example.

The second contribution is a fully effective application of this general
theory to the explicit ten-state digraph displayed in
\Cref{def:sync-kernel} and
Figure~\ref{fig:sync-kernel}. The symbol ``synchronisation kernel'' is
purely terminological: the state names \(\st{000},\ldots,\st{11-1}\)
are labels for the ten vertices of that digraph, and all combinatorial
data appear verbatim in the transition table. The labels come from a
two-track comparison automaton, but that automaton is only provenance
here: every object used below is recovered from the displayed digraph,
transition table, involution, and matrix. In this application the
general endpoint-selection hypotheses are verified from a completed
determinant, whose smooth projective spectral curve has genus \(6\),
whose quotient by the sign involution is a smooth quartic of genus \(3\),
and whose generic Galois group is the full symmetric group~\(S_6\).
The quartic quotient is an application of the general sign-quotient
plane-model theorem, not a separate ad hoc construction.

For the displayed ten-state kernel the permutation implementing the
self-duality exchanges output-zero and output-one edges, so its
completed determinant is denoted by
\(\widehat\Delta(w,s)\). The subsequent kernel-specific statements
verify the hypotheses of the general endpoint-selection theorem and
compute the accompanying geometric and Galois invariants for this
application.
For each fixed \(u\), the matrix \(B(u)\) defines a finite-state
dynamical \(\zeta\)-function through the determinant
\[
  \Delta(z,u):=\det(I-zB(u)).
\]
The same weighted matrix also controls the natural cyclic
skew-product lifts
\[
  \widetilde B_q:=B_0\otimes I_q+B_1\otimes P_q,
\]
where \(P_q\) is the cyclic permutation matrix on \(\ZZ/q\ZZ\). Their
Fourier blocks are \(B(\omega_q^j)\), so the unit-circle twists are the
natural character factors of the labelled cyclic lifts. The mechanism
used below is the additional structure coming from the explicit
self-duality
\[
  \Delta(z,u)=\Delta(uz,u^{-1}),
\]
which compresses the unit-circle twists \(u=e^{2i\theta}\) to a single
real parameter after the standard change of variables
\[
  r=e^{i\theta},\qquad u=r^2,\qquad s=r+r^{-1}=2\cos\theta,\qquad w=zr.
\]
The resulting completed determinant \(\widehat\Delta(w,s)\) provides a
convenient algebraic model for the twisted spectrum, and the
cyclotomic resultants considered below are precisely the primitive real
factors extracted from the Fourier decomposition of the cyclic lifts.
Throughout, such a factor is the conjugate product itself, or
equivalently the ordered resultant with the real cyclotomic polynomial
in the first argument. The opposite resultant order differs by the
usual Sylvester sign and is never used as the definition of \(R_m\).


\paragraph{Terminology.}
We call \((w,s)\mapsto \widehat\Delta(w,s)\) the completed determinant in analogy with completed \(L\)-functions: it is simply the polynomial obtained from \(\Delta(w/r,r^2)\) after rewriting in the self-dual invariants \(w\) and \(s=r+r^{-1}\). Likewise \(\Phi_m^+\) denotes the real cyclotomic polynomial of \(2\cos(\pi/m)\); concretely
\[
  \Phi_m^+(s)
  =\prod_{a\in(\ZZ/2m\ZZ)^\times/\{\pm1\}}
    \bigl(s-2\cos(\pi a/m)\bigr),
\]
so \(\Phi_m^+\) is the standard real Chebyshev factor attached to the primitive \((2m)\)-th roots of unity.



The application has two strands, both serving the theorem. The first is
geometric: the affine curve \(\widehat\Delta(w,s)=0\) admits a natural
involution, and its normalisation and quotient can be described
explicitly. The second is arithmetic: eliminating the parameter \(s\)
along cyclotomic slices produces one-variable root products, equivalently
ordered resultants, whose minimal-modulus roots are selected by the
endpoint theorem. This yields explicit asymptotics for the cyclotomic
twists of the kernel.

\paragraph{Computer-assisted verification.}
The exact determinant, discriminant, finite-field factorisations, and
endpoint jet coefficients were computed in SageMath~10.2 and
cross-checked in Mathematica~14.0. These certificates support the
determinant, quotient, Galois, endpoint-jet, and asymptotic results in
\Cref{sec:completion-twists}. The appendix gives both a
theorem-to-command map and a single Sage
certificate runner, \texttt{certificates/verify\_certificates.py}. The
runner prints exact outputs, Sage and Python versions, and SHA-256
checksums for the paper sources and the certificate script. Items
\textup{(a)}--\textup{(f)} give the corresponding determinant,
completion, smoothness, Galois-theoretic, root-separation, and
endpoint-jet certificates inline. Each item states the exact output
used in the corresponding proof, and no fitted parameters are used.

The general theorem is useful precisely because the cyclotomic
resultants involve all primitive conjugates at once: after completion,
the problem becomes to prove that no interior conjugate of
\(2\cos(\pi/m)\) contributes a smaller root than the endpoint conjugate.
\Cref{thm:self-dual-endpoint-selection} isolates this as a reusable
criterion: a strict unitary spectral gap gives a global lower bound away
from the untwisted endpoint, an effective separation interval isolates
the endpoint branch, and the Taylor jet of that branch then determines
the large-\(m\) expansion by an explicit universal coefficient formula.
The ten-state kernel gives a certificate-level application of the
criterion. Its self-duality
compresses the circle of
unitary twists \(u=e^{2i\theta}\) to \(s=2\cos\theta\), the completed
determinant has degree six, and \Cref{lem:root-separation} verifies the
required constants \(\eta=1/100\), \(\varepsilon=1/200\), and
\(m_0=32\). Once these hypotheses are certified, the quotient geometry,
generic Galois group, normalized cyclotomic resultants, and large-\(m\)
asymptotics can be audited term by term from the same polynomial
\(\widehat\Delta(w,s)\).


\subsection{Main results}

The first main result is the reusable endpoint-selection theorem. It is
stated and proved in \Cref{thm:self-dual-endpoint-selection}; for ease of
reference we record here the form in which it is applied later. Let
\(B(u)=B_0+uB_1\) be any finite non-negative labelled kernel for which
\(B(1)\) is primitive, \(B(1)\mathbf1=q\mathbf1\), and an involution
exchanges \(B_0\) and \(B_1\). Let \(H(w,s)\) be its self-dual
completion. If the unitary twists satisfy the strict spectral-gap
condition \(\rho(B(e^{it}))<q\) for \(t\not\equiv0\), if the endpoint
root \(w_0=1/q\) of \(H(w,2)\) is simple and isolated in modulus, and if
an effective separation interval isolates the corresponding branch
\(w(s)\) from all interior conjugates, then the real cyclotomic root
products
\[
  R_m(w)=\prod_{\alpha\in A_m}H(w,\alpha)
        =
  \Res_s\bigl(\Phi_m^+(s),H(w,s)\bigr)
\]
have their smallest zeros in the endpoint slices
\(\pm2\cos(\pi/m)\), for all sufficiently large \(m\). Consequently
the twisted Perron roots satisfy
\[
  \rho_m=\frac1{w(2\cos(\pi/m))},
\]
and any Taylor jet of \(w(s)\) at \(s=2\) gives the corresponding
large-\(m\) asymptotic expansion by the coefficient-transfer formula
in \Cref{thm:self-dual-endpoint-selection}. The order of the
resultant equality is
part of the statement: reversing the arguments inserts the Sylvester
sign and is not the polynomial \(R_m\) unless that sign happens to be
\(+1\).

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint selection for self-dual kernels]
  \label{thm:intro-general-endpoint-selection}
  Under the hypotheses just stated, for every sufficiently large \(m\)
  the polynomial
  \[
    R_m(w)=\prod_{\alpha\in A_m}H(w,\alpha)
          =\Res_s\bigl(\Phi_m^+(s),H(w,s)\bigr)
  \]
  has its minimal-modulus zeros exactly in the endpoint slice
  \(H(w,2\cos(\pi/m))\), together with the symmetric slice
  \(H(w,-2\cos(\pi/m))\) when that conjugate occurs. Consequently
  \[
    \rho_m=\frac1{w(2\cos(\pi/m))}.
  \]
  If \(w(s)\), or equivalently \(\rho(s)=1/w(s)\), is expanded at
  \(s=2\) to order \(N\), the universal substitution formula in
  \Cref{thm:self-dual-endpoint-selection} gives the asymptotic
  expansion of \(\rho_m\) through order \(m^{-2N}\).
\end{theorem}

This introductory theorem is a restatement of
\Cref{thm:self-dual-endpoint-selection}. It is independent of the
ten-state example; the example supplies an effective verification of
the spectral-gap, separation, and endpoint-jet hypotheses. In
particular, the theorem separates the reusable mechanism from the
certificate computations: the latter prove the hypotheses for one
concrete kernel, while the statement applies to any primitive self-dual
kernel satisfying the same explicit hypotheses.

The next structural result is the reusable quotient normal form from
\Cref{thm:sign-quotient-plane-genus}. In the present self-dual setting,
it says that the parity constraints on a completed determinant force
the sign quotient to be the plane curve obtained from the invariants
\(x=w^2\) and \(y=sw\). Thus smoothness of that plane model is enough
to identify the smooth projective quotient and its genus.

The remaining main results apply these general theorems and compute the
associated invariants for the explicit ten-state kernel. All curves in
the main results are considered over \(\overline{\QQ}\), and
\(\overline{X}\) denotes the normalisation of the projective closure of
the irreducible affine curve \(\widehat\Delta(w,s)=0\).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-kernel-geometry}
Let \(B(u)\) be the weighted synchronisation kernel of
\Cref{def:sync-kernel}. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item The determinant is
  \[
    \Delta(z,u)
    =1-(1+u)z-5uz^2+3u(1+u)z^3-u(u^2-3u+1)z^4
  \]
  \[
    \qquad
    +u(u^3-3u^2-3u+1)z^5+u^2(u^2+u+1)z^6.
  \]
  In particular,
  \[
    \Delta(z,1)=(z-1)(z+1)(3z-1)(z^3-z^2+z+1),
  \]
  Moreover \(B(1)\mathbf1=3\mathbf1\), hence
  \(\rho(B(1))=3\) by the row-sum bound, and
  \(C_B=243/272\).

  \item The completed determinant
  \[
    \widehat\Delta(w,s):=\Delta(w/r,r^2)
    \qquad (u=r^2,\ s=r+r^{-1})
  \]
  is given by
  \[
    \widehat\Delta(w,s)
    =1-sw-5w^2+3sw^3+(5-s^2)w^4+(s^3-6s)w^5+(s^2-1)w^6.
  \]
  It satisfies
  \[
    \widehat\Delta(w,-s)=\widehat\Delta(-w,s).
  \]

  \item The smooth projective curve \(\overline{X}\) has genus \(6\).
  Its quotient by the involution \((w,s)\mapsto(-w,-s)\) is a smooth
  plane quartic of genus \(3\), and the generic Galois group of
  \(\widehat\Delta(w,s)\) over \(\QQ(s)\) is \(S_6\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Our second theorem concerns cyclotomic twists.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-kernel-cyclotomic}
For \(m\ge 2\), let \(s_m=2\cos(\pi/m)\), let \(\Phi_m^+\) be the
minimal polynomial of \(s_m\), let \(A_m\) be its real cyclotomic
conjugate set, and define
\[
  R_m(w):=\prod_{\alpha\in A_m}\widehat\Delta(w,\alpha),
  \qquad
  R_m(w)=\Res_s\bigl(\Phi_m^+(s),\widehat\Delta(w,s)\bigr).
\]
The resultant equality uses the standard Sylvester convention with
\(\Phi_m^+\) in the first slot; the reversed order differs by
\((-1)^{3\deg\Phi_m^+}\) and is not the definition of \(R_m\).
If
\[
  \rho_m:=\frac{1}{\min\{\abs{w}:R_m(w)=0\}},
\]
then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item For every \(m\ge 4\),
  \[
    \deg_w R_m=3\varphi(2m).
  \]
  If \(m\) is even, then \(R_m(w)\) is an even polynomial.

  \item One has
  \[
    \rho_2=\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}.
  \]

  \item As \(m\to\infty\),
  \[
    \rho_m
    =3-\frac{11\pi^2}{17m^2}+O(m^{-4}).
  \]
  More precisely, the branch selection
  \(\rho_m=\rho(2\cos(\pi/m))\) is proved for every \(m\ge32\), and on
  that range \Cref{thm:rho-gap-m12} gives the expansion through order
  \(m^{-12}\). The small values displayed later are
  verified directly from the defining resultant rather than from this
  asymptotic branch-selection statement. In particular,
  \[
    3-\rho_m\sim \frac{11\pi^2}{17m^2}.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Previous work}

The rationality of dynamical \(\zeta\)-functions for shifts of finite
type is classical, beginning with Bowen--Lanford and Manning and
continuing through Parry--Pollicott and Bass
\cite{BowenLanford1970Zeta,Manning1971Axiom,ParryPollicott1983,Bass1992}.
We use this tradition only to place the determinant
\(\det(I-zB(u))\) in context; the determinant itself is computed
directly from the displayed matrix. General symbolic-dynamics
background is available in \cite{ArtinMazur1965,Ruelle1976,
LindMarcus2021,Kitchens1998}. The determinant manipulations also sit
near the multivariable graph-zeta determinant identities of
Foata--Zeilberger \cite{FoataZeilberger1999}. The standard
algebraic-geometry and function-field inputs are cited at the points
where they enter the proof: the smooth plane quartic genus formula,
quadratic ramification, and Riemann--Hurwitz.

The closest literature falls into three layers.

Classical background on graph zeta functions, coverings, and
Artin-style \(L\)-factorisations goes through Hashimoto
\cite{Hashimoto1990,Hashimoto1992}, Stark and Terras
\cite{StarkTerras1996,StarkTerras2000,StarkTerras2007}, and
Kotani--Sunada \cite{KotaniSunada2000}; see also Terras's monograph
\cite{Terras2011}. The voltage-graph and covering language for cyclic
lifts is classical \cite{GrossTucker2001}, while the twisted-operator
viewpoint relevant to the present Fourier decomposition connects to the
covering theory of Cimasoni--Kassel \cite{CimasoniKassel2023}. Spectral
properties of arbitrary lifts of graphs, directly relevant to
\Cref{prop:sync-cyclic-lift-factorisation}, are studied systematically
in \cite{DalfoFiolGarriga2019}.

Direct precedents for determinant expressions and covering
factorisations on digraphs and weighted graphs are due to Mizuno and
Sato \cite{MizunoSato2000,MizunoSato2001,MizunoSato2002,MizunoSato2004},
to Sato \cite{Sato2006}, and, on the dynamical/determinantal side, to
Morita and collaborators
\cite{Morita2020,IdeIshikawaMoritaSatoSegawa2021}. These works are the
closest antecedents to the cyclic-lift/resultant discussion in the
present note.

The reusable increment here is the endpoint-selection mechanism for
completed self-dual determinants. Classical graph-zeta determinant
formulas explain why finite labelled kernels produce rational
determinants; graph-covering and Artin \(L\)-factorisation formulas
explain why cyclic lifts split into character factors; and real
cyclotomic parametrisations are standard. What is not contained in
those inputs is a criterion deciding which conjugate among all primitive
real cyclotomic slices controls the minimal zero of the resultant, nor
a procedure that turns this decision into a high-order Perron-root
asymptotic from a local endpoint jet. \Cref{thm:self-dual-endpoint-selection}
supplies that criterion for primitive self-dual kernels: it combines
the self-dual completion, a strict unitary spectral gap, and an
effective separation of the endpoint branch to force endpoint selection
and to convert an endpoint Taylor jet into the twisted Perron
asymptotic expansion. This statement is independent of the ten-state
example. Once a self-dual kernel supplies \(H(w,s)\), the spectral-gap
and separation hypotheses are exactly the verifications needed to
obtain large-\(m\) twisted Perron asymptotics from the endpoint jet. The
explicit ten-state kernel then supplies a complete application in which
the same completed determinant also carries the genus, quotient,
generic Galois group, and endpoint asymptotic calculations.

Finite-group extensions of shifts of finite type provide the standard
language for tracking output labels in skew products. We use the
foundational framework of Adler--Kitchens--Marcus
\cite{AdlerKitchensMarcus1985} and Fiebig \cite{Fiebig1993}, together
with the \(\ZZ_q\)-extension viewpoint of Pollicott--Sharp
\cite{PollicottSharp1994}. The matrix-over-group-ring perspective of
Boyle--Schmieding \cite{BoyleSchmieding2017} is relevant here through
the cyclic Fourier factorisation of
\Cref{prop:sync-cyclic-lift-factorisation}. The underlying two-track
comparison can be viewed as a fibre product of labelled graphs, in the
sense of Nasu's textile systems \cite{Nasu1995}, but the parent
construction is not needed for the theorem. The role of the ten-state
kernel is to give a fully effective application of the general
endpoint-selection theorem: it verifies the hypotheses and the
algebro-geometric invariants for a self-dual kernel whose completed
determinant remains degree \(6\) while carrying nontrivial quotient
geometry, generic \(S_6\) Galois group, and explicit large-\(m\)
cyclotomic asymptotics.
