\begin{abstract}
We separate three kinds of structure that are easily conflated in
single-primitive representation results.  Expression universality
requires only terms over one binary operation; Odrzywo{\l}ek's
operation \(e^x-\operatorname{Log}y\) gives such terms for a
standard elementary library.  Protocol universality adds computable
  normal forms and value-transported compiled operations, with semantic
  readout identities rather than hidden algorithms on uneffectively
  presented semantic sorts.  With Zeckendorf normal forms, valuation transport
  through \(\NN\), and the classical Church-iteration identities we obtain
  a protocol for \(+\) and \(\times\) on \(\NN\), and we show that multiplication
cannot be read at the same semantic level from the ambient product on one
free monogenic orbit, even after an injective recoding.  This is a
classification of that one-orbit skeleton, not a global lower bound for all
single-primitive protocols.  Finite fibers give
  the generic finite-fiber consequences of Hankel-positive collision
  moments and finite Prony reconstruction,
  but their role in the hierarchy is the separation from finite-state
  transfer, not a new moment-reconstruction theorem: computable
  finite-fiber covers may preserve the normal forms and readouts while
  having second moments of superexponential growth, with an exact one-spike
  boundary showing that the spike sequence is the sole new finite-state
  obstruction.  The Richardson
obstruction is stated with its exact proof interface: the long printed EML
  roots are locked as finite strings here, while the printed-root route uses
  the cited Odrzywo{\l}ek symbolic-verification interface for their
  principal-branch meanings as \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative square
  root; the printed-root Richardson clause is conditional on precisely that
  interface.  There is also a schematic replacement-triple route: any finite
  EML triple whose three real-domain identities have already been proved gives
  the same normal-form obstruction for the translated Richardson fragment.
  Thus the printed-root Richardson clause is a visibly conditional
  interface statement, not an unconditional internal construction of EML
  representatives for \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and \(\sqrt{\phantom{x}}\).
\end{abstract}
