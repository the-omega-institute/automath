\begin{abstract}
We separate three kinds of structure that are easily conflated in
single-primitive representation results.  Expression universality
requires only terms over one binary operation; Odrzywo{\l}ek's
operation \(e^x-\operatorname{Log}y\) gives such terms for a
standard elementary library.  Protocol universality adds computable
  normal forms and value-transported compiled operations, with semantic
  readout identities rather than hidden algorithms on uneffectively
  presented semantic sorts.  With Zeckendorf normal forms, valuation transport
  through \(\NN\), and the classical Church-iteration identities we obtain
  a protocol for \(+\) and \(\times\) on \(\NN\), and we show that multiplication
cannot be read on one free monogenic orbit by the ambient product,
even after an injective recoding.  Finite fibers give
  the generic finite-fiber consequences of Hankel-positive collision
  moments and finite Prony reconstruction,
but they do not force a finite-state transfer law: computable
finite-fiber covers may preserve the normal forms and readouts while
  having second moments of superexponential growth.  The Richardson
obstruction is stated with its exact proof interface: for any finite EML
triple whose real-domain identities for \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the
nonnegative square root are verified, Richardson's theorem rules out
computable canonical forms for the translated real elementary fragment.
The unconditional hierarchy theorem does not use this Richardson add-on;
for the particular long printed EML strings used as witnesses below, the
add-on is conditional on the explicit locked three-witness hypothesis
\((H_{\mathcal W})\), since the source-level table itself does not prove
those three principal-branch real meanings.
\end{abstract}
