\begin{abstract}
We isolate what a one-binary-operation representation theorem does, and does
not, supply.  For the operation \(\eml(x,y)=e^x-\operatorname{Log}y\), the
Odrzywo{\l}ek public-record theorem is used only as an \(L0\)
representative-existence result for a standard elementary library.  The main
positive protocol is smaller and effective: Zeckendorf normal forms, valuation
transport through \(\NN\), and the classical Church-iteration identities give
compiled normal-form operations for \(+\) and \(\times\) on \(\NN\).  The two
primary obstructions are then separated from this positive protocol.  First,
Richardson undecidability forbids a computable semantic normalizer on the
translated Richardson fragment relative to any finite EML witness interface for
\(\pi\), sine, and the nonnegative square root whose three real-domain
identities have already been proved.  This is an interface theorem only: no
concrete printed-root package is used in the unconditional theorem stream.
Second,
multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be injectively read from the ambient
product on one free monogenic orbit; all such same-level readouts reduce to a
prime-weight exponent skeleton, and the faithful restrictions are exactly a
single-prime-power ray (besides the trivial monoid).  The
finite-fiber Hankel--Prony material is only a subordinate certificate layer;
the additional \(L2\) strictness is the finite-state transfer budget
obstruction for computable finite-fiber covers.
\end{abstract}
