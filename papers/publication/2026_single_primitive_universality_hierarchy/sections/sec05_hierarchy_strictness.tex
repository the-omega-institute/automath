\section{Separations}
\label{sec:strictness}

The levels compare structure, not only the functions eventually
represented.  The proof uses three elementary ingredients: a
compilation obstruction, a locked-witness Richardson interface for the
EML fragment, and a finite-fiber cover showing that finite-state
certificate data are additional.  The unconditional hierarchy theorem
uses the Zeckendorf and finite-fiber ingredients.  The Richardson
obstruction is recorded as a separate interface theorem: for the long
Odrzywo{\l}ek witnesses it is conditional throughout on the explicit
three-witness hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\) isolated in
\Cref{prop:locked-three-witness-interface}, while any finite replacement
triple with internally proved real-domain identities gives an
unconditional instance.

\subsection{Compatible compilation}
\label{ssec:L1-geq-L0}

\begin{proposition}[Semantic compilation into a protocol]
\label{prop:L1-compiles-L0}
Let \((\sigma,c_0)\) be expression-universal for a family
\(\mathcal F\).  Let \(T\) be the corresponding admissible term
algebra, and let \(\sim\) denote semantic equivalence of terms on
their specified domains.  Suppose
\((C',N',\mathrm{NF}',\{\Phi'_\ell\})\) is the code, normal-form,
normalization, and readout part of a protocol-universal system, and
that there is a computable map
\(\mathrm{eval}\colon T\to C'\) such that, for all
\(t_1,t_2\in T\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semantic-compatibility}
  t_1\sim t_2
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \mathrm{NF}'(\mathrm{eval}(t_1))
  =\mathrm{NF}'(\mathrm{eval}(t_2)).
\end{equation}
Then \(\mathrm{NF}'\circ\mathrm{eval}\) descends to an injection
\(T/{\sim}\to N'\), and equivalence of expression terms is
decidable by the word problem in the protocol.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The condition \eqref{eq:semantic-compatibility} says precisely that
\(\mathrm{NF}'\circ\mathrm{eval}\) is constant on each
\(\sim\)-class and separates distinct \(\sim\)-classes.  It
therefore induces an injection on \(T/{\sim}\).  To decide whether
\(t_1\sim t_2\), compute the two normal forms in \(N'\) and use the
decidable equality of protocol normal forms.
\end{proof}

The formal consequence needed in the hierarchy is the following
canonical-form obstruction.  Its hypotheses are exactly the semantic
compatibility hypotheses of \Cref{prop:L1-compiles-L0} together with
undecidability of the source equivalence relation.

\begin{corollary}[Canonical-form obstruction to compilation]
\label{cor:compile-scope}
Let \(T\) be an admissible expression-term algebra whose semantic
equivalence relation \(\sim\) is undecidable.  Then there is no
computable evaluator from \(T\) into the code set of a
protocol-universal system with decidable normal-form equality satisfying
the semantic compatibility condition
\eqref{eq:semantic-compatibility}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If such an evaluator existed, \Cref{prop:L1-compiles-L0} would make
semantic equivalence of terms decidable by computing the two protocol
normal forms and comparing them.  This contradicts the assumed
undecidability of \(\sim\).  Thus the obstruction is precisely the
absence of compatible computable canonical forms, not a failure of
expression universality itself.
\end{proof}

In this article the preceding corollary is used only in the
displayed compatibility form of \eqref{eq:semantic-compatibility}.  It
does not assert a general word-problem theorem for arbitrary
single-primitive expression grammars, nor does it add an evaluator to
Odrzywo{\l}ek's EML theorem.

\subsection{The Richardson obstruction}
\label{ssec:L0-not-L1}

Let \(\mathcal R\) denote the Richardson real elementary language used
in \cite[pp.~514, 520, Theorem~2]{Richardson1968}: its expressions
denote real, single-valued, partially defined functions of one real
variable; the language is generated by a real variable, rational
constants, the constants \(\pi\) and \(\log 2\), the operations
\(+,-,\times\), composition, the real functions \(\exp\) and \(\sin\),
and a unary symbol \(\mu\) whose required off-zero semantics are
\[
  \mu(x)=|x|\qquad(x\ne0).
\]
No value of \(\mu(0)\) is prescribed by Richardson's hypothesis; in
this article the concrete representative is fixed in
\Cref{lem:richardson-punctured-mu-reduction}.  The identity problem
asks whether the represented partial real function is zero on its
domain.

\begin{lemma}[Richardson absolute-value interface]
\label{lem:richardson-punctured-mu-reduction}
Richardson's undecidability theorem applies after fixing any one concrete
total completion of the punctured absolute-value representative whose
real-domain identity is supplied to the translation.  More precisely, his
identity-problem theorem on p.~520 assumes a real expression system whose
represented functions contain \(\log 2\), \(\pi\), \(e^x\), \(\sin x\),
the rational constants and identity function, are effectively closed
under \(+,-,\times\) and composition, and contain a unary function
\(\mu\) satisfying only the punctured condition
\[
  \mu(x)=|x|\qquad(x\ne0)
\]
\cite[pp.~514, 520, Theorem~2]{Richardson1968}.  Instantiating that
hypothesis with the concrete choice
\[
  \mu_0(x)=\sqrt{x^2}\qquad(x\in\RR),
\]
which agrees with \(|x|\) off zero.  Consequently, after either a finite
replacement square-root witness with the displayed real-domain identity
is supplied or the printed-root hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\) is
assumed, replacing every occurrence of \(\mu(F)\) by
\(\Abs(F)=R(M(F,F))\) is an effective reduction from Richardson's
identity problem to the corresponding concrete EML-translated fragment.
For the printed roots, \(R=W_{\sqrt{}}\) and this conclusion is
conditional exactly on \((H_{\mathcal W})\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On p.~514 Richardson defines \(E\) as a class of expressions for real,
single-valued, partially defined functions of one real variable, assumes
that \(E^*\) contains the identity and rational constants, and assumes
effective closure under \(+,-,\times\) and composition.  His condition
(1) on the same page adds \(\log2\), \(\pi\), \(e^x\), and \(\sin x\).
Condition (2) asks only for a function \(\mu\in E^*\) with
\(\mu(x)=|x|\) for \(x\ne0\).  The statement of Theorem~2 on p.~520 is
then that, if conditions (1) and (2) hold, the identity problem for
\((E,E^*)\) is unsolvable.  No hypothesis or proof step specifies
\(\mu(0)\); the reduction uses only the displayed off-zero condition.
Equivalently, for any chosen real number \(c\), the same theorem applies
to the concrete total completion
\[
  \mu_c(x)=
  \begin{cases}
    |x|,&x\ne0,\\
    c,&x=0,
  \end{cases}
\]
because \(\mu_c\) satisfies exactly Richardson's condition (2).  This is
not a further uniformity assertion about a family of reductions: it is
the original cited theorem instantiated with one admissible function
\(\mu_c\).  In particular the total completion
\(\mu_0(x)=\sqrt{x^2}\) is an admissible instance.  The semantics used
below are therefore the original real single-valued partial-function
semantics: equality means equality at every real point at which the two
sides are defined, and no multivalued branch convention is imported from
the complex EML presentation.

It remains only to check that the representative used in the EML
translation is exactly this instance and not a stronger, unproved
variant.  Let \(R\) be a supplied finite square-root witness satisfying
\(R(t)=\sqrt t\) on \(\RR_{\ge0}\); in the printed-root route this is
\(R=W_{\sqrt{}}\) under \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  Let \(F\) be any already
translated real subexpression.  The multiplication macro below gives
\(M(F,F)=F^2\) on real inputs, and the square-root witness is used only on
nonnegative real arguments.  Therefore, for every real \(t\) in the
domain of \(F\),
\[
  R(M(F,F))(t)
    = R(F(t)^2)
    = \sqrt{F(t)^2}
    = \mu_0(F(t)).
\]
This includes zeros of \(F\), but Richardson's theorem needs only the
off-zero agreement.  The replacement is a structural recursion on the
finite expression tree: constants and the variable are unchanged,
\(+,-,\times\), \(\exp\), and \(\sin\) are sent to the fixed witnesses
listed below, and \(\mu(G)\) is sent to
\(R(M(\tau(G),\tau(G)))\).  Hence, if identity-to-zero were
decidable for the resulting concrete translated fragment, applying this
recursive map first would decide identity-to-zero for Richardson's
admissible \(\mu_0\)-fragment.  That contradicts the cited theorem.
\end{proof}


Let \((H_{\mathcal W})\) denote the following explicit assertion about
the three finite EML strings printed later in
\Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}: in the principal-branch real
semantics of \Cref{def:eml},
\[
  W_{\pi}=\pi,
  \qquad
  W_{\sin}(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad
  W_{\sqrt{}}(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0}).
\]
The source-level finite part of this interface is proved in
\Cref{prop:locked-three-witness-interface}; the three displayed analytic
identities are not proved in this paper and are precisely the conditional
part of the Richardson obstruction.

\begin{lemma}[Richardson fragment and zero-test reduction]
\label{lem:richardson-fragment}
For the language \(\mathcal R\) just specified, there is no
algorithm which decides, from an expression \(E\in\mathcal R\),
whether the represented partially defined real function is identically
zero on its domain.  Equivalently, if \(0_{\mathcal R}\) denotes the
everywhere-defined zero expression, then no algorithm decides the fixed
zero-comparison predicate
\[
  \mathsf Z_{\mathcal R}(E)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  E(x)=0_{\mathcal R}(x)\text{ for every }x\in\dom(E).
\]
This is a one-sided comparison with a fixed everywhere-defined target,
not a quotient of partial functions by agreement on varying common
domains.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The undecidability of the identity-to-zero problem for this real
elementary fragment is Richardson's Theorem~2
\cite[p.~520]{Richardson1968}, in the punctured-absolute-value form justified
by \Cref{lem:richardson-punctured-mu-reduction}: the fragment contains
the identity function, rational constants, \(\pi\), \(\log 2\),
\(\exp\), \(\sin\), and a unary function agreeing with \(|x|\) away from
zero, and is closed under addition, subtraction, multiplication, and
composition.  The displayed predicate is just Richardson's identity
problem rewritten as comparison with the fixed zero expression: because
\(0_{\mathcal R}\) is defined at every real point, the relevant
comparison domain is \(\dom(E)\) for each input \(E\).  Hence a decision
procedure for \(\mathsf Z_{\mathcal R}\) would decide Richardson's
identity-to-zero problem, contradicting the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Abstract normalizer obstruction]
\label{thm:abstract-normalizer-obstruction}
Let \(\mathcal S\) be a computably enumerable source term language with
an undecidable semantic equality relation \(\equiv_{\mathcal S}\).  Let
\(\mathcal T\) be a computably presented target expression language, and
suppose there is a total computable translation
\(\tau\colon\mathcal S\to\mathcal T\) such that
\[
  A\equiv_{\mathcal S} B
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \tau(A)\equiv_{\tau} \tau(B)
\]
for all source terms \(A,B\), where \(\equiv_{\tau}\) denotes the target
semantic equality transported to the subset \(\tau(\mathcal S)\).
Then there is no computable finite normalizer for this transported
fragment: more precisely, there do not exist a decidable set
\(N\subseteq\{0,1\}^*\), a decidable equality predicate on \(N\), and a
total computable map
\(\mathrm{NF}\colon\tau(\mathcal S)\to N\) satisfying
\[
  \mathrm{NF}(\tau(A))=\mathrm{NF}(\tau(B))
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \tau(A)\equiv_{\tau}\tau(B)
\]
for every pair \(A,B\in\mathcal S\).  Equivalently, an effective
translation of an undecidable source equality problem cannot be equipped
with computable finite decidable canonical forms classifying the
transported target equality.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume such \(N\) and \(\mathrm{NF}\) exist.  Given two source terms
\(A,B\), compute the target expressions \(\tau(A)\) and \(\tau(B)\), then
compute their normal forms.  Because \(N\) consists of finite objects
with a decidable equality predicate, one can decide whether
\(\mathrm{NF}(\tau(A))=\mathrm{NF}(\tau(B))\).  By the defining property
of the normalizer this is equivalent to
\(\tau(A)\equiv_{\tau}\tau(B)\), and by semantic compatibility of
\(\tau\) this is equivalent to \(A\equiv_{\mathcal S}B\).  Thus source
semantic equality would be decidable, contradicting the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Transported zero-test normalizer obstruction]
\label{lem:transported-partial-normalizer-obstruction}
Let \(\mathcal S\) be a computably enumerable source language whose
terms denote single-valued partial functions \(f_A\colon\RR\rightharpoonup
\RR\).  Fix a source term \(0_{\mathcal S}\) whose denotation is the
everywhere-defined zero function, and suppose the source zero-test
predicate
\[
  \mathsf Z_{\mathcal S}(A)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  f_A(x)=0\text{ for every }x\in\dom(f_A)
\]
is undecidable.  Let \(\tau\colon\mathcal S\to\mathcal T\) be a total
computable translation into pure EML terms.  For a pure EML term \(T\),
let \(\llbracket T\rrbracket_{\RR}\) be the single-valued partial real
function whose domain consists of those real inputs for which the
principal-branch EML evaluation is defined at every subterm and the final
value is real.  On the transported fragment define the transported
zero-test predicate by
\[
  \mathsf Z_{\tau}(\tau(A))
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \llbracket\tau(A)\rrbracket_{\RR}(x)=0
  \quad(x\in\dom\llbracket\tau(A)\rrbracket_{\RR}).
\]
If \(\tau\) preserves the source domains and values, meaning
\[
  \dom\llbracket\tau(A)\rrbracket_{\RR}=\dom(f_A),
  \qquad
  \llbracket\tau(A)\rrbracket_{\RR}(x)=f_A(x)
  \quad(x\in\dom(f_A)),
\]
then there is no computably presented codomain of finite objects with
decidable equality and no total computable map
\(\mathrm{NF}\colon\tau(\mathcal S)\to N\) satisfying
\[
  \mathrm{NF}(\tau(A))=\mathrm{NF}(\tau(0_{\mathcal S}))
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \mathsf Z_{\tau}(\tau(A))
\]
for all \(A\in\mathcal S\).  Thus the obstruction is a no-decision
theorem for the transported identity-to-zero predicate; it does not use
agreement on intersections of varying partial domains as a canonical-form
equivalence relation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Assume such \(N\) and \(\mathrm{NF}\) exist.  Given a source term \(A\),
compute the two finite objects
\(\mathrm{NF}(\tau(A))\) and
\(\mathrm{NF}(\tau(0_{\mathcal S}))\), and compare them using decidable
equality in \(N\).  By the displayed property, this decides
\(\mathsf Z_{\tau}(\tau(A))\).  The domain-and-value preservation of
\(\tau\) identifies this transported predicate with
\(\mathsf Z_{\mathcal S}(A)\), contradicting the assumed undecidability
of the source zero test.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Printed EML core witnesses]
\label{lem:eml-printed-core}
In the principal-branch EML semantics of \Cref{def:eml}, the following
displayed macros expand to pure EML terms over the terminal \(1\) and
their indicated input leaves:
\[
  E(u)=\eml(u,1),
  \qquad
  L(u)=\eml\!\bigl(1,E(\eml(1,u))\bigr),
\]
\[
  D(a,b)=\eml\!\bigl(L(a),E(b)\bigr),
  \qquad
  P(u)=\eml\!\bigl(u,E(u)\bigr),
\]
\[
  N(u)=D(P(u),E(u)),
  \qquad
  A(u,v)=D\bigl(E(u),D(P(u),v)\bigr),
\]
\[
  S(u,v)=A(u,N(v)).
\]
They have the following real-domain meanings:
\[
  E(u)=e^u\quad(u\in\RR),
  \qquad
  L(u)=\log u\quad(u>0),
\]
\[
  D(a,b)=a-b\quad(a>0,\ b\in\RR),
  \qquad
  P(u)=e^u-u>0\quad(u\in\RR),
\]
and therefore
\[
  N(u)=-u,
  \qquad
  A(u,v)=u+v,
  \qquad
  S(u,v)=u-v
\]
for all real \(u,v\).  In particular, the exponential,
positive-real logarithm, all-real negation, all-real addition, and
all-real subtraction witnesses used below are printed here and require
no compiler oracle or extended-real convention.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each displayed expression is a pure EML tree after recursively
expanding the macros: the only terminal constant is \(1\), the only
variables are the formal input leaves, and the only operation symbol is
\(\eml\).  For \(u\in\RR\), the second input of \(E(u)=\eml(u,1)\) is
the nonzero constant \(1\), so \(E(u)=e^u\).  If \(u>0\), then
\[
  L(u)=\eml\!\bigl(1,E(\eml(1,u))\bigr)
       =e-\operatorname{Log}(e^e/u)
       =\log u,
\]
because \(e^e/u\) is positive real.  Hence, for \(a>0\) and
\(b\in\RR\), both second EML inputs in
\(D(a,b)=\eml(L(a),E(b))\) are nonzero and
\[
  D(a,b)=\exp(L(a))-\operatorname{Log}(e^b)=a-b .
\]
For every real \(u\),
\[
  P(u)=\eml(u,E(u))=e^u-u.
\]
The function \(e^u-u\) has derivative \(e^u-1\), its unique minimum
on \(\RR\) occurs at \(u=0\), and that minimum is \(1\).  Thus
\(P(u)>0\) for all real \(u\), so every occurrence of \(D(P(u),-)
\) below is within its proved domain.  Consequently
\[
  N(u)=D(P(u),E(u))=(e^u-u)-e^u=-u,
\]
and
\[
  A(u,v)=D(E(u),D(P(u),v))
        =e^u-\bigl((e^u-u)-v\bigr)=u+v,
\]
for all real \(u,v\).  Finally
\[
  S(u,v)=A(u,N(v))=u+(-v)=u-v .
\]
This proves all displayed identities and the asserted all-real domains
for the core arithmetic witnesses.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Printed-table verification cannot replace
\((H_{\mathcal W})\)]
\label{prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface}
The straight-line table in \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}, by
itself, is not a proof in the partial principal-branch EML semantics of
the three identities in \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  In particular, under the
literal semantics of \Cref{def:eml}, the root \(U_{47}\) is not defined
as a closed EML term by the printed recurrence alone.  Consequently the
Richardson obstruction in this paper must either assume or cite the
three analytic identities in \((H_{\mathcal W})\), or else replace the
three printed roots by a different finite pure-EML witness package whose
real-domain identities are proved internally.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first registers of the table give
\[
  U_0=1,
  \qquad U_2=\eml(U_0,U_0)=e-\operatorname{Log}1=e,
  \qquad U_3=\eml(U_2,U_0)=e^e,
\]
and then
\[
  U_4=\eml(U_0,U_3)=e-\operatorname{Log}(e^e)=0.
\]
The next register depending on this zero as a second EML argument is
\[
  U_5=\eml(U_0,U_4).
\]
But \(\eml(x,y)\) is defined in \Cref{def:eml} only for
\(y\ne0\).  Hence \(U_5\), and therefore every later register depending
on \(U_5\), is not defined by the literal partial EML recursion alone.
The displayed root \(U_{47}=W_\pi\) depends on this chain.  This proves
that a source-level parse, acyclicity check, size count, or byte
fingerprint cannot establish the analytic identity \(W_\pi=\pi\) inside
the paper's partial principal-branch semantics.  The same issue prevents
using the table alone as a self-contained analytic proof of the later
roots \(U_{111}\) and \(U_{126}\).  Therefore the reduction may use the
printed roots only through the separate analytic interface
\((H_{\mathcal W})\), or through an internally proved replacement
package.  Since no such replacement package is supplied here, the
Richardson obstruction is conditional exactly at \((H_{\mathcal W})\).
\end{proof}


\begin{theorem}[Exact conditionality of the printed Richardson witness package]
\label{thm:exact-richardson-witness-conditionality}

  \qquad W_{\sin}(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),

\begin{proof}

\Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}: the recurrence is finite and

table alone cannot be promoted to a proof of the three displayed
real-domain identities: in the literal partial semantics, the recurrence
already reaches a register where a zero is placed in the second input of
\(\eml\).  Therefore the analytic interpretation of the three printed
roots is not obtained from the recurrence by ordinary evaluation in this
paper.  The only available source for those meanings is precisely the
hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\), or else an internally proved
replacement triple satisfying the same three displayed identities.

It remains to check that no hidden use of the long table enters the

\Cref{prop:locked-three-witness-interface} audits the translation and

\Cref{thm:conditional-richardson-witness-package} feeds this finite
witness package into Richardson undecidability, and
\Cref{thm:separation} is obtained by applying the abstract normalizer
obstruction to that translated fragment.  Thus the dependency set is
exactly the one stated: the short core is proved internally, the
three-root analytic interface is assumed through \((H_{\mathcal W})\)

substituting finite roots whose three real-domain identities have been
proved independently.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Minimal analytic witness interface]
\label{thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface}
Fix the principal-branch EML semantics of \Cref{def:eml}.  The
Richardson obstruction of \Cref{thm:separation} depends on
Odrzywo{\l}ek's long Table~1 data only through the following three
real-domain assertions for the three printed pure EML trees locked in
\Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}:
\[
  W_{\pi}=\pi,
  \qquad W_{\sin}(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad W_{\sqrt{}}(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0}).
\]
More precisely, let \(\mathsf P\) be any closed pure EML term and let
\(\mathsf S(x)\), \(\mathsf R(t)\) be any unary pure EML terms such that
\[
  \mathsf P=\pi,
  \qquad \mathsf S(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad \mathsf R(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0}).
\]
If the Richardson translation is rebuilt with
\((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) in place of
\((W_{\pi},W_{\sin},W_{\sqrt{}})\), and with the internally proved core
EML witnesses of \Cref{lem:eml-printed-core}, then the resulting
translation is effective, preserves the represented partial real
function and its real domain on Richardson's language \(\mathcal R\), and
admits no computable normalizer
into decidable finite objects whose comparison with the translated zero
normal form decides the transported identity-to-zero predicate.
Consequently the proof of
\Cref{thm:separation} is
stable under replacement of the Odrzywo{\l}ek witnesses by any other
three pure EML terms satisfying exactly these real-domain identities.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Construct the translation by the same structural recursion as in
\Cref{lem:richardson-translation}, replacing only the three non-core
leaves \(W_{\pi}\), \(W_{\sin}\), and \(W_{\sqrt{}}\).  Effectivity is
purely syntactic: the three replacement trees are fixed finite strings,
the core EML macros in \Cref{lem:eml-printed-core} are fixed finite
strings, and a Richardson expression is translated by bounded recursive
substitution into those strings.

The value calculation is identical to the proof of
\Cref{prop:three-witness-stability}.  The core macros give addition,
subtraction, negation, exponentiation, positive logarithm, multiplication,
division away from zero, rational constants, and the positive square-root
macro on their stated real domains.  The replacement \(\mathsf P\) gives
the constant \(\pi\), and the replacement \(\mathsf S\) gives sine on all
real arguments.  The only use of the nonnegative square-root interface in
the Richardson language is the absolute-value representative
\(u\mapsto \mathsf R(M(u,u))\).  Since the multiplication macro satisfies
\(M(u,u)=u^2\ge0\) for every real \(u\) by
\Cref{lem:eml-real-witness-library}, this occurrence is always inside the
assumed domain of \(\mathsf R\) and has value \(|u|\), including at
\(u=0\).  Thus the rebuilt translation preserves the represented real
function of every Richardson expression.

If a computable normalizer into decidable finite objects decided the

\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Three-witness source reduction]
\label{prop:three-witness-stability}

\(\mathsf S(x)\) and \(\mathsf R(t)\) be unary pure EML terms, all in

  \qquad

  \qquad

\]
Then the printed core witnesses of \Cref{lem:eml-printed-core},
together with \(\mathsf P\), \(\mathsf S\), and \(\mathsf R\), give an
effective translation \(E\mapsto\tau_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,
\mathsf R}(E)\) of Richardson expressions into pure EML terms such that
\[
  \llbracket \tau_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R}(E)\rrbracket_{\RR}(x)
  =E(x)\qquad(x\in\dom(E))
\]
and
\(\dom\llbracket \tau_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R}(E)\rrbracket_{\RR}
=\dom(E)\) for every expression in the Richardson fragment.  Moreover,
for this translated fragment there is no computably presented codomain
of finite normal forms with decidable equality whose comparison with the
normal form of the translated zero expression decides whether a
translated term is zero on its transported real domain.  If
\(|E|\) denotes Richardson tree size
after expanding rational constants by the fixed constructor scheme, then
there is a constant \(K_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R}\) such that
\[
  |\tau_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R}(E)|_{\eml}
  \le K_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R}^{2|E|},
  \qquad
  \operatorname{depth}\tau_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R}(E)
  \le K_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R}|E| .
\]
Specializing to the three printed terms
\(W_{\pi}=U_{47}\), \(W_{\sin}=U_{111}\), and
\(W_{\sqrt{}}=U_{126}\) of \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}, the only
external analytic inputs used by \Cref{thm:separation} are precisely the
three principal-branch identities for those entries cited from the fixed
arXiv v2 ancillary file
\texttt{SupplementaryInformation.pdf}, Part~II, Sections~2.1--2.5, with
the accompanying Zenodo archive recorded only for public provenance
\cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator, Odrzywolek2026ZenodoCode}.
The proof does not execute, trust, or appeal to the archived code as a
compiler oracle.  The table in \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate} and
the source-level checker fix the three finite EML strings; their analytic interpretation
as \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and principal \(\sqrt{\phantom{x}}\) on the stated
real domains is imported exactly as this three-witness hypothesis.
Replacing the three cited identities by any independent symbolic
verification of those same strings leaves the obstruction theorem
unchanged.  All remaining Richardson-interface identities used in the
proof are the internally proved core identities of
\Cref{lem:eml-printed-core} and their finite composites below.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the printed core witnesses
\(E,L,D,P,N,A,S\) of \Cref{lem:eml-printed-core}.  These core witnesses give, by direct real-domain calculation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  T(u,v)&=E(A(L(u),L(v)))=uv\qquad(u,v>0),\\
  R_+(u)&=E(N(L(u)))=1/u\qquad(u>0),
\end{aligned}
\]
and hence the all-real multiplication and nonzero-division macros
\[
\begin{aligned}
  M(u,v)&=S\Bigl(A\bigl(T(Q(u),Q(v)),T(P(u),P(v))\bigr),\\
        &\hspace{4.3em}A\bigl(T(Q(u),P(v)),T(P(u),Q(v))\bigr)\Bigr)=uv,\\
  J(u,v)&=M\bigl(u,M(v,R_+(M(v,v)))\bigr)=u/v\qquad(v\ne0),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(Q(u)=E(u)>0\) and \(P(u)=S(E(u),u)>0\).  Thus integer
constants are built from \(1\) by repeated addition and negation,
rational constants are built using one application of \(J\) with a
positive integer denominator, and \(\log 2\) is the core witness
\(L(A(1,1))\).  The assumed terms \(\mathsf P\) and \(\mathsf S\) give
the required witnesses for \(\pi\) and \(\sin\).  Finally
\[
  \Abs_{\mathsf R}(u)=\mathsf R(M(u,u))
\]
is defined for every real \(u\), because \(M(u,u)=u^2\ge0\), and it
satisfies \(\Abs_{\mathsf R}(u)=\sqrt{u^2}=|u|\).  This supplies the
concrete total representative of Richardson's punctured absolute-value
symbol justified in \Cref{lem:richardson-punctured-mu-reduction}.

Define \(\tau_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R}\) by structural recursion:
variables are left as input leaves; constants are replaced by the
corresponding witnesses just listed; \(+,-,\times,\exp\), and \(\sin\)
are replaced by their fixed unary or binary EML macros; and an occurrence
of Richardson's absolute-value symbol is replaced by
\(\Abs_{\mathsf R}\) applied to the translated subterm.  Composition is
translated by substituting the already translated inner tree into every
input leaf of the already translated outer tree.  The preceding
paragraph proves the value identity at each atomic and primitive step,
with the same partial-domain check as in
\Cref{lem:richardson-translation}, and the composition clause preserves

expression tree therefore gives
\(
\llbracket \tau_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R}(E)\rrbracket_{\RR}=E
\)
with equality of domains.

Because \(\mathsf P\), \(\mathsf S\), \(\mathsf R\), and the core macros
are fixed finite trees, there is a constant bounding the size and depth
increase of each primitive replacement.  An elementary induction gives
the displayed \(K_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R}^{2|E|}\) size bound
and linear depth
bound; composition multiplies tree sizes and adds depths, while every
other constructor has uniformly bounded arity and uniformly bounded
replacement size.

If a computable finite normal form with decidable equality decided the
transported zero-test predicate by comparison with the normal form of the
translated zero expression, then, given a Richardson expression \(E\), one
could compute \(\tau_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R}(E)\), compare its
normal form with that of \(\tau_{\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R}(0)\), and
thereby decide whether \(E\) is zero on \(\dom(E)\).  This contradicts
\Cref{lem:richardson-fragment}.  That contradiction is exactly the
identity-to-zero obstruction and uses no variable-domain equivalence
relation.
For the printed witnesses of \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}, the
finite straight-line table supplies the terms and their locked syntactic

three cited Odrzywo{\l}ek symbolic verifications: the constant \(\pi\)

calculation in the proof has been reduced to the printed core witnesses
and the finite algebraic composites displayed above.  Hence the final
assertion follows, and the strictness theorem has no hidden dependence

\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Richardson EML witness library]
\label{lem:eml-real-witness-library}
Assume \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  The core witnesses of
\Cref{lem:eml-printed-core}, together with the finite witnesses
\(W_{\pi}\), \(W_{\sin}\), and \(W_{\sqrt{}}\) of
\Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}, form a finite principal-branch real
witness library for the Richardson primitives used below.  The
remaining algebraic constructors are the following finite macros:
\[
  T(u,v)=E\bigl(A(L(u),L(v))\bigr)\quad(u>0,v>0),
  \qquad R_+(u)=E(N(L(u)))\quad(u>0),
\]
\[
  Q(u)=E(u)>0,\qquad P(u)=S(E(u),u)>0,\qquad u=Q(u)-P(u),
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
  M(u,v)
  &=A\Bigl(S\bigl(T(Q(u),Q(v)),
        A(T(Q(u),P(v)),T(P(u),Q(v)))\bigr),
        T(P(u),P(v))\Bigr),                                      \\
  V(u)&=M(u,u),                                                     \\
  J(u,v)&=M\bigl(u,M(v,R_+(V(v)))\bigr)\qquad(v\ne0),              \\
  H(u)&=J(u,A(1,1)),                                                \\
  G(u)&=E(H(L(u)))\qquad(u>0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Then \(M(u,v)=uv\) on \(\RR^2\), \(J(u,v)=u/v\) for
\(v\ne0\), \(H(u)=u/2\), and \(G(u)=\sqrt u\) for \(u>0\).  The
finite non-core witnesses satisfy
\[
  W_{\pi}=\pi,
  \qquad
  W_{\sin}(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad
  W_{\sqrt{}}(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0}).
\]
Consequently rational constants are constructed effectively from
\(1\).  The nonnegative square-root entry is available on its full real
domain and will be composed with \(M\) in
\Cref{lem:eml-zero-absolute-witness}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(u,v>0\), \Cref{lem:eml-printed-core} gives
\(T(u,v)=\exp(\log u+\log v)=uv\), and
\(R_+(u)=\exp(-\log u)=1/u\).  Since \(Q(u)>0\), \(P(u)>0\),
and \(u=Q(u)-P(u)\), expanding the displayed four positive products
gives
\[
  M(u,v)=(Q(u)-P(u))(Q(v)-P(v))=uv .
\]
If \(v\ne0\), then \(V(v)=v^2>0\), hence
\(J(u,v)=u\,v\,(v^2)^{-1}=u/v\), and \(H(u)=u/2\) follows by
taking the divisor \(2=A(1,1)\).  For \(u>0\), the value
\(L(u)=\log u\) is real, so \(H(L(u))=(\log u)/2\) and
\[
  G(u)=E(H(L(u)))=\exp((\log u)/2)=\sqrt u,
\]
where the last equality is the positive real square root.  All
principal-branch domain conditions in this calculation are explicit:
\(L\) is used only on positive real inputs; \(T\) and \(R_+\) are used
only on positive real inputs; \(J(u,v)\) is used only with
\(v\ne0\), which makes \(V(v)>0\); and every use of \(E\), \(A\),
\(S\), and \(N\) is all-real by \Cref{lem:eml-printed-core}.
Integers are therefore built by binary repeated doubling and addition,
signs by \(N\), and rationals by one use of \(J\) with a positive
integer denominator.

The identities for \(W_{\pi}\), \(W_{\sin}\), and \(W_{\sqrt{}}\) are
exactly the cited real-domain part of \((H_{\mathcal W})\) for the
finite witness table of \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}.
The zero-domain use of square root is isolated in
\Cref{lem:eml-zero-absolute-witness}; the positive macro \(G\) is used
only on strictly positive real inputs.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Effective Richardson translation]
\label{lem:richardson-translation}
Assume \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  Using the fixed witness interface of
\Cref{cor:eml-witness-locking}, there is an effective map
\(E\mapsto\tau(E)\) from Richardson expressions to
principal-branch EML terms over \((\eml,1)\), together with a
transported real semantics
\(\llbracket\tau(E)\rrbracket_{\RR}\), such that
\[
  \llbracket\tau(E)\rrbracket_{\RR}(x)
  =E(x)\qquad(x\in\dom(E))
\]
for every Richardson expression \(E\), and
\(\dom\llbracket\tau(E)\rrbracket_{\RR}=\dom(E)\).  Hence the translation
preserves Richardson's identity-to-zero predicate: writing
\(0_{\mathcal R}\) for the everywhere-defined zero expression,
\[
  E(x)=0_{\mathcal R}(x)\quad(x\in\dom(E))
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \llbracket\tau(E)\rrbracket_{\RR}(x)=0
  \,\quad(x\in\dom\llbracket\tau(E)\rrbracket_{\RR}).
\]
Absolute value is represented by
\[
  |F|=\Abs(F)=W_{\sqrt{}}(M(F,F))
\]
using the principal square root on the nonnegative real axis.
Moreover, let \(|E|\) denote the number of nodes in the expanded
Richardson expression tree.  A rational constant \(a/b\) with
\(b>0\) is not counted as one atomic token: it contributes the size
of the chosen constructor tree for the numerator \(a\), the
denominator \(b\), and the final division, built from \(1\), integer
addition and subtraction, negation when needed, and one division.
Equivalently, if a source syntax writes rationals compactly, then
\(|E|\) means the syntax size after replacing every rational token by
this fixed constructor expansion.  Then there is a constant \(K\),
depending only on the fixed witness library and not on \(E\), such
that the EML tree size and substitution depth satisfy
\[
  |\tau(E)|_{\eml}\le K^{2|E|}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \operatorname{depth}\tau(E)\le K|E| .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Define \(\tau\) by structural recursion.  A variable is sent to the
same variable.  Constants are sent to the constant witnesses from
\Cref{cor:eml-witness-locking}.  If an expression is formed by
\(+,-,\times,\exp\), or \(\sin\), apply the corresponding chosen EML
witness to the translated subexpressions.  If an expression is a
composition \(G\circ F\), substitute the translated inner expression
\(\tau(F)\) into the translated outer expression \(\tau(G)\).  If
\(E=|F|\), translate it as the fixed \(\Abs\)-witness applied to
\(\tau(F)\).

This recursion is algorithmic because all primitive witnesses are
finite terms and syntactic substitution is computable.  We prove the
domain and value assertions by induction on the expression tree.  The
variable and constant cases
are immediate from the witness library.  For
\(+,-,\times,\exp\), and \(\sin\), the induction hypothesis says
that, at exactly the real inputs for which the source subexpressions are
defined, the translated subexpressions are defined and evaluate to the
corresponding real subexpression values;
\Cref{lem:eml-printed-core},
\Cref{lem:eml-real-witness-library}, and
\Cref{cor:eml-witness-locking} then give definedness and the
correct value for the chosen primitive witness on those real
arguments.  For a composition \(G\circ F\), the induction hypothesis
first identifies the domain of \(\tau(F)\) with \(\dom(F)\); for such
an \(x\), the outer translated term is defined exactly when
\(F(x)\in\dom(G)\), and then its value is \(G(F(x))\).  This is exactly
the partial domain and value of \(G\circ F\).  Finally, for
\(E=|F|\), the induction hypothesis gives
\(\dom\llbracket\tau(F)\rrbracket_{\RR}=\dom(F)\) and, on that domain,
\(\llbracket\tau(F)\rrbracket_{\RR}(x)=F(x)\in\RR\).  Then
\Cref{lem:eml-zero-absolute-witness} gives all-real definedness and the
value \(\Abs(F(x))=|F(x)|\).  Thus every translated term agrees with
the original Richardson expression on exactly the same real partial
domain.  The equivalence of identity-to-zero predicates follows by
applying this domain and value identity to \(E\) and to the
everywhere-defined zero expression.  The statement
concerns this transported real semantics; it makes no branch-independent
assertion about equality of arbitrary raw EML terms on a complex domain.

It remains to record the quantitative bound.  The size convention in
the statement charges the full constructor cost of every rational
constant, including the numerator and denominator witnesses; the
estimate is therefore not uniform in arbitrarily large rational
tokens at constant source size.  For each selected unary or binary
witness \(W\), let \(s(W)\) be its EML tree size, let \(d(W)\) be
its depth, and let \(r(W)\) be the largest number of occurrences of
any one formal input variable in \(W\).  The witness library is
finite, and the rational constants have already been expanded by the
fixed constructor scheme before \(|E|\) is measured, so choose an
integer \(K\ge2\)
larger than every one of these finitely many library constants and
larger than the corresponding constants for the constructors
\(0\), \(1\), integer successor by addition, integer predecessor by
subtraction, and division by a nonzero integer.

We prove the two estimates simultaneously by induction on the
Richardson expression tree.  Variables and constants satisfy the
bounds after the preceding choice of \(K\).  For a primitive unary or
binary operation represented by a witness \(W\), substituting the
translated children into the formal input leaves of \(W\) gives
\[
  |\tau(E)|_{\eml}
  \le K\Bigl(1+\sum_i |\tau(E_i)|_{\eml}\Bigr)
  \le K^{2+2\sum_i |E_i|}
  = K^{2|E|},
\]
after increasing \(K\) once if necessary; the depth increases by at
most \(d(W)\le K\), hence is at most \(K|E|\).  For a composition
\(G\circ F\), syntactic substitution of \(\tau(F)\) into every
variable leaf of \(\tau(G)\) gives
\[
  |\tau(G\circ F)|_{\eml}
  \le |\tau(G)|_{\eml}\,|\tau(F)|_{\eml}
  \le K^{2|G|+2|F|}
  \le K^{2|G\circ F|},
\]
and the substitution depth is at most the sum of the two depths,
hence at most \(K(|G|+|F|)\le K|G\circ F|\).  The absolute-value case
is the fixed unary witness \(\Abs\), so it is covered by the same
unary-operation estimate.  This proves the
claimed expression-tree and substitution-depth bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative locked Richardson interface]
\label{prop:richardson-translation-bookkeeping}
Assume \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  The translation
\(E\mapsto\tau(E)\) of \Cref{lem:richardson-translation} has a constant
\(K\ge2\), depending only on the fixed witness library of
\Cref{cor:eml-witness-locking}, with the following properties for every
expanded Richardson expression tree \(E\):
\[
  |\tau(E)|_{\eml}\le K^{2|E|},
  \qquad
  \operatorname{depth}\tau(E)\le K|E| .
\]
The real domains preserved by the translation are exactly these branch
admissibility clauses: \(E\), \(A\), \(S\), \(N\), and \(M\) are used on
all real inputs of their arities; \(L\), \(T\), \(R_+\), and \(G\) are
used only on positive real inputs; \(J(u,v)\) is used only when
\(v\ne0\); \(W_{\sin}\) is used on all real inputs;
\(W_{\sqrt{}}\) is used on \(\RR_{\ge0}\); and
\(\Abs(u)=W_{\sqrt{}}(M(u,u))\) is used on all real inputs.  Therefore
\(\dom\llbracket\tau(E)\rrbracket_{\RR}=\dom(E)\), and the only domain
tests introduced by translation are the same positive-logarithm,
nonzero-division, composition, and nonnegative-square-root tests already
present in these listed clauses.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The domain list is the union of the real-domain interfaces proved in
\Cref{lem:eml-printed-core,lem:eml-real-witness-library,lem:eml-zero-absolute-witness,cor:eml-witness-locking}.
The important point for the absolute-value symbol is that its square-root
argument is always \(M(u,u)=u^2\in\RR_{\ge0}\); hence zeros introduce no
extra branch exception.  In a composition \(G\circ F\), the outer term is
defined exactly when the already translated inner value lies in
\(\dom(G)\), so the composition domain is the original Richardson partial
domain and not a new EML branch condition.  This gives the asserted
domain equality by the same structural induction as
\Cref{lem:richardson-translation}.

For the quantitative estimates, choose \(K\) larger than the size, depth,
and input-leaf multiplicity of every finite witness in
\(\mathcal W_{\mathcal R}\), and larger than the corresponding constants
for the fixed expansion scheme for integer and rational constants.  A
primitive unary or binary constructor replaces its children by a fixed
tree with at most \(K\) overhead and at most \(K\) copies of each input;
after increasing \(K\) once, this sends child bounds
\(|\tau(E_i)|_{\eml}\le K^{2|E_i|}\) to
\(|\tau(E)|_{\eml}\le K^{2|E|}\) and increases depth by at most \(K\).
For a syntactic composition \(G\circ F\), substitution gives
\(|\tau(G\circ F)|_{\eml}\le |\tau(G)|_{\eml}|\tau(F)|_{\eml}\) and depth
at most the sum of the two depths.  Since the expanded source size is
additive up to the root node, these inequalities give
\(|\tau(G\circ F)|_{\eml}\le K^{2|G\circ F|}\) and
\(\operatorname{depth}\tau(G\circ F)\le K|G\circ F|\).  The variable and
constant cases are covered by the choice of \(K\), so induction proves
the bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional finite Richardson witness package]
\label{thm:conditional-richardson-witness-package}
Assume \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  The Richardson obstruction used in this
paper is generated by a finite, source-level package consisting only of
the internally proved core EML witnesses
\(E,L,D,P,N,A,S,T,R_+,M,J,H,G\), the three printed roots
\(W_{\pi}=U_{47}\), \(W_{\sin}=U_{111}\), and
\(W_{\sqrt{}}=U_{126}\) from
\Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}, and the absolute-value composite
\(\Abs(u)=W_{\sqrt{}}(M(u,u))\).  This package supplies an effective
translation of Richardson's language \(\mathcal R\) into pure EML terms
over \((\eml,1)\), preserves the represented partial real function and
its real domain, satisfies the size and substitution-depth bounds of
\Cref{prop:richardson-translation-bookkeeping}, and uses the three long
printed roots only through the three real-domain identities in
\((H_{\mathcal W})\).  Consequently the
translated Richardson fragment has no computable normalizer into finite
objects with decidable equality whose comparison with the translated
zero expression decides identity to zero on the transported real domain.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The package is finite because every named core witness is displayed in
\Cref{lem:eml-printed-core,lem:eml-real-witness-library}, the three
non-core witnesses are the fixed roots of the acyclic table in
\Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}, and \(\Abs\) is one further finite
composition.  The finite syntactic locking of the three non-core roots,
including their sizes, depths, and fingerprints, is proved in
\Cref{lem:eml-source-level-check,prop:locked-three-witness-interface}.

Under \((H_{\mathcal W})\),
\Cref{cor:eml-witness-locking} gives the finite Richardson witness
library and \Cref{lem:richardson-translation} gives the effective
translation, real-domain preservation, and value preservation, while
\Cref{prop:richardson-translation-bookkeeping} gives the uniform size,
depth, and branch-admissibility bookkeeping.  The only occurrence of the square-root
root in the translation is through \(\Abs(u)=W_{\sqrt{}}(M(u,u))\); by
\Cref{lem:eml-real-witness-library}, \(M(u,u)=u^2\ge0\), so this use is
inside the nonnegative real domain specified in \((H_{\mathcal W})\).
The constant \(\pi\) and sine are used only through the other two
identities in \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  This is exactly the dependency audit
of \Cref{lem:three-witness-domain-audit,thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface}.

Finally, if such a computable normalizer existed on the translated
fragment, one could translate a Richardson expression effectively and
compare its normal form with the normal form of the translated zero
expression.  By \Cref{lem:richardson-translation}, this would decide
Richardson's identity-to-zero predicate on \(\mathcal R\), contradicting
\Cref{lem:richardson-fragment}.  Hence the stated normalizer obstruction
follows from the finite witness package and no further compiler
output or Table~1 identity is used.
\end{proof}

\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}

\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[No third Richardson interface]
\label{prop:no-third-richardson-interface}
For the source-level Richardson obstruction in this article there are
exactly two admissible proof interfaces.  Either one assumes
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) for the three printed roots
\((W_{\pi},W_{\sin},W_{\sqrt{}})\), or one supplies a finite replacement
triple \((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) of pure EML terms satisfying
\[
  \mathsf P=\pi,
  \qquad
  \mathsf S(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad
  \mathsf R(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0}).
\]
In the absence of one of these two inputs, the manuscript's finite
source-level checks, fingerprints, and core EML identities do not imply
the L0--L1 Richardson normal-form obstruction.  With either input, the
same transported-normalizer contradiction proves the obstruction and no
additional compiler, code archive, or unstated analytic identity is used.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
First assume the printed-root route.  By
\Cref{prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface}, the straight-line
table of \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate} fixes finite strings but is
not an internal proof that the three relevant entries have values
\(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the principal nonnegative square root on the stated
real domains.  Hence any proof using those printed entries must add
exactly the missing analytic assertions, which are named
\((H_{\mathcal W})\).  Once they are assumed,
\Cref{thm:conditional-richardson-witness-package} gives the effective
translation and \Cref{thm:separation} gives the normal-form obstruction.

Now assume the replacement route.  If a finite triple
\((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) satisfying the three displayed
real-domain identities is supplied, then
\Cref{thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface}, equivalently
\Cref{prop:three-witness-stability}, rebuilds the Richardson translation
with that triple.  The proof is syntactic apart from those identities:
the core arithmetic witnesses are internally proved, the constant
\(\pi\), sine, and nonnegative square root are used only through the
three displayed equations, and the absolute-value occurrence applies the
square-root witness only to \(M(u,u)=u^2\ge0\).  Richardson's undecidable
identity-to-zero predicate is then transported to equality with the
translated zero term, so
\Cref{lem:transported-partial-normalizer-obstruction} rules out a
computable finite normalizer with decidable equality.

Conversely, without \((H_{\mathcal W})\) and without a replacement triple,
the available internal facts are only the finite string locks of
\Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate,lem:eml-source-level-check}, the core
EML identities of \Cref{lem:eml-printed-core}, and the dependency audit of
\Cref{lem:three-witness-domain-audit}.  These
facts omit the semantic interpretations of exactly the three non-core
witnesses required by the Richardson fragment.  The translated terms
therefore have no proved value-preservation theorem for Richardson's
language, and the transported-normalizer contradiction cannot even be
formed.  Thus the two routes listed in the statement exhaust the proof
interfaces available in this article.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional printed-witness Richardson obstruction]
\label{thm:separation}
The EML system \((\eml,1)\) is expression-universal for
\(\mathcal B_{36}\).  On the Richardson sublanguage, however, the
translation used in the obstruction is generated only from the fixed
finite witness list \(\mathcal W_{\mathcal R}\) of
\Cref{cor:eml-witness-locking}.  Conditional on the locked three-witness
hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\) of
\Cref{prop:locked-three-witness-interface}, let \(\tau(E)\) be the effective
principal-branch EML representative of a Richardson expression given by
\Cref{lem:richardson-translation}.  There is no computably presented
normal-form codomain \(N_{\mathcal R}\) of finite objects with decidable
equality and no total computable map
\(\mathrm{NF}\colon\tau(\mathcal R)\to N_{\mathcal R}\) satisfying
\[
  \mathrm{NF}(\tau(E))=\mathrm{NF}(\tau(0_{\mathcal R}))
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \llbracket\tau(E)\rrbracket_{\RR}(x)=0
  \text{ for every }x\in\dom\llbracket\tau(E)\rrbracket_{\RR}
\]
for all Richardson expressions \(E\in\mathcal R\).  Equivalently, the
transported identity-to-zero predicate on \(\tau(\mathcal R)\) has no
decidable finite normal-form test obtained by comparison with the normal
form of the translated zero expression.  For any finite replacement
triple satisfying the three real-domain identities in
\Cref{prop:three-witness-stability}, the identical statement holds
unconditionally for the Richardson fragment translated with that triple.
For the printed roots of \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}, the theorem
is conditional exactly as stated.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The expression-universality assertion is \Cref{thm:eml-universality}.
The obstruction itself is exactly the conditional finite package of
\Cref{thm:conditional-richardson-witness-package}: after
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) is assumed, the map \(E\mapsto\tau(E)\) is effective
and preserves the partial real function and its real domain, using only
syntactic substitution into the locked witness list.  The Richardson
identity-to-zero predicate is undecidable by
\Cref{lem:richardson-fragment}.  Therefore
\Cref{lem:transported-partial-normalizer-obstruction}, applied with
\(\mathcal S=\mathcal R\) and target language the pure EML grammar, rules
out the asserted computable finite normalizer.  Thus the expression
grammar, although universal in the sense of \Cref{def:expr-univ}, cannot
by itself supply the decidable canonical-form component of
\Cref{def:prot-univ}.  The final two sentences are
\Cref{prop:three-witness-stability} and
\Cref{thm:exact-richardson-witness-conditionality}: the abstract
replacement-triple obstruction is unconditional once the three identities
are proved, while the printed roots reach the obstruction only through
\((H_{\mathcal W})\).  The no-hidden-oracle dependency statement is
\Cref{prop:printed-root-no-hidden-oracle}: the imported
\(\mathcal B_{36}\) universality theorem supplies the L0 positive clause
and not the real-domain proof of the three printed Richardson roots.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Strictness for compatible canonical compilation]
\label{cor:strict-L0-L1}
Conditional on \((H_{\mathcal W})\), expression universality does not imply
the compatible canonical compilation component of protocol universality.  More
precisely, the EML
presentation \((\eml,1)\) is expression-universal for
\(\mathcal B_{36}\), but the translated Richardson fragment is only the
finite-witness fragment supplied by
\Cref{cor:eml-witness-locking,lem:richardson-translation}, and there is
no computable compilation of that fragment into protocol normal forms
whose decidable equality predicate detects identity to zero by comparison
with the normal form of the translated zero expression:
\[
  \mathrm{NF}(\tau(E))=\mathrm{NF}(\tau(0_{\mathcal R}))
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  E(x)=0\text{ for every }x\in\dom(E).
\]
Thus the L0--L1 separation proved here is strictness for
semantic-compatible canonical compilation under \((H_{\mathcal W})\), not a statement that no
unrelated protocol can realize the same abstract operation family by
different codes and readouts.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Suppose a compatible canonical compilation as displayed existed on that
finite-witness translated fragment.  Then, given a Richardson expression
\(E\), compute the normal forms of \(\tau(E)\) and
\(\tau(0_{\mathcal R})\) and compare them in the protocol codomain.  The
displayed equivalence would decide whether \(E\) is identically zero on
its own domain.  This contradicts
\Cref{lem:richardson-fragment}, and is exactly the obstruction
formalized in \Cref{thm:separation}.  The final sentence
follows because the argument uses the compatibility equivalence displayed
above, rather than an assertion that every possible protocol for the same
operations must receive its inputs from this EML fragment.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[External universality versus locked obstruction package]
\label{prop:external-versus-locked-package}
The L0 positive clause and the L0--L1 obstruction use different

\((H_{\mathcal W})\), the locked real-domain interface isolated in

consisting of the locked real EML witnesses in

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first sentence is \Cref{thm:eml-universality}: it is stated as

strictness direction, inspect the proof of \Cref{thm:separation}.  Its
analytic translation input is \Cref{lem:richardson-translation}.  That
lemma is obtained by structural recursion from the fixed witnesses for
constants, addition, subtraction, multiplication, exponentiation,
sine, composition, and absolute value; these witnesses are supplied by

\end{proof}

\subsection{Certificate data}
\label{ssec:L2-strict}

\begin{proposition}[Generic and additional certificate clauses]
\label{prop:L2-generic-vs-structural}
For any protocol system with effective finite resolution data whose
normalization map has finite fibers at each resolution, the Hankel
positivity and finite spectral reconstruction clauses in
\Cref{def:cert-univ} follow from the fiber sizes alone, with the
a priori finite bound supplied by the computable number of normal
forms at that resolution.  Finite-state certificate universality is
an additional transfer-operator hypothesis.  \Cref{thm:star-kernel}
describes a compression available after transfer data of its special
nonnegative tensor form have already been supplied; it does not supply
such data for the Zeckendorf fold fibers.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(N_m\) be the finite normal-form set at resolution \(m\), listed
as in \Cref{def:effective-resolution-data}.  Let \(d_m(x)\) be the
finite fiber sizes and set
\[
  S_q(m)=\sum_{x\in N_m} d_m(x)^q .
\]
For every finitely supported coefficient vector \((c_i)\),
\[
  \sum_{i,j}c_i c_j S_{i+j}(m)
  =\sum_{x\in N_m}
    \Bigl(\sum_i c_i d_m(x)^i\Bigr)^2\ge0,
\]
which is Hankel positivity.  Since
\(\sum_{x\in N_m}\delta_{d_m(x)}\) has finite support of size at
most \(|N_m|\), its support points and multiplicities are recovered
from finitely many moments, with the explicit bound
\(2|N_m|-1\) from \Cref{thm:prony}.  Here
\Cref{thm:prony} is invoked only through the finite atomic measure
proof just recalled, applied to
\(\sum_{x\in N_m}\delta_{d_m(x)}\); no property special to
Zeckendorf fibers is used.  The value \(|N_m|\) and the
finite moment segment are computable by bounded enumeration in the
effective resolution data.  Neither argument constructs a
finite-state transfer operator.  That operator is additional
structure; \Cref{thm:star-kernel} applies only conditionally, after a
transfer operator of its special nonnegative tensor form is already
part of the data, and then gives the stated symmetric compression.
\end{proof}

The next construction is only a presentation-level modification.
The finite sets mapping to normal forms are replaced by computable
finite covers of the same normal-form sets.  The normal forms,
valuation, and readout operations are fixed; only the certificate
fibers change.  Thus the theorem is not a claim that the unmodified
Zeckendorf fold lacks a finite-state transfer operator.

\begin{definition}[Computable finite-fiber cover]
\label{def:computable-cover}
A \emph{computable finite-fiber cover} of the Zeckendorf resolution
data is a sequence
\[
  (\Omega_m^\sharp,\Fold_m^\sharp)_{m\ge1}
\]
such that each \(\Omega_m^\sharp\) is a finite decidable set,
the relation \(\omega\in\Omega_m^\sharp\) is decidable uniformly in
\((m,\omega)\), and a uniform algorithm outputs a finite
no-repetition list of all elements of \(\Omega_m^\sharp\), hence the
cardinality \(|\Omega_m^\sharp|\).  Moreover
\[
  \Fold_m^\sharp\colon\Omega_m^\sharp\to X_m
\]
is a uniformly computable surjection.  The covered collision moments
are
\[
  S_q^\sharp(m)
  =\sum_{x\in X_m}|\bigl(\Fold_m^\sharp\bigr)^{-1}(x)|^q .
\]
By definition this operation changes only the finite certificate
fibers over the finite normal-form sets \(X_m\).  The stable
normal-form space \(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\), the decoding
\(\Val\), the operations \(\oplus,\otimes\), and the composition
readouts \(\Phi_0,\Phi_1\) remain the Zeckendorf protocol data.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Covers preserve readouts]
\label{lem:cover-preserves-protocol}
Let \((\Omega_m^\sharp,\Fold_m^\sharp)\) be a computable
finite-fiber cover of the Zeckendorf resolution data.  Every
operation realized by the Zeckendorf protocol with normal forms
\(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\), decoding \(\Val\), and readouts
\(\Phi_0,\Phi_1\) is realized by the covered presentation with the
same realizing map on normal forms, the same level, and the same
single-operation readout term.  In particular, protocol universality
for \(\{+,\times\}\) is preserved.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The realization equations in \Cref{def:prot-univ} are equations on
the stable normal forms and their readouts:
\[
  \nu(\widehat\theta(n_1,\ldots,n_r))
  =\theta(\nu(n_1),\ldots,\nu(n_r))
\]
and
\[
  \Phi_\ell(\widehat\theta(n_1,\ldots,n_r))
  =
  R_\theta(\Phi_\ell(n_1),\ldots,\Phi_\ell(n_r)).
\]
A finite-fiber cover leaves \(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\), \(\Val\),
\(\oplus,\otimes\), and the maps \(\Phi_0,\Phi_1\) unchanged.  It
changes only the cardinalities of the finite preimages over the
finite sets \(X_m\), which enter the collision moments but not the
readout equations.  Therefore the same realization data continue to
realize the same operations.  Applying this to
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ} gives preservation of
protocol universality for \(\{+,\times\}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-fiber certificates do not force finite-state transfer]
\label{thm:finite-state-strictness}
Let \(a_m\ge1\) be any computable integer sequence.  There is a
computable finite-fiber cover of the finite normal-form sets in the
Zeckendorf protocol with exactly the same normal forms and
arithmetic readouts as in
\Cref{thm:semiring,thm:two-level}, hence with the same
protocol-universality for \(\{+,\times\}\), whose resulting second
collision moment is
\[
  S_2^\sharp(m)=(a_m+1)^2+F_{m+2}-1 .
\]
Consequently finite Hankel--Prony certificates, by themselves,
impose no finite-state growth law on the resolution parameter.  In
particular, choosing \(a_m=m!\) gives a certificate-universal
finite-fiber cover that is not finite-state
certificate-universal in the sense of \Cref{def:cert-univ}: already
its second collision moment admits no representation
\[
  S_2^\sharp(m)=u^{\mathsf T}A^m v\qquad(m\ge1)
\]
with \(A\in M_d(\CC)\) a fixed finite complex matrix and
\(u,v\in\CC^d\) fixed complex vectors.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each \(m\ge1\), let \(z_m\in X_m\) be the all-zero normal form.
Define the covered resolution set
\[
  \Omega_m^\sharp
  =
  X_m\sqcup\bigl(\{z_m\}\times\{1,\ldots,a_m\}\bigr)
\]
and the normalization map
\[
  \Fold_m^\sharp(x)=x\quad(x\in X_m),\qquad
  \Fold_m^\sharp(z_m,j)=z_m .
\]
This construction is computable in \(m\): encode the disjoint union
by tags, use decidability of \(X_m\), and decide
\((z_m,j)\) by computing \(a_m\) and checking \(1\le j\le a_m\).
The required finite list is obtained by concatenating the
lexicographic list of \(X_m\) with the list
\((z_m,1),\ldots,(z_m,a_m)\).
The map \(\Fold_m^\sharp\) is then uniformly computable and
surjective onto \(X_m\), and every fiber is finite.  Thus
\((\Omega_m^\sharp,\Fold_m^\sharp)\) is a computable finite-fiber
cover in the sense of \Cref{def:computable-cover}.  By
\Cref{lem:cover-preserves-protocol}, the protocol-universality
statement for addition and multiplication is unchanged from
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}.
Certificate-universality follows from
\Cref{prop:L2-generic-vs-structural}.

The covered fiber sizes are
\[
  d_m^\sharp(z_m)=a_m+1,\qquad
  d_m^\sharp(x)=1\quad(x\in X_m,\ x\ne z_m).
\]
Since \(|X_m|=F_{m+2}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inflated-second-moment}
  S_2^\sharp(m)=(a_m+1)^2+F_{m+2}-1 .
\end{equation}
This proves the asserted computable one-spike cover and preserves
the protocol data.

For the asserted obstruction, specialize to \(a_m=m!\).  If the
cover were finite-state certificate-universal, then the case \(q=2\)
in \Cref{def:cert-univ} would give a representation
\(S_2^\sharp(m)=u^{\mathsf T}A^m v\) for a fixed finite matrix
\(A\in M_d(\CC)\) and fixed vectors \(u,v\in\CC^d\).  By
\Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget} there are constants
\(C>0\) and \(R>0\) such that
\[
  |u^{\mathsf T}A^m v|\le C R^m\qquad(m\ge1).
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:inflated-second-moment} contradicts this bound,
because \((m!)^2/R^m\to\infty\) for every fixed \(R\).  Hence no
fixed finite-dimensional transfer-matrix realization exists for the
second moment.  The finite-fiber certificate clauses are therefore
strictly weaker, as logical requirements, than the finite-state
transfer property.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-state growth budget]
\label{cor:finite-state-growth-budget}
Fix \(q=2\).  Every finite-state certificate realization in the sense
of \Cref{def:cert-univ} has at most exponential growth in the
resolution parameter:
\[
  |S_2^{(m)}|\le C R^m\qquad(m\ge1)
\]
for some constants \(C,R>0\) depending on the fixed transfer data.
By contrast, computable finite-fiber covers of the Zeckendorf
resolution data can realize second collision moments of the form
\[
  S_2^\sharp(m)=(a_m+1)^2+F_{m+2}-1
\]
for an arbitrary computable integer sequence \(a_m\ge1\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The finite-state clause of \Cref{def:cert-univ} gives, for \(q=2\), a
fixed finite matrix representation
\[
  S_2^{(m)}=u_2^{\mathsf T}A_2^m v_2 .
\]
Here \(A_2,u_2,v_2\) live in the fixed finite-dimensional complex
coefficient setting specified in \Cref{def:cert-univ}.  The asserted
exponential bound is therefore exactly
\Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget}.  The realization of
arbitrary computable one-spike growth is
the construction and calculation in
\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-fiber certificates are independent of finite-state transfer]
\label{cor:hankel-psd-independent}
Hankel positivity and finite Prony reconstruction are universal
finite-fiber consequences of effective finite resolution data, but
they do not imply the finite-state transfer clause of
\Cref{def:cert-univ}.  Equivalently, finite-fiber certificate
universality is strictly weaker than finite-state certificate
universality.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The universal finite-fiber part is exactly
\Cref{prop:L2-generic-vs-structural}: at a fixed resolution the fiber
sizes give a finite atomic moment measure, hence Hankel positivity and
finite Prony reconstruction with a computable bound from the finite
normal-form list.  To see that these facts do not imply finite-state
transfer, apply \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness} with \(a_m=m!\).
The resulting computable finite-fiber cover is certificate-universal
in the finite-fiber sense, but its second collision moment admits no
fixed finite-matrix representation in the resolution parameter.  This
contradicts the defining finite-state clause for \(q=2\), and proves
the claimed non-implication.
\end{proof}
