\section{Final remarks}\label{sec:discussion}

The hierarchy separates auxiliary structure.  Expression
universality is a representation statement.  Protocol universality
adds computable canonical forms and readouts.  Finite-fiber
certificates attach moment data to normalization fibers, and the
finite-state version adds a uniform transfer computation of those
moments.

This scope is deliberately limited.  The paper studies computable normal forms
and semantic-equality obstructions for single-primitive expression systems; it
does not develop Zeckendorf fibre thermodynamics, folded histogram laws,
finite-observation escape rates, or normalization automata.

The obstructions are correspondingly narrow.  Odrzywo{\l}ek's
operator gives a one-label grammar for a large elementary library.
The Richardson interface theorem says that any finite EML triple with
verified real-domain identities for \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the
nonnegative square root prevents the translated Richardson fragment from
having a computable canonical form that decides identity to zero on the
transported real domain; for the particular printed roots, those identities
are available in this paper only if the external hypothesis
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) is assumed; the finite table alone proves only the identity
of the printed strings.  This add-on is not used by the unconditional hierarchy
spine or by the clauses that rely only on Zeckendorf and finite-fiber structure.
Likewise, the Zeckendorf protocol reads addition on a
free monogenic orbit, while \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction} shows
that multiplication cannot be read there by an injective recoding of
the positive integers.  Under that level-zero orbit restriction, one
must pass from the iterates \(g^{\circ n}\) to the power operators
\(f\mapsto f^{\circ n}\).

The finite-fiber statements have the same character.  Hankel
positivity and finite Prony reconstruction follow formally from
finiteness of the fibers at each resolution, and effective finite
resolution data already make the fixed-\(m\) enumeration, moment
calculation, and Prony reconstruction computable.  The finite-state
strengthening is different: it asks for one fixed transfer-matrix law
in the resolution parameter \(m\), as specified in
\Cref{def:cert-univ}.  The tensor form in \Cref{thm:star-kernel} is only
a conditional compression result for supplied nonnegative tensor
transfer data, not evidence that the Zeckendorf fold fibers themselves
have such data.  \Cref{cor:finite-state-growth-budget}
isolates the fixed-transfer exponential growth budget, and
\Cref{cor:hankel-psd-independent} records the corresponding logical
non-implication: protocol data and finite-fiber certificates do not by
themselves force such a transfer representation.

Thus the three levels may describe the same operations while carrying
different kinds of evidence for them.  Restricted expression classes,
such as polynomials or rational functions, may have decidable
equivalence by other methods; the obstruction here is specific to a
Richardson-undecidable elementary fragment inside the EML-representable
setting.
