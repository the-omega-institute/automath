\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The paper proves a fixed-degree statement with an explicit quantifier order:
for each \(q\), construct one finite carry matrix and then vary the resolution
\(m\).  The state dimension is allowed to depend on \(q\).  The height theorem
shows why this dependence cannot be removed by a single rational matrix law in
a degree marker.

The losslessness proof is specific to the signed Fibonacci update and to the
target equations coming from fold collisions.  It does not assert that every
Fibonacci numeration problem has a bounded carry set, nor that a bounded
approximation is valid for arbitrary terminal conditions.  Likewise, the
primitive conclusion uses the positive-weight trim graph and its zero-column
loop; it is not a statement about untrimmed matrices with unreachable states.

The height formula imports the ordinary interval maximum theorem cited in
Section~\ref{sec:height}.  The new fold-specific content is the representation
pairing and the shifted half-interval conversion.  We do not classify all
maximizing integers or prove a distribution theorem for the fibre sizes.
The bivariate nonrationality corollary is intentionally presented as a
consequence of the height growth and a standard Cauchy estimate, not as a
separate obstruction of comparable depth.

The results leave several natural questions open.  One is to determine the
dependence of the Perron root \(\lambda_q\) and the minimal transfer dimension
on \(q\).  Another is to describe the fibre-size distribution at a fixed
resolution scale.  Neither question is needed for the fixed-degree theorem.
