\section{Final remarks}\label{sec:discussion}

The submission-facing hierarchy separates auxiliary structure.  Expression
universality is a representation statement; in this article the full
\(\mathcal B_{36}\) headline is citation-only background and the printed table is
not load-bearing.  Protocol universality adds computable canonical forms and
readouts by value transport through \(\NN\), not by an online or bounded-window
Zeckendorf arithmetic algorithm.  Finite-fiber certificates attach moment data to
normalization fibers, and the finite-state version adds a uniform transfer
computation of those moments.  For the unmodified Zeckendorf fold, the paper
proves this transfer degree by degree; the strict finite-state separation is the
cover-relative complement, concerning computable finite-fiber covers over the
fixed normal forms rather than a failure theorem for the original \(\Fold_m\)
fibers.

Equivalently, the submission does not contain Zeckendorf fibre thermodynamics,
folded histogram laws, finite-observation escape rates, online normalization
automata, a global single-primitive protocol-rigidity classification, or an
\(L3\) capacity-density theory for Hankel kernels.  Those phrases describe
stronger intrinsic, dynamical, arithmetic, or higher-layer projects than the
cover-relative certificate statements proved here: the present results fix the
Zeckendorf normal-form system, add finite auxiliary fibres only as certificates,
and prove only the stated transfer-budget obstruction in that cover category,
alongside the direct intrinsic transfer theorem for the original fold.

The obstructions are correspondingly narrow.  Odrzywo{\l}ek's
operator gives a one-label grammar for a large elementary library.
The Richardson interface theorem says that any finite EML triple with proved
real-domain identities for \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the punctured absolute value
prevents the translated Richardson fragment from having a computable canonical
form that decides identity to zero on the transported real domain; for the
particular printed strings, those identities are available in this paper only if
the external hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\) is assumed; the finite table alone
proves only the syntax of the printed strings.  This add-on is not used by the
triple-relative hierarchy spine except when one wants the concrete printed
\(U_{47},U_{111}\) instantiation; it is not used by the clauses that rely only on
Zeckendorf and finite-fiber structure.
Likewise, the Zeckendorf protocol reads addition on a
free monogenic orbit, while \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction} shows
that multiplication cannot be read there by an injective recoding of
the positive integers.  The same theorem identifies the exact cyclic-ray
boundary where faithfulness can still survive.  Under that level-zero orbit
restriction, one must pass from the
iterates \(g^{\circ n}\) to the power operators
\(f\mapsto f^{\circ n}\).

The finite-fiber statements have the same character.  The fixed-resolution
finite-atomic certificates follow formally from finiteness of the fibers at each
resolution, and effective finite-resolution data already make the fixed-\(m\)
enumeration and moment calculation computable.  The finite-state
strengthening is different: it asks for one fixed transfer-matrix law
in the resolution parameter \(m\), as specified in
\Cref{def:cert-univ}.  For the unmodified Zeckendorf fold, the bounded carry
automaton gives such data in each fixed degree by
\Cref{thm:intrinsic-fold-moment-transfer}.  \Cref{cor:finite-state-growth-budget}
isolates the fixed-transfer exponential growth budget, and
\Cref{cor:hankel-psd-independent} records the corresponding logical
non-implication: protocol data and finite-fiber certificates do not by
themselves force such a transfer representation.  By
\Cref{thm:moment-height-cover-trichotomy,thm:intrinsic-cover-transfer-dichotomy,thm:L2-cover-relative-closure,thm:main-spine-scope-closure},
the article's submission claim pairs the intrinsic transfer and height endpoint
theorems with this cover-category budget obstruction, while leaving full
intrinsic histogram and large-deviation classifications outside the proof chain.

Thus the three levels may describe the same operations while carrying
different kinds of evidence for them.  Restricted expression classes,
such as polynomials or rational functions, may have decidable
equivalence by other methods; the obstruction here is specific to a
Richardson-undecidable elementary fragment inside the EML-representable
setting.

Removing the appendix leaves every theorem, proposition, lemma, and corollary in
Sections~\ref{sec:intro}--\ref{sec:discussion} with the same hypotheses and
proofs.  The appendix results are downstream consequences of the finite-fibre
objects, not proof inputs for the hierarchy theorem or its separations.

The \(L0\) clause of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} is proved from the EML anchor and
the import boundary in \Cref{sec:L0}.  The \(L1\) clause is proved from
Zeckendorf normal forms and value transport in \Cref{sec:L1}.  The positive
finite-fibre clause is proved from the fixed-resolution moment, Hankel, and
Prony material in \Cref{sec:L2}.  The strictness clauses are proved in
\Cref{sec:strictness}: the punctured Richardson interface supplies the
normalizer obstruction, the one-free-monogenic-orbit theorem supplies the
multiplication obstruction, the semantic compilation boundary records the
compiler separation, and the computable-cover theorem supplies the finite-state
cover obstruction.  These citations all point to results in
Sections~\ref{sec:intro}--\ref{sec:discussion}.  Hence the appendix can be
excised without changing the proof graph of the main hierarchy or any separation
proved before the appendix begins.
