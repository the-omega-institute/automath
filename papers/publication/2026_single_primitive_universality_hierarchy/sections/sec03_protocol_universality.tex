\section{\texorpdfstring{\(L1\)}{L1}: protocol universality via a Zeckendorf protocol}\label{sec:L1}

We now pass from \(L0\) expression universality to \(L1\),
\emph{protocol universality}, by adding the normal-form data absent
from expression universality.
Let \(\Omega_m=\{0,1\}^m\).  The elements of \(\Omega_m\) may be
regarded as finite itineraries of a one-generator symbolic source,
although no dynamical property will be used.  The fold map sends a
word to its Zeckendorf address.  This elementary construction gives
the two pieces needed later: a computable normal form and readout
maps from normal forms to the intended semantic objects.

\subsection{The fold}
\label{ssec:fold}

Let \(F_1=F_2=1\) and \(F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n\).  For \(m\ge1\), let
\[
  X_m=\{c_1\cdots c_m\in\{0,1\}^m:
        c_kc_{k+1}=0 \text{ for } 1\le k<m\}
\]
be the set of legal Fibonacci words, and define
\[
  \Val_m(c_1\cdots c_m)=\sum_{k=1}^m c_kF_{k+1}.
\]

\begin{definition}[Fold map]\label{def:fold}
For \(\omega=\omega_1\cdots\omega_m\in\Omega_m\), set
\[
  N(\omega)=\sum_{k=1}^m \omega_kF_{k+1}.
\]
The finite fold map at resolution \(m\) is the map
\(\Fold_m\colon\Omega_m\to X_m\) sending \(\omega\) to the unique
legal word \(c\in X_m\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fold-residue}
  \Val_m(c)\equiv N(\omega)\pmod {F_{m+2}}.
\end{equation}
The existence and uniqueness of this residue representative follow
from \Cref{prop:fold-confluence}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Greedy Zeckendorf normalizer]
\label{lem:greedy-zeckendorf-normalizer}
For every \(n\in\NN\) the following greedy procedure terminates and
returns the unique element \(c\in X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\) with
\(\Val(c)=n\).  If \(n=0\), return the zero word.  Otherwise choose
the largest Fibonacci number \(F_j\le n\) with \(j\ge2\), write a
\(1\) in position \(j-1\), replace \(n\) by \(n-F_j\), and iterate.
No two chosen positions are adjacent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At each nonzero step the remainder strictly decreases, so the
procedure terminates.  Suppose the first chosen summand is \(F_j\).
Then the remainder \(r\) satisfies \(0\le r<n-F_j<F_{j+1}-F_j=F_{j-1}\),
because maximality gives \(n<F_{j+1}\).  Thus the next possible
summand is at most \(F_{j-2}\), so the adjacent Fibonacci number
\(F_{j-1}\) is never chosen.  The same argument applies recursively
and proves legality.

For uniqueness, let \(c,d\in X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\) have the same
value and choose the largest index \(j\) at which their digits differ.
Assume \(c_{j-1}=1\) and \(d_{j-1}=0\), so the value contributed by
\(c\) at that top index is \(F_j\).  The largest value representable by
legal digits in positions below \(j-1\) is \(F_j-1\), as follows by
induction from the recurrence
\[
  M_t=\max(M_{t-1},F_{t+1}+M_{t-2}),\qquad M_0=0,\quad M_1=1.
\]
Hence the lower digits of \(d\) cannot compensate for the missing
\(F_j\), a contradiction.  Thus the legal expansion is unique, and the
terminating greedy output is the unique preimage under \(\Val\).  This
is the usual Zeckendorf theorem in its greedy form
\cite{Zeckendorf1972}; digit-local addition and normalization belong to
the standard Zeckendorf--Ostrowski arithmetic literature
\cite{AhlbachEtAl2013,HieronymiTerry2018}.  Stable digit-local
Zeckendorf arithmetic beyond the value-transported readout used here is
kept outside this article and is not a proof input for the protocol
theorem below.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite fold normal forms]
\label{prop:fold-confluence}
For every \(m\ge1\), the map
\[
  \Val_m\colon X_m\longrightarrow \{0,1,\ldots,F_{m+2}-1\}
\]
is a bijection.  Hence \(\Fold_m\) is well defined and computable.
Moreover, if
\[
  X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}
  =\{(c_1,c_2,\ldots)\in\{0,1\}^{\NN}:
    c_kc_{k+1}=0 \text{ for all }k,\ c_k=0\text{ for }k\gg0\},
\]
then
\[
  \Val(c)=\sum_{k\ge1}c_kF_{k+1}
\]
is a bijection \(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\to\NN\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(0\le n<F_{m+2}\).  By
\Cref{lem:greedy-zeckendorf-normalizer}, \(n\) has a unique legal
Zeckendorf expansion.  Since the first summand chosen by the greedy
algorithm is strictly smaller than \(F_{m+2}\), no digit beyond
position \(m\) occurs.  Thus \(n\) is in the image of \(\Val_m\).
The same lemma gives injectivity, because two words in \(X_m\) are in
particular two eventually-zero legal words.  Hence \(\Val_m\) is a
bijection onto \(\{0,1,\ldots,F_{m+2}-1\}\).  The infinite statement is
\Cref{lem:greedy-zeckendorf-normalizer} itself.  Computability follows
from the terminating greedy algorithm.  The finite-automaton viewpoint
on the same Zeckendorf--Ostrowski arithmetic is standard
\cite{AhlbachEtAl2013,HieronymiTerry2018}, but no automaton theorem is
used in this proof.
\end{proof}

The finite map \(\Fold_m\) is a residue-level normalizer.  The
stable normalizer of a finite binary word \(\omega\) is the
unreduced Zeckendorf address
\[
  \mathrm{NF}_{\infty}(\omega)=\Val^{-1}(N(\omega))
  \in X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}},
\]
where \(\omega\) is extended by zeros after its last digit.  Thus the
finite maps \(\Fold_m\) provide the certificate fibers, whereas
arithmetic is read on the countable set of eventually-zero normal
forms.

The normalization used here is the stable, eventually-zero
normalization attached to the value \(\Val\), not a limit operation on
the compact completion.  We use only the classical Zeckendorf uniqueness
and effective arithmetic anchors \cite{Zeckendorf1972,AhlbachEtAl2013,
HieronymiTerry2018}, in the value-transported form proved in
\Cref{lem:greedy-zeckendorf-normalizer,prop:fold-confluence,prop:finite-residue-not-stable,thm:semiring}.
Questions about stable inverse-limit arithmetic and finite-window
thermodynamic statistics are intentionally not invoked here.

The finite legal-word spaces also form an inverse system under
coordinate truncation.  The arithmetic used below lives on its
eventually-zero part, not on the whole compact limit.

\begin{definition}[Finite and compact completions]
\label{def:completions}
The eventually-zero space \(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\) is the
countable set in \Cref{prop:fold-confluence}, identified with
\(\NN\) by \(\Val\).  The compact inverse limit is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:X-infty}
  \widehat X
  =\varprojlim_m X_m
  \cong
  \{(c_1,c_2,\ldots)\in\{0,1\}^{\NN}:
    c_kc_{k+1}=0\text{ for all }k\}.
\end{equation}
It is a compact zero-dimensional space, analogous to a profinite
completion \cite{RibesZalesskii2010}.  The arithmetic operations
below are carried out on \(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:finite-vs-profinite}
The valuation is not a bijection \(\widehat X\to\NN\), since
\(\widehat X\) is uncountable.  Stable arithmetic is therefore an
operation on eventually-zero addresses, not on the full compact
inverse limit.
\end{remark}


\subsection{Arithmetic by readout}
\label{ssec:arith}

\begin{definition}[Stable addition and multiplication]
\label{def:stable-arith}
For \(c,d\in X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\), define
\begin{align}
  c\oplus d
    &=\Val^{-1}\bigl(\Val(c)+\Val(d)\bigr),\label{eq:stable-add}\\
  c\otimes d
    &=\Val^{-1}\bigl(\Val(c)\Val(d)\bigr).
      \label{eq:stable-mul}
\end{align}
Equivalently, \(c\oplus d\) is obtained by adding the two values and
then normalizing; this is the same as coordinatewise digit addition
followed by Zeckendorf carrying.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Finite residue arithmetic is not stable arithmetic]
\label{prop:finite-residue-not-stable}
For each \(m\ge1\), let \(\rho_m\colon
X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\to X_m\) be the finite residue readout
\[
  \rho_m(c)=\Val_m^{-1}\bigl(\Val(c)\bmod F_{m+2}\bigr).
\]
Transporting addition and multiplication on \(\ZZ/F_{m+2}\ZZ\)
through \(\Val_m\) makes \(X_m\) a finite ring, and \(\rho_m\) is the
corresponding semiring homomorphism from
\((X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}},\oplus,\otimes)\) to this residue ring.
However no single finite level \(X_m\) carries the stable arithmetic:
\(\rho_m\) is not injective and therefore cannot distinguish all
stable normal forms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The bijection \(\Val_m\colon X_m\to\ZZ/F_{m+2}\ZZ\) is
\Cref{prop:fold-confluence}; transporting the two ring operations along
this bijection gives a ring on \(X_m\).  By definition of \(\rho_m\),
\[
  \Val_m(\rho_m(c\oplus d))
  \equiv \Val(c)+\Val(d)
  \equiv \Val_m(\rho_m(c))+\Val_m(\rho_m(d))
  \pmod{F_{m+2}},
\]
and the identical calculation with products proves compatibility with
\(\otimes\).  Thus \(\rho_m\) is precisely residue transport through
\(\ZZ/F_{m+2}\ZZ\).

It is not injective: if \(0_X=\Val^{-1}(0)\) and
\(u_m=\Val^{-1}(F_{m+2})\), then \(0_X\ne u_m\) in
\(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\), while
\(\rho_m(0_X)=\rho_m(u_m)\) because their values are congruent modulo
\(F_{m+2}\).  Therefore the finite transported ring records only a
residue class and cannot be the stable arithmetic on the full set of
eventually-zero normal forms.
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}[Transported semiring structure]
\label{thm:semiring}
\((X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}},\oplus,\otimes)\) is a commutative
semiring with additive identity \(0_X=(0,0,\ldots)\) and
multiplicative identity \(\Val^{-1}(1)\).  The valuation map
\[
  \Val\colon
  (X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}},\oplus,\otimes)
  \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }(\NN,+,\times)
\]
is a semiring isomorphism.  At finite resolution, the only arithmetic
asserted is the residue-level transport through \(\ZZ/F_{m+2}\ZZ\)
proved in \Cref{prop:finite-residue-not-stable}; the stable semiring
operation is the one on \(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The operations \(\oplus\) and \(\otimes\) are the pullbacks of
\(+\) and \(\times\) along the bijection \(\Val\).  The semiring
axioms and the asserted isomorphism are immediate.  The finite
statement is \Cref{prop:finite-residue-not-stable}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No finite-level intrinsic arithmetic for the protocol]
\label{thm:no-finite-level-arithmetic}
Fix any resolution \(m\ge1\) and any operations
\(a_m,b_m\colon X_m\times X_m\to X_m\).  There do not exist operations
\(a_m,b_m\) for which the finite readout \(\rho_m\) simultaneously
determines stable addition and stable multiplication in the sense that
\[
  \rho_m(c\oplus d)=a_m(\rho_m(c),\rho_m(d)),
  \qquad
  \rho_m(c\otimes d)=b_m(\rho_m(c),\rho_m(d))
\]
and also separates stable normal forms.  In particular, the protocol
theorem below cannot be read as a theorem that a bounded finite window
\(X_m\) carries the arithmetic of \(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\).  The only
arithmetic interface used in this article is the value-transported one:
compute \(\Val\), perform ordinary integer arithmetic, and return the
greedy Zeckendorf normal form.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The displayed identities alone are not the obstruction: they are supplied
by the residue operations of \Cref{prop:finite-residue-not-stable}, namely
ordinary addition and multiplication transported through
\(\Val_m\colon X_m\to\ZZ/F_{m+2}\ZZ\).  The obstruction is separation of
stable normal forms by one finite window.  By
\Cref{prop:finite-residue-not-stable}, the map \(\rho_m\) is not
injective: with \(0_X=\Val^{-1}(0)\) and
\(u_m=\Val^{-1}(F_{m+2})\), one has \(0_X\ne u_m\) but
\(\rho_m(0_X)=\rho_m(u_m)\).  Hence no data living only on this fixed
finite set \(X_m\), regardless of how its binary operations are chosen,
can both determine the stable operation outputs through \(\rho_m\) and
distinguish all stable addresses.  A protocol for arithmetic on
\(\NN\), whose decoding map is the bijection
\(\Val\colon X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\to\NN\), must therefore use the
stable eventually-zero normal forms rather than a single residue level.
The construction used below is exactly that stable value-transport
construction.
\end{proof}

Thus arithmetic has not been added as a primitive operation on
words.  It is read through valuation and stable Zeckendorf
normalization; residue reduction is only the finite-resolution
version used for certificates.  This article uses only this
value-transported arithmetic.  Digit-local algorithms for Zeckendorf
addition and normalization are classical in the broader literature
\cite{AhlbachEtAl2013,HieronymiTerry2018}, but they are not needed for
the protocol proof below.

\begin{lemma}[Value-transported arithmetic is sufficient for the protocol]
\label{lem:value-transported-suffices}
The operations \(\oplus\) and \(\otimes\) of
\Cref{def:stable-arith} are total computable operations on
\(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\).  Moreover, the proof of
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ} uses no intrinsic online
Zeckendorf addition or multiplication theorem: it uses only the
computable bijection \(\Val\colon X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\to\NN\),
ordinary integer arithmetic in \(\NN\), and re-normalization by
\(\Val^{-1}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An element of \(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\) is a finite legal
Fibonacci word, so \(\Val(c)\) is computed by a finite sum.  Given
\(n\in\NN\), the inverse \(\Val^{-1}(n)\) is computed by the greedy
Zeckendorf algorithm, or equivalently by the finite normalizer of
\Cref{prop:fold-confluence} applied at any resolution large enough to
contain the Zeckendorf support of \(n\).  Hence
\[
  (c,d)\longmapsto \Val^{-1}(\Val(c)+\Val(d))
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  (c,d)\longmapsto \Val^{-1}(\Val(c)\Val(d))
\]
are total computable operations on eventually-zero legal words.  The
realization equations used below are exactly the semiring identities
obtained by applying \(\Val\) to these two displayed formulas and then
reading the resulting integer through the chosen semantic maps.  No
step requires a bounded-window carry rule, an online transducer, or an
intrinsic digit-local multiplication algorithm.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Value transport is exactly the arithmetic used]
\label{prop:value-transport-exact-interface}
Let \(\mathcal A\) be the smallest class of algorithms on
\(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\) obtained from the following three routines:
computing \(\Val(c)\), applying ordinary integer arithmetic to the
computed values, and returning the greedy Zeckendorf normal form
\(\Val^{-1}(n)\).  Then \(\oplus\), \(\otimes\), the stable normalizer
\(\mathrm{NF}_{\infty}\), and all compiled operations used in
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ} belong to \(\mathcal A\).  Conversely,
the proof of \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ} uses no operation on
Zeckendorf strings except members of this value-transport class and the
literal equality test on finite legal words.

Thus the protocol theorem is a computable address/readout theorem.  It is
not a bounded-window, online, finite-state, or intrinsic digit-local
arithmetic theorem for Zeckendorf multiplication.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By definition,
\(c\oplus d=\Val^{-1}(\Val(c)+\Val(d))\) and
\(c\otimes d=\Val^{-1}(\Val(c)\Val(d))\), so both operations are in
\(\mathcal A\).  For a raw finite binary word \(\omega\), the stable
normalizer first computes the integer
\(N(\omega)=\sum_k \omega_kF_{k+1}\) and then returns
\(\Val^{-1}(N(\omega))\), hence it is also in \(\mathcal A\).  Literal
equality of normal forms is decidable because a normal form is a finite
legal word, with trailing zeroes suppressed.

The remaining assertions in \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ} are
readout identities.  At level zero the normal form \(c_n\) is sent to
\(g^{\circ n}\), and the equality
\(g^{\circ(m+n)}=g^{\circ m}\circ g^{\circ n}\) is a semantic identity in
\(\End(A)\).  At level one the normal form \(c_n\) is sent to
\(I_n\), and \(I_{mn}=I_m\circ I_n\) is a semantic identity in
\(\End(\End(A))\).  Neither step manipulates Zeckendorf digits by a local
carry rule; it only reads the value already computed by the
value-transport routines and applies \Cref{thm:two-level}.  Inspecting
the proof of \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ} shows that these are all
the compiled operations and tests used there.  Therefore the theorem has
exactly the advertised value-transport interface and no stronger
digit-local arithmetic content.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Composition as the primitive}
\label{ssec:sole}

\begin{proposition}[Two-level readout of composition]
\label{thm:two-level}
Let \(A\) be a nonempty set and let \(g\colon A\to A\) be an
endomorphism of infinite order.  Define
\begin{align}
  S_0&=\langle g\rangle
      =\{g^{\circ n}:n\in\NN\}\subseteq\End(A),\label{eq:level0}\\
  S_1&=\{I_n:n\in\NN\}\subseteq\End(\End(A)),
      \qquad I_n(f)=f^{\circ n}.\label{eq:level1}
\end{align}
With \(\iota_0(n)=g^{\circ n}\) and \(\iota_1(n)=I_n\), the maps
\(\iota_0\) and \(\iota_1\) are injective and
\begin{align}
  \iota_0(m+n)&=\iota_0(m)\circ\iota_0(n),
    \label{eq:add-from-comp}\\
  \iota_1(mn)&=\iota_1(m)\circ\iota_1(n).
    \label{eq:mul-from-comp}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first identity is
\(g^{\circ m}\circ g^{\circ n}=g^{\circ(m+n)}\), and infinite order
gives injectivity of \(\iota_0\).  The same hypothesis gives
injectivity of \(\iota_1\), since \(I_m(g)=g^{\circ m}\) and
\(I_n(g)=g^{\circ n}\).  Finally,
\[
  (I_m\circ I_n)(f)=I_m(f^{\circ n})
    =(f^{\circ n})^{\circ m}=f^{\circ mn}=I_{mn}(f).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Zeckendorf readout of Church iteration]
\label{cor:minimal}
Under the identification \(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\cong\NN\),
the address \(c_n=\Val^{-1}(n)\) represents the \(n\)-fold iterate.
Addition of addresses is the level-zero composition law, and
multiplication is the level-one composition law.  This is the usual
Church-numeral iteration identity \cite{Church1936}, expressed in
Zeckendorf normal form.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{thm:two-level} after replacing \(n\) by its address
\(c_n\).  The semiring identification in \Cref{thm:semiring}
transports the addition and multiplication laws to stable
addresses.
\end{proof}

No new identity about Church iteration is involved.  The point is
that the usual iteration laws remain decidable after the indices are
placed in Zeckendorf normal form.

\begin{lemma}[Exponent skeleton for a level-zero multiplicative readout]
\label{lem:level-zero-exponent-skeleton}
Let \(M\) be a monoid, let \(g\in M\) have infinite order, and put
\(C=\{g^n:n\in\NN\}\).  If a map
\(\eta\colon\NN_{\ge1}\to C\) satisfies
\(\eta(ab)=\eta(a)\eta(b)\), then there is a unique function
\(e\colon\NN_{\ge1}\to\NN\) such that
\[
  \eta(n)=g^{e(n)}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  e(ab)=e(a)+e(b).
\]
Moreover \(e(1)=0\), and \(e\) is determined by its values on
primes:
\[
  e(n)=\sum_p v_p(n)e(p)
  \qquad(n\ge1).
\]
Consequently \(\eta\) is injective if and only if the additive weight
map \(n\mapsto\sum_p v_p(n)e(p)\) separates all positive integers.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The infinite order of \(g\) makes \(n\mapsto g^n\) injective, so each
element of \(C\) has a unique exponent.  This gives the unique
function \(e\) with \(\eta(n)=g^{e(n)}\).  The multiplicativity of
\(\eta\) is equivalent, by uniqueness of exponents, to
\[
  g^{e(ab)}=g^{e(a)+e(b)},
\]
hence to \(e(ab)=e(a)+e(b)\).  Taking \(a=b=1\) gives
\(e(1)=2e(1)\), so \(e(1)=0\).  If
\(n=\prod_p p^{v_p(n)}\), repeated use of additivity gives
\[
  e(n)=\sum_p v_p(n)e(p).
\]
The final assertion follows because \(\eta(a)=\eta(b)\) is equivalent
to \(e(a)=e(b)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Level-zero obstruction for multiplicative readout]
\label{thm:level-zero-obstruction}
Let \(M\) be a monoid and let \(g\in M\) have infinite order.  Put
\[
  C=\{g^n:n\in\NN\}\subset M .
\]
There is no injective map
\(\eta\colon\NN_{\ge1}\to C\) satisfying
\[
  \eta(ab)=\eta(a)\eta(b)
  \qquad(a,b\ge1).
\]
Consequently multiplication on the positive integers cannot be
represented by the ambient monoid product on a single free
monogenic orbit, even after arbitrary recoding of the numerals into
that orbit.  In the composition model of \Cref{thm:two-level},
therefore, multiplicative readout requires a change of level from
the orbit \(\{g^{\circ n}\}\) to the power operators
\(f\mapsto f^{\circ n}\), under this level-zero monogenic-orbit
restriction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(e\) be the exponent weight supplied by
\Cref{lem:level-zero-exponent-skeleton}.  If \(p\) is prime and
\(e(p)=0\), then \(\eta(p)=\eta(1)\), contrary to injectivity.
Thus \(e(p)>0\) for every prime \(p\).

Choose two distinct primes \(p\) and \(r\), and set
\[
  A=e(p)>0,\qquad B=e(r)>0 .
\]
Then
\[
  e(p^B)=B\,e(p)=AB=A\,e(r)=e(r^A).
\]
Hence \(\eta(p^B)=\eta(r^A)\), while unique factorization gives
\(p^B\ne r^A\).  This contradiction proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Classification of one-orbit multiplicative readouts]
\label{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification}
Let \(M\) be a monoid, let \(g\in M\) have infinite order, and put
\(C=\{g^n:n\in\NN\}\).  The multiplicative readouts
\(\eta\colon\NN_{\ge1}\to C\) satisfying
\[
  \eta(ab)=\eta(a)\eta(b)\qquad(a,b\ge1)
\]
are in bijection with functions \(w\colon\mathcal P\to\NN\) on the set
\(\mathcal P\) of primes, via
\[
  \eta_w(n)=g^{\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}v_p(n)w(p)} .
\]
No such readout is injective.  More sharply, if \(p\ne r\) are primes,
then either \(\eta_w(p)=\eta_w(1)\), or \(\eta_w(r)=\eta_w(1)\), or there
is a collision between two distinct prime powers:
\[
  \eta_w\bigl(p^{w(r)}\bigr)=\eta_w\bigl(r^{w(p)}\bigr).
\]
Equivalently, for any nonempty set of primes \(Q\), the restriction of
\(\eta_w\) to the free commutative submonoid generated by \(Q\) is
injective if and only if \(Q\) consists of exactly one prime and that prime
has positive weight.  Thus the only nontrivial injective pieces of a
level-zero ambient-product readout are one-prime-power rays.
Consequently the obstruction in \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction} is not
an artifact of a chosen coding of numerals: the entire classifiable
skeleton of one-orbit ambient-product readouts collapses either a prime
against \(1\) or two distinct prime powers against each other.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\eta\) be a multiplicative readout.  By
\Cref{lem:level-zero-exponent-skeleton} there is a unique exponent
function \(e\colon\NN_{\ge1}\to\NN\) such that
\(\eta(n)=g^{e(n)}\) and
\[
  e(n)=\sum_p v_p(n)e(p).
\]
Thus \(w(p)=e(p)\) determines \(\eta\), and the displayed formula for
\(\eta_w\) follows.  Conversely, for any function
\(w\colon\mathcal P\to\NN\), the sum
\(e_w(n)=\sum_p v_p(n)w(p)\) is finite and satisfies
\[
  e_w(ab)=e_w(a)+e_w(b),
\]
because \(v_p(ab)=v_p(a)+v_p(b)\) for every prime \(p\).  Hence
\[
  \eta_w(ab)=g^{e_w(ab)}=g^{e_w(a)+e_w(b)}=
  g^{e_w(a)}g^{e_w(b)}=\eta_w(a)\eta_w(b),
\]
so the correspondence is onto; uniqueness of exponents in the free orbit
gives one-to-one correspondence.

It remains to prove the asserted collapse.  Choose two distinct primes
\(p\ne r\).  If \(w(p)=0\), then
\(\eta_w(p)=g^0=\eta_w(1)\).  If \(w(r)=0\), then
\(\eta_w(r)=\eta_w(1)\).  Otherwise both weights are positive, and
\[
  e_w\bigl(p^{w(r)}\bigr)=w(r)w(p)=w(p)w(r)
  =e_w\bigl(r^{w(p)}\bigr).
\]
The infinite order of \(g\) converts equality of exponents into equality
of readouts, while unique factorization gives
\(p^{w(r)}\ne r^{w(p)}\).  Therefore \(\eta_w\) is not injective in every
case.

Now fix a set of primes \(Q\), and write \(\NN(Q)\) for the free
commutative submonoid it generates.  If \(Q\) contains a prime \(p\) with
\(w(p)=0\), then \(\eta_w(p)=\eta_w(1)\), so the restriction is not
injective.  If \(Q\) contains two distinct primes of positive weight, the
prime-power collision just proved lies inside \(\NN(Q)\), so again the
restriction is not injective.  Hence injectivity on \(\NN(Q)\) forces
\(Q\) to contain no zero-weight prime and at most one positive-weight
prime.  Conversely, if \(Q=\{p\}\) and \(w(p)>0\), then
\(\eta_w(p^a)=g^{a w(p)}\); infinite order of \(g\) makes the exponents
\(a w(p)\) distinct, so the restriction to the one-prime ray is injective.
The empty case is the one-point submonoid and is harmless; the nontrivial
injective pieces are exactly the one-prime-power rays.  Since every
one-orbit multiplicative readout is some \(\eta_w\), this proves the final
assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Protocol universality]\label{def:prot-univ}
Let \(\mathcal O\) be a class of operations on a set \(D\).  A
\emph{single-primitive protocol} for \(\mathcal O\) consists of
\begin{enumerate}
\item a decidable set \(C\) of raw codes and a computable
  normalization map \(\mathrm{NF}\colon C\to N\) onto a set \(N\) of
  unique normal forms, with decidable equality on \(N\);
\item a decoding map \(\nu\colon N\to D\);
\item a finite family of semantic sorts \(Y_\ell\), each equipped
  with an interpretation \(\sigma_\ell\) of one binary operation
  symbol \(\sigma\);
\item semantic readout maps \(\Phi_\ell\colon N\to Y_\ell\).
\end{enumerate}
An operation \(\theta\in\mathcal O\) of arity \(r\) is realized if
there are a computable operation
\(\widehat\theta\colon N^r\to N\), a level \(\ell\), and a
\(\sigma\)-term \(R_\theta\) in \(r\) variables such that
\[
  \nu(\widehat\theta(n_1,\ldots,n_r))
  =\theta(\nu(n_1),\ldots,\nu(n_r))
\]
and
\[
  \Phi_\ell(\widehat\theta(n_1,\ldots,n_r))
  =
  R_\theta(\Phi_\ell(n_1),\ldots,\Phi_\ell(n_r))
\]
for all \(n_i\in N\).  The protocol is
\emph{protocol-universal for \(\mathcal O\)} if every operation in
\(\mathcal O\) is realized in this sense.  The computability
requirements in this definition are exactly the normal-form and
compiled-operation requirements on \(C\) and \(N\).  The equations in
the semantic sorts are readout identities; unless an additional
effective presentation of a semantic sort is specified, they are not
claims that the elements of \(Y_\ell\), the operation \(\sigma_\ell\),
or the maps \(\Phi_\ell\) are algorithmically presented.  Thus the
normalizer is algorithmic presentation data, while the only algebraic
operation used in the readout terms is \(\sigma\), as in a canonical
rewriting system with a decidable word problem \cite{BaaderNipkow1998}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Semantic readouts do not add hidden algorithms]
\label{prop:semantic-readout-separation}
In \Cref{def:prot-univ}, equality of compiled normal forms is decided
entirely in \(N\), and realization of an operation
\(\theta\in\mathcal O\) gives a computable normal-form operation
\(\widehat\theta\colon N^r\to N\).  The readout clause adds only the
semantic identity
\[
  \Phi_\ell(\widehat\theta(n_1,\ldots,n_r))
  =R_\theta(\Phi_\ell(n_1),\ldots,\Phi_\ell(n_r))
\]
inside the specified sort \(Y_\ell\).  Consequently a protocol over an
arbitrary semantic set is a genuine algorithmic normal-form system on
\(N\), but it asserts computability of semantic readouts only after
one separately supplies effective presentations of the relevant
semantic sorts, their binary operation, and the maps \(\Phi_\ell\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first item of \Cref{def:prot-univ} supplies the decidable raw-code
set, the computable normalizer, and decidable equality on normal
forms.  In the realization clause, \(\widehat\theta\) is explicitly
required to be computable as a map on normal forms, so the compiled
operation can be executed without performing any computation in the
semantic sorts.  The two displayed identities in the definition then
have different roles: the first is an equality in the represented
domain \(D\), and the second is an equality in the chosen semantic
sort \(Y_\ell\) after interpreting the single binary symbol there.
No enumeration, equality algorithm, or composition algorithm for
\(Y_\ell\) occurs among the data of the definition.  Hence no such
algorithm is implied.  If these additional effective presentations are
provided, then computability of the readout maps is an extra property
of that enriched presentation, not a hidden hypothesis of protocol
universality itself.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Computability locus of the Zeckendorf protocol]
\label{cor:zeckendorf-computability-locus}
In \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}, the computable data are exactly
the raw finite-word set, the greedy Zeckendorf normalizer
\(\mathrm{NF}_{\infty}\), decidable equality of legal finite words, and
the compiled operations \(\oplus\) and \(\otimes\) obtained by valuation
transport.  The maps
\[
  c_n\longmapsto g^{\circ n}\in\End(A),
  \qquad
  c_n\longmapsto I_n\in\End(\End(A))
\]
are semantic readout identities in the supplied sorts
\(\End(A)\) and \(\End(\End(A))\).  Unless \(A\) and these endomorphism
sorts are separately given effective presentations with computable
composition and computable evaluation of the displayed maps, the theorem
does not assert an algorithm for computing inside those semantic sorts.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The raw codes are finite binary words, so they are decidable.  The
normalizer \(\mathrm{NF}_{\infty}\) is computable by first evaluating the
finite weighted sum \(\sum_k\omega_kF_{k+1}\) and then applying the greedy
Zeckendorf normalizer of \Cref{lem:greedy-zeckendorf-normalizer}.  Equality
on normal forms is literal equality of finite legal words, hence is
decidable.  The operations \(\oplus\) and \(\otimes\) are computed by
valuating the two inputs in \(\NN\), applying ordinary integer addition or
multiplication, and returning the greedy Zeckendorf normal form, exactly
as in \Cref{lem:value-transported-suffices}.

The two displayed maps take values in the semantic sorts chosen in
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}.  By
\Cref{prop:semantic-readout-separation}, protocol universality supplies no
enumeration, equality algorithm, composition algorithm, or evaluation
algorithm for an arbitrary supplied semantic sort; it supplies only the
readout identities after the sort and its interpretation of the single
binary operation have been specified.  Therefore the Zeckendorf protocol's
algorithmic content is precisely the finite-word normal-form calculus and
the valuation-transported compiled operations, and any additional
computability of the endomorphism readouts belongs to an enriched effective
presentation not assumed in the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Protocol dichotomy on one level-zero orbit]
\label{cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}
Let a protocol in the sense of \Cref{def:prot-univ} contain a
positive-integer locus represented by a section
\(\rho\colon\NN_{\ge1}\to N\) with
\(\nu(\rho(a))=a\).  Suppose that, at one semantic level, the
single operation symbol is interpreted as the ambient product on a
free monogenic orbit
\[
  C=\{g^n:n\in\NN\}
\]
for an infinite-order element \(g\), and write
\(\eta(a)=\Phi_\ell(\rho(a))\in C\).  If multiplication is read at
that same level by the ambient product in the strict form
\[
  \Phi_\ell(\rho(ab))
  =
  \Phi_\ell(\rho(a))\,\Phi_\ell(\rho(b))
  \qquad(a,b\ge1),
\]
then \(\eta\) is not injective.  Equivalently, under this
level-zero monogenic-orbit restriction, a protocol has the following
dichotomy: either the orbit readout fails to separate positive
integers, or multiplication is not realized by the ambient product on
that single orbit.  Thus any protocol readout which separates
positive integers must move multiplication away from this one
level-zero orbit, as in the level-one power-operator readout of
\Cref{thm:two-level}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The displayed compatibility condition says exactly that
\(\eta(ab)=\eta(a)\eta(b)\) for all \(a,b\ge1\).  Since \(C\) is one
free monogenic orbit generated by an infinite-order element, this is
the situation of \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}.  That theorem
rules out injectivity of such an \(\eta\).  Therefore the two desired
requirements, injective separation of positive integers and
level-zero ambient-product realization of multiplication on the
same orbit, cannot hold simultaneously.  The last sentence is the
contrapositive applied to a readout that does separate the positive
integer locus.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Value-transported Zeckendorf protocol universality]
\label{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}
Let \(A\) be a nonempty set and let \(g\colon A\to A\) be an
endomorphism of infinite order.  The Zeckendorf address system with
raw codes the finite binary words, normal forms
\(N=X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\), normalizer
\(\mathrm{NF}_{\infty}\), decoding map \(\nu=\Val\), and the two
composition readouts
\[
  \Phi_0(c_n)=g^{\circ n},\qquad
  \Phi_1(c_n)=I_n,\qquad c_n=\Val^{-1}(n),
\]
is a single-primitive protocol universal for
\(\mathcal O=\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).  This is a value-transported
protocol: addition is realized by
\(\widehat{+}=\oplus\) at level \(0\), and multiplication is
realized by \(\widehat{\times}=\otimes\) at level \(1\).  In both
cases the readout term is the one binary operation symbol,
  interpreted as composition on the relevant semantic sort.  No bounded-window,
  online, or intrinsic digit-local Zeckendorf arithmetic theorem is asserted;
  \Cref{thm:no-finite-level-arithmetic} records the corresponding obstruction
  to reading the stable arithmetic from any one finite residue level.
The computable part of the theorem is the Zeckendorf normal-form
calculus on \(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\), together with the valuation
transport of \Cref{lem:value-transported-suffices}; the displayed maps into
\(\End(A)\) and \(\End(\End(A))\) are semantic readout identities in
the sense of \Cref{prop:semantic-readout-separation}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(C\) be the decidable set of finite binary words and let
\(N=X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\).  The normal form of a raw word is
\(\mathrm{NF}_{\infty}\), obtained by computing the integer
\(\sum_k\omega_kF_{k+1}\) and applying the greedy Zeckendorf
normalizer of \Cref{lem:greedy-zeckendorf-normalizer}, the classical
Zeckendorf theorem in effective form \cite{Zeckendorf1972}.  Equality
of normal forms is literal equality of finite legal words, hence is
decidable by \Cref{prop:fold-confluence}.  The
decoding map is \(\nu=\Val\).

Interpret the single operation symbol as composition on both
semantic sorts
\[
  Y_0=\End(A),\qquad Y_1=\End(\End(A)).
\]
The readout maps are the displayed maps
\(\Phi_0(c_n)=g^{\circ n}\) and \(\Phi_1(c_n)=I_n\).  They are
well defined because \(\Val\) is a bijection
\(X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\to\NN\).

For addition take \(\widehat{+}=\oplus\).  This operation is
computable: compute the two values, add them in \(\NN\), and return
the Zeckendorf normal form.  The semantic clause is
\[
  \nu(c_m\oplus c_n)=m+n,
\]
by \Cref{thm:semiring}, whose computability input is exactly
\Cref{lem:value-transported-suffices}.  The readout clause at level \(0\) is
\[
  \Phi_0(c_m\oplus c_n)
  =g^{\circ(m+n)}
  =g^{\circ m}\circ g^{\circ n}
  =\Phi_0(c_m)\circ\Phi_0(c_n),
\]
which is \Cref{thm:two-level}.

For multiplication take \(\widehat{\times}=\otimes\).  It is
computable by the same valuation-normalization procedure, using
integer multiplication.  Again \Cref{thm:semiring} gives
\[
  \nu(c_m\otimes c_n)=mn.
\]
At level \(1\), \Cref{thm:two-level} gives
\[
  \Phi_1(c_m\otimes c_n)
  =I_{mn}
  =I_m\circ I_n
  =\Phi_1(c_m)\circ\Phi_1(c_n).
\]
Thus both operations in \(\{+,\times\}\) are realized using only the
single binary operation symbol in the readout.  The finite maps
\(\Fold_m\) are the resolution quotients whose fibers are studied in
\Cref{sec:L2}; by \Cref{prop:finite-residue-not-stable} they are
only residue quotients and do not alter the stable readout just
verified.
Finally, because \(A\) is arbitrary, no effective presentation of
\(\End(A)\), of composition in \(\End(A)\), or of the map
\(n\mapsto g^{\circ n}\) is being asserted.  The algorithmic
assertions are precisely those on the normal forms and compiled
operations, as isolated in \Cref{prop:semantic-readout-separation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Minimal escape supplied by the protocol]
\label{cor:value-transported-minimal-witness}
Within the level-zero monogenic-orbit restriction of
\Cref{cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}, the protocol of
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ} is a minimal witness to what must be
added to the L0 expression picture: it keeps the injective valuation
readout \(c_n\mapsto n\), realizes addition at the ambient composition
level, and realizes multiplication only after passing to the one higher
power-operator readout.  Consequently its positive content is the
existence of this two-level semantic readout package, not a new
bounded-window or digit-local Zeckendorf multiplication algorithm.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{prop:fold-confluence}, \(\Val\colon
X_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}\to\NN\) is a bijection, so the normal forms
separate integers.  The theorem realizes addition by
\(c_a\oplus c_b=c_{a+b}\) and the level-zero readout
\(\Phi_0(c_n)=g^{\circ n}\), hence no extra level is needed for
addition.  The same theorem realizes multiplication by
\(c_a\otimes c_b=c_{ab}\) only through the level-one readout
\(\Phi_1(c_n)=I_n\), where composition of the endomorphisms
\(I_a,I_b\) gives \(I_{ab}\).  On the other hand,
\Cref{cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy} says that an injective readout
of positive integers cannot also realize multiplication by the ambient
product on that same single free monogenic orbit.  Thus the theorem
does exactly the forced escape under that restriction: it moves
multiplication off the level-zero orbit while leaving the normal-form
valuation and the addition readout unchanged.  All displayed
operation equations are transported through \(\Val\) and ordinary
integer arithmetic, so the corollary asserts a minimal protocol
witness rather than an intrinsic digit-local arithmetic algorithm.
\end{proof}
