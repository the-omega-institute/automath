\section{\texorpdfstring{\(L0\)}{L0}: expression universality}\label{sec:L0}

We begin with \(L0\), \emph{expression universality}: the
existential assertion that the desired operations have some terms
over one binary primitive, before any computable normal forms or
fiber certificates are required.  Throughout this section
\(\operatorname{Log}\) denotes the principal branch of the complex
logarithm.

\begin{definition}[EML operator]\label{def:eml}
The \emph{Exp-Minus-Log} operation is the partial binary operation
on \(\CC\) with domain \(\CC\times\CC^*\) given by
\[
  \eml \colon \CC\times\CC^*\longrightarrow \CC,
  \qquad
  \eml(x,y)=e^x-\operatorname{Log}y .
\]
\end{definition}

The complex domain is part of the convention.  Constants such as
\(i\), and hence the trigonometric functions, are obtained from the
usual exponential identities.  All branch choices below are made in
this principal-branch sense.  Thus an EML term is interpreted only at
points where every second argument of \(\eml\) is nonzero and the
principal-branch identities used in its construction are valid.  We
do not assert a branch-independent calculus of arbitrary elementary
expressions.

\begin{proposition}[Odrzywo{\l}ek]
\label{thm:eml-universality}
Let \(\mathcal B_{36}\) be Odrzywo{\l}ek's scientific-calculator
basis.  In the notation of the cited Table~1 this is the following
concrete list:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C&=\{\pi,e,i,-1,1,2,x,y\},\\
\mathcal U&=\{\exp,\ln,\operatorname{inv},\operatorname{half},
  \operatorname{minus},\sqrt{\phantom{x}},\operatorname{sqr},\sigma,\\
&\hspace{2.7em}
  \sin,\cos,\tan,\arcsin,\arccos,\arctan,
  \sinh,\cosh,\tanh,\\
&\hspace{2.7em}
  \operatorname{arsinh},\operatorname{arcosh},
  \operatorname{artanh}\},\\
\mathcal O&=\{+,-,\times,\div,\log_x y,x^y,
  \operatorname{avg},\operatorname{hypot}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{inv}(x)=1/x\),
\(\operatorname{half}(x)=x/2\),
\(\operatorname{minus}(x)=-x\),
\(\operatorname{sqr}(x)=x^2\),
\(\sigma(x)=1/(1+e^{-x})\),
\(\operatorname{avg}(x,y)=(x+y)/2\), and
\(\operatorname{hypot}(x,y)=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\).
Then each operation in \(\mathcal B_{36}\) is represented, on its
corresponding principal-branch domain, by a finite expression built
from \(\eml\) and the constant \(1\).  This is an imported background
theorem, not reproved here.  The archival source is
Odrzywo{\l}ek's arXiv:2603.21852v2: Table~1 supplies the displayed
\(36\)-item basis, the ancillary file
\texttt{SupplementaryInformation.pdf} attached to that arXiv version
supplies the EML compiler, verification, and printed representative
witnesses in Part~II, Sections~2.1--2.5, and the accompanying code and data are archived separately on Zenodo as
public provenance, not as a proof dependency in this article
\cite[Table~1 and Supplementary Information, Part~II,
Secs.~2.1--2.5]{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator, Odrzywolek2026ZenodoCode}.
In particular, this proposition is used below only as the L0
expression-universality anchor.  The later Richardson obstruction does
not appeal to the full \(\mathcal B_{36}\) compiler; its separate finite
proof interface is isolated in \Cref{prop:printed-root-no-hidden-oracle}.
The domains imported here are exactly the ordinary domains of these
real calculator functions on the real axis where appropriate
\((\ln,\operatorname{inv},\div,\log_x y,x^y,\sqrt{\phantom{x}},
\arcsin,\arccos,\operatorname{arcosh},\operatorname{artanh})\),
together with the principal complex branches used internally by
\(\operatorname{Log}\); no branch-independent identity is asserted
outside those domains.
In particular, for \(x\) in the right half-plane,
\begin{align}
  e^x &= \eml(x,1), \label{eq:eml-exp}\\
  \operatorname{Log}x
      &= \eml\!\bigl(1,\eml(\eml(1,x),1)\bigr),
         \label{eq:eml-ln}\\
  e   &= \eml(1,1). \label{eq:eml-e}
\end{align}
Away from the principal domain the same formulas have the expected
branch dependence.  For instance, the right side of
\eqref{eq:eml-ln} gives \(-i\pi\) at \(x=-1\), while
\(\operatorname{Log}(-1)=i\pi\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[External compiler isolation]
\label{cor:external-compiler-isolation}
In the proof of the hierarchy theorem, the imported
\(\mathcal B_{36}\) theorem supplies only the positive \(L0\)
existence statement that the listed calculator primitives have pure EML
representatives on their stated principal-branch domains.  It supplies no
hidden proof step for any \(L1\) protocol normalizer, any \(L2\)
finite-fiber certificate, or any Richardson normal-form obstruction.  The
later Richardson argument may use only the internally displayed core EML
witnesses and, for \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative square root,
the separate locked interface or replacement-triple interface stated in
\Cref{thm:richardson-obstruction-interface,thm:separation}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The imported result in \Cref{thm:eml-universality} is a representation
statement: it asserts the existence of finite EML expressions for the
members of \(\mathcal B_{36}\) on specified principal-branch domains.  The
definition of protocol universality in \Cref{def:prot-univ} requires
additional data, namely computable normal forms, decidable equality of
normal forms, compiled operations on those forms, and readout identities.
None of these data is part of the imported \(\mathcal B_{36}\) statement.
The \(L1\) proof is instead the Zeckendorf construction of
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}, and the \(L2\) proof is the finite
fiber construction in \Cref{sec:L2}.

For the Richardson obstruction, the proof in \Cref{sec:strictness} first
locks the finite strings actually used in the translation and then audits
the three non-core semantic witnesses.  The short arithmetic EML macros
are proved internally in \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses} and
\Cref{lem:eml-printed-core}.  The three remaining meanings are not taken
from the full compiler as an oracle; for the printed roots they are exactly
the hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\) of
\Cref{prop:locked-three-witness-interface}, and for a replacement triple
they are exactly the three real-domain identities in
\Cref{prop:three-witness-stability}.  Therefore the external compiler is
isolated to the advertised positive \(L0\) clause.
\end{proof}

This proposition is used here exactly as Odrzywo{\l}ek's external
representation theorem for the stated calculator basis and as the
background L0 example motivating the hierarchy.  The Richardson
strictness proof below does not call the full \(\mathcal B_{36}\)
compiler as an oracle: it uses only the finite witness package isolated
in \Cref{lem:eml-printed-core,lem:eml-ancillary-certificate,cor:eml-witness-locking}.
The present article does not reprove the whole representation theorem;
it studies what additional data are needed to pass from such
existential expression representatives to protocol and certificate
structures.
Thus the positive L0 clause and the later obstruction have different
inputs: full \(\mathcal B_{36}\) universality is imported only as the
external expression-universality example, whereas the Richardson interface argument is confined to the locked subpackage
and, for the printed roots, to the three principal-branch identities for
the printed \(W_{\pi}\), \(W_{\sin}\), and \(W_{\sqrt{}}\) entries stated as
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) and isolated in
\Cref{cor:conditional-three-witness-obstruction};
\Cref{prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface} explains why this
three-witness interface remains an explicit analytic hypothesis rather
than an internally verified consequence of the printed table alone.
In the cited arXiv version \(2603.21852\mathrm{v}2\), posted with its
ancillary Supplementary Information, Table~1 is the
\(36\)-primitive calculator basis: named constants and variables,
standard unary elementary functions, and the standard binary
operations.  Supplementary Information, Part~II, Sections~2.1--2.5,
is the verification interface used here.  Section~2.1 gives the
compiler macros into pure EML trees, Section~2.3 records symbolic
verification under the stated real-domain assumptions, Section~2.4
prints representative compiled witnesses, and Section~2.5 gives the
constructive completeness proof sketch for the Table~1 class.  The
displayed identities above are principal-branch core identities; on the
positive real axis they reduce to the ordinary real exponential and
logarithm.  The later Richardson obstruction uses a different locked
three-witness interface, namely \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative
real square root.

\begin{proposition}[L0 dependency localization]
\label{prop:L0-dependency-localization}
The positive L0 assertion and the later Richardson obstruction have the
following disjoint dependency profiles.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The statement that \((\eml,1)\) is expression-universal for the
  whole basis \(\mathcal B_{36}\) is precisely the imported, versioned
  result of \Cref{thm:eml-universality}; if that external theorem is
  not used, the unconditional L0 content retained in this article is
  only the internal core of \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}.
\item The proof of the Richardson normalizer obstruction never uses a
  representative for any member of \(\mathcal B_{36}\) outside the
  Richardson witness library.  Its unconditional syntactic input is the
  internally printed core and the finite source-level locks cited in
  \Cref{prop:external-versus-locked-package}; its only non-core
  analytic input is either a replacement triple satisfying the three
  displayed identities of \Cref{thm:separation}, or, for the three
  printed roots, the hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\).
\item Consequently, replacing the external \(\mathcal B_{36}\) theorem
  by the smaller internal core weakens only the positive full-library
  L0 example.  It does not change the Zeckendorf L1 construction, the
  finite-fiber L2 construction, the level-zero multiplication
  obstruction, or the conditional Richardson obstruction under the
  stated three-witness interface.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first item is a restatement of the logical source of
\Cref{thm:eml-universality}: the proof in this article does not derive
the Table~1 representatives for all of \(\mathcal B_{36}\), whereas
\Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses} gives an explicit internal list
of the core operations proved from \(\eml\) and \(1\).  The second item is
checked from the construction used in \Cref{sec:strictness}.  The
Richardson translation is assembled from the core EML identities, the
locked finite strings for the three long witnesses, and Richardson's
undecidability theorem; \Cref{prop:external-versus-locked-package}
records this inspection after the witness lemmas have been stated.  The
normal-form contradiction itself is the abstract transported-normalizer
argument of \Cref{lem:transported-partial-normalizer-obstruction}, so no
operation such as \(\tan\), \(\arccos\), \(\operatorname{hypot}\), or
\(\log_x y\) from the remainder of \(\mathcal B_{36}\) can enter the
proof.  The third item follows formally: the Zeckendorf protocol and the
finite-fiber certificates are proved in \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}
and \Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}, and the free-monogenic obstruction
is proved in \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}; none of
those statements has \Cref{thm:eml-universality} as a hypothesis.  The
Richardson add-on keeps exactly the replacement-triple or
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) interface stated in \Cref{thm:separation}.
\end{proof}

The part of the EML calculus needed as an internal fallback is much
smaller than the imported \(36\)-primitive theorem.  The following
proposition records the self-contained principal-branch witnesses that
are proved in this article and used later in the finite Richardson
subpackage.

\begin{proposition}[Internal core EML witnesses]
\label{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}
In the principal-branch EML semantics of \Cref{def:eml}, the pure EML
grammar generated by \(1\), variables, and \(\eml\) represents the
following functions on the indicated real domains:
\[
  e^x,
  \qquad \log x\ (x>0),
  \qquad 0,
  \qquad -x,
  \qquad x+y,
  \qquad x-y,
  \qquad xy,
  \qquad \log 2 .
\]
Consequently, even if the external \(\mathcal B_{36}\) assertion is
viewed only as background, the manuscript contains a nonempty internal
L0 core sufficient for the algebraic constructors used in the later
Richardson witness library, except for the three explicitly isolated
non-core witnesses \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative square root.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The exponential and logarithm are the displayed identities
\eqref{eq:eml-exp} and \eqref{eq:eml-ln}; on the positive real axis the
principal logarithm is the ordinary real logarithm.  The constant
\(e=\eml(1,1)\) is \eqref{eq:eml-e}.  Hence
\[
  0=\eml(1,\eml(1,1))=e-\operatorname{Log}e.
\]
The explicit unconditional macros \(E,L,D,P,N,A,S\) are printed and
proved in \Cref{lem:eml-printed-core}.  That lemma expands them to pure
EML trees and verifies, from the defining principal-branch semantics,
the all-real identities for negation, addition, and subtraction.  Applying
the addition witness to \(1\) and \(1\) gives the represented constant
\(2\), and then applying the positive-real logarithm witness gives
\(\log 2\).

It remains only to record multiplication without importing any non-core
root.  Put
\[
  T(a,b)=E(A(L(a),L(b)))\qquad(a>0,b>0),
\]
so \(T(a,b)=ab\) for positive real inputs by the already proved
exponential, logarithm, and addition witnesses.  Also put
\(Q(u)=E(u)>0\) and \(P(u)=S(E(u),u)>0\).  Since
\(u=Q(u)-P(u)\), the pure EML macro
\[
  M(u,v)=A\Bigl(S\bigl(T(Q(u),Q(v)),
        A(T(Q(u),P(v)),T(P(u),Q(v)))\bigr),
        T(P(u),P(v))\Bigr)
\]
has value
\((Q(u)-P(u))(Q(v)-P(v))=uv\) for all real \(u,v\).  All inputs to
\(T\) in this formula are positive by construction, so the principal
logarithm is used only on its stated real domain.  Thus the listed core
functions are proved internally and do not use the three non-core roots
\(W_{\pi}\), \(W_{\sin}\), and \(W_{\sqrt{}}\).  The full
\(\mathcal B_{36}\) clause remains the imported background anchor of
\Cref{thm:eml-universality}.
\end{proof}

The induced syntax has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eml-grammar}
  S\longrightarrow 1 \mid x_i \mid \eml(S,S),
\end{equation}
where the \(x_i\) are input variables.

\begin{definition}[Expression universality]\label{def:expr-univ}
Let \(\mathcal F\) be a family of functions.  Let
\(\sigma\colon\dom(\sigma)\subseteq D^2\to D\) be a partial binary
operation on a set \(D\), and let \(c_0\in D\).  Terms in the
grammar
\[
  S\longrightarrow c_0 \mid x_i \mid \sigma(S,S).
\]
are interpreted on their admissible domains.  We say that the pair
\((\sigma,c_0)\) is \emph{expression-universal for
\(\mathcal F\)} if, for every \(f\in\mathcal F\), some such term
agrees with \(f\) on the specified domain of \(f\).
\end{definition}

The definition is deliberately existential.  It asserts that a term
exists, but does not select one.  The grammar
\eqref{eq:eml-grammar} therefore gives no word problem, no canonical
representative, and no internal operation on semantic equivalence
classes.  For example, the identity function can be written as
\[
  x=\eml(\operatorname{Log}x,\eml(1,x))
\]
on the corresponding principal domain.  Substituting the EML term
for \(\operatorname{Log}x\) from \eqref{eq:eml-ln} gives a tree in
the grammar.  Further representatives are obtained by inserting
cancelling subexpressions.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:eml-variants}
Odrzywo{\l}ek also gives closely related one-operation
representations, for instance
\(\mathrm{edl}(x,y)=e^x/\ln y\) paired with \(e\), and the negated
form \(-\eml(y,x)=\ln x-e^y\) paired with \(-\infty\)
\cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator}.  The phenomenon is a feature of the
\(\exp\)--\(\log\) pair rather than of one isolated formula.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:no-canon}
Expression universality supplies no analytic normalization.  This is
not merely a missing step in the construction: by
\Cref{thm:separation}, conditional on \((H_{\mathcal W})\), the EML
representatives obtained from Richardson's elementary fragment cannot
carry a computable canonical form for their transported real semantics.
\end{remark}
