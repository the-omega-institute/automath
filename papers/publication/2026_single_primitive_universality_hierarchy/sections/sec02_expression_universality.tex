\section{\texorpdfstring{\(L0\)}{L0}: expression universality}\label{sec:L0}

This section fixes the single primitive and the precise boundary of the imported
expression theorem.  Throughout, \(\operatorname{Log}\) is the principal complex
logarithm.

\begin{definition}[EML operator]\label{def:eml}
The Exp-Minus-Log operation is the partial binary operation
\[
  \eml\colon\CC\times\CC^*\to\CC,
  \qquad \eml(x,y)=e^x-\operatorname{Log}y .
\]
An EML term is interpreted only where every second argument is nonzero and the
principal-branch expressions used in the term are defined.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[External public EML representation anchor]
\label{thm:eml-universality}
Odrzywo{\l}ek's public arXiv v2 record
\cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator} asserts that every primitive in the
scientific-calculator basis \(\mathcal B_{36}\) displayed there has a finite
representative over \((\eml,1)\) on its stated principal-branch domain.  This
proposition is only the article's external public anchor for that assertion: the
thirty-six representatives and their domain checks are not printed or verified
inside this paper.  The anchor is used here only as an \(L0\)
representative-existence theorem; it supplies no normal form, no equality
algorithm for EML terms, no semantic compiler or normalizer, and no Richardson
witness identity except through the separate interface of \Cref{thm:separation}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is a citation step, not a term-by-term derivation.  The public anchor is the
versioned record arXiv:2603.21852v2, DOI
\texttt{10.48550/arXiv.2603.21852}.  The ancillary file named in \Cref{ass:HW}
is used only for the optional printed-string hypothesis; the main theorem chain
treats the full \(\mathcal B_{36}\) result as the single external existence
assertion stated here.  All self-contained EML calculations used later are the
core calculations isolated in \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}, not the
full \(\mathcal B_{36}\) table.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stable external-anchor criterion for \texorpdfstring{\(L0\)}{L0}]
\label{thm:L0-external-anchor-criterion}
\label{thm:B36-external-anchor-acceptability}
Let \(\mathbf A\) be the assertion that each primitive in the displayed
scientific-calculator basis \(\mathcal B_{36}\) has a finite representative over
\((\eml,1)\) on its stated principal-branch domain.  The main theorem chain of
this article retains the external public theorem \(\mathbf A\) as its \(L0\)
anchor.  Moreover, the later strictness chain is valid under either of the
following two inputs:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the external public theorem \(\mathbf A\), cited as
  \Cref{thm:eml-universality}; or
\item the weaker internal replacement obtained by deleting the global
  \(\mathcal B_{36}\) representative-existence claim and retaining only the
  printed core identities of \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}, in which
  case every headline obstruction and every \(L1\)/\(L2\) construction remains
  unchanged, while the article no longer asserts expression universality for the
  whole calculator basis.
\end{enumerate}
In neither case does \(\mathbf A\) supply a computable normalizer for EML terms,
decidable semantic equality of EML terms, or a Richardson witness triple for
\(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and square root on \(\RR_{\ge0}\).  Thus accepting the
arXiv/ancillary \(L0\) import is exactly the decision to keep the representative
existence clause for \(\mathcal B_{36}\); it is not a hidden premise for the
Richardson, one-orbit, or finite-fibre strictness clauses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The only place where the full calculator-basis assertion enters the manuscript
is \Cref{thm:eml-universality}.  Its statement and proof import precisely finite
representative existence for the listed basis primitives.  The proof does not
claim a canonical representative, an equality algorithm, or the three analytic
identities needed for Richardson.  Therefore retaining \(\mathbf A\) proves
exactly the \(L0\) expression-existence clause and nothing stronger.

For the replacement branch, remove every use of the global \(\mathcal B_{36}\)
existence assertion.  The remaining EML calculations are the printed core
identities collected in \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}.  The Zeckendorf
protocol is proved internally in \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}; the
one-orbit and finite-rank product obstructions are proved in
\Cref{thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy,thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification,thm:finite-rank-product-readout-lower-bound};
and the finite-fibre cover separation is proved in
\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}.  None of these theorems has
\Cref{thm:eml-universality} as a hypothesis.  The Richardson branch also does
not use the global \(\mathcal B_{36}\) table: by \Cref{thm:separation} it uses a
specified finite triple only after the three real-domain identities for
\(\pi\), sine, and nonnegative square root have been proved.  Hence deleting the
global import deletes only the full-basis \(L0\) existence clause, not the
strictness clauses.

Finally, suppose one tried to use \(\mathbf A\) to obtain a normalizer, semantic
equality procedure, or Richardson triple.  Each of those conclusions is strictly
stronger than finite existence of representatives: a normalizer chooses and
compares representatives; a semantic equality procedure decides equality of
arbitrary EML terms; and a Richardson triple proves the three displayed
real-domain identities.  Since none of those data is part of \(\mathbf A\), such
an argument adds an extra hypothesis.  This proves the exact criterion and the
claimed independence of the later theorem chain from the external \(L0\) import.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact scope of the imported \texorpdfstring{\(\mathcal B_{36}\)}{B36} theorem]
\label{thm:B36-import-exact-scope}
\label{thm:no-self-contained-B36-representation-proof}
Within this article the assertion that the whole scientific-calculator basis
\(\mathcal B_{36}\) has finite representatives over \((\eml,1)\) is precisely the
external public anchor \Cref{thm:eml-universality}.  The self-contained part of
the paper proves only the printed core witnesses of
\Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}.  Hence every theorem in the main chain
remains valid if the phrase ``the versioned public record asserts finite representatives for the displayed basis''
is replaced by the single hypothesis ``finite representatives for the displayed
basis exist on their stated principal-branch domains.''  No later theorem may use
the full \(\mathcal B_{36}\) statement as though a self-contained proof of all
thirty-six representatives and their domain checks had been reproduced here, or
as a hidden compiler, normalizer, or verified Richardson package.  In particular,
the import cannot instantiate the Richardson reduction for \(\pi\), sine, and
nonnegative square root: that reduction is available only through the verified
replacement-triple interface of \Cref{thm:separation}, or through the optional
printed-string hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  The last exclusion is the
proof-graph cut of
\Cref{thm:richardson-proof-graph-no-hidden-witness,thm:no-B36-Richardson-instantiation}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{thm:eml-universality} states the full \(\mathcal B_{36}\) result by citing
the versioned public record.  The proof of that proposition explicitly says that
the result is imported, not rederived.  The only EML representatives whose
real-domain identities are calculated inside this manuscript are the finite
macros in \Cref{lem:eml-printed-core,lem:eml-real-witness-library}; these are
collected in \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}.  Inspecting the dependency
cut in \Cref{prop:eml-import-dependency-cut}, the positive protocol theorem, the
certificate theorem, and the Richardson obstruction cite only this internal core
or the separate verified-triple interface, and therefore do not require a
self-contained derivation of every element of \(\mathcal B_{36}\).  Thus the full
calculator-basis statement has exactly the external-anchor scope asserted above:
representative existence on the stated domains.  If one replaces the cited
theorem by any other theorem with that same existence content, no proof in
Sections~\ref{sec:L1}--\ref{sec:strictness} changes.  In particular,
\Cref{thm:richardson-proof-graph-no-hidden-witness,thm:no-core-only-richardson-translation,thm:no-B36-Richardson-instantiation}
shows that the imported basis cannot be used to bypass the verified-triple
hypotheses for \(\pi\), sine, and nonnegative square root.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Import-normal-form separation]
\label{thm:B36-import-normal-form-separation}
The \(\mathcal B_{36}\) import used in this article is logically orthogonal to
every normal-form or Richardson-specialization conclusion in the main theorem
chain.  More precisely, replace \Cref{thm:eml-universality} by any statement of
the same form:
\[
  \text{each displayed basis primitive has some finite principal-branch EML
  representative on its stated domain.}
\]
Then \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy,thm:separation,thm:level-zero-obstruction,thm:finite-state-strictness}
and the corollaries depending on them are unchanged, except for the existence
assertion at level \(L0\).  Conversely, adding the \(\mathcal B_{36}\) import
alone cannot supply a computable equality normalizer, cannot identify a semantic
compiler, and cannot instantiate the Richardson triple for \(\pi\), sine on
\(\RR\), and \(\sqrt{\ }\) on \(\RR_{\ge0}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof of \Cref{thm:B36-import-exact-scope} identifies the only conclusion
drawn from \Cref{thm:eml-universality}: representative existence for the
displayed basis primitives.  The positive protocol theorem is proved in
\Cref{sec:L1} from Zeckendorf normal forms and value transport through
\(\NN\); it does not call the imported representatives.  The Richardson theorem
\Cref{thm:separation} calls instead the internal core witnesses of
\Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses} and a separately supplied verified triple
for \(\pi\), sine, and nonnegative square root.  The free-monogenic-orbit
obstruction \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction} is an exponent-skeleton statement,
and \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness} is a finite-fibre cover budget statement;
neither contains an EML representative as a hypothesis.

Thus any statement with the same representative-existence content can replace
the cited \(\mathcal B_{36}\) anchor without changing the proof of the normalizer,
protocol, one-orbit, or cover-budget clauses.  The converse is a dependency-cut
argument: a normalizer, compiler, or Richardson specialization requires extra
data not present in a bare representative-existence assertion.  In particular,
the verified Richardson input is the three real-domain identities stated in
\Cref{thm:separation}; by \Cref{thm:B36-import-exact-scope} those identities are
not among the conclusions imported from \Cref{thm:eml-universality}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[External compiler isolation]
\label{cor:external-compiler-isolation}

normalizer, an \(L2\) certificate theorem, a semantic compiler into decidable

\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}

\(L1\) normalizer is constructed in \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}; the
\(L2\) certificates are finite-fibre data from \Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ};
and the Richardson obstruction is \Cref{thm:separation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Replacement-invariant \texorpdfstring{\(L0\)}{L0} cut]
\label{thm:L0-import-replacement-invariance}
\label{thm:B36-import-proof-graph-cut}
Let \(\mathbf B\) be any external theorem whose only asserted content for this
article is finite representative existence for the displayed calculator basis on
the stated principal-branch domains.  If \Cref{thm:eml-universality} is replaced
everywhere in the proof of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} by \(\mathbf B\), then every
positive \(L1\) claim, every finite-fibre \(L2\) certificate claim, the one-orbit
obstruction, and the Richardson verified-triple obstruction remain unchanged.
Conversely, any use of the imported \(\mathcal B_{36}\) record to prove equality
of arbitrary EML terms, to choose normal forms, or to verify \(\pi\), sine, or
nonnegative square root for Richardson is an additional hypothesis not present in
this paper.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The forward assertion is a proof-graph check.  The \(L1\) protocol is constructed
from Zeckendorf normal forms and value transport in
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}; the one-orbit obstruction uses only the free
monogenic orbit classification in \Cref{thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy};
the fixed-resolution finite-fibre certificates are
\Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}; and the finite-state transfer obstruction is
the cover construction of \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}.  None of those
statements has the full calculator-basis import as a hypothesis.  The
Richardson part enters the main hierarchy through \Cref{thm:separation}, whose
hypotheses are a finite triple \((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) and the three
proved real-domain identities for \(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and nonnegative square
root on \(\RR_{\ge0}\), not the existence of all \(\mathcal B_{36}\)
representatives.  Thus replacing \Cref{thm:eml-universality} by any theorem with
the same representative-existence content leaves those proofs verbatim.

For the converse, equality of arbitrary EML terms, canonical choice of normal
forms, and semantic verification of the three Richardson witnesses are not part
of \Cref{def:expr-univ} or \Cref{thm:eml-universality}.  They are explicitly
excluded by \Cref{thm:B36-import-exact-scope,cor:external-compiler-isolation}.
Using the public \(\mathcal B_{36}\) record for any of these stronger tasks
therefore adds information beyond the imported \(L0\) theorem.  Such information
is not available in the main proof chain, so the stated cut is exact.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact load-bearing \texorpdfstring{\(L0\)}{L0} and Richardson cut]
\label{thm:L0-richardson-exact-load-bearing-cut}
The Proc. AMS theorem chain uses the expression-universality input and the
Richardson input through disjoint interfaces.  Its \(L0\) interface is exactly
one of the following two choices:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the cited Odrzywo{\l}ek representative-existence theorem for the displayed
  \(\mathcal B_{36}\) basis, with no term-by-term verification reproduced here;
  or
\item the smaller self-contained core witness package of
  \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}, in which case the global
  \(\mathcal B_{36}\) expression-universality sentence is deleted but the later
  strictness theorems are unchanged.
\end{enumerate}
Its Richardson interface is exactly the verified-triple hypothesis of
\Cref{thm:separation}: a specified finite pure EML triple together with proofs of
the three real-domain identities for \(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and
\(t\mapsto\sqrt t\) on \(\RR_{\ge0}\).  Consequently the cited
\(\mathcal B_{36}\) import, the printed syntax of
\((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\), and the optional ancillary-name lock are not
load-bearing substitutes for those identities.  The only printed-string
Richardson instance for \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) in this article is therefore
the conditional specialization obtained after adding \((H_{\mathcal W})\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two possible \(L0\) inputs are precisely the alternatives isolated in
\Cref{thm:L0-external-anchor-criterion}.  The first alternative is the cited
representative-existence proposition \Cref{thm:eml-universality}; by
\Cref{thm:B36-import-exact-scope} and \Cref{thm:B36-import-normal-form-separation}
it imports only existence of representatives on the stated principal-branch
domains, not a normalizer, equality procedure, compiler, or Richardson witness.
The second alternative is the replacement of the global calculator-basis claim by
the finite internal package of \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses};
\Cref{thm:B36-self-contained-replacement-core} proves that every later
Richardson, Zeckendorf, one-orbit, and finite-cover argument still has all of its
actual hypotheses under that replacement.

It remains to identify the Richardson input.  In \Cref{thm:separation} and
\Cref{thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface} the hypotheses are not the
existence of a large calculator-basis representation theorem and not the presence
of three names.  They are the displayed finite pure EML triple
\((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) plus proved principal-branch identities

\(\mathsf R(t)=\sqrt t\) on \(\RR_{\ge0}\).  The proof of

exclude deriving them from the printed names alone.  Finally,
\Cref{thm:printed-root-richardson-conditional-clause} shows that adding

triple and gives only the corresponding conditional specialization.  These
statements prove both the disjointness of the two interfaces and the asserted
load-bearing cut used by \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Expression universality]\label{def:expr-univ}
A system is \(L0\)-expression-universal for a signature if every primitive
operation in the signature has at least one term representative over the chosen
primitive operations on its stated domain.  No normal form, equality procedure,
or fibre certificate is part of the definition.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:eml-variants}
All EML claims in this article use the principal-branch convention of
\Cref{def:eml}; no branch-free complex calculus is asserted.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:no-canon}
Expression universality alone does not choose canonical representatives.  This
is the gap separated by the Richardson obstruction in \Cref{thm:separation}.
\end{remark}
