\section{\texorpdfstring{\(L2\)}{L2}: certificate universality by finite certificates}\label{sec:L2}

An \(L1\) protocol supplies canonical forms.  At level \(L2\),
\emph{certificate universality}, one also records computable finite
invariants of the normalization fibers.  For the Zeckendorf protocol
these are the fibers of \(\Fold_m\colon\Omega_m\to X_m\).
This section supplies the \(L2\) clause of the main hierarchy theorem:
it records what finite-fiber certificates give automatically and
isolates why such certificates are a genuine third level rather than a
finite-state transfer theorem in disguise.
The finite-fiber clauses below are intentionally generic finite-atomic
facts.  The separation from finite-state certificate data is carried by
the one-spike growth obstruction in \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness},
not by any special Zeckendorf strengthening of Hankel positivity or
Prony reconstruction.

\subsection{Collision moments}
\label{ssec:moments}

For \(x\in X_m\), write
\[
  d_m(x)=|\Fold_m^{-1}(x)|.
\]
Since every legal word folds to itself, \(\Fold_m\) is onto and
\(d_m(x)\ge1\).
The detailed asymptotic thermodynamics of these fiber multiplicities is
not used here.  The present article uses only the fixed-resolution
finite lists and the elementary finite-atomic moment facts defined
below.

\begin{definition}[Collision moment]\label{def:collision-moment}
For \(q\ge0\) and \(m\ge1\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sq}
  S_q(m)=\sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^q .
\end{equation}
Thus \(S_q(m)\) is the number of \(q\)-tuples
\((\omega^{(1)},\ldots,\omega^{(q)})\in\Omega_m^q\) whose entries
have the same fold image, with the convention
\(S_0(m)=|X_m|\).
\end{definition}

In particular \(S_0(m)=F_{m+2}\) and \(S_1(m)=2^m\).

\subsection{Hankel positivity}
\label{ssec:hankel}

\begin{proposition}[Hankel positivity and log-convexity]
\label{thm:hankel-psd}
For fixed \(m\ge1\), the Hankel matrix
\[
  H_r^{(m)}=\bigl[S_{i+j}(m)\bigr]_{0\le i,j\le r}
\]
is positive semidefinite for every \(r\ge0\).  Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item all principal minors of \(H_r^{(m)}\) are nonnegative;
\item \(S_q(m)^2\le S_{q-1}(m)S_{q+1}(m)\) for \(q\ge1\);
\item the ratios \(S_{q+1}(m)/S_q(m)\) are nondecreasing in \(q\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
  \nu_m=\sum_{x\in X_m}\delta_{d_m(x)} .
\]
Then \(S_q(m)=\int t^q\,d\nu_m(t)\).  For
\(c_0,\ldots,c_r\in\RR\),
\[
  \sum_{i,j=0}^r c_i c_j S_{i+j}(m)
  =\sum_{x\in X_m}
    \Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^r c_i d_m(x)^i\Bigr)^2\ge0.
\]
This is positive semidefiniteness, the finite case of the Hamburger
moment criterion \cite{Hamburger1920}.  The \(2\times2\) principal
minors give log-convexity, and division by
\(S_q(m)S_{q-1}(m)>0\) gives the monotonicity of the consecutive
ratios.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite spectral reconstruction}
\label{ssec:prony}

\begin{proposition}[Prony--Hankel reconstruction]
\label{thm:prony}
Let \(a_1<\cdots<a_{r_m}\) be the distinct values among the fiber
sizes \(d_m(x)\), and put
\[
  n_i=|\{x\in X_m:d_m(x)=a_i\}|.
\]
Let \(N_m=|X_m|\).  The fiber-size spectrum is determined uniquely
by the finite list
\[
  S_0(m),\ldots,S_{2N_m-1}(m).
\]
More sharply, once \(r_m\) is known, it is determined by
\[
  S_0(m),\ldots,S_{2r_m-1}(m).
\]
More precisely:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(r_m\) is the Hankel rank of the infinite matrix
  \([S_{i+j}(m)]_{i,j\ge0}\), and the preceding finite list with
  \(N_m=|X_m|\) determines this rank, including the full-rank case
  \(r_m=N_m\);
\item the moment sequence satisfies a unique recurrence of minimum
  order \(r_m\);
\item the recurrence polynomial has roots \(a_1,\ldots,a_{r_m}\),
  and the multiplicities \(n_i\) are then recovered from a
  Vandermonde system.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The moments have the representation
\[
  S_q(m)=\sum_{i=1}^{r_m} n_i a_i^q .
\]
The functions \(q\mapsto a_i^q\) are linearly independent, since
the \(a_i\) are distinct.  Moreover, the finite Hankel block
\([S_{i+j}(m)]_{0\le i,j<r_m}\) is positive definite: for a
nonzero polynomial \(Q\) of degree \(<r_m\),
\(\sum_i n_i Q(a_i)^2>0\).  Hence the infinite Hankel matrix has
rank \(r_m\).  Since the multiplicities \(n_i\) are positive
integers, \(r_m\le N_m\).  The available moment segment
\(S_0(m),\ldots,S_{2N_m-1}(m)\) determines every leading Hankel
block \([S_{i+j}(m)]_{0\le i,j\le s}\) for \(0\le s<N_m\), because
the largest index in that block is \(2s\le2N_m-2\).  These leading
blocks are positive definite for \(s<r_m\) and singular for
\(s\ge r_m\).  Therefore the finite moments determine \(r_m\): it
is the least \(s<N_m\) for which
\(\det[S_{i+j}(m)]_{0\le i,j\le s}=0\), while the boundary case
\(r_m=N_m\) is exactly the case in which no singular leading block
appears before size \(N_m\).
Let
\[
  P(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{r_m}(z-a_i)
      =z^{r_m}+p_{r_m-1}z^{r_m-1}+\cdots+p_0 .
\]
Since \(P(a_i)=0\), the moments satisfy
\[
  S_{q+r_m}(m)+p_{r_m-1}S_{q+r_m-1}(m)
  +\cdots+p_0S_q(m)=0\qquad(q\ge0).
\]
Minimality follows from the rank statement.  Conversely, the
invertible \(r_m\times r_m\) Hankel block determines the
coefficients \(p_0,\ldots,p_{r_m-1}\) from the square Hankel system
using \(S_0(m),\ldots,S_{2r_m-1}(m)\); its characteristic
polynomial recovers the support \(\{a_i\}\).  Finally,
\[
  S_q(m)=\sum_i n_i a_i^q,\qquad 0\le q<r_m,
\]
  is a nonsingular Vandermonde system for the \(n_i\).  This is the
  elementary finite Hankel recurrence proof of the reconstruction; it is
  the finite-support special case of the usual Prony method, for which
  \cite{BatenkovYomdin2013} is only background.
\end{proof}

Thus the segment through \(S_{2N_m-1}(m)\) certifies the fiber-size
spectrum at resolution \(m\).  Once the Hankel rank has been
identified, the shorter segment through \(S_{2r_m-1}(m)\) suffices.

\subsection{Finite-state compression}
\label{ssec:kernel}

\begin{proposition}[Symmetric-tensor transfer compression]
\label{thm:star-kernel}
Let \(\{B_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}\subset
M_k(\RR_{\ge0})\) be a finite family and, for \(q\ge1\), set
\[
  A^{(q)}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}B_\alpha^{\otimes(q-1)}
\]
on \(V^{\otimes(q-1)}\), where \(V=\RR^k\).  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(A^{(q)}\) commutes with the action of
  \(\mathfrak S_{q-1}\) by permutation of tensor factors;
\item \(\rho(A^{(q)})=\rho(A^{(q)}|_{\Sym^{q-1}(V)})\);
\item \(\dim\Sym^{q-1}(V)=\binom{k+q-2}{q-1}\), which is
  polynomial in \(q\) for fixed \(k\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, if a moment sequence has the transfer form
\[
  S_q(m)=\langle u_q,(A^{(q)})^m v_q\rangle
\]
with \(u_q,v_q\in\Sym^{q-1}(V)\), then it can be computed on the
symmetric subspace.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every permutation operator \(P_\pi\) and every matrix \(B\),
\[
  P_\pi B^{\otimes(q-1)}=B^{\otimes(q-1)}P_\pi ;
\]
after summing over \(\alpha\), this proves the first assertion.
Since \(A^{(q)}\) is nonnegative, Perron--Frobenius theory gives a
nonzero nonnegative eigenvector \(v\) for its spectral radius
\cite{BermanPlemmons1994}.  The vector
\(\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_{q-1}}P_\pi v\) is nonzero because it is a
sum of nonnegative vectors including the nonzero vector \(v\).  It
is symmetric and still an eigenvector with eigenvalue
\(\rho(A^{(q)})\), proving the second assertion.  The dimension
formula is the usual count of
monomials of degree \(q-1\) in \(k\) variables.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Effective finite resolution data]
\label{def:effective-resolution-data}
Finite resolution data for a protocol are called \emph{effective} if
they consist of finite raw-code sets \(C_m\), finite normal-form sets
\(N_m\), and computable surjections
\[
  \mathrm{NF}_m\colon C_m\longrightarrow N_m\qquad(m\ge1),
\]
together with uniform algorithms which, on input \(m\), output
finite lists without repetition containing exactly the elements of
\(C_m\) and \(N_m\).  In particular the cardinalities
\(|C_m|\) and \(|N_m|\) are computable from the resolution data.
All sums, fibers, searches, and moment segments at resolution \(m\)
below are interpreted as bounded computations over these finite
lists.  Thus a bound ``from the finite resolution data'' means a
computable integer obtained uniformly from \(m\), the two lists, and
their cardinalities.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Certificate universality]
\label{def:cert-univ}
A protocol-universal system equipped with effective finite resolution
data is \emph{certificate-universal in the finite-fiber sense} if,
for
\[
  d_m(n)=|\mathrm{NF}_m^{-1}(n)|,\qquad n\in N_m,
\]
the normalization fibers carry the computable moment sequences
\[
  S_q^{(m)}=\sum_{n\in N_m}d_m(n)^q
\]
such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for each \(m\), the Hankel matrices
  \([S_{i+j}^{(m)}]_{i,j}\) are positive semidefinite;
\item for each \(m\), a finite initial segment of the moments,
  with a computable bound from the finite resolution data,
  determines the full fiber-multiplicity spectrum.
\end{enumerate}
It is \emph{finite-state certificate-universal} if, in addition,
there is a polynomial \(P\) such that, for each \(q\ge1\), the
sequence in the resolution parameter has a transfer-matrix
representation over \(\CC\), equipped with its usual absolute value
and the induced finite-dimensional vector norms,
\[
  S_q^{(m)}=u_q^{\mathsf T}A_q^m v_q\qquad(m\ge1),
\]
where \(A_q\in M_{d_q}(\CC)\),
\(u_q,v_q\in\CC^{d_q}\), and \(d_q\le P(q)\).  Equivalently, real or
rational transfer data are allowed through their standard embeddings
in \(\CC\); no non-Archimedean, order-valued, or varying coefficient
system is included in this finite-state clause.  This is the only
consequence of finite-state structure used below: for each fixed
\(q\), the \(m\)-dependence is represented by one fixed finite matrix
over a fixed normed finite-dimensional complex vector space.  This
definition is not asserted to hold for the Zeckendorf fold fibers in
\Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}; the only Zeckendorf certificate proved
there is the finite-fiber one.  The tensor compression result
\Cref{thm:star-kernel} is supporting infrastructure for one common form
of such additional transfer data and is not a source of transfer data
for the folds.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-state coefficient budget]
\label{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget}
If a sequence \((S^{(m)})_{m\ge1}\) has a representation
\[
  S^{(m)}=u^{\mathsf T}A^m v
\]
with \(A\in M_d(\CC)\) and \(u,v\in\CC^d\), then there are constants
\(C,R>0\), depending only on \(A,u,v\), such that
\[
  |S^{(m)}|\le C R^m\qquad(m\ge1).
\]
Consequently every fixed-\(q\) finite-state certificate moment in the
sense of \Cref{def:cert-univ} has at most exponential growth in the
resolution parameter.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose any norm on \(\CC^d\) and its induced operator norm on
\(M_d(\CC)\).  Since all norms on a finite-dimensional complex vector
space are finite on fixed vectors, Hölder's inequality for the dual
norm gives
\[
  |u^{\mathsf T}A^m v|
  \le \|u\|_*\,\|A^m v\|
  \le \|u\|_*\,\|A\|^m\,\|v\| .
\]
With \(R=\max\{1,\|A\|\}\) and
\(C=\max\{1,\|u\|_*\|v\|\}\), this is the desired bound for every
\(m\ge1\).  Applying the argument to the fixed matrix
\(A_q\) and vectors \(u_q,v_q\) in \Cref{def:cert-univ} proves the
certificate-moment assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Generic finite-fiber Hankel positivity]
\label{rem:hankel-psd-generic}
Let
\[
  \mathrm{NF}_m\colon C_m\longrightarrow N_m
\]
be effective finite resolution data, and put
\[
  d_m(n)=|\mathrm{NF}_m^{-1}(n)|,
  \qquad
  S_q^{(m)}=\sum_{n\in N_m}d_m(n)^q .
\]
For every fixed resolution \(m\) and every \(r\ge0\), the finite
Hankel block
\[
  H_r^{(m)}=\bigl[S_{i+j}^{(m)}\bigr]_{0\le i,j\le r}
\]
is positive semidefinite.  More explicitly, if
\(V_m=(d_m(n)^i)_{n\in N_m,\,0\le i\le r}\), then
\[
  H_r^{(m)}=V_m^{\mathsf T}V_m .
\]
Thus Hankel positivity is a generic finite-fiber consequence of the
listed fibers; it is not a finite-state transfer assertion in the
resolution parameter.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The entries of \(V_m^{\mathsf T}V_m\) are
\[
  (V_m^{\mathsf T}V_m)_{ij}
  =\sum_{n\in N_m}d_m(n)^i d_m(n)^j
  =\sum_{n\in N_m}d_m(n)^{i+j}
  =S_{i+j}^{(m)},
\]
so \(H_r^{(m)}=V_m^{\mathsf T}V_m\).  Hence, for every vector
\(c=(c_0,\ldots,c_r)\),
\[
  c^{\mathsf T}H_r^{(m)}c
  =\sum_{n\in N_m}\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^r c_i d_m(n)^i\Bigr)^2\ge0 .
\]
The effective lists for \(C_m\), \(N_m\), and \(\mathrm{NF}_m\)
make all fiber sizes and the displayed Gram matrix computable by a
bounded finite enumeration at the fixed resolution \(m\).  No matrix
identity relating different values of \(m\) has been constructed;
therefore the statement supplies only the finite-fiber Hankel clause
used in \Cref{prop:finite-fiber-certificate-taxonomy} and not the
additional finite-state clause of \Cref{def:cert-univ}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-atomic certificate taxonomy]
\label{prop:finite-fiber-certificate-taxonomy}
Let
\[
  \mathrm{NF}_m\colon C_m\longrightarrow N_m
\]
be effective finite resolution data, and set
\[
  d_m(n)=|\mathrm{NF}_m^{-1}(n)|,
  \qquad
  S_q^{(m)}=\sum_{n\in N_m}d_m(n)^q .
\]
Then the finite-fiber certificate clauses in \Cref{def:cert-univ} are
automatic finite-atomic consequences of the listed fibers.  More
precisely, the Hankel matrices \([S_{i+j}^{(m)}]_{i,j}\) are positive
semidefinite, and the finite segment
\[
  S_0^{(m)},\ldots,S_{2|N_m|-1}^{(m)}
\]
determines the multiset \(\{d_m(n):n\in N_m\}\), with multiplicities.
No finite-state transfer representation in the resolution parameter
follows from these two assertions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(m\).  The finite atomic measure
\[
  \nu_m=\sum_{n\in N_m}\delta_{d_m(n)}
\]
has moments \(S_q^{(m)}\).  Hankel positivity is the Gram
factorization in \Cref{rem:hankel-psd-generic}.  Since \(\nu_m\) has support size at
most \(|N_m|\), the elementary Prony--Hankel argument of
\Cref{thm:prony}, applied to this finite measure, reconstructs the
distinct values among the \(d_m(n)\) and their multiplicities from the
moments through index \(2|N_m|-1\).  The lists in
\Cref{def:effective-resolution-data} make \(|N_m|\), the fibers, and
the required finite moment segment computable by bounded enumeration.
These arguments are internal to a single fixed resolution.  They do not
produce matrices \(A_q\), vectors \(u_q,v_q\), or a uniform identity
\(S_q^{(m)}=u_q^{\mathsf T}A_q^m v_q\) as \(m\) varies; that transfer
identity is precisely the additional finite-state clause in
\Cref{def:cert-univ}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Zeckendorf finite-fiber certificate universality]
\label{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}
Let \(A\) be a nonempty set and let \(g\colon A\to A\) be an
endomorphism of infinite order.  The Zeckendorf protocol of
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}, together with the finite fold
maps \(\Fold_m\colon\Omega_m\to X_m\), the standard effective finite
resolution lists, and collision moments
\[
  S_q(m)=\sum_{x\in X_m}|\Fold_m^{-1}(x)|^q,
\]
is certificate-universal in the finite-fiber sense of
\Cref{def:cert-univ}.  The assertion is the generic finite-fiber
certificate supplied by effective finite lists and the fold fibers; it
is not an additional Zeckendorf arithmetic theorem or a finite-state
transfer law.  Finite-window Zeckendorf thermodynamics and related
asymptotic transfer questions are not used in this finite-fiber
certificate proof.  More precisely, the Hankel positivity clause
is the finite-atomic positivity clause of
\Cref{prop:finite-fiber-certificate-taxonomy}, and the finite spectral
reconstruction clause is the corresponding finite-atomic Prony clause,
with the computable a priori bound
\(2|X_m|-1=2F_{m+2}-1\) on the required final moment index.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The underlying address system is protocol-universal for
\(\{+,\times\}\) by the value-transported protocol
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}.  It remains
to verify the two finite-fiber certificate clauses.
For each \(m\), the lexicographic list of \(\Omega_m=\{0,1\}^m\) is
computable, and filtering it by the local condition
\(c_kc_{k+1}=0\) gives a computable no-repetition list of \(X_m\).
Thus \(|\Omega_m|=2^m\) and \(|X_m|=F_{m+2}\) are computable from
the finite resolution data.  The map \(\Fold_m\) is a computable
surjection by \Cref{prop:fold-confluence}.  Hence the data fit
\Cref{def:effective-resolution-data} with \(C_m=\Omega_m\),
\(N_m=X_m\), and \(\mathrm{NF}_m=\Fold_m\); the fiber sizes
\(d_m(x)=|\Fold_m^{-1}(x)|\) are finite and define the moments in
\Cref{def:collision-moment}.  Applying
\Cref{prop:finite-fiber-certificate-taxonomy} proves that the Hankel
matrices \([S_{i+j}(m)]_{i,j}\) are positive semidefinite for each fixed
resolution and that the finite list
\[
  S_0(m),\ldots,S_{2|X_m|-1}(m)
\]
determines the full fiber-size spectrum at that resolution, and
\(|X_m|=F_{m+2}\) by \Cref{prop:fold-confluence}.  These are exactly
the two finite-fiber clauses in \Cref{def:cert-univ}.  No step of this
verification supplies the extra transfer-matrix data required in the
finite-state clause of that definition.
\end{proof}


\begin{theorem}[Finite-fiber certificates factor through fiber multisets]
\label{thm:L2-fiber-multiset-factorization}
Fix an L1 protocol with effective finite resolution data
\(\mathrm{NF}_m\colon C_m\to N_m\) and finite fibers.  For each fixed
resolution \(m\), the two finite-fiber certificate clauses of
\Cref{def:cert-univ} factor through the single multiset
\[
  \mathcal D_m=\{|\mathrm{NF}_m^{-1}(n)|:n\in N_m\}.
\]
More precisely, any two effective finite resolution data with the same
normal-form set size and the same multiset \(\mathcal D_m\) have identical
collision moments \(S_q^{(m)}\), identical Hankel blocks, and identical
finite Prony reconstruction output at that resolution.  Therefore these
clauses are invariant under relabelling normal forms and under any
computable finite-fiber cover that preserves the fiber-size multiset at the
fixed resolution.  They do not determine, and are not determined by, any
finite-state transfer representation in the resolution parameter.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For fixed \(m\), the moment sequence is
\[
  S_q^{(m)}=\sum_{n\in N_m}|\mathrm{NF}_m^{-1}(n)|^q
           =\sum_{d\in\mathcal D_m}d^q,
\]
where the second sum counts multiplicities in the multiset.  Thus the
entire sequence \((S_q^{(m)})_{q\ge0}\) depends only on \(\mathcal D_m\),
and two data sets with the same multiset have the same moments.  The
Hankel blocks are matrices whose entries are these moments, so they also
agree.  Their positive semidefiniteness is the Gram factorization of
\Cref{rem:hankel-psd-generic} applied to the same list of numbers
\(d\in\mathcal D_m\).

The finite Prony reconstruction in \Cref{thm:prony} is likewise a function
only of the finite moment segment.  Since the segment through
\(S_{2|N_m|-1}^{(m)}\) is identical for the two data sets, the recovered
support values and multiplicities are identical, namely the distinct
values occurring in \(\mathcal D_m\) and their multiplicities.  This proves
the relabelling and fixed-resolution cover invariance.

A finite-state transfer representation is a uniform assertion in the
variable \(m\): it asks for fixed matrices and vectors with
\(S_q^{(m)}=u_q^{\mathsf T}A_q^m v_q\) for all resolutions.  The preceding
argument used only one finite multiset at one value of \(m\), and gives no
relation between \(\mathcal D_m\) and \(\mathcal D_{m+1}\).  Hence the
finite-fiber clauses neither supply nor characterize finite-state transfer;
that extra condition is addressed separately by the growth obstruction in
\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Generic certificate load and strictness load]
\label{cor:L2-load-split}
In \Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}, Hankel positivity and finite
Prony reconstruction use only the finite list of fiber sizes over
\(X_m\).  They are therefore invariant under replacing the Zeckendorf
fold by any computable finite-fiber cover of the same finite normal-form
sets, after replacing the fiber-size list by the covered fiber-size
list.  No finite-state transfer law follows from these clauses; the
load-bearing obstruction to finite-state transfer is exactly the growth
budget comparison of \Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget} and
\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix a resolution \(m\).  The proofs of \Cref{thm:hankel-psd} and
\Cref{thm:prony} use only the finite atomic measure
\(\sum_x\delta_{d_m(x)}\), where the sum ranges over the finite
normal-form set and \(d_m(x)\) is the corresponding fiber size.  If a
computable finite-fiber cover is substituted, the same argument applies
word for word to the finite atomic measure formed from the covered
fiber sizes.  The proof gives positivity of the finite Hankel forms and
finite spectral reconstruction from finitely many moments, but it never
constructs fixed matrices \(A_q\) and vectors \(u_q,v_q\) satisfying a
formula \(S_q(m)=u_q^{\mathsf T}A_q^m v_q\) uniformly in the
resolution parameter.  Such a formula is a separate finite-state
hypothesis in \Cref{def:cert-univ}.  When it is present,
\Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget} forces exponential growth
in \(m\); \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness} constructs computable
finite-fiber covers whose second moments violate that budget.  Hence
the finite-fiber facts carry the L2 existence clause, while the cited
growth obstruction carries the strictness from finite-state transfer.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Subordinate role of L2 finite-fiber facts]
\label{cor:L2-subordinate-role}
The L2 positive theorem contributes only the following finite-resolution
certificate layer to the main hierarchy: after an L1 normalizer has been
chosen, the fiber-size multiset at each resolution gives Hankel
positivity and finite Prony reconstruction by finite enumeration.  It
does not by itself produce canonical forms, arithmetic readout laws, or a
uniform finite-state transfer representation.  Hence the non-generic
content of the hierarchy is supplied by the Zeckendorf protocol theorem,
the level-zero multiplication obstruction, the Richardson normalizer
obstruction, and the finite-state growth obstruction, not by the
Hankel--Prony reconstruction clauses.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The finite-fiber clauses in \Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ} are obtained
by first fixing the L1 data from \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ} and
then applying \Cref{prop:finite-fiber-certificate-taxonomy} to the finite
surjections \(\Fold_m\colon\Omega_m\to X_m\).  The proof of that taxonomy
uses only the finite list of fiber sizes at the fixed value of \(m\): its
Hankel assertion is the Gram factorization of
\Cref{rem:hankel-psd-generic}, and its reconstruction assertion is the
finite-atomic Prony argument of \Cref{thm:prony}.  These arguments neither
define the normalizer nor transport addition or multiplication; those
data have already been supplied at L1.  They also do not construct fixed
matrices in the resolution variable, as \Cref{cor:L2-load-split} records.
The remaining named theorems are exactly the places where the manuscript
proves non-generic restrictions: \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}
excludes ambient-product multiplication on one free monogenic orbit,
\Cref{thm:separation} gives the Richardson normalizer obstruction under
its explicit witness interface, and \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}
separates finite-fiber certificates from finite-state transfer by a
growth budget violation.
\end{proof}

The finite-state strengthening is the additional transfer-matrix
requirement in \Cref{def:cert-univ}.  The tensor construction in
\Cref{thm:star-kernel} is supporting infrastructure for a common
finite-state form: it shows that, once such transfer data exist in
that nonnegative tensor model, the symmetric subspace suffices.  Thus
the finite-fiber certificate is tied to the finite normalizer, while
the finite-state condition is a separate computational assertion and
is not a consequence of Hankel positivity or finite Prony
reconstruction.
