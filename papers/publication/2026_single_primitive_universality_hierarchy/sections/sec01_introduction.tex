\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Finite-state descriptions of numeration systems are useful only when the
state bound is proved to be exact.  In a collision problem, a truncation that
merely contains the likely large-weight paths can still remove an accepting
path and change every moment.  The first theorem below isolates the
losslessness argument for the signed Fibonacci carries arising from the
Zeckendorf fold.  This gives a fixed matrix for each prescribed moment degree,
with no state set depending on the resolution.

For \(m\ge1\), put \(\Omega_m=\{0,1\}^{m+1}\), let \(X_m\) be the Zeckendorf
normal forms of values in \([0,F_{m+2})\), and define
\[
 d_m(x)=|\Fold_m^{-1}(x)|,\qquad
 S_q(m)=\sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^q .
\]
The index \(m\) is the resolution and \(q\) is the moment degree.  The
quantifiers in our main result are deliberately separate: \(q\) is fixed
before the matrix is constructed, while one matrix must work for every
\(m\ge1\).

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-degree carry transfer and primitive asymptotics]
\label{thm:main-transfer}
For every fixed \(q\ge0\), there are effectively constructible integer data
\(T_q,u_q,v_q\), independent of \(m\), such that
\[
 S_q(m)=u_q^{\mathsf T}T_q^m v_q\qquad(m\ge1).
\]
For \(q\ge1\), the matrix can be trimmed to a primitive nonnegative matrix
\(P_q\) with Perron root \(\lambda_q\), and
\[
 S_q(m)=c_q\lambda_q^m+O(\theta_q^m),
 \qquad 0\le\theta_q<\lambda_q,\quad c_q>0 .
\]
Thus \(\sum_{m\ge1}S_q(m)z^m\) is rational and
\(\lim_{m\to\infty}m^{-1}\log S_q(m)=\log\lambda_q\).
For \(q=0\), the limit is \(\log\varphi\), where
\(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
\end{theorem}

The proof is split into the carry losslessness theorem, the matrix
construction, and the primitivity argument.  The losslessness step is
substantive: it is not a consequence of an exponential growth estimate.

The second result gives a closed recurrence in the first nontrivial moment.
\begin{theorem}[Exact second moment]
\label{thm:intro-second-moment}
With \(S_2(m)\) as above,
\[
 S_2(1)=6,\quad S_2(2)=14,\quad S_2(3)=36,
\]
and
\[
 S_2(m)=2S_2(m-1)+2S_2(m-2)-2S_2(m-3)\qquad(m\ge4).
\]
\end{theorem}

The fibre maximum has a different role.  It supplies a sharp height scale
against which all moments can be compared, and it is the input needed to
state accurately what fixed-degree transfer does not imply.
\begin{theorem}[Exact fibre height]
\label{thm:intro-height}
If \(M_m=\max_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)\), then
\[
 M_{2s-1}=F_{s+1},\qquad M_{2s}=2F_s\qquad(s\ge1).
\]
\end{theorem}

The ordinary interval maximum for unrestricted Fibonacci representations is
used only as cited background in the proof of this height formula.  The
fold-specific shift from an ordinary interval to the residue fibre is proved
in the present paper.

\subsection{Related work and position}
Finite-state normalization is classical in non-standard numeration.  Frougny
gave finite-automaton characterizations for representations of numbers
\cite{Frougny1992Representations}, and Berend and Frougny identified the
Pisot-base setting in which arithmetic predicates are computable by finite
automata \cite{BerendFrougny1994Pisot}; the finite-$\beta$ framework and
on-line digit conversion were developed further in
\cite{FrougnySolomyak1992FiniteBeta,Frougny1999OnlineAddition,Frougny2003DigitSet}.
The Fibonacci-specific conversion to base $\varphi$ is also classical
\cite{FrougnySakarovitch1999FibonacciPhi}, while the combinatorics of
Fibonacci words is surveyed by Berstel \cite{Berstel1986FibonacciWords}.
General automatic-sequence background is given by Allouche and Shallit
\cite{AlloucheShallit2003AutomaticSequences}.
The underlying greedy representation is Zeckendorf's theorem
\cite{Zeckendorf1972}; the present fold uses that normal form after a residue
reduction rather than introducing a new normalization procedure.
The transfer-matrix and Perron viewpoint used for regular-language counting
is standard (see, for example, \cite{FlajoletSedgewick2009}).

There is also a substantial literature on the multiplicity of individual
integers as sums of distinct Fibonacci numbers, including recursive counting
formulas \cite{BicknellJohnsonFielder1999} and sharp interval maxima
\cite{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005}.  Those results count representations of
specified integers (or maximize that count on an interval).  They therefore do
not form the residue fibres of the map $\Fold_m$, and they do not compute the
collision moments $S_q(m)$ (in particular, no displayed $S_2$ recurrence).
What is not supplied by the classical normalization results is a fixed-degree
transfer theorem for these moments, together with a proof that the bounded
signed-carry automaton is lossless.  That proof is the substantive step: a
truncation chosen because it contains the apparently dominant paths may still
delete one accepting path, and deleting that path changes every moment, not
just a lower-order error term.

\subsection{What is new}
The fixed-degree theorem is a transfer theorem for the actual collision
moments of the unmodified fold.  The finite state set is explicit and the
proof shows that every accepting path survives the bounded carry restriction.
The primitive support graph then yields more than rationality: it gives a
single exponential scale and a positive leading coefficient at each fixed
degree.  The recurrence for \(q=2\) is exact rather than an asymptotic fit.
Finally, the height formula lets us state a restrained nonuniformity result:
the fixed-degree matrices cannot be assembled into one rational bivariate
transfer law in the degree marker.

The cited literature does not settle the finite-state transfer question for
these particular fold collision moments: it gives no resolution-independent
losslessness result for the signed-carry truncation and no displayed
second-moment recurrence for this fold.  The present results answer those two
questions for the unmodified fold, degree by degree in \(q\).
