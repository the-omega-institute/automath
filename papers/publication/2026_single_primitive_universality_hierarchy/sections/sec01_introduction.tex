\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Universality statements can mean different things.  A single operation may
represent a large class of expressions, while still giving no effective way to
choose or compare representatives.  We separate three levels for this
phenomenon.  Level \(L0\), \emph{expression universality}, asks only that terms
exist.  Level \(L1\), \emph{protocol universality}, asks for computable normal
forms, compiled operations, and semantic readout identities.  Level \(L2\),
\emph{certificate universality}, adds finite certificates attached to the
fibres of a normalizing map.

The motivating \(L0\) example is Odrzywo{\l}ek's operation
\[
  \eml(x,y)=e^x-\operatorname{Log}y,
\]
with the constant \(1\), which represents the operations in a standard
scientific-calculator basis \cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator}.  We use
that public record only as a representative-existence theorem.  The positive
\(L1\) protocol in this paper is internal: Zeckendorf normal forms, valuation
transport through \(\NN\), and Church iteration give compiled addition and
multiplication on \(\NN\).  The main obstructions show that these two assertions
should not be conflated.

The Richardson obstruction is used in a verified-triple form.  If a finite pure
EML triple \((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) is supplied with proved real-domain
identities
\[
  \mathsf P=\pi,
  \qquad \mathsf S(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad \mathsf R(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0}),
\]
then \Cref{thm:separation} gives the Richardson normalizer obstruction relative
to that verified interface.  This is the explicit scope signoff for the Proc.
AMS claim: the paper proves the Richardson obstruction for a verified
replacement triple and does not assert an unconditional concrete pure-EML triple
for \(\pi\), sine, and nonnegative square root.  The named printed roots
\(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) are retained only as a conditional specialization of
this theorem, not as a load-bearing input to the main hierarchy.

  W_{\pi}=\pi,

  \qquad W_{\sqrt{}}(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0})
\]

the versioned arXiv record \cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator}, DOI
\texttt{10.48550/arXiv.2603.21852}, and the provenance snapshot

The assumption is deliberately not a theorem of this paper.  It records a

only an optional specialization if a reader accepts \((H_{\mathcal W})\).

\label{prop:companion-reference-audit}

representative-existence statement for \((\eml,1)\), Richardson's undecidability
theorem, the standard Zeckendorf normal-form theorem, and the classical
finite-atomic moment facts cited in \Cref{sec:L2}.  No companion manuscript,
unversioned source table, or hidden compiler output is used as a proof input.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The imported \(L0\) statement is isolated in \Cref{thm:eml-universality}.  The
Richardson step in \Cref{thm:separation} cites Richardson's theorem through

normalizer and valuation transport proved in \Cref{sec:L1}.  The \(L2\) facts
are finite sums over finite fibres, with classical references supplied in
\Cref{sec:L2}.  These are exactly the four listed imports.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Obstruction spine]
\label{thm:obstruction-spine}
The hierarchy has two primary obstructions and one subordinate certificate
addendum.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A verified finite EML triple for \(\pi\), sine, and nonnegative square root
  transports Richardson's identity problem into pure EML terms, giving a
  many-one zero-test obstruction and hence ruling out computable semantic
  normalizers and equality-reflecting compilers on that translated fragment.
  This is an interface theorem, not an unconditional printed-root instantiation.
\item Multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be read injectively from the
  ambient product on a single free monogenic orbit; every such readout factors
  through a prime-weight exponent skeleton, and the faithful restrictions are
  exactly the trivial monoid and a single-prime-power ray.
\item Finite-fibre certificates give Hankel positivity and finite Prony
  reconstruction at each fixed resolution, but they do not force a uniform
  finite-state transfer formula in the resolution variable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the interface theorem \Cref{thm:separation}, with the
positive/nonnegative square-root boundary recorded in
\Cref{thm:positive-core-nonnegative-root-boundary} and the non-instantiation
boundary recorded in
\Cref{thm:finite-eml-richardson-trichotomy,thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance,thm:richardson-separation-conditional-status}.
The second is
\Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, with the skeleton and sharp faithful-submonoid
classification in
\Cref{lem:level-zero-exponent-skeleton,thm:one-orbit-readout-classification,thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification}.
The third is
\Cref{thm:L2-subordinate-cover-classification,thm:L2-finite-fiber-no-transfer-content,thm:L2-one-spike-skeleton,thm:finite-state-strictness}.
The three proofs use disjoint data: the Richardson witness interface, the free
monogenic orbit, and computable finite covers, respectively.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Proc. AMS hierarchy spine]\label{thm:main-hierarchy}
With the definitions in \Cref{sec:L0,sec:L1,sec:L2}, the single-primitive
hierarchy has the following theorem-note form.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The operation \((\eml,1)\) gives an imported \(L0\) expression system for
  the public calculator basis, but the full \(\mathcal B_{36}\) assertion is a
  cited existence import rather than a self-contained proof in this paper, and
  that import gives no semantic normalizer.
\item Zeckendorf normal forms and valuation transport give an internal \(L1\)
  protocol for \(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).
\item For every finite pure EML replacement triple
  \((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) whose real-domain identities for \(\pi\),
  sine on \(\RR\), and nonnegative square root on \(\RR_{\ge0}\) are proved, the
  translated pure EML fragment inherits Richardson's undecidable zero-test.
  Thus the \(L0\) representative statement does not promote, relative to that
  verified interface, to a computable semantic normalizer or to a semantic
  compiler into finite decidable protocol objects on the translated Richardson
  fragment.  The named printed roots
  \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) are not counted as an instantiated theorem of
  this paper, by
  \Cref{thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance,thm:root-name-erasure-unconditional-separation}.
\item The multiplication readout in the \(L1\) protocol cannot be collapsed to
  the ambient product on one free monogenic orbit, by
  \Cref{thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}; the sharp faithful
  submonoids in that model are classified by
  \Cref{thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification}.  No global rigidity
  theorem for all single-primitive protocols is used.
\item The finite-fibre \(L2\) layer supplies subordinate fixed-resolution
  certificates, and its strictness result is a computable-cover separation from
  finite-state transfer by
  \Cref{thm:L2-subordinate-cover-classification,thm:L2-one-spike-skeleton,thm:finite-state-strictness},
  not an intrinsic asymptotic theorem for the original \(\Fold_m\) fibres.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The \(L0\) clause is \Cref{thm:eml-universality}; its exact imported scope is
\Cref{thm:B36-import-exact-scope}, and the dependency cut is
\Cref{prop:eml-import-dependency-cut}.  The positive \(L1\) clause is
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}.  The Richardson interface clause is
\Cref{thm:separation}, and the semantic compiler boundary is
\Cref{cor:compile-scope}.  The optional named-root specialization is quarantined
  in \Cref{thm:printed-root-richardson-conditional-clause}; the strengthened
  many-one zero-test and compiler obstruction is
  \Cref{thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface};
\Cref{thm:richardson-interface-proof-graph-cut,thm:richardson-proof-graph-no-hidden-witness,thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance,thm:root-name-erasure-unconditional-separation}
record that it is neither hidden in the \(\mathcal B_{36}\) import nor part of
this concrete named-root spine.  The one-orbit obstruction is
\Cref{thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}, with the source
classification in
\Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification,thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification};
\Cref{prop:no-global-protocol-rigidity-input} records that no broader global
protocol-rigidity theorem enters the proof.  This is equivalently the second
clause of \Cref{thm:obstruction-spine}.  The \(L2\) assertion is the finite-fibre
certificate taxonomy of \Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}, the subordinate-cover
  classification of \Cref{thm:L2-subordinate-cover-classification}, the
  one-spike skeleton of \Cref{thm:L2-one-spike-skeleton}, and the transfer
  separation in \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Subordinate finite-fibre addendum]\label{cor:main-L2-addendum}
The \(L2\) part of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} is independent of the two primary
obstructions: Hankel positivity and Prony reconstruction are fixed-resolution
finite-atomic facts, while the strict \(L2\) content is the separate
finite-state growth obstruction.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{prop:classical-finite-atomic-skeleton} to the fibre-size multiset at
each resolution.  This uses neither Richardson expressions nor the one-orbit
readout.  The absence of any forced finite-state transfer is exactly
\Cref{thm:L2-finite-fiber-no-transfer-content}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Three-anchor formulation]
\label{thm:three-anchor-excision}
\label{prop:main-dependency-splitting}
The submission depends only on three anchors: the public \(L0\) expression
import, the internal Zeckendorf \(L1\) protocol, and the finite-fibre \(L2\)
certificate construction.  All Richardson conclusions pass through the explicit
verified-triple interface of \Cref{thm:separation}; the printed-root package is
only the optional conditional specialization \((H_{\mathcal W})\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The anchors are the three sections \Cref{sec:L0,sec:L1,sec:L2}.  The only
cross-anchor obstruction using EML analytic identities is \Cref{thm:separation},
whose statement is the verified-triple interface rather than a concrete witness
construction.  The conditional
named-root route is isolated in \Cref{thm:printed-root-richardson-conditional-clause}.
Hence no further source table or companion result is a hidden premise.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Richardson submission boundary]\label{cor:richardson-submission-boundary}
The paper proves a Richardson obstruction interface: once a finite EML triple is
specified and its three displayed real-domain identities are proved, Richardson's
undecidability theorem gives the transported EML normalizer obstruction.  It
does not construct such a triple for \(\pi\) and \(\sin\), and it does not assert
an instantiated named-root theorem for \(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) unless
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) is assumed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The interface statement is \Cref{thm:separation}, whose hypothesis includes the
finite triple and the three semantic identities.  The internal core library in
\Cref{lem:eml-real-witness-library} explicitly omits \(\pi\) and sine, so this
paper cannot instantiate the interface from the core witnesses alone.  The only
named-root specialization is isolated in
\Cref{thm:printed-root-richardson-conditional-clause} and uses the definition of
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) in \Cref{ass:HW}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Richardson obstruction interface]\label{thm:richardson-obstruction-interface}
The Richardson input to the main theorem is exactly the following interface: a
finite pure-EML triple with proved identities for \(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and
the nonnegative square root on \(\RR_{\ge0}\).  Under this interface, identity
to zero for Richardson expressions reduces effectively to equality with the
translated zero EML term, so a computable semantic normalizer would decide
Richardson's undecidable problem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is \Cref{thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface} in interface
form.  The proof there constructs the translation by structural recursion and
then applies Richardson's undecidability theorem recorded in
\Cref{lem:richardson-fragment}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Positive realizations]\label{cor:main-positive-realizations}
The positive parts of the hierarchy are constructive: \((\eml,1)\) gives the
imported expression representatives, Zeckendorf valuation transport gives the
compiled \(L1\) arithmetic operations, and finite fibre-size multisets give the
\(L2\) certificates.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The three constructions are respectively
\Cref{thm:eml-universality,thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ,prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}.
\end{proof}
