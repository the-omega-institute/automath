\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Universality statements often hide a difference in kind.  The
Sheffer stroke generates the Boolean operations \cite{Sheffer1913};
lambda abstraction gives a calculus of effective computation
\cite{Church1936}; and combinatory logic replaces variables by a
finite stock of combinators \cite{Schonfinkel1924}.  In such
examples it matters whether the assertion is merely that expressions
exist, or also that representatives can be chosen and compared
effectively.

The same distinction occurs for recent one-operation presentations
of elementary functions.  Odrzywo{\l}ek showed that
\[
  \eml(x,y)=e^x-\operatorname{Log}y
\]
together with the constant \(1\) represents the operations in a
standard scientific-calculator basis
\cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator}.  Thus a
large elementary language can be encoded by binary trees with a
single internal label.  This is a useful expression theorem, but it
does not itself choose a normal form among the many trees defining
the same function.

We shall distinguish three levels.  Level \(L0\), \emph{expression
universality}, only asks for some term over a single binary
operation.  Level \(L1\), \emph{protocol universality}, asks in
addition for computable normal forms and compiled normal-form
operations, together with semantic readout identities.  Our model
example uses Zeckendorf normal forms only as a computable addressing
system: operations are transported through the valuation bijection with
\(\NN\), and the elementary Church-iteration identities then read
addition and multiplication on \(\NN=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\) at two
successive composition levels.  Level \(L2\), \emph{certificate universality},
attaches finite certificates to the fibers of a normalizing map.
Hankel positivity and finite Prony reconstruction are then automatic
finite-fiber facts, whereas a finite-state transfer formula for the
same moments is extra structure.

The results below are separations of these structures, not new
proofs of local or online Zeckendorf arithmetic, Church iteration,
elementary function identities, or finite moment reconstruction.  The EML
operation is expression-universal for Odrzywo{\l}ek's basis, but
Richardson's theorem rules out a computable canonical form on the
translated Richardson fragment even for the fixed predicate of identity
to zero on the transported real domain.  In the Zeckendorf protocol, addition is
read on a free monogenic orbit, while multiplication cannot be read
there by the ambient product, even after injectively recoding the
positive integers.  The certificate result is likewise a generic
finite-fiber taxonomy statement rather than a new Hankel--Prony
reconstruction theorem.  Finally, finite Hankel--Prony certificates do
not imply a finite-state transfer law: one can replace the finite
sets over the same normal forms by computable finite-fiber covers,
leaving the arithmetic readouts unchanged and forcing the second
collision moments to grow faster than any exponential sequence.

This scope is intentionally disjoint from several nearby Zeckendorf
and finite-resolution projects.  Stable Zeckendorf arithmetic and
finite-window Zeckendorf thermodynamics are treated as companion
directions \cite{OmegaStableZeckendorfArithmetic2026,
OmegaFiniteWindowZeckendorfThermodynamics2026}; no result from those
projects is used here.  We do not study Zeckendorf fiber
thermodynamics, folded histogram laws for the actual finite-window
fibers, finite-observation escape rates, or normalization automata.
The Zeckendorf material used here is only the computable normal-form
address system needed for the protocol readouts and for the abstract
finite-fiber certificate comparison.

Section~\ref{sec:L0} records \(L0\) expression universality for the
EML input.  Section~\ref{sec:L1} gives \(L1\) protocol universality
through the Zeckendorf protocol and the level-zero obstruction for
multiplication.  Section~\ref{sec:L2} sets up \(L2\) certificate
universality through finite-fiber certificates.
Section~\ref{sec:strictness} proves the separations.  The appendix
records two supplementary consequences: a finite-mixture decomposition
for the fiber measure and a fold-numeral restatement of the natural
numbers object.

\begin{proposition}[Appendix independence audit]\label{prop:appendix-independence-audit}
Every assertion of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy,cor:main-positive-realizations}
is proved using only the
definitions, lemmas, propositions, theorems, and corollaries in
Sections~\ref{sec:intro}--\ref{sec:discussion}, together with the
external references cited in those sections.  No proof in those
sections invokes a label from the appendix.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proof of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy,cor:main-positive-realizations}
cites only internal inputs from Sections~\ref{sec:L0}--\ref{sec:strictness}:
the EML universality result, the Zeckendorf protocol theorem, the
level-zero obstruction and its dichotomy corollary, the Richardson
separation theorem conditional on the explicit locked three-witness
hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\), with its translation and witness-locking
inputs, the
finite-fiber certificate proposition, the finite-state strictness
theorem, its two growth and independence corollaries, and the
cover-preservation lemma.  Each of these named inputs is introduced
before the final remarks.  The proofs of those inputs use definitions
from the same range of sections and the external references cited
there.  The appendix is placed after the final remarks and contains
only two supplementary consequences.  Since neither supplementary
consequence is cited in the theorem chain just listed, the appendix is
non-load-bearing for the hierarchy theorem and its separations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Main hierarchy theorem]\label{thm:main-hierarchy}
With the definitions made below, the single-primitive hierarchy has the
following five-clause form.  In the protocol clauses, \(A\) is any
nonempty set and \(g\colon A\to A\) is any endomorphism of infinite
order.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The EML system \((\eml,1)\) is \(L0\)
  expression-universal for Odrzywo{\l}ek's scientific-calculator basis
  \(\mathcal B_{36}\), in the principal-branch sense imported from that
  external representation theorem.
\item The Zeckendorf address system, equipped with its stable normal
  forms and the two composition readouts
  \(c_n\mapsto g^{\circ n}\) and \(c_n\mapsto I_n\), is
  \(L1\) protocol-universal for \(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).  The
  multiplication readout is genuinely extra protocol structure:
  multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be read from the ambient
  product on one free monogenic orbit, even after an injective recoding
  of the positive integers.
\item Assuming the explicit locked three-witness hypothesis
  \((H_{\mathcal W})\) of \Cref{prop:locked-three-witness-interface}---that
  the printed finite EML witnesses for \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and
  \(\sqrt{\phantom{x}}\) have the stated principal-branch real meanings---no computable
  normal form into a decidable finite-object codomain can decide identity
  to zero on the transported Richardson fragment by comparison with the
  normal form of the translated zero expression.
\item The Zeckendorf finite-resolution data are \(L2\)
  certificate-universal in the finite-fiber sense: the normalization
  fibers have computable collision moments, Hankel-positive moment
  matrices, and finite Prony reconstruction with the computable bound
  \(2F_{m+2}-1\) at resolution \(m\).  This is the finite-atomic
  certificate supplied by the fold fibers, not a new finite-window
  moment theory.
\item Finite-fiber certificate data do not force a finite-state
  transfer law: there are computable finite-fiber covers preserving the
  protocol normal forms and readouts whose second collision moments have
  superexponential growth.
\end{enumerate}
Thus expression representatives, computable protocol normal forms with
semantic readouts, finite-fiber certificate data, and finite-state
transfer laws are separate kinds of auxiliary structure in the
single-primitive setting.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first clause is exactly \Cref{thm:eml-universality}, stated there as
Odrzywo{\l}ek's external representation theorem for
\(\mathcal B_{36}\) with principal-branch domains.  The positive part of
the second clause is \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}; the
monogenic-orbit obstruction in the same clause is
\Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, in its protocol-facing form
\Cref{cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}.  The third clause is
\Cref{thm:separation}, using the effective translation lemma
  \Cref{lem:richardson-translation}, the finite witness corollary
  \Cref{cor:eml-witness-locking}, and the isolated hypothesis interface
  \Cref{prop:locked-three-witness-interface}.  Equivalently, its
  locked-witness form is
  \Cref{cor:conditional-three-witness-obstruction}; the
  replacement-stability form is \Cref{thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface}.
  The fourth clause is
\Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}, whose proof invokes only the
finite-atomic Hankel and Prony results
\Cref{thm:hankel-psd,thm:prony} and whose generic load is isolated in
\Cref{prop:finite-fiber-certificate-taxonomy,cor:L2-load-split}.  The
fifth clause is \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}, sharpened in
\Cref{cor:finite-state-growth-budget,cor:hankel-psd-independent}, with
preservation of the underlying protocol supplied by
\Cref{lem:cover-preserves-protocol}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Positive realizations extracted from the hierarchy]
\label{cor:main-positive-realizations}
In the same terminology, the three positive levels have the following
concrete single-primitive realizations.  In the protocol clause, \(A\) is any
nonempty set and \(g\colon A\to A\) is any endomorphism of infinite
order.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The EML system \((\eml,1)\) is \(L0\)
  expression-universal for Odrzywo{\l}ek's scientific-calculator basis
  \(\mathcal B_{36}\).
\item The Zeckendorf address system, equipped with its stable normal
  forms and the two composition readouts
  \(c_n\mapsto g^{\circ n}\) and \(c_n\mapsto I_n\), is
  \(L1\) protocol-universal for \(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).
\item The Zeckendorf finite-resolution data are \(L2\)
  certificate-universal in the finite-fiber sense: the normalization
  fibers have computable collision moments, Hankel-positive moment
  matrices, and finite Prony reconstruction with a computable bound.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
These are respectively the first clause, the positive assertion in the
second clause, and the fourth clause of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.
\end{proof}
