\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Universality statements can mean different things.  A single operation may
represent a large class of expressions, while still giving no effective way to
choose or compare representatives.  We separate three levels for this
phenomenon.  Level \(L0\), \emph{expression universality}, asks only that terms
exist.  Level \(L1\), \emph{protocol universality}, asks for computable normal
forms, compiled operations, and semantic readout identities.  Level \(L2\),
\emph{certificate universality}, adds finite certificates attached to the
fibres of a normalizing map.

The motivating \(L0\) example is Odrzywo{\l}ek's operation
\[
  \eml(x,y)=e^x-\operatorname{Log}y,
\]
with the constant \(1\), which represents the operations in a standard
scientific-calculator basis \cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator}.  We use
that public record only as a representative-existence theorem.  The positive
\(L1\) protocol in this paper is internal: Zeckendorf normal forms, valuation
transport through \(\NN\), and Church iteration give compiled addition and
multiplication on \(\NN\).  The main obstructions show that these two assertions
should not be conflated.

The Richardson obstruction is used only in a verified-triple form.  If a finite pure
EML triple \((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) is supplied with proved real-domain
identities
\[
  \mathsf P=\pi,
  \qquad \mathsf S(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad \mathsf R(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0}),
\]
then \Cref{thm:separation} gives the Richardson normalizer obstruction relative
to that verified interface.  No unconditional concrete EML terms for this triple
are printed or proved in the article.  The Proc. AMS claim is the verified
replacement-triple theorem, not an unconditional concrete pure-EML triple theorem
for \(\pi\), sine on all of \(\RR\), and square root on \(\RR_{\ge0}\).  The identifiers
\(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) are retained only as fixed strings in the conditional specialization of
this theorem, not as a load-bearing input to the main hierarchy.

  \qquad W_{\sin}(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad W_{\sqrt{}}(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0})

in the principal-branch semantics of \Cref{def:eml}.  The public identifiers are
the versioned arXiv record \cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator}, DOI
\texttt{10.48550/arXiv.2603.21852}, and the provenance snapshot

Scope is deliberately narrow.  This article proves the \(L0/L1/L2\) hierarchy,
the Richardson interface obstruction, the one-orbit level-zero obstruction, and

thermodynamics, folded histogram laws, finite observation escape rates, or
normalization automata.

The assumption is deliberately not a theorem of this paper.  It records a
versioned external analytic assertion whose proof is not reproduced here.  Thus
all uses of Richardson below pass through the abstract
verified-triple hypothesis, and the printed names \(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) are
only an optional specialization if a reader accepts \((H_{\mathcal W})\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact printed-string Richardson cut]
\label{thm:intro-printed-richardson-cut}
In the unconditional main theorem chain, the Richardson obstruction is exactly
the verified-triple implication of \Cref{thm:separation}.  In particular this
article does not prove the concrete printed-string assertion
\[
  U_{47}=\pi,
  \qquad U_{111}(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad U_{126}(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0})
\]
in the principal-branch real-domain EML semantics.  The printed strings
\((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) instantiate the Richardson obstruction in this
article if and only if the three real-domain identities required of a verified
triple are added as a hypothesis, for example by \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  Their
finite printed syntax alone is not a proof of those identities and is not a
separate input to \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Richardson theorem used in the main chain is \Cref{thm:separation}.  Its
hypotheses are not merely three names: they are a specified finite pure EML triple
\((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) together with proofs that
\(\mathsf P=\pi\), \(\mathsf S(x)=\sin x\) on \(\RR\), and
\(\mathsf R(t)=\sqrt t\) on \(\RR_{\ge0}\) in the principal-branch semantics of
\Cref{def:eml}.  Once those hypotheses hold, \Cref{thm:separation} gives the
many-one Richardson reduction and the normalizer and compiler obstructions, so
every verified triple is a valid interface instance.

For the particular names \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\), the displayed concrete
assertion is exactly the semantic content required to turn those names into a
verified triple.  The only statement in this introduction that supplies that
content is the optional assumption \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  Without that assumption,
the displayed names are only fixed finite strings from a public record; finite
syntax does not imply the principal-branch real-domain identities demanded by
\Cref{thm:separation}.  Therefore deleting \((H_{\mathcal W})\) deletes only the
optional printed-string specialization and leaves the universal verified-triple
Richardson obstruction unchanged.  This is the Richardson branch cited by
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Obstruction spine]
\label{thm:obstruction-spine}
The hierarchy has two primary obstructions and one subordinate certificate
addendum.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A verified finite EML triple for \(\pi\), sine, and nonnegative square root
  transports Richardson's identity problem into pure EML terms, giving a
  many-one zero-test obstruction and hence ruling out computable semantic
  normalizers and equality-reflecting compilers on that translated fragment.
  This is an interface theorem, not an unconditional printed-string instantiation.
\item Multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be read injectively from the
  ambient product on a single free monogenic orbit; every such readout factors
  through a prime-weight exponent skeleton, and the faithful restrictions are
  exactly the trivial monoid and a single-prime-power ray.
\item Finite-fibre certificates give Hankel positivity and finite Prony
  reconstruction at each fixed resolution, but, in the computable
  finite-fibre cover category over the fixed Zeckendorf normal forms, they do
  not force a uniform finite-state transfer formula in the resolution
  variable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the interface theorem \Cref{thm:separation}, with the
positive/nonnegative square-root boundary recorded in
\Cref{thm:positive-core-nonnegative-root-boundary,thm:no-core-only-richardson-translation}
and the non-instantiation boundary recorded in
\Cref{thm:largest-verified-richardson-fragment,thm:maximal-unconditional-richardson-package,thm:finite-eml-richardson-trichotomy,thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance,thm:richardson-separation-conditional-status}.
The formal replacement of any printed-string reading by the universal verified
interface is
\Cref{thm:intro-printed-richardson-cut,thm:main-chain-richardson-replacement-principle}.
The second is
\Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, with the skeleton and sharp faithful-submonoid
classification in
\Cref{lem:level-zero-exponent-skeleton,thm:one-orbit-readout-classification,thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification,thm:finite-rank-product-readout-lower-bound}.
The third is the finite-fibre/finite-state bridge and cover-relative statement
\Cref{thm:L2-terminology-bridge,thm:L2-finite-window-transfer-obstruction,thm:L2-subordinate-cover-classification,thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication,thm:L2-finite-fiber-no-transfer-content,thm:L2-one-spike-skeleton,thm:finite-state-strictness},
whose proof changes only certificate multiplicities above the fixed normal
forms and makes no intrinsic assertion about the original \(\Fold_m\) fibres.
The three proofs use disjoint data: the Richardson witness interface, the free
monogenic orbit, and computable finite covers, respectively.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Proc. AMS hierarchy spine]\label{thm:main-hierarchy}
With the definitions in \Cref{sec:L0,sec:L1,sec:L2}, the single-primitive
hierarchy has the following theorem-note form.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The operation \((\eml,1)\) gives an imported \(L0\) expression system for
  the public calculator basis, but the full \(\mathcal B_{36}\) assertion is a
  cited existence import rather than a self-contained proof in this paper, and
  that import gives no semantic normalizer.  The exact submission decision is
  isolated in \Cref{thm:L0-external-anchor-criterion}: keeping the arXiv/ancillary
  anchor keeps only this representative-existence clause, while deleting it
  leaves the internal strictness theorem chain intact but removes the global
  \(\mathcal B_{36}\) expression-universality assertion.
\item Zeckendorf normal forms and valuation transport give an internal \(L1\)
  protocol for \(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).
\item The Richardson clause is the following unconditional universal
  implication.  For every specified finite pure EML replacement triple
  \((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\), if the three real-domain identities for
  \(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and nonnegative square root on \(\RR_{\ge0}\) have
  been proved, then the translated pure EML fragment inherits Richardson's
  undecidable zero-test.  Consequently no computable semantic normalizer, and no
  equality-reflecting compiler into finite decidable protocol objects, exists on
  that transported Richardson fragment.  This verified-triple implication is the
  scoped Proc. AMS Richardson theorem.  Separately, the named printed strings
  \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) instantiate the clause only in the optional
  \((H_{\mathcal W})\) specialization; they are not an unconditional concrete
  printed-string theorem of this paper, by
  \Cref{thm:printed-string-real-domain-upgrade-criterion,cor:printed-string-richardson-exact-conditionality,thm:exact-unconditional-richardson-scope,thm:no-B36-Richardson-instantiation,thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance,thm:root-name-erasure-unconditional-separation,thm:no-unconditional-printed-richardson-edge,thm:unconditional-richardson-factorization,thm:main-spine-scope-closure}.
\item The multiplication readout in the \(L1\) protocol cannot be collapsed to
  the ambient product on one free monogenic orbit, by
  \Cref{thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}; the sharp faithful
  submonoids in that model are classified by
  \Cref{thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification}.  More generally,
  \Cref{thm:finite-rank-product-readout-lower-bound} proves the finite-rank
  ambient-product budget: rank \(d\) carries at most \(d\) independent prime
  generators.  No global rigidity theorem for all single-primitive protocols is
  used.
\item The finite-fibre \(L2\) layer supplies subordinate fixed-resolution
  certificates, and its strictness result is an addendum: an existential separation in the
  computable finite-fibre cover category over the fixed Zeckendorf normal-form
  sets.  It separates such covers from the stronger finite-state transfer
  condition by
  \Cref{thm:L2-terminology-bridge,thm:L2-finite-window-transfer-obstruction,thm:L2-subordinate-cover-classification,thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication,thm:L2-one-spike-skeleton,thm:L2-cover-relative-closure,thm:finite-state-strictness,thm:main-spine-scope-closure},
  not an intrinsic asymptotic theorem for the original \(\Fold_m\) fibres.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The \(L0\) clause is the imported representative theorem
\Cref{thm:eml-universality}, with exact imported scope recorded in
\Cref{thm:L0-external-anchor-criterion,thm:B36-import-exact-scope,thm:B36-self-contained-replacement-core}.  The positive \(L1\) clause is the internal
Zeckendorf protocol \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}.  The Richardson clause
is the verified-triple obstruction
\Cref{thm:separation,thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface,thm:richardson-interface-proof-graph-cut}; the printed
names have no unconditional semantic role by
\Cref{thm:printed-string-real-domain-upgrade-criterion,cor:printed-string-richardson-exact-conditionality,thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance,thm:root-name-erasure-unconditional-separation,thm:no-unconditional-printed-richardson-edge}.
The one-orbit obstruction, its sharp faithful-submonoid classification, and the
finite-rank product-readout budget are
\Cref{thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy,thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification,thm:finite-rank-product-readout-lower-bound}.
Finally, the \(L2\) clause is the finite-fibre certificate construction together
with the cover-relative bad-example skeleton of
\Cref{thm:L2-finite-window-transfer-obstruction,thm:L2-subordinate-cover-classification,thm:L2-cover-relative-closure,thm:finite-state-strictness}; the
nonimplication for intrinsic \(\Fold_m\) fibres is
\Cref{thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication}.  These cited results prove exactly
the five enumerated clauses.
\end{proof}

\begin{dependencyremark}[Scoped strictness gate]
\label{thm:scoped-strictness-gate}
The strictness assertions exported by \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} have two exact
quantifier cuts.  The Richardson obstruction is universal over verified finite
pure EML triples and becomes a printed-string instance only after the three
principal-branch real-domain identities required in \Cref{thm:separation} have
been proved or assumed.  The finite-state obstruction is existential in the
computable finite-fibre cover category over the fixed Zeckendorf normal forms,
not a recurrence, nonrecurrence, rationality, growth, or transfer classification
for the intrinsic fibre multiplicities of the unmodified maps \(\Fold_m\).  These
cuts are proved in
\Cref{thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance,thm:root-name-erasure-unconditional-separation,thm:L2-subordinate-cover-classification,thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication,thm:finite-state-strictness}.
\end{dependencyremark}

\begin{theorem}[Subordinate finite-fibre addendum]\label{cor:main-L2-addendum}
The \(L2\) part of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} is a subordinate addendum to the
primary \(L0/L1\) separation and one-orbit obstruction.  More precisely, deleting
the \(L2\) clause leaves unchanged the imported \(L0\) representative statement,
the internal Zeckendorf \(L1\) protocol, the verified-triple Richardson
normalizer obstruction, and the one-orbit multiplication obstruction.  The only
strictness added by \(L2\) is the cover-relative finite-state growth obstruction
for computable finite-fibre covers over the fixed normal-form sets; Hankel
positivity and Prony reconstruction remain fixed-resolution certificate facts
and are not proof inputs to the Richardson or one-orbit clauses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first four clauses of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} are proved from
\Cref{thm:eml-universality,thm:L0-external-anchor-criterion,thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ,thm:separation,thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface,thm:printed-string-real-domain-upgrade-criterion,thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy,thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification}.
None of these cited statements has a finite-fibre hypothesis.  Conversely, the
finite-fibre certificate facts begin by applying
\Cref{prop:classical-finite-atomic-skeleton} to the fibre-size multiset at each
resolution, and this uses neither Richardson expressions nor the one-orbit
readout.  The absence of any forced finite-state transfer is exactly the cover
theorem \Cref{thm:L2-finite-fiber-no-transfer-content,thm:L2-finite-window-transfer-obstruction}, and the main strictness
clause is its one-spike specialization \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}; both
leave the original \(\Fold_m\) fibres unchanged.  Thus removing the \(L2\)
addendum removes only that cover-relative budget obstruction and none of the
primary separation machinery.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scoped separation quantifiers]\label{thm:scoped-separation-quantifiers}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The \(L0\) expression clause imports representative existence for the full
  displayed \(\mathcal B_{36}\) basis from the public record.  Its
  self-contained content is only the finite core witness library printed in this
  article; it contains no term-by-term proof of all thirty-six representatives,
  no equality normal form, and no Richardson witness triple.
\item The Richardson clause is the universal implication over verified finite
  pure EML replacement triples: for every triple
  \((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) with proved real-domain identities for
  \(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and nonnegative square root on \(\RR_{\ge0}\), the
  translated Richardson fragment has no computable semantic normalizer into
  finite decidable objects and no equality-reflecting compiler into finite
  decidable protocol objects.  The theorem has no unconditional existential
  quantifier asserting that the printed strings \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\), or
  any other concrete pure EML triple, satisfy those three identities; this is
  the narrowed verified-interface fragment of
  \Cref{thm:narrowed-richardson-fragment-realized}.
\item The one-orbit multiplication obstruction is quantified only over readouts
  into one free monogenic orbit whose multiplication is read by the ambient

\item The \(L2\) finite-state clause is existential only in the computable
  finite-fibre cover category over the fixed Zeckendorf normal-form sets: there

For the \(L0\) clause, \Cref{thm:eml-universality} is explicitly a citation

\begin{theorem}[Main-spine scope closure]
\label{thm:main-spine-scope-closure}
The theorem chain of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} is closed exactly under the
following four interfaces.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The Richardson obstruction is the universal verified-triple implication of
  \Cref{thm:separation,thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface,thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem}.  The named
  strings \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) enter that implication only under the
  optional hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\); without that hypothesis they are fixed
  finite strings, not proved representatives of \(\pi\), sine, and nonnegative
  square root.
\item The internal EML core used unconditionally in the proof supplies arithmetic,
  division on its nonzero domain, \(\log 2\), and positive square root.  It does
  not supply the stronger concrete Richardson triple for \(\pi\), sine on all of
  \(\RR\), and square root at \(0\).
\item The finite-state transfer strictness result is existential and
  cover-relative: it concerns computable finite-fibre covers over the fixed
  Zeckendorf normal-form sets.  It gives no recurrence, nonrecurrence,
  rationality, growth, or finite-state-transfer classification for the
  unmodified fibres of \(\Fold_m\).
\item The multiplication obstruction is the one-free-monogenic-orbit obstruction.
  It is not a global rigidity theorem for every single-primitive protocol
  realizing \(\{+,\times\}\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the displayed hierarchy theorem is the verified-triple, internal
Zeckendorf, cover-relative finite-fibre theorem stated in this article, and none
of the four stronger readings above is a premise or conclusion of the main
spine.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the first item, \Cref{thm:separation} and
\Cref{thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface} are stated with an arbitrary
finite pure EML triple only after the three required real-domain identities have
been proved.  \Cref{thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance},
\Cref{thm:root-name-erasure-unconditional-separation}, and
\Cref{thm:no-unconditional-printed-richardson-edge} prove that the printed names
alone do not provide those identities, while
\Cref{thm:printed-root-richardson-conditional-clause} gives exactly the optional
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) specialization.

For the second item, \Cref{lem:eml-real-witness-library} lists the
self-contained core witnesses, and
\Cref{thm:no-self-contained-concrete-richardson-eml-instance,thm:concrete-richardson-instance-exact-data}
identify the missing data for the stronger concrete Richardson instance.  The
main Richardson clause therefore remains the verified-triple implication rather
than a concrete finite-term construction.

For the third item, \Cref{lem:cover-preserves-protocol},
\Cref{thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication},
\Cref{thm:intrinsic-fold-asymptotics-split}, and
\Cref{thm:L2-cover-relative-closure} show that the bad-example construction
changes only auxiliary cover multiplicities over unchanged normal forms.  The
proof of \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness} therefore proves only the
cover-relative finite-state budget separation.

For the fourth item, \Cref{thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy} assumes
a readout into one free monogenic orbit with multiplication read by the ambient
product, and \Cref{prop:no-global-protocol-rigidity-input} records that the
argument does not classify arbitrary single-primitive protocols.  These four
checks cover every obstruction edge cited in the proof of
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}, so the main spine has no additional hidden stronger
premise or conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Richardson submission boundary]\label{cor:richardson-submission-boundary}
The paper proves a Richardson obstruction interface: once a finite EML triple is
specified and its three displayed real-domain identities are proved, Richardson's
undecidability theorem gives the transported EML normalizer obstruction.  It
does not construct such a triple for \(\pi\) and \(\sin\), and it does not assert
an instantiated printed-string theorem for \(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) unless
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) is assumed.  The interface statement is
\Cref{thm:separation}; the internal core library in
\Cref{lem:eml-real-witness-library} explicitly omits \(\pi\) and sine, and the
only printed-string specialization is isolated in
\Cref{thm:printed-root-richardson-conditional-clause}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Richardson obstruction interface]\label{thm:richardson-obstruction-interface}
The Richardson input to the main theorem is exactly the relative verified
interface of \Cref{thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem}: a finite pure-EML
triple with proved identities for \(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and the nonnegative
square root on \(\RR_{\ge0}\).  Under this interface, identity to zero for
Richardson expressions reduces effectively to equality with the translated zero
EML term, so a computable semantic normalizer would decide Richardson's
undecidable problem.  Without those three identities for a named finite triple,
including \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\), the paper asserts no concrete
printed-string Richardson instance.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is \Cref{thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem} in interface form.  The
proof there constructs the translation by structural recursion, applies
Richardson's undecidability theorem recorded in \Cref{lem:richardson-fragment},
and proves that finite printed names instantiate the result only after the same
three real-domain identities have been supplied for those names.
\end{proof}
