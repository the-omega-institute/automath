\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Universality statements can mean different things.  A single operation may
represent a large class of expressions, while still giving no effective way to
choose or compare representatives.  We separate three levels for this
phenomenon.  Level \(L0\), \emph{expression universality}, asks only that terms
exist.  Level \(L1\), \emph{protocol universality}, asks for computable normal
forms, compiled operations, and semantic readout identities.  Level \(L2\),
\emph{certificate universality}, adds finite certificates attached to the
fibres of a normalizing map.

The motivating \(L0\) example is Odrzywo{\l}ek's operation
\[
  \eml(x,y)=e^x-\operatorname{Log}y,
\]
with the constant \(1\), which represents the operations in a standard
scientific-calculator basis \cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator}.  We use
that public record only as a representative-existence theorem.  The positive
\(L1\) protocol in this paper is internal: Zeckendorf normal forms, valuation
transport through \(\NN\), and Church iteration give compiled addition and
multiplication on \(\NN\).  The main obstructions show that these two assertions
should not be conflated.

The Richardson obstruction is used only in a verified-triple form.  If a finite
pure EML triple \((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) is supplied with proved real-domain
identities
\[
  \mathsf P=\pi,
  \qquad \mathsf S(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad \mathsf R(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0}),
\]
then \Cref{thm:separation} gives the Richardson normalizer obstruction relative
to that verified interface.  No unconditional concrete EML terms for this triple
are printed or proved in the article.  The Proc. AMS claim is the verified
replacement-triple theorem, not an unconditional concrete pure-EML triple theorem
for \(\pi\), sine on all of \(\RR\), and square root on \(\RR_{\ge0}\).  The
identifiers \(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) are retained only as fixed strings in the
conditional specialization of this theorem, not as a load-bearing input to the
main hierarchy.

Scope is deliberately narrow.  This article proves the \(L0/L1/L2\) hierarchy,

thermodynamics, folded histogram laws, finite observation escape rates, or
normalization automata.

The assumption is deliberately not a theorem of this paper.  It records a
versioned external analytic assertion whose proof is not reproduced here.  Thus
all uses of Richardson below pass through the abstract
verified-triple hypothesis, and the printed names \(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) are
only an optional specialization if a reader accepts \((H_{\mathcal W})\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact printed-string Richardson cut]
\label{thm:intro-printed-richardson-cut}
In the unconditional main theorem chain, the Richardson obstruction is exactly
the verified-triple implication of \Cref{thm:separation}.  In particular this
article does not prove the concrete printed-string assertion
\[
  U_{47}=\pi,
  \qquad U_{111}(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad U_{126}(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0})
\]
in the principal-branch real-domain EML semantics.  The printed strings
\((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) instantiate the Richardson obstruction in this
article if and only if the three real-domain identities required of a verified
triple are added as a hypothesis, for example by \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  Their
finite printed syntax alone is not a proof of those identities and is not a
separate input to \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Richardson theorem used in the main chain is \Cref{thm:separation}.  Its
hypotheses are not merely three names: they are a specified finite pure EML triple
\((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) together with proofs that
\(\mathsf P=\pi\), \(\mathsf S(x)=\sin x\) on \(\RR\), and
\(\mathsf R(t)=\sqrt t\) on \(\RR_{\ge0}\) in the principal-branch semantics of
\Cref{def:eml}.  Once those hypotheses hold, \Cref{thm:separation} gives the
many-one Richardson reduction and the normalizer and compiler obstructions, so
every verified triple is a valid interface instance.

For the particular names \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\), the displayed concrete
assertion is exactly the semantic content required to turn those names into a
verified triple.  The only statement in this introduction that supplies that
content is the optional assumption \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  Without that assumption,
the displayed names are only fixed finite strings from a public record; finite
syntax does not imply the principal-branch real-domain identities demanded by
\Cref{thm:separation}.  Therefore deleting \((H_{\mathcal W})\) deletes only the
optional printed-string specialization and leaves the universal verified-triple
Richardson obstruction unchanged.  This is the Richardson branch cited by
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Obstruction spine]
\label{thm:obstruction-spine}
The hierarchy has two primary obstructions and one subordinate certificate
addendum.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A verified finite EML triple for \(\pi\), sine, and nonnegative square root
  transports Richardson's identity problem into pure EML terms, giving a
  many-one zero-test obstruction and hence ruling out computable semantic
  normalizers and equality-reflecting compilers on that translated fragment.
  This is an interface theorem, not an unconditional printed-string instantiation.
\item Multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be read injectively from the
  ambient product on a single free monogenic orbit; every such readout factors
  through a prime-weight exponent skeleton, and the faithful restrictions are
  exactly the trivial monoid and a single-prime-power ray.
\item Finite-fibre certificates give Hankel positivity and finite Prony
  reconstruction at each fixed resolution, but, in the computable
  finite-fibre cover category over the fixed Zeckendorf normal forms, they do
  not force a uniform finite-state transfer formula in the resolution
  variable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the interface theorem \Cref{thm:separation}, with the
positive/nonnegative square-root boundary recorded in
\Cref{thm:positive-core-nonnegative-root-boundary,thm:no-core-only-richardson-translation}
and the non-instantiation boundary recorded in
\Cref{thm:largest-verified-richardson-fragment,thm:maximal-unconditional-richardson-package,thm:finite-eml-richardson-trichotomy,thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance,thm:richardson-separation-conditional-status}.
The formal replacement of any printed-string reading by the universal verified
interface is
\Cref{thm:intro-printed-richardson-cut,thm:main-chain-richardson-replacement-principle}.
The second is
\Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, with the skeleton and sharp faithful-submonoid
classification in
\Cref{lem:level-zero-exponent-skeleton,thm:one-orbit-readout-classification,thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification,thm:finite-rank-product-readout-lower-bound}.
The third is the finite-fibre/finite-state bridge and cover-relative statement
\Cref{thm:L2-terminology-bridge,thm:L2-finite-window-transfer-obstruction,thm:L2-subordinate-cover-classification,thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication,thm:L2-finite-fiber-no-transfer-content,thm:L2-one-spike-skeleton,thm:finite-state-strictness,thm:main-theorem-scope-projection},
whose proof changes only certificate multiplicities above the fixed normal
forms and makes no intrinsic assertion about the original \(\Fold_m\) fibres.
The three proofs use disjoint data: the Richardson witness interface, the free
monogenic orbit, and computable finite covers, respectively.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Single-primitive hierarchy]\label{thm:main-hierarchy}
With the definitions of \(L0\), \(L1\), and \(L2\) in
\Cref{sec:L0,sec:L1,sec:L2}, the following hierarchy holds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The pair \((\eml,1)\) has the cited \(L0\) representative-existence property
  for the pinned public \(\mathcal B_{36}\) calculator basis on its stated
  principal-branch domains.  This is only an external expression-existence
  anchor, not an equality algorithm, normal form, compiler, or Richardson witness
  package.
\item Zeckendorf normal forms and valuation transport give an internal \(L1\)
  protocol for the operations \(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).
\item For every finite pure EML triple \((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) whose
  identities \(\mathsf P=\pi\), \(\mathsf S(x)=\sin x\) on \(\RR\), and
  \(\mathsf R(t)=\sqrt t\) on \(\RR_{\ge0}\) have been proved, Richardson's
  identity-to-zero problem many-one reduces to zero-identity in the associated
  pure EML fragment.  Hence that fragment has neither a computable semantic
  normalizer into finite decidable objects nor an equality-reflecting semantic
  compiler into finite decidable protocol objects.  The printed strings
  \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) enter only under the optional hypothesis
  \((H_{\mathcal W})\); they are not an unconditional concrete instance.
\item Multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be injectively read from the ambient
  product on one free monogenic orbit.  In that model every readout factors
  through the prime-weight exponent skeleton, and the faithful restrictions are
  exactly the trivial monoid and a single-prime-power ray; finite ambient-product
  rank \(d\) carries at most \(d\) independent prime generators.
\item The finite-fibre \(L2\) layer gives fixed-resolution Hankel--Prony
  certificates, but the strict finite-state transfer separation proved here is
  cover-relative: in the computable finite-fibre cover category over the fixed
  Zeckendorf normal-form sets, such certificates do not force a uniform
  finite-state transfer formula.  No intrinsic asymptotic failure theorem for the
  original \(\Fold_m\) fibre multiplicities is asserted.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The \(L0\) assertion is exactly the pinned external anchor
\Cref{thm:eml-universality}, with its domain and dependency boundary fixed by
\Cref{thm:L0-citation-domain-support,thm:L0-external-anchor-criterion,thm:B36-import-exact-scope}.
The \(L1\) assertion is the Zeckendorf protocol theorem
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}.  The Richardson assertion is
\Cref{thm:separation}, proved from the verified replacement-triple interface
\Cref{thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface}; the exclusion of an
unconditional printed-string instance is
\Cref{thm:intro-printed-richardson-cut,thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance,thm:no-unconditional-printed-richardson-edge}.
The one-orbit and finite-rank product obstructions are
\Cref{thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy,thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification,thm:finite-rank-product-readout-lower-bound}.
Finally, the fixed-resolution certificate facts and the cover-relative
finite-state separation are
\Cref{thm:L2-finite-window-transfer-obstruction,thm:L2-subordinate-cover-classification,thm:L2-cover-relative-closure,thm:finite-state-strictness},
while \Cref{thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication} excludes interpreting the
cover construction as an intrinsic statement about the original \(\Fold_m\)
fibres.  These results prove the five clauses and show that the three strictness
mechanisms are independent of any stronger hidden \(L0\) normalizer or compiler.
\end{proof}

\begin{dependencyremark}[Scoped strictness gate]
\label{thm:scoped-strictness-gate}

quantifier cuts.  The Richardson obstruction is universal over verified finite
pure EML triples and becomes a printed-string instance only after the three

for the intrinsic fibre multiplicities of the unmodified maps \(\Fold_m\).  These

\end{dependencyremark}

\begin{theorem}[Subordinate finite-fibre addendum]\label{cor:main-L2-addendum}
The \(L2\) part of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} is a subordinate addendum to the
primary \(L0/L1\) separation and one-orbit obstruction.  More precisely, deleting
the \(L2\) clause leaves unchanged the imported \(L0\) representative statement,
the internal Zeckendorf \(L1\) protocol, the verified-triple Richardson
normalizer obstruction, and the one-orbit multiplication obstruction.  The only
strictness added by \(L2\) is the cover-relative finite-state growth obstruction
for computable finite-fibre covers over the fixed normal-form sets; Hankel
positivity and Prony reconstruction remain fixed-resolution certificate facts
and are not proof inputs to the Richardson or one-orbit clauses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first four clauses of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} are proved from
\Cref{thm:eml-universality,thm:L0-external-anchor-criterion,thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ,thm:separation,thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface,thm:printed-string-real-domain-upgrade-criterion,thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy,thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification}.
None of these cited statements has a finite-fibre hypothesis.  Conversely, the
finite-fibre certificate facts begin by applying
\Cref{prop:classical-finite-atomic-skeleton} to the fibre-size multiset at each
resolution, and this uses neither Richardson expressions nor the one-orbit
readout.  The absence of any forced finite-state transfer is exactly the cover
theorem \Cref{thm:L2-finite-fiber-no-transfer-content,thm:L2-finite-window-transfer-obstruction}, and the main strictness
clause is its one-spike specialization \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}; both
leave the original \(\Fold_m\) fibres unchanged.  Thus removing the \(L2\)
addendum removes only that cover-relative budget obstruction and none of the
primary separation machinery.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Main-spine scope closure]
\label{thm:main-spine-scope-closure}
The theorem chain of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} is closed exactly under the
following five interfaces.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The \(L0\) expression-universality clause uses the public
  \(\mathcal B_{36}\) statement only as the cited representative-existence
  anchor \Cref{thm:eml-universality}.  The self-contained EML input needed by
  the later proof graph is the smaller core of
  \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}; a term-by-term audited table of all
  thirty-six representatives is neither assumed nor proved here.
\item The Richardson obstruction is the universal verified-triple implication of
  \Cref{thm:separation,thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface,thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem}.  The named
  strings \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) enter that implication only under the
  optional hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\); without that hypothesis they are fixed
  finite strings, not proved representatives of \(\pi\), sine, and nonnegative
  square root.
\item The internal EML core used unconditionally in the proof supplies arithmetic,
  division on its nonzero domain, \(\log 2\), and positive square root.  It does
  not supply the stronger concrete Richardson triple for \(\pi\), sine on all of
  \(\RR\), and square root at \(0\).
\item The finite-state transfer strictness result is existential and
  cover-relative: it concerns computable finite-fibre covers over the fixed
  Zeckendorf normal-form sets.  It gives no recurrence, nonrecurrence,
  rationality, growth, or finite-state-transfer classification for the
  unmodified fibres of \(\Fold_m\).
\item The multiplication obstruction is the one-free-monogenic-orbit obstruction.
  It is not a global rigidity theorem for every single-primitive protocol
  realizing \(\{+,\times\}\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the displayed hierarchy theorem is the cited-\(L0\),
verified-triple, internal-Zeckendorf, cover-relative finite-fibre theorem stated
in this article, and none of the five stronger readings above is a premise or
conclusion of the main spine.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the first item, \Cref{thm:eml-universality} is explicitly a citation to the
public representative-existence assertion for \(\mathcal B_{36}\), while
\Cref{thm:B36-import-exact-scope} and
\Cref{thm:B36-self-contained-replacement-core} prove the exact import boundary:
the later Richardson, \(L1\), \(L2\), and one-orbit arguments remain valid after
replacing the full \(\mathcal B_{36}\) sentence by the smaller internal core
proved in \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}.  Thus a self-contained
term-by-term table for all thirty-six representatives is not a hidden input to
the main hierarchy.

For the second item, \Cref{thm:separation} and
\Cref{thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface} are stated with an arbitrary
finite pure EML triple only after the three required real-domain identities have
been proved.  \Cref{thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance},
\Cref{thm:root-name-erasure-unconditional-separation}, and
\Cref{thm:no-unconditional-printed-richardson-edge} prove that the printed names
alone do not provide those identities, while
\Cref{thm:printed-root-richardson-conditional-clause} gives exactly the optional
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) specialization.

For the third item, \Cref{lem:eml-real-witness-library} lists the
self-contained core witnesses, and
\Cref{thm:no-self-contained-concrete-richardson-eml-instance,thm:concrete-richardson-instance-exact-data}
identify the missing data for the stronger concrete Richardson instance.  The
main Richardson clause therefore remains the verified-triple implication rather
than a concrete finite-term construction.

For the fourth item, \Cref{lem:cover-preserves-protocol},
\Cref{thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication},
\Cref{thm:intrinsic-fold-asymptotics-split}, and
\Cref{thm:L2-cover-relative-closure} show that the bad-example construction
changes only auxiliary cover multiplicities over unchanged normal forms.  The
proof of \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness} therefore proves only the
cover-relative finite-state budget separation.

For the fifth item, \Cref{thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy} assumes
a readout into one free monogenic orbit with multiplication read by the ambient
product, and \Cref{prop:no-global-protocol-rigidity-input} records that the
argument does not classify arbitrary single-primitive protocols.  These five
checks cover every obstruction edge cited in the proof of
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}, so the main spine has no additional hidden stronger
premise or conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Scope projection for the hierarchy theorem]
\label{thm:main-theorem-scope-projection}
Let \(\mathcal H\) denote the theorem chain asserted in
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.  Project the three potentially stronger readings of
that chain as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item replace any self-contained reading of the full \(\mathcal B_{36}\) table by
  either the cited representative-existence anchor \Cref{thm:eml-universality} or
  the smaller internal core \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses};
\item replace any concrete printed-string Richardson instance by the universal
  verified-triple implication of \Cref{thm:separation}, using
  \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) only under \((H_{\mathcal W})\); and
\item read every finite-state transfer obstruction as a statement about
  computable finite-fibre cover moments over the fixed Zeckendorf normal-form
  sets \(X_m\), not about the intrinsic multiplicities of the unmodified maps
  \(\Fold_m\).
\end{enumerate}
After this projection, every unconditional positive construction and every
unconditional obstruction in \(\mathcal H\) is still proved in the article.  The
three unprojected stronger readings respectively require extra data not supplied
by \(\mathcal H\): a term-by-term audited \(\mathcal B_{36}\) derivation, the
three real-domain identities for the named printed Richardson strings, or a
comparison theorem from cover moments to intrinsic \(\Fold_m\) moments.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the \(L0\) clause, \Cref{thm:eml-universality} imports only finite
representative existence for the displayed calculator basis.  The proof of
\Cref{thm:B36-import-exact-scope} identifies this as a citation-dependent
existence input and excludes a self-contained table, a normalizer, and a
Richardson interface.  On the other hand,
\Cref{thm:B36-self-contained-replacement-core} checks every later use of EML data
in the hierarchy proof and shows that the internal witnesses of
\Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses} suffice for those uses.  Therefore the
projected \(L0\) input leaves the later theorem chain unchanged, while the
unprojected self-contained \(\mathcal B_{36}\) reading would require the missing
term-by-term derivation.

For the Richardson clause, \Cref{thm:separation} is stated for an arbitrary
finite pure EML triple only after the three real-domain identities for \(\pi\),
sine on \(\RR\), and nonnegative square root on \(\RR_{\ge0}\) have been proved.
\Cref{thm:main-chain-richardson-replacement-principle} identifies this universal
verified-triple implication with the Richardson clause of \(\mathcal H\).  The
named strings \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) become an instance exactly when those
same identities are added for them; by \Cref{ass:HW} and
\Cref{thm:printed-root-richardson-conditional-clause}, that addition is the
optional hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  Without it,
\Cref{thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance,thm:root-name-erasure-unconditional-separation,thm:no-unconditional-printed-richardson-edge}
give the required cut: the printed names are finite syntax but not a verified
interface.  Hence the projected Richardson statement is exactly the theorem
proved unconditionally.

For the \(L2\) clause, the finite certificates in \Cref{sec:L2} are
fixed-resolution fibre data.  The finite-state obstruction is supplied by the
cover construction in \Cref{thm:L2-finite-fiber-no-transfer-content} and the
closure theorem \Cref{thm:L2-cover-relative-closure}; both work with computable
positive cover-size functions over the fixed sets \(X_m\).  The projection to
normal forms and all \(L1\) readouts are unchanged by
\Cref{lem:cover-preserves-protocol}.  Finally,
\Cref{thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication,thm:L2-two-cover-intrinsic-boundary}
show that cover transfer behaviour is not determined by the unmodified
\(\Fold_m\) fibres: two covers of the same intrinsic data can have opposite
finite-state-transfer behaviour.  Therefore an intrinsic \(\Fold_m\) transfer
classification would require an additional bridge identifying or comparing the
intrinsic moment sequence with the chosen cover moment sequence.

The three paragraphs cover all occurrences of the cited \(L0\) anchor, the
Richardson interface, and the finite-state cover obstruction in the proof of
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.  Thus the projected theorem chain proves the same
unconditional hierarchy assertions while precisely isolating the additional data
needed for the three stronger readings.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Audit-complete replacement spine]
\label{thm:audit-complete-replacement-spine}
Let \(\mathcal S\) be the main theorem spine consisting of
\[
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy,thm:eml-universality,thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface,thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy,thm:finite-state-strictness}.
\]
Form \(\mathcal S_{0}\) from \(\mathcal S\) by making the following three
replacements simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}
\item replace the imported \(\mathcal B_{36}\) representative-existence anchor by
  the self-contained core EML package of
  \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses};
\item delete the optional hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\) and all unconditional
  substitutions of the printed names \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) for a verified
  Richardson triple; and
\item read every finite-state strictness assertion only in the computable
  finite-fibre cover category over the fixed Zeckendorf normal-form sets.
\end{enumerate}
Then every unconditional obstruction and every positive protocol conclusion used
by \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} remains valid in \(\mathcal S_{0}\).  Conversely,
the three stronger assertions excluded in the scope audit--an unconditional
printed-string Richardson triple for \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\), a
self-contained audited table of all thirty-six \(\mathcal B_{36}\)
representatives, and an intrinsic finite-state transfer classification for the
original \(\Fold_m\) fibres--are not consequences of \(\mathcal S_{0}\) and are
not premises of \(\mathcal S\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first replacement preserves the later proof graph by
\Cref{thm:B36-self-contained-replacement-core}: its proof lists every later use
of \(L0\) data and checks that the internal core witnesses of
\Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses} suffice for the Richardson interface
bookkeeping, while the Zeckendorf protocol, finite-fibre covers, and one-orbit
obstruction use no calculator-basis representatives.  Hence the full
\(\mathcal B_{36}\) import may be removed from the load-bearing spine without
altering the later conclusions, except that the article no longer asserts the
global calculator-basis existence sentence.  The excluded self-contained
term-by-term table is not recovered by this replacement because
\Cref{thm:B36-import-exact-scope} proves that the public import supplies only
representative existence in this manuscript.

The second replacement preserves the Richardson obstruction by
\Cref{thm:main-chain-richardson-replacement-principle}.  That theorem identifies
the unconditional Richardson clause with the universal implication from a
separately verified triple
\((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) satisfying the three real-domain identities.
After \((H_{\mathcal W})\) is deleted, the printed names
\((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) have only syntactic status by
\Cref{thm:no-unconditional-printed-root-richardson-instance,thm:root-name-erasure-unconditional-separation,thm:no-unconditional-printed-richardson-edge}.
Thus the verified-triple Richardson normalizer obstruction remains, while the
unconditional concrete printed-string triple is not obtained.

The third replacement is exactly the content of
\Cref{thm:L2-cover-relative-closure,thm:finite-state-strictness}.  The bad
example modifies only the auxiliary cover multiplicities above the unchanged
normal-form sets, and \Cref{thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication} proves that a
transfer statement for those cover moments has no consequence for the intrinsic
moments of the unmodified \(\Fold_m\) fibres without an additional comparison
theorem.  Hence the cover-relative finite-state budget obstruction remains, but
no intrinsic recurrence, rationality, growth, or finite-state-transfer
classification is produced.

Combining the three paragraphs shows that \(\mathcal S_{0}\) still proves every
unconditional positive or negative claim used by \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.  The
same paragraphs also identify exactly the extra hypotheses or data needed for
the three stronger readings, so those readings are neither premises of the
original spine nor consequences of the replacement spine.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Richardson submission boundary]\label{cor:richardson-submission-boundary}
The paper proves a Richardson obstruction interface: once a finite EML triple is
specified and its three displayed real-domain identities are proved, Richardson's
undecidability theorem gives the transported EML normalizer obstruction.  It
does not construct such a triple for \(\pi\) and \(\sin\), and it does not assert
an instantiated printed-string theorem for \(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) unless
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) is assumed.  The interface statement is
\Cref{thm:separation}; the internal core library in
\Cref{lem:eml-real-witness-library} explicitly omits \(\pi\) and sine, and the
only printed-string specialization is isolated in
\Cref{thm:printed-root-richardson-conditional-clause}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Richardson obstruction interface]\label{thm:richardson-obstruction-interface}
The Richardson input to the main theorem is exactly the relative verified
interface of \Cref{thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem}: a finite pure-EML
triple with proved identities for \(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and the nonnegative
square root on \(\RR_{\ge0}\).  Under this interface, identity to zero for
Richardson expressions reduces effectively to equality with the translated zero
EML term, so a computable semantic normalizer would decide Richardson's
undecidable problem.  Without those three identities for a named finite triple,
including \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\), the paper asserts no concrete
printed-string Richardson instance.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is \Cref{thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem} in interface form.  The
proof there constructs the translation by structural recursion, applies
Richardson's undecidability theorem recorded in \Cref{lem:richardson-fragment},
and proves that finite printed names instantiate the result only after the same
three real-domain identities have been supplied for those names.
\end{proof}
