\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Universality statements for a single binary primitive split into different
assertions.  We use three levels.  Level \(L0\), \emph{expression universality},
asserts existence of terms.  Level \(L1\), \emph{protocol universality}, asserts
computable normal forms, compiled operations, and semantic readout identities.
Level \(L2\), \emph{certificate universality}, adds finite certificates over the
fibres of a normalizing map.

The motivating \(L0\) primitive is Odrzywo{\l}ek's operation
\[
  \eml(x,y)=e^x-\operatorname{Log}y
\]
with the constant \(1\), cited from the public arXiv v2 record
\cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator}.  We use that record only as a pinned
representative-existence theorem for its displayed scientific-calculator basis,
not as a normal form or equality algorithm for EML terms.  The positive \(L1\)
protocol is internal: Zeckendorf normal forms, valuation transport through
\(\NN\), and Church iteration give compiled addition and multiplication on
\(\NN\).

The Richardson obstruction is stated in Richardson's punctured absolute-value
form.  It is conditional on supplied finite pure EML witnesses for \(\pi\) and
sine, and uses the self-contained positive-root term only through
\[
  \Abs(u)=G(M(u,u))=|u|\qquad(u\ne0).
\]
This is exactly Richardson's condition that the absolute-value symbol agree with
\(|u|\) away from zero.  Hence \Cref{thm:separation} gives, for every supplied
finite triple with proved identities for \(\pi\), sine, and punctured absolute
value, a pure EML Richardson fragment with no computable semantic normalizer into
finite decidable objects and no equality-reflecting compiler into finite
decidable protocol objects.  The endpoint square-root string \(U_{126}\) is not
part of this punctured obstruction.

For submission scope, the paper deliberately does not claim a theory of
Zeckendorf fibre thermodynamics, folded histogram laws, finite-observation
escape rates, or normalization automata.  The finite-fibre and cover results
below attach auxiliary certificate data to the fixed normal-form sets used by the
\(L1\) protocol; they are not asymptotic laws for the original Zeckendorf fold
fibres and are not presented as a digit-local normalization machine.

\begin{theorem}[Obstruction spine]
\label{thm:obstruction-spine}

  \Cref{thm:separation,thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem,thm:relative-richardson-main-spine-closure};

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

punctured triple-interface theorem
\Cref{thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface} and its Proc. AMS formulation
\Cref{thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem}.  Those theorems use supplied
proved rows for \(\pi\) and sine, and the internally proved punctured
absolute-value row when \(\mathsf A=\Abs\).
The second item is \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, refined by the exponent
skeleton and faithful-submonoid classification.  The third item is
\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}; it is formulated for computable finite-fibre
covers over the fixed Zeckendorf normal-form sets and uses only the fixed
finite-state transfer notion and budget of
\Cref{def:fixed-finite-state-transfer,prop:fixed-transfer-interface-budget}.
The final sentence follows from
\Cref{prop:printed-witness-certificate-boundary,prop:printed-pi-sine-instantiation-criterion,thm:concrete-richardson-instance-exact-data}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Proc. AMS obstruction spine]\label{thm:proc-ams-core-obstruction-spine}
The submission-facing strictness spine consists of exactly two primary
obstructions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every finite pure EML punctured witness package
  \((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf A)\), with
  \(\mathsf P=\pi\), \(\mathsf S(x)=\sin x\) on \(\RR\), and
  \(\mathsf A(x)=|x|\) on \(\RR\setminus\{0\}\), the translated Richardson fragment
  has no zero-identity algorithm, no computable semantic normalizer into finite
  objects with decidable equality, and no equality-reflecting semantic compiler
  into finite decidable protocol objects.
\item Multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be faithfully read at level zero
  from the ambient product on one free monogenic orbit.  More sharply, every
  one-orbit readout factors through a prime-weight exponent sum, and its faithful
  restrictions are exactly the trivial monoid and single-prime-power rays.
\end{enumerate}
The endpoint-root string \(U_{126}\), the full
\(\mathcal B_{36}\) representative table, and the cover-relative \(L2\)
finite-state addendum are not primary obstructions in this spine.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first item is the triple-relative punctured Richardson theorem
\Cref{thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem}, equivalently
\Cref{thm:separation}.  Its proof is the effective structural translation of
\Cref{lem:richardson-translation} followed by
Richardson's identity-to-zero theorem \Cref{lem:richardson-fragment}; the
normalizer and compiler exclusions are the standard decidability consequences
spelled out in \Cref{prop:L1-compiles-L0}.  The endpoint-root boundary is exactly
\Cref{thm:submission-richardson-dichotomy}.

The second item is \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}.  The factorization through
prime weights is \Cref{lem:level-zero-exponent-skeleton}, and the sharp faithful
restriction statement is \Cref{thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification}.
These arguments use only the one-free-monogenic-orbit and ambient-product readout
hypotheses.  The final sentence follows because the cited \(\mathcal B_{36}\)
anchor supplies only \(L0\) representative existence, the named Richardson strings
instantiate the first item only after the three identities are proved, and the
finite-state cover theorem \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness} is formulated as a
subordinate cover-category addendum rather than as one of the two primary
obstructions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Punctured Richardson and cover-relative single-primitive hierarchy]\label{thm:main-hierarchy}
With the definitions of \(L0\), \(L1\), and \(L2\) in
\Cref{sec:L0,sec:L1,sec:L2}, the following hierarchy holds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The pair \((\eml,1)\) has the cited \(L0\) representative-existence property
  for the pinned public \(\mathcal B_{36}\) calculator basis on its stated
  principal-branch domains.  This is only an external expression-existence
  anchor, not an equality algorithm, normal form, compiler, or Richardson witness
  package.
\item Zeckendorf normal forms and valuation transport give an internal \(L1\)
  protocol for the operations \(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).
\item The primary strictness statements are the two obstructions of
  \Cref{thm:proc-ams-core-obstruction-spine}: the punctured Richardson
  normalizer obstruction and the one-free-monogenic-orbit multiplication
  obstruction.  The Richardson clause is relative to proved finite rows for
  \(\pi\), sine, and punctured absolute value; the endpoint-root string
  \(U_{126}\) does not enter this obstruction.  The multiplication clause is exactly
  the one-orbit readout theorem and its prime-weight faithful-submonoid
  classification.
\item The finite-fibre \(L2\) layer gives fixed-resolution Hankel--Prony
  certificates.  Its strict finite-state transfer separation is a subordinate
  cover-relative addendum: in the computable finite-fibre cover category over the
  fixed Zeckendorf normal-form sets, such certificates do not force a uniform
  finite-state transfer formula.  The obstruction is witnessed sharply by two
  covers over the same unmodified data \((X_m,\Fold_m)\), one finite-state and
  one not, so no intrinsic asymptotic failure theorem for the original
  \(\Fold_m\) fibre multiplicities is asserted.
\item After deleting the unprinted \(\mathcal B_{36}\) representative table and the
  optional printed-string hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\), the internal core
  witness package, the triple-relative punctured Richardson implication, the
  Zeckendorf protocol, the one-orbit obstruction, and the cover-relative \(L2\)
  obstruction remain proved.  What is removed is the concrete printed
  \(U_{47},U_{111}\) instantiation, the global calculator-basis headline, and any
  endpoint-root specialization.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The \(L0\) assertion is exactly the pinned external anchor
\Cref{thm:eml-universality}, with its domain and dependency boundary fixed by
\Cref{thm:L0-citation-domain-support,thm:L0-external-anchor-criterion,thm:B36-import-exact-scope}.
The \(L1\) assertion is the Zeckendorf protocol theorem
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}.  The primary strictness assertions are
\Cref{thm:proc-ams-core-obstruction-spine}: its Richardson part is
\Cref{thm:separation}, proved from the punctured triple interface
  \Cref{thm:replacement-triple-richardson-interface,thm:relative-richardson-main-spine-closure},
  with the endpoint-root boundary isolated in
  \Cref{thm:submission-richardson-dichotomy,thm:concrete-richardson-instance-exact-data}; its one-orbit part is
\Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction,thm:one-orbit-readout-classification,thm:one-orbit-faithful-submonoid-classification}.
Finally, the fixed-resolution certificate facts and the subordinate cover-relative
finite-state separation are
\Cref{thm:L2-finite-window-transfer-obstruction,thm:L2-subordinate-cover-classification,thm:L2-cover-relative-closure,thm:finite-state-strictness},
with the two-cover sharpness supplied by \Cref{thm:L2-two-cover-intrinsic-boundary},
while \Cref{thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication} excludes interpreting the
cover example as an intrinsic asymptotic theorem for the original fibres.  The
last item is the dependency-cut theorem
\Cref{thm:load-bearing-core-dependency-cut}; it proves that replacing the full
  calculator-basis import by the self-contained core preserves the stated
  internal theorem chain and removes only the advertised optional headline.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact scope closure of the main hierarchy]
\label{thm:main-hierarchy-exact-scope-closure}
Every strictness conclusion used by \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} factors through one
of the following four explicit interfaces, and through no stronger hidden
interface:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the cited \(L0\) representative-existence anchor
  \Cref{thm:eml-universality,thm:B36-import-exact-scope}, which imports the
  public \(\mathcal B_{36}\) existence sentence but no table, normal form,
  equality algorithm, semantic compiler, or Richardson witness identity;
\item the punctured Richardson interface
  \Cref{thm:separation,thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem}, whose
  witnesses prove \(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and \(|x|\) on
  \(\RR\setminus\{0\}\);
\item the one-free-monogenic-orbit ambient-product interface
  \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction,thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy},
  not a classification of arbitrary single-primitive protocols realizing
  \(\{+,\times\}\);
\item the computable finite-fibre cover interface
  \Cref{def:computable-cover,thm:finite-state-strictness}, not an intrinsic
  recurrence, nonrecurrence, rationality, or finite-state-transfer theorem for
  the unmodified \(\Fold_m\) fibre multiplicities.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the submission theorem remains valid after deleting the unprinted
term-by-term \(\mathcal B_{36}\) table, deleting the optional named-string
Richardson hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\), and deleting any proposed global
protocol-rigidity or intrinsic \(\Fold_m\)-asymptotic conclusion; what remains is
exactly the relative punctured Richardson obstruction, the internal
Zeckendorf \(L1\) protocol, the one-orbit multiplication obstruction, and the
cover-relative \(L2\) finite-state obstruction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first interface is isolated in \Cref{thm:eml-universality} and made exact by
\Cref{thm:B36-import-exact-scope}: the cited input is representative existence
for the public \(\mathcal B_{36}\) basis on the source's branch conventions.
\Cref{thm:B36-self-contained-replacement-core,thm:load-bearing-core-dependency-cut}
then show that the later Richardson, Zeckendorf, one-orbit, and finite-cover
arguments use only the internal core witnesses once the global calculator-basis
headline is removed.  Hence no later strictness proof depends on a printed
thirty-six-row table or on a self-contained proof of that whole table.

The second interface is exactly the punctured hypothesis of \Cref{thm:separation}:
finite witnesses for \(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and \(|x|\) on
\(\RR\setminus\{0\}\).  \Cref{thm:submission-richardson-dichotomy} proves that the
endpoint-root string \(U_{126}\) is not part of this interface.  Deleting
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) removes the printed \(U_{47},U_{111}\) instantiation but
leaves the relative implication for any replacement pair whose \(\pi\) and sine
identities have been proved.

The third interface is the one-free-monogenic-orbit obstruction.  By
\Cref{thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy,thm:main-chain-protocol-rigidity-boundary},
the negative multiplication statement assumes a free monogenic orbit and asks
that multiplication be read by the ambient product on that same orbit.  Its proof
uses the prime-weight exponent skeleton
\Cref{lem:level-zero-exponent-skeleton}; without those hypotheses the skeleton is
not available, and the paper asserts only the positive Zeckendorf protocol of
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}.  Thus no global rigidity theorem is a hidden
input to \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.

The fourth interface is the computable-cover category.  \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}
constructs covers over the fixed normal-form sets and proves failure of the fixed
finite-state transfer budget for a cover moment.  \Cref{thm:L2-cover-relative-closure}
and \Cref{thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication} state the formal boundary: the
cover moment is auxiliary certificate data and does not imply any transfer or
non-transfer theorem for the intrinsic multiplicity sequence of the original
maps \(\Fold_m\).  Thus deleting intrinsic \(\Fold_m\)-asymptotic claims changes
no proved assertion in the hierarchy.  Combining the four paragraphs gives the
claimed exact scope closure.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact instantiated strictness interface]
\label{thm:exact-instantiated-strictness-interface}
The strictness conclusions asserted in \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} have the
following exact instantiated content.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The Richardson obstruction is an unconditional theorem for every supplied
  finite pure EML triple \((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf A)\) satisfying
  \(\mathsf P=\pi\), \(\mathsf S(x)=\sin x\) for all real \(x\), and
  \(\mathsf A(x)=|x|\) for all nonzero real \(x\).  The article supplies the
  punctured absolute-value row internally by taking \(\mathsf A=\Abs\).
\item The exact printed strings \((U_{47},U_{111})\) instantiate the first two
  rows of that triple if and only if their real-domain identities are supplied;
  in this manuscript that supply is precisely the optional hypothesis
  \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  Without \((H_{\mathcal W})\), no printed
  \(U_{47}\)/\(U_{111}\) Richardson specialization is part of the theorem.
\item The endpoint square-root string \(U_{126}\) is not a premise, witness, or
  consequence of the Richardson obstruction used here.  Replacing the punctured
  row \(\mathsf A(x)=|x|\) for \(x\ne0\) by a proved endpoint identity for a
  nonnegative square-root string would be an additional specialization, not a
  result of the present proof.
\item The protocol obstruction used by the hierarchy is exactly the
  one-free-monogenic-orbit ambient-product obstruction.  It gives no global
  rigidity or classification theorem for all single-primitive protocols
  realizing \(\{+,\times\}\).
\end{enumerate}
Thus the paper's submission-facing theorem is the relative punctured Richardson
obstruction together with the internal Zeckendorf \(L1\) protocol, the restricted
one-orbit multiplication obstruction, and the cover-relative \(L2\) addendum; it
does not hide an unconditional printed \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) package or a
global protocol-rigidity theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For item~(1), \Cref{thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem}, equivalently
\Cref{thm:separation}, assumes exactly the three displayed real-domain
identities and proves the zero-identity, finite-normalizer, and finite-protocol
compiler obstructions for the resulting translated EML fragment.  The third row
with \(\mathsf A=\Abs\) is supplied by the internal positive-core construction in
\Cref{thm:eml-punctured-richardson-core}; its proof gives
\(\Abs(u)=|u|\) only for \(u\ne0\), which is exactly Richardson's punctured
condition.

For item~(2), \Cref{prop:printed-pi-sine-instantiation-criterion} proves the
if-and-only-if statement for the exact printed strings: the catalogue and parser
certify finite EML syntax, while the real-domain equations
\(U_{47}=\pi\) and \(U_{111}(x)=\sin x\) are precisely the semantic input required
to insert those strings into the relative theorem.  \Cref{ass:HW} is the only
place where this manuscript supplies that input for the printed rows, so deleting
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) deletes only the named printed specialization and not the
relative theorem for any other proved pair.

For item~(3), the proof of \Cref{thm:separation} is mediated through
\Cref{prop:three-witness-stability}: the third witness is required to agree with
\(|x|\) away from zero, and no value at zero is used.  \Cref{thm:submission-richardson-dichotomy}
records the resulting endpoint boundary, and
\Cref{thm:no-unconditional-printed-richardson-edge} identifies any endpoint-root
specialization as extra data rather than a consequence of the punctured
interface.  Hence \(U_{126}\) cannot enter the proved obstruction unless a
separate endpoint verification is added outside the present argument.

For item~(4), the negative multiplication input is
\Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, refined by
\Cref{thm:restricted-level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}.  Both theorems assume a
single free monogenic orbit and ambient-product multiplication readout, and their
proofs pass through the exponent skeleton
\Cref{lem:level-zero-exponent-skeleton}.  \Cref{thm:main-chain-protocol-rigidity-boundary}
then proves that the hierarchy invokes no broader classification; the positive
construction outside that restricted obstruction is the value-transported
Zeckendorf protocol \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}.  These four verifications
are exactly the interfaces listed in \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy-exact-scope-closure},
so the final sentence follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Main-spine scope projection]
\label{thm:main-spine-scope-closure}
\label{thm:main-theorem-scope-projection}
The theorem chain of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} is the image of the following
five load-bearing interfaces, and no larger interface is used:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the cited \(L0\) representative-existence anchor for the public
  \(\mathcal B_{36}\) basis;
\item the value-transported Zeckendorf \(L1\) protocol;
\item the punctured Richardson obstruction for supplied finite EML triples whose
  three real-domain identities have already been proved;
\item the one-free-monogenic-orbit multiplication obstruction; and
\item the cover-relative finite-fibre/finite-state separation over the fixed
  normal-form sets \(X_m\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently any attempted strengthening of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} by adding
one of the following conclusions is not a consequence of the paper's proof
chain: a term-by-term self-contained \(\mathcal B_{36}\) catalogue, an
unconditional endpoint-root Richardson triple for \(U_{126}\), an
intrinsic finite-state theorem for the original \(\Fold_m\) fibres, a
bounded-window or digit-local Zeckendorf arithmetic theorem, or a global rigidity
theorem for all single-primitive protocols realizing \(\{+,\times\}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each positive or negative assertion in \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} is proved by one
of the labels cited in its proof.  Item~(1) is exactly the external anchor
\Cref{thm:eml-universality}, and its proof boundary is fixed by
\Cref{thm:B36-import-exact-scope,thm:L0-citation-domain-support}; those results
state representative existence only and do not print or verify the whole table.
Item~(2) is \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}, together with
\Cref{prop:value-transport-exact-interface,cor:value-transported-minimal-witness},
which identify the operations as decode--compute--encode transports through
\(\Val\) rather than local digit algorithms.  Item~(3) is precisely
\Cref{thm:separation,thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem,thm:richardson-triple-submission-alternative}:
the proof uses the finite punctured witness package and, by
\Cref{prop:printed-pi-sine-instantiation-criterion,thm:concrete-richardson-instance-exact-data,thm:no-unconditional-printed-richardson-edge},
does not use the endpoint-root string \(U_{126}\).  Item~(4) is
\Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, with the faithful-submonoid classification and
the explicitly scoped finite-rank refinement cited in \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy};
the negative inference needed for the hierarchy uses the one-free-monogenic-orbit
hypotheses.  Item~(5) is the cover-category
result \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}, whose nonintrinsic boundary is recorded
in \Cref{thm:L2-cover-relative-closure,thm:L2-cover-intrinsic-nonimplication}.

For the exclusions, suppose one of the five stronger conclusions in the final
sentence were a consequence of the displayed proof chain.  Its proof would have
to factor through one of the five interfaces above, because those are the only
interfaces just enumerated.  The \(L0\) interface is citation-only beyond the
internal core, so it cannot supply a printed table.  The Richardson interface is
triple-relative and punctured, so it cannot supply a concrete printed
\(\pi\)/sine instantiation without extra identities or an endpoint-root
verification of \(U_{126}\).  The Zeckendorf interface is
explicitly value transported, so it cannot supply a bounded-window or digit-local
algorithm.  The finite-fibre interface changes only auxiliary cover
multiplicities over fixed normal forms, so it cannot imply an intrinsic theorem
for the original \(\Fold_m\) fibres.  Finally the multiplication obstruction is
proved only on one free monogenic orbit, so it cannot classify all possible
single-primitive protocols.  This proves both the stated projection and the
listed nonconsequences.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Richardson obstruction interface]\label{thm:richardson-obstruction-interface}
The Richardson input to the main theorem is exactly the punctured interface of
\Cref{thm:proc-ams-relative-richardson-theorem}: finite pure-EML witnesses for
\(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and \(|x|\) on \(\RR\setminus\{0\}\).  Under this

\begin{proof}

Richardson's undecidability theorem recorded in \Cref{lem:richardson-fragment}.
\end{proof}
