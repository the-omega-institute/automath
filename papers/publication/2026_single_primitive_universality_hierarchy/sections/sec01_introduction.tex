\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Universality statements often hide a difference in kind.  The
Sheffer stroke generates the Boolean operations \cite{Sheffer1913};
lambda abstraction gives a calculus of effective computation
\cite{Church1936}; and combinatory logic replaces variables by a
finite stock of combinators \cite{Schonfinkel1924}.  In such
examples it matters whether the assertion is merely that expressions
exist, or also that representatives can be chosen and compared
effectively.

The same distinction occurs for recent one-operation presentations
of elementary functions.  Odrzywo{\l}ek showed that
\[
  \eml(x,y)=e^x-\operatorname{Log}y
\]
together with the constant \(1\) represents the operations in a
standard scientific-calculator basis
\cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator}.  Thus a
large elementary language can be encoded by binary trees with a
single internal label.  This is a useful expression theorem, but it
does not itself choose a normal form among the many trees defining
the same function.

We shall distinguish three levels.  Level \(L0\), \emph{expression
universality}, only asks for some term over a single binary
operation.  Level \(L1\), \emph{protocol universality}, asks in
addition for computable normal forms and compiled normal-form
operations, together with semantic readout identities.  Our model
example uses Zeckendorf normal forms only as a computable addressing
system: operations are transported through the valuation bijection with
\(\NN\), and the elementary Church-iteration identities then read
addition and multiplication on \(\NN=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\) at two
successive composition levels.  Level \(L2\), \emph{certificate universality},
attaches finite certificates to the fibers of a normalizing map.
Hankel positivity and finite Prony reconstruction are then automatic
finite-fiber facts, whereas a finite-state transfer formula for the
same moments is extra structure.

The results below are separations of these structures, not new
proofs of local or online Zeckendorf arithmetic, Church iteration,
elementary function identities, or finite moment reconstruction.  The EML
operation is expression-universal for Odrzywo{\l}ek's basis.

\begin{assumption}[Printed-root interface \((H_{\mathcal W})\)]
\label{ass:HW}
In every statement that invokes the printed-root Richardson route,
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) denotes the following conditional external analytic
interface and no stronger assertion.  The three finite pure EML strings are
the printed roots
\[
  W_{\pi}=U_{47},\qquad W_{\sin}=U_{111},
  \qquad W_{\sqrt{}}=U_{126},
\]
source-locked in \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate,lem:eml-source-level-check}.
They are interpreted in the partial principal-branch EML semantics of
\Cref{def:eml}.  The assumed real-domain identities are exactly
\[
  W_{\pi}=\pi,
  \qquad W_{\sin}(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad W_{\sqrt{}}(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0}).
\]
The source for this interface is Odrzywo{\l}ek's arXiv:2603.21852v2 record,
updated 4 Apr. 2026, DOI \texttt{10.48550/arXiv.2603.21852}, specifically
the ancillary \texttt{SupplementaryInformation.pdf}, Part~I, Sec.~1.3,
Table~S2 and Part~II, Secs.~2.1--2.5, with the stable Zenodo v1.1 source

The logical status is conditional: this article proves the finite string locks
internally, but it does not prove the three displayed real-domain identities
from the printed table alone.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Logical status of the printed-root clause]
\label{prop:intro-printed-root-status}
The printed-root Richardson clause in \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy,thm:separation}
is intentionally conditional on \Cref{ass:HW}.  More precisely, the finite
source table supplies the strings \(W_{\pi},W_{\sin},W_{\sqrt{}}\), but this
manuscript does not internally prove from that table alone the three
principal-branch real-domain identities
\[
  U_{47}=\pi,
  \qquad U_{111}(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad U_{126}(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0}).
\]
Consequently the normal-form obstruction for those printed roots is invoked
only after the three real-domain assertions in \Cref{ass:HW} are assumed or
cited.  If instead one gives any finite pure-EML replacement triple with the
same three proved real-domain identities, the same Richardson obstruction is
unconditional for that replacement triple and does not use \Cref{ass:HW}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The finite part is the source-locking assertion proved later in
\Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate,lem:eml-source-level-check}: the roots
\(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) expand to fixed pure EML strings.  The negative
audit in \Cref{prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface} shows that these
finite source locks and fingerprints do not, by themselves, prove the three
principal-branch real-domain identities.  Thus every printed-root invocation
of the Richardson translation must obtain those identities from the external
interface recorded as \Cref{ass:HW}; this is exactly the dependency isolated in
\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface,prop:locked-three-witness-interface}.

Once the three identities are available, the audited translation of the
Richardson fragment uses no other non-core analytic data:
\Cref{lem:three-witness-domain-audit,prop:three-witness-stability} proves that
\(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and the nonnegative square root on
\(\RR_{\ge0}\) are precisely the needed witnesses, with square root applied
only to squares.  Richardson undecidability then gives the normalizer
contradiction in \Cref{thm:separation}.  The same proof applies verbatim to a
replacement triple whose three identities have been proved internally or by
some specified independent argument, and in that case the proof does not use
the printed roots or \Cref{ass:HW}.  Hence the printed-root clause has exactly
the conditional status claimed.
\end{proof}

Separately,
for any finite EML triple whose real-domain identities realize
\(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative square root, Richardson's theorem
rules out a computable canonical form on the translated Richardson
fragment even for the fixed predicate of identity to zero on the
transported real domain; the particular printed roots used for this
illustration use \Cref{ass:HW} as supplied by the cited
principal-branch symbolic verification, not by the source-level table
alone.  In the
  Zeckendorf protocol, addition is
  read on a free monogenic orbit, while multiplication cannot be read at
  that same semantic level by the ambient product on one free monogenic
  orbit, even after injectively recoding the positive integers.  This is the
  exact one-orbit obstruction; it is not a lower bound for protocols that
  move multiplication to a different semantic level or use a different
  readout law.  The certificate result is likewise a generic
  finite-fiber taxonomy statement rather than a new Hankel--Prony
  reconstruction theorem; the original L2 contribution is the separation of
  these finite-atomic certificates from finite-state transfer.  Finally,
  finite Hankel--Prony certificates do
not imply a finite-state transfer law: one can replace the finite
sets over the same normal forms by computable finite-fiber covers,
leaving the arithmetic readouts unchanged and forcing the second
collision moments to grow faster than any exponential sequence.

This scope is intentionally disjoint from several nearby Zeckendorf
and finite-resolution projects.  We do not study stable digit-local
Zeckendorf arithmetic, finite-window Zeckendorf thermodynamics,
Zeckendorf fiber thermodynamics, folded histogram laws for the actual
finite-window fibers, finite-observation escape rates, or normalization
automata.
The Zeckendorf material used here is only the computable normal-form
address system needed for the protocol readouts and for the abstract
finite-fiber certificate comparison.
Throughout this protocol discussion, computability lives in the finite
normal forms and compiled operations; the maps into endomorphism sorts are
semantic readout identities unless those sorts are separately given
effective presentations.

\begin{theorem}[Obstruction spine of the hierarchy]
\label{thm:obstruction-spine}
With the definitions made below, the non-collapse from expression
universality to protocol universality and from finite-fiber certificates to
finite-state transfer is witnessed by three independent obstructions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For the EML expression grammar, any finite triple of pure EML terms
  whose principal-branch real-domain meanings are \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and
  the nonnegative square root transports Richardson's real elementary
  fragment into pure EML terms.  On that transported fragment there is no
  computable finite normal form with decidable equality whose comparison
  with the translated zero term decides identity to zero.  This is a
  schematic implication for any specified replacement triple whose three
  real-domain identities have been proved; the paper therefore treats the
  printed-root route as conditional rather than as an internally constructed
  replacement triple.  For the printed roots of
  \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}, the route is instead via the formal
  conditional interface \((H_{\mathcal W})\) of \Cref{ass:HW}, organized in
  \Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface}.
\item In any monoid, multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be represented
  injectively by the ambient product on one free monogenic orbit generated
  by an infinite-order element.  Hence a protocol that separates the
  positive integers and reads addition on such an orbit must move the
  multiplication readout to additional protocol structure, as the
  Zeckendorf power-operator readout does.  The obstruction is sharp in the
  one-orbit class: \Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification} classifies
  all ambient-product multiplicative readouts by prime weights and shows
  that every such skeleton collapses a prime or two distinct prime powers.
\item Finite-fiber certificate data do not force finite-state transfer.  More
  precisely, for every computable sequence \(a_m\ge1\) there is a computable
  finite-fiber cover of the Zeckendorf normal-form sets preserving the same
  stable normal forms, valuation, compiled operations, and readouts, with
  second collision moment
  \[
    S_2^\sharp(m)=(a_m+1)^2+F_{m+2}-1 .
  \]
  Taking \(a_m=m!\) gives finite-fiber certificates whose second moment is not
  a coefficient of any fixed finite matrix power; equivalently it satisfies no
  constant-coefficient linear recurrence and has no rational generating series
  in the resolution parameter.  Thus the Hankel--Prony part of \(L2\) is only
  the generic finite-atomic support, while the strict \(L2\) obstruction is the
  finite-state growth budget, formalized as the budget spine of
  \Cref{thm:L2-budget-spine}.  The obstruction is not detectable from any
  bounded initial window of the resolution data: finite prefixes can be held
  fixed while a later factorial spike destroys every finite-state transfer
  representation, as shown in
  \Cref{prop:finite-prefix-no-transfer-certification}.
\end{enumerate}
The first two obstructions are the load-bearing \(L0\)--\(L1\) strictness
mechanisms.  The third is the load-bearing \(L2\) strictness mechanism; its
finite-atomic Hankel--Prony certificates are subordinate support and are not
used to prove the first two obstructions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is exactly \Cref{thm:separation}, with its two proof
interfaces spelled out in
\Cref{ass:HW,thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface,cor:printed-richardson-public-interface,thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface,thm:no-unverified-richardson-instance,cor:richardson-witness-alternative,prop:intro-printed-root-status}:
the printed roots, with \((H_{\mathcal W})\) supplied by
\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface}, give the transported Richardson obstruction,
and replacement triples with proved real-domain identities give the same
obstruction.
The second assertion is \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, sharpened by
the classification theorem \Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification},
its exact boundary \Cref{cor:one-orbit-obstruction-boundary}, and its
protocol dichotomy \Cref{cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}; the
proof is the prime-power collision in the exponent skeleton
\Cref{lem:level-zero-exponent-skeleton}.

For the third assertion, \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness} constructs the
one-spike cover for arbitrary computable \((a_m)\), proves the displayed
formula for \(S_2^\sharp(m)\), and shows that the factorial choice violates
the fixed finite-matrix coefficient budget of
\Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget}.  The recurrence and rational
generating-series formulations are equivalent by the same theorem and by the
standard companion-matrix argument recorded in the proof of
\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}.  The exact boundary for one-spike covers is
\Cref{thm:one-spike-finite-state-boundary}, the scalar budget spine is
\Cref{thm:L2-budget-spine}, the finite-prefix non-certification statement is
\Cref{prop:finite-prefix-no-transfer-certification}, and the generic nature of
the Hankel--Prony clauses is
\Cref{prop:L2-generic-vs-structural,cor:hankel-psd-independent}.
These citations also prove the independence sentence: the Richardson theorem
uses the EML witness interface and Richardson undecidability, the
monogenic-orbit theorem uses only the exponent skeleton in a free orbit, and
the finite-state obstruction uses only computable finite covers and the
finite-matrix growth budget.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Single-primitive hierarchy theorem with conditional printed-root firewall]\label{thm:main-hierarchy}
With the definitions made below, the single-primitive hierarchy separates
four kinds of structure.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The principal-branch EML system \((\eml,1)\) is an imported \(L0\)
  expression-universal representative-existence system for
  Odrzywo{\l}ek's calculator basis \(\mathcal B_{36}\); the import is exactly
  the public-record assertion isolated in
  \Cref{thm:eml-universality,thm:eml-public-record-audit} and supplies no
  normal-form, equality, or hidden Richardson oracle.
\item For every nonempty set \(A\) and infinite-order endomorphism
  \(g\colon A\to A\), the Zeckendorf normal-form address system is an \(L1\)
  protocol for \(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).  Addition is read on the free
  monogenic orbit \(c_n\mapsto g^{\circ n}\), while multiplication is read at
  the power-operator level \(c_n\mapsto I_n\).  This level change is forced for
  same-level ambient-product readouts on one free monogenic orbit: every such
  multiplicative readout is a prime-weight skeleton and has a prime-power
  collision.
\item The Zeckendorf finite-resolution fibers give the subordinate \(L2\)
  finite-fiber certificate data: computable collision moments,
  Hankel-positive moment matrices, and finite Prony reconstruction with the
  computable bound \(2F_{m+2}-1\) at resolution \(m\).  These are generic
  finite-atomic consequences, not the novelty claim.
\item The \(L2\) strictness claim is the finite-state transfer separation:
  computable finite-fiber covers can preserve the same normal forms,
  compiled operations, and readouts while producing second collision moments
  outside every fixed finite-matrix transfer skeleton.  Equivalently, within
  the cover class used here, the finite-state transfer question is exactly the
  scalar budget question for \((S_2^\sharp(m))_{m\ge1}\), not a Hankel--Prony
  question.
\item The Richardson component has exactly two interfaces.  Any verified
  finite EML triple representing \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative square
  root on the stated real domains gives the transported Richardson
  normal-form obstruction unconditionally for that triple.  The printed-root
  instance \(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) is only the conditional specialization
  through \((H_{\mathcal W})\) of \Cref{ass:HW}; without that assumption the
  theorem records only the finite source locks for those three strings and no
  Richardson contradiction for the named printed roots.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently expression representatives, computable protocol normal forms
with semantic readouts, finite-fiber certificate data, finite-state transfer
laws, and the printed-root Richardson interface are distinct kinds of
single-primitive structure.  The unconditional part of this theorem is the
positive L0 import, the Zeckendorf L1 protocol, the one-orbit obstruction,
and the finite-state L2 separation.  The named printed-root Richardson
specialization is conditional exactly on \((H_{\mathcal W})\); without that
interface the printed table supplies only finite EML strings.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We prove the five displayed clauses directly, and then record their
disjoint dependencies.  The \(L0\) clause is precisely the imported
representative-existence theorem \Cref{thm:eml-universality}.  Its
public-record and dependency cut are \Cref{thm:eml-public-record-audit,prop:eml-import-dependency-cut}: the imported assertion is only that the
named principal-branch basis \(\mathcal B_{36}\) has finite EML
representatives over \(\{1,\eml\}\).  In particular it contains no
normalizer, no decidable equality procedure, and no Richardson witness
identity beyond the separate interface stated later.

The \(L1\) positive protocol clause is \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}.
It gives computable Zeckendorf normal forms, compiled addition and
multiplication on those forms, and the two readout identities
\(c_n\mapsto g^{\circ n}\) for addition and \(c_n\mapsto I_n\) for
multiplication.  The asserted necessity of the multiplication level shift is
\Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction,thm:one-orbit-readout-classification}: any
same-level ambient-product readout on one free monogenic orbit factors through
a prime-weight exponent skeleton and therefore has the prime-power collision
recorded in \Cref{cor:one-orbit-obstruction-boundary}.  This proves the
second clause.

For the positive \(L2\) clause, apply \Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}.  At
each resolution \(m\), the fold fibers form finite effective lists, their
collision moments are computable by bounded enumeration, and the finite
atomic Hankel--Prony argument gives Hankel positivity and reconstruction from
moments through index \(2|X_m|-1=2F_{m+2}-1\).  The classification
\Cref{thm:L2-fiber-multiset-factorization,thm:finite-fiber-skeleton-extremal}
shows that this positive clause records only the finite multiset of fiber
sizes at the fixed resolution; it supplies no transfer law as \(m\) varies.

The finite-state strictness clause is the separate growth obstruction.
\Cref{thm:finite-fiber-cover-realization,lem:cover-preserves-protocol}
realize arbitrary computable positive fiber-size data over the fixed
Zeckendorf normal forms without changing the stable normal forms, compiled
operations, or readouts.  \Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget} bounds
every fixed finite-matrix coefficient by an exponential in the resolution
parameter, while \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness} chooses a computable
one-spike cover whose second moment grows faster than every exponential; the
exact one-spike boundary is \Cref{thm:one-spike-finite-state-boundary}, and
\Cref{prop:finite-prefix-no-transfer-certification} shows that no finite
initial window of moment data can certify the missing transfer law.  Hence
finite-fiber certificates do not force finite-state transfer.

Finally, the Richardson clause is \Cref{thm:separation}.  Its unconditional
form is the replacement-triple implication: once a fixed finite EML triple is
proved to represent \(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and the nonnegative square root on
\(\RR_{\ge0}\),
\Cref{thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface,thm:no-unverified-richardson-instance}
translate Richardson's
fragment into pure EML terms and \Cref{lem:transported-partial-normalizer-obstruction,lem:richardson-fragment} rule out a computable finite normal-form comparison deciding identity to zero.  The named printed roots
\(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) enter only through the conditional cited interface
\((H_{\mathcal W})\), by
\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface,thm:printed-root-richardson-conditional-clause}; the string locks and fingerprints alone are not analytic
identity proofs by \Cref{prop:printed-root-conditional-only,prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface}.

These five paragraphs also give the firewall.  The \(L0\) import, the
Zeckendorf protocol and one-orbit obstruction, the fixed-resolution
finite-atomic certificate facts, the finite-state growth obstruction, and the
conditional printed-root Richardson interface are invoked in different
clauses.  The sharper dependency splitting is recorded in
\Cref{thm:main-dependency-splitting}.  Thus expression representatives,
protocol normal forms, finite-fiber certificates, and finite-state transfer
laws cannot be collapsed into one another by any proof step in this theorem,
and the printed-root Richardson statement is conditional exactly as stated.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Detailed hierarchy clause ledger]\label{prop:hierarchy-clause-ledger}
With the definitions made below, the single-primitive hierarchy has two
primary strictness obstructions, recorded in
\Cref{thm:obstruction-spine}, together with four structural clauses and
one Richardson interface clause whose unconditional form is the verified
replacement-triple theorem.  In the protocol clauses, \(A\) is any
nonempty set and \(g\colon A\to A\) is any endomorphism of infinite
order.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The EML system \((\eml,1)\) is \(L0\)
  expression-universal for Odrzywo{\l}ek's scientific-calculator basis
  \(\mathcal B_{36}\), in the principal-branch sense imported from that
  external representation theorem and isolated in
  \Cref{cor:external-compiler-isolation,thm:eml-public-record-audit,prop:eml-import-dependency-cut,thm:expression-import-quarantine}.
  Independently, the manuscript proves
  an internal core EML witness package for the algebraic constructors
  later used in the Richardson interface; only the full \(\mathcal B_{36}\)
  L0 clause is imported.
\item The Zeckendorf address system, equipped with its stable normal
  forms and the two composition readouts
  \(c_n\mapsto g^{\circ n}\) and \(c_n\mapsto I_n\), is
  \(L1\) protocol-universal for \(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).  The
  multiplication readout is genuinely extra protocol structure:
  multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be read from the ambient
  product on one free monogenic orbit, even after an injective recoding
  of the positive integers; indeed all such one-orbit multiplicative
  readouts are the prime-weight skeletons classified in
  \Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification}; the boundary of this
  obstruction is exactly the same-level ambient-product hypothesis of
  \Cref{cor:one-orbit-obstruction-boundary}, and each skeleton has an
  explicit prime-power collision.  The compiled arithmetic is exactly
  valuation transport through ordinary integer arithmetic followed by
  greedy re-normalization.  No bounded-window or online Zeckendorf
  multiplication theorem is part of this clause: in fact
  \Cref{thm:no-finite-level-arithmetic} shows that no single finite
  residue level can both separate the stable addresses and carry the
  stable arithmetic used by the protocol.
\item Subordinately, the Zeckendorf finite-resolution data are \(L2\)
  certificate-universal in the finite-fiber sense: the normalization
  fibers have computable collision moments, Hankel-positive moment
  matrices, and finite Prony reconstruction with the computable bound
  \(2F_{m+2}-1\) at resolution \(m\).  This is the finite-atomic
  certificate supplied by the fold fibers, not a new finite-window
  moment theory and not the structural strictness mechanism of the
  \(L2\) part; the latter is the finite-state budget spine of
  \Cref{thm:L2-budget-spine}.
\item The structural \(L2\) strictness point is instead the finite-state
  transfer obstruction for the cover category just defined: finite-fiber
  certificate data do not force a finite-state transfer law.  More sharply,
  computable finite-fiber covers over the fixed Zeckendorf normal-form sets are
  exactly arbitrary computable positive fiber-size data over those sets.  Hence
  there are covers preserving the protocol normal forms and readouts whose
  second collision moments have superexponential growth.  Thus the L2 novelty
  is the cover-level finite-state separation isolated in
  \Cref{thm:finite-fiber-skeleton-extremal,prop:L2-novelty-finite-state-separation,prop:cover-only-strictness-boundary,thm:L2-budget-spine,thm:one-spike-finite-state-boundary}.
  This violates the finite-state skeleton proved in
  \Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget}: every fixed finite-matrix
  moment has exponential growth, a constant-coefficient linear recurrence,
  and a rational generating series in the resolution parameter.
\item The Richardson normal-form obstruction is first a verified-triple
  theorem: for every specified finite triple of pure EML terms whose
  real-domain identities realizing \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the principal
  nonnegative square root have been proved, the translated Richardson
  fragment has no computable finite normal-form comparison deciding identity
  to zero.  \Cref{thm:richardson-witness-dependency-cut} proves that these
  three identities are exactly the dependency cut for any unconditional
  replacement-triple instance of \Cref{thm:separation}.  The special
  printed-root instance for
  \(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) is a conditional corollary using only the formal
  interface \((H_{\mathcal W})\) of \Cref{ass:HW}, as organized in
  \Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface,prop:printed-root-conditional-only,cor:printed-richardson-public-interface,thm:richardson-interface-boundary}; it is not an unconditional claim about the
  printed table itself.
\end{enumerate}
The load-bearing \(L0\)--\(L1\) strictness claims are the two clauses isolated in
\Cref{thm:obstruction-spine}; the \(L2\) strictness claim is the finite-state
growth obstruction, while the finite-atomic Hankel--Prony clause is only the
  subordinate positive certificate clause.  By \Cref{prop:intro-printed-root-status}, the theorem does not assert an
unconditional Richardson obstruction from the printed table alone.  The
verified-triple version of \Cref{thm:separation} is unconditional exactly once
the three real-domain identities for the chosen finite EML triple have been
proved, as isolated in \Cref{thm:richardson-witness-dependency-cut}.  The printed-root version uses the external symbolic verification of
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) recorded in \Cref{ass:HW}.  No concrete internal
replacement triple for \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and \(\sqrt{\phantom{x}}\) is
exhibited in this article.  Moreover, this is a transported-fragment
separation: it is not an absolute impossibility
  theorem for every expression-universal single-primitive protocol outside
  the semantic-compatible Richardson interface.  The finite-state clause is
  similarly cover-level: it is not a theorem that the unmodified Zeckendorf
  folds \(\Fold_m\colon\Omega_m\to X_m\) lack a transfer matrix.
Thus expression representatives, computable protocol normal forms with
semantic readouts, finite-fiber certificate data, and finite-state
transfer laws are distinct kinds of structure in the single-primitive
setting.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first clause is \Cref{thm:eml-universality,cor:external-compiler-isolation,thm:eml-public-record-audit,prop:eml-import-dependency-cut,thm:expression-import-quarantine}, with the internal core
fallback supplied by \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}.  The second
clause is \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}, with the exact
  value-transport interface isolated in
  \Cref{prop:value-transport-exact-interface,thm:no-finite-level-arithmetic},
together with the
monogenic-orbit obstruction \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, the
classification of its full one-orbit skeleton in
\Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification}, the boundary corollary
\Cref{cor:one-orbit-obstruction-boundary}, and its dichotomy corollary
\Cref{cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}.  The third clause is
\Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}, whose proof invokes only the
finite-atomic Hankel and Prony results
\Cref{thm:hankel-psd,thm:prony} and whose generic load is isolated in
\Cref{prop:finite-fiber-certificate-taxonomy,prop:L2-novelty-finite-state-separation,cor:L2-load-split}.  The
fourth clause is \Cref{thm:finite-fiber-cover-realization,prop:cover-only-strictness-boundary,thm:finite-state-strictness}, sharpened by the
finite-state skeleton \Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget} and in
\Cref{thm:L2-budget-spine,thm:one-spike-finite-state-boundary,cor:finite-state-growth-budget,cor:hankel-psd-independent}, with
preservation of the underlying protocol supplied by
\Cref{lem:cover-preserves-protocol}.  The fifth clause is exactly
\Cref{thm:separation}, whose proof is routed through the effective
translation \Cref{lem:richardson-translation}, the locked finite witness
library \Cref{cor:eml-witness-locking}, the dependency audit
\Cref{prop:locked-three-witness-interface,cor:printed-richardson-public-interface}, and the replacement-triple
form \Cref{prop:three-witness-stability,thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface}.
The three strictness mechanisms--the Richardson normal-form obstruction, the
one-orbit multiplication obstruction, and the finite-state certificate growth
obstruction--are summarized in \Cref{thm:obstruction-spine}; the finite-atomic
certificate clause supplies only the subordinate Hankel--Prony support for the

No step in the first four clauses invokes the Richardson translation or the

replacement triple with proved real-domain identities.  The printed-root

  \Cref{prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface,prop:no-third-richardson-interface,cor:submission-richardson-clause} record why the
  printed table alone does not supply such a proof.  The exact boundary of
  the slogan ``expression universality does not imply protocol universality''
  in this Richardson component is the transported-fragment statement proved in
  \Cref{thm:transported-richardson-boundary}.  This proves the hierarchy
  clauses without promoting the finite table and fingerprints to analytic
  witness verification.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Main-chain dependency firewall]
\label{thm:main-dependency-firewall}
The proof of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} has the following exact dependency
decomposition.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The positive full-basis \(L0\) clause uses Odrzywo{\l}ek's theorem only as
  the representative-existence assertion for the named principal-branch basis
  \(\mathcal B_{36}\).  Replacing that imported assertion by the same assertion
  for a subbasis changes only the named positive \(L0\) clause.
\item The Richardson strictness clause has an unconditional form for every
  specified finite replacement triple whose three real-domain identities
  representing \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and \(\sqrt{\phantom{x}}\) have been proved.
  The printed-root instance is exactly the specialization obtained by adding
  \((H_{\mathcal W})\); no theorem in the Zeckendorf protocol, the one-orbit
  multiplication obstruction, or the finite-fiber certificate chain uses
  \((H_{\mathcal W})\).
\item The Hankel positivity and Prony reconstruction clauses are precisely the
  fixed-resolution finite-atomic consequences of a finite fiber-size multiset.
  They are not used to infer finite-state transfer in the resolution parameter;
  the latter is supplied only by the separate cover-realization and growth
  obstruction chain ending in \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness} and
  \Cref{thm:one-spike-finite-state-boundary}.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the only visibly conditional assertion in the printed-root route
is the printed-root Richardson specialization, the only imported compiler-scale
assertion is the positive full-basis \(L0\) representative-existence clause, and
the structural \(L2\) novelty is the finite-state non-transfer theorem rather
than the classical finite-atomic reconstruction package.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the first item, \Cref{prop:eml-import-dependency-cut} proves that the
imported theorem \Cref{thm:eml-universality} is used in the article exactly as
the statement that every primitive in the displayed basis \(\mathcal B_{36}\)
has a finite principal-branch EML representative.  The proof of
\Cref{prop:hierarchy-clause-ledger} invokes this result only in its first
clause.  The same proposition cites the internal EML core, the Zeckendorf
protocol, the one-orbit obstruction, the finite-fiber certificate results, and
the Richardson witness interface from the separate results named there.  Hence
weakening the external import to a subbasis weakens only the name and extent of
the positive \(L0\) clause, exactly as stated.

For the second item, the fifth clause of \Cref{prop:hierarchy-clause-ledger}
cites \Cref{thm:richardson-witness-dependency-cut,thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface,thm:separation}.
Those results first prove a replacement-triple theorem: after the three
real-domain identities for a finite triple are available, the translation of
Richardson's fragment is computable and preserves the relevant partial real
functions, and any compatible finite normalizer would decide Richardson
identity-to-zero.  In the printed-root route the finite strings are locked by
\Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate,lem:eml-source-level-check}, while the three
analytic identities are exactly the hypothesis \((H_{\mathcal W})\) recorded in
\Cref{ass:HW} and the public-interface results cited in \Cref{thm:separation}.
The proof of \Cref{prop:hierarchy-clause-ledger} explicitly states that its
first four clauses do not invoke the Richardson translation or the printed
roots.  Therefore \((H_{\mathcal W})\) is confined to the printed-root
specialization of the Richardson clause.

For the third item, \Cref{prop:classical-finite-atomic-skeleton,thm:hankel-psd,thm:prony,thm:finite-fiber-skeleton-extremal}
prove that the fixed-resolution certificate is the finite positive atomic
measure determined by the multiset of fiber sizes.  These proofs use only a
single finite multiset at a fixed resolution and explicitly produce no fixed
matrix, recurrence, or rational generating series in the resolution variable.
The resolution-uniform obstruction is instead proved later by the cover
classification \Cref{thm:finite-fiber-cover-realization}, the protocol
preservation lemma \Cref{lem:cover-preserves-protocol}, the finite-state growth
budget \Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget}, and the one-spike
construction and boundary
\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness,thm:one-spike-finite-state-boundary}.  Thus the
classical finite-atomic facts and the finite-state non-transfer theorem occupy
different logical roles.  Combining the three items proves the asserted
dependency decomposition and its consequences for the main theorem chain.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Compact hierarchy form]\label{cor:compact-hierarchy-form}
The article proves the following journal-facing hierarchy statement.  There
is a single-primitive expression system, namely the principal-branch EML
system \((\eml,1)\), which is \(L0\) expression-universal for
Odrzywo{\l}ek's calculator basis in the imported representative-existence
sense.  There is a single-primitive protocol system, namely the Zeckendorf
normal-form protocol, which is \(L1\) protocol-universal for
\(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).  The protocol multiplication readout cannot be
collapsed to the ambient product on one free monogenic orbit; every such
one-orbit ambient-product readout is a prime-weight skeleton and has a
prime-power collision.  The finite-fiber certificate layer gives computable
collision moments, Hankel positivity, and finite Prony reconstruction, but
these finite atomic certificates do not force finite-state transfer: there
are computable finite-fiber covers preserving the same normal forms and
readouts whose second collision moments grow faster than every exponential
sequence.  Finally, for every verified finite EML triple representing
\(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative square root on the stated real domains,
the transported Richardson fragment has no computable finite normal-form
comparison deciding identity to zero; the printed-root instance is only the
conditional specialization through \((H_{\mathcal W})\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The EML expression clause is the first clause of
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.  The Zeckendorf protocol clause is the second
clause of that theorem.  The one-orbit non-collapse and its prime-weight
skeleton are \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction,thm:one-orbit-readout-classification,cor:one-orbit-obstruction-boundary}.  The finite-fiber
positive certificate clause is \Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}; the
finite-state separation and superexponential cover are
\Cref{thm:finite-fiber-cover-realization,thm:finite-state-strictness,thm:one-spike-finite-state-boundary}.  The verified-triple Richardson
obstruction is \Cref{thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface,thm:separation}; the
printed-root conditional boundary is
\Cref{ass:HW,thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface,cor:submission-richardson-clause}.  These are precisely the clauses collected in
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}, so the compact formulation follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Submission boundary for the Richardson clause]
\label{cor:richardson-submission-boundary}
The Richardson component of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy,thm:obstruction-spine}
has exactly the following status.  The first four structural hierarchy
clauses are unconditional and do not use the three printed roots.  The

\[
  \mathsf P=\pi,
  \qquad
  \mathsf S(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad
  \mathsf R(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0})
\]
are proved before applying the Richardson translation.  No such concrete
internal replacement triple is exhibited in this article.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The unconditionality assertion for the first four clauses is the last
paragraph of the proof of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}: those clauses cite only
the EML representation theorem, Zeckendorf protocol theorem, monogenic-orbit
obstruction package, finite-fiber certificate theorem, and finite-state
growth obstruction, and the proof explicitly notes that none of these inputs
uses \(W_{\pi}\), \(W_{\sin}\), or \(W_{\sqrt{}}\).  The printed-root
Richardson clause is \Cref{thm:separation} in its first alternative, whose

\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface,cor:printed-richardson-public-interface}

\Cref{prop:three-witness-stability,thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface}

\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Richardson obstruction interface]\label{thm:richardson-obstruction-interface}

\begin{enumerate}
\item For the printed roots of \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}, it
  uses precisely the three real-domain identities in
  \((H_{\mathcal W})\) for \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the principal
  nonnegative square root, supplied by
  \Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface}.
\item For any specified finite replacement triple of pure EML terms for which
  the same three real-domain identities have been proved, the obstruction is

\item The first four clauses of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} and the

  use the printed roots; the fifth clause uses exactly the cited printed-root or replacement
  three-witness interface stated in the preceding two items.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the paper claims the L0--L1 Richardson separation for the printed
roots only under \((H_{\mathcal W})\), while the Zenodo code/provenance
object remains non-load-bearing for the hierarchy theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is exactly
\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface}: the printed long
roots enter the Richardson translation only through the three displayed
real-domain identities, and
\Cref{prop:printed-root-conditional-only,prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface} proves that the
straight-line table alone is not an internal analytic proof of those
identities in the partial principal-branch EML semantics.  The second

\Cref{prop:three-witness-stability}, which constructs the same effective
Richardson translation from an arbitrary specified finite triple after those
identities have been proved and then applies the transported normalizer

its first four clauses cite only \Cref{thm:eml-universality},
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ,thm:level-zero-obstruction,thm:one-orbit-readout-classification,cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy},

\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness,cor:finite-state-growth-budget,cor:hankel-psd-independent,lem:cover-preserves-protocol}.

\Cref{thm:separation} and uses the cited printed-root interface exactly as

\end{proof}
