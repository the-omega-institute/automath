\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Universality statements often hide a difference in kind.  The
Sheffer stroke generates the Boolean operations \cite{Sheffer1913};
lambda abstraction gives a calculus of effective computation
\cite{Church1936}; and combinatory logic replaces variables by a
finite stock of combinators \cite{Schonfinkel1924}.  In such
examples it matters whether the assertion is merely that expressions
exist, or also that representatives can be chosen and compared
effectively.

The same distinction occurs for recent one-operation presentations
of elementary functions.  Odrzywo{\l}ek showed that
\[
  \eml(x,y)=e^x-\operatorname{Log}y
\]
together with the constant \(1\) represents the operations in a
standard scientific-calculator basis
\cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator}.  Thus a
large elementary language can be encoded by binary trees with a
single internal label.  This is a useful expression theorem, but it
does not itself choose a normal form among the many trees defining
the same function.

We shall distinguish three levels.  Level \(L0\), \emph{expression
universality}, only asks for some term over a single binary
operation.  Level \(L1\), \emph{protocol universality}, asks in
addition for computable normal forms and compiled normal-form
operations, together with semantic readout identities.  Our model
example uses Zeckendorf normal forms only as a computable addressing
system: operations are transported through the valuation bijection with
\(\NN\), and the elementary Church-iteration identities then read
addition and multiplication on \(\NN=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\) at two
successive composition levels.  Level \(L2\), \emph{certificate universality},
attaches finite certificates to the fibers of a normalizing map.
Hankel positivity and finite Prony reconstruction are then automatic
finite-fiber facts, whereas a finite-state transfer formula for the
covered moment sequence is extra structure.

The results below are separations of these structures, not new
proofs of local or online Zeckendorf arithmetic, Church iteration,
elementary function identities, or finite moment reconstruction.  The EML
operation is expression-universal for Odrzywo{\l}ek's basis.

\begin{remark}[Logical status of the printed-root clause]
The printed-root Richardson clause in \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy,thm:separation}
uses the cited witness package of \Cref{ass:HW}.  More precisely, the finite
source table supplies the strings \(W_{\pi},W_{\sin},W_{\sqrt{}}\), but this
manuscript does not internally prove from that table alone the three
principal-branch real-domain identities
\[
  U_{47}=\pi,
  \qquad U_{111}(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad U_{126}(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0}).
\]
Consequently the normal-form obstruction for those named printed roots is
conditional on \((H_{\mathcal W})\): the finite strings are locked internally,
but their use as \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative square root factors
through \Cref{ass:HW,thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface}.  If instead
one gives a finite pure-EML replacement triple together with a proof of the
same three real-domain identities, the Richardson obstruction is unconditional
for that verified replacement triple and does not use \Cref{ass:HW}.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Justification.}
The finite part is the source-locking assertion proved later in
\Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate,lem:eml-source-level-check}: the roots
\(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) expand to fixed pure EML strings.  The negative
audit in \Cref{prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface} shows that these
finite source locks and fingerprints do not, by themselves, prove the three
principal-branch real-domain identities.  Thus every printed-root invocation
of the Richardson translation must obtain those identities from the external
interface recorded as \Cref{ass:HW}; this is exactly the dependency isolated in
\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface,prop:locked-three-witness-interface}.

This proves the conditional status of the printed-root route.  Once the three
identities are available, the audited translation of the Richardson fragment
uses no other non-core analytic data:
\Cref{lem:three-witness-domain-audit,prop:three-witness-stability} proves that
\(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and the nonnegative square root on
\(\RR_{\ge0}\) are precisely the needed witnesses, with square root applied
only to squares.  Richardson undecidability then gives the normalizer
contradiction in \Cref{thm:separation}.  The same proof applies verbatim to a
replacement triple whose three identities have been proved internally or by
some specified independent argument, and in that case the proof does not use
the printed roots or \Cref{ass:HW}.  Hence the manuscript has two and only two
routes used in the main theorem chain: a conditional printed-root specialization
through \((H_{\mathcal W})\), and an unconditional verified-triple implication
after the three analytic identities have been proved for the replacement
triple.  The finite table alone remains insufficient.
\end{remark}

Separately,
for any finite EML triple whose real-domain identities realize
\(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative square root, Richardson's theorem
rules out a computable canonical form on the translated Richardson
fragment even for the fixed predicate of identity to zero on the
transported real domain; the particular printed roots used for this
illustration use the cited principal-branch symbolic verification recorded in \Cref{ass:HW}, not the source-level table alone.  In the
  Zeckendorf protocol, addition is
  read on a free monogenic orbit, while multiplication cannot be read at
  that same semantic level by the ambient product on one free monogenic
  orbit, even after injectively recoding the positive integers.  This is the
  exact one-orbit obstruction; it is not a lower bound for protocols that
  move multiplication to a different semantic level or use a different
  readout law.  The certificate result is likewise a generic
  finite-fiber taxonomy statement rather than a new Hankel--Prony
  reconstruction theorem; the original L2 contribution is the separation of
  these finite-atomic certificates from finite-state transfer.  Finally,
  finite Hankel--Prony certificates do
not imply a finite-state transfer law: one can replace the finite
sets over the same normal forms by computable finite-fiber covers,
leaving the arithmetic readouts unchanged and forcing the second
collision moments to grow faster than any exponential sequence.

This scope is intentionally disjoint from several nearby Zeckendorf
and finite-resolution projects.  We do not study stable digit-local
Zeckendorf arithmetic, finite-window Zeckendorf thermodynamics,
Zeckendorf fiber thermodynamics, folded histogram laws for the actual
finite-window fibers, finite-observation escape rates, or normalization
automata.
The Zeckendorf material used here is only the computable normal-form
address system needed for the protocol readouts and for the abstract
finite-fiber certificate comparison.
Throughout this protocol discussion, computability lives in the finite
normal forms and compiled operations; the maps into endomorphism sorts are
semantic readout identities unless those sorts are separately given
effective presentations.

\begin{proposition}[Two-primary obstruction spine]
\label{thm:obstruction-spine}
With the definitions made below, the hierarchy theorem has two primary
obstructions, with the finite-fiber material serving only as a subordinate
certificate-versus-transfer clause.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(R)}] For the EML expression grammar, any finite triple of pure EML terms
  whose principal-branch real-domain meanings are \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and
  the nonnegative square root transports Richardson's real elementary
  fragment into pure EML terms.  On that transported fragment there is no
  computable finite normal form with decidable equality whose comparison
  with the translated zero term decides identity to zero.  This is the theorem applied to the printed roots of
  \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate} through the cited package
  \((H_{\mathcal W})\) of \Cref{ass:HW}, organized in
  \Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface}.  The same proof applies
  to any specified replacement triple whose three real-domain identities have
  been proved.
\item[\textup{(M)}] In any monoid, multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be represented
  injectively by the ambient product on one free monogenic orbit generated
  by an infinite-order element.  Hence a protocol that separates the
  positive integers and reads addition on such an orbit must move the
  multiplication readout to additional protocol structure, as the
  Zeckendorf power-operator readout does.  The obstruction is sharp in the
  one-orbit class: \Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification} classifies
  all ambient-product multiplicative readouts by prime weights and shows
  that every such skeleton collapses a prime or two distinct prime powers.
\item[\textup{(C)}] Finite-fiber certificate data do not force finite-state transfer.  More
  precisely, for every computable sequence \(a_m\ge1\) there is a computable
  finite-fiber cover of the Zeckendorf normal-form sets preserving the same
  stable normal forms, valuation, compiled operations, and readouts, with
  second collision moment
  \[
    S_2^\sharp(m)=(a_m+1)^2+F_{m+2}-1 .
  \]
  Taking \(a_m=m!\) gives finite-fiber certificates whose second moment is not
  a coefficient of any fixed finite matrix power; equivalently it satisfies no
  constant-coefficient linear recurrence and has no rational generating series
  in the resolution parameter.  Thus the Hankel--Prony part of \(L2\) is only
  the generic finite-atomic support, while the strict \(L2\) obstruction is the
  finite-state growth budget, formalized as the budget spine of
  \Cref{thm:L2-budget-spine}.  The obstruction is not detectable from any
  bounded initial window of the resolution data: finite prefixes can be held
  fixed while a later factorial spike destroys every finite-state transfer
  representation, as shown in
  \Cref{prop:finite-prefix-no-transfer-certification}.
\end{enumerate}
The primary theorem-note spine is (R) plus (M): expression universality gives
neither computable semantic normal forms for the transported Richardson
fragment nor a same-level multiplication readout on one free monogenic orbit.
Clause (C) is the subordinate \(L2\) addendum; its finite-atomic Hankel--Prony
certificates are support for the certificate level and are not used to prove
either primary obstruction.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is exactly \Cref{thm:separation}, with its two proof
interfaces spelled out in
\Cref{ass:HW,thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface,thm:richardson-interface-boundary,thm:verified-finite-richardson-fragment,thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface,thm:no-unverified-richardson-instance,cor:richardson-witness-alternative,prop:printed-root-conditional-only}:
the printed roots, with \((H_{\mathcal W})\) supplied by
\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface}, give the transported Richardson obstruction,
and replacement triples with proved real-domain identities give the same
obstruction.
The second assertion is \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, sharpened by
the classification theorem \Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification},
its exact boundary \Cref{cor:one-orbit-obstruction-boundary}, and its
protocol dichotomy \Cref{cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}; the
proof is the prime-power collision in the exponent skeleton
\Cref{lem:level-zero-exponent-skeleton}.

For the third assertion, \Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness} constructs the
one-spike cover for arbitrary computable \((a_m)\), proves the displayed
formula for \(S_2^\sharp(m)\), and shows that the factorial choice violates
the fixed finite-matrix coefficient budget of
\Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget}.  The recurrence and rational
generating-series formulations are equivalent by the same theorem and by the
standard companion-matrix argument recorded in the proof of
\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness}.  The exact boundary for one-spike covers is
\Cref{thm:one-spike-finite-state-boundary}, the scalar budget spine is
\Cref{thm:L2-budget-spine}, the finite-prefix non-certification statement is
\Cref{prop:finite-prefix-no-transfer-certification}, and the generic nature of
the Hankel--Prony clauses is
\Cref{prop:L2-generic-vs-structural,cor:hankel-psd-independent}.
These citations also prove the independence sentence: the Richardson theorem
uses the EML witness interface and Richardson undecidability, the
monogenic-orbit theorem uses only the exponent skeleton in a free orbit, and
the finite-state obstruction uses only computable finite covers and the
finite-matrix growth budget.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Proc. AMS hierarchy spine]\label{thm:main-hierarchy}
With the definitions made below, the single-primitive hierarchy has the
following compact form.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The EML system \((\eml,1)\) gives an imported \(L0\)
  expression-representative system for Odrzywo{\l}ek's principal-branch
  calculator basis \(\mathcal B_{36}\).  This public-record import is only a
  representative-existence theorem; it supplies no computable semantic
  normalizer, equality algorithm, or hidden Richardson witness identity.
\item There is an internal \(L1\) protocol for \(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\):
  Zeckendorf normal forms are computable, the compiled operations are obtained
  by valuation transport through ordinary arithmetic on \(\NN\), addition is
  read by \(c_n\mapsto g^{\circ n}\), and multiplication is read by the
  Church power-operator map \(c_n\mapsto I_n\).  This multiplication readout
  cannot be collapsed to the ambient product on one free monogenic orbit;
  every same-level one-orbit readout has the prime-weight skeleton and a
  prime-power collision.
\item The same finite-resolution Zeckendorf data give the positive \(L2\)
  finite-fiber certificate layer: at each fixed resolution the collision
  moments are computable, the Hankel blocks are positive semidefinite, and the
  finite fiber-size multiset is recovered by finite Prony reconstruction.
\item Finite-fiber certificates do not force finite-state transfer.  For every
  computable sequence \(a_m\ge1\), a one-spike finite-fiber cover preserves the
  Zeckendorf normal forms, compiled operations, and readouts, while its second
  collision moment is
  \[
    S_2^\sharp(m)=(a_m+1)^2+F_{m+2}-1 .
  \]
  With \(a_m=m!\), this moment has no fixed finite-matrix transfer
  representation, no constant-coefficient recurrence, and no rational
  generating series in the resolution variable.
\item The Richardson normal-form obstruction is a conditional interface.  Any
  finite pure-EML triple whose real-domain meanings have been proved to be
  \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative square root yields a transported
  fragment with no computable finite semantic normalizer.  The named
  printed-root instance \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\) is conditional exactly on
  the cited package \((H_{\mathcal W})\); the finite printed table alone is not
  used as an analytic proof of those identities.
\end{enumerate}
Thus expression representatives, computable protocol normal forms with
semantic readouts, finite-fiber certificates, finite-state transfer laws, and
semantic equality normalizers are distinct data.  The four structural clauses
above are unconditional in the stated public-record and internal senses; the
printed-root Richardson specialization is conditional precisely on
\((H_{\mathcal W})\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first item is \Cref{thm:eml-universality}, with its public-record and
source-boundary refinements
\Cref{thm:eml-public-record-audit,prop:eml-import-dependency-cut}.  These
results assert finite principal-branch EML representatives for the named basis
\(\mathcal B_{36}\) and explicitly isolate that import from any normalizer,
equality procedure, or Richardson witness identity; the no-upgrade statement
on the transported Richardson subfragment is
\Cref{thm:no-hidden-L0-semantic-normalizer}.

The second item is \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}.  Its computable data
are precisely the greedy Zeckendorf normalizer, decidable equality of finite
legal words, and the compiled operations \(\oplus\) and \(\otimes\) obtained by
valuation transport; \Cref{cor:zeckendorf-computability-locus} records that no
bounded-window or intrinsic digit-local arithmetic theorem is used.  The two
semantic readout identities are the classical composition identities
\(g^{\circ(m+n)}=g^{\circ m}\circ g^{\circ n}\) and
\(I_{mn}=I_m\circ I_n\), as proved in \Cref{thm:two-level} and used in
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}.

The one-orbit multiplication obstruction in the second item is
\Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, sharpened by
\Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification,cor:one-orbit-obstruction-boundary,cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}.
The proof classifies every same-level ambient-product readout on one free
monogenic orbit by its prime-weight exponent skeleton and obtains the explicit
prime-power collision.

The third item is the fixed-resolution finite-atomic certificate theorem
\Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}, together with the finite multiset skeleton
\Cref{prop:classical-finite-atomic-skeleton,thm:L2-fiber-multiset-factorization}.
Those results prove computability of the collision moments, Hankel positivity,
and finite Prony recovery from the fiber-size multiset at fixed resolution.
The fourth item is the transfer separation
\Cref{thm:L2-finite-fiber-no-transfer-content,thm:finite-state-strictness}:
\Cref{thm:finite-fiber-cover-realization,lem:cover-preserves-protocol} realize
one-spike computable covers without changing the \(L1\) protocol data, and
\Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget} rules out finite-matrix transfer for
\(a_m=m!\).

For the Richardson obstruction, choose either of the two witness routes.  If a
finite pure-EML replacement triple is supplied with proved identities for
\(\pi\), sine on \(\RR\), and the nonnegative square root on
\(\RR_{\ge0}\), then
\Cref{thm:replacement-triple-richardson-unconditional,prop:three-witness-stability}
construct the domain-preserving Richardson translation, and
\Cref{lem:transported-partial-normalizer-obstruction,lem:richardson-fragment}
rule out a computable finite semantic normalizer.  For the named printed roots,
\Cref{ass:HW,thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface,thm:printed-root-richardson-conditional-clause}
provide exactly the conditional specialization through \((H_{\mathcal W})\),
while
\Cref{prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface,prop:printed-root-conditional-only,thm:no-unverified-richardson-instance,cor:no-unconditional-named-root-consequence}
show that the finite table and source locks alone do not prove the needed
real-domain identities.  Thus the named printed-root Richardson clause is
conditional, and the unconditional route is only the verified-triple theorem.
These clauses prove the compact hierarchy spine.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Subordinate finite-fiber addendum]\label{cor:main-L2-addendum}
The finite-resolution Zeckendorf fibers give the \(L2\) certificate layer used
in this article: computable collision moments, Hankel-positive moment matrices,
and finite Prony reconstruction at each fixed resolution.  These are generic
finite-atomic consequences of finite fibers.  They do not imply finite-state
transfer in the resolution parameter: computable finite-fiber covers can
preserve the same normal forms, valuation, compiled operations, and readouts
while making the second collision moments exceed every fixed finite-matrix
transfer budget.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The fixed-resolution certificate package is
\Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ,prop:classical-finite-atomic-skeleton,thm:L2-fiber-multiset-factorization}:
finite fibers give computable collision moments, Gram-factor Hankel positivity,
and finite Prony reconstruction from the finite atomic moment sequence.  These
proofs depend only on the finite multiset of fiber sizes at the chosen
resolution.  The finite-state separation is the cover-level theorem
\Cref{thm:finite-fiber-cover-realization,lem:cover-preserves-protocol,prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget,thm:finite-state-strictness,thm:L2-budget-spine,thm:one-spike-finite-state-boundary}:
arbitrary computable positive fiber-size data can be realized without changing
the \(L1\) protocol readouts, whereas every fixed finite-matrix transfer
coefficient has exponential-growth and rational-generating-series constraints.
Choosing a one-spike computable cover such as the factorial spike violates
that budget, proving the asserted subordinate \(L2\) strictness.
\end{proof}
\begin{dependencyremark}[Three-anchor excision]
\label{thm:three-anchor-excision}
The three externally classical or conditional ingredients in
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} are logically excisable in the following exact
sense.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Replacing Odrzywo{\l}ek's imported \(L0\) representation theorem by any
  other representative-existence theorem for a principal-branch EML subbasis
  changes only the positive expression-universality clause for the named
  basis.  It does not affect the Zeckendorf \(L1\) protocol, the one-orbit
  multiplication obstruction, the finite-fiber cover construction, the
  finite-state transfer obstruction, or the Richardson normalizer theorem once
  its explicit three-witness interface is supplied.
\item Removing the cited package \((H_{\mathcal W})\) removes exactly the named
  printed-root Richardson specialization for
  \((U_{47},U_{111},U_{126})\).  The unconditional replacement-triple theorem
  remains: any specified finite pure EML triple whose real-domain meanings are
  proved to be \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative square root gives the same
  transported Richardson normal-form obstruction.
\item The Hankel-positive and Prony-reconstructible parts of the \(L2\) clause
  are precisely the fixed-resolution finite-atomic multiset skeleton.  Removing
  them removes only that positive certificate packaging; it does not remove the
  finite-state strictness theorem, whose obstruction is the failure of a fixed
  finite-matrix transfer law in the resolution parameter.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the new structural content of the article is carried by the
Richardson normal-form obstruction under its explicit witness interface, the
free-monogenic-orbit multiplication obstruction, and the finite-state transfer
separation.  The imported \(L0\) theorem is background support, the printed-root
route is conditional on \((H_{\mathcal W})\), and the Hankel--Prony facts are
subordinate finite-atomic tools.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Justification.}
For (1), inspect the proof of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.  The only use of the
full calculator basis \(\mathcal B_{36}\) is the first displayed clause, where
\Cref{thm:eml-universality} supplies finite EML representatives for the named
principal-branch primitives.  The proofs of the Zeckendorf protocol and the
one-orbit obstruction use only the definitions of
\Cref{sec:L1} and \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction,thm:one-orbit-readout-classification}.  The finite-fiber cover and finite-state strictness proofs use the fixed
normal-form sets \(X_m\), the computable cover definition, and the growth
budget of \Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget,thm:finite-state-strictness}.  The Richardson normalizer theorem uses the internally proved core EML
witnesses plus an explicit triple for \(\pi\), sine, and the nonnegative square
root.  None of these arguments invokes representatives for the remaining
members of \(\mathcal B_{36}\).  Thus changing the imported representative
existence result changes exactly the positive \(L0\) basis statement.

For (2), \Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface} identifies
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) with the three real-domain identities for the fixed printed
strings \(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\), while
\Cref{prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface,prop:no-third-richardson-interface}
prove that the source table, recurrence, fingerprints, and checker do not
themselves supply those identities and that no further non-core analytic input
is used.  If \((H_{\mathcal W})\) is removed, exactly those three identities for
the named printed roots are unavailable, so the named specialization cannot be
formed.  In contrast, \Cref{prop:three-witness-stability} assumes the three
displayed real-domain identities for an arbitrary finite replacement triple and
then constructs the effective Richardson translation.  Combining that
translation with \Cref{lem:transported-partial-normalizer-obstruction} and
Richardson undecidability as recorded in \Cref{lem:richardson-fragment} gives
the same normal-form contradiction without the printed roots.

For (3), fix a resolution \(m\).  The \(L2\) finite-fiber package depends on the
finite multiset of fiber sizes
\(\{|\mathrm{NF}_m^{-1}(n)|:n\in N_m\}\).  The Gram-factor proof of Hankel
positivity and the finite Prony reconstruction in
\Cref{prop:classical-finite-atomic-skeleton,thm:L2-fiber-multiset-factorization}
use only that finite multiset; they impose no relation among different
resolutions.  The finite-state strictness proof is different: by
\Cref{thm:finite-fiber-cover-realization}, computable positive fiber-size data
can be realized as computable covers without changing the \(L1\) readouts, and
\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness} chooses such data so that the second moment
violates the exponential budget forced by every fixed finite-matrix coefficient
sequence.  Therefore the finite-atomic Hankel--Prony package is not the source
of the strictness theorem; the strictness rests on the finite-state growth
budget.  The three excision claims prove the announced classification of the
article's load-bearing content.
\end{dependencyremark}

\begin{unnumbereddependencyremark}[Detailed hierarchy dependencies]
With the definitions made below, the single-primitive hierarchy has two
primary strictness obstructions, recorded in
\Cref{thm:obstruction-spine}, together with four structural clauses and
one Richardson interface clause whose unconditional form is the verified
replacement-triple theorem.  In the protocol clauses, \(A\) is any
nonempty set and \(g\colon A\to A\) is any endomorphism of infinite
order.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The EML system \((\eml,1)\) is \(L0\)
  expression-universal for Odrzywo{\l}ek's scientific-calculator basis
  \(\mathcal B_{36}\), in the principal-branch sense imported from that
  external representation theorem and isolated in
  \Cref{cor:external-compiler-isolation,thm:eml-public-record-audit,prop:eml-import-dependency-cut}.
  Independently, the manuscript proves
  an internal core EML witness package for the algebraic constructors
  later used in the Richardson interface; only the full \(\mathcal B_{36}\)
  L0 clause is imported.
\item The Zeckendorf address system, equipped with its stable normal
  forms and the two composition readouts
  \(c_n\mapsto g^{\circ n}\) and \(c_n\mapsto I_n\), is
  \(L1\) protocol-universal for \(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).  The
  multiplication readout is genuinely extra protocol structure:
  multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be read from the ambient
  product on one free monogenic orbit, even after an injective recoding
  of the positive integers; indeed all such one-orbit multiplicative
  readouts are the prime-weight skeletons classified in
  \Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification}; the boundary of this
  obstruction is exactly the same-level ambient-product hypothesis of
  \Cref{cor:one-orbit-obstruction-boundary}, and each skeleton has an
  explicit prime-power collision.  The compiled arithmetic is exactly
  valuation transport through ordinary integer arithmetic followed by
  greedy re-normalization.  No bounded-window or online Zeckendorf
  multiplication theorem is part of this clause: in fact
  \Cref{thm:no-finite-level-arithmetic} shows that no single finite
  residue level can both separate the stable addresses and carry the
  stable arithmetic used by the protocol.
\item Subordinately, the Zeckendorf finite-resolution data are \(L2\)
  certificate-universal in the finite-fiber sense: the normalization
  fibers have computable collision moments, Hankel-positive moment
  matrices, and finite Prony reconstruction with the computable bound
  \(2F_{m+2}-1\) at resolution \(m\).  This is the finite-atomic
  certificate supplied by the fold fibers, not a new finite-window
  moment theory and not the structural strictness mechanism of the
  \(L2\) part; \Cref{thm:L2-finite-fiber-no-transfer-content} records that it
  carries no cross-resolution transfer content, and the latter is the
  finite-state budget spine of \Cref{thm:L2-budget-spine}.
\item The structural \(L2\) strictness point is instead the finite-state
  transfer obstruction for the cover category just defined: finite-fiber
  certificate data do not force a finite-state transfer law.  More sharply,
  computable finite-fiber covers over the fixed Zeckendorf normal-form sets are
  exactly arbitrary computable positive fiber-size data over those sets.  Hence
  there are covers preserving the protocol normal forms and readouts whose
  second collision moments have superexponential growth.  Thus the L2 novelty
  is the cover-level finite-state separation isolated in
  \Cref{thm:finite-fiber-skeleton-extremal,prop:L2-novelty-finite-state-separation,prop:cover-only-strictness-boundary,thm:L2-budget-spine,thm:one-spike-finite-state-boundary}.
  This violates the finite-state skeleton proved in
  \Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget}: every fixed finite-matrix
  moment has exponential growth, a constant-coefficient linear recurrence,
  and a rational generating series in the resolution parameter.
\item The Richardson normal-form obstruction is first a verified-triple
  theorem: for every specified finite triple of pure EML terms whose
  real-domain identities realizing \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the principal
  nonnegative square root have been proved, the translated Richardson
  fragment has no computable finite normal-form comparison deciding identity
  to zero.  \Cref{prop:richardson-witness-dependency-cut} proves that these
  three identities are exactly the dependency cut for any unconditional
  replacement-triple instance of \Cref{thm:separation}.  The special
  printed-root instance for \(U_{47},U_{111},U_{126}\) uses only the cited
  interface \((H_{\mathcal W})\) of \Cref{ass:HW}, as organized in
  \Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface,prop:printed-root-conditional-only,thm:richardson-interface-boundary};
  it is not a claim that the finite printed table alone proves the analytic
  identities.
\end{enumerate}
The load-bearing \(L0\)--\(L1\) strictness claims are the two clauses isolated in
\Cref{thm:obstruction-spine}; the \(L2\) strictness claim is the finite-state
growth obstruction, while the finite-atomic Hankel--Prony clause is only the
  subordinate positive certificate clause.  By \Cref{prop:printed-root-conditional-only}, the theorem does not assert a
Richardson obstruction from the printed table alone.  The
verified-triple version of \Cref{thm:separation} is unconditional exactly once
the three real-domain identities for the chosen finite EML triple have been
proved, as isolated in \Cref{prop:richardson-witness-dependency-cut}.  The
printed-root version uses the external symbolic verification of
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) recorded in \Cref{ass:HW}.  Moreover, this is a transported-fragment
separation: it is not an absolute impossibility
  theorem for every expression-universal single-primitive protocol outside
  the semantic-compatible Richardson interface.  The finite-state clause is
  similarly cover-level: it is not a theorem that the unmodified Zeckendorf
  folds \(\Fold_m\colon\Omega_m\to X_m\) lack a transfer matrix.
Thus expression representatives, computable protocol normal forms with
semantic readouts, finite-fiber certificate data, and finite-state
transfer laws are distinct kinds of structure in the single-primitive
setting.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Justification.}
The first clause is \Cref{thm:eml-universality,cor:external-compiler-isolation,thm:eml-public-record-audit,prop:eml-import-dependency-cut}, with the internal core
fallback supplied by \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}.  The second

  \Cref{prop:value-transport-exact-interface,thm:no-finite-level-arithmetic},
together with the
monogenic-orbit obstruction \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, the

\begin{corollary}[Compact hierarchy form]\label{cor:compact-hierarchy-form}
The article proves the following journal-facing hierarchy statement.  There
is a single-primitive expression system, namely the principal-branch EML
system \((\eml,1)\), which is \(L0\) expression-universal for
Odrzywo{\l}ek's calculator basis in the imported representative-existence
sense.  There is a single-primitive protocol system, namely the Zeckendorf
normal-form protocol, which is \(L1\) protocol-universal for
\(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).  The protocol multiplication readout cannot be
collapsed to the ambient product on one free monogenic orbit; every such
one-orbit ambient-product readout is a prime-weight skeleton and has a
prime-power collision.  The finite-fiber certificate layer gives computable
collision moments, Hankel positivity, and finite Prony reconstruction, but
these finite atomic certificates do not force finite-state transfer: there
are computable finite-fiber covers preserving the same normal forms and
readouts whose second collision moments grow faster than every exponential
sequence.  Finally, for every verified finite EML triple representing
\(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative square root on the stated real domains,
the transported Richardson fragment has no computable finite normal-form
comparison deciding identity to zero; the printed-root instance is the
specialization through the cited package \((H_{\mathcal W})\).

\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The EML expression clause is the first clause of
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.  The Zeckendorf protocol clause is the second
clause of that theorem.  The one-orbit non-collapse and its prime-weight
skeleton are \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction,thm:one-orbit-readout-classification,cor:one-orbit-obstruction-boundary}.  The finite-fiber
positive certificate clause is \Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}; the
finite-state separation and superexponential cover are
\Cref{thm:finite-fiber-cover-realization,thm:finite-state-strictness,thm:one-spike-finite-state-boundary}.  The verified-triple Richardson
obstruction is \Cref{thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface,thm:separation}; the
printed-root cited-interface boundary is
\Cref{ass:HW,thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface,thm:richardson-interface-boundary}.  These are precisely the clauses collected in
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}, so the compact formulation follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{dependencyremark}[Boundary for the Richardson clause]
\label{cor:richardson-submission-boundary}
The Richardson component of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy,thm:obstruction-spine}
has exactly the following status.  The first four structural hierarchy
clauses are unconditional and do not use the three printed roots.  The
printed-root Richardson clause uses the cited public interface
\((H_{\mathcal W})\) for \(W_{\pi}\), \(W_{\sin}\), and
\(W_{\sqrt{}}\).  A replacement Richardson clause uses any finite pure-EML triple
\((\mathsf P,\mathsf S,\mathsf R)\) whose identities
\[
  \mathsf P=\pi,
  \qquad
  \mathsf S(x)=\sin x\quad(x\in\RR),
  \qquad
  \mathsf R(t)=\sqrt t\quad(t\in\RR_{\ge0})
\]
are proved before applying the Richardson translation.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Justification.}
The unconditionality assertion for the non-Richardson clauses follows from
the proof of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} and \Cref{cor:main-L2-addendum}:
those clauses cite only the EML representation theorem, Zeckendorf protocol theorem, monogenic-orbit
obstruction package, finite-fiber certificate theorem, and finite-state
growth obstruction, and the proof explicitly notes that none of these inputs
uses \(W_{\pi}\), \(W_{\sin}\), or \(W_{\sqrt{}}\).  The printed-root
Richardson clause is \Cref{thm:separation} in its first alternative, whose
proof factors through
\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface,thm:richardson-interface-boundary}
and therefore through precisely the three identities named
\((H_{\mathcal W})\).  The same theorem and
\Cref{prop:three-witness-stability,thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface}
give the replacement alternative only after the displayed identities have
already been proved for a specified finite triple.  Finally,
\Cref{prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface,prop:printed-root-conditional-only,prop:no-third-richardson-interface,thm:no-unverified-richardson-instance}
prove that the finite printed table, string locks, and provenance data do not
by themselves supply those analytic identities, and
\Cref{thm:exact-source-boundary-richardson-branch} states the resulting source
boundary as a theorem in the main separation chain.  Hence the manuscript has
no third route beyond the cited printed package and verified replacement
triples.
\end{dependencyremark}
\begin{theorem}[Richardson obstruction interface]\label{thm:richardson-obstruction-interface}
The Richardson normal-form obstruction in \Cref{thm:separation} has the
following exact interface.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For the printed roots of \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}, it
  uses precisely the three real-domain identities in
  \((H_{\mathcal W})\) for \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the principal
  nonnegative square root, supplied by
  \Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface}.
\item For any specified finite replacement triple of pure EML terms for which
  the same three real-domain identities have been proved, the obstruction is
  unconditional for the resulting translated fragment; this paper does not
  exhibit a concrete internal replacement triple.
\item The L0 representative clause, Zeckendorf protocol clause, one-orbit
  obstruction clause, and positive realizations in \Cref{cor:main-positive-realizations} do not
  use the printed roots; the Richardson part of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} uses exactly the cited printed-root or replacement
  three-witness interface stated in the preceding two items.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the paper claims the L0--L1 Richardson separation for the printed
roots only under \((H_{\mathcal W})\), while the Zenodo code/provenance
object remains non-load-bearing for the hierarchy theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is exactly
\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-interface}: the printed long
roots enter the Richardson translation only through the three displayed
real-domain identities, and
\Cref{prop:printed-root-conditional-only,prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface} proves that the
straight-line table alone is not an internal analytic proof of those
identities in the partial principal-branch EML semantics.  The second
assertion is \Cref{thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface}, equivalently
\Cref{prop:three-witness-stability}, which constructs the same effective
Richardson translation from an arbitrary specified finite triple after those
identities have been proved and then applies the transported normalizer
obstruction.  By \Cref{prop:printed-root-conditional-only}, this is a
schematic replacement interface rather than a concrete internal triple
exhibited here.

For the third assertion, inspect the proof of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} and \Cref{cor:main-L2-addendum}:
the non-Richardson clauses cite only \Cref{thm:eml-universality},
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ,thm:level-zero-obstruction,thm:one-orbit-readout-classification,cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy},
\Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ,thm:hankel-psd,thm:prony,prop:finite-fiber-certificate-taxonomy,cor:L2-load-split}, and
\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness,cor:finite-state-growth-budget,cor:hankel-psd-independent,lem:cover-preserves-protocol}.
None of these results assumes \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  The Richardson branch is
\Cref{thm:separation} and uses the cited printed-root interface exactly as
described in the first two items.  The corollary of positive realizations
extracts only the L0 and L1 positives from the main theorem, together with
the subordinate finite-fiber positive from \Cref{cor:main-L2-addendum}.
The Zenodo citation is used only to identify the public
the printed witness provenance; no inference in the unconditional
hierarchy proof appeals to execution of that code.
\end{proof}
