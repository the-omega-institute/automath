\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Universality statements often hide a difference in kind.  The
Sheffer stroke generates the Boolean operations \cite{Sheffer1913};
lambda abstraction gives a calculus of effective computation
\cite{Church1936}; and combinatory logic replaces variables by a
finite stock of combinators \cite{Schonfinkel1924}.  In such
examples it matters whether the assertion is merely that expressions
exist, or also that representatives can be chosen and compared
effectively.

The same distinction occurs for recent one-operation presentations
of elementary functions.  Odrzywo{\l}ek showed that
\[
  \eml(x,y)=e^x-\operatorname{Log}y
\]
together with the constant \(1\) represents the operations in a
standard scientific-calculator basis
\cite{Odrzywolek2026SingleBinaryOperator}.  Thus a
large elementary language can be encoded by binary trees with a
single internal label.  This is a useful expression theorem, but it
does not itself choose a normal form among the many trees defining
the same function.

We shall distinguish three levels.  Level \(L0\), \emph{expression
universality}, only asks for some term over a single binary
operation.  Level \(L1\), \emph{protocol universality}, asks in
addition for computable normal forms and compiled normal-form
operations, together with semantic readout identities.  Our model
example uses Zeckendorf normal forms only as a computable addressing
system: operations are transported through the valuation bijection with
\(\NN\), and the elementary Church-iteration identities then read
addition and multiplication on \(\NN=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\) at two
successive composition levels.  Level \(L2\), \emph{certificate universality},
attaches finite certificates to the fibers of a normalizing map.
Hankel positivity and finite Prony reconstruction are then automatic
finite-fiber facts, whereas a finite-state transfer formula for the
same moments is extra structure.

The results below are separations of these structures, not new
proofs of local or online Zeckendorf arithmetic, Church iteration,
elementary function identities, or finite moment reconstruction.  The EML
operation is expression-universal for Odrzywo{\l}ek's basis.  Separately,
for any finite EML triple whose real-domain identities realize
\(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the nonnegative square root, Richardson's theorem
rules out a computable canonical form on the translated Richardson
fragment even for the fixed predicate of identity to zero on the
transported real domain; the particular printed roots used for this
illustration use \((H_{\mathcal W})\) as supplied by the cited
principal-branch symbolic verification, not by the source-level table
alone.  In the
  Zeckendorf protocol, addition is
  read on a free monogenic orbit, while multiplication cannot be read at
  that same semantic level by the ambient product on one free monogenic
  orbit, even after injectively recoding the positive integers.  This is the
  exact one-orbit obstruction; it is not a lower bound for protocols that
  move multiplication to a different semantic level or use a different
  readout law.  The certificate result is likewise a generic
  finite-fiber taxonomy statement rather than a new Hankel--Prony
  reconstruction theorem; the original L2 contribution is the separation of
  these finite-atomic certificates from finite-state transfer.  Finally,
  finite Hankel--Prony certificates do
not imply a finite-state transfer law: one can replace the finite
sets over the same normal forms by computable finite-fiber covers,
leaving the arithmetic readouts unchanged and forcing the second
collision moments to grow faster than any exponential sequence.

This scope is intentionally disjoint from several nearby Zeckendorf
and finite-resolution projects.  We do not study stable digit-local
Zeckendorf arithmetic, finite-window Zeckendorf thermodynamics,
Zeckendorf fiber thermodynamics, folded histogram laws for the actual
finite-window fibers, finite-observation escape rates, or normalization
automata.
The Zeckendorf material used here is only the computable normal-form
address system needed for the protocol readouts and for the abstract
finite-fiber certificate comparison.
Throughout this protocol discussion, computability lives in the finite
normal forms and compiled operations; the maps into endomorphism sorts are
semantic readout identities unless those sorts are separately given
effective presentations.

\begin{proposition}[External-dependency non-overlap audit]
\label{prop:companion-reference-audit}
Stable digit-local Zeckendorf arithmetic, finite-window Zeckendorf
thermodynamics, folded histogram laws for the actual finite-window
fibers, finite-observation escape rates, and normalization automata are
not proof inputs for the hierarchy theorem.  The present article uses
only the greedy Zeckendorf normal form and its valuation identity, the
transported semiring operations on \(\NN\), finite residue sets, and
generic finite-fiber moment facts proved below.  It does not use, prove,
or require a digit-local stable arithmetic algorithm, an inverse-limit
arithmetic theorem, finite-window fiber thermodynamics, transfer-matrix
asymptotics for the actual Zeckendorf fibers, or folded histogram laws.
Accordingly, no companion-paper citation is a proof dependency in this
article; until such companion manuscripts have stable public identifiers,
they are mentioned only by topic in this non-overlap statement.  No title,
slug, repository path, internal project name, or unpublished identifier for
such a companion project is used below as a citable mathematical input.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The stable normalizer used in \Cref{sec:L1} is obtained from the greedy
Zeckendorf theorem and the valuation map; the proofs of the protocol
readouts cite only the internal normalizer, transported semiring, and
composition-readout results.  The finite-resolution material used in
\Cref{sec:L2} consists of the finite sets \(X_m\), their finite fibers,
and the elementary Hankel--Prony facts for finite atomic measures.  Those
arguments are proved by finite enumeration and by the generic moment
lemmas of \Cref{sec:L2}; they do not invoke any result about stable
digit-local arithmetic or finite-window thermodynamic asymptotics.  The
exclusions above delimit nearby projects and prevent overlap, while every assertion used in
\Cref{thm:main-hierarchy,cor:main-positive-realizations} is supplied by
the internal theorem chain and the standard external references cited
there.  Since the proof just listed all non-internal inputs used by the
hierarchy theorem, and since none of them is an unpublished companion
identifier, replacing a companion topic by a slug or private title would add
a new proof dependency not present in the theorem chain.  Such a dependency
is therefore excluded unless it first becomes a stable public bibliographic
identifier and is separately cited as an external theorem.
\end{proof}

Section~\ref{sec:L0} records \(L0\) expression universality for the
EML input.  Section~\ref{sec:L1} gives \(L1\) protocol universality
through the Zeckendorf protocol and the level-zero obstruction for

Section~\ref{sec:strictness} proves the separations.  The appendix

\begin{theorem}[Obstruction spine of the hierarchy]
\label{thm:obstruction-spine}
With the definitions made below, the non-collapse from expression
universality to protocol universality is witnessed by two independent
obstructions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For the EML expression grammar, any finite triple of pure EML terms
  whose principal-branch real-domain meanings are \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and
  the nonnegative square root transports Richardson's real elementary
  fragment into pure EML terms.  On that transported fragment there is no
  computable finite normal form with decidable equality whose comparison
  with the translated zero term decides identity to zero.  This is
  unconditional for replacement triples whose three real-domain identities
  have been proved.  For the printed roots of
  \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}, the route is instead via the cited
  interface \((H_{\mathcal W})\) organized in
  \Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-verified}.
\item In any monoid, multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be represented
  injectively by the ambient product on one free monogenic orbit generated
  by an infinite-order element.  Hence a protocol that separates the
  positive integers and reads addition on such an orbit must move the
  multiplication readout to additional protocol structure, as the
  Zeckendorf power-operator readout does.  The obstruction is sharp in the
  one-orbit class: \Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification} classifies
  all ambient-product multiplicative readouts by prime weights and shows
  that every such skeleton collapses a prime or two distinct prime powers.
\end{enumerate}
The finite-fiber certificate clauses below are secondary: they separate
finite atomic certificate data from finite-state transfer laws, but they
are not used to prove either obstruction above.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is exactly \Cref{thm:separation}, with its two proof
interfaces spelled out in
\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-verified,cor:printed-richardson-public-interface,thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface,cor:richardson-witness-alternative}:
the printed roots, with \((H_{\mathcal W})\) supplied by
\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-verified}, give the transported Richardson obstruction,
and replacement triples with proved real-domain identities give the same
obstruction.
The second assertion is \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, sharpened by
the classification theorem \Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification},
its exact boundary \Cref{cor:one-orbit-obstruction-boundary}, and its
protocol dichotomy \Cref{cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}; the
proof is the prime-power collision in the exponent skeleton
\Cref{lem:level-zero-exponent-skeleton}.  The last sentence follows from
the location and proof inputs of the two cited obstruction theorems: the
Richardson theorem uses the EML witness interface and Richardson
undecidability, while the monogenic-orbit theorem uses only the exponent
skeleton in a free orbit.  The finite-fiber certificate results enter
later through \Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ,thm:finite-state-strictness}
and are not cited in either obstruction proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hierarchy theorem with Richardson interface]\label{thm:main-hierarchy}
With the definitions made below, the single-primitive hierarchy has two
primary strictness obstructions, recorded in
\Cref{thm:obstruction-spine}, together with four structural clauses and
one explicitly conditional Richardson interface clause.  In the protocol clauses, \(A\) is any
nonempty set and \(g\colon A\to A\) is any endomorphism of infinite
order.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The EML system \((\eml,1)\) is \(L0\)
  expression-universal for Odrzywo{\l}ek's scientific-calculator basis
  \(\mathcal B_{36}\), in the principal-branch sense imported from that
  external representation theorem and isolated in
  \Cref{cor:external-compiler-isolation,prop:eml-import-dependency-cut,thm:expression-import-quarantine}.
  Independently, the manuscript proves
  an internal core EML witness package for the algebraic constructors
  later used in the Richardson interface; only the full \(\mathcal B_{36}\)
  L0 clause is imported.
\item The Zeckendorf address system, equipped with its stable normal
  forms and the two composition readouts
  \(c_n\mapsto g^{\circ n}\) and \(c_n\mapsto I_n\), is
  \(L1\) protocol-universal for \(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\).  The
  multiplication readout is genuinely extra protocol structure:
  multiplication on \(\NN_{\ge1}\) cannot be read from the ambient
  product on one free monogenic orbit, even after an injective recoding
  of the positive integers; indeed all such one-orbit multiplicative
  readouts are the prime-weight skeletons classified in
  \Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification}; the boundary of this
  obstruction is exactly the same-level ambient-product hypothesis of
  \Cref{cor:one-orbit-obstruction-boundary}, and each skeleton has an
  explicit prime-power collision.  The compiled arithmetic is exactly
  valuation transport through ordinary integer arithmetic followed by
  greedy re-normalization.  No bounded-window or online Zeckendorf
  multiplication theorem is part of this clause: in fact
  \Cref{thm:no-finite-level-arithmetic} shows that no single finite
  residue level can both separate the stable addresses and carry the
  stable arithmetic used by the protocol.
\item Subordinately, the Zeckendorf finite-resolution data are \(L2\)
  certificate-universal in the finite-fiber sense: the normalization
  fibers have computable collision moments, Hankel-positive moment
  matrices, and finite Prony reconstruction with the computable bound
  \(2F_{m+2}-1\) at resolution \(m\).  This is the finite-atomic
  certificate supplied by the fold fibers, not a new finite-window
  moment theory and not the structural strictness mechanism of the
  \(L2\) part.
\item The structural \(L2\) strictness point is instead the finite-state
  transfer obstruction: finite-fiber certificate data do not force a finite-state
  transfer law.  More sharply, computable finite-fiber covers over the fixed
  Zeckendorf normal-form sets are exactly arbitrary computable positive
  fiber-size data over those sets.  Hence there are covers preserving the
  protocol normal forms and readouts whose second collision moments have
  superexponential growth.  Thus the L2 novelty is the finite-state
  separation isolated in \Cref{prop:L2-novelty-finite-state-separation}.
  This violates the finite-state skeleton proved in
  \Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget}: every fixed finite-matrix
  moment has exponential growth, a constant-coefficient linear recurrence,
  and a rational generating series in the resolution parameter.
\item The Richardson normal-form obstruction is exactly the interface of
  \Cref{thm:separation}: for the printed roots of
  \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate} it uses
  \((H_{\mathcal W})\) as organized in
  \Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-verified,cor:printed-richardson-public-interface}, while for any finite replacement triple of pure
  EML terms whose real-domain identities realize \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and
  the principal nonnegative square root, the same obstruction is
  unconditional for the translated Richardson fragment.
\end{enumerate}
The load-bearing \(L0\)--\(L1\) strictness claims are the two clauses isolated in
\Cref{thm:obstruction-spine}; the \(L2\) strictness claim is the finite-state
growth obstruction, while the finite-atomic Hankel--Prony clause is only the
  subordinate positive certificate clause.  The theorem does not assert an
unconditional Richardson obstruction from the printed table alone.  The
printed-root version of \Cref{thm:separation} uses the external symbolic
verification of \((H_{\mathcal W})\); a replacement version is available
for any finite EML triple whose real-domain identities for \(\pi\),
\(\sin\), and \(\sqrt{\phantom{x}}\) are proved independently.
Thus expression representatives, computable protocol normal forms with
semantic readouts, finite-fiber certificate data, and finite-state
transfer laws are distinct kinds of structure in the single-primitive
setting.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first clause is \Cref{thm:eml-universality,cor:external-compiler-isolation,prop:eml-import-dependency-cut,thm:expression-import-quarantine}, with the internal core
fallback supplied by \Cref{prop:internal-core-eml-witnesses}.  The second
clause is \Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ}, with the exact
  value-transport interface isolated in
  \Cref{prop:value-transport-exact-interface,thm:no-finite-level-arithmetic},
together with the
monogenic-orbit obstruction \Cref{thm:level-zero-obstruction}, the
classification of its full one-orbit skeleton in
\Cref{thm:one-orbit-readout-classification}, the boundary corollary
\Cref{cor:one-orbit-obstruction-boundary}, and its dichotomy corollary
\Cref{cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy}.  These two
strictness mechanisms are summarized in \Cref{thm:obstruction-spine}.  The third clause is
\Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ}, whose proof invokes only the
finite-atomic Hankel and Prony results
\Cref{thm:hankel-psd,thm:prony} and whose generic load is isolated in
\Cref{prop:finite-fiber-certificate-taxonomy,prop:L2-novelty-finite-state-separation,cor:L2-load-split}.  The
fourth clause is \Cref{thm:finite-fiber-cover-realization,thm:finite-state-strictness}, sharpened by the
finite-state skeleton \Cref{prop:finite-state-coefficient-budget} and in
\Cref{cor:finite-state-growth-budget,cor:hankel-psd-independent}, with
preservation of the underlying protocol supplied by
\Cref{lem:cover-preserves-protocol}.  The fifth clause is exactly
\Cref{thm:separation}, whose proof is routed through the effective
translation \Cref{lem:richardson-translation}, the locked finite witness
library \Cref{cor:eml-witness-locking}, the dependency audit
\Cref{prop:locked-three-witness-interface,cor:printed-richardson-public-interface}, and the replacement-triple
form \Cref{prop:three-witness-stability,thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface}.

No step in the first four clauses invokes the Richardson translation or the
three printed roots \(W_\pi\), \(W_{\sin}\), and \(W_{\sqrt{}}\).  The
Richardson clause invokes them only in the audited form just cited:
it uses the exact interface \((H_{\mathcal W})\) organized in
\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-verified} for the printed roots, and the
replacement-stability form \Cref{thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface}
shows that the same obstruction is unconditional for any finite
replacement triple with internally proved real-domain identities, while
\Cref{prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface,prop:no-third-richardson-interface,cor:submission-richardson-clause} record why the
printed table alone does not supply such a proof.  This proves the hierarchy
clauses without promoting the finite table and fingerprints to analytic
witness verification.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Richardson obstruction interface]\label{thm:richardson-obstruction-interface}
The Richardson normal-form obstruction in \Cref{thm:separation} has the
following exact interface.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For the printed roots of \Cref{lem:eml-ancillary-certificate}, it
  uses precisely the three real-domain identities in
  \((H_{\mathcal W})\) for \(\pi\), \(\sin\), and the principal
  nonnegative square root, supplied by
  \Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-verified}.
\item For any finite replacement triple of pure EML terms satisfying the
  same three real-domain identities, the obstruction is unconditional.
\item The first four clauses of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} and the
  positive realizations in \Cref{cor:main-positive-realizations} do not
  use the printed roots; the fifth clause uses exactly the cited printed-root or replacement
  three-witness interface stated in the preceding two items.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the paper claims the L0--L1 Richardson separation for the printed
roots only under \((H_{\mathcal W})\), while the Zenodo code/provenance
object remains non-load-bearing for the hierarchy theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is exactly
\Cref{thm:printed-richardson-witnesses-verified}: the printed long
roots enter the Richardson translation only through the three displayed
real-domain identities, and
\Cref{prop:printed-table-needs-analytic-interface} proves that the
straight-line table alone is not an internal analytic proof of those
identities in the partial principal-branch EML semantics.  The second
assertion is \Cref{thm:minimal-eml-witness-interface}, equivalently
\Cref{prop:three-witness-stability}, which constructs the same effective
Richardson translation from an arbitrary finite triple satisfying those
identities and then applies the transported normalizer obstruction.

For the third assertion, inspect the proof of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}:
its first four clauses cite only \Cref{thm:eml-universality},
\Cref{thm:zeckendorf-protocol-univ,thm:level-zero-obstruction,thm:one-orbit-readout-classification,cor:level-zero-protocol-dichotomy},
\Cref{prop:zeckendorf-cert-univ,thm:hankel-psd,thm:prony,prop:finite-fiber-certificate-taxonomy,cor:L2-load-split}, and
\Cref{thm:finite-state-strictness,cor:finite-state-growth-budget,cor:hankel-psd-independent,lem:cover-preserves-protocol}.
None of these results assumes \((H_{\mathcal W})\).  The fifth clause is
\Cref{thm:separation} and uses the cited printed-root interface exactly as
described in the first two items.  The corollary of positive realizations
extracts only the first three positive clauses of that theorem.  The
Zenodo citation is used only to identify the public
archive accompanying Odrzywo{\l}ek's external representation theorem and
the printed witness provenance; no inference in the unconditional
hierarchy proof appeals to execution of that code.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Positive realizations extracted from the hierarchy]
\label{cor:main-positive-realizations}
The positive realization clauses of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy} give the
following three single-primitive examples: the imported EML expression
realization for Odrzywo{\l}ek's basis \(\mathcal B_{36}\), the Zeckendorf
\(L1\) protocol for \(\{+,\times\}\) on \(\NN\) using the two readouts
\(c_n\mapsto g^{\circ n}\) and \(c_n\mapsto I_n\), and the Zeckendorf
finite-fiber \(L2\) certificate package consisting of computable collision
moments, Hankel positivity, and finite Prony reconstruction.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
These are respectively the first clause, the positive assertion in the
second clause, and the finite-fiber positive assertion in the third clause
of \Cref{thm:main-hierarchy}.  The finite-state obstruction clause of that
theorem is not part of this positive extraction.
\end{proof}
