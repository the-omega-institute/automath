\noindent\textbf{Manuscript:} A quartic cover of \(37\mathrm{a}1\) and its regular \(S_4\)-closure

\bigskip

We thank the editor and the referee for the careful reading and constructive report. The main revision is a reframing of the manuscript: we now present the \(S_4\)-Prym decomposition as an application of the known fourfold-cover Prym formalism, and emphasize the new contribution as the explicit arithmetic analysis of the present cover of \(37\mathrm{a}1\) and its branch cubic.

\section*{Response to the editor}

The editor asked for a clearer distinction between the known general theory used in the paper and the genuinely new arithmetic computations carried out in this example. We have revised the abstract, introduction, and Section~4 accordingly. In particular:
\begin{itemize}
\item The abstract now states that we ``specialize the known \(S_4\)-Prym decomposition for fourfold covers'' to the explicit cover.
\item The introduction now contains a table separating general input from what is explicit in the present paper.
\item Section~4 now begins with an explicit comparison with the fourfold-cover Prym formalism of Lange--Rodr{\'i}guez, and Theorem~4.8 is described as the specialization of this known formalism together with the concrete identification of the factors in this cover.
\item We added a reproducibility table and a separate \texttt{REPRODUCIBILITY.md} file.
\item We clarified the limited status of the Prym threefold \(Q\), revised the language throughout, and reordered the bibliography alphabetically by author.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Response to the referee}

\subsection*{Main concern: originality of the \(S_4\)-Prym decomposition}

We agree with the referee that the general \(S_4\)-Prym decomposition associated with a fourfold covering should not be presented as a new structural theorem. We have rewritten the manuscript so that the abstract decomposition is attributed to the known fourfold-cover Prym formalism, especially Lange--Rodr{\'i}guez, \emph{Decomposition of Jacobians by Prym Varieties}, Chapter~5, Section~5.7.

At the same time, we retained Theorem~4.8 because the theorem records the concrete realization in this example:
\[
\Jac(X)\sim_{\QQ}\Jac(X_A)\times E_{\mathrm{res}}^2\times E^3\times Q^3.
\]
The revised text states that the general pattern is known, while the concrete identifications of \(X_A\), \(E_{\mathrm{res}}\), \(E\), \(P\), and \(Q\) for this cover are part of the present work.

\subsection*{Required revision 1: Reframe the contribution}

We have reframed the abstract and introduction. The abstract now opens the structural statement with:
\begin{quote}
We specialize the known \(S_4\)-Prym decomposition for fourfold covers to this explicit cover.
\end{quote}
The introduction now states that the contribution is explicit rather than abstract, and that the point is not a new abstract decomposition theorem but a complete arithmetic realization of the known formalism for this regular \(S_4\)-quartic cover.

\subsection*{Required revision 2: Compare explicitly with Lange--Rodr{\'i}guez}

We added a direct comparison with Lange--Rodr{\'i}guez in the introduction and at the beginning of Section~4. We cite Chapter~5, Section~5.7 of their book. We also added Remark~4.7 before Theorem~4.8, explaining that the theorem should be read as a specialization of the known fourfold-cover Prym formalism and identifying what is specific to this cover: the equations for \(X_A\) and \(E_{\mathrm{res}}\), the conductor \(1147\) of \(E_{\mathrm{res}}\), the splitting \(P\sim_{\QQ}E_{\mathrm{res}}^2\), and the placement of the remaining multiplicity space in \(Q=\Prym(Y/E_{\mathrm{res}})\).

\subsection*{Required revision 3: Separate structural material from new arithmetic computations}

We added a table in the introduction with columns ``General input'' and ``What is explicit here.'' It separates the general \(S_4\)-Galois closure and Prym formalism from the computations specific to this cover: the concrete map \(y=\lambda^2+\xi\), the branch cubic \(c(y)\), the quotient curves and factors, the ray class field over \(\QQ(\sqrt{-111})\), the weight-one form, the mod-\(2\) torsion, and the local calculation \(\delta_3=1\).

\subsection*{Required revision 4: Clarify the role of computation}

We added a reproducibility table after Section~7. The table lists each computational claim, its location in the paper, and the exact script or verification file. We also added a separate file \texttt{REPRODUCIBILITY.md}, which records the role of each source file and the expected generated outputs. The table is now aligned with the actual source tree and does not refer to missing files.

\subsection*{Required revision 5: Avoid overstating incomplete arithmetic information}

We revised the abstract and relevant sections to avoid suggesting that the full conductor of \(\Jac(X_A)\) is known. The abstract now says that we analyze the tame bad reduction at \(31\) and \(37\), determine the \(2\)-adic and \(3\)-adic cluster pictures, and compute the wild contribution \(\delta_3=1\) at \(3\). The further-directions section continues to state that determining the remaining conductor information, including the exponent at \(2\), requires additional work.

We also clarified that the Frobenius data for \(\Jac(X_A)\) do not determine the full geometric endomorphism algebra. The wording ``irreducibility witnesses'' has been replaced by the more cautious ``irreducibility checks'' where appropriate.

\subsection*{Required revision 6: Clarify the status of the Prym threefold \(Q\)}

We added Remark~6.9 after Proposition~6.8 on \(Q=\Prym(Y/E_{\mathrm{res}})\). It states explicitly that \(Q\) is explicit through its Prym definition and cohomological factor, but that we do not give an affine or projective model for \(Y=X/C_4\), nor do we determine the conductor, endomorphism algebra, or full geometry of \(Q\). The trace table is described as a consistency check and arithmetic profile rather than a complete determination.

\section*{English and presentation}

We revised the language in the introduction and throughout the paper to avoid overstating the novelty of the structural decomposition and to distinguish structural input from example-specific computations. In particular:
\begin{itemize}
\item ``the source of a weight-1 cusp form'' was replaced by wording involving a weight-one cuspidal newform;
\item ``the resulting package is large enough'' was replaced by ``the resulting example is rich enough'';
\item ``irreducibility witnesses'' was replaced by ``irreducibility checks'';
\item ``the branch cubic collapses to'' was replaced by ``the branch cubic reduces to'';
\item ``Database information from the LMFDB is used'' was replaced by ``Data from the LMFDB are used.''
\end{itemize}
