\noindent\textbf{Manuscript:} A quartic cover of \(37\mathrm{a}1\) and its regular \(S_4\)-closure

\section*{Cover note to the editor}

We thank the editor and the referee for the careful second-round report. We have addressed all three minor revisions and all three presentation comments. While implementing the requested clarifications, we identified and corrected two issues in the previous version. In Proposition~6.11 (Proposition~6.10 in the previous version) the point-count formula itself was incorrect, and in Proposition~6.24 (Proposition~6.22 in the previous version) the stated Tamagawa numbers were correct but the proof used an incorrect general principle. These corrections affect the arithmetic trace profile and the proof of the component-group statement, but not Theorem~4.8 or any of the structural decompositions. We have corrected both points throughout the manuscript and its computational supplements, as detailed below.

Two corollaries were added in Section~6, Corollary~6.2 and Corollary~6.22, so the items after Proposition~6.1 were renumbered. On first mention in each reply below, we give the number in the present manuscript followed by the number in the previous version in parentheses.

\section*{Point-by-point response}

\subsection*{Minor revision 1: rational points in the ramified fibres}

\begin{quote}
Clarify the point count for the ramified fibers in Proposition~6.10. \dots{} The contribution of the unramified fibers is well explained. However, the contribution of the ramified fibers should be justified a little more explicitly. \dots{} For the purpose of counting \(\mathbb F_p\)-rational points, the authors should add a sentence explaining why these ramified fibers contribute exactly \(12\) and \(6\) rational points, respectively, or otherwise state the relevant rationality argument.
\end{quote}

We thank the referee for this request. While writing the requested justification, we found that \(12\) and \(6\) are the numbers of \emph{geometric} points in the relevant ramified fibres, and that those numbers are attained rationally only under conditions that we had not imposed. The point-count formula in Proposition~6.11 (Proposition~6.10 in the previous version) was therefore incorrect.

We corrected Proposition~6.11 in Section~6.4. At a finite rational point \(y_0\), with decomposition and inertia groups \(D\) and \(I\), the fibre contributes \([S_4:I]\) rational points exactly when \(D=I\), equivalently when \(\Pi(\lambda,y_0)\) splits into linear factors over the residue field, and contributes none otherwise. The fibres at \(0\) and \(1\) always contribute \(12\). A rational root of the branch cubic contributes \(12\) only when the residual quadratic splits. At infinity the contribution is \(6\) when \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) and \(0\) when \(p\equiv3\pmod4\). Thus the corrected formula is
\[
\#X(\mathbb F_p)=24N_{\mathrm{split}}(p)+12\bigl(2+n_c(p)\bigr)+6\varepsilon_\infty(p),
\qquad
\varepsilon_\infty(p)=\frac{1+\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)}2.
\]

We also corrected the trace table: \(9\) of the \(15\) tabulated values of \(a_1(Q,p)\) change. The companion generator now checks all good primes below \(120\), asserts the Weil bound and the required divisibility, and provides a second quotient-theoretic check of the trace through
\[
a_1(Q,p)=a_1(Y,p)-a_1(E_{\mathrm{res}},p),\qquad Y=X/C_4.
\]
This second route does not independently test the contribution at infinity, which is verified separately by the exact local expansion and a dedicated symbolic assertion. As a self-match control, the same decomposition-group counter applied to \(E=X/S_3\), \(E_{\mathrm{res}}=X/D_4\), and \(X_A=X/A_4\) reproduces the point counts of their explicit models and tests the finite-fibre criterion. We thank the referee for prompting this correction.

\subsection*{Minor revision 2: the component group in Proposition~6.22}

\begin{quote}
Clarify the component group statement in Proposition~6.22. \dots{} Since the distinction between split and non-split nodes is involved, it would be useful to clarify whether \(\Phi_p\) denotes the geometric component group, the component group scheme, or the group of rational components over the residue field. In the non-split case, the statement that the component group is trivial should be phrased with the precise field of definition in mind.
\end{quote}

We now define \(\Phi_p\) in the statement of Proposition~6.24 (Proposition~6.22 in the previous version) as the finite \(\mathbb F_p\)-group scheme \(\pi_0\) of the special fibre of the N\'eron model of \(\Jac(X_A)\). We distinguish the geometric component group \(\Phi_p(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)\), with its Frobenius action, from the rational components \(\Phi_p(\mathbb F_p)\), and state \(c_p=\#\Phi_p(\mathbb F_p)\).

The conclusions \(c_{31}=2\) and \(c_{37}=1\) stand, but the mechanism in the former proof was wrong. The proof incorrectly treated non-splitness as forcing a trivial component group. We have replaced that argument by the thickness computation. The node has thickness \(2\) at \(31\), so \(\Phi_{31}(\overline{\mathbb F}_{31})\cong\ZZ/2\ZZ\); the node is split, so Frobenius acts trivially. At \(37\) the node has thickness \(1\), hence \(\Phi_{37}=0\) as a group scheme. Its non-splitness affects the toric part, which is the norm-one torus, but is not the reason the component group vanishes. We also record the general non-split formula \(c_p=\gcd(2,n)\) for thickness \(n\).

Finally, we checked the comparison with \(E_{\mathrm{res}}\): its minimal discriminant is \(31^2\cdot37\), giving types \(I_2\) at \(31\) and \(I_1\) at \(37\), with Tamagawa numbers \(2\) and \(1\). Thus the agreement stated in the summary of results remains correct.

\begin{quote}
In the highlights, the sentence `The branch field is a cubic ray class subfield over \(\QQ(\sqrt{-111})\).' is slightly ambiguous. It would be clearer to write: `The splitting field of the branch cubic is cubic over \(\QQ(\sqrt{-111})\) and lies in the corresponding ray class field.'

We replaced the third highlight by:

The referee's proposed sentence exceeds Elsevier's highlight limit, so we condensed it while keeping its three assertions: the splitting field of the branch cubic, that it is cubic over \(\QQ(\sqrt{-111})\), and that it lies in the corresponding ray class field. The other four highlights are unchanged and also remain within the limit.

\end{itemize}
