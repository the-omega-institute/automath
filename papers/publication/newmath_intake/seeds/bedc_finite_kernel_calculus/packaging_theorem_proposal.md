## Problem

The current exact spine is internally coherent, but most selected declarations
are local constructor, equality, totality, determinacy, associativity, or
interface-projection statements.  That is not enough for a strong standalone
logic paper unless the manuscript can cite one compact theorem that packages the
finite kernel as a compositional interface rather than a list of elementary
facts.

## Proposed Upstream Statement

Add or identify a theorem in `D:/omega/newmath`, preferably near the existing
`lean4/BEDC/FKernel` interface files, with a role like:

```text
finite_kernel_interface_soundness
```

The exact Lean name can differ.  The manuscript-level content should be:

1. marks and histories are generated by their declared constructors, with no
   hidden constructor identifications;
2. one-step extension is total, deterministic, injective in the source-mark
   pair, and exactly characterized by the two extension constructors;
3. continuation composition agrees with recursive append and is associative;
4. finite bundle append is associative and membership splits over append;
5. an `AskPolicy` is equivalent to its totality, deterministic-answer, and
   history-respect fields.

The theorem should package these as one finite-kernel interface certificate.
It should not claim that BEDC replaces ordinary foundations, proves semantic
completeness, or supplies an interpretation of all mathematics.

## Acceptable Alternatives

If an upstream theorem is too large for one Lean statement, a small namespace of
three packaged theorems is acceptable:

| Package theorem | Required content |
|---|---|
| `finite_syntax_certificate` | mark/history generation, sameness-as-equality, and no-confusion |
| `extension_continuation_certificate` | extension totality/determinacy/injectivity/characterization plus continuation associativity |
| `bundle_ask_interface_certificate` | bundle append/membership laws plus `AskPolicy` field characterization |

A standalone paper should have at least one introduction-level theorem that
combines or cites these packages and states the finite-kernel interface in prose
without overclaiming.
