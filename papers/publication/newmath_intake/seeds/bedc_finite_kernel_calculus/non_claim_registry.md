# Non-Claim Registry: BEDC Finite Kernel Calculus

This registry constrains the proposed finite-kernel paper before promotion.
It should be carried forward into any promoted manuscript as either an explicit
limitations paragraph or a short methodological note.

## Kernel-Level Claims Allowed

The paper may claim that the pinned BEDC source develops a finite calculus of:

- primitive marks and histories;
- generated sameness and constructor separation;
- relational one-step extension;
- relational continuation and associativity;
- finite probe bundles;
- ask policies and deterministic ask events.

These claims must cite exact Lean declaration names from the selected spine.

## Claims Not Allowed

The paper must not claim:

- that BEDC replaces set theory, type theory, category theory, or ordinary
  mathematical foundations;
- that downstream semantic interpretations are primitive objects of the kernel;
- that Rule 110 artifacts prove the finite-kernel calculus;
- that AI automation contributes proof evidence;
- that GroundCompiler executable encodings are themselves mathematical
  theoremhood;
- that the kernel gives complete coverage of all intended BEDC applications;
- that naming certificates, gap ledgers, packages, or signatures are mature
  main theorems before their exact declarations are separately audited.

## Interface Claims Allowed With Care

The paper may use GroundCompiler and implementation-interface material only as
boundary evidence:

- encodings can be discussed as a controlled channel between finite syntax and
  executable artifacts;
- reject taxonomies can be discussed as engineering obligations;
- implementation soundness/completeness structures can be listed as interface
  obligations, not as already-proved kernel closure.

## Promotion Consequence

If a promoted manuscript needs a stronger global statement, the preferred
route is to add one explicit packaging theorem in `D:/omega/newmath` and then
update the automath seed with a source update note.  The manuscript should not
infer a global theorem by prose from a collection of local declarations.
