\section{Illustrative numerical checks}

This section does not add new estimates; it only illustrates the preceding
theorem chain.  We start with
the canonical slice \(\beta=\alpha\), where the finite-\(m\) statements of
Sections~4 and~5 are sharp, and then consider nearby parameter families and an
independent sampling baseline.  The relevant distinction is between
convergence to \(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\) and comparison with \(\pi_m\).  In
the canonical slice, Theorem~\ref{thm:sequence-level-transparency} rules out
information loss under the fold, while
Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal-injective-relabeling} identifies the best injective
placement of the realized Sturmian law in \(X_m\).

\subsection{Cross-resolution residuals}

\input{generated/tab_rotation_multiscale_residual_summary_en}

Table~\ref{tab:rotation_multiscale_residual_summary} displays the separation
between finite-sample and limiting effects.  At \(N=3\times 10^5\), the mean
star discrepancy in the reported golden slice is of order \(10^{-5}\), whereas
the projective residual remains of order \(10^{-1}\).  For the family
\([0;100,1,1,\dots]\), the residual is larger.  This is the phenomenon
captured by
Theorem~\ref{thm:multiscale-residual-by-boundary}: small orbit discrepancy
does not force small cross-resolution residual, because the residual is driven
by the frequency with which a final \(1\) is appended to prefixes whose
Fibonacci value has already crossed the threshold \(F_{m+1}\).

Corollary~\ref{cor:multiscale-residual-comparison-bound} gives the corresponding
quantitative bound: the empirical residual is controlled by an explicit
discrepancy term and by the deterministic total-variation gaps to the Parry
baselines at levels \(m\) and \(m+1\).  Once the discrepancy term is smaller
than these gaps, the source/reference comparison controls the bound.

Corollary~\ref{cor:multiscale-residual-comparison-bound} identifies the

Corollary~\ref{cor:injective-relabeling-bounded-type} gives the bounded-type

bounded additive distance of that scale.

Across the displayed families, the metallic and resonant examples differ in
constants, but they exhibit the same qualitative obstruction inside the
present model: after folding into \(X_m\), the deterministic gap to the Parry
baseline dominates the sampling term once \(m\) is moderately large.

\subsection{Comparison with independent baselines}

\input{generated/tab_iid_sources_fold_vs_parry_ci_en}

The independent comparison separates two bookkeeping regimes.  When the
data-generating law is matched to the reference baseline, the classical
finite-alphabet sampling envelopes give the right scale but grow with the
alphabet size.  The table reports the observed seed averages only; it is not
used as a concentration certificate for the deterministic rotation examples.
For the rotation data, Theorem~\ref{thm:tv-certificate-hist} controls the
folded histogram relative to \(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\), while
Corollary~\ref{cor:tv-decomposition-parry} is the exact KL identity for the
microstate law relative to the Parry lift \(Q_m\pi_m\).  The remaining
comparison with the Parry block law \(\pi_m\) is therefore deterministic and
comes from the folded-versus-Parry mismatch isolated in Section~5.

For reference, set \(K:=|E|=F_{m+2}\).  Standard high-probability
envelopes on a finite alphabet \(E\) have the form
\[
\TV(\hat p_N,p)
\lesssim
\sqrt{\frac{K\log 2+\log(1/\delta)}{2N}},
\qquad
\KL(\hat p_N\|p)
\lesssim
\frac{K\log(N+1)+\log(1/\delta)}{N}
\]
at confidence level \(1-\delta\), with constants and lower-order terms
depending on the chosen form of the inequality.  In particular, the TV
envelope is of order \(\sqrt{K/N}\), up to constants and logarithmic
confidence terms, rather than \(\sqrt{\log K/N}\); see
\cite{WeissmanEtAl2003L1Empirical,DemboZeitouni2010LargeDeviations}. These
are independent sampling envelopes on the finite alphabet \(E\). The rotation
experiments are controlled instead by the deterministic discrepancy
certificates of Section~3. When
\(p=\pi_m\) and the data are Parry-distributed, the observed errors in the table
are at the expected independent-sampling scale. When \(p=\pi_m\) but the data
come from folded rotation orbits, the deterministic Parry gap dominates.

\subsection{A nonlinear circle-map illustration}

Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-map-conjugacy-transfer} shows that the symbolic
comparison proved earlier transfers unchanged to circle maps that are
topologically conjugate to the irrational rotation.

For example, consider the standard circle map
\[
f_{K,\Omega}(x)=x+\Omega-\frac{K}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi x)\pmod 1
\]
with \(K=0.3\) and \(\Omega\) tuned by bisection on the lift so that the
rotation number equals \(\varphi^{-1}\), giving
\(\Omega\approx 0.6166931270470741\). With anchor \(a=0.2\), coding window
\(I=[a,f(a))\), and initial point \(x_0=a\), the resulting symbolic sequence
is treated here only as an illustration.  The proof input in
Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-map-conjugacy-transfer} would be the exact pulled-back
interval \(h^{-1}([0,\varphi^{-1}))\); the displayed interval and tuned
parameter are not used as theorem evidence.
At \(m=12\) and \(N=10^5\), the empirical folded histogram satisfies
\[
\TV(\hat\pi_{12,N},\pi_{12}^{\mathrm{fold}})
=2.43\times 10^{-5},
\qquad
\KL(\hat\pi_{12,N}\|\pi_{12}^{\mathrm{fold}})
=2.95\times 10^{-9},
\]
while the gaps to the Parry baseline remain
\[
\TV(\hat\pi_{12,N},\pi_{12})=0.9650,
\qquad
\KL(\hat\pi_{12,N}\|\pi_{12})=3.4443.
\]
The distance to the folded limit is already at the \(10^{-5}\) level, while the
Parry discrepancy remains of order \(10^0\).  The same comparison therefore
applies to this nonlinear state-space model.

\section{Concluding remarks}

The construction gives a finite-resolution setting in which sampling error and
Parry comparison can be separated explicitly.  Discrepancy estimates control
the empirical folded histogram, while the support and placement results
describe the limiting folded law.  In the Sturmian slice the fold is injective
on realized blocks and is conjugate to the classical higher-block
presentation, so the Parry gap is not caused by collisions under the fold.
More generally, Theorem~\ref{thm:window-injectivity-classification} shows that
losslessness at every resolution holds exactly in the two extreme window
regimes \(\beta\le\|\alpha\|\) and
\(\beta\ge1-\|\alpha\|\); in the intervening regime the obstruction is
already present at resolution two.

The main obstruction is the low effective complexity of the source inside the
ambient golden-mean language. Even after optimizing over all injective
placements of the realized Sturmian law, one obtains
\[
\inf_{\iota\in\mathcal I_m(\alpha)}D_q(\iota_*\mu_m\|\pi_m)
=
m\log\varphi-\log m+O_\alpha(1)
\]
for bounded-type slopes, uniformly in the R\'enyi order. The canonical fold is
within a bounded additive distance of this optimum.

The exact formulas rely on the endpoint collapse of golden-mean Parry
cylinders.  Thus the paper should be read as an explicit model, rather than as
a classification of arbitrary folds.
