\section{The Fibonacci fold}\label{sec:fold}

Let \(F_0=0\), \(F_1=1\), and
\[
F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n\qquad(n\geq0).
\]
For \(m\geq1\), put
\[
\Omega_m:=\{0,1\}^m
\]
and let
\[
X_m:=\{u=u_1\cdots u_m\in\{0,1\}^m:
u_iu_{i+1}=0\text{ for }1\leq i<m\}
\]
be the length-\(m\) golden-mean language.  Assign to
\(\omega=\omega_1\cdots\omega_m\in\Omega_m\) the Fibonacci value
\[
N_m(\omega):=\sum_{j=1}^m\omega_jF_{j+1}.
\]

By Zeckendorf's theorem, every nonnegative integer \(n\) has a unique
expansion
\[
n=\sum_{j\geq1}z_jF_{j+1},
\qquad z_j\in\{0,1\},\qquad z_jz_{j+1}=0.
\]
We write this digit sequence as \(Z(n)=(z_1,z_2,\ldots)\).

\begin{definition}[Fibonacci fold]\label{def:canonical-fold}
For \(\omega\in\Omega_m\), define
\[
\Fold_m(\omega):=z_1\cdots z_m,
\qquad Z(N_m(\omega))=(z_1,z_2,\ldots).
\]
Thus \(\Fold_m:\Omega_m\to X_m\) is Zeckendorf normalization followed by
truncation to the original length.
\end{definition}

The following proposition is the entire arithmetic input to the
classification.  It combines the basic range property of Fibonacci
representations with the residue characterization that appeared as
Proposition 2.6 in the longer version.

\begin{proposition}[Residue characterization]\label{prop:fold-residue-characterization}
For every \(m\geq1\), the restriction of \(N_m\) is a bijection
\[
N_m:X_m\longrightarrow\{0,1,\ldots,F_{m+2}-1\}.
\]
The fold fixes \(X_m\) pointwise.  Moreover, for every
\(\omega\in\Omega_m\) and \(x\in X_m\),
\[
\Fold_m(\omega)=x
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N_m(\omega)\equiv N_m(x)\pmod{F_{m+2}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The legal strings in \(X_m\) are precisely the Zeckendorf digit strings
supported in positions \(1,\ldots,m\).  Zeckendorf uniqueness therefore
makes \(N_m\) injective on \(X_m\).  The image is the stated interval: this
is immediate for \(m=1\), and the legal strings ending in \(0\) and \(1\)
give, respectively,
\[
\{0,\ldots,F_{m+1}-1\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
F_{m+1}+\{0,\ldots,F_m-1\}.
\]
These adjacent intervals have total length
\(F_{m+1}+F_m=F_{m+2}\).  Since a legal word is already the Zeckendorf
expansion of its own value, \(\Fold_m\) fixes \(X_m\).

For arbitrary \(\omega\in\Omega_m\),
\[
N_m(\omega)\leq\sum_{j=1}^mF_{j+1}
=F_{m+3}-2<F_{m+3}.
\]
Consequently, the Zeckendorf expansion of \(N_m(\omega)\) has no nonzero
digit beyond position \(m+1\).  If its digits are
\((z_1,\ldots,z_m,z_{m+1},0,\ldots)\), then
\[
N_m(\omega)=N_m(\Fold_m(\omega))+z_{m+1}F_{m+2}.
\]
This proves the forward congruence.

Conversely, let \(y=\Fold_m(\omega)\), and suppose that
\(N_m(\omega)\equiv N_m(x)\pmod{F_{m+2}}\).  The forward congruence gives
\[
N_m(y)\equiv N_m(x)\pmod{F_{m+2}}.
\]
Both values lie in \(\{0,\ldots,F_{m+2}-1\}\), so they are equal.
The injectivity of \(N_m|_{X_m}\) gives \(x=y\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The two-letter fold]\label{rem:two-letter-fold}
At length two, \(F_2=1\), \(F_3=2\), and \(F_4=3\).  Hence
\[
\Fold_2(00)=00,\qquad
\Fold_2(10)=10,\qquad
\Fold_2(01)=01,\qquad
\Fold_2(11)=00.
\]
Thus \(00\) and \(11\) are the only possible collision among realized
two-letter words.  The main theorem says that the presence or absence of
this collision already decides injectivity at every length.
\end{remark}
