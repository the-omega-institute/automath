\begin{abstract}
For each positive integer $n$, let $B_n$ denote the set of divisors of the
Fibonacci number $F_n$ whose rank of apparition equals~$n$. We prove that
$B_n$ is an upper set in the divisor lattice of $F_n$ obtained by removing
the covering ideals associated with the maximal proper divisors of~$n$, and
that every minimal generator factors uniquely as a pairwise coprime product
of atomic prime-power birth moduli forming a witness cover whose support spans
the prime coordinates of~$n$. This yields the bound
$\omega(m)\le\omega(n)$ for every minimal generator $m$ and a
primitive-versus-ladder dichotomy for atomic
factors. The arithmetic increment here is a finite rank-checkable
nonemptiness criterion for the three-coordinate witness families; together
with the one- and two-coordinate cases, this gives complete classifications of
\(\mathcal M_n\) for \(\omega(n)\le 3\). We also show that
both $B_n$ and its minimal-generator set admit unique factorizations into
connected coordinate blocks, producing partition-lattice formulas for $A(n) =
\#B_n$ and $\#\mathcal{M}_n$. As consequences, nonexceptional birth layers
contain canonical principal intervals, Boolean subposets of rank $\tau(n) -
O(1)$, and satisfy Bell-type lower bounds on odd layers. Numerical data for
$n\le 30$ illustrate these structures.
\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Fibonacci numbers, rank of apparition, divisor
lattices, upper sets, witness covers, atomic decompositions.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% ====================================================================

For an integer $q\ge 2$, the \emph{rank of apparition} (or \emph{order of
appearance}) $\alpha(q)$ is the least positive index $n$ such that $q\mid
F_n$, where $(F_n)_{n\ge 0}$ denotes the Fibonacci sequence with $F_0=0$,
$F_1=1$. For each $n\ge 2$, write
\[
D_n:=\operatorname{Div}(F_n),
\qquad
B_n:=\{q\in D_n:\ \alpha(q)=n\},
\qquad
A(n):=\#B_n.
\]
Thus $B_n$ consists of those moduli that first divide a Fibonacci number at
index~$n$. Since $F_2=1$, the boundary layer is empty: throughout the paper
we use the convention $B_2=\mathcal M_2=\varnothing$ and $A(2)=0$.
The counting function $A(n)$ is subsumed by the
$\alpha$-contraction formalism of Stroi\'nski \cite{Stroinski2016AlphaContraction};
see also the classical M\"obius inversions discussed in
\cite{Halton1966DivisibilityFibonacci,FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions}.
In this paper we go beyond enumeration and study $B_n$ as an
ordered subset of the divisor lattice $(D_n,\mid)$, asking how it sits inside
$D_n$, how it is generated, and what combinatorial structure it carries.

Three classical facts underlie the analysis. First, $q\mid F_m$ if and only if
$\alpha(q)\mid m$
\cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm,Vinson1963RankApparition}. Second, the Fibonacci
sequence satisfies strong divisibility: $\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}$.
Third, ranks of apparition respect least common multiples:
$\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))=\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))$. These properties, combined
with the primitive divisor theorem for Fibonacci numbers
\cite{Carmichael1913PrimitiveDivisors,BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas},
are the sole inputs for the main results.

This point of view complements several nearby strands in the literature.
Luca and Pomerance \cite{LucaPomerance2014LocalBehavior} study the local
behavior of $\alpha$ under additive shifts; Trojovsk\'a
\cite{Trojovska2020PeriodicPoints} and FitzGibbons--Javaheri--Miller--Verga
\cite{FitzGibbonsJavaheriMillerVerga2025Dynamics} study periodic points and
iteration dynamics of $\alpha$; Cameron--Counts--Lund--Miller--Piechnik--Wong
\cite{CameronCountsLundMillerPiechnikWong2022Fibotomic} study fibotomic
polynomials and related algebraic factorizations; and Sanna
\cite{Sanna2016LucasSequences} and Marques \cite{Marques2012OrderAppearance}
study the growth and fixed-point behavior of the order-of-appearance map. The
present paper instead fixes a single fiber $B_n$ and studies its divisibility
geometry inside $D_n$. We are not aware of earlier work that formulates these
fibers as upper sets in divisor lattices or that gives the two-support and
three-support classifications proved here.

Our first main result identifies $B_n$ as an upper fiber obtained by sieve.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-upper-fiber}
For every \(n\ge2\),
\[
B_n=D_n\setminus\bigcup_{p\mid n}D_{n/p}.
\]
Consequently \(B_n\) is an upper set in the divisor lattice \(D_n\), and
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal M_n}\{q\in D_n:\ m\mid q\},
\]
where \(\mathcal M_n\) is the set of minimal elements of \(B_n\) under
divisibility.
\end{theorem}

Call a prime power $\theta=p^e$ \emph{atomic for the index $d$} if
$\alpha(\theta)=d$ and $\alpha(p^{e-1})<d$. An \emph{$n$-witness cover} is a
pairwise coprime set of such atomic prime powers whose apparition indices
collectively span the prime factorization of $n$ in a minimal, irredundant
fashion (see Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover} for the precise formulation).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-witness}
For every \(n\ge2\), multiplication gives a bijection from \(n\)-witness
covers to minimal generators:
\[
\mathcal M_n=
\bigl\{\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta:\Theta\text{ is an \(n\)-witness cover}\bigr\}.
\]
In particular every \(m\in\mathcal M_n\) satisfies
\(\omega(m)\le\omega(n)\).
\end{theorem}

Making this classification effective requires the prime-power apparition
formulas of Fulton--Morris \cite{FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions} and
Lengyel \cite{Lengyel1995OrderFibonacciLucas}. These show that every atomic
factor is either \emph{primitive} (arising from a new prime) or lies on a
\emph{prime-power ladder} (changing exactly one prime coordinate of the
apparition index). This dichotomy
(Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}) enables complete
classifications of $\mathcal M_n$ when $\omega(n)\le 3$.  The one-coordinate
case is exactly the atomic family \(\Atom(n)\), explaining prime-power-index
exceptions such as \(n=19,25,27\). In the three-coordinate case the criterion
is not merely formal: the primitive and ladder families are computed by
finite rank tests over the divisors of \(n\) and the prime-power factors of
\(F_n\)
(Corollaries~\ref{cor:one-coordinate-classification} and
\ref{cor:two-support-complete-classification}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}, with the finite recipe in
Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-finite-recipe}).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-connected}
Let $q\in B_n$. Form the support hypergraph on $\mathcal P(n)$ whose
hyperedges are the prime-coordinate supports of the apparition indices of
the prime-power factors of $q$. If its connected components are
$C_1,\ldots,C_s$, then
\[
q=q_{C_1}\cdots q_{C_s},\qquad \alpha(q_{C_j})=n_{C_j}
\quad(1\le j\le s),
\]
where $q_{C_j}\in B_{n_{C_j}}$ is connected. This factorization is unique.
The same statement holds for minimal generators, with
$q_{C_j}\in\mathcal M_{n_{C_j}}$. Consequently
\[
A(n)=\sum_{\pi\in\Part(\mathcal P(n))}
       \prod_{C\in\pi} A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n_C),
\]
and the analogous formula holds for $\#\mathcal M_n$. The full statement,
including the partition-lattice inversion, is proved in
Theorems~\ref{thm:birth-connected-factorization} and
\ref{thm:mingenerator-connected-factorization}.
\end{theorem}

As secondary consequences, nonexceptional birth layers contain canonical
principal intervals
(Corollary~\ref{thm:canonical-principal-interval}), Boolean subposets of rank
$\tau(n)-O(1)$ (Corollary~\ref{thm:boolean-skeleton}), and---for odd
$n$---Bell-type lower bounds on $\#\mathcal M_n$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:odd-primitive-cover-realization}).

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:prelim} collects the
background on Fibonacci divisibility and the prime-power apparition formulas.
Section~\ref{sec:upper-fibers} proves the upper-fiber and witness-cover
theory. Section~\ref{sec:low-support} treats the
primitive--ladder dichotomy and the low-support classifications.
Section~\ref{sec:connected} records the connected-block factorization
(Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-connected}) and the partition-lattice formulas.
Section~\ref{sec:numerical} presents numerical data, and
Section~\ref{sec:questions} records further questions.

\subsection{Rank of apparition and Fibonacci divisibility}\label{subsec:fib-div}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rank-basics}
Let \(q,u,v\ge2\) and \(a,b,m\ge1\). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \(q\mid F_m\) if and only if \(\alpha(q)\mid m\).
\item \(\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}\).
\item \(\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))=\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the standard rank-divisibility property of the
Fibonacci rank of apparition, as proved for example by Wall
\cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm} and Vinson \cite{Vinson1963RankApparition}: once
a modulus first appears at index \(\alpha(q)\), it appears exactly at the
multiples of that index. The second assertion is the strong-divisibility
identity for the Fibonacci sequence.

For (iii), put \(L=\lcmop(u,v)\). For every \(m\ge1\),
\[
L\mid F_m
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u\mid F_m\ \text{and}\ v\mid F_m
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\alpha(u)\mid m\ \text{and}\ \alpha(v)\mid m
\]
by (i). This is equivalent to
\(\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))\mid m\). Therefore the least positive \(m\)
for which \(L\mid F_m\) is \(\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v))\), which is (iii).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Primitive divisors}\label{subsec:primitive}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:primitive-divisors}
For every $n\ge 3$ with $n\notin\{6,12\}$, there exists a prime $p_n$ with
$\alpha(p_n)=n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $n=3,4,5$ one takes $p_3=2$, $p_4=3$, $p_5=5$. For $n\ge 7$, $n\neq 12$,
the primitive divisor theorem
\cite{Carmichael1913PrimitiveDivisors,Yabuta2001PrimitiveDivisorFibonacci,%
BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas}
gives a prime dividing $F_n$ but no earlier Fibonacci number. Since
$\alpha(p_n)\mid n$ and $p_n$ divides no $F_d$ with $d<n$, necessarily
$\alpha(p_n)=n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Counting corollaries}\label{subsec:counting}

The standard M\"obius-inversion formulas for first-appearance counts follow
from Stroi\'nski's $\alpha$-contraction identity
\cite{Stroinski2016AlphaContraction}: for any $f:\NN\to\mathbb{C}$ with
$f(1)=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stroinski}
\sum_{d\mid F_n}f(d)=\sum_{d\mid n}f_\alpha(d),
\qquad
f_\alpha(d):=\sum_{\alpha(m)=d}f(m).
\end{equation}
Taking $f$ to be the indicator of integers at least $2$, of primes, or of
prime powers, and applying M\"obius inversion, one obtains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mobius-birth}
A(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\,\tau(F_d),
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{pr}}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\,\omega(F_d).
\end{equation}
We regard these as known corollaries; the results of
Sections~\ref{sec:upper-fibers}--\ref{sec:connected} are independent of them.

\subsection{Prime-power apparition formulas}\label{subsec:prime-power}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:prime-power-ladders}
See \cite[Proposition~2.3]{MaZhang2026UpperFibersWitnessCoversFQ}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:atomic-families}
For a prime power \(\theta=p^e\) with \(e\ge1\), set
\[
d(\theta):=\alpha(\theta),\qquad
d^-(\theta):=\alpha(p^{e-1}),
\]
with the convention \(\alpha(1)=1\). For \(d\ge1\) define the atomic
birth family
\[
\Atom(d):=\{p^e:\ \alpha(p^e)=d,\ \alpha(p^{e-1})<d\}.
\]
Thus \(\Atom(d)\) consists of the prime-power factors born exactly at rank
\(d\).

Let \(d(\theta)\mid n\). The full-coordinate support of \(\theta\) relative
to \(n\) is
\[
T_n(\theta):=
\{\ell\in\mathcal P(n):\nu_\ell(d(\theta))=\nu_\ell(n)\},
\]
and its essential-coordinate support is
\[
E_n(\theta):=
\{\ell\in T_n(\theta):\nu_\ell(d^-(\theta))<\nu_\ell(n)\}.
\]
Equivalently, \(T_n(\theta)\) records the prime coordinates of \(n\) supplied
at full exponent by \(d(\theta)\), while \(E_n(\theta)\) records the full
coordinates lost when \(\theta=p^e\) is lowered to \(p^{e-1}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:atomic-classification}
The atomic prime powers are exactly the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \(2\in\Atom(3)\), \(4\in\Atom(6)\), and
\(2^e\in\Atom(3\cdot2^{e-2})\) for every \(e\ge4\). The power \(8\) is not
atomic.
\item \(5^e\in\Atom(5^e)\) for every \(e\ge1\).
\item If \(p\ne2,5\), \(z_p=\alpha(p)\), and
\(h_p=\nu_p(F_{z_p})\), then \(p\in\Atom(z_p)\) and
\[
p^e\in\Atom(p^{e-h_p}z_p)\qquad(e>h_p).
\]
No power \(p^e\) with \(1<e\le h_p\) is atomic.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently every atom is either a primitive atom \(p\in\Atom(\alpha(p))\)
or a ladder atom \(p^e\) with \(e>1\), and every ladder atom changes only one
prime coordinate of its apparition index when it is lowered by one power of
its underlying prime.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For \(p=2\), Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders} gives
\[
\alpha(2)=3,\quad \alpha(4)=6,\quad \alpha(8)=6,\quad
\alpha(2^e)=3\cdot2^{e-2}\ (e\ge3).
\]
Thus \(2\) and \(4\) are atomic, \(8\) is not atomic because
\(\alpha(8)=\alpha(4)\), and every \(2^e\) with \(e\ge4\) is atomic because
\(3\cdot2^{e-2}>3\cdot2^{e-3}\).

For \(p=5\), the formula \(\alpha(5^e)=5^e\) gives a strict increase at every
step, so every \(5^e\) is atomic. For \(p\ne2,5\), the formula
\(\alpha(p^e)=p^{\max\{e-h_p,0\}}z_p\) is constant for
\(1\le e\le h_p\) and is then multiplied by \(p\) at each further step.
Hence \(p\) is atomic, no power \(p^e\) with \(1<e\le h_p\) is atomic, and
every \(p^e\) with \(e>h_p\) is atomic with apparition index
\(p^{e-h_p}z_p\).

The final dichotomy follows from the same list. An atom of exponent \(1\) is
primitive. An atom of exponent \(e>1\) is a ladder step; comparing
\(d(p^e)\) with \(d^-(p^e)\) in the displayed formulas shows that the lowered
rank differs by a single factor \(2\), \(5\), or \(p\), respectively, with the
exceptional \(4\)-step changing \(3\) to \(6\), again only in the \(2\)-adic
coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:birth-layer}
See \cite[Definition~3.1]{MaZhang2026UpperFibersWitnessCoversFQ}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:upper-fiber}
For every $n\ge 2$,
\[
B_n=D_n\setminus\bigcup_{p\mid n}D_{n/p}.
\]
Moreover $B_n$ is an upper set in $D_n$, and
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal M_n}\{q\in D_n:\ m\mid q\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $q\in B_n$, then $\alpha(q)=n$, so $q\nmid F_{n/p}$ for every prime
$p\mid n$; otherwise $n=\alpha(q)\mid n/p<n$, a contradiction. Conversely, if
$q\in D_n\setminus\bigcup D_{n/p}$, then $d:=\alpha(q)\mid n$. Were $d<n$,
some prime $p$ would divide $n/d$, giving $d\mid n/p$ and $q\mid F_{n/p}$, a
contradiction. Hence $\alpha(q)=n$.

If $q\in B_n$, $q\mid r$, and $r\in D_n$, then $n=\alpha(q)\mid\alpha(r)$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}, while $\alpha(r)\mid n$ because
$r\mid F_n$. Thus $\alpha(r)=n$, proving that $B_n$ is an upper set. Since
$D_n$ is finite, every element of $B_n$ lies above a minimal element of
$B_n$, which gives the displayed decomposition.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Witness covers}

\begin{definition}\label{def:witness-cover}
See \cite[Definition~3.4]{MaZhang2026UpperFibersWitnessCoversFQ}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:witness-classification}
For every $n\ge 2$,
$\mathcal M_n=\bigl\{\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta:\
\Theta\text{ is an $n$-witness cover}\bigr\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$, $m=\prod_{i=1}^r\theta_i$ ($\theta_i=p_i^{e_i}$).
Each $\theta_i$ is atomic: if $\alpha(p_i^{e_i-1})=\alpha(p_i^{e_i})$, then
replacing $\theta_i$ by $p_i^{e_i-1}$ preserves the lcm, contradicting
the minimal-generator criterion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators}. Since
$n=\lcmop(d(\theta_i))$, the sets $T_n(\theta_i)$ cover $\mathcal P(n)$. The
condition $\alpha(m/p_i)<n$ forces
$E_n(\theta_i)\nsubseteq\bigcup_{j\neq i}T_n(\theta_j)$; otherwise the lost
coordinates would be supplied by other factors, keeping the lcm equal to $n$.
Thus $\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\}$ is an $n$-witness cover.

Conversely, let $\Theta$ be an $n$-witness cover and
$m=\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta$. By
the lcm property recorded in Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics},
$\alpha(m)=\lcmop(d(\theta):\theta\in\Theta)=n$. For each
$\theta=p^e\in\Theta$, the witness condition
provides $\ell\in E_n(\theta)\setminus\bigcup_{\eta\neq\theta}T_n(\eta)$; at
this coordinate, $\alpha(m/p)<n$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators} then gives $m\in\mathcal M_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:support-bound}
Every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ satisfies $\omega(m)\le\omega(n)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The witness condition provides an injection
$\theta\mapsto\ell(\theta)$ from $\Theta$ into $\mathcal P(n)$: choose
$\ell(\theta)\in E_n(\theta)\setminus\bigcup_{\eta\neq\theta}T_n(\eta)$.
Hence $\omega(m)=\#\Theta\le\omega(n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:n20}
For $n=20$, $F_{20}=6765=3\cdot 5\cdot 11\cdot 41$. The relevant atomic
factors are $3\in\Atom(4)$, $5\in\Atom(5)$, $11\in\Atom(10)$,
$41\in\Atom(20)$. The witness covers are $\{41\}$, $\{3,5\}$, $\{3,11\}$,
yielding $\mathcal M_{20}=\{15,33,41\}$ and $A(20)=11$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}\label{ex:n30}
For $n=30$, $F_{30}=832040=2^3\cdot 5\cdot 11\cdot 31\cdot 61$. The witness
covers are $\{31\}$, $\{4,5\}$, $\{2,11\}$, $\{4,61\}$, $\{11,61\}$, giving
$\mathcal M_{30}=\{20,22,31,244,671\}$ and $A(30)=52$.
With coordinates $(2,3,5)$ for $30=2\cdot3\cdot5$, these covers realize
respectively the types $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_8$, $\Gamma_7$, $\Gamma_5$, and
$\Gamma_4$.
\end{example}

\subsection*{Substructures of nonexceptional birth layers}

\begin{corollary}\label{thm:canonical-principal-interval}
Let $n\ge3$ and $n\notin\{6,12\}$. Choose a primitive prime
$\pi_n$ with $\alpha(\pi_n)=n$. Then the principal interval
\[
[\pi_n,F_n]:=\{q\in D_n:\ \pi_n\mid q\}
\]
is contained in $B_n$. Consequently $B_n$ contains a canonical copy of the
divisor lattice of $F_n/\pi_n$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The primitive prime $\pi_n$ exists by Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-divisors}.
If $q\in D_n$ and $\pi_n\mid q$, then
$n=\alpha(\pi_n)\mid\alpha(q)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics}. Since
$q\mid F_n$, also $\alpha(q)\mid n$. Hence $\alpha(q)=n$ and $q\in B_n$.
Dividing by $\pi_n$ identifies the interval $[\pi_n,F_n]$ with
$\operatorname{Div}(F_n/\pi_n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{thm:boolean-skeleton}
Let $n\ge3$ and $n\notin\{6,12\}$. Put
\[
R_n:=\{d:\ d\mid n,\ 3\le d<n,\ d\notin\{6,12\}\}.
\]
Then $B_n$ contains a Boolean subposet of rank $\#R_n=\tau(n)-O(1)$. In
particular,
\[
\widthop(B_n)\ge \binom{\#R_n}{\lfloor \#R_n/2\rfloor}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose a primitive prime $\pi_n$ with $\alpha(\pi_n)=n$, and for each
$d\in R_n$ choose a primitive prime $\pi_d$ with $\alpha(\pi_d)=d$. These
primes are distinct because their ranks of apparition are distinct, and
$\pi_d\mid F_d\mid F_n$. The map
\[
S\subseteq R_n\longmapsto \pi_n\prod_{d\in S}\pi_d
\]
embeds the Boolean lattice $2^{R_n}$ into the principal interval
$[\pi_n,F_n]\subseteq B_n$ from
Corollary~\ref{thm:canonical-principal-interval}. Sperner's theorem
\cite{Sperner1928Subsets} gives an antichain of size
$\binom{\#R_n}{\lfloor \#R_n/2\rfloor}$ in this Boolean subposet, hence the
same lower bound for $\widthop(B_n)$. Since $R_n$ omits from the divisor set
of $n$ only $1$, possibly $2$, $n$, and possibly $6$ and $12$, its size is
$\tau(n)-O(1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:maximal-order}
For infinitely many nonexceptional $n$,
\[
\widthop(B_n)\ge
\exp\!\left((\log 2+o(1))\,\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $n$ through any sequence for which the divisor function has maximal order
in Wigert's theorem \cite{Wigert1907Divisors}, omitting the finitely many
exceptional values $6$ and $12$. Then
$\tau(n)=\exp((\log2+o(1))\log n/\log\log n)$. By
Corollary~\ref{thm:boolean-skeleton}, $B_n$ contains a Boolean subposet of
rank $\tau(n)-O(1)$, and hence contains an antichain of size at least
\[
\binom{\tau(n)-O(1)}{\lfloor(\tau(n)-O(1))/2\rfloor}.
\]
This is stronger than the stated lower bound along the chosen sequence.
\end{proof}

\section{Low-support classifications}\label{sec:low-support}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}
Let $\theta\in\Atom(d)$ and assume $d\mid n$. Then exactly one of the
following holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\theta$ is primitive, and $E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$.
\item $\theta$ is a ladder atom. In this case $E_n(\theta)$ is either empty
or a singleton. When it is nonempty, its unique element is the single
coordinate whose exponent is changed by lowering $\theta$ to $\theta/p$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular no Fibonacci atom has $1<\#E_n(\theta)<\#T_n(\theta)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $\theta=p$ is primitive, then $d^-(\theta)=\alpha(1)=1$, so every full
coordinate in $T_n(\theta)$ is lost on lowering and
$E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$.

Now suppose $e>1$. The classification in
Corollary~\ref{cor:atomic-classification} shows that the only strict change
from $d^-(\theta)$ to $d(\theta)$ is multiplication by one prime coordinate:
by $2$ for the atomic powers of $2$ beyond the first, by $5$ for powers of
$5$, and by $p$ for the post-$h_p$ ladder powers of an odd prime
$p\ne5$. Thus lowering a ladder atom can remove full support at only one
coordinate. In the ordinary ladder cases that coordinate is the base prime
$p$. The exceptional atom $4$ has $d(4)=6$ and $d^-(4)=3$, so relative to any
multiple $n$ of $6$ it removes only the $2$-coordinate. Hence
$E_n(\theta)$ is empty if that changed coordinate is not full in $n$, and is
that singleton otherwise.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One-coordinate classification]\label{cor:one-coordinate-classification}
Let \(n\ge3\) with \(\omega(n)=1\). Then
\[
\mathcal M_n=\Atom(n).
\]
Equivalently, the minimal generators in the \(n\)-th birth layer are exactly
the prime powers \(p^e\) with \(\alpha(p^e)=n\) and
\(\alpha(p^{e-1})<n\). In particular a prime-power index may have more than
one minimal generator: the table entries
\[
\mathcal M_{19}=\{37,113\},\qquad
\mathcal M_{25}=\{25,3001\},\qquad
\mathcal M_{27}=\{53,109\}
\]
come from distinct atomic families at the same one-coordinate index.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell\}\). If \(m\in\mathcal M_n\), then
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} writes \(m\) as the product of an
\(n\)-witness cover \(\Theta\). The support bound gives \(\#\Theta\le1\), and
\(\Theta\) cannot be empty because its \(T_n\)-supports must cover
\(\{\ell\}\). Thus \(\Theta=\{\theta\}\). Since \(d(\theta)\mid n\), the
condition \(T_n(\theta)=\{\ell\}\) is equivalent to
\(\nu_\ell(d(\theta))=\nu_\ell(n)\), hence \(d(\theta)=n\). Therefore
\(\theta\in\Atom(n)\) and \(m=\theta\).

Conversely, let \(\theta=p^e\in\Atom(n)\). Then \(d(\theta)=n\), so
\(T_n(\theta)=\{\ell\}\). Also \(d^-(\theta)\mid n\), because
\(p^{e-1}\mid p^e\mid F_n\), and \(d^-(\theta)<n\) by atomicity. Since \(n\)
has only the prime coordinate \(\ell\), this strict divisibility gives
\(\nu_\ell(d^-(\theta))<\nu_\ell(n)\), so
\(E_n(\theta)=\{\ell\}\). Hence \(\{\theta\}\) is an \(n\)-witness cover, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} gives
\(\theta\in\mathcal M_n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:extremal-coordinate-separation}
Let $m=\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta\in\mathcal M_n$ be represented by its
witness cover. If $\#\Theta=\omega(n)$, then the witness coordinates are
forced to be disjoint: each atom $\theta\in\Theta$ has a unique coordinate
$\ell(\theta)\in E_n(\theta)$ not lying in any other $T_n(\eta)$, and
$\theta\mapsto\ell(\theta)$ is a bijection from $\Theta$ to $\mathcal P(n)$.
Consequently, when $\omega(n)=2$, every non-atomic minimal generator is a
product of one atom whose full support is the first coordinate and one atom
whose full support is the second coordinate, after relabeling.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $\theta\in\Theta$, the witness-cover condition gives at least one
coordinate
\[
\ell(\theta)\in
E_n(\theta)\setminus\bigcup_{\eta\ne\theta}T_n(\eta).
\]
No two atoms can choose the same coordinate, because
$\ell(\theta)\in T_n(\theta)$ while the displayed condition says that this
coordinate lies in no other $T_n(\eta)$. Hence
$\theta\mapsto\ell(\theta)$ is an injection into $\mathcal P(n)$. If
$\#\Theta=\omega(n)$, it is a bijection.

For $\omega(n)=2$, a non-atomic minimal generator has two factors by the
support bound and the assumption that it is not a single atom. The preceding
paragraph gives one private coordinate for each factor. If one factor had both
coordinates in its full support, then the other factor's private coordinate
would lie in the first factor's $T$-set, impossible. Thus each factor has
exactly one full-support coordinate, as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:two-support-complete-classification}
Assume $\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}$ and put
\[
\Atom_i(n):=\{\theta:\theta\in\Atom(d),\ d\mid n,\
E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)=\{\ell_i\}\}
\quad(i=1,2),
\]
and
\[
\Atom_{12}(n):=\{\theta:\theta\in\Atom(d),\ d\mid n,\ 
T_n(\theta)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}\}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal M_n=
\Atom_{12}(n)\ \cup\
\{\theta_1\theta_2:\theta_i\in\Atom_i(n)\ (i=1,2),\
\gcd(\theta_1,\theta_2)=1\}.
\]
The first union is interpreted as the set of one-factor minimal generators,
and the second as the set of two-factor minimal generators.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification}, every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ is the
product of an $n$-witness cover. The support bound gives either one or two
factors. If there is one factor $\theta$, its full-coordinate support must
cover both coordinates, so $\theta\in\Atom_{12}(n)$. Conversely any
$\theta\in\Atom_{12}(n)$ is an $n$-witness cover by itself: its $T_n$-support
covers both coordinates and its irredundance condition is automatic because
$E_n(\theta)$ is nonempty for an atom appearing in a minimal generator; more
directly, $\alpha(\theta)=n$ and atomicity gives
$\alpha(\theta/p)<n$ for its underlying prime $p$.

If the cover has two factors, Theorem~\ref{thm:extremal-coordinate-separation}
shows after relabeling that one factor has private coordinate $\ell_1$ and the
other has private coordinate $\ell_2$, and neither factor can have both
coordinates in its $T$-support. Thus
\(T_n(\theta_i)=\{\ell_i\}\). Since the private coordinate belongs to
\(E_n(\theta_i)\subseteq T_n(\theta_i)\), also
\(E_n(\theta_i)=\{\ell_i\}\). Hence the factors lie in
\(\Atom_1(n)\) and \(\Atom_2(n)\). Pairwise coprimeness is part of the
witness-cover definition.
Conversely, if $\theta_i\in\Atom_i(n)$ are coprime, then their
$T_n$-supports cover $\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}$, and each factor has its unique
coordinate as an essential private coordinate because
$E_n(\theta_i)=T_n(\theta_i)=\{\ell_i\}$. Thus the two factors form an
$n$-witness cover, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} gives
\(\theta_1\theta_2\in\mathcal M_n\).
\end{proof}

We now classify the three-support case. The abstract enumeration is a finite
exercise; the arithmetic content lies in identifying which abstract types
survive the primitive--ladder constraint.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:abstract-three-types}
Let $U=\{1,2,3\}$. Consider finite collections
\[
\Delta=\{(I_j,J_j):1\le j\le r\}
\]
with $\varnothing\ne I_j\subseteq J_j\subseteq U$, with
\(\bigcup_jJ_j=U\), and with
\[
I_j\nsubseteq\bigcup_{h\ne j}J_h\qquad(1\le j\le r).
\]
Up to a permutation of $U$, exactly the following nine collections occur:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\Gamma_1:\ \{(\{1,2,3\},\{1,2,3\})\},&
\Gamma_2:\ \{(\{1,2\},\{1,2,3\})\},\\[2mm]
\Gamma_3:\ \{(\{1\},\{1,2,3\})\},&
\Gamma_4:\ \{(\{1,2\},\{1,2\}),(\{1,3\},\{1,3\})\},\\[2mm]
\Gamma_5:\ \{(\{1\},\{1,2\}),(\{2,3\},\{2,3\})\},&
\Gamma_6:\ \{(\{1\},\{1,2\}),(\{3\},\{2,3\})\},\\[2mm]
\Gamma_7:\ \{(\{1\},\{1\}),(\{2,3\},\{2,3\})\},&
\Gamma_8:\ \{(\{1\},\{1\}),(\{2\},\{2,3\})\},\\[2mm]
\Gamma_9:\ \{(\{1\},\{1\}),(\{2\},\{2\}),(\{3\},\{3\})\}.&
\end{array}
\]
For each listed collection the sets $J_j$ cover $U$ and every pair has an
irredundant witness coordinate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The listed collections are checked directly. In each row the union of the
second components is $U$, and for every $(I_j,J_j)$ one of the elements of
$I_j$ is absent from all other $J_h$.

It remains to prove that there are no others. The irredundance condition
assigns to each pair a private coordinate
\[
x_j\in I_j\setminus\bigcup_{h\ne j}J_h .
\]
The coordinates $x_j$ are distinct, hence $r\le3$. If $r=1$, then
$J_1=U$ and the three possibilities for $I_1$, up to relabeling, are
\(\{1,2,3\}\), \(\{1,2\}\), and \(\{1\}\), giving
\(\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2,\Gamma_3\).

Let $r=2$. The two private coordinates may be labeled $1$ and $3$. Thus,
after relabeling the two factors if necessary,
\[
1\in I_1\subseteq J_1,\quad 1\notin J_2,\qquad
3\in I_2\subseteq J_2,\quad 3\notin J_1.
\]
The coordinate $2$ must lie in at least one of $J_1,J_2$. If it lies in both,
then \(J_1=\{1,2\}\) and \(J_2=\{2,3\}\), and the four possibilities for
\((I_1,I_2)\), modulo reversing the line, are
\[
(\{1\},\{3\}),\quad
(\{1\},\{2,3\}),\quad
(\{1,2\},\{3\}),\quad
(\{1,2\},\{2,3\}),
\]
which are represented by \(\Gamma_6,\Gamma_5,\Gamma_5,\Gamma_4\).
If $2$ lies in exactly one of $J_1,J_2$, then the supports have the form
\(\{1\}\) and \(\{2,3\}\), and the two possibilities for the second
essential set are \(\{3\}\) and \(\{2,3\}\), giving
\(\Gamma_8\) and \(\Gamma_7\).

Finally let $r=3$. The three private coordinates exhaust $U$. Since a
private coordinate of one factor lies in no other support, each support is
the singleton containing its private coordinate. This is \(\Gamma_9\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types}
Let \(\omega(n)=3\), and label \(\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}\).
For \(m\in\mathcal M_n\), form the collection of indexed pairs
\[
\Delta_n(m)=\{(E_n^\#(\theta),T_n^\#(\theta)):\theta\in\Theta\},
\]
where \(\Theta\) is the witness cover of \(m\),
\[
T_n^\#(\theta):=\{i:\ell_i\in T_n(\theta)\},\qquad
E_n^\#(\theta):=\{i:\ell_i\in E_n(\theta)\}.
\]
Then, up to a permutation of the three coordinates, \(\Delta_n(m)\) is one of
\(\Gamma_1,\Gamma_3,\Gamma_4,\Gamma_5,\Gamma_6,\Gamma_7,\Gamma_8,\Gamma_9\).
The abstract type \(\Gamma_2\) is not a Fibonacci witness type.

Conversely, this exclusion is the only formal obstruction coming from the
primitive--ladder dichotomy: any realized three-coordinate witness type must be
one of the eight displayed types, but the arithmetic nonemptiness of a displayed
type is a separate selection problem in the atom families of
Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-atom-families}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(m\in\mathcal M_n\), and let \(\Theta\) be its witness cover, which is
unique because it is the set of prime-power factors of \(m\). The witness-cover
conditions give
\[
\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta}T_n^\#(\theta)=\{1,2,3\},\qquad
E_n^\#(\theta)\nsubseteq
 \bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta,\eta\ne\theta}T_n^\#(\eta)
 \quad(\theta\in\Theta).
\]
Moreover \(E_n^\#(\theta)\) is nonempty for every \(\theta\in\Theta\), since
the displayed irredundance condition could not hold for the empty set. Thus
\(\Delta_n(m)\) satisfies the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-three-types}. The abstract enumeration gives one of
\(\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_9\), up to relabeling.

It remains to remove \(\Gamma_2\). Its unique pair is
\((\{1,2\},\{1,2,3\})\), after relabeling. This has
\[
1<\#E_n^\#(\theta)<\#T_n^\#(\theta),
\]
which is impossible by Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}: primitive
atoms have \(E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)\), while ladder atoms have essential
support empty or a singleton. The other eight abstract types contain only
diagonal pairs \(I=J\) or singleton proper-essential pairs
\(\{i\}\subsetneq J\), so they are not excluded by this formal dichotomy.
Their actual occurrence depends on the arithmetic availability and coprimeness
of atoms in the relevant families, which is exactly the content of
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} below.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Arithmetic atom families in three coordinates]
\label{prop:three-coordinate-atom-families}
Assume \(\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}\). For
\(\varnothing\ne I\subseteq J\subseteq[3]\), define
\[
\mathcal A_{I,J}(n):=
\{\theta:\theta\in\Atom(d),\ d\mid n,\ E_n^\#(\theta)=I,\
T_n^\#(\theta)=J\}.
\]
For nonempty \(J\subseteq[3]\), define the primitive family
\[
P_J(n):=\{p\in\mathcal A_{J,J}(n):p\text{ is prime}\},
\]
and, for \(i\in J\), define the ladder-step family
\[
L_{i,J}(n):=
\{p^e\in\mathcal A_{\{i\},J}(n):e>1\}.
\]
Then the three-coordinate atom families decompose as follows:
\[
\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)=
\begin{cases}
P_J(n),& I=J\text{ and }\#J\ge2,\\
P_{\{i\}}(n)\cup L_{i,\{i\}}(n),& I=J=\{i\},\\
L_{i,J}(n),& I=\{i\}\subsetneq J,\\
\varnothing,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\theta\in\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)\). If \(\theta\) is primitive, then
Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy} gives
\(E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)\), hence \(I=J\), and \(\theta\) is a prime in
\(P_J(n)\). Conversely every prime in \(P_J(n)\) belongs to
\(\mathcal A_{J,J}(n)\) by definition.

If \(\theta\) is not primitive, then it is a ladder atom. Again by
Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}, \(E_n(\theta)\) is either empty
or a singleton. The family \(\mathcal A_{I,J}(n)\) only allows nonempty
\(I\), so \(I=\{i\}\) for some \(i\in J\). If \(I=J\), then \(J\) is also the
singleton \(\{i\}\), and the atom lies in \(L_{i,\{i\}}(n)\). If
\(I\subsetneq J\), it is exactly an element of
\(L_{i,J}(n)\) by definition. No ladder atom can contribute a family with
\(\#I\ge2\), and no primitive atom can contribute a family with \(I\ne J\).
The displayed alternatives follow.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The finite-rank tests in Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-finite-recipe}
make this decomposition effective using only the prime-power ranks of the
finitely many prime-power divisors of \(F_n\).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Finite rank tests for three-coordinate atom families]
\label{prop:three-coordinate-finite-recipe}
Assume \(\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}\), and put
\[
a_i:=\nu_{\ell_i}(n)\qquad(1\le i\le3).
\]
For a nonempty set \(J\subseteq[3]\), the primitive family \(P_J(n)\) is
exactly the set of primes \(p\mid F_n\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primitive-rank-test}
\alpha(p)\mid n,\qquad
\nu_{\ell_i}(\alpha(p))=a_i\ (i\in J),\qquad
\nu_{\ell_i}(\alpha(p))<a_i\ (i\notin J).
\end{equation}
For \(i\in J\), the ladder family \(L_{i,J}(n)\) is exactly the set of prime
powers \(p^e\mid F_n\), \(e>1\), satisfying the following finite conditions:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ladder-atomic-rank-test}
\alpha(p^e)\mid n,\qquad \alpha(p^{e-1})<\alpha(p^e),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ladder-full-rank-test}
\nu_{\ell_k}(\alpha(p^e))=a_k\ (k\in J),\qquad
\nu_{\ell_k}(\alpha(p^e))<a_k\ (k\notin J),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ladder-essential-rank-test}
\nu_{\ell_i}(\alpha(p^{e-1}))<a_i,\qquad
\nu_{\ell_k}(\alpha(p^{e-1}))=a_k\quad(k\in J,\ k\ne i).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for any one of the eight Fibonacci-admissible
three-coordinate types in Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness},
nonemptiness is decided by the following finite computation: factor \(F_n\),
compute \(\alpha(p^e)\) and \(\alpha(p^{e-1})\) for the finitely many
prime-power divisors \(p^e\mid F_n\), place each atom into the families determined
by \eqref{eq:primitive-rank-test}--\eqref{eq:ladder-essential-rank-test}, and
then test the finitely many displayed pairwise-coprime selections.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The set of primes \(p\mid F_n\) is finite, and for each such prime the possible
exponents \(e\) are bounded by \(\nu_p(F_n)\). Thus the proposed procedure has
only finitely many candidates.

Let \(p\mid F_n\) be prime. Since \(\alpha(p)\mid n\), the definition of
\(T_n^\#\) gives
\[
T_n^\#(p)=
\{i:\nu_{\ell_i}(\alpha(p))=a_i\}.
\]
Because \(p\) is primitive, \(d^-(p)=\alpha(1)=1\), so
\(E_n^\#(p)=T_n^\#(p)\). Hence \(p\in P_J(n)\) if and only if the full
coordinates of \(\alpha(p)\) are exactly \(J\), which is precisely
\eqref{eq:primitive-rank-test}.

Now let \(p^e\mid F_n\) with \(e>1\). The inequality
\(\alpha(p^{e-1})<\alpha(p^e)\) is exactly the atomicity condition
\(p^e\in\Atom(\alpha(p^e))\). The divisibility \(\alpha(p^e)\mid n\) is the
condition that the atom is relevant to the \(n\)-layer. Under these two
conditions,
\[
T_n^\#(p^e)=
\{k:\nu_{\ell_k}(\alpha(p^e))=a_k\},
\]
so \(T_n^\#(p^e)=J\) is equivalent to
\eqref{eq:ladder-full-rank-test}. Similarly, by the definition of \(E_n\),
\[
E_n^\#(p^e)=
\{k\in J:\nu_{\ell_k}(\alpha(p^{e-1}))<a_k\}.
\]
Thus \(E_n^\#(p^e)=\{i\}\) is equivalent to
\eqref{eq:ladder-essential-rank-test}. This proves the displayed
characterization of \(L_{i,J}(n)\). Combining these two exact descriptions
with the pairwise-coprime selection list in
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} gives the stated finite
decision procedure.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Three-coordinate nonemptiness criterion]
\label{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness}
Assume \(\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}\). A three-coordinate type is
realized by some \(m\in\mathcal M_n\) if and only if, after a simultaneous
permutation of the coordinate labels, one of the following pairwise-coprime
selections exists:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\Gamma_1: & \theta\in P_{\{1,2,3\}}(n),\\[1mm]
\Gamma_3: & \theta\in L_{1,\{1,2,3\}}(n),\\[1mm]
\Gamma_4: & \theta_{12}\in P_{\{1,2\}}(n),\quad
             \theta_{13}\in P_{\{1,3\}}(n),\quad
             \gcd(\theta_{12},\theta_{13})=1,\\[1mm]
\Gamma_5: & \theta_{1,12}\in L_{1,\{1,2\}}(n),\quad
             \theta_{23}\in P_{\{2,3\}}(n),\quad
             \gcd(\theta_{1,12},\theta_{23})=1,\\[1mm]
\Gamma_6: & \theta_{1,12}\in L_{1,\{1,2\}}(n),\quad
             \theta_{3,23}\in L_{3,\{2,3\}}(n),\quad
             \gcd(\theta_{1,12},\theta_{3,23})=1,\\[1mm]
\Gamma_7: & \theta_1\in P_{\{1\}}(n)\cup L_{1,\{1\}}(n),\quad
             \theta_{23}\in P_{\{2,3\}}(n),\quad
             \gcd(\theta_1,\theta_{23})=1,\\[1mm]
\Gamma_8: & \theta_1\in P_{\{1\}}(n)\cup L_{1,\{1\}}(n),\quad
             \theta_{2,23}\in L_{2,\{2,3\}}(n),\quad
             \gcd(\theta_1,\theta_{2,23})=1,\\[1mm]
\Gamma_9: & \theta_i\in P_{\{i\}}(n)\cup L_{i,\{i\}}(n)\quad(1\le i\le3),
             \quad \gcd(\theta_i,\theta_j)=1\ (i\ne j).
\end{array}
\]
The abstract type \(\Gamma_2\) is never realized.
The families \(P_J(n)\) and \(L_{i,J}(n)\) appearing in the list are the finite
rank-test families of Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-finite-recipe}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The nonrealizability of \(\Gamma_2\) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types}. For the remaining types,
combine the abstract list in Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-three-types} with the
atom-family decomposition in Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-atom-families}.
That proposition says precisely that a pair \((I,J)\) can be filled by an atom
from \(P_J(n)\) when \(I=J\), by an atom from \(L_{i,J}(n)\) when
\(I=\{i\}\subsetneq J\), and by either \(P_{\{i\}}(n)\) or
\(L_{i,\{i\}}(n)\) in the singleton diagonal case \(I=J=\{i\}\).
Substituting these alternatives into the eight surviving \(\Gamma\)-patterns
gives exactly the displayed list.

If a displayed selection exists, pairwise coprimeness is one of the conditions
in Definition~\ref{def:witness-cover}; the listed \(T_n^\#\)-sets cover
\(\{1,2,3\}\), and the listed \(E_n^\#\)-sets provide the private coordinates
specified by the corresponding \(\Gamma\)-pattern. Hence the selected atoms form
an \(n\)-witness cover. Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} then puts the
product of the selected atoms in \(\mathcal M_n\), with the asserted type.

Conversely, let \(m\in\mathcal M_n\) realize one of the eight surviving types,
and let \(\Theta\) be its witness cover. Each atom in \(\Theta\) belongs to the
family prescribed for its pair \((E_n^\#(\theta),T_n^\#(\theta))\) by
Proposition~\ref{prop:three-coordinate-atom-families}. Since \(\Theta\) is a
witness cover, its atoms are pairwise coprime. Thus \(m\) supplies one of the
displayed selections, after the coordinate permutation identifying its type
with the representative \(\Gamma_j\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:three-coordinate-realization}
Assume \(\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}\). The set
\(\mathcal M_n\) is the disjoint union, by witness cover, of the products
obtained from the pairwise-coprime selections in
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} and all simultaneous
permutations of the coordinate labels. Thus an admissible type
\(\Gamma_j\) with \(j\ne2\) is realized at \(n\) exactly when its corresponding
selection set in Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} is nonempty;
\(\Gamma_2\) is never realized.

For \(n=30\), with coordinates \((2,3,5)\), the atoms
\[
\begin{gathered}
31\in P_{\{1,2,3\}}(30),\quad
4\in L_{1,\{1,2\}}(30),\quad
2\in P_{\{2\}}(30),\\
11\in P_{\{1,3\}}(30),\quad
5\in P_{\{3\}}(30),\quad
61\in P_{\{2,3\}}(30)
\end{gathered}
\]
give exactly the five witness covers
\(\{31\},\{4,5\},\{2,11\},\{4,61\},\{11,61\}\). These realize, up to
coordinate relabeling, \(\Gamma_1,\Gamma_8,\Gamma_7,\Gamma_5\), and
\(\Gamma_4\), respectively; they do not realize
\(\Gamma_3,\Gamma_6\), or \(\Gamma_9\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} identifies \(\mathcal M_n\) with the
products of \(n\)-witness covers, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} identifies exactly which
three-coordinate witness covers exist. A product determines its witness cover
uniquely as its set of pairwise coprime prime-power factors, so the union is
disjoint when indexed by the actual selected cover. The assertion for \(n=30\)
is a direct verification from the ranks
\[
\alpha(31)=30,\quad \alpha(4)=6,\quad \alpha(2)=3,\quad
\alpha(11)=10,\quad \alpha(5)=5,\quad \alpha(61)=15,
\]
and the definitions of \(P_J(30)\) and \(L_{i,J}(30)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Primitive three-coordinate skeleton]
\label{cor:primitive-three-skeleton}
Assume \(\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}\). The primitive
three-coordinate witness covers are exactly the following pairwise-coprime
selections, after simultaneous permutation of the coordinate labels:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\Gamma_1: & p\in P_{\{1,2,3\}}(n),\\[1mm]
\Gamma_4: & p_{12}\in P_{\{1,2\}}(n),\quad
             p_{13}\in P_{\{1,3\}}(n),\quad
             p_{12}\ne p_{13},\\[1mm]
\Gamma_7: & p_1\in P_{\{1\}}(n),\quad
             p_{23}\in P_{\{2,3\}}(n),\quad
             p_1\ne p_{23},\\[1mm]
\Gamma_9: & p_i\in P_{\{i\}}(n)\quad(1\le i\le3),\quad
             p_1,p_2,p_3\text{ distinct}.
\end{array}
\]
The corresponding primitive minimal generators are the products of the selected
primes.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If a witness cover is primitive, then
Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy} gives
\(E_n^\#(\theta)=T_n^\#(\theta)\) for every atom in it. Therefore its type can
contain only diagonal pairs \((I,I)\). Among the eight Fibonacci-admissible
three-coordinate types, the diagonal-only types are
\(\Gamma_1,\Gamma_4,\Gamma_7\), and \(\Gamma_9\). Filling their diagonal pairs
with primitive atoms is exactly the displayed list. Since the selected atoms are
primes, pairwise coprimeness is equivalent to distinctness. Conversely, every
displayed selection has full coordinate coverage and the required private
coordinates, hence is a primitive \(n\)-witness cover; multiplying its primes
gives an element of \(\mathcal M_n\) by
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:wss}
The Wall--Sun--Sun condition enters through
the prime-power formula in Proposition~\ref{prop:prime-power-ladders}: for
$p\neq 2,5$, one has
$\alpha(p^2)=\alpha(p)$ if and only if $\nu_p(F_{\alpha(p)})\ge 2$, which is
precisely the definition of a Wall--Sun--Sun prime
\cite{SunSun1992FibonacciFLT}. No such primes are currently known, and their
nonexistence would imply that every odd prime contributes an immediate ladder
step above the first atomic level.
\end{remark}

\section{Connected block factorizations}\label{sec:connected}
%% ====================================================================

Throughout this section, write
$n_C:=\prod_{\ell\in C}\ell^{\nu_\ell(n)}$ for nonempty
$C\subseteq\mathcal P(n)$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:support-hypergraph}
Let $q\in D_n$ and write
\[
q=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{e_i}
\]
with distinct primes $p_i$. The \emph{support hypergraph} $H_n(q)$ has vertex
set $\mathcal P(n)$ and nonempty hyperedges
\[
\operatorname{supp}_n(p_i^{e_i})
:=\{\ell\in\mathcal P(n):\nu_\ell(\alpha(p_i^{e_i}))>0\}.
\]
Prime-power factors with empty support are ignored. We call $q$ \emph{connected}
if $H_n(q)$ is connected on its non-isolated vertices; for $q\in B_n$ this is
equivalently connected on all of $\mathcal P(n)$.
Define
\[
A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n):=\#\{q\in B_n:q\text{ is connected}\},
\qquad
M^{\mathrm{conn}}(n):=\#\{m\in\mathcal M_n:m\text{ is connected}\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:coprime-embedding}
Let $C_1,\ldots,C_s$ be pairwise disjoint nonempty subsets of
$\mathcal P(n)$. If $q_j\in B_{n_{C_j}}$ for each $j$, then the integers
$q_1,\ldots,q_s$ are pairwise coprime, their product divides
$F_{n_{C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_s}}$, and
\[
\alpha(q_1\cdots q_s)=n_{C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_s}.
\]
In particular, if $\{C_1,\ldots,C_s\}$ is a partition of $\mathcal P(n)$,
then $q_1\cdots q_s\in B_n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $i\ne j$, the indices $n_{C_i}$ and $n_{C_j}$ are coprime. Hence
\[
\gcd(q_i,q_j)\mid \gcd(F_{n_{C_i}},F_{n_{C_j}})
=F_{\gcd(n_{C_i},n_{C_j})}=F_1=1
\]
by strong divisibility, so the factors are pairwise coprime. Since
$n_{C_j}\mid n_{C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_s}$, each $q_j$ divides
$F_{n_{C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_s}}$, and so does the product. Finally,
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-basics} gives
\[
\alpha(q_1\cdots q_s)=\lcmop(n_{C_1},\ldots,n_{C_s})
=n_{C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_s}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:birth-connected-factorization}
Let $q\in B_n$, and let $C_1,\ldots,C_s$ be the connected components of
$H_n(q)$. For each component $C$, let $q_C$ be the product of those
prime-power factors $p^e\Vert q$ for which
\(\operatorname{supp}_n(p^e)\subseteq C\). Then
\[
q=q_{C_1}\cdots q_{C_s},\qquad q_C\in B_{n_C},
\qquad \alpha(q_C)=n_C,
\]
and every $q_C$ is connected. This factorization is unique.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every prime-power factor of $q$ has support contained in exactly one connected
component, so the displayed product and uniqueness are immediate from unique
factorization once the components are known.

Fix a component $C$. If \(p^e\Vert q_C\), then
\(\alpha(p^e)\mid n\) and all prime coordinates of \(\alpha(p^e)\) lie in
$C$, hence \(\alpha(p^e)\mid n_C\). Therefore \(p^e\mid F_{n_C}\), and
\(q_C\mid F_{n_C}\). Thus \(\alpha(q_C)\mid n_C\).
Conversely, for each \(\ell\in C\), the equality \(\alpha(q)=n\) implies
that some prime-power factor \(p^e\Vert q\) has
\(\nu_\ell(\alpha(p^e))=\nu_\ell(n)\). Its support lies in the same component
$C$, so it divides \(q_C\). Hence the \(\ell\)-coordinate of
\(\alpha(q_C)\) has full exponent \(\nu_\ell(n)\). This holds for every
\(\ell\in C\), proving \(\alpha(q_C)=n_C\). The hypergraph of \(q_C\) is the
component \(C\), so \(q_C\) is connected.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mingenerator-connected-factorization}
Let \(m\in\mathcal M_n\), and write
\[
m=\prod_{C\in\pi}m_C
\]
for the connected-block factorization of
Theorem~\ref{thm:birth-connected-factorization}. Then
\(m_C\in\mathcal M_{n_C}\) for every block \(C\in\pi\). Conversely, if
\(\pi\in\Part(\mathcal P(n))\) and \(m_C\in\mathcal M_{n_C}\) is connected
for every \(C\in\pi\), then \(\prod_{C\in\pi}m_C\in\mathcal M_n\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(m\in\mathcal M_n\). The preceding theorem gives \(m_C\in B_{n_C}\).
If some proper divisor \(r_C<m_C\) belonged to \(B_{n_C}\), then
\[
r_C\prod_{D\in\pi,\ D\ne C}m_D
\]
would divide \(m\) properly and would have rank
\(\lcmop(n_C,n_D:D\ne C)=n\) by Theorem~\ref{thm:coprime-embedding},
contradicting the minimality of \(m\). Thus \(m_C\in\mathcal M_{n_C}\).

Conversely, let \(m=\prod_{C\in\pi}m_C\) with connected
\(m_C\in\mathcal M_{n_C}\). Theorem~\ref{thm:coprime-embedding} gives
\(m\in B_n\). Suppose \(s\mid m\) and \(s\in B_n\). Put
\[
s_C:=\gcd(s,m_C)\qquad(C\in\pi).
\]
The factors \(m_C\) are pairwise coprime, so \(s=\prod_C s_C\). Since
\(s_C\mid F_{n_C}\), the rank \(\alpha(s_C)\) divides \(n_C\). The equality
\(\alpha(s)=n\) forces, in every block \(C\), full exponent in every
coordinate of \(C\); because no other block contains those coordinates, this
means \(\alpha(s_C)=n_C\), or \(s_C\in B_{n_C}\). Minimality of \(m_C\) then
forces \(s_C=m_C\) for all \(C\), so \(s=m\). Hence \(m\in\mathcal M_n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Partition formulas]\label{cor:partition-formulas}
For every \(n\ge2\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition-formula}
A(n)=\sum_{\pi\in\Part(\mathcal P(n))}
\prod_{C\in\pi}A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n_C),
\qquad
\#\mathcal M_n=\sum_{\pi\in\Part(\mathcal P(n))}
\prod_{C\in\pi}M^{\mathrm{conn}}(n_C).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, by Mobius inversion on the partition lattice,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:connected-inversion}
A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n)=
\sum_{\pi\in\Part(\mathcal P(n))}
(-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)!\prod_{C\in\pi}A(n_C),
\end{equation}
and the same formula with \(A\) replaced by \(\#\mathcal M\) and
\(A^{\mathrm{conn}}\) replaced by \(M^{\mathrm{conn}}\) holds for minimal
generators.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:birth-connected-factorization} gives a unique decomposition
of each \(q\in B_n\) into connected elements indexed by a partition of
\(\mathcal P(n)\), and Theorem~\ref{thm:coprime-embedding} gives the converse
product construction. Counting these decompositions gives the formula for
\(A(n)\). The same argument using
Theorem~\ref{thm:mingenerator-connected-factorization} gives the formula for
\(\#\mathcal M_n\).

The displayed inversion is the standard Mobius inversion formula for the
partition lattice. The Mobius value from a partition \(\pi\) to the top
one-block partition is
\((-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)!\), which gives
\eqref{eq:connected-inversion}; the minimal-generator formula is identical.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:four-support-reduction}
Assume \(\omega(n)=4\). Every disconnected element of \(\mathcal M_n\) is a
product of connected minimal generators supported on proper coordinate blocks.
Consequently a complete classification for \(\mathcal M_n\) at four
coordinates reduces to the connected four-coordinate case together with the
one-, two-, and three-coordinate classifications of the preceding sections.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is the minimal-generator factorization theorem applied to the partitions
of a four-element set. If the partition has more than one block, every block
has size at most three, so each factor belongs to a previously classified
lower-support birth layer. The only part not supplied by lower-support data is
the one-block, connected case.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:odd-primitive-cover-realization}
Let \(n\) be odd. For every partition
\(\pi\in\Part(\mathcal P(n))\) there is a minimal generator
\[
m_\pi=\prod_{C\in\pi}\rho_C\in\mathcal M_n
\]
whose connected blocks are the blocks of \(\pi\), with
\(\alpha(\rho_C)=n_C\). Hence
\[
\#\mathcal M_n\ge \#\Part(\mathcal P(n)),
\]
the Bell number of \(\omega(n)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If \(n\) is odd and \(C\ne\varnothing\), then \(n_C\ge3\) and
\(n_C\notin\{6,12\}\). The primitive-divisor theorem therefore provides a
prime \(\rho_C\) with \(\alpha(\rho_C)=n_C\). For a fixed partition \(\pi\),
the primes \(\rho_C\) are distinct because their ranks are distinct, and the
sets \(T_n(\rho_C)=E_n(\rho_C)=C\) partition \(\mathcal P(n)\). Thus
\(\{\rho_C:C\in\pi\}\) is an \(n\)-witness cover, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:witness-classification} gives
\(m_\pi\in\mathcal M_n\).

The support hypergraph of \(m_\pi\) has connected components exactly the
blocks of \(\pi\). The connected-block factorization is unique, so distinct
partitions give distinct minimal generators. This proves the Bell-type lower
bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Further questions}\label{sec:questions}
%% ====================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Use the three-coordinate nonemptiness criterion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-coordinate-nonemptiness} to study the distribution of
the admissible types, then resolve the connected cores when $\omega(n)=4$. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:four-support-reduction}, only the connected part is
genuinely new.
\item Obtain arithmetic or asymptotic control of the connected counts
$A^{\mathrm{conn}}(n)$ and $M^{\mathrm{conn}}(n)$. The partition-lattice
formulas reduce global invariants to these kernels but do not yet estimate
their frequency or size.
\item Extend the witness-cover formalism to Lucas sequences and, more
generally, to strong divisibility sequences equipped with a
rank-of-apparition map and a primitive-divisor theorem.
\end{enumerate}
