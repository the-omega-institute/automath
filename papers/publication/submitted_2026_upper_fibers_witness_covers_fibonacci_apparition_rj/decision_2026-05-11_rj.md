# Decision record: Ramanujan Journal rejection and FQ route

Date recorded: 2026-05-11

Manuscript: "Upper fibers and witness covers for the Fibonacci order-of-apparition map"

Submission ID: a789f52c-27ee-4112-80cb-9e1f10ed05d3

Latest decision: rejected by The Ramanujan Journal. The editor did not
recommend in-depth review.

## Editorial signal

The rejection is not just a journal-fit mismatch. The substantive criticism is:

- The structural statements appear too close to formal consequences of standard
  facts about ranks of apparition, least common multiples, strong divisibility,
  and primitive divisors.
- The witness-cover and connected-block language is clean, but the editor did
  not see enough new arithmetic depth for The Ramanujan Journal.
- The manuscript lacks sharper applications, nontrivial estimates, or a
  genuinely new arithmetic contribution.
- There is a concrete correctness problem: the text says the first
  three-coordinate layer already exhibits all eight admissible types, while the
  displayed data for n=30 give only five minimal generators. This cannot
  literally support the stated claim.

## Venue judgment

Do not submit the current version directly to The Fibonacci Quarterly.

FQ is a plausible next venue only after an FQ-facing deep revision. The paper is
much closer to FQ's topic area than to RJ: it is explicitly about Fibonacci
numbers, ranks of apparition, and integer-sequence structure. However, FQ will
likely reject or desk-reject the current version if it still reads as a long
formal repackaging with an unresolved numerical-data inconsistency.

## Required revision before any FQ submission

1. Fix the n=30/eight-types statement. Either provide verified examples that
   realize all eight admissible three-coordinate types, or weaken the text to
   the exact types actually realized by the displayed n=30 data.

2. Recast the paper for a Fibonacci-number audience. Move the poset and
   hypergraph language behind concrete rank-of-apparition phenomena, worked
   examples, and explicit formulas.

3. Add a real increment beyond the RJ-rejected version. Acceptable increments:
   a sharp nonemptiness criterion for the three-coordinate families, a new
   nontrivial estimate for A(n) or #M_n, a verified larger computational table
   with reproducible scripts and a theorem-level pattern extracted from it, or
   an extension to Lucas/strong-divisibility sequences with a precise new
   theorem.

4. Shorten and sharpen the manuscript. FQ is a better target for an
   intelligible, well-motivated Fibonacci article than for a broad,
   framework-heavy 35+ page number-theory manuscript.

5. Update the cover letter honestly: this is a substantially revised
   manuscript after RJ rejection, with the RJ criticism addressed by a concrete
   new result and a corrected numerical section.
