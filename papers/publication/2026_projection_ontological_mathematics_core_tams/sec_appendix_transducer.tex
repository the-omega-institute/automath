\section{A padded bounded-delay transducer model for the finite-window fold}
\label{app:transducer}

To make the finite-state kernel construction of
\Cref{thm:collision-kernel,thm:symmetric-compression} self-contained, we spell
out a corrected transducer model for the finite-window fold.  The key point is
that the transducer output must carry the natural leading-zero padding; this is
exactly the convention needed to identify the transducer output with the
finite-window fold itself, even for the zero word.  The automata-theoretic
normalization viewpoint goes back to Frougny
\cite{Frougny1991FibonacciAutomata,Frougny1992NumbersAutomata}; see also
\cite{AhlbachUsatineFrougnyPippenger2013} for algorithmic background.

For a most-significant-digit-first binary word \(a=a_1\cdots a_m\), write
\[
  \operatorname{val}(a):=\sum_{j=1}^m a_jF_{m+2-j}.
\]
If \(\omega=\omega_1\cdots\omega_m\in\Omega_m\), then
\[
  \omega^{\mathrm{rev}}:=\omega_m\cdots\omega_1
\]
satisfies \(\operatorname{val}(\omega^{\mathrm{rev}})=N_m(\omega)\).

\begin{definition}[Padded normalization]
\label{def:appendix-padded-normalization}
For \(a=a_1\cdots a_m\in\{0,1\}^m\), let
\[
  \widetilde\Lambda(a)=b_0b_1\cdots b_m\in\{0,1\}^{m+1}
\]
be the unique length-\((m+1)\) word with no adjacent \(1\)'s and
\[
  \operatorname{val}(a)
  =
  \sum_{j=0}^{m} b_jF_{m+2-j}.
\]
Thus \(\widetilde\Lambda(a)\) is the Zeckendorf normalization of
\(\operatorname{val}(a)\), padded on the left to length \(m+1\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Padded fold transducer]
\label{prop:appendix-padded-transducer}
There exists a deterministic subsequential transducer
\[
  \mathcal T=(Q,q_0,\delta,\lambda,\tau)
\]
with input alphabet \(\{0,1\}\), output alphabet \(\{0,1\}\), and bounded
terminal output length
\[
  L:=\max_{s\in Q}|\tau(s)|,
\]
such that the total output
\[
  \Lambda(a_1\cdots a_m)
  :=
  \lambda^*(q_0,a_1\cdots a_m)\,
  \tau(\delta^*(q_0,a_1\cdots a_m))
\]
is obtained from \(\widetilde\Lambda(a_1\cdots a_m)\) by deleting its first
symbol.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Frougny's Fibonacci-normalization theorem
\cite[pp.~397--398]{Frougny1991FibonacciAutomata} states the following finite-word
form.  In the Fibonacci system \(F_2=1,F_3=2\), the map which sends every
most-significant-digit-first binary word \(a_1\cdots a_m\) (leading zeros
allowed) to the unique admissible word \(b_0\cdots b_m\) with no adjacent
ones and
\[
  \sum_{j=1}^m a_jF_{m+2-j}
  =
  \sum_{j=0}^{m} b_jF_{m+2-j}
\]
is realized by a deterministic subsequential transducer.  Her weight indexing
is therefore exactly the convention here, and the extra \(b_0\) is the leading
zero-padding position in \(\widetilde\Lambda\).  Finally compose that machine
with the two-state subsequential transducer which, in its initial state,
deletes the first output symbol and, in its second state, copies every later
symbol.  Closure under composition gives a deterministic subsequential
transducer whose total output is precisely
\(\widetilde\Lambda(a_1\cdots a_m)\) with its first symbol deleted, as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Direct fold output]
\label{lem:appendix-overflow-digit}
For every \(\omega\in\Omega_m\), if \(x=\Fold_m(\omega)\), then
\[
  \Lambda(\omega^{\mathrm{rev}})=x_m\cdots x_1.
\]
In particular,
\[
  \Lambda(0^m)=0^m.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{prop:single-overflow}, there exists a unique
\(\varepsilon_m(\omega)\in\{0,1\}\) such that
\[
  N_m(\omega)
  =
  \varepsilon_m(\omega)F_{m+2}+V_m(x)
  =
  \varepsilon_m(\omega)F_{m+2}+\sum_{j=1}^m x_jF_{j+1}.
\]
Hence the length-\((m+1)\) admissible word
\[
  \varepsilon_m(\omega)\,x_m\cdots x_1
\]
represents \(N_m(\omega)=\operatorname{val}(\omega^{\mathrm{rev}})\).  When
\(\varepsilon_m(\omega)=1\), one has
\[
  V_m(x)=N_m(\omega)-F_{m+2}<F_{m+1},
\]
so \(x_m=0\), and the displayed word is indeed admissible.  Therefore, by the
uniqueness in \Cref{def:appendix-padded-normalization},
\[
  \widetilde\Lambda(\omega^{\mathrm{rev}})
  =
  \varepsilon_m(\omega)\,x_m\cdots x_1.
\]
Deleting the first symbol gives the claimed identity for
\(\Lambda(\omega^{\mathrm{rev}})\).  The zero-word example is the special case
\(\omega=0^m\), for which \(x=0^m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Prefix stability for visible outputs]
\label{lem:appendix-prefix-stability}
If \(u\) is a prefix of \(v\) in \(\{0,1\}^*\), then \(\Lambda(u)\) and
\(\Lambda(v)\) have a common prefix of length at least
\[
  |\Lambda(u)|-L.
\]
Equivalently, after any processed prefix, at most the final \(L\) visible
symbols can still be altered by extending the input.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(v=uw\), let \(s=\delta^*(q_0,u)\), and decompose the run as
\[
  \Lambda(u)=\alpha\,\tau(s),
  \qquad
  \Lambda(v)=\alpha\,\beta\,\tau(t)
\]
for some word \(\beta\) and some state \(t\).  Thus \(\Lambda(u)\) and
\(\Lambda(v)\) have a common prefix of length
\[
  |\alpha|=|\Lambda(u)|-|\tau(s)|\ge |\Lambda(u)|-L.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Cross-copy residual buffers]
\label{def:appendix-cross-copy-buffers}
For words \(w_1,\dots,w_q\), let
\(\operatorname{lcp}(w_1,\dots,w_q)\) denote their longest common prefix.
A product state stores, for each copy, the suffix left after the common
emitted prefix has been cancelled.  These are cross-copy delay buffers, not
independently chosen terminal buffers.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Finite local residual state set]
\label{prop:appendix-one-copy}
Set
\[
  \widehat Q:=Q\times\{0,1\}^{\le L}.
\]
The asynchronous product stores one transducer state and one binary delay
buffer of length at most \(L\) for each copy.  The local state set has cardinality
\[
  k:=\card{\widehat Q}
  =
  |Q|\sum_{j=0}^{L}2^j
  =
  |Q|\,(2^{L+1}-1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
There are \(|Q|\) choices for \(s\) and
\(\sum_{j=0}^{L}2^j=2^{L+1}-1\) binary words of length at most \(L\).
\end{proof}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Asynchronous common-prefix cancellation.}
For words \(u,v\), write \(u\parallel v\) when one is a prefix of the other.
Given a state \(((s_i,r_i))_{i=1}^q\) and an input tuple
\(\mathbf a=(a_1,\dots,a_q)\), set
\[
  w_i:=r_i\lambda(s_i,a_i),
  \qquad c:=\operatorname{lcp}(w_1,\dots,w_q),
  \qquad w_i=cr_i'.
\]
The transition is admissible when every \(r_i'\) has length at most \(L\) and
\(r_i'\parallel r_j'\) for every \(i,j\); its new state is
\(((\delta(s_i,a_i),r_i'))_{i=1}^q\).  Otherwise it goes to a rejecting sink.
The common prefix \(c\) is recorded only implicitly: it is the output already
matched by every copy.

\begin{example}[An asynchronous \texorpdfstring{$q=2$}{q=2} update]
\label{ex:appendix-q2-update}
If \(w_1=001\) and \(w_2=00101\), then the common prefix is \(c=001\) and
the residuals are \(r_1'=\varepsilon\), \(r_2'=01\).  The second copy is
temporarily ahead; it is not rejected, because the first copy may emit the
missing symbols on a later input step.  If instead \(w_1=00100\) and
\(w_2=00101\), the residuals \(00\) and \(01\) are not prefix-compatible, so
no continuation can make the outputs equal.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Finite collision automaton]
\label{prop:appendix-collision-automaton}
Fix \(q\ge 2\).  There is a finite automaton on \(\widehat Q^q\) with one
transition per input digit such that, for every \(m\ge 1\), accepted
length-\(m\) paths are in canonical bijection with \(q\)-tuples
\[
  (\omega^{(1)},\dots,\omega^{(q)})\in\Omega_m^q
\]
satisfying
\[
  \Fold_m(\omega^{(1)})=\cdots=\Fold_m(\omega^{(q)}).
\]
Consequently there exist a nonnegative integer matrix \(A_q\) and nonnegative
integer vectors \(u_q,v_q\) such that
\[
  S_q(m)=u_q^{\mathsf T}A_q^m v_q
  \qquad (m\ge 1).
\]
The non-sink state count is at most
\[
  k^q=\bigl(|Q|(2^{L+1}-1)\bigr)^q,
  \qquad k=|Q|\,(2^{L+1}-1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Start at \(((q_0,\varepsilon),\dots,(q_0,\varepsilon))\).  A path input tuple
\(\mathbf a\in\{0,1\}^q\) is read as
\(a_i=(\omega^{(i)})^{\mathrm{rev}}\); reversal is a bijection of
\(\Omega_m\), so this is a canonical reparametrization of the input tuples.
Use the asynchronous update above.  The invariant
is that there is a word \(C\), common to all copies, such that the already
emitted transition output of copy \(i\) is
\[
  \lambda^*(q_0,u_i)=Cr_i.
\]
Appending the next transition output and cancelling the new longest common
prefix preserves the invariant.

If two new residuals are not prefix-compatible, their emitted words disagree
before the shorter one ends, so no continuation can make the final outputs
equal.  It is therefore sound to reject.  Conversely, suppose a continuation
can make all final visible outputs equal to a word \(Y\).  For every processed
prefix \(u_i\) of common input length \(n\), \(\Lambda(u_i)\) has length
\(n\), because the padded normalization has length \(n+1\) and one leading
symbol is deleted.  Thus
\[
  n-L\le |\lambda^*(q_0,u_i)|\le n.
\]
Every emitted transition output is a prefix of \(Y\).  Their common-prefix
cancellation therefore leaves buffers of length at most \(L\), and neither
the length test nor the prefix-compatibility test can reject a tuple that
extends to a collision.

At the end of a length-\(m\) input, let the final transducer states be \(t_i\)
and append the terminal outputs to the buffers:
\[
  z_i:=r_i\tau(t_i).
\]
Cancel \(c_{\mathrm{end}}:=\operatorname{lcp}(z_1,\dots,z_q)\) and write
\(z_i=c_{\mathrm{end}}\bar r_i\).  The terminal state is accepting exactly
when all final buffers are empty, \(\bar r_i=\varepsilon\) for every \(i\).
This is equivalent to \(z_1=\cdots=z_q\), hence to equality of the complete
visible outputs because the already cancelled word \(C\) is common to all
copies.  By \Cref{lem:appendix-overflow-digit}, this is equivalent to
\[
  \Fold_m(\omega^{(1)})=\cdots=\Fold_m(\omega^{(q)}).
\]
Every input tuple determines a unique path, and the preceding two implications
show that the accepted paths are exactly the colliding tuples.  This gives the
claimed canonical bijection.

Let \(A_q\) be the adjacency matrix of the non-sink part of this finite
automaton, let \(u_q\) select the unique initial product state
\(((q_0,\varepsilon),\dots,(q_0,\varepsilon))\), and let \(v_q\) mark the
accepting terminal states.  Then the accepted path count is
\[
  u_q^{\mathsf T}A_q^m v_q=S_q(m).
\]
\end{proof}

The proof of \Cref{thm:symmetric-compression} is now immediate from
\Cref{prop:appendix-collision-automaton}.  The asynchronous update and terminal
condition are invariant under permutation of the copies.  Hence the histogram
quotient acts on weak compositions of \(q\) into \(k=\card{\widehat Q}\)
parts.  Its dimension is \(\binom{k+q-1}{q}\): polynomial in \(q\) for a
fixed transducer, but exponential in the buffer bound \(L\); the unquotiented
product has the exponential bound \(k^q\) above.
