The Editors
Journal of Number Theory

Dear Editors,

We submit for your consideration the manuscript "Finite-Window Zeckendorf
Fibers and the Discrete Thermodynamics of Fibonacci Partition Differences."

The paper studies the fiber spectrum of finite-window Zeckendorf stabilization
and its relation to the classical Fibonacci partition function.  Its principal
result is an exact affine reindexing that converts each fiber multiplicity into
a Fibonacci-lag discrete derivative of the indexed partition function.  This
identity transfers Sanna's partition moment constants to the finite-window
collision moments and yields the associated pressure, concentration, and
zero-temperature statements.

For each q >= 2, an asynchronous finite-state collision automaton realizes the
partition constant lambda_q as the spectral radius of a nonnegative integer
matrix.  If L is the terminal-output bound of the fixed fold transducer and
k = |Q|(2^(L+1)-1), the raw product has at most k^q states.  Permutation of the
q copies gives a histogram quotient with at most binomial(k+q-1,q) states.
Thus the quotient is polynomial in q only for the fixed transducer; the paper
does not claim a bound polynomial jointly in q and L.

The manuscript also records a finite arithmetic computation for q = 9,...,17.
The supplied exact tables and deterministic verifier certify the displayed
polynomials' secondary-root separation.  The identification of those
polynomials as the minimal recurrences of the actual moment sequences remains
conditional because the current package does not contain the upstream exact
sequence data, recurrence derivation, minimality exports, or complete Galois
exports.  The manuscript labels that boundary explicitly and does not present
the nine cases as a uniform theorem for an infinite family of q.

We believe the Journal of Number Theory is the appropriate venue because the
paper's contribution is a specialized exact identity and finite arithmetic
dossier in Fibonacci partition theory, rather than a general theorem for a
broad class of numeration systems.

Thank you for considering this submission.

Sincerely,
The Authors
