1. Verdict: reject
Single strongest reason: after prior work is netted out, the manuscript does not contain a theorem of Transactions-level significance.
The affine identification of the finite-window fibers with Fibonacci partition differences is clean and genuinely worthwhile. But the rest of the uniform theory is largely obtained by transferring Sanna’s exponential constants and then applying standard moment convexity, norm comparison, and exponential-Markov arguments. The manuscript itself displays that dependence: Theorem B imports Sanna’s constants, while Theorems C–F build the pressure and concentration package around them.  
More damagingly for the novelty claim around Theorem C, Sanna already proved that λq​ is the Perron–Frobenius eigenvalue of a finite automaton and explicitly concluded that it is an algebraic integer. arXiv+1 The genuinely new portion of Theorem C is therefore the polynomial-size symmetric compression, not the existence of an integral matrix realization or algebraicity itself. The compression is useful, but it is not enough to carry a TAMS paper. 
I would not recommend “major revision” at TAMS because the problem is not presentation or one missing lemma. Even with the proof issue below repaired and all computations fully certified, the venue mismatch remains.
2. Does it clear the TAMS significance threshold?
No. It is mathematically respectable but too small for Transactions.
The right-sized journal is the Journal of Number Theory.
My assessment is not that the work is trivial. Theorem A is an attractive exact identity, and the polynomial-size compression has value. But the manuscript does not alter the general theory of Fibonacci partitions, numeration systems, finite-state normalization, thermodynamic formalism, or algebraic recurrence constants. It studies one specially constructed finite-window fold, shows that its multiplicities are an affine reindexing of a classical coefficient spectrum, and then transports known exponential data to that model. The pressure-band statements are mostly formal consequences once the moment growth rates are available; for example, the canonical concentration proof is a one-line comparison of adjacent moments, and the zero-temperature theorem is an ℓq-norm squeeze using Sanna’s endpoint limit.  
For TAMS, I would need at least one of the following kinds of upgrade: a theorem for a broad class of numeration systems, a genuinely new structural theorem about Fibonacci partition constants, or a uniform arithmetic theorem explaining the recurrence and secondary-root phenomena. The submitted paper has none of those. It has a good specialized identity and a finite arithmetic dossier.
At JNT, after correcting the proof issue and cleaning up the computational status, this is a substantial and coherent paper. At TAMS, it is below the significance line.
3. The point a hostile referee attacks first
The first serious attack is Proposition A.8:

“A transition … is admissible exactly when the corresponding committed blocks agree in all coordinates.” 

That assertion is not justified by the preceding bounded-delay lemma.
The transducer in Proposition A.2 is subsequential: its total output is the concatenation of the transition outputs and a state-dependent terminal output τ(s), whose length is merely bounded by L.  Proposition A.6 then lets each copy independently split
rλ(s,a)=cr′
and calls c its newly committed block. 
The problem is that the commitment boundaries of different copies need not be synchronized. Two runs can eventually have the same total output while emitting different amounts of output at the current input step. A bounded terminal-output length bounds the possible lag; it does not make that lag zero. Thus one copy may produce a nonempty c1​ while another still holds the corresponding symbols in its residual or terminal buffer. Rejecting whenever c1​,…,cq​ are not literally equal at that step can reject runs whose completed visible outputs are equal.
The standard repair is to make the product machine store the unmatched cross-copy output delay—a residual word representing which copy is ahead—and cancel common prefixes asynchronously. Alternatively, the authors could prove that their chosen normalization transducer admits a stronger fixed-delay normal form in which all copies have synchronized commitment lengths. Neither construction nor theorem appears here.
This is not a cosmetic objection. As written, Proposition A.8 does not prove that accepted paths are in bijection with colliding q-tuples, and therefore Theorem 5.2, the symmetric quotient in Theorem 5.3, and the claimed polynomial-size matrix realization are not established. 
I believe the defect is probably repairable, but it requires a real reconstruction of the collision automaton, not a sentence added to the existing proof.
4. The nine explicit values q=9,…,17
In its present form, it reads as a computation, not as a TAMS theorem.
Exact rational root isolation is perfectly legitimate mathematics. The objection is not “a computer was used.” The objection is that the result gives nine isolated cases without a uniform mechanism, does not settle a recognized conjecture, and does not explain why the negative subdominant root must persist. Certification upgrades numerical observation to a rigorous finite statement; it does not upgrade a finite table to broad conceptual significance.
There is also a serious status problem internal to the submission. Section 7 expressly says that, because the submission lacks a machine-verifiable archive containing the source code, raw factorization logs, and exported certificates, the arithmetic window is being treated as “audited computational evidence rather than as part of the theorem chain.”  The appendix repeats that the recurrence and Galois source computations and raw exports are not bundled.  Yet Theorem 7.9 is then stated as an unconditional theorem and uses the asserted minimality and irreducibility of the recurrence polynomials to conclude that every recurrence-root coefficient is nonzero.  
The exact root-isolation verifier can certify the ordering of the roots of the supplied polynomials. It does not by itself certify that those polynomials are the minimal recurrence polynomials of the actual sequences Sq​(m). That upstream identification depends on computational material the paper labels merely audited. Therefore the manuscript currently overstates the logical status of Theorem 7.9.
With a complete archival package—exact sequence data, recurrence derivation, minimality certificates, Galois certificates, deterministic verification scripts, and instructions for independent reproduction—the nine-case result would be publishable as a legitimate computational theorem in a JNT-level paper. It would still not justify publication in TAMS. For TAMS, the authors need a uniform theorem or at least a conceptual criterion implying the negative subdominant root and its modulus separation for an infinite family of q, not certification of the next nine entries in a table.
