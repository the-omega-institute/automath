\section{Metallic threshold classification and the Fischer cover}\label{sec:metallic-cover}

We now classify the overlap threshold throughout the constant-type metallic
quadratic Pisot-unit family.  The golden system is the unique member whose
nontrivial threshold is three: every parameter $A\ge2$ is invertible at every
aperture $m\ge2$.  We prove this first as a finite-block bijection theorem and
then pass to the two-sided SFT conjugacy.  We finally identify the canonical
right-resolving presentation of the Fibonacci image shift in the presented
stabilized alphabet.

\subsection{The metallic two-window boundary}

\begin{definition}[Boundary-modified constant-type rank data]\label{def:metallic-window-data}
Fix an integer $A\ge 1$ and define the metallic quadratic Pisot unit
\[
\beta_A:=\frac{A+\sqrt{A^2+4}}2.
\]
It is the positive root of $x^2-Ax-1$, and its algebraic conjugate is
$-\beta_A^{-1}$, of modulus less than one.  Define
\[
Q_0^{(A)}:=1,\qquad Q_1^{(A)}:=A+1,\qquad
Q_{j+2}^{(A)}:=A Q_{j+1}^{(A)}+Q_j^{(A)}.
\]
This is the metallic recurrence associated with the continued-fraction tail
$[A,A,\ldots]$, shifted so that the golden case $A=1$ gives
$Q_j^{(1)}=F_{j+2}$.
Let
\[
\mathcal A_A:=\{0,1,\ldots,A\}.
\]
For $m\ge 1$, set
\[
X_m^{(A)}
:=
\{x\in \mathcal A_A^m:\ x_k=A\Rightarrow x_{k-1}=0
\text{ for }2\le k\le m\},
\]
and define
\[
\Rank_{A,m}(x):=\sum_{k=1}^m x_k Q_{k-1}^{(A)}.
\]
The corresponding cyclic rank recoding is
\[
\Fold_{A,m}(\omega)
:=
\bigl(\Rank_{A,m}|_{X_m^{(A)}}\bigr)^{-1}
\bigl(\Rank_{A,m}(\omega)\bmod Q_m^{(A)}\bigr),
\]
whenever the displayed restriction is bijective. For $m\ge2$, define the
sliding stabilized-window map and its image by
\[
(\Phi_{A,m}(a))_t:=\Fold_{A,m}(a_t,\ldots,a_{t+m-1}),
\qquad
Y_{A,m}:=\Phi_{A,m}(\mathcal A_A^{\mathbb Z}).
\]
For $n\ge1$, the corresponding finite-block map is
\[
\Phi_{A,m,n}:\mathcal A_A^{n+m-1}\longrightarrow (X_m^{(A)})^n,
\qquad
\Phi_{A,m,n}(a_0\ldots a_{n+m-2})
:=\bigl(\Fold_{A,m}(a_t,\ldots,a_{t+m-1})\bigr)_{t=0}^{n-1}.
\]
The notation $\Phi_{A,2}$ and $Y_{A,2}$ is the specialization $m=2$.
\end{definition}

The bijectivity clause in Definition~\ref{def:metallic-window-data} holds for
every $A\ge1$ and $m\ge1$: for $A=1$ it is
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-window-range}, via
Lemma~\ref{lem:metallic-golden-member} below, and for $A\ge2$ it is proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:metallic-two-window-rigidity}(i).

\begin{definition}[Nontrivial metallic conjugacy threshold]
\label{def:metallic-conjugacy-threshold}
For $A\ge1$, define
\[
m_*(\beta_A):=\min\{m\ge2:\Phi_{A,m}\text{ is a topological conjugacy
onto the SFT }Y_{A,m}\}.
\]
The restriction $m\ge2$ is essential: at $m=1$ the legal alphabet is all of
$\mathcal A_A$, the modulus is $Q_1^{(A)}=A+1$, and $\Fold_{A,1}$ is the
identity.  Including this trivial aperture would therefore obscure the
golden failure at $m=2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the beta-family statement]
\label{rem:metallic-beta-scope}
In this section, ``metallic quadratic Pisot base'' means precisely
$\beta_A$, the positive root of $x^2-Ax-1$, with integer $A\ge1$, together
with the digit set, admissibility rule, language rank and cyclic modulus in
Definition~\ref{def:metallic-window-data}.  The extension to other quadratic
minimal polynomials uses the distinct canonical rank construction of
Definition~\ref{def:quadratic-parry-fold} and is proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:full-quadratic-pisot-threshold}.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Boundary-modified constant-type recurrence model]
\label{lem:constant-type-ostrowski-origin}
Let
\[
\alpha_A=[0;A,A,A,\ldots],\qquad A\ge1,
\]
and let $(q_n)$ be the denominators of its continued-fraction convergents, with
$q_{-1}=0$ and $q_0=1$. Then
\[
q_{n+1}=Aq_n+q_{n-1}\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
With the finite-window indexing
\[
Q_j^{(A)}:=q_j+q_{j-1}\qquad(j\ge0),
\]
this is exactly
\[
Q_0^{(A)}=1,\qquad Q_1^{(A)}=A+1,\qquad
Q_{j+2}^{(A)}=AQ_{j+1}^{(A)}+Q_j^{(A)}.
\]
The local constraint used in Definition~\ref{def:metallic-window-data} is the
constant-type Ostrowski constraint
\[
x_k\in\{0,\ldots,A\},\qquad x_k=A\Rightarrow x_{k-1}=0 .
\]
However, the model is not the standard Ostrowski representation associated
with $\alpha_A$: standard digits are weighted by $q_j$, and the first digit
has the additional boundary restriction determined by the first partial
quotient.  Here the weights are $Q_j^{(A)}=q_j+q_{j-1}$ and the lowest digit
$x_1=A$ is permitted.  It is therefore a boundary-modified constant-type
recurrence model using the Ostrowski local constraint, followed by cyclic
language-rank reduction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a continued fraction $[0;a_1,a_2,\ldots]$, the denominator recurrence is
\[
q_{n+1}=a_{n+1}q_n+q_{n-1},
\qquad q_{-1}=0,\quad q_0=1 .
\]
Since every partial quotient here is $A$, the displayed recurrence follows.
The sequence
$Q_j^{(A)}=q_j+q_{j-1}$ has $Q_0^{(A)}=1$ and $Q_1^{(A)}=A+1$, and it satisfies
the same constant recurrence by linearity:
\[
Q_{j+2}^{(A)}
=q_{j+2}+q_{j+1}
=A(q_{j+1}+q_j)+(q_j+q_{j-1})
=AQ_{j+1}^{(A)}+Q_j^{(A)}.
\]
Thus the modulus obeys the usual constant-type denominator recurrence but has
the boundary modification $Q_j^{(A)}=q_j+q_{j-1}$, analogous to using
$F_{m+2}$ in the golden finite-window model.

Away from the lowest digit, the Ostrowski admissibility rule for constant
partial quotients is $0\le x_k\le A$ and, whenever $x_k=A$, the preceding
lower digit is zero.  This is the local greedy uniqueness condition: if
$x_k=A$ and $x_{k-1}>0$, then the recurrence permits a carry replacing
$Aq_k+x_{k-1}q_{k-1}$ by $q_{k+1}+(x_{k-1}-1)q_{k-1}$, so the expansion is not
greedy; conversely the greedy algorithm never creates the forbidden adjacent
pattern.  Definition~\ref{def:metallic-window-data} deliberately retains this
local rule while changing both the weights and the lower boundary.  Standard
Ostrowski conventions and their first-digit restriction are described in
\cite{HieronymiTerry2018}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Golden member of the metallic notation]\label{lem:metallic-golden-member}
If the formulas of Definition~\ref{def:metallic-window-data} are evaluated at
$A=1$, then
\[
Q_j^{(1)}=F_{j+2},\qquad
X_m^{(1)}=X_m,\qquad
\Rank_{1,m}=\Val_m,\qquad
\Fold_{1,m}=\Fold_m.
\]
In particular $\Phi_{1,2}$ is exactly the Fibonacci two-window map $\Phi_2$.
Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:constant-type-ostrowski-origin}, this identifies
the golden case as the formal $A=1$ endpoint of the same self-contained
boundary-modified constant-type recurrence model; no identification with a
standard Ostrowski representation is used in the comparison theorem.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The sequence $(Q_j^{(1)})$ has initial values $Q_0^{(1)}=1=F_2$ and
$Q_1^{(1)}=2=F_3$, and satisfies
$Q_{j+2}^{(1)}=Q_{j+1}^{(1)}+Q_j^{(1)}$.  Hence
$Q_j^{(1)}=F_{j+2}$ for all $j$.

When $A=1$, the alphabet is $\{0,1\}$ and the legality condition
$x_k=1\Rightarrow x_{k-1}=0$ is exactly the no-adjacent-ones condition
defining $X_m$.  The language-rank formula becomes
\[
\Rank_{1,m}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^m x_kQ_{k-1}^{(1)}
=\sum_{k=1}^m x_kF_{k+1}=\Val_m(x).
\]
The modulus $Q_m^{(1)}$ is $F_{m+2}$, so the two definitions of finite-window
cyclic rank recoding agree.  Applying this at every coordinate for $m=2$ gives
$\Phi_{1,2}=\Phi_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-window rigidity]
\label{thm:metallic-two-window-rigidity}
Within the boundary-modified constant-type cyclic rank family of
Definition~\ref{def:metallic-window-data}, for every integer $A\ge 2$ the
following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item For every $m\ge 1$, the restriction
  \[
  \Rank_{A,m}|_{X_m^{(A)}}:X_m^{(A)}\to
  \{0,1,\ldots,Q_m^{(A)}-1\}
  \]
  is a bijection.
  \item At $m=2$ the cyclic rank recoding is explicit:
  \[
  \Fold_{A,2}(u,v)=
  \begin{cases}
  (u,v), & v<A\text{ or }u=0,\\
  (u-1,0), & v=A\text{ and }u\ge 1.
  \end{cases}
  \]
  \item The two-window sliding map
  \[
  \Phi_{A,2}:(\mathcal A_A^{\mathbb Z},\sigma)\to
  (Y_{A,2},\sigma),
  \qquad
  Y_{A,2}:=\Phi_{A,2}(\mathcal A_A^{\mathbb Z}),
  \]
  is a topological conjugacy onto its image.  Its inverse is the
  two-symbol sliding-block code
  \[
  a_t=\psi_A(y_{t-1},y_t),\qquad y=\Phi_{A,2}(a),
  \]
  where, for $y_s=(p_s,q_s)\in X_2^{(A)}$,
  \[
  \psi_A(y_{t-1},y_t)=
  \begin{cases}
  q_{t-1}, & q_{t-1}>0,\\
  0, & q_{t-1}=0\text{ and }p_t=0,\\
  A, & q_{t-1}=0\text{ and }p_t\in\{A-1,A\}.
  \end{cases}
  \]
  The three alternatives are exhaustive for pairs in $\mathcal L_2(Y_{A,2})$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, within the boundary-modified constant-type cyclic rank family
of Definition~\ref{def:metallic-window-data}, the golden case $A=1$ is
the only member of this model in which a nontrivial two-window branch locus
remains.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First prove the finite-window range statement.  Let $N_m:=|X_m^{(A)}|$.
Clearly $N_0=1$ and $N_1=A+1$.  For $m\ge2$, a legal word ending in a digit
$0,\ldots,A-1$ has an arbitrary legal prefix of length $m-1$, while a legal
word ending in $A$ must have penultimate digit $0$ and therefore has an
arbitrary legal prefix of length $m-2$.  Hence
\[
N_m=A N_{m-1}+N_{m-2}.
\]
Thus $N_m=Q_m^{(A)}$ for all $m$.

The same last-digit decomposition proves that the rank gives disjoint
consecutive intervals. For $m=1$ this is immediate. Assume the statement
known up to length $m-1$. If the last digit is $d<A$, then
\[
\Rank_{A,m}(x)=\Rank_{A,m-1}(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1})+dQ_{m-1}^{(A)}
\]
runs through the interval
\[
[dQ_{m-1}^{(A)},(d+1)Q_{m-1}^{(A)}-1].
\]
As $d=0,\ldots,A-1$, these intervals cover
$[0,AQ_{m-1}^{(A)}-1]$. If the last digit is $A$, legality forces
$x_{m-1}=0$, and the remaining prefix of length $m-2$ gives precisely
\[
[AQ_{m-1}^{(A)},AQ_{m-1}^{(A)}+Q_{m-2}^{(A)}-1]
=[AQ_{m-1}^{(A)},Q_m^{(A)}-1].
\]
The intervals are disjoint and cover exactly
$\{0,\ldots,Q_m^{(A)}-1\}$.  This proves (i).

For $m=2$ one has $Q_2^{(A)}=A^2+A+1$ and
\[
\Rank_{A,2}(u,v)=u+(A+1)v.
\]
If $v<A$ or $u=0$, the pair $(u,v)$ is legal and its rank lies in
$[0,Q_2^{(A)}-1]$, so it is fixed by the recoding.  If $v=A$ and $u\ge1$,
then
\[
u+(A+1)A=Q_2^{(A)}+(u-1),
\]
whose residue is $u-1$.  The unique legal pair of rank $u-1$ is
$(u-1,0)$.  This proves (ii).

It remains to prove the path-space assertion. Let
$a\in\mathcal A_A^{\mathbb Z}$ and $y=\Phi_{A,2}(a)$, with
$y_t=(p_t,q_t)$.  By the explicit formula in (ii), if $q_{t-1}>0$ then the
raw second digit of the pair $(a_{t-1},a_t)$ is exactly $q_{t-1}$, hence
$a_t=q_{t-1}$.

Suppose next that $q_{t-1}=0$.  The explicit formula in (ii) shows that the
only possible values of $a_t$ are $0$ and $A$.  If $a_t=0$, then the next raw
pair starts with $0$, is already legal for every value of $a_{t+1}$, and its
recoded output has first coordinate $p_t=0$.  If $a_t=A$, then the next raw
pair starts with $A$.  When $a_{t+1}<A$, the pair is legal and the next output
has first coordinate $p_t=A$; when $a_{t+1}=A$, the pair $(A,A)$ is recoded to
$(A-1,0)$ and the next output has first coordinate $p_t=A-1$.  Since
$A\ge2$, the sets $\{0\}$ and $\{A-1,A\}$ are disjoint.  Therefore the displayed
formula for $\psi_A$ recovers $a_t$ uniquely from $(y_{t-1},y_t)$.

The map $\Phi_{A,2}$ is continuous, shift-equivariant and surjective onto
$Y_{A,2}$ by definition. The preceding local formula gives a continuous
shift-equivariant inverse on $Y_{A,2}$, proving that $\Phi_{A,2}$ is a
topological conjugacy onto its image.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:metallic-golden-member}, the formal $A=1$ member of this
notation is the Fibonacci map $\Phi_2$.  For $A=1$, the separating sets become
$\{0\}$ and $\{0,1\}$, so the preceding local distinction collapses. The
remaining ambiguity is exactly the golden two-window branch locus classified
in Theorem~\ref{thm:m2-branch-locus}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The family-wide block threshold and conjugacy}

\begin{theorem}[Metallic family block-bijection theorem]
\label{thm:metallic-family-block-bijection}
Let $A\ge1$ be an integer, let $m\ge2$, and assume
\[
(A,m)\ne(1,2).
\]
Then, for every $n\ge m$, the finite-block map
\[
\Phi_{A,m,n}:\mathcal A_A^{n+m-1}\longrightarrow (X_m^{(A)})^n
\]
is injective.  Equivalently, it is a bijection from its domain onto the
length-$n$ language of $Y_{A,m}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metallic-block-count}
|\mathcal L_n(Y_{A,m})|=(A+1)^{n+m-1}\qquad(n\ge m).
\end{equation}
For $A=1$, this is the Fibonacci block-bijection theorem
Theorem~\ref{thm:block-invertibility}; in particular it retains its explicit
three-window decoder from Lemma~\ref{lem:m3-decoder}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The specialization $A=1$ is exactly
Theorem~\ref{thm:block-invertibility}, in view of
Lemma~\ref{lem:metallic-golden-member}.  We therefore assume $A\ge2$.
Write $Q_j=Q_j^{(A)}$ for brevity.  Since
Theorem~\ref{thm:metallic-two-window-rigidity}(i) makes
$\Rank_{A,m}|_{X_m^{(A)}}$ bijective, equality of stabilized output words is
equivalent to equality of their language-rank residues modulo $Q_m$.

We first prove injectivity for $n=m$, treating the two small apertures
separately.

Suppose $m=2$.  A raw block $a_0a_1a_2$ produces two outputs
$y_0,y_1$.  The local rule in
Theorem~\ref{thm:metallic-two-window-rigidity}(iii), applied with $t=1$,
recovers $a_1$ from $(y_0,y_1)$.  If
\[
r_i:=\Rank_{A,2}(y_i),\qquad i=0,1,
\]
then
\[
r_0\equiv a_0+Q_1a_1,\qquad
r_1\equiv a_1+Q_1a_2\pmod {Q_2}.
\]
The first congruence recovers $a_0$, because two candidates differ by an
integer of modulus at most $A<Q_2$.  The second recovers $a_2$: indeed
$\gcd(Q_1,Q_2)=\gcd(A+1,A^2+A+1)=1$, so equality for two candidates would
imply that $Q_2$ divides their difference, whose modulus is at most $A$.
Thus $\Phi_{A,2,2}$ is injective.

Suppose next that $m=3$.  Let $a_0\ldots a_4$ and $b_0\ldots b_4$ have the
same three output residues, and put
\[
d_i:=a_i-b_i\in[-A,A].
\]
Using $Q_0=1$, $Q_1=A+1$ and
$Q_{j+2}=AQ_{j+1}+Q_j$, the combination of the three residue congruences
with coefficients $1,-A,-1$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metallic-m3-difference}
d_0+d_1-Q_2d_4\equiv0\pmod {Q_3}.
\end{equation}
Set $s=d_0+d_1$, so $|s|\le2A$.  Since
\[
Q_2=A^2+A+1>2A,\qquad Q_3=AQ_2+Q_1,
\]
the distance of $dQ_2$ from $Q_3\ZZ$ is greater than $2A$ whenever
$1\le |d|\le A-1$.  For $d=A$ one has
$AQ_2=Q_3-Q_1$, and the case $d=-A$ is symmetric.  Hence
\eqref{eq:metallic-m3-difference} leaves precisely the alternatives
\[
(d_4,s)=(0,0),\qquad (A,-Q_1),\qquad(-A,Q_1).
\]

If $d_4=0$, equality of the last output residue gives
\[
d_2+Q_1d_3\equiv0\pmod {Q_3}.
\]
Its left-hand side has modulus at most $A+AQ_1=A^2+2A<Q_3$, so it is zero
as an integer.  Since $|d_2|\le A<Q_1$, this forces $d_2=d_3=0$.  The first
residue then similarly gives $d_0+Q_1d_1=0$, whence $d_0=d_1=0$.

If $d_4=A$, the last residue congruence and
$AQ_2=Q_3-Q_1$ give
\[
d_2+Q_1(d_3-1)\equiv0\pmod {Q_3}.
\]
The modulus of the left-hand side is at most
$A+Q_1(A+1)=A^2+3A+1<Q_3$, where the last inequality is
$A^3-A>0$.  Thus it is zero as an integer, and
$|d_2|<Q_1$ forces $(d_2,d_3)=(0,1)$.  The middle residue congruence would
then be
\[
d_1+Q_2\equiv0\pmod {Q_3},
\]
which is impossible because
$0<Q_2-A\le d_1+Q_2\le Q_2+A<Q_3$.  The case $d_4=-A$ is symmetric: it
forces $(d_2,d_3)=(0,-1)$ and then the nonzero integer $d_1-Q_2$, of modulus
less than $Q_3$, would be divisible by $Q_3$.  Therefore all $d_i$ vanish,
and $\Phi_{A,3,3}$ is injective.

Now let $m\ge4$.  Suppose two raw blocks
$a_0\ldots a_{2m-2}$ and $b_0\ldots b_{2m-2}$ have the same $m$ output
residues, and again put $d_i=a_i-b_i$.  For $0\le t\le m-3$, take the
linear combination with coefficients $1,-A,-1$ of the residue congruences
at $t,t+1,t+2$.  All interior coefficients cancel by the recurrence, the
coefficient at $d_{t+m}$ is $-AQ_{m-1}-Q_{m-2}=-Q_m$, and the final
coefficient is $-Q_{m-1}$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metallic-large-m-difference}
d_t+d_{t+1}-Q_{m-1}d_{t+m+1}\equiv0\pmod {Q_m}.
\end{equation}
We claim that this forces $d_{t+m+1}=0$.  Indeed,
\[
Q_{m-2}\ge Q_2=A^2+A+1>2A.
\]
For $1\le d\le A$, the number $dQ_{m-1}$ lies strictly between $0$ and
$Q_m=AQ_{m-1}+Q_{m-2}$, and its distances from the two endpoints satisfy
\[
dQ_{m-1}\ge Q_{m-1}>2A,
\]
\[
Q_m-dQ_{m-1}=(A-d)Q_{m-1}+Q_{m-2}>2A.
\]
The same holds for negative $d$ by symmetry.  Since
$|d_t+d_{t+1}|\le2A$, congruence
\eqref{eq:metallic-large-m-difference} is therefore possible only when
$d_{t+m+1}=0$; it then also gives $d_t+d_{t+1}=0$.  Varying $t$ yields
\[
d_{m+1}=d_{m+2}=\cdots=d_{2m-2}=0.
\]

The last output-residue congruence now reduces to
\[
d_{m-1}+Q_1d_m\equiv0\pmod {Q_m}.
\]
Its left-hand side has modulus at most $A+AQ_1=A^2+2A<Q_m$, so it vanishes
as an integer.  Because $Q_1=A+1>|d_{m-1}|$, this forces
$d_{m-1}=d_m=0$.  Moving backwards through the residue congruences, at
stage $j=m-2,m-3,\ldots,0$ every term except $d_jQ_0=d_j$ is already zero.
Thus $d_j\equiv0\pmod {Q_m}$, and $|d_j|\le A<Q_m$ gives $d_j=0$.  This
proves injectivity of $\Phi_{A,m,m}$ for every $m\ge4$.

Finally let $n\ge m$.  If two raw blocks have the same length-$n$ output,
then every consecutive length-$m$ output subblock is equal.  The established
injectivity of $\Phi_{A,m,m}$ makes the corresponding raw subblocks of length
$2m-1$ equal.  These subblocks cover all $n+m-1$ raw coordinates, so the raw
blocks are equal.  Every raw finite block extends to a bi-infinite element of
the full shift $\mathcal A_A^{\ZZ}$; hence the image of
$\Phi_{A,m,n}$ is exactly $\mathcal L_n(Y_{A,m})$.  Counting the domain gives
\eqref{eq:metallic-block-count}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp metallic threshold classification]
\label{thm:metallic-threshold-classification}
Let $A\ge1$ be an integer and let
$\beta_A=(A+\sqrt{A^2+4})/2$.  Use exactly the alphabet, legal language,
rank, modulus and fold of Definition~\ref{def:metallic-window-data}, and
exclude the trivial aperture $m=1$ as in
Definition~\ref{def:metallic-conjugacy-threshold}.  Then
\[
\{m\ge2:\Phi_{A,m}\text{ is a topological conjugacy onto the SFT }
Y_{A,m}\}
=
\begin{cases}
\{3,4,5,\ldots\},&A=1,\\
\{2,3,4,\ldots\},&A\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metallic-threshold-function}
m_*(\beta_A)=
\begin{cases}
3,&A=1,\\
2,&A\ge2.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
In particular, the golden mean
$\beta_1=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ is the unique metallic quadratic Pisot base with
$m_*(\beta_A)=3$.  At every aperture in the displayed conjugacy set, the
inverse is a sliding-block code with memory $0$ and anticipation $m-1$, and
$Y_{A,m}$ is defined by finitely many forbidden words of length at most
$m+1$ over $X_m^{(A)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $(A,m)\ne(1,2)$ with $A\ge1$ and $m\ge2$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:metallic-family-block-bijection} says that every word
$W\in\mathcal L_m(Y_{A,m})$ has a unique raw preimage
\[
\Psi_{A,m,m}(W)\in\mathcal A_A^{2m-1}.
\]
Let $\psi_{A,m}(W)$ be the first symbol of this raw block.  If
$y=\Phi_{A,m}(a)$, then the output block
$(y_t,\ldots,y_{t+m-1})$ is the image of
$a_t\ldots a_{t+2m-2}$, so uniqueness gives
\[
a_t=\psi_{A,m}(y_t,\ldots,y_{t+m-1}).
\]
This is a memory-$0$, anticipation-$(m-1)$ continuous shift-commuting inverse
on $Y_{A,m}$.  Since $Y_{A,m}$ is the image by definition, it is a two-sided
inverse and $\Phi_{A,m}$ is a topological conjugacy.

For completeness, the image is of finite type by the same decoded-block
criterion.  A sequence $y\in(X_m^{(A)})^{\ZZ}$ belongs to $Y_{A,m}$ if and
only if every length-$m$ word
$W_t=(y_t,\ldots,y_{t+m-1})$ lies in $\mathcal L_m(Y_{A,m})$ and
\[
\Psi_{A,m,m}(W_t)_{1,\ldots,2m-2}
=\Psi_{A,m,m}(W_{t+1})_{0,\ldots,2m-3}
\quad(t\in\ZZ).
\]
Necessity follows from overlapping raw windows.  Conversely, these equalities
glue the decoded blocks into a unique $a\in\mathcal A_A^{\ZZ}$ and applying
$\Phi_{A,m,m}$ to each glued block gives $y=\Phi_{A,m}(a)$.  The criterion
depends only on output words of length $m+1$, so finitely many such words form
a forbidden list.

It remains only to identify the excluded pair.  When $A=1$, the data is
Fibonacci by Lemma~\ref{lem:metallic-golden-member}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:m2-branch-locus} gives
\[
\Phi_{1,2}^{-1}((\ldots,00,00,\ldots))=\{0^{\ZZ},1^{\ZZ}\}.
\]
Thus $\Phi_{1,2}$ is not injective.  Every other pair $(A,m)$ is conjugate by
the preceding argument.  The two alternatives exhaust all $A\ge1$ and
$m\ge2$, proving the conjugacy sets, the threshold formula, and golden
uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Entropy and dynamical-zeta certificate for golden extremality]
\label{thm:metallic-entropy-zeta-extremality}
Under the hypotheses and notation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:metallic-threshold-classification}, for every $m\ge2$,
\[
h_{\rm top}(Y_{A,m})=\log(A+1).
\]
For every $n\ge1$, the periodic-point counts and Artin--Mazur zeta functions
are
\[
\#\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n|_{Y_{A,m}})
=
\begin{cases}
2^n-1,&(A,m)=(1,2),\\
(A+1)^n,&(A,m)\ne(1,2),
\end{cases}
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metallic-zeta-classification}
\zeta_{Y_{A,m}}(z)
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1-z}{1-2z},&(A,m)=(1,2),\\[6pt]
\dfrac{1}{1-(A+1)z},&(A,m)\ne(1,2).
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
In particular, the entropy-normalized two-window zeta defect
\[
\mathcal D_A(z):=(1-(A+1)z)\zeta_{Y_{A,2}}(z)
\]
satisfies
\[
\mathcal D_A(z)=
\begin{cases}
1-z,&A=1,\\
1,&A\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus entropy records the full-shift exponential growth but does not see the
golden two-window branch, whereas the zeta numerator detects exactly the
unique parameter with $m_*(\beta_A)=3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $(A,m)\ne(1,2)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:metallic-threshold-classification}
gives a topological conjugacy with the full shift on $A+1$ symbols.  Entropy,
periodic-point counts and the Artin--Mazur zeta function are conjugacy
invariants, so in this case they are
\[
\log(A+1),\qquad (A+1)^n,\qquad
\exp\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(A+1)^nz^n}{n}\right)
=\frac1{1-(A+1)z}.
\]

Consider $(A,m)=(1,2)$.  Let $\mathbf0$ denote the constant output point
$(\ldots,00,00,\ldots)$.  If $y\in Y_{1,2}$ satisfies $\sigma^ny=y$ and
$y\ne\mathbf0$, choose a lift $a$.  Then
\[
\Phi_{1,2}(\sigma^na)=\sigma^ny=y=\Phi_{1,2}(a).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:m2-branch-locus} says that the fiber of $y$ is a singleton,
so $\sigma^na=a$.  The fiber of $\mathbf0$ consists exactly of the two fixed
points $0^{\ZZ}$ and $1^{\ZZ}$.  Hence the map from the $2^n$ points fixed by
$\sigma^n$ in the full two-shift onto
$\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n|_{Y_{1,2}})$ identifies precisely these two
constant points and nothing else.  Therefore the image has $2^n-1$ fixed
points.  It follows that
\[
\zeta_{Y_{1,2}}(z)
=\exp\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(2^n-1)z^n}{n}\right)
=\frac{1-z}{1-2z}.
\]
The factor inequality gives $h_{\rm top}(Y_{1,2})\le\log2$, while the
$2^n-1$ distinct $n$-periodic points give
\[
h_{\rm top}(Y_{1,2})\ge
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log(2^n-1)=\log2.
\]
This proves the entropy identity in the remaining case.  Multiplication of
the two-window zeta formulas by $1-(A+1)z$ gives the stated defect.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Fibonacci image as a Fischer cover}

The overlap graph $\mathcal G_m$ gives a concrete presentation of $Y_m$. Above
threshold it is the canonical minimal right-resolving presentation of $Y_m$
in the stabilized output alphabet.

We use the following standard characterization of the right Fischer cover of
an irreducible synchronized sofic shift: the right Fischer cover is the
canonical right-resolving graph whose vertices are the follower sets of
intrinsically synchronizing words, with transitions
\[
F_X(w)\xrightarrow{a}F_X(wa)
\quad\text{whenever }wa\in\mathcal L(X).
\]
It is follower-separated and is minimal among essential irreducible
right-resolving presentations. This is the Fischer--Krieger cover
construction in its right-resolving form; see
\cite{Fischer1975,Krieger1984,Krieger1987,LindMarcus1995,Kitchens1998,BoyleKitchensMarcus1985,Johansen2011}.
The lemma below is the concrete criterion needed for the overlap graph.

For a vertex $u\in V_m=\{0,1\}^{m-1}$, let
\[
\mathcal F^+(u):=
\{w\in X_m^*:\text{ there exists a path in }\mathcal G_m
\text{ beginning at }u\text{ with label }w\}
\]
be its right follower language.

\begin{lemma}[Fischer-cover criterion]\label{lem:fischer-cover-criterion}
Let $X$ be an irreducible sofic shift and let $G$ be an essential irreducible
right-resolving presentation of $X$. Assume in addition that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item $G$ is follower-separated, meaning distinct vertices have distinct
  right follower languages in $X$;
  \item every vertex $v$ of $G$ is the terminal vertex of a path whose label
  $w_v$ is intrinsically synchronizing for $X$ and labels a unique path in
  $G$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $G$ is the right Fischer cover of $X$. In particular, every essential
irreducible right-resolving presentation of $X$ has at least as many vertices
as $G$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the standard Fischer-cover theorem for irreducible sofic shifts:
among essential irreducible right-resolving presentations, the unique
follower-separated presentation in which each state is reached by an
intrinsically synchronizing word is the right Fischer cover; equivalently it
is the canonical graph of synchronizing follower sets. See
\cite[Theorem~3.3.18]{LindMarcus1995} and the Fischer--Krieger cover
references cited above.

The unique-path clause in (ii) identifies the terminal vertex reached by
$w_v$ with the canonical follower-set vertex $F_X(w_v)$, while
follower-separatedness prevents two vertices of $G$ from representing the
same follower set. Right-resolvingness gives the canonical transition rule on
these follower sets. Hence the standard theorem identifies $G$ with the right
Fischer cover. Its minimality among essential irreducible right-resolving
presentations gives the stated state lower bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fischer cover of the stabilized image]
\label{thm:fischer-cover-stabilized-image}
Assume $m\ge3$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item Every word $w\in\mathcal L_m(Y_m)$ labels exactly one path of length
  $m$ in the overlap graph $\mathcal G_m$. Consequently every word in
  $\mathcal L_m(Y_m)$ is intrinsically synchronizing for $Y_m$.
  \item The follower languages $\mathcal F^+(u)$, $u\in V_m$, are pairwise
  distinct.  Hence $\mathcal G_m$ is follower-separated.
  \item The labeled graph $\mathcal G_m$ is the right Fischer cover of $Y_m$.
  In particular, every essential irreducible right-resolving presentation of
  $Y_m$ has at least $2^{m-1}$ vertices.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A path of length $m$ in $\mathcal G_m$ is exactly a raw binary block
\[
b_0b_1\cdots b_{2m-2}\in\{0,1\}^{2m-1},
\]
and its label is
\[
\Phi_{m,m}(b_0b_1\cdots b_{2m-2}).
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:block-invertibility}, the map $\Phi_{m,m}$ is injective.
Thus each legal word $w\in\mathcal L_m(Y_m)$ has a unique preimage raw
block and hence labels exactly one path in $\mathcal G_m$.

The same uniqueness gives intrinsic synchronization. If $uw$ and $wv$ are
admissible words in $Y_m$, then in any path labeled by $uw$ the subpath labeled
by $w$ is the unique path labeled by $w$, and similarly for any path labeled by
$wv$.  The first path therefore reaches the terminal vertex of this unique
$w$-path, and the second path leaves that same terminal vertex after reading
$w$.  Concatenation gives an admissible path labeled by $uwv$.  Hence $w$ is
intrinsically synchronizing.  This proves (i).

Let $u,v\in V_m$ with $u\ne v$.  Choose any path of length $m$ beginning at
$u$, and let $w$ be its label.  If $w$ also belonged to $\mathcal F^+(v)$, then
$w$ would label a second length-$m$ path, now beginning at $v$.  This contradicts
(i), since the initial vertices are different.  Thus
$\mathcal F^+(u)\ne\mathcal F^+(v)$, proving (ii).

It remains to verify the hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fischer-cover-criterion}. The graph $\mathcal G_m$ is finite by
definition. The underlying de Bruijn graph on
$\{0,1\}^{m-1}$ is essential: every vertex
$u=(u_1,\ldots,u_{m-1})$ lies on a bi-infinite path, obtained for example by
using the periodic raw sequence with period $u$ when $u$ is nonempty. It is
irreducible because, from any vertex $u$ to any vertex $v$, one follows the
raw word obtained by appending the symbols of $v$ after $u$, which gives a
path whose terminal vertex is $v$. Hence the presented shift $Y_m$ is
irreducible and $\mathcal G_m$ is an essential irreducible presentation.
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-state-lift} proves that the labeling is
right-resolving, so $\mathcal G_m$ is an essential irreducible
right-resolving presentation of $Y_m$. Statement (ii) above proves the
additional follower-separation hypothesis.

Finally, every vertex is the terminal vertex of some length-$m$ path: append
the symbols of that vertex after any initial bit. By (i), the label of every
length-$m$ path labels a unique path and is intrinsically synchronizing. Thus
every vertex is reached by a unique-path intrinsically synchronizing word,
verifying the remaining synchronizing-word hypothesis.
Lemma~\ref{lem:fischer-cover-criterion} therefore identifies $\mathcal G_m$
with the right Fischer cover of $Y_m$ and gives the lower bound for all
essential irreducible right-resolving presentations. The vertex set has
cardinality $|V_m|=2^{m-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Alphabet dependence of the state count]
The $2^{m-1}$ states are forced among essential irreducible right-resolving
presentations of the particular labeled shift $Y_m\subset X_m^{\mathbb Z}$.
This is not a conjugacy-invariant complexity measure.  Indeed
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-sided-conjugacy} conjugates $Y_m$ to the binary full
shift, whose usual Fischer cover has one state.  The state conclusion is
therefore canonical relative to the stabilized output alphabet and its
language, not under arbitrary recoding of the dynamical system.
\end{remark}
