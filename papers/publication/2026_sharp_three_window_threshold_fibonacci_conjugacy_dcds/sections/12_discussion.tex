\section{Concluding remarks}\label{sec:discussion}

The central conclusion is a fixed-system complexity theorem on a standard
numeration class.  For every strictly increasing zero-preserving Pisot
numeration $U$, including systems with nonstandard initial values, Theorem
\ref{thm:pisot-linear-depth-sharpness} gives an effective constant $C_U$ and
the uniform implication
\[
\Phi_{U,m}\text{ injective}\Longrightarrow
\ell_{\rm cau}(U,m)\le C_Um.
\]
The constant comes from the recurrence, canonical digit set, bounded-zero
normalization automaton, and finitely many exact small-aperture searches.
Its large-aperture mechanism is Pisot contraction: adjacent collision-window
carries collapse before any exponential difference-state count is used.
Corollary~\ref{cor:pisot-zero-strip-synchronization} states the same global
dichotomy without the fold: either some overlapping bounded-zero strip pumps
periodically, or every nonzero strip has $O_U(m)$ rows.

This order cannot be reduced to $o(m)$.  In the single fixed binary cubic
system $U_\theta$ associated with $x^3-2x^2+x-1$, Theorem
\ref{thm:pisot-linear-depth-sharpness} proves
\[
\ell_{\rm cau}(\theta,m)=2\lfloor m/2\rfloor-1\qquad(m\ge4)
\]
and classifies the terminal obstruction set as exactly $\{\pm E_m\}$.
Thus the fixed-$\theta$ formula is a matching lower bound for the standard
Pisot theorem, rather than an isolated exact rank-fold example.

The first conclusion is a complete finite-type classification for the cyclic
fixed-length recodings attached to quadratic Pisot bases.  Theorem
\ref{thm:full-quadratic-pisot-threshold} proves that the nontrivial threshold
is always two or three, and identifies exactly the two families attaining
three.  Above threshold the classification is not merely asymptotic: the
same theorem gives the exact onset of the block bijection, Theorem
\ref{thm:fischer-cover-stabilized-image}
identifies the right Fischer cover, and the corresponding Markov order is
sharp.  In particular,
\[
 |\mathcal L_n(Y_{\beta,m})|
 =(\lfloor\beta\rfloor+1)^{n+m-1}
 \qquad(n\ge m\ge m_*(\beta)).
\]
The canonical Parry--Bertrand rank used to define the recoding belongs to
the established numeration theory.  The new step is its cyclic reduction at
fixed word length and the parameter-uniform overlap analysis that yields
these exact dynamical invariants.

The second conclusion concerns the simple-Parry arithmetic difference graph.
Pair graphs, fiber products, directed-cycle tests for injectivity, and finite
inverse-window decisions are classical tools for one-dimensional local codes
and tessellation automata.  Theorem
\ref{thm:simple-parry-causal-collision} applies that decision after passing
to a coordinatewise-difference quotient adapted to the Parry recurrence.
What is specific here is the proof that this quotient captures every
collision, the longest-path formula for the exact memory-zero inverse
anticipation, the explicit bounds, and the unique-zero-predecessor mechanism
that turns the classical decision into a future-only inverse in this setting.
Corollary~\ref{cor:simple-parry-causal-completeness} therefore leaves no
intermediate regime between a finite future-only inverse and a bounded
periodic collision certificate.  The aperture-two classification in
Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-parry-aperture-two} and the quadratic aperture
trichotomy give the promised special compression and dichotomy.

The remaining cubic conclusion is that algebraic degree alone does not bound
this decoding depth across varying systems.  For Bassino's family
\[
 x^3-(n+2)x^2+2nx-n,
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-unbounded-causal-depth} proves exact memory-zero
inverse anticipation $n-2$, equivalently future-only inverse length $n-1$, at aperture
$n-1$.  Thus the depth is unbounded already among degree-three simple-Parry
systems and injective apertures.  The point is the exact sliding collision
depth for this established family, not the existence of its Parry expansion.

The distinction from numerical normalization remains explicit.  The cyclic
language-rank construction does not identify numerical $\beta$-values, and
it does not treat signed or redundant alphabets, non-greedy transformations,
higher-degree non-simple-Parry bases, or general Pisot substitutions; these
interfaces are recorded in Remark~\ref{rem:quadratic-open-interface}.  In the
quadratic negative-conjugate chamber, aperture three has exact future-only inverse length
two, whereas for $m\ge4$ the present bounds are
$2\le\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,m)\le m$.  Remark
\ref{rem:negative-two-window-open-interface} isolates the additional
parameter-uniform carry classification needed to close that narrower gap.

The entropy, Blackwell, pressure, fiber, discrepancy, bounded-multiple, and
zeta calculations are supplementary consequences.
They provide comparisons and invariant checks, but none enters the
separation estimate, the Fischer-cover argument, the simple-Parry
two-window exclusion, or the cubic exhaustion.  The main proof chain is the
fixed-base Pisot linear theorem and sharpness, the quadratic dynamical
classification, and the exact simple-Parry arithmetic quotient calculations
above.
