\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Finite-window recodings may identify words when a single window is observed
and nevertheless become injective when consecutive windows overlap.  We
determine the exact overlap threshold for a cyclic rank recoding attached to
every quadratic Pisot $\beta$-language, and we prove that its optimal causal
inverse length is unbounded already among cubic simple-Parry Pisot bases.

The construction is an ordered-language construction.  For each length $m$,
we reverse the legal greedy $\beta$-words, order them lexicographically (or,
equivalently, order the unreversed words colexicographically), and identify
them with the integer interval $\{0,\ldots,Q_m(\beta)-1\}$.  The same weighted
rank formula is evaluated on the full digit cube and reduced cyclically modulo
$Q_m(\beta)$.  The residue is then returned to the corresponding legal word.
We call this operation a \emph{cyclic language-rank recoding}.

This terminology is essential.  The congruence is a congruence of integer
language ranks, not of numerical $\beta$-values.  In particular, the paper
does not construct a quotient of $\mathbb Z[\beta]$ and does not prove that an
input word and its recoded word represent the same real number.  For example,
if $\beta^2-3\beta+1=0$, then $(Q_0,Q_1,Q_2)=(1,3,8)$ and the rank of $(2,2)$
is $2+2Q_1=8$.  Thus the cyclic rank recoding sends $(2,2)$ to $(0,0)$, but
this says only that the two language ranks are congruent modulo $8$; it does
not assert $2+2\beta\equiv0$ in any arithmetic quotient.  Standard
$\beta$-normalization and finite-transducer results therefore provide
important context but concern a different operation
\cite{Frougny1992,FrougnySolomyak1992,BerendFrougny1994,FrougnySteiner2008}.

Every quadratic Pisot minimal polynomial belongs to one of the two chambers
\[
P^-_{a,b}(x)=x^2-ax-b,\quad a\ge b\ge1,
\qquad
P^+_{a,b}(x)=x^2-ax+b,\quad a\ge3,\ 1\le b\le a-2.
\]
For the cyclic rank recoding $\Phi_{\beta,m}$, let $m_*(\beta)$ be the least
nontrivial aperture $m\ge2$ for which the sliding map is a topological
conjugacy from the full digit shift onto its finite-type image.  Our main
result, Theorem~\ref{thm:full-quadratic-pisot-threshold}, is
\[
m_*(\beta)=3
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{minpoly}(\beta)\in
\{x^2-ax-a:a\ge1\}\cup\{x^2-ax+1:a\ge3\},
\]
and $m_*(\beta)=2$ otherwise.  At every aperture at or above threshold, the
finite-block maps are injective and
\[
|\mathcal L_n(Y_{\beta,m})|
=(\lfloor\beta\rfloor+1)^{n+m-1}\qquad(n\ge m).
\]
The inverse has memory zero and finite anticipation.

The threshold alone does not describe the local information content of the
recoding.  Theorem~\ref{thm:quadratic-aperture-two-duality} shows that
aperture two identifies a positive- and a negative-conjugate chamber member
pointwise, while aperture three first separates them.
Theorem~\ref{thm:quadratic-optimal-causal-inverse} gives the exact
anticipation two in the positive chamber at every $m\ge3$ and anticipation
one in the negative chamber at $m=3$.  The corresponding parameter-uniform
negative statement for $m\ge4$ remains open.  The critical extremizers all
have the same
two-fixed-point strictly sofic normal form, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:quadratic-exact-local-order} proves that both the onset of
finite-block injectivity and the Markov step are exactly $m$.

The quadratic threshold bound does not imply a uniform bound on decoding
depth in higher degree.  In
Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-parry-causal-collision} we identify the controlling
finite object: a bounded-difference collision graph, equivalently a banded
Toeplitz system of congruences modulo $Q_m$.  Corollary
\ref{cor:simple-parry-causal-completeness} shows that every injective
simple-Parry rank fold has a finite future-only inverse and that every
failure of injectivity has a bounded periodic witness.  Theorem
\ref{thm:cubic-unbounded-causal-depth} then proves that no degree-three
uniform bound exists.  For the root $\beta_n\in(n,n+1)$ of
\[
x^3-(n+2)x^2+2nx-n
\]
at aperture $m_n=n-1$, the fold is injective and its optimal causal inverse
uses exactly $n-1$ consecutive outputs.  Bassino's cubic expansion
classification supplies the published simple-Parry data
\cite{Bassino2002}; the new step is a parameter-uniform carry exclusion and
an exact terminal-path induction in the modulus-$Q_m$ collision graph.
Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-parry-aperture-two} gives a complete aperture-two
trichotomy from the second Parry digit, including the exact constant branch
pair in the noninjective case.

The negative norm-one subfamily is the boundary-modified constant-type
recurrence model
\[
\beta_A=\frac{A+\sqrt{A^2+4}}2,\qquad
Q_0=1,\quad Q_1=A+1,\quad Q_{j+2}=AQ_{j+1}+Q_j.
\]
Its digit strings obey the Ostrowski local constraint
$x_k=A\Rightarrow x_{k-1}=0$.  The model is not identified with standard
Ostrowski numeration: its weights are $q_j+q_{j-1}$ rather than the convergent
denominators $q_j$, and its boundary convention permits the lowest digit to
equal $A$; see Lemma~\ref{lem:constant-type-ostrowski-origin}.  In this
subfamily,
\[
m_*(\beta_A)=3\quad(A=1),
\qquad
m_*(\beta_A)=2\quad(A\ge2).
\]

At $A=1$ the ranks are the Fibonacci ranks of binary words with no adjacent
ones.  The three-window decoder is explicit, while at aperture two the two
constant raw sequences form the sole nontrivial path-space fiber.  The natural
overlap graph above threshold is also identified with the right Fischer cover
of the image shift in its presented output alphabet.  The resulting
$2^{m-1}$ state count is canonical for that labeled shift presentation; it is
not invariant under arbitrary topological conjugacy, since the same image
shift is conjugate to the binary full shift.

The closest general framework is ordered numeration on languages.
Lecomte--Rigo assign integers by genealogically ordering an arbitrary
infinite regular language, and Bruy\`ere--Hansel study recognizability in
Bertrand numeration systems
\cite{BruyereHansel1997,LecomteRigo2001}.  Bertrand--Mathis and Loraud connect
the admissible languages with $\beta$-shifts
\cite{BertrandMathis1986,Loraud1995}.
Classical $\beta$-expansion and $\beta$-shift results establish the greedy
language, its dynamical realization, and automata-theoretic properties
\cite{Renyi1957,ItoTakahashi1974,Parry1960,Blanchard1989,FrougnySolomyak1992}.
These works already supply the ordered-language, recognizability, and
admissibility stages.  The additional operation studied here is only the
cyclic reduction of the fixed-length rank modulo the number of length-$m$
legal words, followed by sliding overlap; we have not found this threshold
problem in the cited frameworks.

Section~\ref{sec:stabilization} develops the finite Fibonacci rank recoding.
Sections~\ref{sec:stab:conjugacy} and the following branch-locus section prove
the sharp Fibonacci decoder and the two-window exception.
Section~\ref{sec:metallic-cover} treats the boundary-modified constant-type
family and its Fischer cover.  Section~\ref{sec:quadratic-pisot-extension}
proves the two-chamber theorem and gives four worked nonmetallic examples.
Section~\ref{sec:simple-parry-causal-obstructions} proves the higher-degree
causal obstruction formula, the unbounded cubic theorem, and the
causal-completeness and aperture-two classifications.  The bounded-multiple
counterexamples are retained there as secondary consequences.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} records the scope and remaining interfaces.
Routine source-law, entropy, Blackwell-equivalence, pressure, fiber-moment,
discrepancy, and geometric consequences have been moved to the separate
supplementary material; none is used in the threshold proofs.
