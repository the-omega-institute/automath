\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Finite-window factor codes can collapse individual words and nevertheless
become invertible when successive windows overlap.  For subshifts generated
by $\beta$-expansions, this raises a concrete classification problem: how many
overlapping cyclic rank windows are needed before the full digit shift is
recovered, and how much future output is required by an inverse with memory
zero?  We answer the threshold problem for every quadratic Pisot base and
show that the optimal future depth is already unbounded in algebraic degree
three.  The resulting statements concern finite-type images, Fischer covers,
follower separation, and exact Markov order, which are the natural symbolic
dynamical invariants of the construction.

Fix a greedy $\beta$-language and let $Q_m(\beta)$ be the number of its legal
words of length $m$.  Reverse those words, order them lexicographically, and
identify them with $\{0,\ldots,Q_m(\beta)-1\}$.  Equivalently, order the
unreversed words colexicographically.  Evaluate the same positional rank on
the full digit cube, reduce it modulo $Q_m(\beta)$, return the legal word with
that residue, and overlap the resulting windows along the shift.  We call
this map a \emph{cyclic language-rank recoding}.

The positional rank is established numeration theory.  Bertrand--Mathis,
Bruy\`ere--Hansel, and Lecomte--Rigo develop nonintegral-base and
ordered-language numeration \cite{BertrandMathis1989,BruyereHansel1997,
LecomteRigo2001}; Parry's admissibility theorem supplies the canonical greedy
language \cite{Parry1960}.  Charlier--Cisternino--Stipulanti correct the
classification of Bertrand systems and distinguish, for a simple-Parry base,
the canonical system determined by $d_\beta^*(1)$ from the noncanonical
system generated directly by the finite word $d_\beta(1)$
\cite{CharlierCisterninoStipulanti2022}.  We always use the canonical
language.  Proposition~\ref{prop:parry-rank-bijection} gives a self-contained
two-chamber interval proof of this standard positional rank because its exact
endpoints enter the later estimates.  The map-specific operation begins only
with cyclic reduction of the fixed-length rank and sliding overlap.

This distinction also separates the construction from numerical
$\beta$-normalization.  The congruence is a congruence of integer language
ranks, not of values in $\mathbb Z[\beta]$.  For instance, if
$\beta^2-3\beta+1=0$, then $(Q_0,Q_1,Q_2)=(1,3,8)$ and the rank of $(2,2)$ is
$2+2Q_1=8$.  Its cyclic recoding is therefore $(0,0)$, but no equality
$2+2\beta=0$ is asserted.  Finite-transducer normalization results provide
important context while studying a different map
\cite{Frougny1992,FrougnySolomyak1992,BerendFrougny1994,FrougnySteiner2008}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Quadratic classification.}
Every quadratic Pisot minimal polynomial belongs to one of two chambers,
\[
P^-_{a,b}(x)=x^2-ax-b,\quad a\ge b\ge1,
\qquad
P^+_{a,b}(x)=x^2-ax+b,\quad a\ge3,\quad 1\le b\le a-2.
\]
Let $m_*(\beta)$ be the least aperture $m\ge2$ for which the sliding cyclic
rank recoding is a topological conjugacy from the full digit shift onto its
finite-type image.  Theorem~\ref{thm:full-quadratic-pisot-threshold} gives the
complete classification
\[
m_*(\beta)=3
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{minpoly}(\beta)\in
\{x^2-ax-a:a\ge1\}\cup\{x^2-ax+1:a\ge3\},
\]
with $m_*(\beta)=2$ otherwise.  To the authors' knowledge, the exact
threshold for this cyclic fixed-length language-rank recoding has not
previously been determined.  The claim is deliberately restricted to this
map and is not a claim about $\beta$-normalization in general.

At every aperture at or above threshold,
\[
|\mathcal L_N(Y_{\beta,m})|
=(\lfloor\beta\rfloor+1)^{N+m-1}\qquad(N\ge m).
\]
The proof rests on a parameter-uniform nearest-multiple separation in both
quadratic chambers and the resulting bounded sliding-congruence lemma.  It
also identifies the exact extremal bases rather than only producing an upper
bound for the aperture.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Exact local structure.}
The threshold has a sharp finite-block and presentation-theoretic form.
Theorem~\ref{thm:quadratic-exact-local-order} proves simultaneously that
finite-block injectivity first occurs at block length $N=m$, that the natural
overlap presentation is the right Fischer cover with exactly
$(\lfloor\beta\rfloor+1)^{m-1}$ states, and that the image in its fixed output
alphabet is $m$-step but not $(m-1)$-step.  These conclusions use standard
Fischer--Krieger and resolving-cover theory
\cite{Fischer1975,Krieger1984,LindMarcus1995}; the new content is the exact
identification and order for this arithmetic family.  Theorem
\ref{thm:quadratic-aperture-two-duality} gives the associated cross-chamber
compression at aperture two, and Theorem
\ref{thm:quadratic-critical-normal-form} identifies the unique two-point
fiber and its strictly sofic normal form at the critical parameters.

The Fibonacci and boundary-modified metallic cases provide the prototype.
At the golden base the three-window decoder is explicit, while at aperture
two the two constant raw sequences form the sole nontrivial path-space fiber.
For the constant-type family
\[
\beta_A=\frac{A+\sqrt{A^2+4}}2,
\qquad Q_{j+2}=AQ_{j+1}+Q_j,
\]
the threshold is three only for $A=1$ and is two for every $A\ge2$.  The
weights and boundary convention differ from standard Ostrowski numeration;
that relationship is recorded without identifying the two systems.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Degree-three unboundedness.}
For an injective recoding define $\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta,m)-1$ to be the least
anticipation of an inverse sliding-block code with memory zero.  This is a
directional optimum, not a generic name for right-closing delay.  For every
$n\ge4$, let $\beta_n\in(n,n+1)$ be the real root of
\[
x^3-(n+2)x^2+2nx-n
\]
and put $m_n=n-1$.  Bassino established this cubic simple-Parry Pisot family
and its expansion of one \cite{Bassino2002}.  Theorem
\ref{thm:cubic-unbounded-causal-depth} proves the new map-specific assertion
\[
\ell_{\rm cau}(\beta_n,m_n)=n-1.
\]
Thus exact memory-zero inverse anticipation is unbounded already within
algebraic degree three.  A parameter-uniform two-window carry exclusion and
an exhaustive terminal-path induction provide the lower and upper bounds.

The proof uses a classical decision in a specialized form.  Pair graphs,
bundle graphs, and fiber products encode pairs of input paths with the same
output; reachable off-diagonal cycles decide noninjectivity and yield
periodic witnesses.  This tradition includes tessellation automata,
one-dimensional cellular automata, and symbolic factor maps
\cite{AmorosoPatt1972,Richardson1972,Nasu1977,Head1989,LindMarcus1995,
Nasu1995}.  Finite decoder-window bounds and resolving maps are likewise
classical \cite{Ashley1988,Ashley1991,Ashley1996}.  Theorem
\ref{thm:simple-parry-causal-collision} applies that decision after an exact
arithmetic quotient: an input pair is replaced by its coordinatewise
difference in $[-d,d]^{m-1}$.  For cyclic Parry ranks this quotient retains
all collisions, computes $\ell_{\rm cau}$ by a longest-path formula, and has
an explicit state bound.  Corollary
\ref{cor:simple-parry-causal-completeness} uses the unique predecessor of the
zero difference state to strengthen the general inverse conclusion to a
future-only inverse.  Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-parry-aperture-two} then gives
the exact aperture-two trichotomy.  These arithmetic quotient and depth
computations are the contribution; the pair-graph/cycle decision itself is
not.

The three headline results therefore form a single dynamical narrative.  The
quadratic theorem classifies every Pisot base in degree two and fixes its
finite-type onset and canonical right-resolving presentation.  The
simple-Parry difference quotient isolates the arithmetic responsible for
future decoding.  Bassino's cubic family then shows that the uniform
quadratic behavior breaks immediately in degree three.

Section~\ref{sec:stabilization} defines the Fibonacci rank recoding, and the
following sections establish its decoder and critical branch locus.  Section
\ref{sec:metallic-cover} treats the metallic prototype.  Section
\ref{sec:quadratic-pisot-extension} proves the full quadratic classification
and exact local structure.  Section
\ref{sec:simple-parry-causal-obstructions} first separates the classical
pair-graph input from the arithmetic difference quotient and then proves the
cubic unbounded-depth theorem.  Section~\ref{sec:discussion} records the
remaining map-specific interfaces.  Entropy, Blackwell-equivalence,
pressure, Fourier fiber statistics, discrepancy, and geometric consequences
are kept outside the main proof chain in the supplementary material.
