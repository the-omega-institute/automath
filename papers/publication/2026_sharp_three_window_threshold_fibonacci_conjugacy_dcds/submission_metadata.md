Abstract:
We study the finite-window Fibonacci normalization which sends a binary word of length m to the unique golden-mean admissible word with the same Fibonacci valuation modulo F_{m+2}. The map is usually many-to-one on a single window, but its sliding overlap extension has a sharp threshold. We prove that for every m >= 3 it is a finite-memory conjugacy from the full two-shift onto a subshift of finite type, with an explicit decoder. For m = 2 the only nontrivial path-space fiber is the pair of constant sequences; this yields exact conditional-entropy and Kullback-Leibler defect formulas and a mod-zero inverse off the branch. The proof combines a bi-resolving overlap presentation with a residue decoder, so the degree-one conclusion is an arithmetic fact rather than a formal consequence of resolving theory. Within the constant-type metallic model of Definition 5.1, we also show that the golden case is exceptional: every integer parameter A >= 2 is already invertible at window length two. Above threshold the overlap presentation is identified with the right Fischer cover. Further residue, Fourier, second-moment and Sturmian discrepancy estimates record the associated finite-window statistics.
