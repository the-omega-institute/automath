\opening{Dear Editors,}

We submit our manuscript entitled

\medskip
\centerline{\textbf{A sharp three-window threshold and finite-memory}}
\centerline{\textbf{conjugacy in Fibonacci stabilization}}
\medskip

\noindent for consideration in \textit{Discrete and Continuous Dynamical Systems - Series A} (DCDS-A).

The paper proves a sharp overlap threshold for the canonical Fibonacci stabilization map $\mathrm{Fold}_m:\{0,1\}^m\to X_m$, which sends a binary word to the unique admissible Fibonacci word with the same valuation modulo $F_{m+2}$. The main contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item A sharp three-window threshold: for every $m\ge 3$, the sliding stabilized-window map is injective and admits an explicit finite-memory inverse, identifying the image with a subshift of finite type topologically conjugate to the full two-shift. The constructive decoder at $m=3$ is given by explicit modular arithmetic.

\item A complete classification of the exceptional case $m=2$: injectivity fails on exactly one branch orbit, with exact conditional entropy and Kullback--Leibler defect formulas. Off this branch set the map is a measure-theoretic isomorphism.

\item Above threshold, the conjugacy transports invariant measures, continuous potentials, pressure, and equilibrium states exactly. Finite-range Hamiltonians and block-level information geometry are preserved by the local inverse.

\item In the Sturmian benchmark, quantitative discrepancy bounds control stabilized block laws and the recovery of finite-range observables, with explicit error terms involving the star discrepancy and boundary-layer mass.
\end{itemize}

We believe the manuscript fits the scope of \textit{Discrete and Continuous Dynamical Systems - Series A} (DCDS-A) because it is a structural symbolic-dynamics paper with explicit arithmetic content. The main theorem gives a sharp three-window threshold for a sliding-block factor, proves finite-memory conjugacy onto a subshift of finite type, identifies the overlap presentation as the Fischer cover, and explains the exceptional two-window golden branch by comparison with metallic systems. The residue decoder, branch-locus classification, and Fischer-cover minimality are specific to this Fibonacci normalization and do not follow from general sofic-shift frameworks alone.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission. The authors declare no competing interests.
