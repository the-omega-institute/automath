\section{Unconditional extremal fibers and the real-tilt interface}
\label{sec:extremal-ldp}

The affine coefficient model gives one description of the fiber spectrum.  For
extremal questions there is a second, particularly rigid description: the
fibers are consecutive values of the ordinary Fibonacci partition function on
two explicit intervals.  This removes the recurrence assumptions that are
sometimes imposed in experimental treatments of the largest fibers.

\subsection{An exact interval model}

\begin{theorem}[Fiber--partition interval identity]
\label{thm:fiber-partition-interval}
Let \(m\ge0\) and \(0\le r<F_{m+2}\) be integers.  Then
\[
 d_m^\#(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 R(F_{m+2}+r),&0\le r\le F_{m+1}-2,\\
 R(r),&F_{m+1}-1\le r<F_{m+2}.
 \end{cases}
\]
An interval with lower endpoint larger than its upper endpoint is understood
to be empty.  In particular, this convention covers \(m=0,1\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The raw value \(N_m(\omega)\) is a sum of a subset of
\(F_2,F_3,\ldots,F_{m+1}\).  Since the single unit weight is \(F_2\), the
number of words with raw value \(n<F_{m+2}\) is exactly \(R(n)\).  By
\Cref{prop:single-overflow}, a word in the fiber over residue \(r\) has raw
value either \(r\) or \(F_{m+2}+r\).

Suppose first that \(0\le r\le F_{m+1}-2\).  Then
\[
 F_{m+2}+r\le F_{m+3}-2<F_{m+3}.
\]
Consequently every ordinary Fibonacci partition of \(F_{m+2}+r\) uses only
parts from \(F_2,\ldots,F_{m+2}\).  Those using \(F_{m+2}\) are in bijection,
by deletion of that part, with the \(R(r)\) partitions of \(r\).  Those not
using \(F_{m+2}\) are precisely the raw words of value \(F_{m+2}+r\).  The
fiber also contains the \(R(r)\) raw words of value \(r\).  Therefore the two
copies of \(R(r)\) cancel between this decomposition and the fiber sum, giving
\[
 d_m^\#(r)
 =R(r)+\bigl(R(F_{m+2}+r)-R(r)\bigr)
 =R(F_{m+2}+r).
\]

Now suppose that \(F_{m+1}-1\le r<F_{m+2}\).  The largest possible raw value is
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m F_{j+1}=F_{m+3}-2,
\]
whereas
\[
 F_{m+2}+r\ge F_{m+3}-1.
\]
Thus no word in this fiber can have the overflow value \(F_{m+2}+r\).  Every
partition of \(r<F_{m+2}\) uses only \(F_2,\ldots,F_{m+1}\), so the remaining
raw words are counted by \(R(r)\).  This proves the second case.
\end{proof}

For a compact statement of the maximizing residues, put
\[
 G_j:=F_{j+1}\qquad(j\ge0),
\]
so \(G_0=G_1=1\) and \(G_{j+1}=G_j+G_{j-1}\).  For \(k\ge5\), define
\[
 I_{2k+1}:=
 \begin{cases}
 G_{k+1}G_{k-3}+1,&k\text{ even},\\
 G_kG_{k-2}+1,&k\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]
For \(k\ge7\), define
\[
 I_{2k}:=
\begin{cases}
 G_{k+2}G_{k-5}+G_3+1,&k\text{ even},\\
 G_{k+1}G_{k-4}+G_3+1,&k\text{ odd},
 \end{cases}
\]
\[
 J_{2k}:=
 \begin{cases}
 G_{k+1}G_{k-3}+1,&k\text{ even},\\
 G_kG_{k-2}+1,&k\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact largest fibers]
\label{thm:unconditional-extremal-fibers}
Let \(D_m=\max_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)\).  Then
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 D_0=D_1=1,\qquad D_{2k}=F_{k+2}\quad(k\ge0),\\
 D_{2k+1}=2F_{k+1}\quad(k\ge1).
 \end{gathered}
\]
No auxiliary recurrence hypothesis is assumed.

For every \(m\ge0\), a visible word \(x\in X_m\) is a maximizer if and only if
its residue \(r=V_m(x)\) belongs to the following set.  If \(m=2k\ge10\), the
set is
\[
 \mathcal M_{2k}
 =\{I_{2k+1}-1,\;G_{2k}-1-I_{2k+1}\}.
\]
If \(m=2k-1\ge13\), the set is
\[
 \mathcal M_{2k-1}
 =\{I_{2k}-1,\;J_{2k}-1,\;
       G_{2k-1}-1-I_{2k},\;G_{2k-1}-1-J_{2k}\}.
\]
The remaining maximizing sets are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
m&D_m&\mathcal M_m\\ \hline
0&1&\{0\}\\
1&1&\{0,1\}\\
2&2&\{0\}\\
3&2&\{0,1,3\}\\
4&3&\{0,3\}\\
5&4&\{3\}\\
6&5&\{3,8\}\\
7&6&\{3,8,11,16\}\\
8&8&\{8,16,24\}\\
9&10&\{16,24,29,37\}\\
11&16&\{37,63,71,79,105\}.
\end{array}
\]
In particular, apart from the displayed initial exceptions, the degeneracy of
the maximum is exactly \(2\) in even windows and exactly \(4\) in odd windows.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 M_\ell:=\max_{F_{\ell+1}\le n<F_{\ell+2}}R(n).
\]
After translating the Fibonacci indexing in
\cite[Thm.~4.7]{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005}, one has
\[
 M_{2a+1}=F_{a+2}\quad(a\ge0),
 \qquad
 M_{2a+2}=2F_{a+1}\quad(a\ge1),
\]
with \(M_1=M_2=1\).
The same paper classifies every argument attaining these maxima
\cite[Thm.~5.3]{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005}.

By \Cref{thm:fiber-partition-interval}, the first block of the fiber list is
\[
 \bigl(R(n)\bigr)_{F_{m+2}\le n\le F_{m+3}-2}.
\]
Its maximum is \(M_{m+1}\).  The second block consists of values \(R(n)\) with
\(F_{m+1}-1\le n<F_{m+2}\).  For \(m\ge4\), its maximum is at most \(M_m\), and
the displayed formulas give \(M_m<M_{m+1}\): if \(m=2a\), then
\(2F_a<F_{a+2}\), while if \(m=2a+1\), then
\(F_{a+2}<2F_{a+1}\).  Hence \(D_m=M_{m+1}\) for \(m\ge4\).  The same equality
for \(m=0,1,2,3\), with the stated exceptional degeneracies, follows by direct
enumeration of the at most eight raw words.  Substitution of \(m=2k\) and
\(m=2k+1\) into the formulas for \(M_{m+1}\) proves the asserted closed forms.

It remains to translate the arguments of the maximum.  In the notation
\(G_j=F_{j+1}\) used above, \cite[Thm.~5.3]{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005}
states that the two generic maximizing integers of odd length \(2k+1\) are
\[
 G_{2k+1}-1+I_{2k+1},
 \qquad
 G_{2k+2}-1-I_{2k+1}.
\]
Subtracting the left endpoint \(G_{2k+1}=F_{m+2}\), as prescribed by the first
case of \Cref{thm:fiber-partition-interval}, gives
\(I_{2k+1}-1\) and \(G_{2k}-1-I_{2k+1}\).  Similarly, the four generic
maximizing integers of even length \(2k\) are obtained by using \(I_{2k}\) and
\(J_{2k}\) in the same two symmetric positions.  Subtraction of
\(G_{2k}=F_{m+2}\) gives the four residues displayed in the theorem.  The
exceptional length-nine and length-twelve central maximizers in the cited
classification give \(m=8\) and \(m=11\), respectively.  Direct evaluation of
the finitely many shorter cases gives the table.  Since \(V_m\) is bijective,
this residue list classifies the visible maximizing words themselves.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact two-step extremal recurrence]
\label{cor:extremal-fiber-recurrence}
For every integer \(m\ge6\),
\[
 D_m=D_{m-2}+D_{m-4}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If \(m=2k\), then the three terms are \(F_{k+2},F_{k+1},F_k\), respectively.
If \(m=2k+1\), they are \(2F_{k+1},2F_k,2F_{k-1}\), respectively.  In both
cases the assertion is the Fibonacci recurrence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Higher extremal ranks]
\label{rem:higher-extremal-ranks-open}
The interval identity is an unconditional reduction of every distinct
multiplicity level and its degeneracy to the corresponding level sets of
\(R(n)\) on the two displayed intervals.  The cited extremal theorem classifies
the first distinct level, but it does not classify the second or arbitrary
fixed \(k\)-th distinct level.  Consequently no closed formula for all top-
\(k\) levels is asserted here.  Establishing such formulas is a separate open
interface; the accompanying finite computations are not used as a substitute
for that proof.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Negative-temperature freezing and the residual LDP interface}

For real \(t\), define the real collision sum
\[
 S_t(m):=\sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^t.
\]
This is well defined for all \(t\in\RR\), since every fiber is nonempty.  The
integer values agree with \Cref{def:collision-moments}.

We first show that the previously natural all-real analyticity ansatz cannot
hold.  The obstruction is not a defect of the integer collision kernels: it
is an actual frozen phase of the Fibonacci partition spectrum.  Let
\(\sigma_0>2\) be the unique real solution of
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\zeta(\sigma_0-1)}{\zeta(\sigma_0)}=2.
 \label{eq:freezing-exponent}
\end{equation}
Numerically, \(\sigma_0=2.478750785733960\ldots\).

\begin{theorem}[Negative-temperature freezing]
\label{thm:negative-temperature-freezing}
For every real \(t\le-\sigma_0\), the pressure exists unconditionally and
\[
 P(t):=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log S_t(m)=0.
\]
Consequently there is no real-analytic function on \(\RR\) which agrees with
the finite-window pressure limits wherever those limits exist and also
satisfies \(P(0)=\log\varphi\).  In particular, the pressure is not strictly
convex on \(\RR\), and no compact, uniformly positive real-tilt transfer
operator with a globally analytic simple leading eigenvalue can represent
these partition sums.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We use the published level-set decomposition of the ordinary Fibonacci
partition function rather than reproducing it.  In Weinstein's notation, let
\(\Psi(k)\) be the number of generating orbits at level \(k\).  His orbit
classification and Fibonacci-layer theorem
\cite[Thms.~3.8, 5.1, and 10.3]{Weinstein2016} imply the following two facts.
First, in every Fibonacci layer
\[
 [F_j-1,F_{j+1}-1)
\]
the equation \(R(n)=k>1\) has at most \(2\Psi(k)\) solutions (and it has
exactly that many once the layer index is at least \(2k\)); the level \(k=1\)
occurs once.  Second, in its half-plane of convergence,
\begin{equation}
 1+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^s}
 =\left(2-\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}\right)^{-1}.
 \label{eq:weinstein-level-dirichlet-series}
\end{equation}
Indeed, Weinstein obtains the right side as the nonnegative free-monoid
series
\[
 \sum_{r\ge0}
 \left(\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}-1\right)^r.
\]
Writing \(\phi_{\rm E}\) for Euler's totient function, the identity
\(\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\phi_{\rm E}(n)n^{-s}\) shows that the ratio is
strictly decreasing from \(+\infty\) to \(1\) on \((2,\infty)\).  Hence
\Cref{eq:freezing-exponent} has exactly one solution, and Tonelli's theorem
shows that the nonnegative Dirichlet series in
\Cref{eq:weinstein-level-dirichlet-series} converges precisely for
\(s>\sigma_0\).

By \Cref{thm:fiber-partition-interval}, the collision sum is exactly the sum
of \(R(n)^t\) over two adjacent Fibonacci layers:
\begin{equation}
 S_t(m)=\sum_{n=F_{m+1}-1}^{F_{m+3}-2}R(n)^t.
 \label{eq:real-collision-two-layers}
\end{equation}
If \(t<-\sigma_0\), the level-set bound and
\Cref{eq:weinstein-level-dirichlet-series} give, uniformly in \(m\),
\[
 1\le S_t(m)
 \le 2+4\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\Psi(k)k^t<\infty.
\]
Thus \(m^{-1}\log S_t(m)\to0\).

At \(t=-\sigma_0\), fix \(\varepsilon>0\).  Since no fiber exceeds \(D_m\),
the same level-set estimate gives
\[
 S_{-\sigma_0}(m)
 \le 2+4D_m^{\varepsilon}
       \sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^{\sigma_0+\varepsilon}}.
\]
The series is finite, while
\(m^{-1}\log D_m\to\frac12\log\varphi\) by
\Cref{thm:unconditional-extremal-fibers}.  Therefore
\[
 0\le\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log S_{-\sigma_0}(m)
 \le\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\log\varphi.
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves the endpoint assertion.

Finally, an analytic function which vanishes on the open interval
\(( -\infty,-\sigma_0)\) vanishes identically, whereas
\[
 P(0)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log|X_m|=\log\varphi.
\]
This proves the analytic and strict-convexity obstructions.
\end{proof}

The remaining issue is therefore not global analyticity but the construction
and regularity of the pressure above the freezing threshold.

\begin{definition}[Residual real-pressure hypothesis]
\label{hyp:residual-real-pressure}
We say that the residual real-pressure hypothesis holds if the following
assertions are true.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every \(t> -\sigma_0\), the finite limit
\[
 P(t)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log S_t(m)
\]
exists.
\item The function obtained by adjoining the proven value \(P(t)=0\) for
\(t\le-\sigma_0\) is differentiable on \(\RR\); its restriction to
\(( -\sigma_0,\infty)\) is real analytic and satisfies \(P''(t)>0\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Full real-tilt LDP, residual conditional form]
\label{thm:conditional-full-ldp}
Assume \Cref{hyp:residual-real-pressure}.  Let \(X^{(m)}\) be uniformly distributed on
\(X_m\), and set
\[
 Y_m:=\frac1m\log d_m(X^{(m)}).
\]
Then \((Y_m)\) satisfies a full large deviation principle on \(\RR\) with speed
\(m\) and good convex rate function
\[
 I(\alpha)=\sup_{t\in\RR}
 \{t\alpha-P(t)+\log\varphi\}.
\]
Thus, for every Borel set \(A\subseteq\RR\),
\[
 -\inf_{\alpha\in A^\circ}I(\alpha)
 \le \liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log\Pr(Y_m\in A),
\]
\[
 \limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log\Pr(Y_m\in A)
 \le-\inf_{\alpha\in\overline A}I(\alpha).
\]
The lower endpoint is \(\alpha_-=0\), the upper endpoint is
\(\alpha_+=\frac12\log\varphi\), and \(I(0)=\log\varphi\).  The function
\(I\) is real analytic and strictly convex on \((0,\alpha_+)\).  More
precisely, for the unique \(t(\alpha)>-\sigma_0\) satisfying
\(P'(t(\alpha))=\alpha\),
\[
 I'(\alpha)=t(\alpha),
 \qquad
 I''(\alpha)=\frac1{P''(t(\alpha))}>0.
\]
Moreover, \(\lim_{t\to+\infty}P(t)/t=\frac12\log\varphi\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\nu_m\) be the uniform law on \(X_m\).  For every \(t\in\RR\),
\begin{align*}
 \frac1m\log\EE_{\nu_m}e^{mtY_m}
 &=\frac1m\log\left(\frac1{F_{m+2}}
       \sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^t\right)\\
 &\longrightarrow P(t)-\log\varphi=:\Lambda(t).
\end{align*}
By hypothesis, \(\Lambda\) is finite and differentiable on all of \(\RR\).
Moreover, \(0\le Y_m\le m^{-1}\log D_m\), and
\Cref{thm:unconditional-extremal-fibers} gives
\[
 \frac1m\log D_m\longrightarrow\frac12\log\varphi.
\]
The laws of \(Y_m\) are therefore exponentially tight (indeed, they are
eventually supported in one fixed compact interval).  The
G\"artner--Ellis theorem
\cite{Gartner1977,Ellis1984}\cite[Thm.~2.3.6]{DemboZeitouni2010}, applied on the
full effective domain \(\RR\), yields the stated full LDP with good rate
\[
 \Lambda^*(\alpha)
 =\sup_t\{t\alpha-\Lambda(t)\}
 =\sup_t\{t\alpha-P(t)+\log\varphi\}.
\]

On \(( -\sigma_0,\infty)\), the derivative \(P'\) is strictly increasing.
The differentiability at \(-\sigma_0\) and
\Cref{thm:negative-temperature-freezing} give
\(P'(-\sigma_0)=0\), so the open derivative range is
\((0,\alpha_+)\).  The analytic
inverse function theorem gives an analytic inverse \(t(\alpha)\) on the open
range \((0,\alpha_+)\).  The supremum defining \(I(\alpha)\) is attained
there uniquely at \(t(\alpha)\).  Differentiating
\[
 I(\alpha)=t(\alpha)\alpha-P(t(\alpha))+\log\varphi
\]
gives \(I'(\alpha)=t(\alpha)\) and
\(I''(\alpha)=1/P''(t(\alpha))>0\).

For \(\alpha=0\), every \(t\le-\sigma_0\) gives
\(t\alpha-P(t)+\log\varphi=\log\varphi\).  Since \(S_t(m)\ge1\), every
existing pressure is nonnegative, and hence \(I(0)=\log\varphi\).

It remains to identify the upper endpoint.  For every real \(t>0\),
\[
 D_m^t\le S_t(m)\le F_{m+2}D_m^t.
\]
Taking logarithms, dividing by \(m\), and passing to the limit gives
\[
 \frac{t}{2}\log\varphi
 \le P(t)\le
 \log\varphi+\frac{t}{2}\log\varphi.
\]
Division by \(t\) proves the asserted limit of \(P(t)/t\).  For a differentiable
convex function, convergence of \(P(t)/t\) to a finite limit forces the
increasing derivative \(P'(t)\) to converge to the same limit: for any fixed
\(0<a<1<b\), convexity bounds \(P'(t)\) between the secant slopes on
\([at,t]\) and \([t,bt]\), and both tend to \(\frac12\log\varphi\).
Thus \(\alpha_+=\frac12\log\varphi\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Residual real-pressure interface]
\label{rem:real-pressure-open-interface}
\Cref{thm:negative-temperature-freezing} closes the former all-real analytic
interface negatively: such a pressure cannot exist.  The integer collision
kernels prove \(P(q)=\log\lambda_q\) for integer \(q\ge0\), but tensor-power
kernels do not define \(P(t)\) at noninteger \(t\), and the freezing theorem
only treats \(t\le-\sigma_0\).  Existence for every
\(t\in(-\sigma_0,\infty)\), differentiability at the freezing point, and
analytic strict convexity on the open positive-pressure phase remain open.
These require an induced or countable-state real-tilt operator with explicit
recurrence and spectral-gap estimates; the compact finite-alphabet
Ruelle--Perron--Frobenius paradigm alone cannot remove the frozen phase
\cite{Ruelle2004,Sarig1999,Sarig2001}.  Consequently the full LDP in
\Cref{thm:conditional-full-ldp} remains conditional precisely on
\Cref{hyp:residual-real-pressure}, and no unconditional strictly convex full
LDP is asserted.
\end{remark}
