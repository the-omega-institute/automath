\section{Local fiber spectra and the critical Dirichlet law}
\label{sec:local-spectrum}

The pressure records only exponential growth.  On the proved frozen
half-line, a finer limit survives without exponential normalization.  It is
controlled by the fixed-level stabilization in the preceding section.

For \(m\ge1\) and \(k\ge1\), put
\[
 C_m(k):=\card{\{x\in X_m:d_m(x)=k\}},
 \qquad
 \nu_m:=\sum_{k\ge1}C_m(k)\delta_k.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Local microcanonical spectrum]
\label{thm:local-fiber-spectrum}
For every \(m\ge1\),
\[
 C_m(1)=2.
\]
The two singleton fibers have residues
\(F_{m+1}-1\) and \(F_{m+2}-1\).  For every \(k\ge2\),
\[
 C_m(k)\le4\Psi(k),
 \qquad
 C_m(k)=4\Psi(k)\quad(m\ge2k).
\]
In particular, \(C_m(2)=4\) for \(m\ge4\).  Moreover, the locally finite
measures \(\nu_m\) converge vaguely on the discrete space \(\mathbb N\) to
\[
 \nu_\infty=2\delta_1+4\sum_{k\ge2}\Psi(k)\delta_k.
\]
Equivalently, the masses of every finite subset of \(\mathbb N\) eventually
stabilize.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fiber--partition interval identity gives, as multisets, the values of
\(R\) on the disjoint consecutive layers \(\mathcal I_m\) and
\(\mathcal I_{m+1}\).  Hence
\begin{equation}
 C_m(k)=N_m(k)+N_{m+1}(k).
 \label{eq:fiber-level-two-layer-count}
\end{equation}
The uniform layer-count lemma gives one value equal to \(1\) in each layer,
the bound \(N_j(k)\le2\Psi(k)\) for \(k\ge2\), and equality for
\(j\ge2k\).  Summing the two layers proves all the asserted counts.  Under
the interval identity, the two level-one points are the left endpoints
\(F_{m+1}-1\) and \(F_{m+2}-1\); inspection of the two cases in
\Cref{thm:fiber-partition-interval} shows that these are also their fiber
residues.  Finally, every compact subset of the discrete space
\(\mathbb N\) is finite, and each of its point masses eventually has the
displayed value.  This is precisely vague convergence.
\end{proof}

Define the unnormalized limiting spectral partition function by
\[
 \mathcal Z(s):=\frac{4}{2-\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)}-2
 \qquad(s>\sigma_0).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact negative-tilt spectral limit]
\label{thm:spectral-partition-function}
For every \(s>\sigma_0\),
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}S_{-s}(m)=\mathcal Z(s)<\infty.
\]
For an arbitrary real tilt \(t\), the exact dichotomy is
\[
 \begin{cases}
 S_t(m)\longrightarrow
 \displaystyle\frac{4}{2-\zeta(-t-1)/\zeta(-t)}-2,&t< -\sigma_0,\\[8pt]
 S_t(m)\longrightarrow+\infty,&t\ge-\sigma_0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus \(-\sigma_0\) is the convergence boundary for the unnormalized
partition functions, independently of whether it is the boundary of the
zero-pressure set.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(s>\sigma_0\), \Cref{thm:local-fiber-spectrum} and dominated convergence
give
\[
 S_{-s}(m)
 =\sum_{k\ge1}C_m(k)k^{-s}
 \longrightarrow
 2+4\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^s}.
\]
The domination follows from \(C_m(k)\le4\Psi(k)\) for \(k\ge2\), and the
majorant is summable by
\eqref{eq:weinstein-level-dirichlet-series}.  Weinstein's Dirichlet identity
then reduces the last expression to \(\mathcal Z(s)\).

Suppose next that \(0<s\le\sigma_0\).  If \(2<s\le\sigma_0\), the geometric
series in \eqref{eq:weinstein-level-dirichlet-series} diverges.  If
\(0<s\le2\), the one-factor generating words give
\(\Psi(k)\ge\phi_{\rm E}(k)\), and the totient Dirichlet series diverges.
Thus \(\sum_{k\ge2}\Psi(k)k^{-s}=+\infty\) throughout this range.  For each
fixed \(K\), \Cref{thm:local-fiber-spectrum} gives, once \(m\ge2K\),
\[
 S_{-s}(m)\ge2+4\sum_{2\le k\le K}\Psi(k)k^{-s}.
\]
Letting \(K\to\infty\) proves divergence for
\(-\sigma_0\le t<0\).  For \(t\ge0\), nonempty fibers satisfy
\(d_m(x)^t\ge1\), and hence
\(S_t(m)\ge\card{X_m}=F_{m+2}\to\infty\).
\end{proof}

The singularity of \(\mathcal Z\) also determines a limiting Gibbs law.  Put
\[
 \kappa:=-\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}
          \right|_{s=\sigma_0}
 =\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\phi_{\rm E}(n)\log n}{n^{\sigma_0}}
 =2.589184379946924\ldots.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Critical Gibbs spectrum]
\label{thm:critical-gibbs-spectrum}
As \(s\downarrow\sigma_0\),
\[
 \mathcal Z(s)=\frac{4}{\kappa(s-\sigma_0)}+O(1).
\]
For \(s>\sigma_0\), define
\[
 Q_{m,s}(k):=\frac{C_m(k)k^{-s}}{S_{-s}(m)}.
\]
Then \(Q_{m,s}\) converges in total variation to the probability measure
\[
 Q_s(1)=\frac2{\mathcal Z(s)},
 \qquad
 Q_s(k)=\frac{4\Psi(k)k^{-s}}{\mathcal Z(s)}\quad(k\ge2).
\]
In particular,
\[
 Q_s(1)\sim\frac\kappa2(s-\sigma_0).
\]
If \(K\) has law \(Q_s\), then
\[
 \mathbb E_s[\log K]
 =\frac1{s-\sigma_0}+O(1),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_s(\log K)
 =\frac1{(s-\sigma_0)^2}+O(1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The totient series is absolutely differentiable in a neighborhood of
\(\sigma_0>2\), and therefore
\[
 2-\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}
 =\kappa(s-\sigma_0)+O((s-\sigma_0)^2).
\]
The pole expansion and the asymptotic for \(Q_s(1)\) follow.  For fixed
\(s>\sigma_0\), the pointwise convergence and summable domination used in
\Cref{thm:spectral-partition-function} give convergence of the unnormalized
weights in \(\ell^1(\mathbb N)\).  Normalizing therefore gives total
variation convergence.

Absolute differentiation of the limiting Dirichlet series yields
\[
 -\frac{d}{ds}\log\mathcal Z(s)=\mathbb E_s[\log K],
 \qquad
 \frac{d^2}{ds^2}\log\mathcal Z(s)
 =\operatorname{Var}_s(\log K).
\]
Since the denominator defining \(\mathcal Z\) is analytic at \(\sigma_0\)
and has a simple zero there, its Taylor expansion gives
\[
 \log\mathcal Z(s)
 =-\log(s-\sigma_0)+\log(4/\kappa)+O(s-\sigma_0),
\]
where the remainder is analytic.  Differentiating this local analytic
expansion proves the two moment estimates.
\end{proof}

The same pole has a Tauberian consequence for the generating-orbit counts.

\begin{theorem}[Generating-orbit Tauberian law]
\label{thm:psi-tauberian}
As \(x\to\infty\),
\[
 \sum_{k\le x}\Psi(k)
 \sim\frac{x^{\sigma_0}}{\sigma_0\kappa},
 \qquad
 \sum_{k\le x}\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^{\sigma_0}}
 \sim\frac1\kappa\log x.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \left(\frac4\kappa+o(1)\right)\log m
 \le S_{-\sigma_0}(m)
 \le\left(\frac{2\log\varphi}{\kappa}+o(1)\right)m.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set
\[
 A(s)=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\phi_{\rm E}(n)}{n^s}
 =\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}-1.
\]
For \(\Re s>\sigma_0\), Weinstein's identity reads
\[
 1+\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^s}=\frac1{1-A(s)}.
\]
On the line \(\Re s=\sigma_0\), if \(\tau\ne0\), then
\[
 |A(\sigma_0+i\tau)|<A(\sigma_0)=1.
\]
Indeed, equality in the triangle inequality would force all
\(n^{-i\tau}\) with \(n\ge2\) to have the same phase; since the sum would
have to equal the positive number \(1\), it would in particular force
\(2^{-i\tau}=3^{-i\tau}=1\).  This contradicts
\(\log2/\log3\notin\mathbb Q\).  Thus the Dirichlet series has no other
boundary singularity and has at \(s=\sigma_0\) a simple pole of residue
\(1/\kappa\).  The Wiener--Ikehara theorem
\cite{Korevaar2004} now gives
\[
 \sum_{k\le x}\Psi(k)
 \sim\frac{x^{\sigma_0}}{\sigma_0\kappa}.
\]
Partial summation gives the second asymptotic.

For the critical finite-window sum, stabilization gives
\[
 S_{-\sigma_0}(m)
 \ge2+4\sum_{2\le k\le m/2}\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^{\sigma_0}},
\]
which proves the lower bound.  The uniform layer estimate and the absence of
levels above \(D_m\) give
\[
 S_{-\sigma_0}(m)
 \le2+4\sum_{2\le k\le D_m}\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^{\sigma_0}}.
\]
Now use the second Tauberian asymptotic and
\(\log D_m\sim\tfrac12m\log\varphi\).
\end{proof}

Finally, the same estimates narrow the possible location of the zero-pressure
boundary.  Write \(p_m(t)=m^{-1}\log S_t(m)\), and set
\[
 \beta_*:=\frac{\log\varphi}{\log(2/\varphi)}
 =2.270559453959664\ldots.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Pressure bounds in the transition strip]
\label{thm:pressure-transition-strip}
For \(0<s<\sigma_0\),
\[
 \max\left\{0,\log\varphi-s\log\frac2\varphi\right\}
 \le\liminf_{m\to\infty}p_m(-s)
\]
and
\[
 \limsup_{m\to\infty}p_m(-s)
 \le\min\left\{\log\varphi,
       \frac{\sigma_0-s}{2}\log\varphi\right\}.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}p_m(t)>0\qquad(t>-\beta_*).
\]
Together with \Cref{thm:negative-temperature-freezing}, this confines any
boundary of the zero-pressure set to
\[
 [-\sigma_0,-\beta_*]
 =[-2.478750785733960\ldots,-2.270559453959664\ldots].
\]
The width of this candidate strip is
\[
 \sigma_0-\beta_*=0.208191331774296\ldots.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under the uniform distribution on \(X_m\), the mean fiber multiplicity is
\(2^m/F_{m+2}\).  Since \(u\mapsto u^{-s}\) is convex, Jensen's inequality
gives
\[
 \frac{S_{-s}(m)}{F_{m+2}}
 \ge\left(\frac{2^m}{F_{m+2}}\right)^{-s}.
\]
After taking logarithms and dividing by \(m\), this gives the nonzero term in
the lower bound; the term zero follows from the two singleton fibers.

For the upper bound, fix \(a>\sigma_0\).  Since all occurring levels satisfy
\(k\le D_m\), \Cref{thm:local-fiber-spectrum} gives
\[
 S_{-s}(m)
 \le2+4D_m^{a-s}\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{\Psi(k)}{k^a}.
\]
Using \(m^{-1}\log D_m\to\tfrac12\log\varphi\) and then letting
\(a\downarrow\sigma_0\) proves the second term in the upper bound.  The
first follows directly from \(d_m(x)^{-s}\le1\).  If
\(-\beta_*<t<0\), the lower bound is strictly positive by the definition of
\(\beta_*\); if \(t\ge0\), then \(S_t(m)\ge F_{m+2}\).  This proves the
last assertion without assuming that the pressure limit exists.
\end{proof}

The remaining transition strip admits an exact renewal description.  For a
reduced fraction \(p/q\in(0,1)\), write its unique negative continued fraction
as
\[
 \frac pq=
 \cfrac1{a_1-\cfrac1{a_2-\ddots-\cfrac1{a_r}}},
 \qquad a_i\ge2,
\]
and set
\[
 d(p/q):=\sum_{i=1}^r(a_i-1),
 \qquad c(p/q):=2d(p/q)+1.
\]
For a finite vector \(\mathbf a=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\), define its negative
continuant by
\[
 \begin{gathered}
  \mathcal D(\varnothing)=1,\qquad \mathcal D(a_1)=a_1,\\
  \mathcal D(a_1,\ldots,a_r)
  =a_r\mathcal D(a_1,\ldots,a_{r-1})
   -\mathcal D(a_1,\ldots,a_{r-2}).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Thus the denominator of the displayed negative continued fraction is
\(\mathcal D(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\).

For real \(s\), put
\begin{equation}
 b_\ell(s):=
 \sum_{\substack{0<p<q,\ (p,q)=1\\c(p/q)=\ell}}q^{-s},
 \qquad
 u_0(s):=1,\qquad
 u_j(s):=\sum_{\ell=1}^j b_\ell(s)u_{j-\ell}(s).
 \label{eq:weighted-renewal-coefficients}
\end{equation}
Every sum defining \(b_\ell(s)\) is finite.  More explicitly,
\begin{equation}
 b_{2d+1}(s)
 =\sum_{r=1}^d
   \sum_{\substack{e_1+\cdots+e_r=d\\e_i\ge1}}
   \mathcal D(e_1+1,\ldots,e_r+1)^{-s},
 \qquad b_{2d}(s)=0.
 \label{eq:weighted-letter-coefficients}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Weighted generator renewal]
\label{prop:weighted-generator-renewal}
For \(j\ge1\), let
\[
 \Lambda_j(s):=\sum_{n\in\mathcal I_j}R(n)^{-s}.
\]
Then, for every real \(s\),
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_j(s)
 =1+u_j(s)+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}u_\ell(s)
 =2\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}u_\ell(s)+u_j(s)-1.
 \label{eq:weighted-layer-renewal}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every \(m\ge1\),
\begin{equation}
 S_{-s}(m)
 =4\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}u_j(s)+3u_m(s)+u_{m+1}(s)-2.
 \label{eq:weighted-window-renewal}
\end{equation}
Moreover, with formal power series
\[
 B_s(z):=\sum_{\ell\ge1}b_\ell(s)z^\ell,
 \qquad U_s(z):=\sum_{j\ge0}u_j(s)z^j,
\]
one has
\begin{equation}
 U_s(z)=\frac1{1-B_s(z)}.
 \label{eq:weighted-renewal-generating-function}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We use the generating-number parameterization in
\cite[Secs.~3--4]{Weinstein2016}, but not its layer-stabilization theorem.
Under Weinstein's free-monoid bijection, a generating number \(g\) corresponds
uniquely to a nonempty word
\[
 w=\frac{p_1}{q_1}\times\cdots\times\frac{p_r}{q_r}
\]
in reduced fractions from \((0,1)\).  Proposition~3.3 and formula~(16) of
that paper give, in the present notation,
\begin{equation}
 R(g)=\prod_{i=1}^r q_i,
 \qquad L(g)=\sum_{i=1}^r c(p_i/q_i).
 \label{eq:generator-weight-length}
\end{equation}
The continued-fraction bijection also gives
\eqref{eq:weighted-letter-coefficients}.  Unique decomposition of a free word
therefore shows that \(u_j(s)\) is exactly the sum of \(R(g)^{-s}\) over all
generating numbers with \(L(g)=j\), and proves
\eqref{eq:weighted-renewal-generating-function} as a formal identity.

It remains to count each generating orbit in a fixed layer.  A generating
number belongs to Weinstein's odd first-index sheet, so its initial-block
parameter is \(1\).  The formulas already established in
\eqref{eq:L-a-shift}--\eqref{eq:L-sigma-shift} consequently specialize to
\[
 L([a]g)=L(g)+2a,\qquad
 L([a]\tau g)=L(g)+1+2a,\qquad
 L(\sigma n)=L(n)+1
\]
for \(a\ge0\).  Hence the two branches in Weinstein's first sheet contribute
one point to every layer \(j\ge L(g)\); their sigma images contribute one
further point precisely when \(j>L(g)\).  The action is free and distinct
generating orbits are disjoint.  Since \(\mathcal I_j\) contains exactly one
point of partition multiplicity \(1\) for \(j\ge1\), summing the orbit weights
gives \eqref{eq:weighted-layer-renewal}.  The two-layer identity
\eqref{eq:real-collision-two-layers} then gives
\eqref{eq:weighted-window-renewal}.  Notice that the restriction \(j\ge1\) is
essential in \eqref{eq:weighted-layer-renewal}, because \(\mathcal I_0\) is
empty.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All-real pressure and the freezing corner]
\label{thm:exact-negative-pressure}
\label{thm:all-real-pressure}
Let \(\mathfrak P\) denote the Stern--Brocot pressure
\[
 \mathfrak P(\theta)
 :=\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac1d
   \log\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_d}|T|^\theta .
\]
For every \(s\in\mathbb R\), the radius of convergence of \(B_s\) is
\begin{equation}
 R_B(s)=\exp\{-\mathfrak P(s/2)/2\}.
 \label{eq:Bs-radius}
\end{equation}
Thus \(R_B(s)<1\) for \(s<2\), and \(R_B(s)=1\) for \(s\ge2\).
The nonnegative real roots of \(B_s(r)=1\) are classified as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
s<\sigma_0 & \text{a unique root }r_s\in(0,R_B(s)),\\
s=\sigma_0 & \text{the unique root }r_s=1=R_B(s),\\
s>\sigma_0 & \text{no root in }[0,R_B(s)].
\end{array}
\]
The pressure exists for every real tilt and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 P(-s):=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log S_{-s}(m)
 =\begin{cases}
   -\log r_s,&s<\sigma_0,\\
   0,&s\ge\sigma_0.
  \end{cases}
 \label{eq:all-real-pressure}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for \(t>-\sigma_0\), \(P(t)>0\) is the unique solution of
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{q=2}^{\infty}
 \sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
 q^t e^{-P(t)c(p/q)}=1,
 \label{eq:exact-negative-pressure}
\end{equation}
whereas \(P(t)=0\) for \(t\le-\sigma_0\).  In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \{t\in\mathbb R:P(t)>0\}=(-\sigma_0,\infty).
 \label{eq:exact-zero-pressure-set}
\end{equation}

The restriction of \(P\) to \((-\sigma_0,\infty)\) is real analytic and
strictly convex.  At \(t_c=-\sigma_0\), it has the finite one-sided
derivatives
\begin{equation}
 P'_-(t_c)=0,
 \qquad
 P'_+(t_c)=
 \frac{\displaystyle
  \sum_{q\ge2}\sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
       q^{-\sigma_0}\log q}
 {\displaystyle
  \sum_{q\ge2}\sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
       c(p/q)q^{-\sigma_0}}>0.
 \label{eq:critical-pressure-slopes}
\end{equation}
Consequently, \(P\) is not differentiable at the freezing point.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(a_d(s):=b_{2d+1}(s)\).  The negative-continued-fraction
parameterization gives
\[
 a_d(s)=\sum_{\substack{0<p<q,\ (p,q)=1\\d(p/q)=d}}q^{-s}.
\]
There are \(2^{d-1}\) fractions in this layer, and their denominators satisfy
\(d+1\le q\le F_{d+2}\).  In particular, every \(B_s\) has positive finite
radius.  The layer is precisely the new-denominator layer of the Knauf model.
The published Stern--Brocot/Knauf pressure comparison
\cite{FialaKlebanOzluk2003,KessebohmerStratmann2007} gives, for fixed \(s<2\),
\begin{equation}
 a_d(s)\asymp_s \exp\{d\mathfrak P(s/2)\},
 \label{eq:knauf-layer-pressure}
\end{equation}
and gives
\(d^{-1}\log a_d(s)\to0\) for \(s\ge2\).  Here only this published
denominator-layer estimate is imported.  Cauchy--Hadamard now proves
\eqref{eq:Bs-radius}.  Moreover, \eqref{eq:knauf-layer-pressure} shows that
the terms of \(B_s\) do not tend to zero on \(|z|=R_B(s)\) when \(s<2\).
At \(s=2\), the positive real boundary also diverges.  For \(s>2\), Tonelli's
theorem gives
\begin{equation}
 B_s(1)=\sum_{q\ge2}\frac{\phi_{\rm E}(q)}{q^s}
 =\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}-1<\infty.
 \label{eq:weighted-renewal-at-one}
\end{equation}

The coefficients are nonnegative and the support contains both \(3\) and
\(5\), so \(B_s\) is strictly increasing on its positive interval of
convergence.  If \(s\le2\), its positive boundary value is infinite; if
\(2<s<\sigma_0\), \eqref{eq:weighted-renewal-at-one} is greater than one.
These facts give the unique interior root for \(s<\sigma_0\).  At
\(s=\sigma_0\), the boundary value is one, while it is less than one for
\(s>\sigma_0\).  This proves the asserted real-root classification.  The
triangle inequality also shows that, for \(s<\sigma_0\), the only zero of
\(1-B_s(z)\) in \(|z|\le r_s\) is the simple zero \(r_s\): equality forces
the degree-three and degree-five terms to have the same positive phase, and
\(\gcd(3,5)=1\).

Concatenation of generating words gives
\(u_{m+n}(s)\ge u_m(s)u_n(s)\).  The letters of costs \(3\) and \(5\) imply
\(u_j(s)>0\) for every \(j\ge8\).  The shifted Fekete argument used in the
proof of the strip result therefore applies without any restriction on
\(s\), and yields
\[
 \gamma_s:=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac1j\log u_j(s).
\]
If \(s<\sigma_0\), the formal identity
\(U_s=(1-B_s)^{-1}\), positivity, and the preceding root classification show
that the radius of \(U_s\) is \(r_s\).  At \(s=\sigma_0\), it is one.  If
\(s>\sigma_0\), then \(U_s(1)=(1-B_s(1))^{-1}<\infty\), while
\(u_j(s)\ge b_j(s)\) prevents the radius of \(U_s\) from exceeding
\(R_B(s)=1\).  Hence
\[
 R_U(s)=\begin{cases}r_s,&s<\sigma_0,\\1,&s\ge\sigma_0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \gamma_s=-\log R_U(s)\ge0.
\]
For \(H_m(s)=\sum_{j=0}^m u_j(s)\), the exact renewal identity gives
\[
 H_m(s)\le S_{-s}(m)\le4H_{m+1}(s).
\]
Since \(m^{-1}\log H_m(s)\to\gamma_s\), this proves
\eqref{eq:all-real-pressure} and the root equation.

For \(t>-\sigma_0\), put
\[
 \mu_t(p/q):=q^t e^{-P(t)c(p/q)}.
\]
The root equation makes this a probability measure.  The root lies strictly
inside the relevant convergence circle, and
\(\log q\ll d(p/q)\), so the defining series and all its parameter
derivatives converge locally uniformly.  The analytic implicit-function
theorem applies.  With \(X=\log q\) and \(C=c(p/q)\), differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 P'(t)=\frac{\mathbb E_{\mu_t}X}{\mathbb E_{\mu_t}C},
 \qquad
 P''(t)=
 \frac{\mathbb E_{\mu_t}[(X-P'(t)C)^2]}
      {\mathbb E_{\mu_t}C}.
 \label{eq:pressure-derivatives}
\end{equation}
The fractions \(1/2\) and \(1/3\) have \((C,q)=(3,2)\) and \((5,3)\),
and \(\log2/3\ne\log3/5\).  Thus the variance in
\eqref{eq:pressure-derivatives} is strictly positive.

It remains to justify passage to the critical endpoint.  For the canonical
regular continued fraction \(p/q=[0;a_1,\ldots,a_r]\), with \(a_r>1\),
the conversion between regular and negative continued fractions gives
\[
 d(p/q)=a_1+\cdots+a_r-1,
 \qquad c(p/q)=2(a_1+\cdots+a_r)-1.
\]
The fixed-denominator mean theorem of Panov and Liehl
\cite{Panov1982,Liehl1983,AistleitnerBordaHauke2023} states that
\[
 \frac1{\phi_{\rm E}(q)}
 \sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}(a_1+\cdots+a_r)
 \sim\frac6{\pi^2}(\log q)^2.
\]
Consequently, both the numerator and denominator in
\eqref{eq:critical-pressure-slopes} converge absolutely, since they are
bounded by constant multiples of
\(\sum q^{1-\sigma_0}\log q\) and
\(\sum q^{1-\sigma_0}(\log q)^2\), respectively.  Dominated convergence in
the first identity of \eqref{eq:pressure-derivatives} gives the stated finite
positive right derivative.  The pressure is identically zero to the left of
\(t_c\), so its left derivative is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full large deviations across the freezing corner]
\label{thm:full-fiber-ldp}
Let \(\mathbf X_m\) be uniformly distributed on \(X_m\), and set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 y_m(x):=\frac1m\log d_m(x),\qquad
 Y_m:=y_m(\mathbf X_m),\\
 t_c:=-\sigma_0,\qquad v_c:=P'_+(t_c),
 \qquad \alpha_+:=\frac12\log\varphi.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Then \((Y_m)\) satisfies a full large deviation principle with speed \(m\)
and good rate function
\begin{equation}
 I(\alpha)=\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}
 \{t\alpha-P(t)+\log\varphi\}.
 \label{eq:full-fiber-ldp-rate}
\end{equation}
Its effective domain is \([0,\alpha_+]\).  On the coexistence interval,
\begin{equation}
 I(\alpha)=\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha
 \qquad(0\le\alpha\le v_c).
 \label{eq:frozen-affine-rate}
\end{equation}
More precisely, for every \(0<\alpha<v_c\),
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 &\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\liminf_{m\to\infty}
 \frac1m\log\Pr\{|Y_m-\alpha|<\varepsilon\}\\
 &\quad=
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\limsup_{m\to\infty}
 \frac1m\log\Pr\{|Y_m-\alpha|<\varepsilon\}\\
 &\quad=-\bigl(\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:frozen-local-large-deviation}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(\alpha\in\mathbb R\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), write
\[
 A_m(\alpha,\varepsilon)
 :=\card{\left\{x\in X_m:
 \left|m^{-1}\log d_m(x)-\alpha\right|<\varepsilon\right\}}.
\]
We first establish the lower bound at the nonexposed slopes.  Fix
\(t>t_c\), put \(v=P'(t)>v_c\), and define the tilted law on \(X_n\) by
\[
 \nu_{n,t}(x):=\frac{d_n(x)^t}{S_t(n)}.
\]
For sufficiently small \(s>0\), the exponential Markov inequality and
\Cref{thm:all-real-pressure} give
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
 \log\nu_{n,t}\{y_n\ge v+\delta\}
 \le P(t+s)-P(t)-s(v+\delta)<0.
\]
The corresponding estimate with \(s<0\) gives the same conclusion for
\(y_n\le v-\delta\).  Consequently,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{x\in X_n\\|y_n(x)-v|<\delta}}d_n(x)^t
 =\exp\{n(P(t)+o(1))\}.
\]
Every summand on the left is at most
\(\exp\{n(tv+|t|\delta)\}\), and hence
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log A_n(v,\delta)
 \ge P(t)-tv-|t|\delta.
 \label{eq:exposed-source-count}
\end{equation}

We now transfer these source states to a larger layer without changing their
multiplicities.  The proof of \Cref{thm:fiber-partition-interval} gives the
explicit bijection
\[
 \chi_n(x)=
 \begin{cases}
  F_{n+2}+V_n(x),&0\le V_n(x)\le F_{n+1}-2,\\
  V_n(x),&F_{n+1}-1\le V_n(x)<F_{n+2},
 \end{cases}
\]
from \(X_n\) onto the integer points of
\(\mathcal I_n\sqcup\mathcal I_{n+1}\), with
\(d_n(x)=R(\chi_n(x))\).  Take
\(\delta<v/2\), so their multiplicities exceed two for all sufficiently
large \(n\).  The uniform layer-count lemma shows that each Weinstein
generating orbit supplies at most four source states to these two layers.
The specialized formulas in the proof of
\Cref{prop:weighted-generator-renewal} give
\[
 L([a]g)=L(g)+2a,\qquad
 L([a]\tau g)=L(g)+1+2a,
\]
and the two sigma branches increase these lengths by one.  Hence every orbit
meeting \(\mathcal I_n\sqcup\mathcal I_{n+1}\) has generator length at most
\(n+1\).
Keeping the generating number of every orbit which occurs therefore gives a
set \(\mathcal G_n\) such that
\begin{equation}
 \card{\mathcal G_n}\ge\frac14 A_n(v,\delta),
 \qquad L(g)\le n+1\quad(g\in\mathcal G_n),
 \label{eq:source-generating-orbits}
\end{equation}
and \(n^{-1}\log R(g)\in(v-\delta,v+\delta)\), since the orbit action
preserves \(R\).  Distinct elements of
\(\mathcal G_n\) generate disjoint orbits.

Fix \(0<\alpha<v_c\), set \(\rho=\alpha/v\), and let
\(n_m=\lfloor\rho m\rfloor\).  For sufficiently large \(m\), every
\(g\in\mathcal G_{n_m}\) has \(L(g)<m\).  The generator formulas in the
proof of \Cref{prop:weighted-generator-renewal} permit the parity-free
definition
\[
 z_m(g):=
 \begin{cases}
 [a]g,&m-L(g)=2a,\\
 [a]\tau g,&m-L(g)=2a+1.
 \end{cases}
\]
They give \(L(z_m(g))=m\) and \(R(z_m(g))=R(g)>1\).  Thus
\(z_m(g)\in\mathcal I_m\), and \(\chi_m^{-1}\) turns these into distinct
states of \(X_m\).  Moreover,
\[
 \left|m^{-1}\log R(z_m(g))-\alpha\right|
 \le \frac{n_m}{m}\delta+\frac vm.
\]
Combining this injection with
\eqref{eq:exposed-source-count}--\eqref{eq:source-generating-orbits} yields,
for every \(\varepsilon>\rho\delta\),
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log A_m(\alpha,\varepsilon)
 \ge \rho\bigl(P(t)-tv-|t|\delta\bigr).
 \label{eq:orbit-filling-lower-bound}
\end{equation}
First let \(\delta\downarrow0\), and then let \(t\downarrow t_c\).  Since
\(P(t)\to0\), \(P'(t)=v\to v_c\), and
\[
 \rho(P(t)-tv)=\alpha\left(\frac{P(t)}v-t\right)
 \longrightarrow-t_c\alpha=\sigma_0\alpha,
\]
we obtain
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\liminf_{m\to\infty}
 \frac1m\log A_m(\alpha,\varepsilon)
 \ge\sigma_0\alpha.
 \label{eq:frozen-count-lower}
\end{equation}

The matching upper bound uses the critical pressure directly.  On the set
counted by \(A_m(\alpha,\varepsilon)\),
\(d_m(x)^{-\sigma_0}\ge
\exp\{-\sigma_0m(\alpha+\varepsilon)\}\).  Therefore
\[
 A_m(\alpha,\varepsilon)
 e^{-\sigma_0m(\alpha+\varepsilon)}
 \le S_{-\sigma_0}(m)=e^{o(m)},
\]
which, together with \eqref{eq:frozen-count-lower}, proves the corresponding
counting limit.  Since \(\card{X_m}=F_{m+2}\) and
\(m^{-1}\log F_{m+2}\to\log\varphi\), this is
\eqref{eq:frozen-local-large-deviation}.

We next identify the convex dual.  If \(0\le\alpha\le v_c\), then
\(P(t)=0\) for \(t\le t_c\), while convexity and the right derivative at
\(t_c\) give \(P(t)\ge v_c(t-t_c)\) for \(t\ge t_c\).  It follows that
\(t\alpha-P(t)\le t_c\alpha\) for every \(t\), with equality at
\(t=t_c\), proving \eqref{eq:frozen-affine-rate}.

The exact maximum theorem gives
\[
 \max_{x\in X_m}m^{-1}\log d_m(x)\longrightarrow\alpha_+.
\]
For \(t>0\), the bounds
\(D_m^t\le S_t(m)\le F_{m+2}D_m^t\) imply
\[
 \alpha_+\le\frac{P(t)}t
 \le\alpha_++\frac{\log\varphi}{t}.
\]
By convexity, (P') is increasing and therefore has a limit in
\((0,+\infty]\).  The identity
\[
 \frac{P(t)}t=\frac{P(0)}t+\frac1t\int_0^t P'(u)\,du
\]
and Ces\`aro convergence show that this limit is \(\alpha_+\).  Thus
\(P'(t)\uparrow\alpha_+\) as \(t\to\infty\).  Strict convexity and
analyticity on \((t_c,\infty)\) now show that every
\(\alpha\in(v_c,\alpha_+)\) is exposed by a unique \(t>t_c\).  The tilted
estimate above with \(n=m\) supplies
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\liminf_{m\to\infty}
 \frac1m\log\Pr\{|Y_m-\alpha|<\varepsilon\}
 \ge P(t)-t\alpha-\log\varphi=-I(\alpha).
 \label{eq:exposed-local-large-deviation}
\end{equation}

Finally, the logarithmic moment generating functions under the uniform law
converge on all of \(\mathbb R\) to
\[
 \Lambda(t)=P(t)-\log\varphi.
\]
The variables are eventually supported in a fixed compact interval, so they
are exponentially tight.  The G\"artner--Ellis upper bound
\cite[Theorem~2.3.6(a)]{DemboZeitouni2010} therefore gives the
large-deviation upper bound for all closed sets.

It remains to justify the effective domain and endpoint lower bounds.  If
\(\alpha<0\), letting \(t\to-\infty\) in
\eqref{eq:full-fiber-ldp-rate} and using \(P(t)=0\) gives
\(I(\alpha)=+\infty\).  If \(\alpha>\alpha_+\), the limit
\(P(t)/t\to\alpha_+\) gives the same conclusion as \(t\to\infty\).
Conversely, if \(0\le\alpha\le\alpha_+\), then
\(t\alpha-P(t)\) is bounded above on \(( -\infty,t_c]\), on the compact
interval \([t_c,0]\), and on \([0,\infty)\), where
\(P(t)\ge t\alpha_+\).  Thus the effective domain is exactly
\([0,\alpha_+]\).  The rate is a lower-semicontinuous convex function, and
its finiteness on this closed interval implies continuity relative to the
interval, including at the endpoints.

The local lower bounds already proved cover the coexistence and exposed
parts of \((0,\alpha_+)\), and points from either side approximate \(v_c\).
At \(0\), the two singleton fibers give the lower bound directly.  At
\(\alpha_+\), continuity of \(I\) and exposed points approaching from below
supply the lower bound for every open neighborhood.  Hence the lower bound
holds for every open set.  The effective domain is compact and \(I\) is
lower semicontinuous, so \(I\) is good and the full large deviation principle
follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Imported and new components]
Weinstein's free-monoid parameterization, multiplicativity of \(R(g)\), and
largest-index formula \eqref{eq:generator-weight-length} are published inputs.
The weighted cost \(c(p/q)\), the finite-layer renewal identity
\eqref{eq:weighted-layer-renewal}, its transfer to the finite-window sums, and
the all-real finite-window pressure formula are the additional conclusions
here.  The orbit thinning and parity-complete filling in
\Cref{thm:full-fiber-ldp} are the additional ingredients that close the
nonexposed large-deviation slopes.  The standard upper bound and the
exposed-slope tilting argument are not claimed as new.  The Stern--Brocot
multifractal theorem of Kesseb\"ohmer and Stratmann
\cite{KessebohmerStratmann2004,KessebohmerStratmann2007} concerns interval
growth rates and does not state the finite-window LDP above.  The
Stern--Brocot pressure and the Knauf
denominator-layer estimate
are cited published inputs.  In
particular, the argument does not reprove or invoke Weinstein's eventual
layer-stabilization theorem.
\end{remark}
