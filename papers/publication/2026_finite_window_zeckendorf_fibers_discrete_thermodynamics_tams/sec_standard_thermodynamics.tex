\section{Thermodynamics of the Fibonacci partition function}
\label{sec:standard-thermodynamics}

The renewal identity was derived from Weinstein's standard Fibonacci
partition function before it was transferred to the finite-window fold.  We
now state its one-layer consequences intrinsically.  This removes the bespoke
fold from the probabilistic conclusions.

For \(m\ge1\), recall
\[
 \mathcal I_m=[F_{m+1}-1,F_{m+2}-1)
\]
and define
\[
 Z_m^R(t):=\sum_{n\in\mathcal I_m}R(n)^t
 \qquad(t\in\RR).
\]
If \(\mathbf N_m\) is uniform on \(\mathcal I_m\), put
\[
 Y_m^R:=\frac1m\log R(\mathbf N_m).
\]

\begin{theorem}[One-layer Fibonacci partition thermodynamics]
\label{thm:standard-fibonacci-thermodynamics}
For every \(t\in\RR\),
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log Z_m^R(t)=P(t),
 \label{eq:standard-layer-pressure}
\end{equation}
where \(P\) is the pressure in \Cref{thm:all-real-pressure}.  Thus the
one-layer pressure is zero on \((-\infty,-\sigma_0]\), is positive, real
analytic, and strictly convex on \((-\sigma_0,\infty)\), and has the
one-sided derivatives in \eqref{eq:critical-pressure-slopes}.

For \(s>\sigma_0\), the unnormalized sums satisfy
\begin{equation}
 Z_m^R(-s)\longrightarrow
 \mathcal Z_R(s):=
 \frac2{2-\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)}-1.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-frozen-limit}
\end{equation}
At the critical tilt,
\begin{equation}
 Z_m^R(-\sigma_0)\sim\frac{2m}{\mu_C}.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-critical-size}
\end{equation}

For \(n\in\mathcal I_m\) with \(R(n)>2\), let \(g(n)\) be the generating
number of its Weinstein orbit and put \(\ell_m^R(n)=L(g(n))\); set
\(\ell_m^R(n)=0\) when \(R(n)\le2\).  Let \(\widehat{\mathbf N}_m\) have the
critical Gibbs law
\[
 \Pr\{\widehat{\mathbf N}_m=n\}
 =\frac{R(n)^{-\sigma_0}}{Z_m^R(-\sigma_0)}
 \qquad(n\in\mathcal I_m).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \left(
  \frac1m\log R(\widehat{\mathbf N}_m),
  \frac1m\ell_m^R(\widehat{\mathbf N}_m)
 \right)
 \xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{\mathrm d}(v_cU,U),
 \label{eq:standard-layer-critical-gibbs}
\end{equation}
where \(U\) is uniform on \([0,1]\).

Finally, \((Y_m^R)\) satisfies a full large deviation principle with speed
\(m\) and the good rate function
\begin{equation}
 I(\alpha)=\sup_{t\in\RR}
 \{t\alpha-P(t)+\log\varphi\}.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-ldp-rate}
\end{equation}
Its effective domain is \([0,\alpha_+]\), where
\(\alpha_+=\tfrac12\log\varphi\), and
\[
 I(\alpha)=\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha
 \qquad(0\le\alpha\le v_c).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(H_m(s)=\sum_{j=0}^m u_j(s)\).  The exact one-layer renewal identity
\eqref{eq:weighted-layer-renewal} says
\begin{equation}
 Z_m^R(-s)=2H_{m-1}(s)+u_m(s)-1.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-renewal}
\end{equation}
The proof of \Cref{thm:all-real-pressure} established
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log u_m(s)
 =\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log H_m(s)
 =P(-s).
\]
Since \(u_m(s)\le Z_m^R(-s)\le3H_m(s)\), this proves
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-pressure}.

If \(s>\sigma_0\), then \(U_s(1)=\sum_{j\ge0}u_j(s)<\infty\), so
\(u_m(s)\to0\).  Equations
\eqref{eq:weighted-renewal-generating-function} and
\eqref{eq:weighted-renewal-at-one} give
\[
 U_s(1)=\frac1{1-B_s(1)}
 =\frac1{2-\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)}.
\]
Letting \(m\to\infty\) in \eqref{eq:standard-layer-renewal} proves
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-frozen-limit}.  At \(s=\sigma_0\), the arithmetic
renewal theorem used in \Cref{thm:critical-finite-window-gibbs} gives
\(u_m(\sigma_0)\to1/\mu_C\).  Ces\`aro summation in
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-renewal} proves
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-critical-size}.

We next retain the generating cost.  The four orbit branches displayed above
\eqref{eq:marked-microcanonical-count} show that a generator of cost \(c\)
contributes two points to \(\mathcal I_m\) if \(c<m\), one if \(c=m\), and
none if \(c>m\).  Thus the one-layer boundary weight is
\[
 K_m^R(c)=
 \begin{cases}
  2,&c<m,\\
  1,&c=m,\\
  0,&c>m.
 \end{cases}
\]
With the marked renewal measure \(\mathcal U\) defined before
\eqref{eq:marked-microcanonical-count}, the same free-monoid bijection gives,
for every Borel set
\(B\subset\mathbb R\times(\log2,\infty)\),
\begin{equation}
 \card{\{n\in\mathcal I_m:
   (\ell_m^R(n),\log R(n))\in B\}}
 =\int_B K_m^R(c)e^{\sigma_0h}\,\mathcal U(dc,dh).
 \label{eq:standard-layer-marked-count}
\end{equation}
The points with \(R(n)\le2\) have bounded total critical weight, uniformly in
\(m\), by the uniform layer-count lemma.

For every bounded continuous \(f\) on \([0,1]\), the arithmetic renewal
theorem and a Riemann-sum argument give
\[
 \frac1m\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}u_j(\sigma_0)f(j/m)
 \longrightarrow\frac1{\mu_C}\int_0^1f(y)\,dy.
\]
After multiplication of \eqref{eq:standard-layer-marked-count} by
\(e^{-\sigma_0h}\), the weights are two for \(j<m\) and one at \(j=m\).
Together with \eqref{eq:standard-layer-critical-size}, this proves
\[
 \frac{\ell_m^R(\widehat{\mathbf N}_m)}m
 \xrightarrow{\mathrm d}U.
\]
For the reward coordinate, the critical renewal increments
\((C_i,H_i)\) satisfy
\[
 \frac{H_1+\cdots+H_r}{C_1+\cdots+C_r}
 \longrightarrow\frac{\mu_H}{\mu_C}=v_c
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Every cost increment is at least three.  Consequently the number of renewal
epochs with total cost at most \(m\) and reward-to-cost ratio outside a fixed
\(\delta\)-neighborhood of \(v_c\) is \(o(m)\), pathwise and, by bounded
convergence after division by \(m\), in expectation.  Since \(K_m^R\le2\),
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-marked-count} and
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-critical-size} imply
\[
 \frac1m\log R(\widehat{\mathbf N}_m)
 -v_c\frac{\ell_m^R(\widehat{\mathbf N}_m)}m
 \xrightarrow{\Pr}0.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:standard-layer-critical-gibbs}.

It remains to prove the full LDP, in particular the lower bound at the
nonexposed slopes.  The layer has
\(\card{\mathcal I_m}=F_m\), and hence
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log\card{\mathcal I_m}=\log\varphi.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:standard-layer-pressure} therefore gives the limiting
logarithmic moment generating function
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m
 \log\mathbb E e^{tmY_m^R}=P(t)-\log\varphi.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-log-mgf}
\]
The translated extremal theorem in the Fibonacci-convention lemma gives
\[
 \max_{n\in\mathcal I_m}\frac1m\log R(n)
 \longrightarrow\alpha_+.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-maximum}
\]
Indeed, the two shifted endpoints have partition value one, so they do not
change the maximum on the intervening Fibonacci block.  Thus the variables
are exponentially tight, and the G\"artner--Ellis upper bound gives the
required upper bound for closed sets.

For the lower bound, fix \(t>t_c=-\sigma_0\), write \(v=P'(t)>v_c\), and
tilt the uniform law on \(\mathcal I_n\) by \(R(k)^t\).  Analyticity of \(P\)
and the exponential Markov inequality applied to
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-pressure} show that this tilted law assigns
exponentially vanishing mass to
\[
 \left\{k\in\mathcal I_n:
   \left|n^{-1}\log R(k)-v\right|\ge\delta\right\}.
\]
Consequently, if
\[
 A_n^R(v,\delta)
 :=\card{\{k\in\mathcal I_n:
 |n^{-1}\log R(k)-v|<\delta\}},
\]
then
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log A_n^R(v,\delta)
 \ge P(t)-tv-|t|\delta.
 \label{eq:standard-layer-exposed-source}
\end{equation}

For large \(n\), every point counted here has multiplicity greater than two.
Each generating orbit contributes at most two points to \(\mathcal I_n\).
Keeping one generator from each represented orbit therefore produces a set
\(\mathcal G_n^R\) with
\[
 \card{\mathcal G_n^R}\ge\tfrac12A_n^R(v,\delta),
 \qquad L(g)\le n\quad(g\in\mathcal G_n^R),
\]
and \(n^{-1}\log R(g)\in(v-\delta,v+\delta)\).

Fix \(0<\alpha<\alpha_+\), set \(\rho=\alpha/v<1\), and put
\(n_m=\lfloor\rho m\rfloor\).  For \(g\in\mathcal G_{n_m}^R\), define
\[
 z_m(g)=
 \begin{cases}
  [a]g,&m-L(g)=2a,\\
  [a]\tau g,&m-L(g)=2a+1.
 \end{cases}
\]
The orbit formulas give \(L(z_m(g))=m\) and
\(R(z_m(g))=R(g)\), so \(z_m(g)\in\mathcal I_m\).  Freeness makes this map
injective.  From \eqref{eq:standard-layer-exposed-source}, first letting
\(\delta\downarrow0\), we obtain the local counting lower bound
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac1m
 \log A_m^R(\alpha,\varepsilon)
 \ge\frac{\alpha}{v}\bigl(P(t)-tv\bigr).
 \label{eq:standard-layer-orbit-filling}
\end{equation}

If \(0<\alpha\le v_c\), let \(t\downarrow t_c\) in
\eqref{eq:standard-layer-orbit-filling}; then \(v\downarrow v_c\) and the
right side tends to \(\sigma_0\alpha\).  If
\(v_c<\alpha<\alpha_+\), let \(t_\alpha\) be the unique point with
\(P'(t_\alpha)=\alpha\), and let \(t\downarrow t_\alpha\) through values with
\(P'(t)>\alpha\).  The right side then tends to
\(P(t_\alpha)-t_\alpha\alpha\).  In both cases this is
\(\log\varphi-I(\alpha)\).  Division by
\(\card{\mathcal I_m}=F_m\) proves the LDP lower bound at every interior
point of the effective domain.

The effective domain and the affine formula follow from the same convex-dual
calculation as in \Cref{thm:full-fiber-ldp}, since the pressure is the same.
At \(0\), the left endpoint \(F_{m+1}-1\) has \(R=1\) and supplies the lower
bound directly.  At \(\alpha_+\), use interior points tending to the endpoint
and continuity of the finite convex rate on \([0,\alpha_+]\).  Hence the
lower bound holds for every open set, completing the full LDP.
\end{proof}
