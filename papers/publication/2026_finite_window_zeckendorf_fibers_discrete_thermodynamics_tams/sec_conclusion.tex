\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

On the standard Fibonacci layer
\(\mathcal I_m=[F_{m+1}-1,F_{m+2}-1)\), the classical partition function
\(R\) has a pressure \(P\) that is exactly the affine normalization
\(P(t)=t\log2-\tau_\mu(t)\log\varphi\) of the known golden-ratio Bernoulli
convolution spectrum.  The all-real spectrum and its first-order transition
are due to Lau--Ngai, Feng--Olivier, and Feng
\cite{LauNgai1998,FengOlivier2003,Feng2005}.  In the renewal coordinates of
this paper one obtains the exact frozen limit
\[
 \sum_{n\in\mathcal I_m}R(n)^{-s}
 \longrightarrow\frac2{2-\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)}-1
 \qquad(s>\sigma_0),
\]
 and the critical leading law \(2m/\mu_C\).  In the historical line from
 Moshchevitin--Zhigljavsky to Dushistova
 \cite{MoshchevitinZhigljavsky2004,Dushistova2007}, the same local continuant
 sum was already treated by Dushistova with a fuller claimed expansion.  The
 arithmetic theorem here identifies the sums term by term, corrects the
 printed leading coefficient from \(R_s+2R_s^2\) to \(2R_s^2\), and gives a
 simplified proof.  Its renewal/Fibonacci transfer sharpens the critical law
 to
 \[
  \sum_{n\in\mathcal I_m}R(n)^{-\sigma_0}
  =\frac{2m}{\mu_C}
  +\frac{2K_C}{\mu_C^2(\sigma_0-2)(3-\sigma_0)}m^{3-\sigma_0}
  +o(m^{3-\sigma_0}),
 \]
 where \(b_C=8\) is an explicit absolutely convergent continuant context sum
 and \(K_C=2^{\sigma_0-1}b_C/(\sigma_0-1)\).  Its critical Gibbs measure
has the coexistence limit \((v_cU,U)\), and its uniform logarithmic
multiplicity satisfies the full LDP with the affine rate
\(\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha\) on \([0,v_c]\).  These are one-layer theorems
about the standard partition function, not consequences inferred from a
two-layer mixture.

The finite-window fiber spectrum provides an exact finite model and admits
two descriptions.  The affine
permutation \(\pi_m\) identifies each multiplicity with a coefficient of a
finite Fibonacci subset-sum polynomial and with a Fibonacci-lag difference of
the indexed partition function.  The interval identity identifies the same
fiber list with ordinary partition values on two consecutive Fibonacci
layers.  The latter correspondence transfers the published extremal
classification, after an explicit convention change, and yields
\[
 D_{2k}=F_{k+2},\qquad D_{2k+1}=2F_{k+1},\qquad
 D_m=D_{m-2}+D_{m-4}\quad(m\ge6),
\]
together with the complete maximizing-residue list.

For negative real tilts, Weinstein's orbit counts recover the known frozen
half-line and evaluate its critical point arithmetically:
\[
 P(t)=0\qquad(t\le-\sigma_0),
 \qquad
 \frac{\zeta(\sigma_0-1)}{\zeta(\sigma_0)}=2.
\]
The uniform layer-count lemma treats the two unit conventions, the levels
\(1\) and \(2\), shared endpoints, and early layers explicitly.
At the unnormalized scale, however, the local spectrum stabilizes exactly and
\[
 S_{-s}(m)\longrightarrow
 \frac4{2-\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)}-2
 \qquad(s>\sigma_0).
\]
This function has a simple pole at \(\sigma_0\), produces an explicit
total-variation Gibbs limit, and yields the Tauberian law
\(\sum_{k\le x}\Psi(k)\sim
x^{\sigma_0}/(\sigma_0\kappa)\).  A separate Jensen lower bound proves
positive lower exponential growth for \(t>-\beta_*\), where
  \(\beta_*=2.2705594539\ldots\).  The weighted free-generator renewal equation,
together with the published Stern--Brocot denominator pressure, gives an
independent derivation in the present finite-layer coordinates of
\[
 \{t:P(t)>0\}=(-\sigma_0,\infty).
\]
The analyticity, strict convexity, and derivative corner on the positive
phase agree with Feng's all-real spectrum.  The renewal coordinates give the
finite positive continued-fraction formula for the right derivative, while
the frozen branch gives the zero left derivative.
At the critical tilt itself, the arithmetic local law and the exact renewal
identity give
\[
 S_{-\sigma_0}(m)
 =\frac{4m}{\mu_C}
 +\frac{4K_C}{\mu_C^2(\sigma_0-2)(3-\sigma_0)}m^{3-\sigma_0}
 +o(m^{3-\sigma_0}).
\]
The input is the corrected estimate
\(b_{2d+1}(\sigma_0)\sim8d^{-\sigma_0}\), not an assumed regular-variation
hypothesis.  Its one-large-partial-quotient proof controls two large digits
and the all-moderate regime separately.  Therefore there is no nontrivial
lattice, logarithmically periodic, or semistable modulation in the leading
\(d^{-\sigma_0}\) term; the result makes no claim about lower-order
oscillations.
The associated finite-window Gibbs measure has a nondegenerate coexistence
limit: the generating-layer fraction is uniform on \([0,1]\), and the
normalized log multiplicity is its product with \(v_c\).

The derivative jump prevents the G\"artner--Ellis theorem alone from treating
the intervening slopes.  A finite-window orbit-filling construction supplies
their matching local lower bounds and proves a full large deviation principle;
its good rate function is affine on the coexistence interval.

On a standard Fibonacci layer, retaining normalized generator cost together
with normalized log multiplicity yields a full two-dimensional large
deviation principle.  Its rate is the perspective of the scalar rate, and its
coexistence region is the affine face
\(J(\beta,\alpha)=\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha\) for
\(0\le\alpha\le\beta v_c\).  The lower bound combines fixed-cost exponential
tilting with an exact low-reward cost filler.

The exact finite-prime-support generating function isolates a remaining
arithmetic boundary.  Its cost-summed coefficients are rational, but the
marked cost has infinite second moment already on the dyadic ray and has no
positive exponential moment.  A directional coefficient law would require
both a parameter-uniform multivariate smooth-point theorem and uniform tail
control for that unbounded cost.  Neither is proved here, so no directional
quenched velocity or active-cutoff asymptotic for \(C_m(k)\) is claimed.

Conditional arithmetic and integer-moment consequences are collected in the
separately compiled supplement.  No transducer-based collision kernel,
algebraicity statement for \(\lambda_q\), arithmetic-window recurrence
polynomial, exact-kernel certificate, Galois group, or Chebotarev density is
claimed in the main article or supplement.
