\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Let \(F_0=0\), \(F_1=F_2=1\), and
\(F_{j+2}=F_{j+1}+F_j\), and put
\(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).  Let \(R(n)\) be the classical Fibonacci
partition function: the number of partitions of \(n\) into distinct
Fibonacci numbers, with the two unit terms identified.  Our main
thermodynamic results concern \(R\) on the standard layers
\[
 \mathcal I_m=[F_{m+1}-1,F_{m+2}-1).
\]
The moment pressure for every real tilt and its first-order transition are
already contained, under an affine normalization, in the golden-ratio
Bernoulli-convolution literature \cite{LauNgai1998,FengOlivier2003,Feng2005}.
We make that normalization explicit and use it as the starting point for
finite-window identities, arithmetic critical refinements, and full
large-deviation lower bounds for \(m^{-1}\log R(n)\) under the uniform law on
\(\mathcal I_m\).
For the arithmetic refinement, Moshchevitin--Zhigljavsky are the historical
Farey-tree predecessor and Dushistova is the direct local predecessor
\cite{MoshchevitinZhigljavsky2004,Dushistova2007}.  Her Theorem~3 treats the
same fixed-digit-sum continuant sum and states a fuller expansion.  The
contribution here is a termwise reconciliation, a correction of its printed
leading coefficient, a simplified proof, and the subsequent critical
renewal/Fibonacci transfer.

A finite-window Zeckendorf fold gives an exact finite model for this standard
object.  A word
\(\omega\in\{0,1\}^m\) determines the raw sum
\[
  N_m(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m\omega_jF_{j+1}.
\]
We consider the finite-window fold obtained by reducing this sum modulo
\(F_{m+2}\) and taking the unique legal Zeckendorf word in the visible
window.  If \(d_m(x)\) is the cardinality of the fiber over a legal word
\(x\), the corresponding finite problem is to identify the multiset
\(\{d_m(x):x\in X_m\}\) and its extremal and moment statistics.

The bridge is exact: the complete fiber list is the list of \(R\) on
\(\mathcal I_m\sqcup\mathcal I_{m+1}\).  A second correspondence is an affine
reindexing of a finite subset-sum spectrum.  Both are finite identities and
do not require a normalization transducer.

\subsection{Main results}

Put \(R(n)=0\) for \(n<0\).  Let \(\sigma_0>2\) be the unique solution of
\[
 \frac{\zeta(\sigma_0-1)}{\zeta(\sigma_0)}=2;
 \qquad \sigma_0=2.4787507857\ldots.
\]
For \(t>-\sigma_0\), let \(P(t)>0\) be the unique solution of
\[
 \sum_{q\ge2}\sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
 q^t e^{-P(t)c(p/q)}=1,
\]
where \(c(p/q)\) is the odd continued-fraction cost defined before
\Cref{prop:weighted-generator-renewal}, and put \(P(t)=0\) for
\(t\le-\sigma_0\).  Set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mu_C:=\sum_{q\ge2}\sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
 c(p/q)q^{-\sigma_0},\\
 v_c:=\frac{\displaystyle
 \sum_{q\ge2}\sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
 q^{-\sigma_0}\log q}{\mu_C},
 \qquad
 t_c:=-\sigma_0,\qquad
 \alpha_+:=\frac12\log\varphi.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Define
\[
 Z_m^R(t):=\sum_{n\in\mathcal I_m}R(n)^t.
\]

The arithmetic input at the critical point is a corrected local theorem,
rather than a tail assumption.  Dushistova's sum satisfies exactly
\(b_{2d+1}(s)=\sigma_{s/2}(d+1)\).  Her printed leading coefficient and the
corrected coefficient proved here are, side by side,
\[
 \text{Dushistova: }R_s+2R_s^2,
 \qquad
 \text{this paper: }2R_s^2,
 \qquad R_s=\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}.
\]
Thus, for every fixed \(s>2\),
\begin{equation}
 b_{2d+1}(s)
 \sim2\left(\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}\right)^2d^{-s}.
 \label{eq:introduction-low-temperature-layer}
\end{equation}
Its proof first separates the left endpoints of digit sums zero and one,
then cuts a canonical regular continued fraction at its unique large partial
quotient.  This gives the constant as an absolutely convergent left--right
continuant context sum.  At \(s=\sigma_0\), the corrected constant is
\(b_C=8\), and
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\{C>x\}\sim K_Cx^{1-\sigma_0},
 \qquad K_C=\frac{2^{\sigma_0-1}}{\sigma_0-1}b_C.
 \label{eq:introduction-critical-cost-tail}
\end{equation}
Consequently, \Cref{thm:arithmetic-critical-stable-renewal} proves
\begin{equation}
 Z_m^R(-\sigma_0)
 =\frac{2m}{\mu_C}
 +\frac{2K_C}{\mu_C^2(\sigma_0-2)(3-\sigma_0)}m^{3-\sigma_0}
 +o(m^{3-\sigma_0}),
 \label{eq:introduction-standard-critical-second-order}
\end{equation}
with the corresponding coefficient doubled for \(S_{-\sigma_0}(m)\).  The
critical cost also lies in the domain of attraction of a spectrally positive
\((\sigma_0-1)\)-stable law.  Regular variation in these statements is derived
from \eqref{eq:introduction-low-temperature-layer}; it is not assumed.

\begin{theoremA}[One-layer Fibonacci partition thermodynamics]
For every \(t\in\RR\),
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log Z_m^R(t)=P(t).
\]
For \(s>\sigma_0\),
\[
 Z_m^R(-s)\longrightarrow
 \frac2{2-\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)}-1,
\]
whereas \(Z_m^R(-\sigma_0)\sim2m/\mu_C\).  Under the critical Gibbs law
proportional to \(R(n)^{-\sigma_0}\), the normalized log multiplicity and
generating-layer cost converge jointly to \((v_cU,U)\), with \(U\) uniform on
\([0,1]\).

If \(\mathbf N_m\) is uniform on \(\mathcal I_m\), then
\(m^{-1}\log R(\mathbf N_m)\) satisfies a full large deviation principle
with good rate function
\[
 I(\alpha)=\sup_{t\in\RR}\{t\alpha-P(t)+\log\varphi\}.
\]
Its effective domain is \([0,\alpha_+]\), and
\(I(\alpha)=\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha\) on \([0,v_c]\).
\end{theoremA}

This is \Cref{thm:standard-fibonacci-thermodynamics}.  Its pressure assertion
is the Lau--Ngai/Feng spectrum under
\eqref{eq:erdos-pressure-dictionary}; it is included to normalize the critical
finite-size law and the uniform-layer LDP.  The latter statements use the
one-layer orbit weights \(2,1\), rather than a deduction from the two-layer
fiber mixture.

The same renewal representation retains the normalized generating cost
\(B_m^R=m^{-1}\ell_m^R(\mathbf N_m)\).
\Cref{thm:joint-cost-reward-ldp} proves a full LDP for
\((B_m^R,Y_m^R)\).  Its rate is
\[
 J(\beta,\alpha)=
 (1-\beta)\log\varphi+\beta I(\alpha/\beta)
 \quad(0<\beta\le1,\ 0\le\alpha\le\beta\alpha_+),
\]
with \(J(0,0)=\log\varphi\) and \(J=+\infty\) elsewhere.  In particular,
\[
 J(\beta,\alpha)=\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha
 \qquad(0\le\alpha\le\beta v_c),
\]
so coexistence is an affine face of the joint rate surface rather than an
artifact of one-dimensional projection.  Fixed-cost exponential tilting
supplies the strictly convex part; an exact one- or two-letter low-reward
suffix fills every sufficiently large remaining cost on the affine face.

For the exact finite-window model, put
\(R^\dagger(n)=R(n)+R(n-1)\).  Let
\[
  \widetilde d_m(n)=[z^n]\prod_{j=1}^m(1+z^{F_j}),
  \qquad 0\le n<F_{m+2}.
\]
For residues modulo \(F_{m+2}\), define
\[
  \pi_m(n)\equiv F_{m+1}n+F_{m+1}-1\pmod{F_{m+2}}.
\]

\begin{theoremB}[Affine fiber--partition correspondence]
For every \(m\ge1\), the map \(\pi_m\) is a permutation and
\[
  d_m^\#(\pi_m(n))
  =\widetilde d_m(n)
  =R^\dagger(n)-R^\dagger(n-F_{m+1})
  \qquad(0\le n<F_{m+2}).
\]
\end{theoremB}

This combines \Cref{thm:affine-transfer,thm:partition-difference}.  It gives a
pointwise correspondence, not only an equality of aggregate moments.

There is also a direct interval description:
\[
 d_m^\#(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 R(F_{m+2}+r),&0\le r\le F_{m+1}-2,\\
 R(r),&F_{m+1}-1\le r<F_{m+2}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The known extremal classification of Koc\'abov\'a, Mas\'akov\'a, and
Pelantov\'a can therefore be transferred exactly after an explicit change of
Fibonacci convention.

\begin{theoremC}[Interval identity and largest fibers]
Let \(D_m=\max_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)\).  Then
\[
 D_0=D_1=1,\qquad D_{2k}=F_{k+2}\ (k\ge0),\qquad
 D_{2k+1}=2F_{k+1}\ (k\ge1),
\]
and \(D_m=D_{m-2}+D_{m-4}\) for every \(m\ge6\).  The maximizing residues,
including the exceptional initial cases, are given in
\Cref{thm:unconditional-extremal-fibers}.
\end{theoremC}

For a real tilt \(t\), put
\[
  S_t(m)=\sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^t.
\]
The identity
\[
  \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log S_t(m)=0
  \qquad(t\le-\sigma_0)
\]
is recorded in \Cref{prop:negative-temperature-freezing} as a finite-layer
recovery of the frozen branch proved by Feng--Olivier and Feng
\cite{FengOlivier2003,Feng2005}.  The renewal coordinates evaluate the
boundary arithmetically and supply the finite-size consequences below.

The fixed-level information is sharper than the exponential pressure.  Put
\(C_m(k)=\card{\{x\in X_m:d_m(x)=k\}}\), and let \(\Psi(k)\) be Weinstein's
number of generating orbits at level \(k\).

\begin{theoremE}[Local spectrum and critical Gibbs law]
For \(m\ge1\), \(C_m(1)=2\), while for \(k\ge2\),
\[
 C_m(k)\le4\Psi(k),
 \qquad C_m(k)=4\Psi(k)\quad(m\ge2k).
\]
For every \(s>\sigma_0\),
\[
 S_{-s}(m)\longrightarrow
 \mathcal Z(s):=\frac4{2-\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)}-2.
\]
This is the exact convergence range for the unnormalized sums.  The function
\(\mathcal Z\) has a simple pole at \(\sigma_0\), and the corresponding
fiber-level Gibbs measures converge in total variation with explicit
critical logarithmic moments.
At the endpoint itself,
\[
 S_{-\sigma_0}(m)\sim\frac{4m}{\mu_C}.
\]
Under the finite-window Gibbs law proportional to
\(d_m(x)^{-\sigma_0}\), the normalized pair consisting of log multiplicity
and generating-layer cost converges in distribution to
\((v_cU,U)\), where \(U\) is uniform on \([0,1]\).
\end{theoremE}

The complete statements are
\Cref{thm:local-fiber-spectrum,thm:spectral-partition-function,thm:critical-gibbs-spectrum,thm:critical-finite-window-gibbs}.
They separate an unnormalized critical phenomenon inside the region where
the exponential pressure vanishes.

\begin{theoremF}[Orbit growth and arithmetic critical refinements]
With
\[
 \kappa=-\left.\frac{d}{ds}\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(s)}
 \right|_{s=\sigma_0},
\]
one has
\[
 \sum_{k\le x}\Psi(k)\sim
 \frac{x^{\sigma_0}}{\sigma_0\kappa}.
\]
Moreover, if
\(\beta_*=\log\varphi/\log(2/\varphi)=2.2705594539\ldots\), then
\[
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log S_t(m)>0
 \qquad(t>-\beta_*).
\]
At the known critical point \(t_c=-\sigma_0\), the renewal equation becomes
\[
 \sum_{q\ge2}\frac{\phi_{\rm E}(q)}{q^{\sigma_0}}=1,
 \qquad
 \frac{\zeta(\sigma_0-1)}{\zeta(\sigma_0)}=2,
\]
and the positive-phase one-sided derivative is the explicitly convergent
ratio
\[
 P'_+(t_c)=
 \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{q\ge2}\phi_{\rm E}(q)q^{-\sigma_0}\log q}
      {\displaystyle\sum_{q\ge2}\sum_{\substack{1\le p<q\\(p,q)=1}}
       c(p/q)q^{-\sigma_0}}.
\]
\end{theoremF}

The all-real existence, implicit branch, frozen phase, and derivative corner
come from Feng's spectrum theorem \cite[Thm.~1.3]{Feng2005} and the
Erd\H{o}s-measure phase transition of Feng--Olivier
\cite[Thms.~2.9--2.10 and App.~A]{FengOlivier2003}.  The theorem above retains
only the Tauberian orbit law, the totient evaluation of the critical equation,
and the one-sided critical moment in the present renewal coordinates.

Let \(\mathbf X_m\) be uniformly distributed on \(X_m\), and put
\[
 Y_m=m^{-1}\log d_m(\mathbf X_m).
\]

\begin{theoremG}[Full large deviations across the freezing corner]
The sequence \((Y_m)\) satisfies a large deviation principle with speed
\(m\) and good rate function
\[
 I(\alpha)=\sup_{t\in\RR}\{t\alpha-P(t)+\log\varphi\}.
\]
Its effective domain is \([0,\alpha_+]\), and
\[
 I(\alpha)=\log\varphi-\sigma_0\alpha
 \qquad(0\le\alpha\le v_c).
\]
For every \(0<\alpha<v_c\), the probabilities of shrinking neighborhoods
have the exact exponential rate \(-I(\alpha)\).
\end{theoremG}

The complete statement and proof are given in \Cref{thm:full-fiber-ldp}.
The new orbit-filling step fills both parities of every sufficiently large
target layer while preserving the source multiplicity.

For a fixed finite set of primes, \Cref{prop:prime-support-interface} gives
the exact joint generating function for multiplier exponents and generator
cost and transfers it to \(C_m(k)\) with the active boundary weights
\(4,3,1\).  We do not assert a directional coefficient or quenched-velocity
asymptotic: the needed parameter-uniform smooth-point estimate and uniform
integrability for the unbounded cost are not supplied by the cited ACSV
results.  \Cref{rem:prime-support-local-open-interface} records the exact
remaining analytic input.

The proof of \Cref{prop:negative-temperature-freezing} includes a separate
layer-count lemma transferring Weinstein's convention \(f_j=F_{j+1}\).  It
distinguishes the levels \(k=1,2\), endpoints, early layers, and the eventual
equality asserted in Weinstein's theorem.

The pressure corner prevents a direct full application of the
G\"artner--Ellis theorem: its lower-bound conclusion does not cover slopes
strictly between the one-sided derivatives at \(-\sigma_0\).  The
orbit-filling argument in \Cref{thm:full-fiber-ldp} supplies those missing
local lower bounds and proves the full large deviation principle, with an
affine rate on the coexistence interval.

\subsection{Contribution boundary}

For auditability, the dependency boundary is summarized before the detailed
discussion.
\noindent\textbf{Koc\'abov\'a--Mas\'akov\'a--Pelantov\'a.}
Imported: extremal values and maximizing integers for the ordinary Fibonacci
partition function.  Proved here from that input: exact convention and
interval transfer to all maximizing finite-window residues.

\noindent\textbf{Weinstein.}
Imported: the free generator monoid, multiplier product, orbit decomposition,
and eventual layer stabilization.  Proved here from that input: the uniform
early-layer bound, weighted cost renewal, exact finite-window transfer, and
the prime-support coefficient interface.

\noindent\textbf{Moshchevitin--Zhigljavsky; Dushistova.}
Imported: the Farey-tree entropy setting of Moshchevitin--Zhigljavsky and
Dushistova's local fixed-digit-sum continuant sum, its \(n^{-2\beta}\) order,
and her stated fuller expansion
\cite{MoshchevitinZhigljavsky2004,Dushistova2007}.  Proved here: the exact
identity \(b_{2d+1}(s)=\sigma_{s/2}(d+1)\), the endpoint correction from the
printed \(R_s+2R_s^2\) to \(2R_s^2\), a simplified context proof of the
corrected leading term, and its new critical renewal/Fibonacci transfer.  No
claim is made here about the other coefficients in Dushistova's expansion.

\noindent\textbf{Erd\H{o}s measure and Bernoulli convolutions.}
Sidorov--Vershik use Fibonacci numbers
\(F^{\rm SV}_1=1,F^{\rm SV}_2=2\), hence
\(F^{\rm SV}_j=F_{j+1}\) in our convention.  Their level-\(m\) frequency is
therefore exactly
\begin{align*}
 f_m^{\rm SV}(k)
 &=\#\left\{\boldsymbol\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^m:
   \sum_{j=1}^m\varepsilon_jF_j^{\rm SV}=k\right\}\\
 &=[z^k]\prod_{j=1}^m(1+z^{F_{j+1}})\\
 &=\#\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^m:N_m(\omega)=k\}.
\end{align*}
Thus the difference is the index shift \(F_j^{\rm SV}=F_{j+1}\), not a
different counting object.  They identify \(2^{-m}f_m^{\rm SV}(k)\) with the
finite masses approximating the Erd\H{o}s measure
\cite[Sec.~3]{SidorovVershik1998}.  Hu studied its golden-ratio local dimensions
\cite{Hu1997}; Lau--Ngai gave the exact \(L^q\)-spectrum for \(q>0\)
\cite{LauNgai1998}; Feng--Olivier and Feng gave the complete real spectrum and
the negative first-order transition \cite{FengOlivier2003,Feng2005}.  These
are imported results, not merely related background.

If \(\mu\) denotes the golden-ratio Bernoulli convolution and
\(\tau_\mu(t)\) uses the convention
\(\tau_\mu(t)=\lim \log\sum_I\mu(I)^t/\log|I|\), then the level masses are
\(2^{-m}\) times the finite Fibonacci representation counts.  Since the net
scale is \(\varphi^{-m}\), the exact exponential-rate dictionary is
\begin{equation}
 P(t)=t\log2-\tau_\mu(t)\log\varphi.
 \label{eq:erdos-pressure-dictionary}
\end{equation}
Thus the all-real pressure, its positive implicit branch, the negative frozen
branch, the first-order corner, and the generic Legendre multifractal
description are prior results.  The present paper adds the exact
  finite-window/fiber correspondences, evaluates Feng's implicit critical
  condition as \(\zeta(\sigma_0-1)/\zeta(\sigma_0)=2\), corrects Dushistova's
  printed local context constant and gives a simpler proof, transfers that
  corrected input to the stable second-order critical terms and uniform
  coexistence law, and proves the stated finite-layer nonexposed-slope lower
  bounds.

\noindent\textbf{Feller; Wiener--Ikehara; G\"artner--Ellis.}
Imported: classical renewal, Tauberian, and exposed-slope large-deviation

The residue characterization in \Cref{thm:residue-fibers} is a direct
restatement of the fold definition, and \Cref{thm:fourier-inversion} is the

\Cref{sec:residue-affine} are the explicit reversal--complement bijection and
affine residue permutation of \Cref{thm:affine-transfer}, together with the
pointwise fiber formula in \Cref{thm:partition-difference}; the latter follows

The values and maximizing residues in
\Cref{thm:unconditional-extremal-fibers} are not a new extremal classification:
they are the exact convention-and-interval transfer of Theorems~4.7 and~5.3 of

\cite{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005};
\Cref{cor:extremal-fiber-recurrence} is then an immediate Fibonacci-recurrence
consequence.  The additional ingredient in the negative-tilt argument is the
uniform early-layer estimate proved from Weinstein's orbit formulas.  That
estimate, which strengthens eventual layer stabilization to a bound valid in
every layer, yields \Cref{prop:negative-temperature-freezing}.  The local
microcanonical limit and the exact unnormalized
partition function in \Cref{sec:local-spectrum} combine this finite-window
two-layer identity with Weinstein's published orbit decomposition.  The
Wiener--Ikehara theorem itself is classical \cite{Korevaar2004};
\Cref{thm:psi-tauberian} is its application to the specific orbit Dirichlet
series, and \Cref{thm:pressure-transition-strip} is a separate finite-window
Jensen and maximum-level estimate.  Weinstein's free-monoid bijection,
multiplier multiplicativity, orbit decomposition, and largest-index formula
are published
  \cite[Prop.~3.3 and Thms.~3.8, 5.1, and 10.3]{Weinstein2016}; none of
  these mechanisms is claimed here.  The Stern--Brocot/Knauf denominator
  pressure is likewise published.  The weighted length renewal in
  \Cref{prop:weighted-generator-renewal} and its exact finite-window transfer
  are additional steps here; the resulting pressure is the
  Bernoulli-convolution spectrum under \eqref{eq:erdos-pressure-dictionary},
  not a new pressure theorem.  The corrected local denominator-layer theorem
  \Cref{thm:arithmetic-denominator-layer} is a separate arithmetic result; its
  proof does not assume the regular variation later used by the renewal
  transfer.  Its local sum and polynomial order are due to Dushistova; the
  correction of the leading coefficient and the simplified proof are the
  arithmetic contributions here.
The one-layer pressure, critical Gibbs law, and LDP in
\Cref{thm:standard-fibonacci-thermodynamics} are consequences for the
standard function \(R\) itself.  Their lower-bound proof uses the one-layer
orbit weights directly and does not infer componentwise behavior from the
two-layer fiber mixture.
The standard large-deviation upper bound and the lower bounds at exposed
slopes are separated in \Cref{thm:full-fiber-ldp} from the new finite-window
orbit-filling argument, which supplies the missing lower bounds at the
nonexposed coexistence slopes.
The marked critical renewal identity is a bivariate restatement of
\Cref{prop:weighted-generator-renewal}, whereas its conditional consequences
are additional finite-window results.  The arithmetic renewal theorem and
Blackwell's renewal theorem are classical inputs and are not reproved; the
stable second-order coefficients and uniform coexistence law are their
finite-window transfers through the exact boundary identities after the
critical cost tail has been proved arithmetically.

The separately compiled supplement keeps the conditional arithmetic and
integer-moment consequences outside the main theorem spine.  It labels the
moment constants as transfers of Sanna's theorem and the governing quadratic
cubic as already present in work of Chow and Jones \cite{ChowJones2024}.

\subsection{Related work}

The uniqueness theorem underlying the legal expansion is due to Zeckendorf
\cite{Zeckendorf1972}; Lekkerkerker's work provides classical background on
the same numeration system \cite{Lekkerkerker1952}.  Berstel developed the
normalization and automata viewpoint for Fibonacci representations
\cite{Berstel2001}.  The representation-counting function itself has been
studied through recurrence formulas, partition identities, extremal bounds,
and automata by Carlitz \cite{Carlitz1968}, Robbins \cite{Robbins1996},
Bicknell-Johnson and Fielder \cite{BicknellJohnsonFielder1999}, Stockmeyer
\cite{Stockmeyer2008}, Chow and Slattery \cite{ChowSlattery2021}, and Shallit
\cite{Shallit2021}.  Weinstein's orbit and level-set analysis
\cite{Weinstein2016} and the exact extremal classification of Koc\'abov\'a,
Mas\'akov\'a, and Pelantov\'a \cite{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005} are the
specific inputs to \Cref{sec:extremal-ldp}.  Sanna proved the power-sum
asymptotics used here and the endpoint law
\(\lim_{q\to\infty}\lambda_q^{1/q}=\varphi^{1/2}\)
\cite[Thms.~1.1 and~1.2]{Sanna2025}.  Zhou gives a broader treatment of
representation functions for related numeration systems and, in particular,
resolves the logarithmic-average conjecture of Chow and Slattery
\cite[Cor.~1.5]{Zhou2023}.
Chow and Jones obtained the same cubic that appears in the quadratic
finite-window recurrence in their analysis of the variance of the partition
function \cite{ChowJones2024}.
The probabilistic version of these finite counts is the Erd\H{o}s measure:
Sidorov and Vershik identify its level masses as normalized Fibonacci
representation frequencies \cite{SidorovVershik1998}.  Hu's local-dimension
analysis \cite{Hu1997}, Lau and Ngai's exact positive-\(q\) spectrum
\cite{LauNgai1998}, Feng and Olivier's weak-Gibbs phase-transition theorem
\cite{FengOlivier2003}, and Feng's explicit all-real golden-ratio formula
\cite{Feng2005} are the direct priority line for the pressure in this paper.
The Stern--Brocot pressure and its Knauf-model realization are taken from
Kesseb\"ohmer--Stratmann and Fiala--Kleban--\"Ozl\"uk
\cite{KessebohmerStratmann2007,FialaKlebanOzluk2003}.  The earlier
Stern--Brocot multifractal theorem of Kesseb\"ohmer and Stratmann
\cite{KessebohmerStratmann2004} concerns interval growth rates, rather than
Fibonacci partition multiplicities or the finite-window uniform law.
The Farey-tree entropy analysis of Moshchevitin and Zhigljavsky
\cite{MoshchevitinZhigljavsky2004} is the direct historical predecessor of
Dushistova's study of Brocot partitions.  Dushistova's auxiliary Theorem~3
\cite{Dushistova2007} defines
\(\sigma_\beta(n)=\sum_{\mathbf a\in\mathcal A_n^{\rm D}}
\mathcal K(\mathbf a)^{-2\beta}\), exactly the local continuant sum used here,
and states a fuller expansion.  \Cref{prop:dushistova-exact-identification}
compares the conventions term by term.  Her printed leading coefficient is
\(R_s+2R_s^2\); \Cref{lem:dushistova-endpoint-correction} localizes the loss of
the restriction \(u>1\), causing an endpoint overcount, to the proof of her
Lemma~7.  The empty left context is included in the doubled canonical
convolution, so that endpoint is counted twice and the excess is exactly
\(R_s\); the corrected coefficient is \(2R_s^2\).  The claim is deliberately
narrow: it concerns that leading coefficient, not Dushistova's paper as a
whole.
The critical continued-fraction moment uses the fixed-denominator mean theorem of Panov and
Liehl, as recorded in the quantitative modern treatment of
Aistleitner--Borda--Hauke
\cite{Panov1982,Liehl1983,AistleitnerBordaHauke2023}.
