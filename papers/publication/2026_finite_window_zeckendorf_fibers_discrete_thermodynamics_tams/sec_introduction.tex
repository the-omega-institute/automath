\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Let \(F_0=0\), \(F_1=F_2=1\), and
\(F_{j+2}=F_{j+1}+F_j\).  A word
\(\omega\in\{0,1\}^m\) determines the raw sum
\[
  N_m(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m\omega_jF_{j+1}.
\]
We consider the finite-window fold obtained by reducing this sum modulo
\(F_{m+2}\) and taking the unique legal Zeckendorf word in the visible
window.  If \(d_m(x)\) is the cardinality of the fiber over a legal word
\(x\), the basic problem is to identify the finite multiset
\(\{d_m(x):x\in X_m\}\) and its extremal and moment statistics.

The paper is centered on two exact correspondences with the classical
Fibonacci partition function.  The first is an affine reindexing of a finite
subset-sum spectrum; the second expresses the complete fiber list as two
consecutive blocks of ordinary partition values.  These correspondences are
finite identities and do not require a normalization transducer.

\subsection{Main results}

Let \(R(n)\) be the number of partitions of \(n\) into distinct Fibonacci
numbers when the two unit weights are not distinguished, with \(R(n)=0\) for
\(n<0\), and put \(R^\dagger(n)=R(n)+R(n-1)\).  Let
\[
  \widetilde d_m(n)=[z^n]\prod_{j=1}^m(1+z^{F_j}),
  \qquad 0\le n<F_{m+2}.
\]
For residues modulo \(F_{m+2}\), define
\[
  \pi_m(n)\equiv F_{m+1}n+F_{m+1}-1\pmod{F_{m+2}}.
\]

\begin{theoremA}[Affine fiber--partition correspondence]
For every \(m\ge1\), the map \(\pi_m\) is a permutation and
\[
  d_m^\#(\pi_m(n))
  =\widetilde d_m(n)
  =R^\dagger(n)-R^\dagger(n-F_{m+1})
  \qquad(0\le n<F_{m+2}).
\]
\end{theoremA}

This combines \Cref{thm:affine-transfer,thm:partition-difference}.  It gives a
pointwise correspondence, not only an equality of aggregate moments.

There is also a direct interval description:
\[
 d_m^\#(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 R(F_{m+2}+r),&0\le r\le F_{m+1}-2,\\
 R(r),&F_{m+1}-1\le r<F_{m+2}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The known extremal classification of Koc\'abov\'a, Mas\'akov\'a, and
Pelantov\'a can therefore be transferred exactly after an explicit change of
Fibonacci convention.

\begin{theoremB}[Interval identity and largest fibers]
Let \(D_m=\max_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)\).  Then
\[
 D_0=D_1=1,\qquad D_{2k}=F_{k+2}\ (k\ge0),\qquad
 D_{2k+1}=2F_{k+1}\ (k\ge1),
\]
and \(D_m=D_{m-2}+D_{m-4}\) for every \(m\ge6\).  The maximizing residues,
including the exceptional initial cases, are given in
\Cref{thm:unconditional-extremal-fibers}.
\end{theoremB}

For a real tilt \(t\), put
\[
  S_t(m)=\sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^t.
\]
The level sets of \(R\) are sufficiently sparse to produce a frozen
half-line.  Let \(\sigma_0>2\) be the unique solution of
\[
  \frac{\zeta(\sigma_0-1)}{\zeta(\sigma_0)}=2;
  \qquad \sigma_0=2.4787507857\ldots.
\]

\begin{theoremC}[Negative-temperature freezing]
For every real \(t\le-\sigma_0\),
\[
  \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log S_t(m)=0.
\]
Consequently these pressure limits cannot be represented on all real tilts by
the logarithm of a positive simple isolated eigenvalue of an analytic compact
operator family on a fixed Banach space.
\end{theoremC}

This theorem certifies the frozen region \(t\le-\sigma_0\).  It does not prove
that \(-\sigma_0\) is the exact boundary of the zero-pressure set; in
particular, no positivity assertion is made for \(t> -\sigma_0\).

The proof of \Cref{thm:negative-temperature-freezing} includes a separate
layer-count lemma transferring Weinstein's convention \(f_j=F_{j+1}\).  It
distinguishes the levels \(k=1,2\), endpoints, early layers, and the eventual
equality asserted in Weinstein's theorem.  The compact-operator conclusion is
stated precisely in \Cref{prop:no-analytic-compact-representation} and follows
from analytic perturbation theory.

Two further comparisons are useful.  First, for every positive integer
\(q\), the fiber moment \(S_q(m)\) is trapped between two adjacent Fibonacci
windows of the partition power sum.  Sanna's theorem therefore gives
\(S_q(m)\asymp_q\lambda_q^m\).  Sanna also proved
\(\lambda_q^{1/q}\to\varphi^{1/2}\); our norm squeeze is recorded only as an
alternative finite-window proof.  The additional diagonal consequence is
\[
  \frac{1}{m q_m}\log S_{q_m}(m)
  \longrightarrow\frac12\log\varphi
\]
for every sequence of positive integers \(q_m\to\infty\); the same squeeze
also applies to real tilts \(t_m\to\infty\).  Second, the quadratic moment has
the independently proved recurrence
\[
  S_2(m)=2S_2(m-1)+2S_2(m-2)-2S_2(m-3)\qquad(m\ge4).
\]

The residual pressure problem on \(( -\sigma_0,\infty)\) remains open.  We
state the full real-tilt large deviation principle only as a conditional
corollary under explicit existence, differentiability, and curvature
hypotheses; it is not counted among the principal results.

\subsection{Contribution boundary}

The residue characterization in \Cref{thm:residue-fibers} is a direct
restatement of the fold definition, and \Cref{thm:fourier-inversion} is the
standard character-orthogonality formula.  The finite-window contributions in
\Cref{sec:residue-affine} are the explicit reversal--complement bijection and
affine residue permutation of \Cref{thm:affine-transfer}, together with the
pointwise fiber formula in \Cref{thm:partition-difference}; the latter follows
by truncating the indexed Fibonacci partition generating function.  The
two-interval fiber identity in \Cref{thm:fiber-partition-interval} is also
specific to the finite-window fold.

The values and maximizing residues in
\Cref{thm:unconditional-extremal-fibers} are not a new extremal classification:
they are the exact convention-and-interval transfer of Theorems~4.7 and~5.3 of
Koc\'abov\'a, Mas\'akov\'a, and Pelantov\'a
\cite{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005};
\Cref{cor:extremal-fiber-recurrence} is then an immediate Fibonacci-recurrence
consequence.  The additional ingredient in the negative-tilt argument is the
uniform early-layer estimate proved from Weinstein's orbit formulas.  That
estimate, which strengthens eventual layer stabilization to a bound valid in
every layer, yields \Cref{thm:negative-temperature-freezing};
\Cref{prop:no-analytic-compact-representation} is its analytic-perturbation
consequence.

In the moment theory, \Cref{thm:difference-power-sums} is a reindexing of the
pointwise formula, and \Cref{thm:fibonacci-window-sandwich} is an elementary
truncation bound.  The asymptotic constants in \Cref{thm:all-q-transfer} are a
direct transfer of Sanna's theorem.  The endpoint argument in
\Cref{cor:renyi-endpoint} is an alternative proof of Sanna's endpoint law,
while \Cref{thm:diagonal-high-moments} is a diagonal consequence of the same norm
squeeze.  The independent contribution in the quadratic case is the direct
finite-window derivation of \Cref{thm:cubic-recurrence}; the governing cubic
was already present in work of Chow and Jones \cite{ChowJones2024}.

\subsection{Related work}

The uniqueness theorem underlying the legal expansion is due to Zeckendorf
\cite{Zeckendorf1972}; Lekkerkerker's work provides classical background on
the same numeration system \cite{Lekkerkerker1952}.  Berstel developed the
normalization and automata viewpoint for Fibonacci representations
\cite{Berstel2001}.  The representation-counting function itself has been
studied through recurrence formulas, partition identities, extremal bounds,
and automata by Carlitz \cite{Carlitz1968}, Robbins \cite{Robbins1996},
Bicknell-Johnson and Fielder \cite{BicknellJohnsonFielder1999}, Stockmeyer
\cite{Stockmeyer2008}, Chow and Slattery \cite{ChowSlattery2021}, and Shallit
\cite{Shallit2021}.  Weinstein's orbit and level-set analysis
\cite{Weinstein2016} and the exact extremal classification of Koc\'abov\'a,
Mas\'akov\'a, and Pelantov\'a \cite{KocabovaMasakovaPelantova2005} are the
specific inputs to \Cref{sec:extremal-ldp}.  Sanna proved the power-sum
asymptotics used here and the endpoint law
\(\lim_{q\to\infty}\lambda_q^{1/q}=\varphi^{1/2}\)
\cite[Thms.~1.1 and~1.2]{Sanna2025}; Zhou gives a broader recent treatment of
representation functions for related numeration systems \cite{Zhou2023}.
Chow and Jones obtained the same cubic that appears in the quadratic
finite-window recurrence in their analysis of the variance of the partition
function \cite{ChowJones2024}.

\subsection{Organization}

\Cref{sec:preliminaries} defines the finite-window fold and fixes notation.
\Cref{sec:residue-affine} proves the residue, affine, and partition-difference
formulas.  \Cref{sec:extremal-ldp} proves the interval identity, transfers the
extremal classification, establishes the freezing theorem and compact-operator
obstruction, and states the conditional large deviation corollary.
\Cref{sec:second-collision} proves the moment transfer and the quadratic
recurrence.  \Cref{sec:integer-pressure} records Sanna's endpoint law with an
alternative proof and isolates the diagonal high-tilt consequence.
