\begin{abstract}
Let \(R(n)\) be the number of partitions of \(n\) into distinct Fibonacci
numbers, with the two unit terms identified.  On the standard Fibonacci layer
\(\mathcal I_m=[F_{m+1}-1,F_{m+2}-1)\), we determine the all-real moment
pressure of \(R\).  If \(\sigma_0>2\) is defined by
\(\zeta(\sigma_0-1)/\zeta(\sigma_0)=2\), this pressure is zero for
\(t\le-\sigma_0\), positive, real analytic, and strictly convex for
\(t>-\sigma_0\), and has a first-order corner at the endpoint.  At criticality
the one-layer partition sum is asymptotic to \(2m/\mu_C\), and its Gibbs law
converges to a uniform mixture of generating-layer fractions.  Under the
uniform law on \(\mathcal I_m\), \(m^{-1}\log R(n)\) satisfies a full large
deviation principle; the rate is affine on the coexistence interval.  The
proof uses a weighted renewal identity for Weinstein's free generators,
published Stern--Brocot denominator pressure, and a parity-free orbit-filling
argument for the nonexposed slopes.

A finite-window Zeckendorf fold provides an exact finite model for the same
spectrum: its fiber list is the list of \(R\) on two consecutive layers.
An affine permutation also identifies each fiber with a finite Fibonacci
subset-sum coefficient and a partition-function difference.  This yields the
exact largest fibers
\(D_{2k}=F_{k+2}\), \(D_{2k+1}=2F_{k+1}\), and
\(D_m=D_{m-2}+D_{m-4}\), including every maximizing residue.  The two-layer
model has the same pressure and large-deviation rate, an exact frozen
Dirichlet limit, and a critical finite-size constant \(4/\mu_C\).  The frozen
half-line also rules out a globally analytic compact-operator representation
under precise spectral hypotheses.  On every fixed finite prime support we
also determine the directional quenched velocity and transfer it exactly to
the finite window below the active cost threshold.
\end{abstract}
