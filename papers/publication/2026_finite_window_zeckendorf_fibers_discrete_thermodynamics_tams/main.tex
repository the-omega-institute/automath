\begin{abstract}
We study the fibers of a finite-window map that sends a binary Fibonacci sum
to the legal word representing its residue modulo the next Fibonacci number.
An explicit affine permutation identifies the fiber multiplicities with the
coefficients of a finite Fibonacci subset-sum polynomial and, pointwise, with
a Fibonacci-lag difference of the classical partition function.  A second
identity identifies the complete fiber list with values of that partition
function on two consecutive intervals.  The known extremal classification on
these intervals then gives
\(D_{2k}=F_{k+2}\), \(D_{2k+1}=2F_{k+1}\), and
\(D_m=D_{m-2}+D_{m-4}\) for \(m\ge6\), together with all maximizing
residues.  For integer \(q\ge1\), the fiber power sums have the same
exponential constants as the partition power sums studied by Sanna; in the
quadratic case we give a direct cubic recurrence.  For real negative tilts we
prove a Dirichlet-series freezing bound.  If \(\sigma_0>2\) is determined by
\(\zeta(\sigma_0-1)/\zeta(\sigma_0)=2\), then
\(\lim_{m\to\infty}m^{-1}\log\sum_x d_m(x)^t=0\) for every
\(t\le-\sigma_0\).  The endpoint proof uses an explicit transfer of
Weinstein's orbit formulas, including the exceptional levels and early
layers.  At the finer, unnormalized scale, the fixed multiplicity counts
stabilize and the negative-tilt sums converge exactly to
\(4(2-\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s))^{-1}-2\) for \(s>\sigma_0\), with a simple pole
at \(\sigma_0\) and a total-variation Gibbs limit.  A Wiener--Ikehara
argument gives the associated generating-orbit asymptotic.  A weighted renewal
identity for Weinstein's free generators, combined with the published
Stern--Brocot denominator pressure, proves that the pressure exists for every
real tilt.  It is real analytic and strictly convex on
\((-\sigma_0,\infty)\), but has a first-order corner at \(-\sigma_0\).
Exactly at that corner the unnormalized partition function grows linearly,
and the critical finite-window Gibbs law converges to a uniform mixture of
coexisting layer fractions; its log multiplicity is locked to the layer
fraction by the critical velocity.
An orbit-filling construction closes the nonexposed slopes at this corner:
the uniform log-fiber thickness satisfies a full large deviation principle,
whose rate is affine on the coexistence interval.
Conditioning on a fixed microcanonical band inside that interval produces a
sharper phase separation: the log multiplicity concentrates at the upper band
edge and the generating-layer proportion converges to that edge divided by
the critical velocity.  The conclusion persists for shrinking bands with
positive limiting lattice width, while a family of heavy generator letters
gives a polynomial conditional upper tail.
At exact dyadic multiplicities, an arithmetic renewal law gives the quenched
generator-cost velocity and its finite-window transfer under a positive layer
margin.  An explicit interval certificate proves that this dyadic velocity is
strictly larger than the critical annealed velocity, furnishing realizable
exact multiplier sequences on which the critical band speed fails.
The frozen half-line rules out a globally analytic compact-operator
representation under precise spectral hypotheses.
\end{abstract}
