\section{Finite serial coordinates at collision}

The next proposition retains only the serial tail facts needed for the
statistical experiment.  It is not a statement about general phase-type
representations or hidden-realization geometry.

\begin{proposition}[Confluent sampled-tail coordinates]
\label{prop:serial-tail-coordinates-main}
Fix \(n\ge2\), known \(\Delta\tau>0\), and positive rates
\(\theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n)\), with repetitions allowed.  If the
distinct rates are \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\), with multiplicities
\(m_1,\ldots,m_r\), and \(z_j=e^{-\lambda_j\Delta\tau}\), then the sampled
survival tail in \textup{(1.1)} has the confluent exponential-polynomial form
\[
 S_\theta(k)=\sum_{j=1}^r\sum_{\ell=0}^{m_j-1}
 c_{j\ell}k^\ell z_j^k,qquad c_{j,m_j-1}\ne0.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Its minimal recurrence polynomial is
\[
 \chi_\theta(x)=\prod_{j=1}^r(x-z_j)^{m_j}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
The leading \(n\times n\) Hankel matrix
\((S_\theta(i+j))_{0\le i,j<n}\) is invertible.  Consequently
\(S_\theta(0),\ldots,S_\theta(2n-1)\) determine \(\chi_\theta\), the
collision multiplicities, and the unordered sampled-rate multiset
\(\{\!\{z_1,\ldots,z_1,\ldots,z_r,\ldots,z_r\}\!\}\); known
\(\Delta\tau\) then recovers the unordered rate multiset.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Laplace transform of \(W=X_1+\cdots+X_n\) and that of its survival
function are
\[
 \EE e^{-sW}=\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{\theta_i}{s+\theta_i},\qquad
 \int_0^\infty e^{-st}\PP(W>t)\,dt
 =\frac{1-\prod_{i=1}^n\theta_i/(s+\theta_i)}s.
\tag{2.3}
\]
The second transform has at \(-\lambda_j\) a pole of exact order \(m_j\).
Partial-fraction inversion and sampling at \(t=k\Delta\tau\) give
\textup{(2.1)} with nonzero top confluent coefficient.  Standard confluent
Prony algebra gives the exact minimal annihilator \textup{(2.2)}
\cite{KunisRoemerVonDerOhe2020Prony}.

It remains to justify the finite-coordinate claim at repeated roots.  Let
\(\mathcal O(Q)\) have successive rows \(e_n^\top Q^j\), and let
\(\mathcal C(Q)\) have successive columns \(Q^j\mathbf1\), for
\(0\le j<n\).  Both are nonsingular because the nonzero bidiagonal products
are exposed triangularly.  The exponential is injective on the real spectrum
of \(Q\), so Hermite interpolation gives a polynomial \(p\) satisfying
\(Q=p(e^{\Delta\tau Q})\).  The corresponding observation and reachability
spaces for \(K=e^{\Delta\tau Q}\) are therefore full.  Hence
\[
 (S_\theta(i+j))_{0\le i,j<n}=\mathcal O(K)\mathcal C(K)
\tag{2.4}
\]
is invertible.  The first \(2n\) tails uniquely solve the order-\(n\)
recurrence.  Its roots and algebraic multiplicities recover
\(\lambda_j=-\Delta\tau^{-1}\log z_j\) and \(m_j\), proving the claim.
\end{proof}
