\begin{abstract}
We prove local asymptotic equivalence, in Le Cam distance, between a
deterministic window of a stationary lattice renewal indicator and an
undershot fixed-size independent sample from its Palm interarrival law.  The
comparison is two-sided and is implemented by explicit parameter-free Markov
kernels.  It transfers bounded-loss risks and converts Palm information and
semiparametric efficiency bounds to calendar time through the mean cycle
length.

For a sampled serial generalized-Erlang renewal model, the regular coordinate
at a double pole is the squared half-separation
\(\delta=\{(\theta_1-\theta_2)/2\}^2\).  A pole-order argument proves that its
score is not absorbed by the nuisance scores: the collision, centre, and
separated-rate derivatives have respectively fourth-, third-, and second-order
poles.  The stationary experiment is uniformly locally asymptotically normal
and converges to a Gaussian shift on
\(\mathbb R^{n-1}\times[0,\infty)\).  The residualized score test attains the
one-sided local power envelope.  A measurable recurrence estimator attains
\(N^{-1/4}\) recovery of the unordered colliding rates, and a fixed-base
two-point threshold lower bound proves optimality in rate.

For an isolated pole of arbitrary multiplicity \(m\ge3\), the local
experiment at the fourth-root splitting scale collapses the cluster onto its
quadratic dispersion.  This gives the efficient power envelope for testing
an \(m\)-fold collision.  Equal-dispersion cluster shapes are asymptotically
indistinguishable in total variation, which proves that the scaled shape is
not consistently estimable and that the fourth-root multiset rate remains
optimal.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} asymptotic equivalence; deficiency; stationary
renewal process; singular LAN; generalized-Erlang distribution; pole
collision; minimax rate

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction-main}

A stationary renewal record observed through a deterministic calendar window
does not consist of a deterministic number of complete cycles.  The first and
last cycles are censored, the number of intervening cycles is random, and a
window may contain no renewal.  These features are harmless for many
first-order calculations, but they matter when an i.i.d. Palm argument is used
as a substitute for the actual experiment.  Conditioning a prescribed number
of cycles to fit in the window is not legitimate: it changes their joint law.

Our first result removes that boundary-cycle problem at the level of
statistical experiments.  Given a stationary renewal window of length \(N\),
one kernel extracts the first
\(m_N=\lfloor N/\mu_0-N^\gamma\rfloor\) complete interarrivals, where
\(1/2<\gamma<1\).  In the reverse direction, a kernel uses the supplied Palm
sample and appends base-law cycles after it is exhausted.  The two couplings
fail only if the record contains too few complete cycles, or if generation in
the reverse direction needs more than the supplied cycles plus a short
base-law continuation.  Hellinger locality controls that continuation.  Both
deficiency directions therefore vanish uniformly, without conditioning Palm
gaps on fitting inside the window.

The equivalence theorem is useful beyond a parametric model.  Bounded-loss
procedures transfer in both directions, information per calendar unit is Palm
information divided by mean cycle length, and an i.i.d. canonical-gradient
variance is multiplied by mean cycle length.  The same statements remain
valid for a fixed local cone or constrained local subset, with efficiency and
minimax bounds interpreted in that restricted experiment.  This qualification
is essential at a singular boundary.

Our first application is the collision of two rates in a sampled serial
generalized-Erlang renewal law.  If independent exponential holding times have
rates \(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n\), their sum is sampled at interval
\(\Delta\tau\).  At \(\theta_1=\theta_2=\lambda\), the sign of the rate split
is invisible.  The smooth quotient coordinate is
\[
 c=\frac{\theta_1+\theta_2}{2},\qquad
 \delta=\left(\frac{\theta_1-\theta_2}{2}\right)^2\ge0.
\]
Root-\(N\) inference for \(\delta\) therefore becomes fourth-root recovery of
the unordered pair \(\{\!\{c-\sqrt\delta,c+\sqrt\delta\}\!\}\).

Three ingredients make the singular conclusion statistically complete.
First, the sampled survival tail is a confluent exponential-polynomial
sequence whose minimal recurrence and nonsingular leading Hankel matrix give
regular finite-dimensional quotient coordinates even at collision.  Second,
the collision derivative has an exact fourth-order pole, whereas the centre
derivative has order three and each separated-rate derivative has its own
order-two pole.  After sampling, their distinguishing components are
\(k^3e^{-\lambda\Delta\tau k}\),
\(k^2e^{-\lambda\Delta\tau k}\), and
\(ke^{-\theta_j\Delta\tau k}\).  This proves positive information rather
than merely population identifiability.  Third, a measurable finite-atlas
recurrence estimator estimates the regular cluster coordinates at the
root-\(N\) rate; projection of \(\delta\) onto \([0,\infty)\) and a square
root yield the attainable \(N^{-1/4}\) multiset rate.  A two-point threshold
argument supplies the matching lower rate.

The same pole-order mechanism reveals a different phenomenon when \(m\ge3\)
rates collide.  Under centred \(N^{-1/4}\) splits, only the quadratic
dispersion enters the root-\(N\) likelihood; all higher symmetric coordinates
fall below local statistical resolution.  The resulting Gaussian half-line
experiment yields an efficient omnibus collision test.  Two nonpermutation
splits with the same dispersion have stationary-window laws converging in
total variation, so their local cluster shapes cannot be consistently
recovered even though the multiset remains estimable at the fourth-root rate.

\Cref{sec:renewal-equivalence-main} proves the general experiment equivalence.
\Cref{sec:serial-coordinates-main,sec:collision-experiment-main,sec:main-proofs-main} develop
the collision coordinates and derive the singular limit experiment, efficient
test, and recovery rate for a double pole.  \Cref{sec:multiple-collision-main}
identifies the collapsed experiment and shape obstruction at higher
multiplicity.

Stationary renewal censoring has been studied through its distinct sampling
patterns and Fisher information \cite{GillKeiding2010RenewalSampling,
ZhaoNagaraja2011WindowCensoredRenewal}.  Our first theorem strengthens the
information comparison to two-sided local experiment equivalence and
decision-theoretic transfer.  General Le Cam theory provides the language for
deficiency and local minimax risk \cite{LeCamYang2000}, while ordinary LAN and
Gaussian-shift optimality provide the regular local comparison
\cite{vanDerVaart1998AsymptoticStatistics}.

The boundary coordinate connects the application to locally conic and
loss-of-identifiability theory
\cite{DacunhaCastelleGassiat1997LocallyConic,LiuShao2003LossIdentifiability}
and to classical boundary likelihood theory
\cite{Chernoff1954LikelihoodRatio,SelfLiang1987NonstandardLRT}.  The serial
tail algebra is a finite-coordinate sampled specialization of
generalized-Erlang and Coxian theory
\cite{HeZhang2008MinimalCoxian,HeZhang2009GeneralizedErlang}, with confluent
Prony recovery as an algebraic antecedent
\cite{BatenkovYomdin2013ConfluentProny,KunisRoemerVonDerOhe2020Prony}.
Finite-mixture work shows that collision rates depend on the particular
singularity structure
\cite{Chen1995FiniteMixtureRate,HeinrichKahn2018FiniteMixtureMinimax,
HoNguyen2019SingularityStructures}.

\subsection*{Observation model and notation}

Let \(W=X_1+\cdots+X_n\), where the \(X_i\) are independent exponentials
with positive rates \(\theta_i\).  For \(\Delta\tau>0\), define the
integer zero-run length \(G\) by
\begin{equation}
 S_\theta(k)=\PP_\theta\{W>k\Delta\tau\},\qquad
 g_\theta(k)=S_\theta(k)-S_\theta(k+1),\qquad k\ge0,
\label{eq:main-observation-gap}
\end{equation}
and the interrenewal length \(D=G+1\), with
\(\mu_\theta=\EE_\theta D=\sum_{k\ge0}S_\theta(k)\).
The visible observation is the stationary binary renewal indicator: a one at
each renewal epoch and zeros otherwise.

Equivalently, in the serial positive realization, let
\begin{equation}
 Q(\theta)_{ii}=-\theta_i,\qquad
 Q(\theta)_{i,i-1}=\theta_i\quad(2\le i\le n),
\label{eq:main-killed-generator}
\end{equation}
with all other entries zero, and put
\begin{equation}
 K_\theta=e^{\Delta\tau Q(\theta)},\qquad
 T_{1,\theta}=(I-K_\theta)\mathbf1e_n^\top.
\label{eq:main-killed-kernel}
\end{equation}
The rank-one marked kernel resets after every renewal and gives the Palm law
in \eqref{eq:main-observation-gap}.

For two finite multisets \(A=\{\!\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\!\}\) and
\(B=\{\!\{b_1,\ldots,b_n\}\!\}\), write
\begin{equation}
 d_{\rm m}(A,B)=\min_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_n}
 \max_{1\le i\le n}|a_i-b_{\pi(i)}|
\label{eq:main-matching-distance}
\end{equation}
for matching distance, retaining multiplicities.
