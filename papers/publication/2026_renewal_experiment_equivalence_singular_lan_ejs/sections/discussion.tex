\section{Discussion and relation to prior work}

Two-sided deficiency convergence strengthens the classical calendar-time
information calculation for stationary renewal sampling
\cite{GillKeiding2010RenewalSampling,ZhaoNagaraja2011WindowCensoredRenewal}.
It shows that the Palm and stationary-window experiments have asymptotically
the same bounded-loss decision problems, not merely matching Fisher
information.  The effective sample-size conversion then yields Palm
information divided by mean cycle length and multiplies an i.i.d.
canonical-gradient covariance by that mean.

At the collision boundary, the ambient DQM chart determines the information
matrix while the half-space \(\RR^{n-1}\times[0,\infty)\) determines the
decision problem.  This distinction explains both the one-sided Gaussian
power envelope and the need to interpret efficiency and minimax bounds inside
the fixed constrained experiment
\cite{Chernoff1954LikelihoodRatio,SelfLiang1987NonstandardLRT}.

Locally conic and loss-of-identifiability theory predicts a quotient
coordinate and a boundary limit
\cite{DacunhaCastelleGassiat1997LocallyConic,LiuShao2003LossIdentifiability},
but positive collision information remains model-specific.  Here the exact
fourth-order collision pole cannot be synthesized from the order-three centre
pole or the separated order-two poles.  Sampling preserves the corresponding
exponential-polynomial separation, so the efficient-information Schur
complement stays positive on the compact stratum.

The recurrence construction connects this statistical calculation with
confluent Prony algebra
\cite{BatenkovYomdin2013ConfluentProny,KunisRoemerVonDerOhe2020Prony}.  It
estimates symmetric coordinates of the two-root cluster instead of labelling
the colliding roots.  Root-\(N\) estimation of the squared half-separation,
followed by projection and a square root, gives the fourth-root multiset rate;
the fixed-base two-point experiment shows that this rate cannot be improved
locally.  This mechanism is consistent with the broader lesson from finite
mixtures that collision rates depend on the singularity structure
\cite{Chen1995FiniteMixtureRate,HeinrichKahn2018FiniteMixtureMinimax,
HoNguyen2019SingularityStructures}.

Fourth-root scales also occur in super-resolution, but under different
observation operators, noise laws, parameter spaces, and losses.  Results for
bandlimited data with deterministic error or for blurred-source microscopy
therefore describe related singular geometry rather than the renewal-window
experiment studied here
\cite{BatenkovGoldmanYomdin2021SuperResolution,
KulaitisMunkWerner2021Resolution}.

The present application treats one isolated double pole in a fixed-order
serial model at known sampling interval; multiple collisions, unknown order
or interval, and nonserial phase-type realizations require different local
coordinates and score-separation arguments.
