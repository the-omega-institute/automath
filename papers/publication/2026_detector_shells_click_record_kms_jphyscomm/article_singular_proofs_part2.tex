\subsection{Pole-order nondegeneracy and the fixed-order serial proof}
\label{subsec:serial-pole-order-proof-main}

\begin{lemma}[Sampled isolated-double-pole nondegeneracy]
\label{lem:sampled-double-pole-nondegeneracy-main}
On \(\mathcal C_n(\varepsilon,M,c_0)\), the Palm gap family is analytic in
\((\eta,\delta)\) on a fixed one-sided neighbourhood of \(\delta=0\), obeys
the bounds in \textup{(SL1)}, and its one-cycle score coordinates are linearly
independent.  Consequently \textup{(SC16)} holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(W=X_1+\cdots+X_n\), where the independent exponential holding times
have rates \(c+\sqrt\delta,c-\sqrt\delta,\theta_3,\ldots,\theta_n\), and put
\[
 B_\eta(s)=\prod_{j=3}^n\frac{\theta_j}{s+\theta_j}.
\]
The empty product is one.  The Laplace transform of the continuous survival
function \(\overline F_{\eta,\delta}(t)=P(W>t)\) is
\[
 H_{\eta,\delta}(s)=\frac1s\left\{1-
 \frac{c^2-\delta}{(s+c)^2-\delta}B_\eta(s)\right\}.
\tag{P1}
\]
At a base point with \(c=\lambda\), direct differentiation gives
\[
 \partial_\delta H_{\eta,0}(s)
 =B_\eta(s)\frac{s+2\lambda}{(s+\lambda)^4},
 \qquad
 \partial_cH_{\eta,0}(s)
 =-\frac{2\lambda B_\eta(s)}{(s+\lambda)^3},
\tag{P2}
\]
and, for \(j\ge3\),
\[
 \partial_{\theta_j}H_{\eta,0}(s)
 =-\frac{\lambda^2}{(s+\lambda)^2}
 \frac{1}{(s+\theta_j)^2}
 \prod_{\substack{\ell=3\\\ell\ne j}}^n
 \frac{\theta_\ell}{s+\theta_\ell}.
\tag{P3}
\]
The displayed expression follows after differentiating
\(\theta_j/(s+\theta_j)\) and cancelling its factor \(s\) against the outer
\(1/s\); all remaining numerator factors are nonzero at the relevant
separated poles.

Uniform separation makes \(B_\eta(-\lambda)\ne0\).  Thus
\(\partial_\delta H\) has an exact order-four pole at \(-\lambda\), with
nonzero leading coefficient \(\lambda B_\eta(-\lambda)\), while
\(\partial_cH\) has order three there.  At \(-\theta_j\), the derivative in
\(\theta_j\) has order two, and every other coordinate derivative has at most
order one there.  Inverse Laplace transformation therefore gives the following
distinguishing sampled terms:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{coordinate}&\text{term not attainable by the remaining coordinates}\ \\
 \hline
 \delta& k^3e^{-\lambda\Delta\tau k}\\
 c& k^2e^{-\lambda\Delta\tau k}\quad\text{after the \(\delta\) coefficient is zero}\\
 \theta_j& k e^{-\theta_j\Delta\tau k}\quad(3\le j\le n).
 \end{array}
\tag{P4}
\]
All leading coefficients in \textup{(P4)} are nonzero.

Suppose \(a^\top\dot\ell_{\eta,\delta}=0\) in
\(L^2(g_{\eta,0})\).  Full support gives
\(a^\top\partial g_{\eta,0}(k)=0\) for every \(k\).  Absolute summability
then gives the same identity for the tail derivatives.  Exponential-polynomial
sequences at distinct bases are linearly independent: applying successively
the difference operators \(E-z_j\), or equivalently using their rational
generating functions, separates each base and its highest polynomial degree.
The cubic term at \(e^{-\lambda\Delta\tau}\) first forces
\(a_\delta=0\); the unique linear term at every separated base forces
\(a_{\theta_j}=0\); and the remaining quadratic term at the collision base
forces \(a_c=0\).  The scores are therefore linearly independent.

It remains to record uniform analytic control.  Formula \textup{(P1)} is
rational and analytic in \((\eta,\delta)\) on a common complex neighbourhood
whose pole clusters remain separated by compactness and \(c_0\).  Partial
fractions, including the confluent pole at \(-c\), give for every fixed
derivative order
\[
 \sup_{(\eta,\delta)}
 |\partial_{\eta,\delta}^a S_{\eta,\delta}(k)|
 \le C_a(1+k)^{m_a}e^{-\varepsilon\Delta\tau k/2}.
\tag{P5}
\]
First differences give the same bound for \(g\).  Ratios by \(S\) or \(g\)
have polynomial growth uniformly: this follows directly from the finite
confluent expansion on the separated pole clusters, and also from the
conditional holding-time score representation of these derivatives.  Hence
the log derivatives through order three satisfy \textup{(SL1)}.  Dominated
summation makes the information continuous.  Pointwise positive definiteness,
continuity, and compactness yield the strictly positive uniform minimum in
\textup{(SC16)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform quotient estimator from sampled tails]
\label{lem:serial-collision-recurrence-estimator-main}
Under the restrictions of \Cref{thm:stationary-serial-double-pole-main}, there
are estimators \((\widehat\eta_N,\widehat\delta_N)\), with
\(\widehat\delta_N\ge0\), such that for every bounded set of local parameters
\[
 \widehat\eta_N-\eta_N=O_P(N^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\delta_N-\delta_N=O_P(N^{-1/2})
\tag{P6}
\]
uniformly over the compact stratum.  The induced rate multiset satisfies
\textup{(SC19)}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Delete the initial and terminal incomplete cycles and write the observed
complete gaps as \(G_1,\ldots,G_{K_N}\).  Define
\[
 \widehat S_k=K_N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}\mathbf1_{\{G_j\ge k\}},
 \qquad 0\le k\le2n-1,
\tag{P7}
\]
with arbitrary fixed values on \(K_N=0\).  The common exponential moment,
the stopped i.i.d. CLT, and \(K_N/N\to1/\mu\) imply
\[
 \max_{0\le k\le2n-1}|\widehat S_k-S_k|=O_P(N^{-1/2})
\tag{P8}
\]
uniformly over the stratum and bounded local alternatives.

Write the monic recurrence polynomial as
\[
 \chi(x)=x^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0
 =\prod_{i=1}^n(x-z_i),\qquad z_i=e^{-\theta_i\Delta\tau}.
\tag{P9}
\]
The equations
\[
 S_{k+n}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_jS_{k+j}=0,\qquad0\le k<n,
\tag{P10}
\]
have coefficient matrix \((S_{i+j})_{0\le i,j<n}\).  It is nonsingular even
at collisions by
\Cref{cor:nstate-serial-all-collision-identifiability-main}.  Its determinant
has a positive minimum in absolute value on the compact stratum; the same is
true on a sufficiently small common neighbourhood.  Solving the empirical
version of \textup{(P10)} and using \textup{(P8)} therefore gives
\[
 \max_j|\widehat a_j-a_j|=O_P(N^{-1/2})
\tag{P11}
\]
uniformly.

Uniform separation permits disjoint fixed contours around the double root and
the \(n-2\) simple roots of \(\chi\).  The argument principle, or the
implicit-function theorem applied to the contour power sums, makes every
simple root and the two elementary symmetric functions \(A=z_1+z_2\),
\(B=z_1z_2\) of the double cluster analytic functions of the polynomial
coefficients on these neighbourhoods.  Thus their empirical versions are
root-\(N\) by \textup{(P11)}.  The separated rates are
\(-\log z_j/\Delta\tau\), while the double-cluster quotient coordinates are
the analytic functions in \textup{(T27)}.  Define the estimator by these
local analytic maps whenever \(\widehat\chi\) is in their common
neighbourhood, use any fixed value otherwise, and project the continued
\(\widetilde\delta_N\) onto \([0,\infty)\).  The exceptional event has
probability tending to zero uniformly, and projection is nonexpansive, which
proves \textup{(P6)}.

Finally form the estimated double pair
\(\widehat c_N\pm\sqrt{\widehat\delta_N}\) and the estimated separated
rates.  The square-root inequality used after \textup{(T28)} gives
\(O_P(N^{-1/4})\) for the colliding pair, while all other components are
root-\(N\).  The separation \(c_0\) fixes the optimal matching with
probability tending to one uniformly, proving \textup{(SC19)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:stationary-serial-double-pole-main}]
The serial killed-reset construction makes the visible record a stationary
renewal record whose Palm cycle is the sampled absorption bin of
\(W=X_1+\cdots+X_n\).  In particular,
\[
 S_{\eta,\delta}(k)=P_{\eta,\delta}
 \{W>k\Delta\tau\},\qquad
 g_{\eta,\delta}(k)=S_{\eta,\delta}(k)-S_{\eta,\delta}(k+1).
\tag{G1}
\]
\Cref{lem:sampled-double-pole-nondegeneracy-main} verifies every analytic,
tail, and information hypothesis of
\Cref{lem:compact-uniform-stationary-renewal-lan-main}.  Applying that lemma
with \(q=(\eta,\delta)\) proves \textup{(SC15)}--\textup{(SC16)}.  Notice
that the proof uses the exact stationary factorization \textup{(SL4)}: its
random number of complete gaps supplies the factor \(1/\mu\) in
\textup{(SC14)}, and its two survival factors and \(1/\mu\) are
\(o_P(1)\) in the local log likelihood.  Thus no deterministic-product
equivalence is being assumed.

For testing, define the efficient collision central sequence
\[
 \Delta_{\delta\cdot\eta,N}
 =\Delta_{\delta,N}
 -I_{\delta\eta}I_{\eta\eta}^{-1}\Delta_{\eta,N}.
\tag{G2}
\]
It has null variance \(J_n\), which is strictly positive by the Schur
complement of \textup{(SC16)}.  Under
\((\eta_N,\delta_N)=(\eta_0+u/\sqrt N,v/\sqrt N)\), the third lemma gives
mean \(vJ_n\); the nuisance shift cancels in \textup{(G2)}.  A null fit from
\Cref{lem:serial-collision-recurrence-estimator-main}, followed by evaluation
of the scores and information at \((\widehat\eta_N,0)\), changes
\(\Delta_{\delta\cdot\eta,N}/\sqrt{J_n}\) by \(o_P(1)\) uniformly.  Rejecting
above \(z_{1-\alpha}\) therefore gives \textup{(SC18)}.  The Gaussian
Neyman--Pearson bound after residualizing the nuisance proves the asserted
power envelope; maximizing the local quadratic likelihood over
\(v\ge0\) yields the same rejection rule.

The estimator lemma proves the upper rate \textup{(SC19)}, including the
root-\(N\) rate for the centre, collision square, and all noncolliding rates.
For the lower bound, fix a base point and hold \(\eta=\eta_0\) fixed while
comparing \(v=0\) with \(v=v_0>0\).  Their unordered rate multisets are
separated by exactly \(\sqrt{v_0}N^{-1/4}\) in \(d_{\rm m}\).  The LAN
likelihood ratio in \textup{(SC15)} converges to a finite nondegenerate
Gaussian likelihood ratio, so these two sequences are mutually contiguous
and no test separates them consistently.  An estimator with
\(o_P(N^{-1/4})\) error under both sequences would, by choosing the closer
candidate multiset, provide such a test.  More quantitatively, for every
\(a<\sqrt{v_0}/2\) the maximum of the two error probabilities has a strictly
positive lower limit, exactly as in \textup{(SC10)}.  This proves the local
minimax lower bound.  Together with \textup{(SC19)} it establishes the
matching \(N^{-1/4}\) rate and completes the theorem.
\end{proof}
