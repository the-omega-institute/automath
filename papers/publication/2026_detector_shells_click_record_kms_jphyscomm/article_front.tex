\begin{abstract}
We determine the stationary-record limit experiment when two rates collide in
a sampled generalized-Erlang, or serial discrete phase-type, renewal
process.  For fixed serial order, one isolated double collision, compact
positive rate sets, uniformly separated remaining rates, and known sampling
interval, the smooth identifiable coordinate is the squared half-separation
\(\delta=\{(\theta_1-\theta_2)/2\}^2\).  More generally, we prove local
asymptotic equivalence, in Le Cam distance, between a deterministic window of
a stationary lattice renewal indicator and an undershot fixed-size i.i.d.
sample from its Palm interarrival law.  On records containing a renewal, the
exact stationary likelihood factorization includes the forward-recurrence
(residual-life) and backward-recurrence (age) factors and random renewal
stopping; the all-zero record is a separate uniformly negligible atom.  The
collision experiment is uniformly locally asymptotically normal in the regular nuisance
coordinates and \(\delta\) at rate \(N^{-1/2}\).  The collision derivative has
an exact fourth-order pole and a nonzero
\(k^3e^{-\lambda\Delta\tau k}\) sampled component; this proves uniform
information nondegeneracy against all nuisance derivatives.  The limit is a
Gaussian shift on a half-space, its residualized score test attains the
one-sided local power envelope.  The unordered rate multiset has a uniformly
attainable \(N^{-1/4}\) rate and a matching pointwise fixed-base two-point
threshold lower rate; no exact minimax-risk constant is claimed.  The centre
and all canonically ordered noncolliding rates remain root-\(N\).  We first
prove the complete stationary experiment
for the present two-state sampled counter and then prove the fixed-order serial
theorem.

The underlying killed-reset D-MAP makes the visible process renewal but does
not make its hidden representation identifiable.  We retain the explicit
two-state Palm law, weak-equivalence cone intersection, and fixed-setting
record tools as population and inferential complements.  Three visible
inclusion probabilities give a rational inverse to \((p+s,ps)\).  This
three-inclusion formula is presented as a candidate original result pending
complete reduction against canonical DMAP2 coordinates; it identifies the
unordered pair, not a labelled realization.  The pure serial population
inverse remains a finite-coordinate sampled-tail specialization of the
generalized-Erlang and Coxian representation theory of He--Zhang.  Supplementary
calculations are separated from the stationary collision theorem.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} generalized-Erlang distribution, serial phase
type, singular LAN, minimax rate, stationary renewal record, double pole,
constrained D-MAP

\section{Introduction}

The main question is statistical rather than representational: what is the
complete local experiment, and the sharp recovery boundary, when two rates in
a sampled generalized-Erlang renewal process collide?  At a double collision
the labelled split disappears to first order.  Its identifiable coordinate is
the squared half-separation, so root-\(N\) inference in that quotient becomes
\(N^{-1/4}\) recovery of the unordered roots.  Theorems~F and G prove this for
the actual stationary binary record, including renewal stopping and censored
endpoint cycles, and match the estimation upper bound with a contiguity lower
bound.  A preceding experiment-equivalence theorem first shows, for a general
local class of stationary lattice renewal laws, that the deterministic window
is asymptotically equivalent to an i.i.d. sample of
\(\lfloor N/\mu_0-N^\gamma\rfloor\) Palm interarrivals for every
\(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\).

The original applied-probability question remains as the two-state instance:
what the binary arrival record of a constrained D-MAP determines when every
marked transition resets the hidden chain to a fixed state.  This reset creates
renewal structure, while the usual nonuniqueness of Markovian and phase-type
representations remains.  The model therefore sits at the intersection of
renewal theory, D-MAP weak equivalence, phase-type representation, and singular
stationary-record inference.

Our two-state parametrization has hidden states \(R\) and \(D\).  During a
sampling interval of length \(\Delta\tau\), an attempt clock has rate
\(\Gamma\) and a recovery clock has rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).  A marked
transition is latched in \(D\) until the next boundary.  This construction is
only a realization of the probability model: the results concern the labelled
kernels and their visible binary law.  Its deterministic restart is a
special case of the essential rank-one condition \(T_1=c\beta\): a fixed
nondegenerate post-click law \(\beta\) would also regenerate.  Arbitrary
marked leakage into \(R\) no longer guarantees renewal; the present proof
fails precisely when the normalized post-click law depends on the pre-click
state.

Classical MAP and D-MAP theory supplies the arrival-process framework
\cite{Neuts1979MAP,Lucantoni1991BMAP,AlfaNeuts1995DMAP}.  Zamparo's
renewal-binary results supply the finite-dimensional renewal-law language and
the finite-order Markov criterion used here \cite{Zamparo2022RenewalBinary}.
Ram\'irez-Cobo and Lillo give the complete weak-equivalence classification for
MAP2 and MAP3 processes \cite{RamirezCoboLillo2012WeakEquivalentMAP};
M\'esz\'aros and Telek give canonical coordinates for discrete MAP2/RAP2
models \cite{MeszarosTelek2013DiscreteMAP2Canonical}.  Those results are the
primary comparison objects for both the quotient inverse and the killed-reset
cone intersection.  The direct three-inclusion inverse below is a candidate
original result pending complete reduction against canonical DMAP2
coordinates.  It is not asserted as an established priority claim.

For serial kernels, generalized-Erlang/Coxian representation and minimality
theory are the primary antecedents \cite{HeZhang2008MinimalCoxian,
HeZhang2009GeneralizedErlang}.  Our serial statement is limited to the
finite-coordinate sampled-tail specialization, including confluent repeated
poles.  For general killed-reset kernels, realization uniqueness identifies a
similarity orbit, but only its intersection with the Markov cone consists of
valid stochastic representations.  This distinction leads to an explicit
two-state cone-intersection arc and a local dimension statement on the minimal
interior stratum; neither is a claim about the global dimension of every fibre.

The physical-image test, two-state fibre arc, serial corollary, and minimal
interior similarity-manifold result use ordinary theorem numbering.  The
Markov--Palm tangent, moving-dimensional Helmert coupling, exact
complete-Palm-gap split experiment, and pointwise projection calculation are
supplementary results.  In particular, Bickel--Kwon's known-marginal Markov
tangent and additive projection are antecedents of the supplementary tangent
calculation \cite{BickelKwon2001Semiparametric}; the added restrictions there
are the killed-reset atom constraint, positivity-compatible path realization,
and calendar-time scaling.

The specification statistic imports distributional-distance consistency from
stationary-ergodic testing \cite{RyabkoRyabko2010ErgodicInference,
Ryabko2012StationaryTesting}.  The physical-image statistic instead tests the
finite-dimensional inclusion image under the sampled-counter model.  These are
different questions, and neither diagnostic is used to select a model and then
authorize another inferential statement from the same record.

\begin{assumption}[Constrained sampled-counter D-MAP model]\label{ass:model-inference-contract-main}
Fix \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\in(0,\infty)^3\) and define
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},
\]
\[
a=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\bigr)\,dr,\qquad
b=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr .
\]
The record model is the two-state D-MAP with hidden states ordered as
\((R,D)\) and labelled kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The convention is
\((T_i)_{XY}=\PP(A_n=i,H_{n+1}=Y\mid H_n=X)\), the visible process
\((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) is the stationary binary process generated by these
kernels, and formulas on \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) are interpreted by
continuous limits.  The killed entries \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\) are a structural
constraint of this D-MAP, not optional boundary cases of the finite-record
theorems.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Scope of finite-record reports}\label{subsec:finite-record-report-scope-main}

The observation throughout is a stationary binary renewal indicator on
\(\mathbb Z\).  Its interrenewal mass is obtained by binning the serial
absorption time.  The reset-and-boundary protocol excludes any further
post-click renewal within the same bin by construction.  The results are not
statements about generic discretely observed event data, interval counts that
can contain multiple arrivals, or sampled hidden point processes.

The retained-record limits are fixed-setting asymptotic statements inside the
stationary law specified in \Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}.  The
model, population point, complete-cycle convention, and deterministic batch
sequence are fixed before the record is interpreted.  Theorems~C and D are CLT
and specification-test statements, respectively; Theorem~E is the quotient
delta method.  The pointwise projection example is supplementary and has no
confidence-set, testing, or coverage conclusion.

The only model interpretation assumed by this contract is the latched
killed-leakage sampled-counter protocol of
\Cref{lem:classical-counter-kernel-construction-main}.  If the sampling
interval \(\Delta\tau\) is a known design interval, the visible stationary
record identifies the unordered survival-factor set
\(\{p,s\}=\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}\) and
hence the unordered numerical rate set
\(\{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\}\).  It does not identify which member is the
attempt rate and which is the recovery rate without side information.  If
\(\Delta\tau\) is not treated as a known design interval, the visible law
identifies only the dimensionless unordered product set
\(\{\Gamma\Delta\tau,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\}\); the same record does
not put those products on an absolute numerical rate scale.

The supplementary singular likelihood calculation observes a deterministic
number of independent complete Palm gaps.  Its exact product experiment is
uniform for \(z\) in compact subsets of \((0,1)\) and bounded local split
\(h\), and it identifies only \(h^2\).  It does not establish an endpoint
factorization or asymptotic equivalence for the stationary finite record; that
gap is closed separately by Theorems~F and G.  The
separate projection result is only the pointwise limit at the preselected
population point \(z_0=1/2\), using the printed covariance certificate in
\Cref{lem:z-half-diagonal-branch-certificate-main}.  No confidence set, test,
critical value, coverage conclusion, or uniform projection claim is attached
to it.
Ordered labelled rates require external side information fixed independently
of this record.  The paper does not claim
simultaneous inversion over \(0<z<1\), nor identification of UDW, KMS,
thermodynamic, response-profile, or ordered-rate quantities unless the
particular result supplies the extra hypotheses and proof.

\subsection{Visible-record diagnostics for the model contract}\label{subsec:visible-record-model-diagnostics-main}

\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main} and
\Cref{subsec:finite-record-report-scope-main} form a conditional contract, not a
model-selection theorem.  The retained-record limits below are statements
inside the killed-leakage sampled-counter D-MAP with known \(\Delta\tau\);
they are not procedures that first validate that model from the same record and
then authorize inference under it.  The physical-image test and the
complete-visible-law test have only the size and power guarantees stated in
\Cref{thm:uniform-joint-physical-image-test-main,thm:complete-visible-law-specification-main}.
Auxiliary complete-gap renewal
checks, inclusion-product residuals, and low-lag covariance residuals have no
separate calibration theorem: they can falsify the standing interpretation,
but they do not certify it or validate assumptions used by another result.


\begin{lemma}[Latched sampled-counter derivation of the D-MAP kernels]\label{lem:classical-counter-kernel-construction-main}
The kernels in \Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main} are exactly the
one-step kernels of the following discrete sampled-counter protocol.  At the
start of a sampling interval of length \(\Delta\tau\), the hidden state is
either \(R\) or \(D\).  From \(R\), an independent attempt clock with rate
\(\Gamma\) either rings before the end of the interval, in which case the
visible output is \(1\) and the counter is latched in \(D\) until the boundary,
or it does not ring, in which case the visible output is \(0\) and the next
state remains \(R\).
From \(D\), an independent recovery clock with rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\)
either does not ring, giving output \(0\) and next state \(D\), or rings at
time \(r\in(0,\Delta\tau)\).  Conditional on recovery at \(r\), the same
attempt clock is run only during the remaining time \(\Delta\tau-r\): no
attempt gives output \(0\) and next state \(R\), while an attempt gives output
\(1\) and latches the counter in \(D\) until the boundary.  No post-click
recovery or further within-bin evolution is permitted.  This protocol gives
\[
T_0(R,R)=p,\quad T_1(R,D)=1-p,\quad T_0(D,D)=s,
\]
\[
T_0(D,R)=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr=b,
\quad
T_1(D,D)=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\bigr)\,dr=a,
\]
and the two killed entries \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The \(R\)-row is the survival and ringing decomposition of an exponential
clock over one interval.  For the \(D\)-row, no recovery before
\(\Delta\tau\) has probability \(s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\) and
contributes to \(T_0(D,D)\).  If recovery occurs at \(r\), its density is
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}\,dr\).  Survival of the attempt
clock for the remaining time \(\Delta\tau-r\) gives the \(T_0(D,R)\) integral,
and its complement gives the \(T_1(D,D)\) integral.  The latching rule never
allows a marked transition landing in \(R\), which gives the two killed
entries.  These mutually exclusive cases exhaust each row, since
\(s+a+b=1\) and \(p+(1-p)=1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Rank-one regeneration boundary under one-labelled leakage]\label{prop:model-fragility-boundary-reset-main}
The killed-leakage convention in
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main} is not a removable presentation
choice.  It is a structural hypothesis behind the renewal law, quotient
inverse, and retained-record inference in Theorems~A, B, C, and E.  Let
\((\widetilde T_0,\widetilde T_1)\) be any two-state labelled kernel with the
same visible convention
\[
(\widetilde T_i)_{XY}
=\PP(A_n=i,H_{n+1}=Y\mid H_n=X),\qquad X,Y\in\{R,D\},
\]
and put \(c=\widetilde T_1\mathbf1\).  If a probability row \(\beta\)
satisfies
\[
 \widetilde T_1=c\beta,
\tag{R1}
\]
then every click restarts the hidden chain from \(\beta\), independently of
the pre-click state.  The click epochs are regeneration epochs and their Palm
zero-run mass is
\[
 \widetilde g_k=\beta\widetilde T_0^k
                  \widetilde T_1\mathbf1,\qquad k\ge0.
\tag{R2}
\]
Condition \textup{(R1)} permits \(\beta\) to have positive mass on both
states; deterministic restart at \(D\) is this model's specialization.

If the normalized post-click rows
\[
 \frac{\widetilde T_1(X,\cdot)}
      {\widetilde T_1(X,\cdot)\mathbf1}
\tag{R3}
\]
differ between pre-click states having positive marked-row mass, then the
fixed-restart argument is unavailable.  In particular, the Palm renewal product
\[
\PP(B\subset\Xi)=\rho\prod_j u_{i_{j+1}-i_j}
\]
for \(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\) does not follow from the D-MAP structure alone;
nor do the Palm gap formula
\(g_k=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1\), the single-mode covariance identity
\[
u_2=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid},
\]
or the quotient recovery
\[
\lambda_{\rm hid}=(r_2-r_0^2)/(r_1-r_0^2),\qquad
\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps).
\]
Therefore Theorems~A and B, and Theorems~C--E wherever they use the same
renewal law, inverse, or covariance route, fail to be valid generic
sampled-counter or generic D-MAP(2) statements unless additional hypotheses
restore the rank-one restart or supply a replacement regeneration theory.
A positive marked entry into \(R\), by itself, does not imply failure:
arbitrary leakage need not regenerate, but rank-one leakage can.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~A starts from the general factorization
\(T_1=c\beta\).  Conditional on a click from any row with positive marked
mass, division by that mass leaves the same distribution \(\beta\).  The
Markov property then makes the post-click future independent of the pre-click
state and proves \textup{(R2)}.  In the present kernel,
\(\beta=e_D^\top\) because \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\).
The inter-click zero runs are then i.i.d. under the Palm law, their tail is
computed by iterating \(T_0\) from \(e_D^\top\), and inclusion probabilities factor
as the displayed renewal product.

Conversely, the rows in \textup{(R3)} are exactly the conditional post-click
laws.  If they differ, the post-click distribution retains pre-click-state
information.  The D-MAP property alone then does not supply the fixed Palm
restart used by the renewal proof.

In a continuous same-bin recovery comparator, paths ending in either hidden
state can make both columns of \(\widetilde T_1\) positive.  Renewal then
requires the additional proportional-row check \textup{(R1)}; it is no
longer a consequence of the protocol description alone.  The renewal product
formulas and the covariance transfer lemma are downstream of that Palm renewal
law, while the quotient inverse in Theorem~B explicitly divides the covariance
identity \(r_2-r_0^2=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid}\) by
\(r_1-r_0^2=\rho(a-\rho)\).  Once the renewal identity is unavailable, the
displayed inverse and the retained-record covariance estimator are no longer
proved for the modified kernel.  This proves the stated logical dependence.
\end{proof}

The main text below retains the five paper-level statements A--E for the
constrained D-MAP(2) subfamily and adds the stationary collision Theorems~F
and G.  Supporting lemmas, propositions, corollaries, certificate interfaces,
and algorithms prove or apply these seven statements.  The killed-leakage dependency is
explicit in \Cref{prop:model-fragility-boundary-reset-main}: if same-bin
recovery after a click is retained, this theorem chain applies only after the
resulting marked kernel is shown to retain the rank-one restart property.
