Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, \emph{``A conditional note on fixed calibrated response-model shells from a classical sampled counter with later UDW-rate calibration in static KMS spacetimes''}, for publication as a Research Article in \emph{Journal of Physics A: Mathematical and Theoretical}.

The paper shows that the full stationary binary record of a sampled classical counter, whose attempt intensity is calibrated by the stationary Unruh--DeWitt excitation rate only through an explicit later closure, contains record-level thresholds that rate thermometry alone would miss. Dead time and finite recovery are intrinsic features of counting detectors, and exploiting their full stochastic output goes beyond summarizing the detector by its mean transition rate. The mathematical core of the work---symbolic dynamics, renewal-process theory, spectral analysis, exact finite-dependence criteria, and fixed-record asymptotics---is presented as a self-contained theorem chain suited to \emph{Journal of Physics A: Mathematical and Theoretical}.

The present manuscript is a self-contained treatment of the sampled-record law and its narrow fixed-response calibration interface. Geometry-facing quotient, near-horizon, fixed-radius inverse, and Schwarzschild calculations are retained only as appendix-level conditional corollaries or supplementary bookkeeping, not as the main theorem narrative.

The principal results are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item In the ideal dead-time model, the sampled record is a Markov chain on the golden-mean shift, and the Parry benchmark is realized at a unique detector threshold.
\item In the finite-recovery model, the sampled record is a full-support renewal process with strictly increasing hazard, so the Parry benchmark is excluded. The relevant selector is instead the memory-transition threshold at exact $1$-dependence, which is inserted into the response model only later.
\item Finite recovery approaches the ideal grammar only softly: consecutive-click violations are quadratically suppressed in the sampling interval, yet the hazard remains genuinely history dependent at every positive sampling interval.
\item The finite-recovery record is universally antibunched, with strictly increasing spectral density on $[0,\pi]$ and an asymptotic Fano factor below one across the whole parameter range.
\item After the record thresholds are proved, a fixed calibrated stationary response profile turns them into response loci subject to independent range and regularity checks; KMS detailed balance fixes only the one-gap thermal ratio and does not determine those loci.
\item The appendices record the conditional homogeneous, near-horizon, fixed-radius inverse, and Schwarzschild toy-model consequences only after their extra homogeneous, small-shell, admissibility, Jacobian, and external ratio-data hypotheses are stated.
\end{enumerate}

The main article contains the complete sampled-record theorem proofs and the
fixed-response locus theorem. The Supplementary Material records auxiliary
calculations, finite-counting checks, coordinate expansions, numerical
bookkeeping, and a deterministic reproduction recipe for solving
$\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$ and regenerating the figures and
worked table directly from the displayed formulas.

The work is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission. We have no competing interests to declare.

Thank you for your consideration.
