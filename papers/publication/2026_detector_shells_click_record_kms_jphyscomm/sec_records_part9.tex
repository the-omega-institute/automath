that retained word, so the same probability limits transfer without changing
the estimator definitions.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Inverse delta method and projected exchange-diagonal charts.}
The next propositions keep three operations distinct.  First, the population
quotient inverse is the map \(r\mapsto(p+s,ps)\).  Second, separated labelled
rates require \(\Delta_\sigma>0\) and the deterministic oracle branch map of
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}, or an estimated map with the

finite-record implementation uses explicit denominator thresholds, projection

\begin{lemma}[Feasible symmetric-coordinate set and computable projection]\label{lem:feasible-set-computable-projection-main}
Let
\[
F=\{(x+y,xy):0\le x,y\le1\}.
\]
For \(0\le t\le2\), set
\[
\ell_F(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\le t\le1,\\
t-1,&1\le t\le2 .
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feasible-symmetric-coordinate-set-main}
F=\{(\sigma_1,\sigma_2):0\le\sigma_1\le2,\quad
\ell_F(\sigma_1)\le\sigma_2\le\sigma_1^2/4\}.
\end{equation}
For any \(v=(v_1,v_2)\in\mathbb R^2\), define
\[
c_v(t)
=\min\{\max\{v_2,\ell_F(t)\},\,t^2/4\},
\qquad 0\le t\le2,
\]
and
\[
q_v(t)=(t-v_1)^2+(c_v(t)-v_2)^2 .
\]
The deterministic projection used below is the one-dimensional rule
\begin{equation}\label{eq:computable-projection-rule-main}
t_v=\min\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{0\le t\le2}q_v(t),
\qquad
\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(v)=(t_v,c_v(t_v)).
\end{equation}
This rule is Borel measurable, is a Euclidean nearest-point projection onto
\(F\), and resolves global nonuniqueness by choosing the smallest minimizing
\(\sigma_1\).  On any neighbourhood where the Euclidean nearest point is
unique, \(\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\) agrees with the ordinary projection.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(t=x+y\).  The unit-square constraints imply \(0\le t\le2\).  Among
points in \([0,1]^2\) with sum \(t\), the product \(xy\) is maximized at
\(x=y=t/2\), giving \(xy\le t^2/4\).  The minimum is attained at the boundary:
it is \(0\) for \(0\le t\le1\), and \(t-1\) for \(1\le t\le2\), because then
one coordinate can be \(1\) and the other \(t-1\).  Conversely, every value
between these two endpoints is obtained by continuously moving \(x\) along the
line segment \(\{(x,t-x):0\le x,t-x\le1\}\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:feasible-symmetric-coordinate-set-main}.

For fixed first coordinate \(t\), the closest feasible second coordinate to
\(v_2\) is exactly the clipping \(c_v(t)\) to the closed interval
\([\ell_F(t),t^2/4]\).  Therefore minimizing the squared Euclidean distance
from \(v\) to \(F\) reduces to minimizing \(q_v\) over the compact interval
\([0,2]\).  The minimum exists because \(q_v\) is continuous.  Choosing the
smallest minimizer gives the displayed deterministic tie-break.  Since the
argmin graph of a continuous function on a compact metric space is closed, the
leftmost argmin selection is Borel measurable.  The selected point is a nearest
point by the preceding fixed-\(t\) reduction, and uniqueness makes the
tie-break irrelevant.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-window error bound for projected plug-in word-law reconstructions]\label{lem:projected-complete-law-fixed-window-error-main}
Let
\[
F_{\rm sep}^{\circ}
=\{(x+y,xy):0<x<1,\ 0<y<1,\ x\ne y\}.
\]
Fix a compact set \(K\Subset F_{\rm sep}^{\circ}\).  For
\(\sigma\in K\), let \(\PP_\sigma\) denote the stationary renewal law
obtained by the projected plug-in word-law reconstruction induced by
\Cref{lem:algorithmic-complete-law-reconstruction-main}: compute the two
unordered roots of \(z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2=0\), form the corresponding
gap law \(g_k(\sigma)\), set
\(\rho_*(\sigma)=\{1+\sum_{k\ge0}k g_k(\sigma)\}^{-1}\), and then use the
renewal inclusion product and finite inclusion--exclusion.

For every finite window \(I\subset\mathbb Z\) there is a finite constant
\(C_I(K)\) such that, for all \(\sigma,\sigma'\in K\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-complete-law-fixed-window-lipschitz-main}
\max_{S\subset I}
\left|\PP_\sigma\{\varXi\cap I=S\}
      -\PP_{\sigma'}\{\varXi\cap I=S\}\right|
\le C_I(K)\,\|\sigma-\sigma'\| .
\end{equation}
Consequently, if the population point \(\sigma_0=\Phi(\Rinc)\) lies in
\(F_{\rm sep}^{\circ}\) and the finite-record projected coordinate
\(\widetilde\sigma_N\) is retained in such a compact \(K\) with
\(\sigma_0\in K\), then the reconstructed projected plug-in word probabilities
satisfy the fixed-window deterministic bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-record-projected-word-error-bound-main}
\max_{S\subset I}
\left|\widetilde\PP_N\{\varXi\cap I=S\}
      -\PP_{\sigma_0}\{\varXi\cap I=S\}\right|
\le C_I(K)\,\|\widetilde\sigma_N-\sigma_0\|.
\end{equation}
On a denominator-stable neighbourhood of \(\Rinc\), if projection is inactive and
\(\Phi\) has Lipschitz constant \(L_\Phi\), then the same event also gives
\[
\max_{S\subset I}
\left|\widetilde\PP_N\{\varXi\cap I=S\}
      -\PP_{\sigma_0}\{\varXi\cap I=S\}\right|
\le C_I(K)L_\Phi\,\|\Rinch_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-\Rinc\|.
\]
In particular, under the finite-record CLT hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main}, every fixed-window projected
plug-in word-law reconstruction has error \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) at separated
interior population points.  This is only a consistency and fixed-window error
statement for the plug-in projection; it is not a finite-sample uncertainty
band, confidence theorem, or critical-value construction, and no
word-probability confidence intervals are proved here.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Compactness in \(F_{\rm sep}^{\circ}\) gives constants \(\eta>0\) and
\(\delta>0\) such that every root pair over \(K\) lies in
\([\eta,1-\eta]^2\) and has separation at least \(\delta\).  Therefore the
ordered roots
\[
z_-(\sigma)=\frac{\sigma_1-\sqrt{\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2}}2,\qquad
z_+(\sigma)=\frac{\sigma_1+\sqrt{\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2}}2
\]
are \(C^1\), with uniformly bounded derivatives on a neighbourhood of \(K\).
The logarithms \(\theta_\pm(\sigma)=-\log z_\pm(\sigma)\) are also \(C^1\)
there.  For each fixed \(k\), the unequal-root formula in
\eqref{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-gap-main} is therefore \(C^1\) in
\(\sigma\) on that neighbourhood.

The derivative bounds are uniform for the infinite mean appearing in
\(\rho_*\).  Indeed \(z_\pm\le1-\eta\) on a neighbourhood of \(K\), and the
derivatives of \(z_\pm^k\) are bounded by a constant times
\((k+1)(1-\eta/2)^k\) after shrinking the neighbourhood.  Hence
\(\sum_{k\ge0} k g_k(\sigma)\) and its first derivatives converge uniformly
on \(K\).  Thus \(\rho_*(\sigma)\) is Lipschitz on \(K\).

If \(I=\emptyset\), the only word has probability \(1\) under every
\(\PP_\sigma\), so the claim is trivial.  Otherwise let
\(d_I=\max I-\min I\), with \(d_I=0\) for a one-point window.  The Palm
renewal mass \(u_n(\sigma)\), \(0\le n\le d_I\), is a finite polynomial in
\(g_0(\sigma),\ldots,g_{n-1}(\sigma)\), with \(u_0=1\).  Hence each
\(u_n\) needed by the inclusion products in the window is Lipschitz on
\(K\).  For a nonempty \(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\subset I\), the inclusion
functional
\[
\rho_*(\sigma)\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}
u_{i_{\ell+1}-i_\ell}(\sigma)
\]
is therefore Lipschitz on \(K\), with a constant depending only on \(I\) and
\(K\).  The empty inclusion has constant value \(1\).  Finally, each word
probability is a finite signed sum of at most \(2^{|I|}\) such inclusion
functionals, by
\eqref{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-word-main}.  Taking the maximum over the
finite set of \(S\subset I\) gives
\eqref{eq:projected-complete-law-fixed-window-lipschitz-main}.

The finite-record statement is obtained by substituting
\(\sigma'=\widetilde\sigma_N\) and \(\sigma=\sigma_0\) in
\eqref{eq:projected-complete-law-fixed-window-lipschitz-main}; the reported
\(\widetilde\PP_N\) is exactly \(\PP_{\widetilde\sigma_N}\) on the retained
projected plug-in word-law reconstruction branch.  On a denominator-stable neighbourhood the map
\(\Phi\) is \(C^1\), hence Lipschitz.  When projection is inactive,
\(\widetilde\sigma_N=\Phi(\Rinch_N^{\mathrm{cyc}})\), and the final
display follows.  The \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) conclusion follows from
\(\sqrt N(\Rinch_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-\Rinc)=O_{\PP}(1)\) in
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main} and from the fact that
projection is eventually inactive with probability tending to one at
separated interior points.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Projected plug-in word-law reconstruction]\label{prop:finite-record-complete-law-renewal-consistency-main}
Let \(\Rinch_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}\) be the complete-cycle inclusion estimator,
let \(\Phi\) be the quotient inverse map, and let
\(\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\) be the feasible symmetric-coordinate projection.  Use the
finite-record reporting gate \(\mathcal E_N\) of
\eqref{eq:finite-record-reporting-event-main} and the deterministic thresholds
\(\varepsilon_N,\eta_N\) in \eqref{prot:finite-record-main}.  The projected
plug-in word-law reconstruction is the following partial finite-record
procedure.  On
\(\mathcal E_N\), define
\[
\widetilde\sigma_N
=\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\{\Phi(\Rinch_N^{\mathrm{cyc}})\},
\]
let \(\{ \widetilde z_{1,N},\widetilde z_{2,N}\}\) be the unordered roots of
\[
z^2-\widetilde\sigma_{1,N}z+\widetilde\sigma_{2,N}=0,
\]
and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complete-law-retained-event-main}
\mathcal E_N^{\rm law}
:=\mathcal E_N\cap
\{\widetilde z_{1,N},\widetilde z_{2,N}\in[\eta_N,1-\eta_N]\}.
\end{equation}
On \((\mathcal E_N^{\rm law})^c\), no projected plug-in word probabilities and
no finite word probabilities are formed.  On \(\mathcal E_N^{\rm law}\), the
projected gap law
\(\widetilde g_{k,N}\) computed from
\Cref{lem:algorithmic-complete-law-reconstruction-main} is nonnegative,
normalized, and is the \(D\)-row path law of a stochastic triangular kernel:
\[
\widetilde g_{k,N}\ge0,\qquad
\sum_{k\ge0}\widetilde g_{k,N}=1 .
\]
Only on this same retained event define
\[
\widetilde\rho_N
=\rho_*(\widetilde\sigma_N)
=\left(1+\sum_{k\ge0}k\,\widetilde g_{k,N}\right)^{-1},
\]
let \(\widetilde u_{n,N}\) be the Palm renewal masses of this same projected
gap law, and define the finite-record law \(\widetilde\PP_N\) by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-record-renewal-consistent-inclusions-main}
\widetilde\PP_N(B\subset\varXi)
=\widetilde\rho_N
 \prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\widetilde u_{i_{\ell+1}-i_\ell,N},
\qquad B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\ne\emptyset,
\end{equation}
with empty-set inclusion value \(1\), and then by finite inclusion--exclusion
for words.  This law is an exact stationary renewal law.  Equivalently, on
\(\mathcal E_N^{\rm law}\),
\[
(\widetilde\rho_N,\widetilde\sigma_N)\in
\mathcal M_{\rm ren}
:=\{(\rho_*(\sigma),\sigma):\sigma\in F\}.
\]
The empirical coordinate \(\widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}\) is used only to
construct the quotient coordinate and to test denominator stability; after
projection, the renewal intensity is \(\widetilde\rho_N\), not
\(\widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}\).

Assume now that the population point is separated and interior:
\(\Phi(\Rinc)\in F_{\rm sep}^{\circ}\), with the inverse denominators bounded
away from zero.  If
\(\Rinch_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}\to \Rinc\) in probability, then the projected
coordinate satisfies \(\widetilde\sigma_N\to\Phi(\Rinc)\) and
\(\widetilde\rho_N\to r_0\) in probability on the retained branch, whose
probability tends to one for any thresholds tending to zero slowly enough.
For every fixed finite window \(I\) there are a compact separated-root
neighbourhood \(K\) of \(\Phi(\Rinc)\) and a constant \(C_I(K)<\infty\) such that,
with probability tending to one,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complete-law-reporting-fixed-window-bound-main}
\max_{S\subset I}
\left|\widetilde\PP_N\{\varXi\cap I=S\}
      -\PP\{\varXi\cap I=S\}\right|
\le C_I(K)\,\|\widetilde\sigma_N-\Phi(\Rinc)\|.
\end{equation}
If the finite-record CLT hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main} hold, the right-hand side is
\(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).  Thus each fixed-window retained projected word
probability converges in probability to its population value with an explicit
projection-coordinate error bound.  The output is a projected plug-in word-law
reconstruction.  It is not a finite-sample confidence procedure for word
probabilities: no word-probability confidence intervals, bands, or
record-specific critical values are proved here.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On \((\mathcal E_N^{\rm law})^c\) the proposition asserts only the prescribed
withholding rule, so no renewal law or word-probability reconstruction has to be
interpreted there.  On the pre-specified event \(\mathcal E_N^{\rm law}\), the
fixed-model construction evaluates the quotient inverse and first projects
the symmetric root coordinate into \(F\).  Since the projected roots lie in
\([\eta_N,1-\eta_N]\subset(0,1)\),
\Cref{lem:projected-root-gap-law-valid-main} applies to those roots and shows
that the quotient-root formulas are \(D\)-row path probabilities of a
stochastic triangular labelled kernel.  They therefore give a nonnegative
unit-mass gap law, hence a single Palm renewal law.  The intensity
\(\widetilde\rho_N\) is then defined from that same gap law by the renewal
identity \(1/\EE(G+1)\).  Therefore
\((\widetilde\rho_N,\widetilde\sigma_N)\) lies on \(\mathcal M_{\rm ren}\),
and the inclusion products are exactly the renewal products for the projected
finite-record renewal law.

At a separated interior population point, the inverse denominators are bounded
away from zero and \(\Phi(\Rinc)\) lies in the relative interior of \(F\).  The
complete-cycle inclusion estimator is consistent by
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main}; hence
\(\Phi(\Rinch_N^{\mathrm{cyc}})\to\Phi(\Rinc)\) in probability and
\(\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\) is eventually the identity with probability tending to
one.  The denominator thresholds in \(\mathcal E_N\) pass with probability
tending to one by \Cref{lem:finite-record-reporting-gate-main}.  The two
population roots lie in \((0,1)\), while \(\eta_N\downarrow0\), so the
interior-root test in \(\mathcal E_N^{\rm law}\) also passes with probability
tending to one.  Thus the conditional law-reporting event itself satisfies
\[
\PP\{(\mathcal E_N^{\rm law})^c\}\to0 .
\]
The
roots, gap probabilities for any fixed finite range, renewal masses for any
fixed finite range, and \(\rho_*(\sigma)\) are continuous on this separated
interior chart.  The quantitative fixed-window statement is exactly
\Cref{lem:projected-complete-law-fixed-window-error-main}, applied on a compact
separated-root neighbourhood of \(\Phi(\Rinc)\).  The displayed fixed-window
convergence follows from that bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}

\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Root splitting after the exchange-diagonal projection]\label{lem:exchange-diagonal-projection-main}
If \(y_N=\sigma_z+Z_N/\sqrt N+o_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) and
\(Z_N\Rightarrow Z\), then
\[
\sqrt N\{\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(y_N)-\sigma_z\}\Rightarrow \Pi_{H_z}(Z),
\qquad H_z=\{h:h_2\le zh_1\}.
\]
Let \(d_z=(1,z)^\top\) be the tangent direction to the exchange diagonal and
put
\[
V=\Pi_{H_z}(Z),\qquad
\Delta(v)=4(zv_1-v_2).
\]
If \(\PP\{V\notin\operatorname{span}(d_z)\}=1\), then for the unordered roots
\(\widehat z_{1,N},\widehat z_{2,N}\) of the projected polynomial
\[
t^2-\{\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(y_N)\}_1t+\{\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(y_N)\}_2=0
\]
one has the \(N^{-1/4}\) splitting law
\[
N^{1/4}|\widehat z_{1,N}-\widehat z_{2,N}|
\Rightarrow \sqrt{\Delta(V)} .
\]
More generally this convergence holds with the possibly degenerate right-hand
side \(\sqrt{\Delta(V)}\); on the event
\(V\in\operatorname{span}(d_z)\), the first-order discriminant vanishes and no
nondegenerate \(N^{-1/4}\) separation is asserted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:exchange-diagonal-local-reach-before-main} is the sole local
projection result used here.  Put
\[
H_N=\sqrt N(y_N-\sigma_z).
\]
Then \(H_N=Z_N+o_{\PP}(1)\Rightarrow Z\), hence \(H_N=O_{\PP}(1)\).  The
stochastic consequence in
\Cref{lem:exchange-diagonal-local-reach-before-main} gives
\[
\sqrt N\{\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(y_N)-\sigma_z\}
=\Pi_{H_z}H_N+o_{\PP}(1)\Rightarrow\Pi_{H_z}Z .
\]
Write
\[
\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(y_N)=\sigma_z+\frac{V_N}{\sqrt N}+o_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}),
\qquad V_N\Rightarrow V .
\]
For \(\sigma=\sigma_z+v/\sqrt N\), the polynomial discriminant is
\[
\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2
=\left(2z+\frac{v_1}{\sqrt N}\right)^2
 -4\left(z^2+\frac{v_2}{\sqrt N}\right)
=\frac{4(zv_1-v_2)}{\sqrt N}+\frac{v_1^2}{N}.
\]
The projection puts the limit in the tangent half-space
\(H_z=\{v:v_2\le zv_1\}\), hence \(\Delta(V)\ge0\).  Since the
distance between the two roots of a monic quadratic equals the square root of
its discriminant,
\[
N^{1/4}|\widehat z_{1,N}-\widehat z_{2,N}|
=\left(4(zV_{N,1}-V_{N,2})+o_{\PP}(1)\right)^{1/2}
\Rightarrow \sqrt{\Delta(V)}
\]
by the continuous mapping theorem.  The limit is nonzero exactly off the
tangent line \(v_2=zv_1\), which is
\(\operatorname{span}(d_z)\).  Thus the \(N^{-1/4}\) root separation is a
conditional nondegenerate statement, not a generic assertion made on tangent
directions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exchange-diagonal self-dual inverse]\label{cor:critical_self_dual_inverse}
At the exchange diagonal \(p=s\), the visible quotient inverse returns the
self-dual unordered pair \(\{p,p\}\), and root splitting is governed by
\Cref{lem:exchange-diagonal-projection-main}.  This exchange-diagonal
self-duality is not, by itself, the exact-dependence critical point: along
\(p=s=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\),
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\) only at
\(\Gamma\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau=1\), equivalently
\(p=s=e^{-1}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Hidden-label exchange non-identifiability]\label{prop:hidden_label_exchange_nonidentifiability}
No statistic of one visible D-MAP record can distinguish the two separated
ordered points \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\) and
\((\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)\) without external labelling information.
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main} records the resulting reporting
convention.  This exchange
non-identifiability does not remove the diagonal common-rate estimand in
\Cref{lem:diagonal-common-rate-main}, where the two entries are equal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two parameter points induce the same finite-dimensional visible law by
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}; hence every visible-record
statistic has the same distribution under them.  This is the exchange
specialization inside the present constrained D-MAP(2) family, consistent with the
broader MAP2 lesson that a hidden arrival representation need not be uniquely
identified from the stationary observable arrival law
\cite{RamirezCoboLilloWiper2010MAP2Nonidentifiability}.
\end{proof}
