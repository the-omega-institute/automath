\section{Record, inverse, and finite-record results}\label{sec:records}

This section gives the record-side results in compressed form.  Each main
result is read under the constrained D-MAP record assumption
\Cref{ass:constrained-dmap-record-main}, with any extra regularity or
finite-record protocol stated locally.  Between those statements, all
probabilities below are probabilities of the stationary visible binary record
generated by the stipulated labelled kernels \(T_0,T_1\).
Throughout this section \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) denotes that two-sided
stationary extension.
One-sided strings such as \(A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\) are finite restrictions used
only for observed-record statistics; when a proof conditions on a past event
or shifts a renewal epoch, it is using the same stationary two-sided process.

\subsection{Model, benchmark, and renewal law}

\begin{proposition}[Constrained D-MAP labelled kernels]\label{prop:instrument-kernels-main}
Fix \(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\).  With hidden states ordered as
\((R,D)\), let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t0-t1-main}
T_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
p&0\\ b&s
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1-p\\0&a
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p-c-main}
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},
\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a-b-main}
a=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\bigr)\,dr,
\qquad
b=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr .
\end{equation}
For \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\ne\Gamma\),
\[
a=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)-\Gamma(1-s)}
        {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad
b=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(p-s)}
        {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
with continuous equal-rate limits.  The labelled-kernel convention is
\((T_i)_{XY}=\PP(A_n=i,H_{n+1}=Y\mid H_n=X)\), and word probabilities are
\(\pi T_{u_0}\cdots T_{u_{m-1}}\mathbf1\) from the stationary hidden law.
These are the kernels fixed in \Cref{ass:constrained-dmap-record-main}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two rows define a labelled transition kernel directly.  From state \(R\),
the zero-labelled transition remains in \(R\) with probability \(p\), while the
one-labelled transition goes to \(D\) with probability \(1-p\).  From state
\(D\), the zero-labelled transition has weights \(b\) to \(R\) and \(s\) to
\(D\), and the one-labelled transition has weight \(a\) to \(D\).  The
integrals define nonnegative weights with \(a+b=1-s\), so the two row sums of
\(T_0+T_1\) are one.  Thus \(T_0,T_1\) form the stated D-MAP kernels.  The
displayed unequal-rate formulas are the elementary evaluations of the two
integrals, with continuous limits on the equal-rate diagonal.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Reference criteria]\label{def:thermality-notions-main}
Detailed-balance thermality is the rate-ratio condition
\(\Gamma_+/\Gamma_-=e^{-\beta\Omega}\).  Gibbs thermalization is convergence
of the reduced detector state to \(e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{det}}}/Z\).  The
record-side Parry benchmark is the Parry measure on the golden-mean shift,
the maximum-entropy law under \(11\notin\operatorname{supp}\mu\)
\cite{Parry1964,LindMarcus1995}.  Only the third criterion is a benchmark for
the binary record studied here.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Standard Parry benchmark for the stipulated ideal blanking law]\label{prop:ideal-main}
For the ideal one-step blanking comparison law with transition matrix
\[
P_\beta=
\begin{pmatrix}
p_\beta&1-p_\beta\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad p_\beta=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},
\]
the binary output is the Parry law on the golden-mean shift iff
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parry-threshold-main}
p_\beta=\varphi^{-1}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The standard golden-mean Parry calculation gives
\(P^{\mathrm P}(0,0)=\varphi^{-1}\) and \(P^{\mathrm P}(1,0)=1\)
\cite{Parry1964,LindMarcus1995}.  The stipulated blanking law has the
same support and the same forced transition from \(1\), so equality is
equivalent to \(p_\beta=\varphi^{-1}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Equal-rate and critical notation]\label{lem:equal-rate-critical-convention-main}
The survival factors are always
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},
\]
and throughout the stipulated-kernel algebra
\[
q=1-p .
\]
The hidden eigenvalue is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda-hid-main}
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=p-b
=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}s-\Gamma p}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}
\quad(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\ne\Gamma),
\end{equation}
with continuous equal-rate value
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=e^{-y}(1-y)\), where
\(y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau=\Gamma\Delta\tau\) on the equal-rate
diagonal.  Put
\[
x=\Gamma\Delta\tau,\qquad y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau .
\]
For \(x\ne y\),
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x e^{-x}=y e^{-y}.
\]
The equation \(x e^{-x}=y e^{-y}\) always has the formal solution \(x=y\).
For \(y\ne1\) this diagonal solution is only the removable zero of the
multiplied quotient numerator, not a zero of the continuous eigenvalue
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\).  The critical rate is therefore the non-removable
branch
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-crit-piecewise-main}
\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)
=\Delta\tau^{-1}
\begin{cases}
x_+(y),&0<y<1,\quad x_+(y)>1,\quad x_+(y)e^{-x_+(y)}=y e^{-y},\\
1,&y=1,\\
x_-(y),&y>1,\quad 0<x_-(y)<1,\quad x_-(y)e^{-x_-(y)}=y e^{-y}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for \(y\ne1\) it is the unique positive solution of
\(x e^{-x}=y e^{-y}\) on the opposite side of \(1\) from \(y\), and the
removable equal-rate branch \(x=y\) is explicitly excluded.  Only at \(y=1\)
does the equal-rate branch become a genuine zero, giving
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}=\kappa_{\mathrm r}=1/\Delta\tau\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identity \(p-b\) and the quotient form follow from
\eqref{eq:a-b-main}.  The equal-rate value is l'Hopital's rule, equivalently
\(b=y e^{-y}\) when \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}=\Gamma\), and hence
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=e^{-y}(1-y)\).  For \(x\ne y\), multiplying the
quotient numerator by \(\Delta\tau\) gives the scalar equation
\(x e^{-x}=y e^{-y}\).  The function \(x\mapsto xe^{-x}\) is strictly
increasing on \((0,1)\), strictly decreasing on \((1,\infty)\), and has its
only maximum at \(1\).  Hence the level through \(y\ne1\) has exactly one
positive solution on the opposite side of \(1\), in addition to the removable
solution \(x=y\).  This gives the two non-removable branches in
\eqref{eq:gamma-crit-piecewise-main}.  When \(y=1\), the opposite branch
coalesces with the diagonal and the equal-rate limit \(e^{-1}(1-1)=0\) gives
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}=1/\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).
\end{proof}

For later audits, the equal-rate diagonal
\(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), \(y=\Gamma\Delta\tau\), \(p=e^{-y}\), is
summarized by the single dictionary
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equal-rate-dictionary-main}
\begin{aligned}
a&=1-(1+y)p,&
b&=yp,\\
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}&=p-b=(1-y)p,&
S_k&=\PP(G\ge k)=p^k(1+yk),\\
g_k&=p^k\bigl((1-p)(1+yk)-yp\bigr),&
h_k&=\frac{(1-p)+y(k(1-p)-p)}{1+yk},
\qquad k\ge0 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Regeneration and zero-run law]\label{lem:renewal-gap-main}
Let \(G\) be the number of zeros between consecutive clicks in the stationary
constrained D-MAP record.  Click times are regeneration times.  For
\(k\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-main}
g_k=\PP(G=k)=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1 .
\end{equation}
If \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\ne\Gamma\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-closed-main}
g_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-s)s^k}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}.
\end{equation}
The gap law has full support on \(\mathbb N_0\) and an exponential tail.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After a click the next hidden state is \(D\), independently of the earlier
record.  Thus a gap of \(k\) zeros followed by a click has probability
\eqref{eq:gap-main}.  Since \(T_0\) is lower triangular,
\[
\textstyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}s^jp^{k-1-j}=0\quad(k=0),
\]
with the displayed sum interpreted as empty when \(k=0\), and
\[
T_0^k=
\begin{pmatrix}
p^k&0\\
b\textstyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}s^jp^{k-1-j}&s^k
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Therefore
\[
e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1
=b(1-p)\textstyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}s^jp^{k-1-j}+as^k,
\]
where the comma only separates the display from the following substitution.
For \(p\ne s\),
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}s^jp^{k-1-j}=\frac{p^k-s^k}{p-s}.
\]
Substituting
\[
a=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)-\Gamma(1-s)}
        {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad
b=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(p-s)}
        {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}
\]
therefore gives
\[
b(1-p)\frac{p^k-s^k}{p-s}+as^k
=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-s)s^k}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:gap-closed-main}.  If \(p=s\), equivalently
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}=\Gamma\), the same identity is read by the continuous
equal-rate dictionary \eqref{eq:equal-rate-dictionary-main}.  All terms are
probabilities of disjoint paths, so they are positive, and the two survival
factors lie in \((0,1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Support of finite binary words]\label{cor:word-support-main}
Every finite binary word has positive probability under the stationary
constrained D-MAP record.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The gap law in \Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main} has \(g_k>0\) for every
\(k\ge0\).  Decompose any finite word into the observed portions of successive
zero runs between clicks, extending at the two ends if needed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact dependence, hazard, and count diagnostics}

The hazard, Fano, and low-lag formulas in this subsection have the inferential
status fixed in \hyperref[par:inference-scope-main]{the inference-scope
paragraph}.  In particular, the only scalar interval below that is fully
studentized from the visible record is the lag-two covariance interval for
\(\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)\) in
\Cref{cor:finite-record-lag-two-studentized-main}.  The hazard, spectral
density, and Fano displays in this subsection are population identities or
inputs to later sandwich/projection procedures, not stand-alone studentized
confidence intervals.

\begin{lemma}[Stationary hidden law]\label{lem:stationary-main}
For \(P=T_0+T_1=\begin{psmallmatrix}p&q\\ b&1-b\end{psmallmatrix}\), the
stationary hidden law is
\[
\pi=\left(\frac b{q+b},\frac{q}{q+b}\right).
\]
With \(q=1-p\) as fixed in \Cref{lem:equal-rate-critical-convention-main}, the
stationary click density is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho-main}
\rho=\pi T_1\mathbf1=\frac{q(1-s)}{q+b}.
\end{equation}
Moreover \(a<q(1-s)<q\), and therefore
\(a-\rho=-b(q-a)/(q+b)<0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Solving \(\pi P=\pi\) gives the displayed law.  Since
\(u=T_1\mathbf1=(q,a)^\top\) and \(a+b=1-s\), \(\rho=\pi u\) gives
\eqref{eq:rho-main}.  For \(r>0\),
\[
1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}<1-e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}=q,
\]
so the defining integral for \(a\) gives
\[
a<q\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}\,dr
=q(1-s)<q .
\]
The sign follows from
\[
a-\rho=-\frac{b(q-a)}{q+b}
\]
and the strict positivity of \(b\) and \(q-a\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Covariance transfer through the hidden mode]\label{lem:covariance-transfer-main}
For the stationary D-MAP record,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cov-transfer-main}
\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)
=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}
\end{equation}
for \(n\ge1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With \(q=1-p\) and \(u=(q,a)^\top\),
\[
u-\rho\mathbf1=\frac{q-a}{q+b}\binom {q}{-b},
\qquad
P\binom {q}{-b}=(p-b)\binom {q}{-b}.
\]
Thus
\[
P^{n-1}u=\rho\mathbf1+
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{n-1}(u-\rho\mathbf1).
\]
A click at time \(0\) sets the next hidden state to \(D\).  Hence
\[
\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)
=e_D^\top P^{n-1}u
=\rho+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{n-1}(a-\rho),
\]
because the \(D\)-component of \(u-\rho\mathbf1\) is \(a-\rho\).  Multiplying
by \(\rho=\PP(A_0=1)\) and subtracting \(\rho^2\) gives the covariance in
\eqref{eq:cov-transfer-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Critical-rate uniqueness and covariance phase]\label{lem:critical-rate-main}
For fixed \(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\), the equation
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}(\Gamma)=0\) has the unique positive solution
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) defined by the
piecewise non-removable branch
\eqref{eq:gamma-crit-piecewise-main}.  When
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}\ne\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), this is equivalently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-c-main}
\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}e^{-\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}\Delta\tau}
=\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}.
\end{equation}
The displayed scalar equation also has the diagonal solution
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), but that solution is removable
for \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\) unless
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau=1\).  Thus on the diagonal
\(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), the zero occurs only at
\(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}=1/\Delta\tau\).
The sign of \(\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)\) is the sign of
\(-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The uniqueness is the branch statement in
\Cref{lem:equal-rate-critical-convention-main}.  The covariance sign follows
from \(\rho>0\) and \(a-\rho<0\) in \Cref{lem:stationary-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Hidden-mode contraction]\label{lem:hidden-mode-contraction-main}
For every positive constrained D-MAP setting,
\[
|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|<1 .
\]
In particular the shifted geometric Fourier series in
\Cref{prop:spectrum-main} is absolutely convergent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The transition matrix
\[
P=T_0+T_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
p&1-p\\ b&1-b
\end{pmatrix}
\]
is a two-state stochastic matrix with strictly positive off-diagonal entries
\(1-p>0\) and \(b>0\).  Its eigenvalues are \(1\) and
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b\).  The upper bound is immediate from \(p<1\)
and \(b>0\): \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}<1\).  For the lower bound, \(p>0\) and
\[
b=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr
<\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}\,dr
=1-s<1 .
\]
Thus \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b>-1\).  Hence
\(|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|<1\).  Absolute convergence follows from
\(\sum_{n\ge1}|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|^{n-1}<\infty\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite hidden-start path-space contraction]\label{lem:finite-hidden-start-path-contraction-main}
Fix one positive constrained D-MAP setting and put \(P=T_0+T_1\).  Let
\(\pi\) be the stationary hidden law.  For a hidden initial law \(\nu\), let
\(\PP_\nu\) denote the one-sided hidden/visible law started from \(H_0\sim\nu\),
and let \(\PP_\pi\) denote the stationary-start law.  There are constants
\(C<\infty\) and \(\theta\in(0,1)\), depending only on the fixed setting, such
that for every burn-in length \(L\ge0\), every retained length \(N\ge1\), and
every set \(B\subset\{0,1\}^N\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-hidden-start-path-tv-main}
\left|
\PP_\nu\bigl((A_L,\ldots,A_{L+N-1})\in B\bigr)
-
\PP_\pi\bigl((A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1})\in B\bigr)
\right|
\le C\theta^L .
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
\sup_{B\subset\{0,1\}^N}
\left|
\PP_\nu\bigl((A_L,\ldots,A_{L+N-1})\in B\bigr)
-
\PP_\pi\bigl((A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1})\in B\bigr)
\right|
\le C\theta^L ,
\]
up to the harmless convention-dependent factor in the definition of total
variation.  The bound is uniform in \(N\).  Consequently, if \(L_N\to\infty\)
and \(S_N\) is any measurable statistic of the retained visible word
\((A_{L_N},\ldots,A_{L_N+N-1})\), then every stationary-start convergence in
distribution, convergence in probability, or event-probability limit for
\(S_N\) transfers unchanged to the finite hidden start.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a word \(w=(w_0,\ldots,w_{N-1})\), write
\(T_w=T_{w_0}\cdots T_{w_{N-1}}\).  For a set
\(B\subset\{0,1\}^N\), put \(M_B=\sum_{w\in B}T_w\).  Since
\(\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^N}T_w=P^N\), the matrix \(M_B\) is substochastic: its
entries are nonnegative and \(0\le M_B\mathbf1\le\mathbf1\) coordinatewise.
The retained visible word probability from the finite start is therefore
\[
\PP_\nu\bigl((A_L,\ldots,A_{L+N-1})\in B\bigr)
=\nu P^L M_B\mathbf1,
\]
while stationarity gives
\[
\PP_\pi\bigl((A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1})\in B\bigr)
=\pi M_B\mathbf1 .
\]
Thus the difference is
\[
(\nu P^L-\pi)M_B\mathbf1 .
\]
Because \(0\le M_B\mathbf1\le\mathbf1\), the total-variation dual inequality
gives
\[
\left|(\nu P^L-\pi)M_B\mathbf1\right|
\le \|\nu P^L-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}} .
\]

It remains only to bound the hidden-chain term.  The matrix
\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}p&1-p\\ b&1-b\end{pmatrix}
\]
has strictly positive off-diagonal entries, stationary law
\(\pi=(b/(1-p+b),(1-p)/(1-p+b))\), and nontrivial eigenvalue
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b\).  By
\Cref{lem:hidden-mode-contraction-main}, \(|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|<1\).
On the two-state zero-sum subspace, multiplication by \(P\) is multiplication
by \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\).  Hence, for every initial law \(\nu\),
\[
\|\nu P^L-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le C|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|^L
\]
with a finite constant \(C\) uniform over \(\nu\).  Taking any
\(\theta\in(|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|,1)\), or \(\theta=|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|\)
when \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\ne0\) and increasing \(C\) if desired, proves
\eqref{eq:finite-hidden-start-path-tv-main}.

The final assertion follows because every statistic of the retained word is a
measurable image of that \(N\)-word.  Total variation cannot increase under a
measurable map, so the law of \(S_N\) under the finite hidden start differs
from its stationary-start law by at most \(C\theta^{L_N}\to0\).  Applying this
bound to continuity sets gives distributional convergence; applying it to the
events \(\{|S_N-s|>\varepsilon\}\), or to the events appearing in a stated
probability limit, gives convergence in probability and event-probability
limits.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Hazard obstruction to finite symbolic memory]\label{lem:hazard-obstruction-main}
A renewal binary process with full gap support is a finite-order Markov chain
only if its gap hazard is eventually constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a renewal process, after observing \(k\) consecutive zeros since the last
click, the conditional click probability is the zero-run hazard \(h_k\).
If an \(r\)-step Markov rule generated the same full-support law, then all
histories with \(k\ge r\) trailing zeros would have the same last \(r\) symbols
and hence the same click probability.  Therefore \(h_k\) would be constant for
all \(k\ge r\).

This condition concerns a transition rule conditioned on the most recent
symbols.  It is different from finite dependence of separated sigma-fields:
\(1\)-dependence says that events separated by one unobserved sample
factorize, while an \(r\)-step Markov rule says that every future one-step
transition probability is a function of only the last \(r\) symbols.  For a
renewal record with full gap support, the latter would force the click
probability after a long zero run to stop depending on the run length.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cylinder factorization at the memory threshold]\label{lem:kernel-factorization-main}
If \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), then \(P=T_0+T_1=\mathbf1\pi\).  More
explicitly, let \(u=(u_0,\ldots,u_{r-1})\) and
\(v=(v_0,\ldots,v_{\ell-1})\) be arbitrary finite binary words and write
\(T_u=T_{u_0}\cdots T_{u_{r-1}}\), \(T_v=T_{v_0}\cdots T_{v_{\ell-1}}\).
If the word \(u\) is observed on one block and \(v\) is observed on a second
block beginning two samples later, with the intervening sample unobserved,
then
\[
\PP(A_0^{r-1}=u,\ A_{r+1}^{r+\ell}=v)
=\pi T_u P T_v\mathbf1
=\bigl(\pi T_u\mathbf1\bigr)\bigl(\pi T_v\mathbf1\bigr).
\]
Consequently every two visible blocks separated by one unobserved sample are
independent, so the visible record is exact \(1\)-dependent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The second eigenvalue of \(P\) is \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\), while the first is
\(1\).  At \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), the primitive stochastic matrix
\(P\) has rank one and both rows equal \(\pi\), hence \(P=\mathbf1\pi\).
For fixed words \(u\) and \(v\), summing over the unobserved sample between
the two displayed blocks inserts \(T_0+T_1=P\).  The stationary cylinder
probability is therefore
\[
\pi T_u(T_0+T_1)T_v\mathbf1=\pi T_uPT_v\mathbf1.
\]
At the threshold,
\[
\pi T_uPT_v\mathbf1
=\pi T_u\mathbf1\,\pi T_v\mathbf1
=\PP(A_0^{r-1}=u)\PP(A_0^{\ell-1}=v),
\]
where the last equality is stationarity applied to the two single-block
cylinders.  Thus every pair of finite cylinder events supported on two
finite blocks with one omitted sample between them factorizes.  Translating
the indices gives the same statement for any blocks
\(\{i,\ldots,m\}\) and \(\{m+2,\ldots,j\}\).  Since finite cylinders generate
the corresponding finite-block sigma algebras, the two finite-block sigma
algebras are independent.  Letting the left and right block lengths increase
and using the monotone-class theorem gives independence of
\(\sigma(A_j:j\le m)\) and \(\sigma(A_j:j\ge m+2)\) for every \(m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact \texorpdfstring{\(1\)}{1}-dependence criterion]\label{lem:one-dep-criterion-main}
The constrained D-MAP record is exact \(1\)-dependent iff
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), equivalently
\(\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The forward direction at the threshold is
\Cref{lem:kernel-factorization-main}.  Conversely, exact \(1\)-dependence
would make \(A_0\) and \(A_2\) independent.  But
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}},
\]
and the prefactor is nonzero by \Cref{lem:stationary-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Survival tails and hazard monotonicity]\label{lem:survival-hazard-main}
For \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\ne\Gamma\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:survival-tail-main}
S_k=\PP(G\ge k)=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hazard-main}
h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k)=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-s)s^k}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k}.
\end{equation}
The equal-rate limit is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equal-rate-survival-hazard-main}
\PP(G\ge k)=p^k(1+\Gamma\Delta\tau\,k),
\qquad
h_k=
\frac{(1-p)+\Gamma\Delta\tau(k(1-p)-p)}
     {1+\Gamma\Delta\tau\,k}.
\end{equation}
In all cases \(h_{k+1}>h_k\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Summing \eqref{eq:gap-closed-main} over \(j\ge k\) gives
\eqref{eq:survival-tail-main}.  Since \(g_k=S_k-S_{k+1}\), the hazard has the
equivalent survival-ratio form
\[
h_k=\frac{g_k}{S_k}=1-\frac{S_{k+1}}{S_k}.
\]
Substituting \eqref{eq:survival-tail-main} into this identity gives
\eqref{eq:hazard-main}.  The equal-rate display is the continuous limit.
For the unequal-rate case, subtracting the two survival ratios gives
\[
h_{k+1}-h_k
=\frac{\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}(p-s)^2p^ks^k}
        {(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k)
         (\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^{k+1}-\Gamma s^{k+1})}
\]
and the denominator has positive product sign.  Indeed
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^m-\Gamma s^m
=(\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma)S_m,\qquad m=k,k+1,
\]
by \eqref{eq:survival-tail-main}.  Since \(S_m=\PP(G\ge m)>0\), both factors
have the sign of \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma\): they are both positive when
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}>\Gamma\) and both negative when
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}<\Gamma\).  Their product is therefore
\((\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma)^2S_kS_{k+1}>0\).  The numerator is also
positive because \(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}>0\), \(p,s\in(0,1)\), and
\(p\ne s\) in the unequal-rate case.

On the equal-rate diagonal, write
\(y=\Gamma\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\) and
\(p=e^{-y}\).  The displayed equal-rate hazard can be rewritten as
\[
h_k
=1-p-\frac{y p}{1+y k}.
\]
Thus
\[
h_{k+1}-h_k
=yp\left(\frac1{1+yk}-\frac1{1+y(k+1)}\right)
=\frac{y^2p}{(1+yk)(1+y(k+1))}>0.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:main-record-wrapper}]
The kernels are those of \Cref{prop:instrument-kernels-main}.  Both nonzero
entries of \(T_1\) land in the \(D\)-column.  Thus, after a click, the next
hidden state is \(D\), independently of the pre-click hidden state and of the
earlier visible record.  Click epochs are therefore regeneration epochs for
the future visible record, and a Palm gap with \(k\) intervening zeros has
probability
\[
g_k=\PP(G=k)=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1,\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
Here
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1\mathbf1=(1-p,a)^\top .
\]
For \(k\ge1\), lower-triangular multiplication gives
\[
T_0^k=
\begin{pmatrix}
p^k&0\\
b\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}s^jp^{k-1-j}&s^k
\end{pmatrix};
\]
for \(k=0\), the same formula is read with the sum empty and \(T_0^0=I\).
Consequently
\[
g_k=b(1-p)\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}s^jp^{k-1-j}+as^k ,
\]
including \(g_0=a\).  If \(p\ne s\), then
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}s^jp^{k-1-j}=\frac{p^k-s^k}{p-s}.
\]
Using the scalar identities
\[
b=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(p-s)}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad
a=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)-\Gamma(1-s)}
        {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
one obtains
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_k
&=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)(p^k-s^k)}
        {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}
  +\frac{\{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)-\Gamma(1-s)\}s^k}
        {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma} \\
&=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-s)s^k}
        {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the displayed off-diagonal gap law.  The case \(p=s\), equivalently
\(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), is the continuous equal-rate limit recorded in
\eqref{eq:equal-rate-dictionary-main}.  Since the path that remains in \(D\)
for \(k\) zero-labelled steps and then clicks has probability \(s^ka>0\), every
\(g_k\) is positive.  Thus the renewal gap law has full support.

For the hazard, summing the just-proved gap law gives, off the equal-rate
diagonal,
\[
S_k=\PP(G\ge k)=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}.
\]
Since \(g_k=S_k-S_{k+1}\),
\[
h_k=\frac{g_k}{S_k}=1-\frac{S_{k+1}}{S_k}.
\]
This hazard formula is a population diagnostic; the only fully studentized
visible-record scalar interval proved in this paper is the lag-two
\(\gamma_2\) interval of
\Cref{cor:finite-record-lag-two-studentized-main}.
Substitution yields the theorem's hazard formula and
\[
h_{k+1}-h_k
=\frac{\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}(p-s)^2p^ks^k}
        {(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k)
         (\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^{k+1}-\Gamma s^{k+1})}.
\]
The denominator product is positive because
\[
(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k)
(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^{k+1}-\Gamma s^{k+1})
=(\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma)^2S_kS_{k+1}>0,
\]
so \(h_{k+1}>h_k\) off the diagonal.  On the equal-rate diagonal,
\eqref{eq:equal-rate-dictionary-main} gives
\[
h_k=1-p-\frac{yp}{1+yk},\qquad
h_{k+1}-h_k=
\frac{y^2p}{(1+yk)(1+y(k+1))}>0,
\]
where \(y=\Gamma\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\).  Hence the
zero-run hazard is strictly increasing in all positive parameter cases.

It remains to verify the covariance and dependence assertions.  Let
\(P=T_0+T_1\) and \(u=T_1\mathbf1=(1-p,a)^\top\).  By
\Cref{lem:stationary-main}, the stationary hidden law is \(\pi\), the click
density is \(\rho=\pi u\), and \(a-\rho\ne0\).  The two eigenvalues of \(P\)
are \(1\) and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b\).  The centered click vector lies
in the hidden eigenspace:
\[
u-\rho\mathbf1=\frac{1-p-a}{1-p+b}\binom{1-p}{-b},
\qquad
P^{n-1}u=\rho\mathbf1+
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}(u-\rho\mathbf1).
\]
Conditioning on \(A_0=1\) again leaves the next hidden state equal to \(D\);
therefore
\[
\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)
=e_D^\top P^{n-1}u
=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}.
\]
Multiplying by \(\PP(A_0=1)=\rho\) and subtracting \(\rho^2\) gives
\[
\gamma_n=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},\qquad n\ge1 .
\]

If \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), then \(\det P=0\).  Since \(P\) is stochastic,
both rows of \(P\) equal the stationary row \(\pi\), so \(P=\mathbf1\pi\).
Summing over one unobserved sample between two visible blocks inserts this
rank-one matrix and factorizes the two cylinder probabilities, as in
\Cref{lem:kernel-factorization-main}; hence the visible record is
\(1\)-dependent.  Conversely, \(1\)-dependence makes \(A_0\) and \(A_2\)
independent, so \(0=\gamma_2=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\).  The
prefactor is nonzero by \Cref{lem:stationary-main}, and hence
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\).

Finally, suppose the renewal record were an \(r\)-step Markov chain.  Because
all finite zero runs have positive probability, the conditional click
probability after a run of \(k\) zeros would depend only on the final \(r\)
zeros once \(k\ge r\).  Thus \(h_k\) would be eventually constant.  The strict
increase proved above rules this out for every \(r\), so the visible process
is not a finite-order Markov chain.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Threshold finite-window count variance]\label{prop:threshold-finite-window-count-main}
Fix the constrained D-MAP kernels of
\Cref{prop:instrument-kernels-main} in stationarity and suppose that the
hidden transfer mode is at the exact-dependence threshold
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\).  For
\[
N_m=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}A_n,\qquad m\ge1,
\]
one has the exact finite-window identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threshold-finite-window-count-main}
\Var(N_m)
=m\rho(1-\rho)+2(m-1)\rho(a-\rho).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the finite-window variance is the one-dependent covariance
sum with only the adjacent covariance retained.

The paper uses only \eqref{eq:threshold-finite-window-count-main}.  Higher
finite-window count laws at the threshold are obtained from the ordinary
probability generating polynomial
\[
P_m(z)=\EE z^{N_m}
=\sum_{u\in\{0,1\}^m}z^{u_0+\cdots+u_{m-1}}
  \,\PP(A_0=u_0,\ldots,A_{m-1}=u_{m-1}),
\]
with the cylinder probabilities evaluated by the renewal inclusion product
of \Cref{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}.  This is the finite-window
specialization of the standard \(1\)-dependent count generating-function
bookkeeping; no separate closed formula for every higher count law is used in
the sequel.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Stationarity gives the exact variance decomposition
\[
\Var(N_m)
=m\gamma_0+2\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}(m-h)\gamma_h,
\qquad
\gamma_h=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_h).
\]
Here \(\gamma_0=\rho(1-\rho)\).  The covariance tail proved in
\Cref{thm:main-record-wrapper} is
\[
\gamma_h=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,h-1},
\qquad h\ge1.
\]
At \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), exact \(1\)-dependence is
\Cref{lem:one-dep-criterion-main} and the displayed tail leaves
\(\gamma_1=\rho(a-\rho)\) and \(\gamma_h=0\) for every \(h\ge2\).  Substitution
in the stationary variance sum gives
\[
\Var(N_m)
=m\rho(1-\rho)+2(m-1)\rho(a-\rho),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:threshold-finite-window-count-main}.

For the final assertion, the polynomial \(P_m\) is just the definition of the
finite-window count distribution.  At the threshold the law is \(1\)-dependent
by \Cref{lem:one-dep-criterion-main}; the same finite cylinder probabilities
are explicitly determined from renewal inclusion products in
\Cref{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}.  Taking coefficients of \(P_m\) therefore
recovers the count probabilities.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Discrete renewal count-variance identity]\label{lem:discrete-renewal-fano-main}
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be a stationary binary renewal process on the
sample lattice, with click epochs separated by i.i.d. integer cycle lengths
\(L_j\ge1\), mean \(\mu=\EE L_j<\infty\), variance
\(\sigma_L^2=\Var(L_j)<\infty\), and exponentially decaying tails.  For
\(N_m=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}A_n\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discrete-renewal-fano-identity-main}
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\Var(N_m)}{\EE N_m}
=\frac{\sigma_L^2}{\mu^2}.
\end{equation}
If, in addition, the autocovariances are summable and \(f(0)\) denotes the
zero-frequency spectral density
\(\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_0)+2\sum_{h\ge1}\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_h)\),
then the same limit equals \(f(0)/\rho\), where \(\rho=\EE A_0=1/\mu\).
Throughout this paper \(f\) uses this covariance-series convention:
\[
f(\theta)=\gamma_0+2\sum_{h\ge1}\gamma_h\cos(h\theta),
\qquad
\gamma_h=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_h).
\]
There is no \(2\pi\) normalization in \(f\); with the Fourier-transform
convention \(S(\theta)=(2\pi)^{-1}\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}\gamma_he^{-ih\theta}\),
one has \(f(\theta)=2\pi S(\theta)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work first in Palm coordinates with a click at the left endpoint and write
\(S_k=L_1+\cdots+L_k\) and
\(K_m=\max\{k:S_k\le m\}\).  The stationary count \(N_m\) differs from the
corresponding Palm count only through the initial delayed cycle and the
terminal residual cycle.  The stationary residual cycle has the size-biased
cycle tail, which is still exponentially decaying, and the maximum of the
complete cycles intersecting \([0,m)\) is \(O_{\PP}(\log m)\).  Hence this
endpoint replacement changes \(m^{-1/2}(N_m-\EE N_m)\) by \(o_{\PP}(1)\), and
the squared normalized family is uniformly integrable.

At the renewal stopping index,
\[
S_{K_m}\le m<S_{K_m+1}.
\]
Thus, with \(W_j=L_j-\mu\),
\[
\mu\left(K_m-\frac m\mu\right)
=-\sum_{j=1}^{K_m}W_j+O_{\PP}(\log m).
\]
Since \(K_m/m\to1/\mu\) and the partial sums of the centered i.i.d. sequence
\((W_j)\) satisfy the usual random-time-change estimate,
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{K_m}W_j
=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor m/\mu\rfloor}W_j+o_{\PP}(\sqrt m).
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{K_m-m/\mu}{\sqrt m}
=-\frac1\mu\,
\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor m/\mu\rfloor}W_j}{\sqrt m}
+o_{\PP}(1),
\]
and uniform integrability yields
\[
\frac{\Var(N_m)}m
\longrightarrow
\frac{\sigma_L^2}{\mu^3}.
\]
Stationarity gives \(\EE N_m=m\rho=m/\mu\), which proves
\eqref{eq:discrete-renewal-fano-identity-main}.

For the spectral form, stationarity gives the exact sampled-window identity
\[
\Var(N_m)=
m\gamma_0+2\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}(m-h)\gamma_h,\qquad
\gamma_h=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_h).
\]
Summability of \((\gamma_h)\) implies
\[
\frac{\Var(N_m)}m\longrightarrow
\gamma_0+2\sum_{h\ge1}\gamma_h=f(0).
\]
Dividing by \(\EE N_m/m=\rho\) gives the equivalent limit \(f(0)/\rho\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral density and classical sub-Poisson count noise for the constrained D-MAP renewal record]\label{prop:spectrum-main}
Fix one positive constrained D-MAP setting
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\), let \(T_0,T_1\) be the
killed-leakage labelled kernels in \eqref{eq:t0-t1-main} with
\(p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}\), \(s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\), and
the lower-row weights \(a,b\) in \eqref{eq:a-b-main}.  Thus
\(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\), \(P=T_0+T_1\) is stochastic, \(0<p,s<1\),
\(a>0\), \(b>0\), and \(a+b=1-s\).  Let
\((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be the stationary visible binary process generated
by these kernels, put \(\rho=\PP(A_0=1)\) and
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b\), and interpret equal-rate expressions by
continuous limits when \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).  The stationary
covariance spectral density is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrum-main}
f(\theta)=
\rho(1-\rho)+
2\rho(a-\rho)
\frac{\cos\theta-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}
     {1-2\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\cos\theta+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^2}.
\end{equation}
This is the covariance-series normalization
\[
f(\theta)=\gamma_0+2\sum_{n\ge1}\gamma_n\cos(n\theta),
\qquad
\gamma_n=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n),
\]
so no \(2\pi\) factor is included in \(f\).
The displayed \(f(\theta)\) is a population spectral diagnostic; it is not a
fully studentized finite-record interval.  The only scalar interval in the
paper with a visible-record studentizer is the lag-two
\(\gamma_2\) interval in
\Cref{cor:finite-record-lag-two-studentized-main}.
If \(N_m=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}A_n\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fano-main}
0\le
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\Var(N_m)}{\EE N_m}
=\frac{\Var(G+1)}{\bigl(\EE(G+1)\bigr)^2}
=1-\rho+\frac{2(a-\rho)}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}
<1,
\end{equation}
with \(G\) distributed as in \Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}.
This Fano display has the same population status: finite-record uncertainty
for quotient coordinates is handled later by sandwich/projection statements,
whereas the only fully studentized scalar interval is the lag-two
\(\gamma_2\) interval.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Insert the covariance tail from \Cref{lem:covariance-transfer-main} into the Herglotz
Fourier series:
\[
f(\theta)
=\rho(1-\rho)
+2\rho(a-\rho)\sum_{n\ge1}
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}\cos(n\theta).
\]
The exponent is \(n-1\), not \(n\), because the covariance tail begins with
\(\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_1)=\rho(a-\rho)\).  Thus the relevant geometric
series is the shifted series below; using
\(\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{n}e^{in\theta}\) would insert an
extraneous factor of \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\) and give the wrong numerator.
Since \(|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|<1\),
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}e^{in\theta}
=
\frac{e^{i\theta}}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}e^{i\theta}},
\]
and taking real parts gives
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}\cos(n\theta)
=
\frac{\cos\theta-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}
       {1-2\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\cos\theta+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^2}.
\]
This includes the exact \(1\)-dependent case
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), where only the adjacent covariance contributes.
For this discrete count convention,
\Cref{lem:discrete-renewal-fano-main} gives, with \(L=G+1\),
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\Var(N_m)}{\EE N_m}
=\frac{\Var(L)}{(\EE L)^2}.
\]
The same lemma identifies this renewal-count limit with \(f(0)/\rho\), since
\(\rho=1/\EE L\) for the stationary renewal record.  Evaluating the displayed
spectral density at \(\theta=0\) gives the direct cycle-variance computation
\[
\frac{\Var(G+1)}{\bigl(\EE(G+1)\bigr)^2}
=\frac{f(0)}{\rho}
=1-\rho+\frac{2(a-\rho)}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}.
\]
The lower bound in \eqref{eq:fano-main} is therefore inherited from the
nonnegative variance quotient, not from the sign of the closed algebraic
correction.  The upper bound is direct: \(\rho>0\), \(a-\rho<0\) by
\Cref{lem:stationary-main}, and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}<1\) by
\Cref{lem:hidden-mode-contraction-main}.  Hence
\[
\frac{f(0)}{\rho}-1
=-\rho+\frac{2(a-\rho)}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}<0.
\]
\end{proof}

For the fixed constrained D-MAP kernels in

\subsection{Renewal inclusion products and quotient inverse}

In Markovian-arrival-process language, the pair \((T_0,T_1)\) in
\eqref{eq:t0-t1-main} is a constrained two-state discrete-time MAP:
\(T_0+T_1\) is stochastic, \(T_0\) records a no-arrival transition, and
\(T_1\) records a one-arrival transition.  This places the model inside the
MAP/D-MAP family introduced and developed for correlated arrival streams
\cite{Neuts1979MAP,Lucantoni1991BMAP,AlfaNeuts1995DMAP,
Chakravarthy2010MAPSurvey,AsanjaraniNazarathy2022MAP}.  The closest
representation comparators are the D-MAP construction of
\cite{AlfaNeuts1995DMAP} and the MAP2 canonical-form and weak-equivalence
results of
\cite{Ryden1996Aggregated,BodrogHeindlHorvathTelek2008MAP2Canonical,
RamirezCoboLilloWiper2010MAP2Nonidentifiability,
RamirezCoboLillo2012WeakEquivalentMAP}, which show that hidden MAP and
phase-type representations are generally nonunique and must be quotiented or
put in a specified representation chart.  The theorem below proves a different
and narrower statement for the sampled killed-leakage D-MAP(2) subfamily:
three visible inclusion probabilities reduce the renewal law to the quotient
coordinates \((p+s,ps)\), so the visible law identifies the unordered pair
\(\{p,s\}\) and no ordered branch label beyond it.

\begin{corollary}[Renewal inclusion and finite word law]\label{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}
For a stationary renewal click set \(\varXi\) with intensity \(\rho\) and Palm
renewal masses \(u_n=\PP(n\in\varXi\mid0\in\varXi)\), every nonempty finite
ordered set \(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renewal-inclusion-product-main}
\PP(B\subset\varXi)
=\rho\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}.
\end{equation}
For the empty set this inclusion probability is \(1\).  Exact finite
configuration probabilities follow by finite inclusion-exclusion:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-exact-configuration-probabilities-main}
\PP(\varXi\cap I=S)
=\sum_{C\subset I\setminus S}(-1)^{|C|}
\PP(S\cup C\subset\varXi).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Condition on the leftmost click in \(B\).  Under the Palm law at that click,
the future click process renews, so the conditional probability of the
successive gaps \(i_{r+1}-i_r\) being occupied is
\(\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}\).  Multiplying by the stationary click
intensity \(\rho\) gives \eqref{eq:renewal-inclusion-product-main}.  The
finite word formula is the ordinary inclusion-exclusion identity over the
unprescribed click sites \(I\setminus S\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Constrained D-MAP specialization of the renewal product notation]\label{cor:boundary_reset_toeplitz_renewal_specialization}\label{cor:finite_window_principal_minor_word_law}\label{prop:finite_inclusion_tail_phase}
Fix one positive constrained D-MAP setting
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\), and let
\((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be the stationary visible binary process generated
by the killed-leakage kernels \eqref{eq:t0-t1-main} with
\(p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}\), \(s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\), the
positive \(a,b\) from \eqref{eq:a-b-main}, \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\), and
continuous equal-rate interpretation on \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-inclusion-u-n-main}
u_n=\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)
=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1.
\end{equation}
Thus the constrained D-MAP renewal inclusion products are obtained from
\eqref{eq:renewal-inclusion-product-main} by this \(u_n\), and finite word
laws are given by
\eqref{eq:finite-exact-configuration-probabilities-main}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The formula for \(u_n\) is \Cref{lem:covariance-transfer-main}.  Insert that
Palm renewal mass into \Cref{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Symmetric-coordinate algebra for the quotient inverse]\label{lem:quotient-inverse-symmetric-algebra-main}
Let \(p\in(0,1)\), put \(q=1-p\), and let the constrained D-MAP parameters satisfy
\[
a+b=1-s,\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b,\qquad
\rho=\frac{q(1-s)}{q+b}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-inverse-s1-algebra-main}
1-a+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p+s
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-inverse-s2-algebra-main}
\rho(1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}})-a+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=ps .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(a+b=1-s\), we have \(a=1-s-b\).  Therefore
\[
1-a+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=1-(1-s-b)+(p-b)
=p+s,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:quotient-inverse-s1-algebra-main}.  For the second
identity, first note that
\[
1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=1-p+b=q+b.
\]
Thus
\[
\rho(1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}})
=\frac{q(1-s)}{q+b}\,(q+b)
=q(1-s).
\]
Combining this with \(a=1-s-b\) and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b\) gives
\[
\rho(1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}})-a+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=q(1-s)-(1-s-b)+(p-b)
=q(1-s)+p+s-1.
\]
Because \(p+q=1\), the last expression is
\[
q(1-s)+p+s-1
=s-qs
=ps,
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exchange symmetry in dimensionless survival coordinates]\label{lem:gap-law-exchange-symmetry-main}
Put
\[
x=-\log p=\Gamma\Delta\tau,\qquad
y=-\log s=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau,
\qquad p=e^{-x},\qquad s=e^{-y}.
\]
For \(x\ne y\), the zero-run law in \eqref{eq:gap-closed-main} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main}
g_k=
\frac{y(1-p)p^k-x(1-s)s^k}{y-x},
\qquad k\ge0.
\end{equation}
The hidden eigenvalue in \eqref{eq:lambda-hid-main} is, in the same
coordinates,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-lambda-hid-main}
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=\frac{y s-x p}{y-x}
=\frac{y e^{-y}-x e^{-x}}{y-x}.
\end{equation}
Both \eqref{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main} and
\eqref{eq:symmetric-lambda-hid-main} are invariant under the simultaneous
exchange \((x,p)\leftrightarrow(y,s)\).  The equal-rate laws are their
continuous limits.  Consequently the stationary visible renewal law is
unchanged by exchanging \(\Gamma\) and \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Multiplying numerator and denominator in \eqref{eq:gap-closed-main} by
\(\Delta\tau\) gives \eqref{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main}.  Similarly,
\eqref{eq:lambda-hid-main} becomes
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}s-\Gamma p}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}
=\frac{y s-x p}{y-x}.
\]
Under \((x,p)\leftrightarrow(y,s)\), the numerator
\[
y(1-p)p^k-x(1-s)s^k
\]
and the denominator \(y-x\) both change sign, so \(g_k\) is unchanged.  The
same sign cancellation gives
\[
y s-x p\longmapsto x p-y s=-(y s-x p),
\qquad
y-x\longmapsto x-y=-(y-x),
\]
and hence \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\) is unchanged.  The diagonal formulas
follow by taking the continuous limits \(x\to y\).  Since
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main} proves that the visible law is the stationary
renewal law with gap probabilities \(g_k\), the unordered pair
\(\{(x,p),(y,s)\}\) determines the whole gap distribution.  It also determines
the stationary renewal intensity \(1/\EE(G+1)\).  Therefore the stationary
renewal measures generated before and after the exchange have the same
finite-dimensional visible distributions.
\end{proof}

\begin{convention}\label{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}
\textbf{Identifiable estimands and branch-map convention.}
From one stationary visible record the identifiable objects are the inclusion
vector \((r_0,r_1,r_2)\), the quotient
\(\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps)\) on \(D=r_1-r_0^2\ne0\), the unordered survival-factor
pair \(\{p,s\}\), and visible functionals such as
\(\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)\).  A separated ordered labelled-rate
vector \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\) is reported only when
\(\Delta_\sigma=(p-s)^2>0\) and a branch map \(\mathcal B\) assigns the two
roots to the attempt and recovery labels.  Theorem statements condition on a
deterministic branch map; estimated branch maps are reduced to this case in
\Cref{app:branch-map-oracle-reduction-main}.  On the exchange diagonal
\((2z,z^2)\), the invariant visible-law estimand is the common numerical rate
\(-\log z/\Delta\tau\), not a separated ordered chart.
\end{convention}

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem 2: quotient inverse]\label{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}
Work under the constrained D-MAP record model of
\Cref{ass:constrained-dmap-record-main}.  Thus \(p,s,a,b,T_0,T_1\) and the
stationary visible process \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) are the quantities fixed
there; in particular \(0<p,s<1\), \(a>0\), \(b>0\), \(a+b=1-s\), and
equal-rate formulas are interpreted by continuous limits.  Let
\[
r_0=\rho,\qquad r_1=\rho a,\qquad r_2=\rho u_2 .
\]
The population inverse is defined on the domain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:population-inverse-domain-main}
r_0>0,\qquad a-r_0\ne0
\quad
\left(\text{equivalently } r_0>0,\ r_1-r_0^2\ne0\right).
\end{equation}
We write the inverse-map denominator as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-map-denominator-main}
D:=r_1-r_0^2=r_0(a-r_0).
\end{equation}
For the constrained D-MAP kernels this domain condition holds:
\(r_0=\rho>0\) and \(a-r_0=a-\rho<0\) by
\Cref{lem:stationary-main}.  On this domain,
\[
a=\frac{r_1}{r_0},\qquad
u_2=\frac{r_2}{r_0},\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=\frac{u_2-r_0}{a-r_0}
=\frac{r_2-r_0^2}{r_1-r_0^2}.
\]
The symmetric coordinate vector is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-symmetric-coordinates-main}
\sigma_{\rm sym}=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2),\qquad
\sigma_1=1-a+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}},\qquad
\sigma_2=r_0(1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}})-a+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}.
\end{equation}
They equal
\[
\sigma_1=p+s,\qquad \sigma_2=ps,
\]
so the visible law determines the unordered survival-factor pair
\[
\{p,s\}=\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\},
\]
or equivalently the unordered roots of \(z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2=0\).  Its
root discriminant is
\[
\Delta_\sigma:=\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2=(p-s)^2 .
\]
Within this constrained D-MAP(2) subfamily, the population inverse is
the quotient map
\[
r\longmapsto (p+s,ps).
\]
The branch-map convention for reading these roots as labelled rates is
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.  The first population singularity
is the inverse denominator
\[
D=r_1-r_0^2=r_0(a-r_0),
\]
which must be nonzero to recover \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\).  The second is
not a singularity of the quotient inverse itself: at
\(\Delta_\sigma=0\) the unordered pair simply collapses to the common root.
Only the separated-root labelled chart of
\Cref{prop:population-separated-root-labelled-chart-main} is singular there.  The
dimensionless formulas
\eqref{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main}--\eqref{eq:symmetric-lambda-hid-main} show
that the stationary visible law generated by \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\)
equals that generated by \((\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)\).  Thus the visible
law identifies only the exchange quotient, not an ordered branch labelling.
At the exchange diagonal
\(\sigma=(2z,z^2)\), \(0<z<1\), the unordered pair collapses to the single
survival factor \(z\), so the common numerical rate
\(-\log z/\Delta\tau\) remains identifiable even though no separated ordered
branch is.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:gap-law-exchange-symmetry-main} proves the exchange invariance of
the stationary visible renewal law by rewriting both \(g_k\) and
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\) in the coordinates
\((x,p)=(-\log p,p)\) and \((y,s)=(-\log s,s)\).  Conversely, from
\[
r_0=\rho,\qquad r_1=\PP(A_0=A_1=1)=\rho a,\qquad
r_2=\PP(A_0=A_2=1)=\rho u_2
\]
the domain condition \eqref{eq:population-inverse-domain-main} permits the
divisions below.  Hence one obtains \(a=r_1/r_0\), \(u_2=r_2/r_0\), and
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=\frac{u_2-r_0}{a-r_0}
=\frac{r_2/r_0-r_0}{r_1/r_0-r_0}
=\frac{r_2-r_0^2}{r_1-r_0^2}.
\]
The last equality uses only scalar division by \(r_0>0\) and by
\(D=r_1-r_0^2=r_0(a-r_0)\ne0\), precisely the stated inverse domain.
For the constrained D-MAP this domain is automatic, because
\Cref{lem:stationary-main} gives \(r_0=\rho>0\) and
\(a-r_0=a-\rho<0\); no cancellation of a possibly zero quotient denominator
is used.
Then the identities
\[
\sigma_1=1-a+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}},
\qquad
\sigma_2=r_0(1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}})-a+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
\]
recover \(p+s\) and \(ps\) by
\Cref{lem:quotient-inverse-symmetric-algebra-main}, since \(r_0=\rho\) for
the constrained D-MAP law.  The unordered roots of
\(z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2=0\) are therefore identified, but their labels are
not.  Finally, \Cref{lem:gap-law-exchange-symmetry-main} proves that swapping
the two roots also leaves the stationary renewal gap law, and hence all
finite-dimensional visible distributions, unchanged.  The inverse therefore
lands on the quotient by the exchange \((p,\Gamma)\leftrightarrow
(s,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\), not on separated labelled rates.  At a double root
the quotient itself contains the common numerical rate, as made explicit in
\Cref{lem:diagonal-common-rate-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{algorithmiclemma}[Complete-law reconstruction from three inclusions]\label{lem:algorithmic-complete-law-reconstruction-main}
Work in the stable quotient domain of
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}.  The following deterministic
procedure reconstructs the complete stationary visible renewal law from the
population three-inclusion coordinates
\[
r=(r_0,r_1,r_2)
=\bigl(\PP(A_0=1),\PP(A_0=A_1=1),\PP(A_0=A_2=1)\bigr).
\]
It is a population reconstruction lemma only.  A finite empirical triple
\(\widehat r\) must not be substituted directly into the final inclusion
product: after sampling error and feasible-set projection, the empirical
single-site intensity \(\widehat r_0\) need not equal the stationary renewal
intensity implied by the recovered root pair.  The finite-record protocol below
therefore either withholds complete-law reporting or first projects onto the
full renewal-consistency graph
\[
\mathcal M_{\rm ren}
=\{(\rho_*(\sigma),\sigma):\sigma\in F\},
\qquad
F=\{(x+y,xy):0\le x,y\le1\},
\]
before forming word probabilities.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Start from the population three inclusion coordinates
\[
r_0=\PP(A_0=1),\qquad
r_1=\PP(A_0=A_1=1),\qquad
r_2=\PP(A_0=A_2=1),
\]
and retain the triple only when
\[
r_0>0,\qquad D:=r_1-r_0^2\ne0 .
\]

\item Compute the adjacent Palm mass and hidden-tail ratio
\[
a=\frac{r_1}{r_0},\qquad
\lambda=\frac{r_2-r_0^2}{r_1-r_0^2}.
\]
Then form the symmetric root coordinates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-sigma-main}
\sigma_1=1-a+\lambda,\qquad
\sigma_2=r_0(1-\lambda)-a+\lambda .
\end{equation}
For the constrained D-MAP population,
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality} gives
\[
(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)=(p+s,ps).
\]

\item Recover the unordered survival-factor pair
\[
\{z_1,z_2\}
=\{ \text{roots of } z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2=0\}.
\]
Only this unordered pair is a visible-law object.  At the population
constrained D-MAP point, \(\sigma\) lies in the feasible set \(F\) equivalently
characterized in \eqref{eq:feasible-symmetric-coordinate-set-main}.

\item Recover the Palm zero-run gap law.  Put
\(\theta_i=-\log z_i\).  If \(z_1\ne z_2\), set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-gap-main}
g_k=
\frac{\theta_2(1-z_1)z_1^k-\theta_1(1-z_2)z_2^k}
     {\theta_2-\theta_1},
\qquad k\ge0 .
\end{equation}
If \(z_1=z_2=z\), put \(\theta=-\log z\) and use the continuous-limit law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-gap-diagonal-main}
g_k=z^k\bigl((1-z)(1+\theta k)-\theta z\bigr),
\qquad k\ge0 .
\end{equation}

\item Compute the Palm renewal masses
\[
u_0=1,\qquad
u_n=\PP(n\in\varXi\mid0\in\varXi)
    =\sum_{j=1}^{n}\!
      \sum_{\substack{k_1,\ldots,k_j\ge0\\
      (k_1+1)+\cdots+(k_j+1)=n}}
      g_{k_1}\cdots g_{k_j},
\qquad n\ge1 .
\]
Equivalently, if \(G\) has law \(g\), then
\[
u_n=\sum_{j\ge1}\PP\bigl((G_1+1)+\cdots+(G_j+1)=n\bigr),
\]
where the sum is finite because each cycle length \(G_i+1\) is at least
\(1\).

\item Compute the stationary renewal intensity from the same gap law,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algorithmic-rho-star-main}
\rho_*(\sigma)
=\frac{1}{\EE(G+1)}
=\left(1+\sum_{k\ge0}k\,g_k\right)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
At the population input produced by the constrained D-MAP, this value equals
the original inclusion coordinate \(r_0\).  This equality is a conclusion of
the population model, not a license to combine a noisy finite-record
\(\widehat r_0\) with a separately projected root pair.

\item Apply the renewal inclusion product and then finite
inclusion--exclusion.  For every nonempty
\(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-inclusion-main}
\PP(B\subset\varXi)
=\rho_*(\sigma)\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}u_{i_{\ell+1}-i_\ell},
\end{equation}
with the empty-set inclusion probability equal to \(1\).  For every finite
window \(I\) and word \(S\subset I\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-word-main}
\PP(\varXi\cap I=S)
=\sum_{C\subset I\setminus S}(-1)^{|C|}
\PP(S\cup C\subset\varXi).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

At the population constrained D-MAP point the procedure is exact and
determines the complete finite-dimensional visible law on the exchange
quotient.  No step assigns the two roots to labelled attempt/recovery rates;
that additional ordering is precisely the branch-map convention of
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.
\end{algorithmiclemma}

\begin{proof}
The domain test permits the scalar divisions in Step~2.  The formulas for
\(a\) and \(\lambda\) are those in
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}, and
\Cref{lem:quotient-inverse-symmetric-algebra-main} identifies
\eqref{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-sigma-main} with \((p+s,ps)\) at the
constrained D-MAP population point.  Thus the roots in Step~3 are the
unordered pair \(\{p,s\}\), with no labelled branch information.

Step~4 is exactly \Cref{lem:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-main}: the
unequal-root formula is the symmetric dimensionless gap law
\eqref{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main}, and the equal-root formula is its continuous
limit.  Given this gap law, the visible process is the stationary renewal
process supplied by \Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}.  Step~5 is the renewal
equation for Palm return probabilities, written as a finite convolution sum at
fixed time \(n\).  The stationary renewal intensity in Step~6 is
\(1/\EE(G+1)\); for the constrained D-MAP population this equals
\(\PP(A_0=1)=r_0\) by stationarity of the renewal process already identified
in \Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}.  Finally, Step~7 is
\Cref{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}: first multiply Palm renewal
masses to obtain marked-set inclusions, then use finite inclusion--exclusion
over the unprescribed sites.  These operations determine every finite word
probability and therefore the complete stationary visible law.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Separated-root labelled chart]\label{prop:population-separated-root-labelled-chart-main}
In the setting of \Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}, suppose that the
quotient inverse gives
\(\sigma=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)=(p+s,ps)\) with
\[
\Delta_\sigma=\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2>0 .
\]
Then the two roots of \(z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2=0\) are distinct elements of
\((0,1)\).  Under the branch-map convention of
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}, a supplied branch map
\(\mathcal B\) orders those roots as \((p_{\mathcal B},s_{\mathcal B})\), and
on that chart
\[
(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})
=\left(-\frac{\log p_{\mathcal B}}{\Delta\tau},
       -\frac{\log s_{\mathcal B}}{\Delta\tau}\right).
\]
If \(\Delta_\sigma=0\), the separated labelled chart is not defined; the
quotient still identifies the common root and hence the common numerical rate
of \Cref{lem:diagonal-common-rate-main}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The roots of \(z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2=0\) are the unordered pair
\(\{p,s\}\) by \Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}.  The strict
discriminant condition is exactly \(p\ne s\), so the two roots can be
separated as numerical values.  The exchange symmetry proved in
\Cref{lem:gap-law-exchange-symmetry-main} shows that the visible stationary
law is unchanged by swapping the two roots.  Therefore the assignment of one
root to the attempt branch and the other to the recovery branch cannot be
inferred from the visible law and must be supplied externally.  Applying
\(z\mapsto-\log z/\Delta\tau\) to the two externally ordered roots gives the
displayed labelled rates.  At \(\Delta_\sigma=0\) the two roots coalesce, and
the denominator of the separated-root differential vanishes; only the quotient
common-rate statement remains.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Gap law from the quotient root pair]\label{lem:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-main}
Let \((\sigma_1,\sigma_2)\) be the symmetric coordinates recovered in
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}, and let
\(\{z_1,z_2\}\subset(0,1)\) be the unordered roots of
\[
z^2-\sigma_1 z+\sigma_2=0 .
\]
Put \(\theta_i=-\log z_i\).  If \(z_1\ne z_2\), then the Palm zero-run law is
the symmetric expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-main}
g_k=
\frac{\theta_2(1-z_1)z_1^k-\theta_1(1-z_2)z_2^k}
     {\theta_2-\theta_1},
\qquad k\ge0 .
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is unchanged by exchanging \(z_1\) and \(z_2\).  On the
diagonal \(\sigma=(2z,z^2)\), \(0<z<1\), where
\(\theta=-\log z\), the same quotient coordinate gives the continuous-limit
law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-diagonal-main}
g_k=z^k\bigl((1-z)(1+\theta k)-\theta z\bigr),
\qquad k\ge0 .
\end{equation}
Consequently the recovered unordered root pair determines the complete renewal
gap law, including at a double root.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For distinct roots choose an arbitrary ordering and write
\[
p=z_1,\qquad s=z_2,\qquad
x=-\log p=\theta_1,\qquad y=-\log s=\theta_2 .
\]
Substitution in the dimensionless form
\eqref{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main} gives
\eqref{eq:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-main}.  If the two roots are exchanged,
both the numerator and the denominator in
\eqref{eq:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-main} change sign, so the value of
\(g_k\) is an unordered-root function.  At a double root \(z_1=z_2=z\),
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main} reads the unequal-root expression by the continuous
equal-rate dictionary \eqref{eq:equal-rate-dictionary-main}; with
\(\theta=-\log z\), this is exactly
\eqref{eq:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-diagonal-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Three inclusion probabilities are complete quotient coordinates]\label{cor:three_inclusion_complete_coordinates}
For the stationary visible law of the constrained D-MAP kernels
in \Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}, with positive
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau\), \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\), and
equal-rate formulas interpreted continuously, the three inclusion probabilities
\((r_0,r_1,r_2)=(\rho,\rho a,\rho u_2)\) determine the full stationary visible
law up to the exchange
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\leftrightarrow(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:algorithmic-complete-law-reconstruction-main} gives the explicit
reconstruction: from \((r_0,r_1,r_2)\) compute \((p+s,ps)\), recover the
unordered roots, compute \(g_k\), compute \(u_n\), and then apply the renewal
product and inclusion--exclusion.  The constrained D-MAP population satisfies
the retained denominator condition by
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}.  The procedure therefore determines
the whole finite-word visible law on the exchange quotient, and the only
remaining ambiguity is the exchange of the two roots.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Diagonal common-rate estimand]\label{lem:diagonal-common-rate-main}
On the exchange diagonal
\[
\sigma=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)=(2z,z^2),\qquad 0<z<1,
\]
the visible quotient law identifies the common labelled numerical rate
\[
\gamma_{\rm diag}:=-\frac{\log z}{\Delta\tau}.
\]
Equivalently, it identifies the invariant ordered pair
\[
(\gamma_{\rm diag},\gamma_{\rm diag}).
\]
It does not identify a separated ordered branch, because the
separated-root map has derivative denominator \(p-s=0\).  Thus separated-root
Wald intervals are invalid on the diagonal, but reporting
\((\gamma_{\rm diag},\gamma_{\rm diag})\) is valid as a quotient invariant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The polynomial with symmetric coordinate \((2z,z^2)\) is
\[
t^2-2zt+z^2=(t-z)^2,
\]
so both unordered roots equal \(z\).  Applying the fixed sampling map
\(w\mapsto-\log w/\Delta\tau\) to both entries gives the ordered pair
\((\gamma_{\rm diag},\gamma_{\rm diag})\).  Because the two entries are equal,
exchanging the two branch labels leaves this pair unchanged, so it is a
visible-law quotient invariant.  By contrast, the separated-root
differential
\[
dw=\frac{w\,d\sigma_1-d\sigma_2}{2w-\sigma_1}
\]
has denominator \(2z-2z=0\) at the double root, equivalently \(p-s=0\).
Therefore the ordinary separated-root delta method and its Wald intervals do
not apply on the exchange diagonal; the available first-order finite-record
limit is the projected symmetric-coordinate limit in
\Cref{cor:exchange-diagonal-projected-gaussian-confidence-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Identifiable estimands]\label{thm:identifiable-finite-record-estimands-main}
In the fixed-setting constrained D-MAP model of
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}, a stationary visible record
identifies only functionals of its stationary visible law.  The finite-record
CLT and covariance statements below therefore concern
\[
(r_0,r_1,r_2),\qquad
\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps),\qquad
\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2),
\]
and their regenerative covariance matrices.  These quantities are invariant
under the hidden-label exchange
\((p,\Gamma)\leftrightarrow(s,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\).  All later separated
labelled-rate confidence statements are to be read through the single branch
box \Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality} identifies the quotient
coordinates \((p+s,ps)\) from \((r_0,r_1,r_2)\) and proves that the stationary
visible law is unchanged by the exchange
\((p,\Gamma)\leftrightarrow(s,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\).  Hence no statistic of
one visible record can distinguish which separated root is the attempt
survival factor and which is the recovery survival factor.  The listed
estimands are functions of the visible law and are therefore identifiable.
At separated roots, an ordered labelled-rate vector is obtained from the
unordered roots only after an externally supplied branch map chooses the
ordering, as in \Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.  At a double root the
ordering is immaterial, and \Cref{lem:diagonal-common-rate-main} records the
identified common-rate vector.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Separated-root labelled-rate differential]\label{lem:separated-root-rate-delta-main}
Let \(r=(r_0,r_1,r_2)\) lie in the population inverse domain of
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}, let
\[
\Phi(r)=\sigma_{\rm sym}=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)=(p+s,ps),
\]
and suppose the two roots \(p\ne s\) both lie in \((0,1)\).  A labelled root
chart is the deterministic oracle branch map of
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}, or an estimated branch map only on
an event whose probability tends to one and on which it agrees with that
oracle as in the estimated-map reduction following
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.  On any
neighbourhood where \(|p-s|\) is bounded away from zero and the oracle map is
fixed, this chart is \(C^1\), and its Jacobian with respect to the
symmetric coordinates is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separated-root-jacobian-main}
J_{\mathrm{root}}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{p}{p-s} & -\dfrac1{p-s}\\[1.1em]
\dfrac{s}{s-p} & -\dfrac1{s-p}
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for either labelled root \(z\in\{p,s\}\),
\[
dz=\frac{z\,d\sigma_1-d\sigma_2}{2z-\sigma_1}.
\]
For the fixed sampling interval \(\Delta\tau\), the labelled rate map
\[
R(p,s)=\left(-\frac{\log p}{\Delta\tau},
              -\frac{\log s}{\Delta\tau}\right)
=(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})
\]
has Jacobian
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separated-rate-jacobian-main}
J_{\mathrm{rate}}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
-\dfrac1{\Delta\tau p} & 0\\[0.8em]
0 & -\dfrac1{\Delta\tau s}
\end{pmatrix}
J_{\mathrm{root}} .
\end{equation}
The full Jacobian from inclusion coordinates to labelled rates is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inclusion-to-rate-jacobian-main}
J_{\Gamma,\kappa}(r)
=J_{\mathrm{rate}}\,J_\Phi(r),
\qquad
J_\Phi(r)=D\Phi(r).
\end{equation}
Writing
\[
D=r_1-r_0^2,\qquad E=r_2-r_0^2,\qquad
a=\frac{r_1}{r_0},\qquad \lambda=\frac{E}{D},
\]
the entries of \(J_\Phi\) are explicit:
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial r_0}
&=-\frac{2r_0(r_1-r_2)}{D^2},&
\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial r_1}
&=-\frac{E}{D^2},&
\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial r_2}
&=\frac1D, \label{eq:lambda-gradient-main}\\
D\Phi_1
&=
\left(
\frac{a}{r_0}+\lambda_{0},\
-\frac1{r_0}+\lambda_{1},\
\lambda_{2}
\right),\nonumber\\
D\Phi_2
&=
\left(
1-\lambda+\frac{a}{r_0}+(1-r_0)\lambda_{0},\
-\frac1{r_0}+(1-r_0)\lambda_{1},\
(1-r_0)\lambda_{2}
\right), \label{eq:phi-jacobian-main}
\end{align}
where \(\lambda_i=\partial\lambda/\partial r_i\).

Thus the labelled-rate chart has covariance push-forward
\[
\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa}
=J_{\Gamma,\kappa}(r)\,\Sigma_r\,J_{\Gamma,\kappa}(r)^\top
\]
whenever a separate side-data hypothesis supplies a valid ordered chart and a
Gaussian limit for \(\widehat r_N\).  The visible record alone supplies only
the symmetric input \(J_\Phi(r)\) and the unordered roots; the ordered
labelled-rate stochastic statement is
\Cref{cor:side-data-labelled-rate-wald-main}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The implicit equation for a root is
\(z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2=0\).  Differentiating gives
\[
(2z-\sigma_1)\,dz-z\,d\sigma_1+d\sigma_2=0.
\]
At separated roots \(2p-\sigma_1=p-s\) and \(2s-\sigma_1=s-p\), which gives
\eqref{eq:separated-root-jacobian-main}.  Thus the labelled-root differential
itself has a pole at \(p=s\); the quotient map \(\Phi\) remains the symmetric
coordinate map there, but the labelled-root and labelled-rate charts do not
extend through the exchange diagonal.  The logarithmic rate Jacobian is
the ordinary derivative of
\((p,s)\mapsto(-\log p/\Delta\tau,-\log s/\Delta\tau)\), proving
\eqref{eq:separated-rate-jacobian-main}.  The displayed formula for
\(\lambda=E/D\) is the quotient rule, and
\eqref{eq:phi-jacobian-main} follows by differentiating
\[
\Phi_1=1-a+\lambda,\qquad
\Phi_2=r_0-a+(1-r_0)\lambda .
\]
All denominators are bounded away from zero on the stated neighbourhood, so
the maps and plug-in Jacobians are continuous there.  The displayed covariance
push-forward is only the Jacobian sandwich for this externally supplied chart;
the probability limit and Wald statistic are stated separately in
\Cref{cor:side-data-labelled-rate-wald-main}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fixed-setting retained-record asymptotics and the exchange diagonal}

The finite-record argument has three layers: the complete-cycle
protocol, the D-MAP-specific reward identities, and the quotient/projection
consequences.  The standard regenerative, random-prefix batching, and plug-in
centering proofs are stated here only as reusable lemmas and proved in
\Cref{app:finite-record-regenerative-tools-main}.  Thus the model-specific
content in this subsection is the reduction of the inclusion coordinates to
the adjacent-gap rewards
\[
U_j^{(0)}=1,\qquad
U_j^{(1)}=\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0\}},\qquad
U_j^{(2)}
=\mathbf1_{\{G_j=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0,\ G_{j+1}=0\}},
\]
followed by the quotient inverse and the exchange-diagonal projection.  The

\begin{assumption}[Standing fixed constrained D-MAP record]\label{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}
All D-MAP finite-record statements in this subsection and in
\Cref{subsec:studentized-lag-two-main} are relative to the following single
record experiment, unless a statement explicitly says otherwise.  Fix
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\), put
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},
\]
and use the killed-leakage labelled kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(a,b\) are the lower-row labelled-kernel weights in
\eqref{eq:a-b-main}.  Thus \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\),
\((T_i)_{XY}=\PP(A_n=i,H_{n+1}=Y\mid H_n=X)\),
\(P=T_0+T_1\) is stochastic, \(0<p,s<1\), \(a>0\), \(b>0\), and
\(a+b=1-s\).  Equal-rate formulas are interpreted by the continuous dictionary
\eqref{eq:equal-rate-dictionary-main}.  In the stationary version,
\((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) is the stationary visible binary process generated by
these kernels.  In the finite-start version, an arbitrary hidden initial law is
run through a deterministic burn-in \(L_N\to\infty\); the observed record is the
retained post-burn-in block of length \(N\), and every complete-cycle index,
batch, projection, Wald statistic, and confidence set is formed only from that
retained visible word.
\end{assumption}

\paragraph{Generic regenerative tools.}
This block fixes the finite-record complete-cycle convention and records the
model-independent renewal tools used throughout the finite-record section:
bounded local calendar windows become measurable cycle rewards,
calendar-stopped separated batches have a consistent covariance rate, and
projected-Gaussian calibration handles the exchange diagonal.  These tools do
not by themselves assert a studentized interval.  They are standard
simulation-output devices, used here only after the killed-leakage renewal gap
law has verified the required exponential tail
\cite{GlynnIglehart1990STS,AsmussenGlynn2007,
VatsFlegalJones2019Multivariate}.

The covariance estimator below should be read in this regenerative sense, not
as an MCMC theorem imported wholesale.  MCMC batch-means papers supply useful
terminology for long-run covariance estimation and retained-rank numerical
reporting, but the proof here uses the observed click cycles as regeneration
blocks.  Once local calendar observables are rewritten as cycle rewards,
separated batches are independent at distance one because the reward sequence
is \(1\)-dependent; the rate proof then reduces to endpoint deletion,
calendar-stopped random-prefix replacement, empirical-mean subtraction, and
plug-in centering for those regenerative rewards.

\begin{definition}[Cycle-index inclusion estimator]\label{def:cycle-index-inclusion-estimator-main}
For a finite observed record \(A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\), write the complete click
epochs in the observed window as
\[
\tau_0<\tau_1<\cdots<\tau_{K_N},\qquad K_N=\max\{k:\tau_k<N\}.
\]
If the observed window contains no click, set \(K_N=-1\) and every
all-complete-cycle index set below to the empty set.  Thus \(K_N+1\) is the
number of observed clicks, and \(K_N\) is one less than the number of click
epochs from which complete observed cycles can be formed.  Set
\(L_j=\tau_{j+1}-\tau_j\) whenever both endpoints are observed.  For
\(m=0,1,2\), put
\[
X_n^{(m)}=A_nA_{n+m},\qquad X_n^{(0)}=A_n,
\]
and define the complete-cycle uncentered reward
\[
U_j^{(m)}=\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}X_n^{(m)}.
\]
Set \(c_0=c_1=0\) and \(c_2=1\).  The admissible all-complete-cycle index set is
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{m,N}
=\{0\le j\le K_N-c_m-1:\;&
\tau_j,\tau_{j+1}\in[0,N-1],\\
&\text{and every }n+m\text{ entering }U_j^{(m)}
\text{ lies in }[0,N-1]\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The cycle-index estimator of the three inclusion coordinates
\[
r=(r_0,r_1,r_2)=(\PP(A_0=1),\PP(A_0=A_1=1),\PP(A_0=A_2=1))
\]
is
\[
\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}
=\bigl(\widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}},
\widehat r_{1,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}},
\widehat r_{2,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}\bigr)^\top,\qquad
\widehat r_{m,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}
=\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_{m,N}}U_j^{(m)} .
\]
The displayed sum is an operational estimator only after the relevant
complete-cycle set is nonempty.  Records with no click, too few complete
cycles, or no admissible terminal look-ahead cycles are not assigned the
numerical value \(0\) as an inferential estimate; the finite-record protocol
below instead stops with the message ``insufficient complete cycles; no
confidence set.''  In asymptotic proofs only, we use the harmless auxiliary
extension in which an empty sum is interpreted as \(0\); that extension is not
part of the finite-record report.
For covariance estimation the three coordinates are evaluated on the common
complete-cycle set \(J_N=J_{0,N}\cap J_{1,N}\cap J_{2,N}\).  With plug-in
centering, the vector reward used in the separated batches is
\[
\widehat Y_j=
\bigl(U_j^{(0)}-\widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}L_j,\,
U_j^{(1)}-\widehat r_{1,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}L_j,\,
U_j^{(2)}-\widehat r_{2,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}L_j\bigr)^\top .
\]
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Reusable regenerative lemmas.}
The local-window-to-cycle decomposition, the generic complete-cycle

\paragraph{Inclusion-coordinate covariance estimation.}
The main text uses one covariance estimator notation.  Algorithm~1 reports
\(\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}\) only on the retained reporting event; it does not use a
zero matrix or any proof-only completion as an operational replacement.  The
generic rate, including separated batches, calendar-stopped random batch
counts, endpoint/look-ahead deletion, empirical-mean subtraction, and plug-in
centering, is the reusable appendix theorem
\Cref{lem:separated-batch-covariance-estimator-main}.  The next theorem records
only the D-MAP reward specialization needed to invoke that result.

\begin{theorem}[D-MAP specialization of separated-batch covariance]\label{thm:finite-record-covariance-main}
Work under \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}.
For \(m=0,1,2\), let
\[
U_j^{(m)}=\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}A_nA_{n+m},
\qquad U_j^{(0)}=\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}A_n,
\]
on the all-complete-cycle set on which the displayed lagged terms are
observed.  Let
\[
r=(r_0,r_1,r_2)^\top,\qquad r_m=\PP(A_0=A_m=1),
\qquad L_j=\tau_{j+1}-\tau_j,
\]
and define the centered reward vector
\[
Y_j=
\bigl(U_j^{(0)}-r_0L_j,\,
      U_j^{(1)}-r_1L_j,\,
      U_j^{(2)}-r_2L_j\bigr)^\top .
\]
Equivalently, in Palm gap coordinates,
\[
U_j^{(0)}=1,\qquad
U_j^{(1)}=\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0\}},\qquad
U_j^{(2)}
=\mathbf1_{\{G_j=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0,\ G_{j+1}=0\}}.
\]
Thus \(Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1})\), \((Y_j)\) is one-dependent,
\(\EE Y_j=0\), and
\[
\Sigma_r=\frac1\mu\left\{
\operatorname{Var}(Y_0)+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0)\right\},
\qquad \mu=\EE L_0 ,
\]
is the calendar-time covariance in the inclusion-coordinate CLT.  Let the
separated batch length be deterministic,
\[
b_N=\lfloor N^\beta\rfloor,\qquad 0<\beta<1,
\]
and set \(d_N=b_N+1\).  The separated batch grid is
\[
I_{\ell,N}=\{(\ell-1)d_N,\ldots,(\ell-1)d_N+b_N-1\},
\qquad \ell\ge1,
\]
so the single complete-cycle reward with index \(\ell d_N-1\) is omitted as
the separator between adjacent batches.  Let
\[
\widehat r_N=(\widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}},
\widehat r_{1,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}},
\widehat r_{2,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}})^\top .
\]
On the common all-complete-cycle set
\[
J_N=J_{0,N}\cap J_{1,N}\cap J_{2,N},
\]
use the observable plug-in centered reward vector
\[
\widehat Y_j=
\bigl(U_j^{(0)}-\widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}L_j,\,
      U_j^{(1)}-\widehat r_{1,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}L_j,\,
      U_j^{(2)}-\widehat r_{2,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}L_j\bigr)^\top .
\]
Let \(M_N\) be the retained all-complete-cycle batch count from Algorithm~1, so
unfinished terminal batches and endpoint/look-ahead cycles outside \(J_N\) are
omitted.  On the reporting event \(\mathcal E_N\), Algorithm~1 forms
\[
\widehat B_{\ell,N}=\sum_{j\in I_{\ell,N}}\widehat Y_j,\qquad
\overline{\widehat B}_N=M_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}\widehat B_{\ell,N},
\]
and reports the single covariance estimator
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}
=
\frac{K_N}{N}\,
\frac1{b_N(M_N-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}
(\widehat B_{\ell,N}-\overline{\widehat B}_N)
(\widehat B_{\ell,N}-\overline{\widehat B}_N)^\top .
\]
On \(\mathcal E_N^c\), no covariance matrix is reported.  Since
\(\PP(\mathcal E_N^c)\to0\) on the stable quotient chart, the estimator on
\(\mathcal E_N\) satisfies
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=
O_{\PP}\left(b_N^{-1}+\sqrt{\frac{b_N}{N}}\right)
=
O_{\PP}\left(N^{-\beta}+N^{-(1-\beta)/2}\right).
\]
With
\[
\omega_\beta:=\min\{\beta,(1-\beta)/2\},
\]
any rank-adaptive deterministic threshold \(t_N\) must satisfy
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=o_{\PP}(t_N).
\]
A concrete admissible choice is
\[
t_N=N^{-\alpha},
\qquad
0<\alpha<\omega_\beta;
\]
for the balanced choice \(b_N=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\), this becomes any
\(0<\alpha<1/3\), for instance \(t_N=N^{-1/4}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The half-open Palm cycle \([\tau_j,\tau_{j+1})\) contains only the click at
\(\tau_j\), so \(U_j^{(0)}=1\).  A one-step look-ahead is a click exactly
when the next click occurs one sampled step later, so
\(U_j^{(1)}=\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0\}}\).  For lag two, the contributing time is
again the click at \(\tau_j\); a click two sampled steps later occurs either
when \(G_j=1\), or when \(G_j=0\) and the next gap is also zero.  These
identities show that \(Y_j\) is a function of the adjacent gap pair
\((G_j,G_{j+1})\), hence is one-dependent.  Palm inversion in
\Cref{lem:local-window-regenerative-decomposition-main}, applied to
\(Z_n^{(m)}=A_nA_{n+m}\), gives
\(\EE U_j^{(m)}=r_m\EE L_j\), so \(\EE Y_j=0\).  The exponential moment from
\Cref{lem:killed-leakage-gap-exponential-moment-main} gives the fourth
moments needed by the separated regenerative batch theorem.

Apply \Cref{lem:separated-batch-covariance-estimator-main} with
\(Y_j=U_j-rL_j\), \(m=1\), \(T_m=T_1^{(r)}\), \(d_N=b_N+1\), and
\(\widehat Y_j=Y_j-(\widehat r_N-r)L_j\).  The CLT gives
\(\widehat r_N-r=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), so the theorem gives the displayed
plug-in separated-batch rate, including the random retained batch count
\(M_N\), the \(K_N/N\) calendar scaling, and endpoint/look-ahead deletion.
Substituting \(b_N=\lfloor N^\beta\rfloor\), \(0<\beta<1\), gives
\[
O_{\PP}\left(N^{-\beta}+N^{-(1-\beta)/2}\right).
\]
The threshold claim is immediate from Weyl's eigenvalue inequality: every
positive limiting eigenvalue stays above \(t_N\), and every zero limiting
eigenvalue falls below \(t_N\), with probability tending to one whenever
\(\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=o_{\PP}(t_N)\).
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Simulated projected-Gaussian calibration at a moving half-space]\label{lem:projected-gaussian-bootstrap-halfspace-main}
For \(t\in(0,1)\), write
\[
H_t=\{h\in\RR^2:h_2\le th_1\}.
\]
Let \(z_N\to z\in(0,1)\) in probability and let
\(\widehat\Sigma_N\) be a symmetric nonnegative definite covariance estimator
such that
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_N-\Sigma\|_{\operatorname{op}}=o_{\PP}(t_N),
\qquad t_N\downarrow0,
\]
for a fixed nonnegative definite \(2\times2\) matrix \(\Sigma\).  Let
\[
\widehat\Sigma_N^+
=\sum_{\widehat\lambda_{i,N}>t_N}
\widehat\lambda_{i,N}^{-1}
\widehat v_{i,N}\widehat v_{i,N}^{\top}
\]
be the thresholded Moore--Penrose inverse.  Suppose that
\[
X_N\Rightarrow X:=\Pi_{H_z}(Z),
\qquad Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma).
\]
This is the local constrained-estimation limit: the Gaussian perturbation is
projected onto the tangent half-space before the quadratic statistic is formed.
It is the same projected-normal structure that appears in constrained
\(M\)-estimation limits \cite{Geyer1994ConstrainedM}.  The simulation below is
therefore not an ordinary nonparametric or parametric bootstrap from the
record; it is conditional Monte Carlo calibration of the limiting projected
Gaussian law, with plug-in covariance and retained empirical rank.
Define
\[
T_N=X_N^\top\widehat\Sigma_N^+X_N
\]
and, conditionally on the observed record,
\[
Z_N^\ast\sim \mathcal N(0,\widehat\Sigma_N),\qquad
X_N^\ast=\Pi_{H_{z_N}}(Z_N^\ast),\qquad
T_N^\ast=(X_N^\ast)^\top\widehat\Sigma_N^+X_N^\ast .
\]
Then
\[
T_N\Rightarrow T:=X^\top\Sigma^+X,
\]
and the conditional law of \(T_N^\ast\) converges weakly in probability to
the law of \(T\).  Consequently, if \(c_{1-\alpha,N}^{\ast,-}\) is the lower
conditional \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(T_N^\ast\) and the limiting quantile
\[
c_{1-\alpha}:=\inf\{c:\PP(T\le c)\ge1-\alpha\}
\]
is a continuity point of the distribution function of \(T\), then
\[
c_{1-\alpha,N}^{\ast,-}\to c_{1-\alpha}
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]
If in addition \(\PP(T\le c_{1-\alpha})=1-\alpha\), then
\[
\PP\{T_N\le c_{1-\alpha,N}^{\ast,-}\}\to1-\alpha .
\]
If \(\Sigma=0\), then the retained-rank inverse is the zero matrix with
probability tending to one; consequently \(T_N=0\) and \(T_N^\ast=0\) with
probability tending to one, and the fixed positive-buffer lower-coverage
conclusion follows from
\Cref{lem:buffered-random-quantile-coverage-main}.

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Weyl's inequality and the condition
\(\|\widehat\Sigma_N-\Sigma\|_{\operatorname{op}}=o_{\PP}(t_N)\) imply that,
with probability tending to one, every positive eigenvalue of \(\Sigma\) is
retained and every zero eigenvalue is removed by the threshold.  On that event
the retained spectral projection converges to the range projection of
\(\Sigma\), and
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_N^+-\Sigma^+\|_{\operatorname{op}}\to0
\]
in probability.  Slutsky's theorem therefore gives
\[
X_N^\top\widehat\Sigma_N^+X_N
\Rightarrow X^\top\Sigma^+X .
\]
In the special case \(\Sigma=0\), the same eigenvalue bound says
\[
\PP\{\|\widehat\Sigma_N\|_{\operatorname{op}}\le t_N\}\to1,
\]
so the thresholded retained-rank inverse is exactly zero with probability
tending to one.  Hence \(T_N=0\) and, conditionally, \(T_N^\ast=0\) on that
high-probability event.  The limiting distribution is the point mass at zero,
whose lower \(p\)-quantile is zero for every \(0<p<1\); the positive-buffer
coverage statement is therefore the buffered random-quantile conclusion.

The remaining issue is conditional Gaussian convergence after the moving
projection.  The half-space projection has the explicit formula
\[
\Pi_{H_t}(u)
=u-\frac{(u_2-tu_1)_+}{1+t^2}\binom{-t}{1}.
\]
Thus \(u\mapsto\Pi_{H_t}(u)\) is one-Lipschitz and
\((t,u)\mapsto\Pi_{H_t}(u)\) is continuous, uniformly for \(t\) in compact
subintervals of \((0,1)\) and \(u\) in bounded sets.  Since
\(\widehat\Sigma_N\to\Sigma\) in probability, the conditional Gaussian laws
\(\mathcal N(0,\widehat\Sigma_N)\) converge weakly in probability to
\(\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)\).  Combining this Gaussian continuity with
\(z_N\to z\) and \(\widehat\Sigma_N^+\to\Sigma^+\) shows that, for every
bounded continuous \(\varphi:\RR\to\RR\),
\[
\EE^\ast\varphi(T_N^\ast)
\to
\EE\varphi\!\left((\Pi_{H_z}Z)^\top\Sigma^+\Pi_{H_z}Z\right)
\]
in probability, where \(\EE^\ast\) denotes conditional expectation given the
record.  This is conditional weak convergence in probability.  The quantile
conclusion is the standard consequence of weak convergence at a continuity
point of the limiting distribution function; the final coverage statement
then follows from the convergence of \(T_N\) and the random quantile
\(c_{1-\alpha,N}^{\ast,-}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Buffered random-quantile lower coverage]\label{lem:buffered-random-quantile-coverage-main}
Let \(T_N\Rightarrow T\), and let \(F_N^\ast\) be random conditional
distribution functions such that
\[
F_N^\ast(t)\to F(t):=\PP(T\le t)
\qquad\text{in probability}
\]
at every continuity point \(t\) of \(F\).  Fix \(0<p<1\), and define the
lower random conditional \(p\)-quantile and the limiting lower \(p\)-quantile
by
\[
c_N^-=\inf\{t:F_N^\ast(t)\ge p\},
\qquad
c^-=\inf\{t:F(t)\ge p\}.
\]
Assume \(c^-\) is finite.  Then, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\PP\{T_N\le c_N^-+\varepsilon\}\ge p .
\]
No continuity of \(F\) at \(c^-\), and no equality \(F(c^-)=p\), is required.
In particular, this lemma applies to the diagonal projected-Gaussian
zero-quantile alternatives after replacing \(p\) by \(1-\alpha\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First the random lower quantile cannot fall a fixed distance below the
limiting lower quantile.  Fix \(\delta>0\).  By the definition of \(c^-\),
\(F(c^--\delta)<p\).  Choose a continuity point
\[
x\in(c^--\delta,c^-)
\]
with \(F(x)<p\); distribution functions have at most countably many
discontinuities, so such a point is available after decreasing \(x\) slightly
if needed.  The pointwise conditional convergence gives
\[
F_N^\ast(x)\to F(x)
\qquad\text{in probability}.
\]
For any distribution function \(G\), the event
\(\inf\{t:G(t)\ge p\}\le x\) implies \(G(x)\ge p\).  Hence
\[
\PP\{c_N^-\le x\}
\le
\PP\{F_N^\ast(x)\ge p\}\to0,
\]
and \(c_N^->x\) with probability tending to one.

Choose \(x>c^- -\varepsilon\), and then choose a continuity point
\[
y\in(c^-,x+\varepsilon).
\]
This is possible because \(x+\varepsilon>c^-\).  On the event
\(\{c_N^->x\}\), the inclusion
\[
\{T_N\le y\}\subseteq\{T_N\le c_N^-+\varepsilon\}
\]
holds.  Therefore
\[
\liminf_N\PP\{T_N\le c_N^-+\varepsilon\}
\ge
\liminf_N\PP\{T_N\le y\}.
\]
Weak convergence at the continuity point \(y\) gives
\[
\lim_N\PP\{T_N\le y\}=F(y).
\]
Finally \(y>c^-\) implies \(F(y)\ge p\) by the lower-quantile definition.
The displayed lower bound follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional Monte Carlo quantile error]\label{lem:conditional-monte-carlo-quantile-main}
Fix \(0<z<1\) and condition on the observed record.  Let
\[
T_N^\ast(z)=
\Pi_{H_z}(Z_N^\ast)^\top\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}^{+}
\Pi_{H_z}(Z_N^\ast),
\qquad
Z_N^\ast\mid A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\sim
\mathcal N(0,\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}),
\]
where \(H_z=\{h:h_2\le zh_1\}\), and let
\[
F_N^\ast(t;z)=\PP^\ast\{T_N^\ast(z)\le t\}
\]
be its conditional distribution function.  If
\(T_{N,1}^\ast(z),\ldots,T_{N,R}^\ast(z)\) are conditionally independent draws
from this law and
\[
\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast(t;z)
=\frac1R\sum_{m=1}^R\mathbf1_{\{T_{N,m}^\ast(z)\le t\}},
\]
then for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\PP^\ast\!\left(
\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}
\left|\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast(t;z)-F_N^\ast(t;z)\right|>\varepsilon
\right)
\le 2e^{-2R\varepsilon^2}.
\]
Equivalently, for every \(0<\delta<1\), with conditional probability at least
\(1-\delta\),
\[
\sup_t|\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast(t;z)-F_N^\ast(t;z)|
\le
\varepsilon_{R,\delta}:=
\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{2R}} .
\]
Let \(p_\alpha=1-\alpha\), let
\[
c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)
=\inf\{t:F_N^\ast(t;z)\ge p_\alpha\},
\qquad
\widehat c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N,R}(z)
=T_{N,(\lceil p_\alpha R\rceil)}^\ast(z),
\]
where the draws are sorted increasingly.  Suppose that, on the conditioned
record, there are numbers \(\eta_N>0\) and \(m_N>0\) such that the conditional
quantile is locally separated:
\[
F_N^\ast(c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)+u;z)\ge p_\alpha+m_Nu,
\qquad
F_N^\ast(c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)-u;z)\le p_\alpha-m_Nu
\]
for every \(0\le u\le\eta_N\).  This finite condition excludes a locally flat
or atomic quantile; those are exactly the cases handled by the deterministic
fallbacks in the theorem statement.
If
\[
d_{R,\delta,N}:=\frac{2\varepsilon_{R,\delta}+R^{-1}}{m_N}\le\eta_N,
\]
then
\[
\PP^\ast\!\left(
\left|\widehat c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N,R}(z)
-c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)\right|>d_{R,\delta,N}\right)
\le\delta .
\]
Let \(F_z\) be the limiting distribution function of
\((\Pi_{H_z}Z)^\top\Sigma_\sigma^+\Pi_{H_z}Z\), and let
\(c_{1-\alpha}(z)\) be its lower \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile.  Suppose that in a
neighbourhood \(I\) of \(c_{1-\alpha}(z)\) the limiting law is continuous and
has a density bounded below by \(m>0\).  On every event on which
\(F_N^\ast(\cdot;z)\) is uniformly close to \(F_z\) on \(I\), the empirical
lower quantile
obeys
\[
\widehat c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N,R}(z)-c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)
=O_{\PP^\ast}(R^{-1/2})/m,
\]
with \(c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)\) the lower conditional
\((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(F_N^\ast(\cdot;z)\).  In particular the
Monte Carlo part of Algorithm~3 is an implementable conditional approximation
to the ideal projected-Gaussian quantile whenever
\(R=R_N^{\mathrm{MC}}\to\infty\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on the record, the variables
\(T_{N,1}^\ast(z),\ldots,T_{N,R}^\ast(z)\) are independent real-valued draws
with distribution function \(F_N^\ast(\cdot;z)\).  The displayed exponential
bound is therefore the Dvoretzky--Kiefer--Wolfowitz inequality applied under
\(\PP^\ast\).

Let
\[
\Delta_{N,R}(z)=
\sup_t|\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast(t;z)-F_N^\ast(t;z)|.
\]
The DKW bound gives \(\Delta_{N,R}(z)=O_{\PP^\ast}(R^{-1/2})\), and its
inverted form gives
\(\Delta_{N,R}(z)\le\varepsilon_{R,\delta}\) with conditional probability at
least \(1-\delta\).  Work on this event.
If
\(\widehat c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N,R}(z)>c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)+d_{R,\delta,N}\),
put \(x=c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)+d_{R,\delta,N}\).  Since the
\(\lceil p_\alpha R\rceil\)-th order statistic is larger than \(x\),
\(\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast(x;z)\le(\lceil p_\alpha R\rceil-1)/R\le p_\alpha\).
Thus \(F_N^\ast(x;z)\le p_\alpha+\Delta_{N,R}(z)\).  The local separation
assumption gives
\[
F_N^\ast(x;z)
\ge p_\alpha+m_Nd_{R,\delta,N},
\]
which contradicts
\(m_Nd_{R,\delta,N}\ge2\varepsilon_{R,\delta}+R^{-1}>\Delta_{N,R}(z)\).
The case
\(\widehat c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N,R}(z)<c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)-d_{R,\delta,N}\)
is the same argument with
\(x=c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)-d_{R,\delta,N}\): then
\(\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast(x;z)\ge\lceil p_\alpha R\rceil/R\ge p_\alpha\), so
\(F_N^\ast(x;z)\ge p_\alpha-\Delta_{N,R}(z)\), contradicting
\[
F_N^\ast(x;z)\le p_\alpha-m_Nd_{R,\delta,N}.
\]
Hence the finite conditional quantile-error display follows.

If
\(F_N^\ast\) is uniformly close to \(F_z\) on \(I\), then, after shrinking
\(I\) and increasing \(N\) on this event if necessary, the conditional CDF has
the local inverse modulus
\[
F_N^\ast(y;z)-F_N^\ast(x;z)\ge (m/2)(y-x),
\qquad x<y,\quad x,y\in I .
\]
Whenever the empirical and conditional quantiles lie in \(I\), the usual
quantile-inversion argument gives
\[
|\widehat c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N,R}(z)-c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)|
\le 2\{\Delta_{N,R}(z)+R^{-1}\}/m ,
\]
where the last term is the harmless lower-order effect of the order statistic
index \(\lceil(1-\alpha)R\rceil\).  This proves the asserted
\(O_{\PP^\ast}(R^{-1/2})/m\) bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Projected-Gaussian continuity and rank degeneracy]\label{thm:projected-gaussian-continuity-degeneracy-main}
Fix \(0<z<1\), put
\[
n_z=(-z,1)^\top,\qquad H_z=\{h:n_z^\top h\le0\},
\]
and let
\[
T_z=(\Pi_{H_z}Z)^\top\Sigma^+\Pi_{H_z}Z,
\qquad Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma),
\]
where \(\Sigma\) is a nonnegative definite \(2\times2\) matrix.  Then the
distribution of \(T_z\) is continuous in the following cases:
\[
\operatorname{rank}\Sigma=2,
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\operatorname{rank}\Sigma=1
\text{ and }
\operatorname{Range}\Sigma\ne \operatorname{span}\{n_z\}.
\]
If \(\operatorname{rank}\Sigma=1\) and
\(\operatorname{Range}\Sigma=\operatorname{span}\{n_z\}\), then
\[
\PP(T_z=0)=\frac12
\]
and \(T_z\) has no atom on \((0,\infty)\).  If \(\Sigma=0\), then
\(T_z=0\) almost surely.  Consequently the continuity condition in
\Cref{lem:projected-gaussian-bootstrap-halfspace-main} is automatic except
for the zero covariance case and for the rank-one case in which the retained
Gaussian direction is normal to the exchange-diagonal tangent half-space; in
that rank-one normal case the only possible discontinuity is at the zero
quantile.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(\Sigma=0\), the assertion is immediate.  Suppose first that
\(\operatorname{rank}\Sigma=2\).  The Gaussian law has a Lebesgue density on
\(\RR^2\).  On \(H_z\), the statistic is the nonzero quadratic form
\(u^\top\Sigma^+u\).  On \(H_z^c\), the projection is the linear map
\[
L_z u=u-\frac{n_z^\top u}{\|n_z\|^2}n_z
\]
onto the boundary line \(n_z^\top h=0\), and the statistic is the quadratic
form \(u^\top L_z^\top\Sigma^+L_z u\), not identically zero on that half-plane.
For each fixed \(c\), the level set of either nonzero quadratic polynomial has
Lebesgue measure zero.  Hence \(\PP(T_z=c)=0\) for every \(c\).

Now let \(\operatorname{rank}\Sigma=1\).  Write
\(\Sigma=\lambda vv^\top\), where \(\lambda>0\) and \(\|v\|=1\).  Then
\(Z=\xi v\), with \(\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,\lambda)\), and
\[
\Sigma^+=\lambda^{-1}vv^\top .
\]
If \(n_z^\top v=0\), the line
\(\operatorname{span}\{v\}\) lies in the boundary of \(H_z\), so
\(T_z=\xi^2/\lambda\), which has a continuous \(\chi^2_1\) law.  If
\(n_z^\top v\ne0\), one half-line is already in \(H_z\), where again
\(T_z=\xi^2/\lambda\).  On the other half-line,
\[
\Pi_{H_z}(\xi v)=
\xi\left(v-\frac{n_z^\top v}{\|n_z\|^2}n_z\right).
\]
The corresponding coefficient in the seminorm \(\Sigma^+\) is
\[
\lambda^{-1}
\left\{v^\top\left(v-\frac{n_z^\top v}{\|n_z\|^2}n_z\right)\right\}^2 .
\]
This coefficient is positive unless \(v\) is parallel to \(n_z\).  Therefore,
when \(\operatorname{Range}\Sigma\ne\operatorname{span}\{n_z\}\), \(T_z\) is
a two-sided mixture of positive multiples of \(\xi^2\) and has no atoms.  When
\(\operatorname{Range}\Sigma=\operatorname{span}\{n_z\}\), the outside
half-line projects to the origin and contributes mass \(1/2\) at zero, while
the inside half-line contributes a continuous positive component.  This proves
the rank-one alternatives.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Computable diagonal covariance]\label{lem:dmap-diagonal-covariance-finite-sum-main}\label{prop:computable-diagonal-covariance-main}
Work at a fixed constrained D-MAP exchange-diagonal population point
\(\sigma_z=(2z,z^2)\), \(0<z<1\), equivalently \(p=s=z\).  Put
\[
\theta=-\log z.
\]
Then the diagonal Palm gap law is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diagonal-gap-mass-computable-main}
g_k=\PP(G=k)
=z^k\{(1-z)(1+\theta k)-\theta z\},\qquad k\ge0 .
\end{equation}
In particular
\[
g_0=(1-z)-\theta z,\qquad
g_1=z\{(1-z)(1+\theta)-\theta z\}.
\]
Define
\[
\mu=\frac1{1-z}+\frac{\theta z}{(1-z)^2},
\qquad
\nu=\frac{1+z}{(1-z)^2}
     +\frac{\theta z(3+z)}{(1-z)^3},
\qquad
m_2=g_1+g_0^2 .
\]
Then the three inclusion coordinates at this diagonal point are
\[
r(z)=\frac1\mu(1,g_0,m_2)^\top ,
\]
and the quotient denominator is
\[
D(z)=r_1(z)-r_0(z)^2
=-\frac{\theta^2z^2(1-z)^2}{(1-z+\theta z)^2}<0 .
\]
For the cycle reward variables
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diagonal-cycle-reward-y-main}
U_j=\left(1,\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0\}},
\mathbf1_{\{G_j=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0,G_{j+1}=0\}}\right)^\top,
\qquad
L_j=G_j+1,\qquad
Y_j=U_j-r(z)L_j ,
\end{equation}
the \(Y_j\) sequence is one-dependent, centered, and has covariance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diagonal-sigma-r-y-main}
\Sigma_r(z)=\frac1{\mu(z)}
\left[\operatorname{Var}(Y_0)+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0)\right].
\end{equation}
Every entry in \eqref{eq:diagonal-sigma-r-y-main} is an absolutely convergent
finite or double sum under the explicit masses \eqref{eq:diagonal-gap-mass-computable-main};
indeed the indicators in \(U_j\) restrict the nontrivial terms to
\(G_j\in\{0,1\}\), while the factors containing \(L_j\) are sums of
polynomials in \(k\) against \(g_k=O(kz^k)\).
Let
\[
A_z=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&g_0&m_2\\
g_0&g_0&g_0^2\\
m_2&g_0^2&m_2
\end{pmatrix},
\quad
e_z=
\begin{pmatrix}
\mu\\ g_0\\ 2g_1+g_0^2
\end{pmatrix},
\quad
d_z=
\begin{pmatrix}
\mu\\ \mu g_0\\ \mu g_1+g_0^2
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and
\[
B_z=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&g_0&m_2\\
g_0&g_0^2&g_0m_2\\
m_2&g_0(g_1+g_0)&g_1^2+g_1g_0^2+g_0^3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
With \(r=r(z)\), the complete-cycle inclusion covariance on the exchange
diagonal is the finite expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dmap-diagonal-sigma-r-finite-main}
\Sigma_r(z)=\frac1\mu\left\{
A_z-e_zr^\top-re_z^\top+\nu rr^\top
+B_z-d_zr^\top+B_z^\top-rd_z^\top
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently the actual diagonal covariance used by the projected-Gaussian
statistic is the reproducible \(2\times2\) matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dmap-diagonal-sigma-finite-main}
\Sigma_\sigma(z)=D\Phi(r(z))\,\Sigma_r(z)\,D\Phi(r(z))^\top
\end{equation}
where \(D\Phi\) is the explicit Jacobian in
\eqref{eq:lambda-gradient-main}--\eqref{eq:phi-jacobian-main}, evaluated at
the displayed \(r(z)\).  No infinite covariance-tail truncation or simulation
input is hidden in \eqref{eq:dmap-diagonal-sigma-finite-main}: for a fixed
\(z\), all entries are obtained by ordinary arithmetic from
\((z,\theta,g_0,g_1)\), equivalently from the absolutely convergent
finite/double sums in \eqref{eq:diagonal-sigma-r-y-main}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the equal-rate diagonal the gap law is the one in
\eqref{eq:equal-rate-dictionary-main}, which gives
\eqref{eq:diagonal-gap-mass-computable-main}.  Hence
\[
\EE(G+1)=\sum_{k\ge0}\PP(G\ge k)=\mu,\qquad
\EE(G+1)^2=\sum_{k\ge0}(2k+1)\PP(G\ge k)=\nu ,
\]
which gives the displayed closed forms by summing the two elementary geometric
series and their first two derivatives.

For the inclusion-cycle reward in
\Cref{lem:three-inclusion-clt-reduction-main},
\[
U_j=\left(1,\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0\}},
\mathbf1_{\{G_j=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0,G_{j+1}=0\}}\right)^\top .
\]
Since the gaps are i.i.d., \(\EE U_j=(1,g_0,g_1+g_0^2)^\top\), proving
\(r(z)=\EE U_j/\mu\).  The quotient denominator formula follows by direct
substitution and simplification; it is strictly negative because
\(0<z<1\) and \(\theta>0\).

The matrix \(A_z\) is \(\EE(U_0U_0^\top)\), \(e_z=\EE((G_0+1)U_0)\), and
\(B_z=\EE(U_0U_1^\top)\).  The vector \(d_z=\EE(U_0(G_1+1))\); the only
non-factorized entry is the third component, where
\[
\EE\{(\mathbf1_{\{G_0=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_0=0,G_1=0\}})(G_1+1)\}
=\mu g_1+g_0^2 .
\]
Writing \(Y_j=U_j-r(G_j+1)\), the reward sequence is one-dependent and
\[
\Sigma_r(z)
=\frac1\mu\{\EE(Y_0Y_0^\top)+\EE(Y_0Y_1^\top)+\EE(Y_1Y_0^\top)\}.
\]
Expanding the three expectations gives exactly
\eqref{eq:dmap-diagonal-sigma-r-finite-main}.  The last display is then the
delta-method sandwich for the symmetric quotient coordinates
\(\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps)\), whose Jacobian was computed in
\eqref{eq:lambda-gradient-main}--\eqref{eq:phi-jacobian-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[D-MAP diagonal covariance alternatives]\label{lem:dmap-diagonal-covariance-alternatives-main}
Work at a fixed constrained D-MAP exchange-diagonal population point
\(\sigma_z=(2z,z^2)\), \(0<z<1\), and form the actual diagonal covariance
\(\Sigma_\sigma(z)\) by the finite formula
\eqref{eq:dmap-diagonal-sigma-finite-main}.  Let \(n_z=(-z,1)^\top\).  Exactly
one of the following mutually exclusive
alternatives holds:
\[
\operatorname{rank}\Sigma_\sigma(z)=2,
\]
\[
\operatorname{rank}\Sigma_\sigma(z)=1
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{Range}\Sigma_\sigma(z)\ne\operatorname{span}\{n_z\},
\]
\[
\operatorname{rank}\Sigma_\sigma(z)=1
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{Range}\Sigma_\sigma(z)=\operatorname{span}\{n_z\},
\]
or
\[
\Sigma_\sigma(z)=0 .
\]
The first two alternatives have a continuous projected-Gaussian statistic and
therefore supply exact-level projected-Gaussian cases for all confidence
levels.  In the third alternative the limiting statistic has a half-mass at
zero and no atom on \((0,\infty)\).  The fourth alternative has an identically
zero limiting statistic.  The occurrence of the alternative is not a modelling
choice: it is obtained by evaluating the finite matrix
\eqref{eq:dmap-diagonal-sigma-finite-main}.  The paper does not prove a
closed-form analytic sign classification of this determinant for every
\(0<z<1\); therefore exact diagonal coverage is stated only as the conditional
corollary below.  The theorem-level diagonal statement remains the conservative
positive-buffer report in the exceptional rank-one-normal and zero-covariance
branches.

Equivalently, because \(\Sigma_\sigma(z)\) is \(2\times2\) and nonnegative
definite, these cases are checked directly from the actual D-MAP covariance by
\[
\operatorname{rank}\Sigma_\sigma(z)=2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\det\Sigma_\sigma(z)>0,
\]
\[
\Sigma_\sigma(z)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_\sigma(z)=0,
\]
and, with \(t_z=(1,z)^\top\) tangent to the exchange diagonal,
\[
\operatorname{Range}\Sigma_\sigma(z)=\operatorname{span}\{n_z\}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\det\Sigma_\sigma(z)=0,\quad
\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_\sigma(z)>0,\quad
\Sigma_\sigma(z)t_z=0 .
\]
The remaining case
\[
\det\Sigma_\sigma(z)=0,\quad
\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_\sigma(z)>0,\quad
\Sigma_\sigma(z)t_z\ne0
\]
is the rank-one non-normal exact-level alternative.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The matrix \(\Sigma_\sigma(z)\) is a \(2\times2\) nonnegative definite matrix
because it is obtained by sandwiching the nonnegative definite regenerative
covariance \(\Sigma_r(z)\) with the deterministic Jacobian \(D\Phi(r(z))\).
Thus its rank is \(0\), \(1\), or \(2\).  In rank one its range is a single
line, which is either the normal line \(\operatorname{span}\{n_z\}\) or is not
that line.  These alternatives are exhaustive and mutually exclusive.

For a \(2\times2\) nonnegative definite matrix, full rank is equivalent to
positive determinant, and the zero matrix is equivalent to zero trace.  In the
remaining rank-one case, \(\det\Sigma_\sigma(z)=0\) and
\(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_\sigma(z)>0\).  Since
\(t_z=(1,z)^\top\) spans the tangent line orthogonal to \(n_z=(-z,1)^\top\),
the range is \(\operatorname{span}\{n_z\}\) exactly when the kernel is the
tangent line, equivalently \(\Sigma_\sigma(z)t_z=0\).  If this kernel test
fails, the rank-one range is not the normal line.  The statements about
continuity, the half-mass at zero, and the zero statistic are precisely
\Cref{thm:projected-gaussian-continuity-degeneracy-main} applied to
\(\Sigma=\Sigma_\sigma(z)\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Model verification.}
We now specialize the generic renewal tools to the standing record experiment
of \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}.  The exponential-tail lemma
verifies the renewal hypothesis for this model.  Main Theorem~3 then states the
exact finite-record statistic for the three inclusion coordinates and applies
the generic complete-cycle CLT to that statistic.

\begin{lemma}[Killed-leakage gap exponential moment]\label{lem:killed-leakage-gap-exponential-moment-main}
Under \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}, set
\[
r=\max\{p,s\}<1 .
\]
If \(p\ne s\), then there is a finite constant \(C\), depending only on the
fixed constrained D-MAP setting, such that
\[
g_k\le Cr^k\qquad(k\ge0).
\]
If \(p=s=r\), then there is a finite constant \(C\), depending only on the
fixed constrained D-MAP setting, such that
\[
g_k\le C(1+k)r^k\qquad(k\ge0).
\]
Equivalently, in all cases, for every \(\bar r\in(r,1)\) there is a finite
constant \(C_{\bar r}\) such that
\[
g_k\le C_{\bar r}\bar r^k\qquad(k\ge0).
\]
Consequently, for every
\[
0<\eta<-\log r,
\]
the gap and cycle length satisfy
\[
\EE e^{\eta G}<\infty,\qquad
\EE e^{\eta (G+1)}<\infty .
\]
Thus the regenerative exponential-moment hypothesis used below is a verified
consequence of the killed-leakage model, not an additional assumption.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
When \(p\ne s\), the displayed unequal-rate formula
\eqref{eq:gap-closed-main} gives
\[
|g_k|
\le
\left|\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}\right|p^k
+
\left|\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}\right|s^k
\le C_1(p^k+s^k)
\le 2C_1r^k .
\]
Since \(g_k\ge0\), this is the stated unequal-rate bound.

On the equal-rate diagonal \(p=s=r=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}\), write
\(y=\Gamma\Delta\tau\).  The equal-rate dictionary
\eqref{eq:equal-rate-dictionary-main} gives
\[
g_k=r^k\bigl((1-r)(1+yk)-yr\bigr).
\]
Hence \(|g_k|\le C_2(1+k)r^k\), proving the stated equal-rate bound.

The slack-rate formulation follows from these two bounds.  In the unequal-rate
case \(r^k\le\bar r^k\).  In the equal-rate case, with
\(q=r/\bar r<1\),
\[
(1+k)r^k=(1+k)q^k\bar r^k
\le \left(\sup_{m\ge0}(1+m)q^m\right)\bar r^k,
\]
and the supremum is finite.  Absorbing the displayed finite factors into
\(C_{\bar r}\) gives the claimed envelope.

Finally fix \(0<\eta<-\log r\).  In the equal-rate case,
\[
\EE e^{\eta G}
=\sum_{k\ge0}e^{\eta k}g_k
\le C_2\sum_{k\ge0}(1+k)(re^\eta)^k
\]
is finite because \(re^\eta<1\).  In the unequal-rate case the same argument
uses \(g_k\le Cr^k\) and the geometric series
\(\sum_{k\ge0}(re^\eta)^k\).  Finally,
\(\EE e^{\eta(G+1)}=e^\eta\EE e^{\eta G}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Stationary killed-leakage three-inclusion complete-cycle CLT]\label{cor:three-inclusion-complete-cycle-clt-main}
In the stationary version of \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}, let
\(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}\) be the exact finite-record statistic from
\Cref{def:cycle-index-inclusion-estimator-main}:
\[
\widehat r_{m,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}
=\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_{m,N}}U_j^{(m)},\qquad m=0,1,2,
\]
with empty sums equal to \(0\).  Put
\[
r=(r_0,r_1,r_2)^\top,\qquad
r_m=\PP(A_0=A_m=1),\qquad
\mu=\EE(G+1).
\]
In Palm cycle coordinates,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inclusion-cycle-reward-identities-main}
U_j^{(0)}=1,\qquad
U_j^{(1)}=\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0\}},\qquad
U_j^{(2)}
=\mathbf1_{\{G_j=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0,\ G_{j+1}=0\}},
\end{equation}
and the centered reward is
\[
Y_j=
\bigl(U_j^{(0)}-r_0L_j,\,
U_j^{(1)}-r_1L_j,\,
U_j^{(2)}-r_2L_j\bigr)^\top .
\]
Then \(Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1})\), \(\EE Y_j=0\), and the reward sequence is
\(1\)-dependent.  With
\[
\mathcal T_1^{(r)}
=\Var(Y_0)+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0),
\qquad
\Sigma_r=\frac{\mathcal T_1^{(r)}}{\mu},
\]
one has
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-r)
\Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,\Sigma_r).
\]
The proof uses the \(O_{\PP}(\log N)\) endpoint replacement and the
renewal random-index replacement from
\Cref{prop:generic-complete-cycle-regenerative-clt-main}; the covariance
scale is exactly \(\mathcal T_1^{(r)}/\mu\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The half-open cycle \([\tau_j,\tau_{j+1})\) contains only the click at
\(\tau_j\), so \(U_j^{(0)}=1\).  A one-step look-ahead is a click exactly
when the next click occurs one sampled step later, so
\(U_j^{(1)}=\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0\}}\).  For lag two, the contributing time must
again be the click at \(\tau_j\); the click two steps later occurs either
when \(G_j=1\), or when \(G_j=0\) and the following gap also has length zero.
This gives the displayed reward identities.

\Cref{lem:local-window-regenerative-decomposition-main}, applied to
\(Z_n^{(m)}=A_nA_{n+m}\), gives the Palm inversion identity
\[
\EE U_j^{(m)}=r_m\EE L_j,\qquad m=0,1,2.
\]
Thus \(\EE Y_j=0\).  The displayed identities show that \(Y_j\) is a
function only of \((G_j,G_{j+1})\), hence \(1\)-dependent, and
\[
\|Y_j\|\le C(1+G_j)
\]
for a fixed finite \(C\).  The exponential moment required by the generic
proposition is supplied by
\Cref{lem:killed-leakage-gap-exponential-moment-main}.

For each coordinate,
\[
\widehat r_{m,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}-r_m
=\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_{m,N}}\{U_j^{(m)}-r_mL_j\}
+\frac{r_m}{N}\left\{\sum_{j\in J_{m,N}}L_j-N\right\}.
\]
The complete cycles used by \(J_{m,N}\) differ from the calendar window
\([0,N)\) by only the initial incomplete cycle, the terminal incomplete
cycle, and a fixed look-ahead cycle.  Their total length is
\(O_{\PP}(\log N)\), by the endpoint proof in
\Cref{prop:generic-complete-cycle-regenerative-clt-main}.  Therefore the
second displayed term is \(o_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), and the first term has the
same \(N^{1/2}\)-limit as \(N^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{K_N-1}Y_j\).
\Cref{prop:generic-complete-cycle-regenerative-clt-main} then supplies the
deterministic \(1\)-dependent CLT, the endpoint replacement, and the
random-index replacement \(K_N/N\to1/\mu\).  The resulting calendar-time
covariance is \(\mathcal T_1^{(r)}/\mu\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[CLT reduction for inclusion coordinates]\label{lem:three-inclusion-clt-reduction-main}
Under \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}, let
\(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}\), \(K_N\), \(L_j=G_j+1\), \(U_j^{(m)}\),
\(r=(r_0,r_1,r_2)^\top\), and \(\mu=\EE L_0\) be as in
\Cref{cor:three-inclusion-complete-cycle-clt-main}.  Define
\[
Y_j=
\bigl(U_j^{(0)}-r_0L_j,\,
U_j^{(1)}-r_1L_j,\,
U_j^{(2)}-r_2L_j\bigr)^\top
=f(G_j,G_{j+1}),
\]
where
\[
U_j^{(0)}=1,\qquad
U_j^{(1)}=\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0\}},\qquad
U_j^{(2)}
=\mathbf1_{\{G_j=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0,G_{j+1}=0\}} .
\]
Then
\[
\EE Y_j=0,\qquad (Y_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}\ \text{is \(1\)-dependent},
\qquad
\frac{K_N}{N}\xrightarrow{\PP}\frac1\mu,
\]
and the finite-record statistic has the cycle-sum reduction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-inclusion-clt-reduction-main}
\sqrt N(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-r)
=
\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{j=0}^{K_N-1}Y_j+o_{\PP}(1).
\end{equation}
Consequently, with
\[
T_1^{(r)}
=\operatorname{Var}(Y_0)+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)
 +\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0),
\qquad
\Sigma_r=\frac{T_1^{(r)}}{\mu},
\]
one has
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-r)
\Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,\Sigma_r).
\]
The same reduction and limit hold for a finite hidden start after a
post-burn-in block of length \(L_N\to\infty\), with \(N\) interpreted as the
retained block length and with all cycle indices formed from that retained
word.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Palm reward identities are exactly
\eqref{eq:inclusion-cycle-reward-identities-main}.  Palm inversion for the
local windows gives \(\EE U_j^{(m)}=r_m\EE L_j\), hence \(\EE Y_j=0\).  The
displayed formulas make \(Y_j\) a function of the two adjacent gaps
\((G_j,G_{j+1})\), so independent renewal gaps imply \(1\)-dependence.

It remains to isolate the calendar-window error.  For each coordinate,
\[
\widehat r_{m,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}-r_m
=
\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_{m,N}}\{U_j^{(m)}-r_mL_j\}
+\frac{r_m}{N}\left\{\sum_{j\in J_{m,N}}L_j-N\right\}.
\]
The complete cycles entering \(J_{m,N}\) differ from the prefix
\(\{0,\ldots,K_N-1\}\) only by the initial incomplete cycle, the terminal
incomplete cycle, and the fixed look-ahead cycles needed by the local
windows.  By the endpoint estimate in
\Cref{prop:generic-complete-cycle-regenerative-clt-main} and the exponential
gap moment of \Cref{lem:killed-leakage-gap-exponential-moment-main}, the total
omitted length is \(O_{\PP}(\log N)\).  Therefore both the length correction
and the replacement of \(J_{m,N}\) by the common cycle prefix are
\(o_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), which proves
\eqref{eq:three-inclusion-clt-reduction-main}.

The renewal law of large numbers gives \(K_N/N\to1/\mu\).  Applying the
fixed-\(1\)-dependent multivariate CLT to the deterministic cycle prefix and
then the random-index replacement in
\Cref{prop:generic-complete-cycle-regenerative-clt-main} gives the displayed
Gaussian limit with covariance \(T_1^{(r)}/\mu\).

For a finite hidden start, apply
\Cref{lem:finite-hidden-start-path-contraction-main} with \(L=L_N\).  The
retained cycle-index estimator, the retained complete-cycle count, and the
retained cycle-sum approximation are measurable functions of the retained
visible \(N\)-word.  Hence their joint law under the finite-start protocol
differs from the stationary-start retained-word law by at most
\(C\theta^{L_N}\to0\) in total variation, uniformly in \(N\).  The stationary
\(o_{\PP}(1)\) reduction, the convergence \(K_N/N\to1/\mu\), and the Gaussian
limit therefore transfer to the finite-start retained block.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local reach and tie-broken projection at the exchange diagonal]\label{lem:exchange-diagonal-local-reach-before-main}
Let
\[
F=\{(x+y,xy):0\le x,y\le1\},\qquad
\sigma_z=(2z,z^2),\quad 0<z<1.
\]
There is an \(\varepsilon_z>0\) such that Euclidean projection onto the full
feasible set \(F\) is single-valued on \(B_{\varepsilon_z}(\sigma_z)\).  On
this ball the deterministic global tie-broken projection
\(\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\) of
\Cref{lem:feasible-set-computable-projection-main} agrees with that unique
ordinary projection, and with the unique local projection onto the hypograph
of the parabola \(\xi_2=\xi_1^2/4\) near \(\sigma_z\).

Consequently, if \(Y_N=\sigma_z+O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), then
\[
\PP\{Y_N\in B_{\varepsilon_z}(\sigma_z)\}\to1
\]
and, with probability tending to one,
\[
\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(Y_N)=\Pi_F(Y_N)=\Pi_{F,z}^{\rm loc}(Y_N),
\]
where \(\Pi_{F,z}^{\rm loc}\) denotes the unique nearest point inside the
local tubular neighbourhood of the exchange arc.  In the same neighbourhood,
for every fixed \(h\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
N^{1/2}\{\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(\sigma_z+h/\sqrt N)-\sigma_z\}
\longrightarrow
\Pi_{H_z}h,\qquad H_z=\{u:u_2\le zu_1\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(\delta>0\) so small that \(2z-\delta>0\), \(2z+\delta<2\), and the
box-boundary pieces of \(F\) other than the exchange parabola are outside
\(B_\delta(\sigma_z)\).  By
\Cref{lem:feasible-set-computable-projection-main}, after possibly shrinking
\(\delta\),
\[
F\cap B_\delta(\sigma_z)
=\{(\xi_1,\xi_2)\in B_\delta(\sigma_z):\xi_2\le \xi_1^2/4\}.
\]
The boundary arc
\(\Gamma_z=\{(t,t^2/4): |t-2z|<\delta\}\) is \(C^2\), hence has a positive
local tubular radius.  Inside this tube the nearest point on \(\Gamma_z\) is
unique, and points on the hypograph side are already feasible.  Since the
closed remote set \(F\setminus B_\delta(\sigma_z)\) has positive distance from
a smaller ball about \(\sigma_z\), one can choose
\(\varepsilon_z>0\) so that no remote feasible point can tie the local nearest
point for any \(v\in B_{\varepsilon_z}(\sigma_z)\).  Thus projection onto the
full, globally nonconvex set \(F\) is single-valued on this ball.  The
tie-broken map \(\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\) is a nearest-point selection; where the
nearest point is unique, its tie-break is inactive, giving the displayed
agreement.

The probability statement follows immediately from
\(\|Y_N-\sigma_z\|=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).  For
\(v=\sigma_z+h/\sqrt N\), the local feasibility inequality is
\[
z^2+\frac{h_2}{\sqrt N}
\le
\frac{(2z+h_1/\sqrt N)^2}{4},
\]
or
\[
h_2\le zh_1+\frac{h_1^2}{4\sqrt N}.
\]
Thus the scaled local feasible sets converge on bounded \(h\)-sets to the
closed half-space \(H_z=\{h:h_2\le zh_1\}\).  Shapiro's boundary projection
differentiability theorem for sets with positive local reach
\cite{Shapiro1987MetricProjection}, equivalently elementary
Painleve--Kuratowski convergence to the closed convex half-space with unique
nearest points, gives the directional projection limit.  The preceding
global--local agreement transfers this limit from the local projection to
\(\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite-record protocol and algorithms for the main theorems.}
\phantomsection\label{alg:all-complete-cycle-separated-batch-main}
The operational rules used by
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main,thm:finite-record-quotient-delta-main,thm:finite-record-exchange-diagonal-main}
are fixed before the theorem statements.  The deterministic thresholds are
\begin{equation}\label{prot:finite-record-main}
b_N=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor,\qquad
t_N=N^{-1/4},\qquad
\varepsilon_N=N^{-1/4},\qquad
\eta_N=N^{-1/8},\qquad
\delta_N=N^{-1/8}.
\end{equation}
Fix once and for all an integer \(K_{\min}\ge2\).  After the complete-cycle
sets, batch count, and quotient denominator have been formed, the operational
reporting event is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-record-reporting-event-main}
\mathcal E_N=
\{K_N\ge K_{\min},\ M_N\ge2,\ \widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}\ge\varepsilon_N,
|\widehat D_N|\ge\varepsilon_N\}.
\end{equation}
On \(\mathcal E_N\) the protocol computes the estimator, sandwich covariance,
rank, and any confidence set specified below.  On
\(\mathcal E_N^c\) it reports ``insufficient complete cycles; no confidence
set.''  Since \(K_{\min}\) is fixed, \(\PP(\mathcal E_N^c)\to0\) at every
population point in the stable quotient chart by the complete-cycle law and
the denominator consistency already used in the inverse delta method; the
asymptotic statements are therefore unchanged.  The event is a finite-record
reporting gate, not a population-level exclusion.
The separated-batch covariance rate is \(N^{-1/3}\), the retained-rank
threshold is larger than the covariance-estimation error, and
\(N^{1/2}\delta_N^2=N^{1/4}\to\infty\).  In the exceptional rank-one diagonal
calibration use the deterministic angle threshold \(a_N=N^{-1/8}\), so
\(t_N/a_N\to0\).  The main retained-record asymptotics below stop at visible-law
coordinates: \(r\), the symmetric quotient coordinate \((p+s,ps)\), the
unordered root and rate sets obtained from that coordinate, and, on the
exchange diagonal, the common numerical rate.  Separated labelled rates are not
finite-record estimands of the visible record alone.  Their Gaussian and Wald
limits are isolated later in \Cref{cor:side-data-labelled-rate-wald-main},
where an external side-data model supplies the missing branch label.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.35em]

U_j^{(1)}-\widehat r_{1,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}L_j,\,
U_j^{(2)}-\widehat r_{2,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}L_j\bigr)^\top .
\]

\item With \(b_N=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\), set \(d_N=b_N+1\) and use separated

I_{\ell,N}:=\{(\ell-1)d_N,\ldots,(\ell-1)d_N+b_N-1\}.

The omitted index \(\ell d_N-1\) is the separator.

\item Let

\text{ for every }1\le\ell\le m\},

\widehat D_N=
\widehat r_{1,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}-(\widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}})^2,
\qquad
\mathcal E_N=

\]

\widehat B_{\ell,N}:=\sum_{j\in I_{\ell,N}}\widehat Y_j,\qquad
\overline{\widehat B}_N:=M_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}\widehat B_{\ell,N}
\quad\text{on }\mathcal E_N.
\]
Report the covariance estimator only on \(\mathcal E_N\):
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}
=
\frac{K_N}{N}\,
\frac1{b_N(M_N-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}
(\widehat B_{\ell,N}-\overline{\widehat B}_N)
(\widehat B_{\ell,N}-\overline{\widehat B}_N)^\top .
\]
On \(\mathcal E_N^c\), do not form a zero replacement; report
``insufficient complete cycles; no confidence set.''
\end{enumerate}

\begin{assumption}[Finite-record master assumption]\label{ass:finite-record-master-main}
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main,thm:finite-record-quotient-delta-main,thm:finite-record-exchange-diagonal-main}
are read under the following named hypotheses.  These hypotheses collect the
finite-record law, estimand, reporting gate, covariance-estimation, projection,
and calibration conventions; the theorem statements below invoke these names
rather than restating the full protocol.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{FR-\arabic*.},ref=FR-\arabic*,leftmargin=3.0em,itemsep=0.45em]
\item\label{fr:law-main}\textbf{Record law and retained word.}
Assumption~\ref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main} holds.  The observed
data are the retained post-burn-in visible word of length \(N\).  All
complete-cycle sets, batches, projections, Wald statistics, and confidence
sets are functions only of that retained word.

\item\label{fr:estimands-main}\textbf{Visible-record estimands.}
The inclusion estimand is
\[
r=(r_0,r_1,r_2),\qquad r_m=\PP(A_0=A_m=1).
\]
On the stable quotient chart \(r_0>0\) and \(D=r_1-r_0^2\ne0\), the symmetric
quotient estimand is
\[
\sigma_{\rm sym}=\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps),
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{r_2-r_0^2}{r_1-r_0^2}.
\]
The finite visible record identifies \(\{p,s\}\), the unordered numerical rate
set
\[
\left\{-\frac{\log p}{\Delta\tau},-\frac{\log s}{\Delta\tau}\right\},
\]
and, on the exchange diagonal \(p=s=z\), the common numerical rate
\(-\log z/\Delta\tau\).  Ordered labelled rates are not visible-record
estimands here; they require the separate side-data convention in
\Cref{cor:side-data-labelled-rate-wald-main}.

\item\label{fr:protocol-main}\textbf{Operational gate and deterministic thresholds.}
The deterministic thresholds are those in \eqref{prot:finite-record-main}, with
fixed \(K_{\min}\ge2\), and the reporting event is \(\mathcal E_N\) in
\eqref{eq:finite-record-reporting-event-main}.  On \(\mathcal E_N^c\) the
protocol reports insufficient complete cycles and forms no confidence set.  At
every stable quotient population point, \(\PP(\mathcal E_N^c)\to0\).

\item\label{fr:covariance-main}\textbf{Covariance estimator and retained rank.}
Algorithm~1 is the covariance rule.  On \(\mathcal E_N\) it reports
\(\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}\); no auxiliary zero matrix is used as an operational
replacement.  The quotient covariance is the sandwich
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}
=D\Phi(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}})
 \widehat\Sigma_{r,N}
 D\Phi(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}})^\top,
\]
after the same feasible projection and chart retention as Algorithm~2.
Diagonal statistics use exactly the empirical eigenvalues of
\(\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}\) retained above \(t_N=N^{-1/4}\); rank retention
is justified by
\(\|\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}-\Sigma_\sigma\|_{\operatorname{op}}=o_{\PP}(t_N)\).

\item\label{fr:projection-main}\textbf{Feasible projection and chart retention.}
Algorithm~2 is the projection and chart-retention rule.  The raw symmetric
coordinate is projected to
\[
\widetilde\sigma_N=\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(\Phi(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}})),
\qquad
F=\{(x+y,xy):0\le x,y\le1\}.
\]
At separated roots with \(\Delta_\sigma>0\), the theorem reports only the
unordered root and rate sets.  A labelled separated-rate report is withheld
unless an external branch map satisfies
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.

\item\label{fr:diagonal-main}\textbf{Exchange-diagonal local projection.}
At \(\sigma_z=(2z,z^2)\), \(0<z<1\), the local-reach hypothesis is exactly
\Cref{lem:exchange-diagonal-local-reach-before-main}: with probability tending
to one, the global tie-broken projection agrees with the unique local
projection, and its tangent projection is onto
\[
H_z=\{h\in\mathbb R^2:h_2\le zh_1\}.
\]

\item\label{fr:mc-main}\textbf{Diagonal calibration and conservative reporting.}
The exchange-diagonal statistic uses the \(t_N\)-thresholded pseudoinverse,
the ideal conditional projected-Gaussian lower quantile
\(c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)\), and the optional simulated order statistic
\(\widehat c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N,R_N^{\mathrm{MC}}}(z)\) defined by Algorithm~3,
which is stated after Main Theorem~3C.  The theorem itself is the sole
coverage statement: it classifies the computable D-MAP covariance
\(\Sigma_\sigma(z)\) from \eqref{eq:dmap-diagonal-sigma-finite-main} into the
full-rank/non-normal rank-one exact case, the rank-one-normal atom-at-zero
conservative case, and the zero-covariance conservative case.  Replacing the
ideal conditional quantile by finitely many Monte Carlo draws adds only the
conditional DKW/quantile error in
\Cref{lem:conditional-monte-carlo-quantile-main}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{maintheorem}[Fixed-setting retained-record inclusion CLT]\label{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main}\label{prop:three-coordinate-vector-batch-main}
Assume the finite-record master assumption
\Cref{ass:finite-record-master-main}, specifically
\ref{fr:law-main}, \ref{fr:estimands-main}, \ref{fr:protocol-main}, and
\ref{fr:covariance-main}.  This is a fixed-setting retained-record theorem:
fix \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) in the stable quotient chart
and let \(\mathcal E_N\) be the reporting event.  Then
\(\PP_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau}(\mathcal E_N^c)\to0\), and all
stated confidence sets are reported only on \(\mathcal E_N\).  For every fixed
population point,
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-r)\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_r),
\]
with no uniformity in \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\) unless separately
proved.  On \(\mathcal E_N^c\), no confidence set is reported.
From the retained visible word form the complete-cycle inclusion estimator
\[
\widehat r_{m,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}
=\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_{m,N}}U_j^{(m)},\qquad m=0,1,2,
\]
with the empty-sum convention in
\Cref{def:cycle-index-inclusion-estimator-main}, and write
\(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}
=(\widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}},
  \widehat r_{1,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}},
  \widehat r_{2,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}})^\top\).  Let
\[
r=(r_0,r_1,r_2)^\top,\qquad r_m=\PP(A_0=A_m=1).
\]
In Palm cycle coordinates put
\[
U_j=
\bigl(1,\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0\}},
\mathbf1_{\{G_j=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0,G_{j+1}=0\}}\bigr)^\top,
\qquad
L_j=G_j+1,\qquad
Y_j=U_j-rL_j .
\]
Then
\[
\EE Y_j=0,\qquad (Y_j)\text{ is \(1\)-dependent},
\]
and
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-r)
=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{j=0}^{K_N-1}Y_j+o_{\PP}(1).
\]

Consequently
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-r)
\Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,\Sigma_r),
\]
where
\[
\Sigma_r=\frac1\mu\left\{\operatorname{Var}(Y_0)
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0)\right\},
\qquad \mu=\EE L_0 .
\]
The separated-batch estimator \(\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}\) in Algorithm~1 obeys
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/3}).
\]
This covariance assertion is the direct specialization of the separated
regenerative batch theorem
\Cref{lem:separated-batch-covariance-estimator-main} with \(m=1\),
\(T_m=T_1^{(r)}\), and \(b_N=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\).  Its displayed plug-in
bound applies to \(\widehat Y_j=U_j-\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}L_j\).
\end{maintheorem}

\begin{maintheorem}[Fixed-setting retained-record quotient delta method]\label{thm:finite-record-quotient-delta-main}
Assume the finite-record master assumption
\Cref{ass:finite-record-master-main}, specifically
\ref{fr:law-main}--\ref{fr:projection-main}.  Assume also that the population
inverse is in the stable quotient chart
\[
r_0>0,\qquad D=r_1-r_0^2\ne0 .
\]
This is a fixed-setting retained-record theorem: fix
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) in this stable quotient chart and
let \(\mathcal E_N\) be the reporting event.  Then
\(\PP_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau}(\mathcal E_N^c)\to0\), and all
stated confidence sets are reported only on \(\mathcal E_N\).  For every fixed
population point,
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-r)\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_r),
\]
with no uniformity in \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\) unless separately
proved.  On \(\mathcal E_N^c\), no confidence set is reported.  Define only the quotient map
\[
\Phi(r)=\left(1-\frac{r_1}{r_0}+\lambda,\;
r_0(1-\lambda)-\frac{r_1}{r_0}+\lambda\right),
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{r_2-r_0^2}{r_1-r_0^2}.
\]
Then
\[
\sqrt N\{\Phi(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}})-\Phi(r)\}
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_\sigma),
\qquad
\Sigma_\sigma=D\Phi(r)\Sigma_rD\Phi(r)^\top.
\]
For the constrained D-MAP population, \(\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps)\).  If
\(\Delta_\sigma=\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2>0\), the polynomial
\(z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2\) identifies the unordered survival-factor set
\(\{p,s\}\), and hence the unordered numerical rate set
\[
\left\{-\frac{\log p}{\Delta\tau},-\frac{\log s}{\Delta\tau}\right\}.
\]
This theorem contains no stochastic theorem for learning a branch map and no
Wald statement for an ordered labelled vector
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\).  Ordered labelled-rate inference is a separate
side-data conclusion, stated in
\Cref{cor:side-data-labelled-rate-wald-main}.
\end{maintheorem}

\begin{maintheorem}[Fixed-setting retained-record exchange-diagonal projected Gaussian confidence]\label{thm:finite-record-exchange-diagonal-main}
Assume the finite-record master assumption
\Cref{ass:finite-record-master-main}, specifically
\ref{fr:law-main}--\ref{fr:mc-main}.  Fix a true exchange-diagonal point
\(\sigma_{z_0}=(2z_0,z_0^2)\), \(0<z_0<1\), and a confidence level
\(0<\alpha<1\).  This is a fixed-setting retained-record theorem: all limits
are pointwise in the fixed D-MAP parameter, and all diagonal confidence sets
are reported only on \(\mathcal E_N\), with \(\PP(\mathcal E_N^c)\to0\).  No
uniformity in \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\), no finite-sample coverage, and
no model-selection-valid coverage across reporting branches is asserted.  The
separated-root labelled-rate chart is not used on the diagonal.

Let
\[
\widehat\sigma_N=\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(\widehat\sigma_N^{\rm raw}),\qquad
\sigma_z=(2z,z^2),\qquad
H_z=\{h\in\mathbb R^2:h_2\le zh_1\}.
\]
Assume
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat\sigma_N^{\rm raw}-\sigma_{z_0})
\Rightarrow Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_\sigma(z_0)),\qquad
\|\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}-\Sigma_\sigma(z_0)\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=o_{\PP}(t_N).
\]
By \Cref{lem:exchange-diagonal-local-reach-before-main}, the global
tie-broken projection agrees with the ordinary local projection near
\(\sigma_{z_0}\) with probability tending to one, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diagonal-projected-limit-main}
\sqrt N(\widehat\sigma_N-\sigma_{z_0})
\Rightarrow
\Pi_{H_{z_0}}(Z),\qquad
Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_\sigma(z_0)).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for every fixed \(h\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
N^{1/2}\bigl(\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(\sigma_z+h/\sqrt N)-\sigma_z\bigr)
\longrightarrow \Pi_{H_z}h .
\]

For each candidate \(0<z<1\), form the retained-rank statistic
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-projected-statistic-main}
Q_N(z)=
N(\widehat\sigma_N-\sigma_z)^\top\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}^{+}
(\widehat\sigma_N-\sigma_z),
\end{equation}
where \(\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}^{+}\) is the \(t_N\)-thresholded
pseudoinverse.  Let \(c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)\) be the ideal lower conditional
\((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of
\[
T_N^\ast(z)=
\Pi_{H_z}(Z_N^\ast)^\top\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}^{+}\Pi_{H_z}(Z_N^\ast),
\qquad
Z_N^\ast\mid A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\sim
\mathcal N(0,\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}).
\]
Define the unbuffered and buffered diagonal sets
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-projected-confidence-set-main}
C^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\sigma,N}
:=\{(2z,z^2):0<z<1,\ Q_N(z)\le c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)\},
\end{equation}
\[
C^{\mathrm{diag},\varepsilon}_{\sigma,N}
:=\{(2z,z^2):0<z<1,\ Q_N(z)\le c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)+\varepsilon\},
\qquad \varepsilon>0.
\]
Let
\[
T_z=(\Pi_{H_z}Z)^\top\Sigma_\sigma(z)^+(\Pi_{H_z}Z),\qquad
c_{1-\alpha}(z)=\inf\{c:\PP(T_z\le c)\ge1-\alpha\},
\]
with \(Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_\sigma(z))\).  The covariance
\(\Sigma_\sigma(z)\) is not an abstract rank assumption: it is the computable
D-MAP matrix in \eqref{eq:dmap-diagonal-sigma-finite-main}, classified by
\Cref{lem:dmap-diagonal-covariance-alternatives-main}.  Exactly one of the
following three coverage branches applies at the true \(z_0\).

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.5em,itemsep=0.55em]
\item \textbf{Continuous exact lower quantile.}
If the lower \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of
\[
(\Pi_{H_{z_0}}Z)^\top\Sigma_\sigma(z_0)^+(\Pi_{H_{z_0}}Z)
\]
is a continuity point and the ideal projected-Gaussian calibration is exact,
then the unbuffered diagonal set has pointwise first-order coverage
\[
\PP\{\sigma_{z_0}\in C^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\sigma,N}\}\longrightarrow
1-\alpha .
\]
For the constrained D-MAP covariance in
\eqref{eq:dmap-diagonal-sigma-finite-main}, this branch applies in particular
when either
\[
\operatorname{rank}\Sigma_\sigma(z_0)=2
\]
or
\[
\operatorname{rank}\Sigma_\sigma(z_0)=1,\qquad
\operatorname{Range}\Sigma_\sigma(z_0)\ne
\operatorname{span}\{(-z_0,1)^\top\},
\]
because \(T_{z_0}\) is then continuous.
This is the only diagonal branch in which the theorem reports exact
unbuffered projected-Gaussian coverage.

\item \textbf{Discontinuous lower quantile: buffered lower coverage.}
If the limiting projected statistic has an atom at its lower quantile, no exact
unbuffered coverage statement is made from this branch alone.  For every fixed
\(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\liminf_{N\to\infty}
\PP\{\sigma_{z_0}\in C^{\mathrm{diag},\varepsilon}_{\sigma,N}\}
\ge1-\alpha .
\]
For the constrained D-MAP covariance this is exactly the rank-one normal
direction case
\[
\operatorname{rank}\Sigma_\sigma(z_0)=1,\qquad
\operatorname{Range}\Sigma_\sigma(z_0)=
\operatorname{span}\{(-z_0,1)^\top\},
\]
where
\[
\PP(T_{z_0}=0)=\frac12,\qquad
\PP(T_{z_0}\in\{c\})=0\quad(c>0).
\]

\item \textbf{Zero covariance: zero-rank fallback and buffered coverage.}
If
\[
\Sigma_\sigma(z_0)=0,
\]
then the covariance-consistency assumption above implies that all empirical
eigenvalues are below \(t_N\) with probability
tending to one.  Hence the retained pseudoinverse is zero, \(Q_N(z_0)=0\), and
the conditional projected-Gaussian statistic \(T_N^\ast(z_0)\) is also zero
with probability tending to one.  The protocol reports the retained-rank-zero
fallback, not an exact unbuffered interval.  For every fixed
\(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\liminf_{N\to\infty}
\PP\{\sigma_{z_0}\in C^{\mathrm{diag},\varepsilon}_{\sigma,N}\}
\ge1-\alpha .
\]
\end{enumerate}

The conservative lower bounds in (ii) and (iii) are the buffered
random-quantile conclusion of
\Cref{lem:buffered-random-quantile-coverage-main}, applied to
\[
T_N=Q_N(z_0),\qquad T=T_{z_0},\qquad
F_N^\ast(t)=\PP^\ast\{T_N^\ast(z_0)\le t\},\qquad p=1-\alpha .
\]
They do not require continuity of the lower limiting quantile.

Algorithm~3 below gives the implementable simulation of
\(c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)\).  The theorem's coverage statements use the ideal
conditional quantile.  Replacing it by the
\(R_N^{\mathrm{MC}}\)-draw order statistic
\(\widehat c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N,R_N^{\mathrm{MC}}}(z)\) adds exactly the
conditional DKW/quantile error of
\Cref{lem:conditional-monte-carlo-quantile-main}; if the conditional quantile
is locally separated with modulus \(m_N\) on radius \(\eta_N\), then with
conditional probability at least \(1-\delta_{\mathrm{MC}}\) this additional
critical-value error is at most
\[
\frac{2\sqrt{\log(2/\delta_{\mathrm{MC}})/
        (2R_N^{\mathrm{MC}})}+(R_N^{\mathrm{MC}})^{-1}}{m_N}
\]
whenever the displayed value is at most \(\eta_N\).  This is a finite
simulation guarantee and is separate from the record asymptotic limit.

Pushing any of the diagonal sets forward by
\[
z\longmapsto -\frac{\log z}{\Delta\tau}
\]
gives the corresponding finite-record confidence set for the identified common
numerical rate \(\gamma_{\rm diag}\), equivalently for the invariant ordered
quotient pair \((\gamma_{\rm diag},\gamma_{\rm diag})\).  The coverage event is
identical before and after this push-forward.  All diagonal coverage
statements are pointwise in the true \(z_0\); no uniform coverage over
\(z\in(0,1)\) is claimed.
\end{maintheorem}

\paragraph{Inverse delta method and projected exchange-diagonal charts.}
The next propositions keep three operations distinct.  First, the population
quotient inverse is the map \(r\mapsto(p+s,ps)\).  Second, separated labelled
rates require \(\Delta_\sigma>0\) and the deterministic oracle branch map of
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}, or an estimated map with the
high-probability oracle-agreement condition stated there.  Third, a
finite-record implementation uses explicit denominator thresholds, projection
onto the feasible symmetric-coordinate set, and a no-label rule when the
empirical roots are not retained.

\begin{lemma}[Feasible symmetric-coordinate set and computable projection]\label{lem:feasible-set-computable-projection-main}
Let
\[
F=\{(x+y,xy):0\le x,y\le1\}.
\]
For \(0\le t\le2\), set
\[
\ell_F(t)=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\le t\le1,\\
t-1,&1\le t\le2 .
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feasible-symmetric-coordinate-set-main}
F=\{(\sigma_1,\sigma_2):0\le\sigma_1\le2,\quad
\ell_F(\sigma_1)\le\sigma_2\le\sigma_1^2/4\}.
\end{equation}
For any \(v=(v_1,v_2)\in\mathbb R^2\), define
\[
c_v(t)
=\min\{\max\{v_2,\ell_F(t)\},\,t^2/4\},
\qquad 0\le t\le2,
\]
and
\[
q_v(t)=(t-v_1)^2+(c_v(t)-v_2)^2 .
\]
The deterministic projection used below is the one-dimensional rule
\begin{equation}\label{eq:computable-projection-rule-main}
t_v=\min\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{0\le t\le2}q_v(t),
\qquad
\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(v)=(t_v,c_v(t_v)).
\end{equation}
This rule is Borel measurable, is a Euclidean nearest-point projection onto
\(F\), and resolves global nonuniqueness by choosing the smallest minimizing
\(\sigma_1\).  On any neighbourhood where the Euclidean nearest point is
unique, \(\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\) agrees with the ordinary projection.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(t=x+y\).  The unit-square constraints imply \(0\le t\le2\).  Among
points in \([0,1]^2\) with sum \(t\), the product \(xy\) is maximized at
\(x=y=t/2\), giving \(xy\le t^2/4\).  The minimum is attained at the boundary:
it is \(0\) for \(0\le t\le1\), and \(t-1\) for \(1\le t\le2\), because then
one coordinate can be \(1\) and the other \(t-1\).  Conversely, every value
between these two endpoints is obtained by continuously moving \(x\) along the
line segment \(\{(x,t-x):0\le x,t-x\le1\}\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:feasible-symmetric-coordinate-set-main}.

For fixed first coordinate \(t\), the closest feasible second coordinate to
\(v_2\) is exactly the clipping \(c_v(t)\) to the closed interval
\([\ell_F(t),t^2/4]\).  Therefore minimizing the squared Euclidean distance
from \(v\) to \(F\) reduces to minimizing \(q_v\) over the compact interval
\([0,2]\).  The minimum exists because \(q_v\) is continuous.  Choosing the
smallest minimizer gives the displayed deterministic tie-break.  Since the
argmin graph of a continuous function on a compact metric space is closed, the
leftmost argmin selection is Borel measurable.  The selected point is a nearest
point by the preceding fixed-\(t\) reduction, and uniqueness makes the
tie-break irrelevant.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-record quotient-inverse reporting rule]\label{prop:finite-record-threshold-projection-rule-main}
Let \(\widehat r_N\) be any three-coordinate finite-record estimator.  On the
population inverse domain
\[
r_0>0,\qquad r_1/r_0\ne r_0,
\]
define
\[
a(r)=\frac{r_1}{r_0},\qquad
u_2(r)=\frac{r_2}{r_0},\qquad
\lambda(r)=\frac{u_2(r)-r_0}{a(r)-r_0},
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-inverse-delta-map-main}
\Phi(r)=
\bigl(1-a(r)+\lambda(r),\,
r_0(1-\lambda(r))-a(r)+\lambda(r)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Let
\[
F=\{(x+y,xy):0\le x,y\le1\},\qquad \sigma^\circ=(1,1/4),
\]
and let \(\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\) be the computable tie-broken projection
\eqref{eq:computable-projection-rule-main}.  Equivalently, for
\(v=(v_1,v_2)\), one minimizes the one-variable continuous function
\((t-v_1)^2+(c_v(t)-v_2)^2\) over \(0\le t\le2\), where \(c_v(t)\) clips
\(v_2\) to the feasible vertical interval
\([\ell_F(t),t^2/4]\), and chooses the smallest minimizing \(t\) if there is a
global tie.  Choose deterministic thresholds
\[
\varepsilon_N\downarrow0,\qquad \eta_N\downarrow0,\qquad
\delta_N\downarrow0,\qquad N^{1/2}\delta_N^2\to\infty .
\]
For every finite record, set
\[
\widehat D_N=\widehat r_{1,N}-\widehat r_{0,N}^2
\]
and define the internal raw symmetric-coordinate diagnostic by
\[
\widehat\sigma^{\rm raw}_N=
\begin{cases}
\Phi(\widehat r_N),
&\widehat r_{0,N}\ge\varepsilon_N,\quad |\widehat D_N|\ge\varepsilon_N,\\
\sigma^\circ,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
The reported symmetric estimator is
\[
\widetilde\sigma_N=\Pi_F^{\rm tb}(\widehat\sigma^{\rm raw}_N).
\]
Define also
\[
\widehat J_{\Phi,N}=
\begin{cases}
D\Phi(\widehat r_N),
&\widehat r_{0,N}\ge\varepsilon_N,\quad |\widehat D_N|\ge\varepsilon_N,\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\qquad
\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}
=\widehat J_{\Phi,N}\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}
\widehat J_{\Phi,N}^{\top}.
\]
For labelled reporting define the empirical projected discriminant
\[
\widehat\Delta_{\sigma,N}
=\widetilde\sigma_{1,N}^{\,2}-4\widetilde\sigma_{2,N}\ge0 .
\]
Labelled reporting also requires the deterministic oracle separated-root
branch map \(\mathcal B\) of
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}, or an estimated map
\(\widehat{\mathcal B}_N\) that is used only together with the correctness
event in the estimated-map reduction following
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.  We write
\(\mathcal B(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{root}}(\sigma))\) for the resulting ordered
pair of roots when the oracle assignment is unique.
The finite-record rule reports no separated labelled roots and no separated
labelled rates whenever
\(\widehat\Delta_{\sigma,N}<\delta_N^2\).  This is a no-branch decision, not a
claim that the diagonal common-rate estimand is absent.  If the population
point is \(\sigma_z=(2z,z^2)\), the target common rate is instead
\(-\log z/\Delta\tau\) and its finite-record calibration is the projected
boundary construction of
\Cref{thm:finite-record-exchange-diagonal-main} pushed forward
by \Cref{cor:exchange-diagonal-common-rate-confidence-main}.  On the
complementary separated-root event, compute the two roots of
\(z^2-\widetilde\sigma_{1,N}z+\widetilde\sigma_{2,N}=0\).  If either root lies
outside \([\eta_N,1-\eta_N]\), or if \(\mathcal B\) is not unique on those
roots, the rule withholds separated labelled-rate reporting.  If an estimated
map \(\widehat{\mathcal B}_N\) is used and its high-probability correctness
condition is not an explicit assumption of the reported inference, the rule
also withholds separated labelled-rate reporting.  Only on the retained event
are the separated labelled roots and rates recorded as
\[
(\widehat p_N,\widehat s_N)
=\mathcal B(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{root}}(\widetilde\sigma_N)),
\qquad
(\widehat\Gamma_N,\widehat\kappa_{\mathrm r,N})
=\left(-\frac{\log\widehat p_N}{\Delta\tau},
       -\frac{\log\widehat s_N}{\Delta\tau}\right).
\]
The rule is total as a diagnostic map for every finite record, but inferential
reporting is gated by \(\mathcal E_N\) in
\eqref{eq:finite-record-reporting-event-main}.  On \(\mathcal E_N^c\), the
finite-record protocol reports ``insufficient complete cycles; no confidence
set'' rather than substituting \(\sigma^\circ\) or a zero covariance matrix as
an inferential output.  On \(\mathcal E_N\), it produces projected symmetric
coordinates and it produces separated labelled rates only on the retained
event just described.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each displayed object is either a deterministic threshold, the explicit
projection of \Cref{lem:feasible-set-computable-projection-main}, a
finite-record statistic, or the deterministic oracle branch-map input of
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.  An estimated branch map is not
silently folded into the finite-record statistic; it is admissible for
labelled reporting only under the separate high-probability correctness event
of the estimated-map reduction following
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.  The diagnostic cases cover all
records: small denominator gives the internal placeholder \(\sigma^\circ\),
otherwise \(\Phi(\widehat r_N)\) is computed and projected onto \(F\) by the
one-dimensional clipped-interval minimization.  The finite-record reporting
gate \(\mathcal E_N\) is then checked before any estimator, covariance, or
confidence set is reported.  The remaining tests either retain a separated
labelled chart or withhold separated labelled rates.  Hence the computational
rule is well-defined for every finite record, while bad finite records do not
produce misleading inferential output.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-window error bound for projected renewal reports]\label{lem:projected-complete-law-fixed-window-error-main}
Let
\[
F_{\rm sep}^{\circ}
=\{(x+y,xy):0<x<1,\ 0<y<1,\ x\ne y\}.
\]
Fix a compact set \(K\Subset F_{\rm sep}^{\circ}\).  For
\(\sigma\in K\), let \(\PP_\sigma\) denote the stationary renewal law
obtained by the projected complete-law reconstruction of
\Cref{lem:algorithmic-complete-law-reconstruction-main}: compute the two
unordered roots of \(z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2=0\), form the corresponding
gap law \(g_k(\sigma)\), set
\(\rho_*(\sigma)=\{1+\sum_{k\ge0}k g_k(\sigma)\}^{-1}\), and then use the
renewal inclusion product and finite inclusion--exclusion.

For every finite window \(I\subset\mathbb Z\) there is a finite constant
\(C_I(K)\) such that, for all \(\sigma,\sigma'\in K\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-complete-law-fixed-window-lipschitz-main}
\max_{S\subset I}
\left|\PP_\sigma\{\varXi\cap I=S\}
      -\PP_{\sigma'}\{\varXi\cap I=S\}\right|
\le C_I(K)\,\|\sigma-\sigma'\| .
\end{equation}
Consequently, if the population point \(\sigma_0=\Phi(r)\) lies in
\(F_{\rm sep}^{\circ}\) and the finite-record projected coordinate
\(\widetilde\sigma_N\) is retained in such a compact \(K\) with
\(\sigma_0\in K\), then the reported projected complete-law probabilities
satisfy the fixed-window deterministic bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-record-projected-word-error-bound-main}
\max_{S\subset I}
\left|\widetilde\PP_N\{\varXi\cap I=S\}
      -\PP_{\sigma_0}\{\varXi\cap I=S\}\right|
\le C_I(K)\,\|\widetilde\sigma_N-\sigma_0\|.
\end{equation}
On a denominator-stable neighbourhood of \(r\), if projection is inactive and
\(\Phi\) has Lipschitz constant \(L_\Phi\), then the same event also gives
\[
\max_{S\subset I}
\left|\widetilde\PP_N\{\varXi\cap I=S\}
      -\PP_{\sigma_0}\{\varXi\cap I=S\}\right|
\le C_I(K)L_\Phi\,\|\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-r\|.
\]
In particular, under the finite-record CLT hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main}, every fixed-window projected
complete-law report has error \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) at separated interior
population points.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Compactness in \(F_{\rm sep}^{\circ}\) gives constants \(\eta>0\) and
\(\delta>0\) such that every root pair over \(K\) lies in
\([\eta,1-\eta]^2\) and has separation at least \(\delta\).  Therefore the
ordered roots
\[
z_-(\sigma)=\frac{\sigma_1-\sqrt{\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2}}2,\qquad
z_+(\sigma)=\frac{\sigma_1+\sqrt{\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2}}2
\]
are \(C^1\), with uniformly bounded derivatives on a neighbourhood of \(K\).
The logarithms \(\theta_\pm(\sigma)=-\log z_\pm(\sigma)\) are also \(C^1\)
there.  For each fixed \(k\), the unequal-root formula in
\eqref{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-gap-main} is therefore \(C^1\) in
\(\sigma\) on that neighbourhood.

The derivative bounds are uniform for the infinite mean appearing in
\(\rho_*\).  Indeed \(z_\pm\le1-\eta\) on a neighbourhood of \(K\), and the
derivatives of \(z_\pm^k\) are bounded by a constant times
\((k+1)(1-\eta/2)^k\) after shrinking the neighbourhood.  Hence
\(\sum_{k\ge0} k g_k(\sigma)\) and its first derivatives converge uniformly
on \(K\).  Thus \(\rho_*(\sigma)\) is Lipschitz on \(K\).

If \(I=\emptyset\), the only word has probability \(1\) under every
\(\PP_\sigma\), so the claim is trivial.  Otherwise let
\(d_I=\max I-\min I\), with \(d_I=0\) for a one-point window.  The Palm
renewal mass \(u_n(\sigma)\), \(0\le n\le d_I\), is a finite polynomial in
\(g_0(\sigma),\ldots,g_{n-1}(\sigma)\), with \(u_0=1\).  Hence each
\(u_n\) needed by the inclusion products in the window is Lipschitz on
\(K\).  For a nonempty \(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\subset I\), the inclusion
functional
\[
\rho_*(\sigma)\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}
u_{i_{\ell+1}-i_\ell}(\sigma)
\]
is therefore Lipschitz on \(K\), with a constant depending only on \(I\) and
\(K\).  The empty inclusion has constant value \(1\).  Finally, each word
probability is a finite signed sum of at most \(2^{|I|}\) such inclusion
functionals, by
\eqref{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-word-main}.  Taking the maximum over the
finite set of \(S\subset I\) gives
\eqref{eq:projected-complete-law-fixed-window-lipschitz-main}.

The finite-record statement is obtained by substituting
\(\sigma'=\widetilde\sigma_N\) and \(\sigma=\sigma_0\) in
\eqref{eq:projected-complete-law-fixed-window-lipschitz-main}; the reported
\(\widetilde\PP_N\) is exactly \(\PP_{\widetilde\sigma_N}\) on the retained
complete-law branch.  On a denominator-stable neighbourhood the map
\(\Phi\) is \(C^1\), hence Lipschitz.  When projection is inactive,
\(\widetilde\sigma_N=\Phi(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}})\), and the final
display follows.  The \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) conclusion follows from
\(\sqrt N(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-r)=O_{\PP}(1)\) in
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main} and from the fact that
projection is eventually inactive with probability tending to one at
separated interior points.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-record complete-law reporting is renewal-consistent]\label{prop:finite-record-complete-law-renewal-consistency-main}
Use the finite-record reporting gate \(\mathcal E_N\), the raw inverse map
\(\Phi\), the feasible symmetric-coordinate projection \(\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\), and
the thresholds of
\Cref{prop:finite-record-threshold-projection-rule-main}.  A finite-record
complete-law report is formed only on \(\mathcal E_N\) and only when the
inverse denominators are retained:
\[
\widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}\ge\varepsilon_N,\qquad
|\widehat r_{1,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}
-(\widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}})^2|\ge\varepsilon_N .
\]
On that event compute
\[
\widetilde\sigma_N
=\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\{\Phi(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}})\}.
\]
Then compute the projected root pair from
\[
z^2-\widetilde\sigma_{1,N}z+\widetilde\sigma_{2,N}=0,
\]
and withhold complete-law probabilities if either projected root lies outside
\([\eta_N,1-\eta_N]\).  On the retained interior-root event, compute the
projected gap law \(\widetilde g_{k,N}\) by the formulas in
\Cref{lem:algorithmic-complete-law-reconstruction-main}, the projected Palm
renewal masses \(\widetilde u_{n,N}\), and the projected stationary renewal
intensity
\[
\widetilde\rho_N
=\rho_*(\widetilde\sigma_N)
=\left(1+\sum_{k\ge0}k\,\widetilde g_{k,N}\right)^{-1}.
\]
The finite-record inclusion product for a nonempty
\(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\) is then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-record-renewal-consistent-inclusions-main}
\widetilde\PP_N(B\subset\varXi)
=\widetilde\rho_N
 \prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\widetilde u_{i_{\ell+1}-i_\ell,N},
\end{equation}
with the empty-set value \(1\), and finite words are obtained from
\eqref{eq:finite-record-renewal-consistent-inclusions-main} by the same
finite inclusion--exclusion formula as
\eqref{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-word-main}.  If the reporting gate or
the denominator or interior-root tests fail, no finite-record complete-law
probabilities are reported.

Thus the complete-law finite-record object is a point on the full
renewal-consistency manifold
\[
\mathcal M_{\rm ren}
=\{(\rho_*(\sigma),\sigma):\sigma\in F\}.
\]
The raw empirical intensity \(\widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}\) is used to
construct the inverse coordinate and to test denominator stability, but it is
not inserted as the renewal intensity after the root coordinate has been
projected.  Consequently the reported finite word probabilities cannot mix a
noisy single-site estimate with a different projected renewal gap law.
At any separated interior population point, the projection is locally
eventually inactive with probability tending to one, so
\(\widetilde\sigma_N\to\Phi(r)\),
\(\widetilde\rho_N\to r_0\).  More precisely, for every fixed finite window
\(I\) there are a compact separated-root neighbourhood \(K\) of
\(\Phi(r)\) and a constant \(C_I(K)<\infty\) such that, on the retained
complete-law branch and with probability tending to one,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complete-law-reporting-fixed-window-bound-main}
\max_{S\subset I}
\left|\widetilde\PP_N\{\varXi\cap I=S\}
      -\PP\{\varXi\cap I=S\}\right|
\le C_I(K)\,\|\widetilde\sigma_N-\Phi(r)\|.
\end{equation}
If the finite-record CLT hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main} hold, the right-hand side is
\(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).  Thus each fixed-window reported word probability
converges in probability to its population value with an explicit
projection-coordinate error bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The construction first projects the symmetric root coordinate into \(F\), and
the interior-root retention test keeps only roots in \((0,1)\).  On that
retained event the quotient-root formulas give a single renewal gap law.  The
intensity \(\widetilde\rho_N\) is then defined from that same gap law by the
renewal identity \(1/\EE(G+1)\).  Therefore
\((\widetilde\rho_N,\widetilde\sigma_N)\) lies on
\(\mathcal M_{\rm ren}\), and the inclusion products are exactly the renewal
products for the projected finite-record renewal law.  The non-reporting
branches are the same retained-record and denominator branches already fixed
in \Cref{prop:finite-record-threshold-projection-rule-main}.

At a separated interior population point, the inverse denominators are bounded
away from zero and \(\Phi(r)\) lies in the relative interior of \(F\).  The
complete-cycle inclusion estimator is consistent by
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main}; hence
\(\Phi(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}})\to\Phi(r)\) in probability and
\(\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\) is eventually the identity with probability tending to
one.  The roots, gap probabilities for any fixed finite range, renewal masses
for any fixed finite range, and \(\rho_*(\sigma)\) are continuous on this
separated interior chart.  The quantitative fixed-window statement is exactly
\Cref{lem:projected-complete-law-fixed-window-error-main}, applied on a
compact separated-root neighbourhood of \(\Phi(r)\).  The displayed
fixed-window convergence follows from that bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Symmetric-coordinate inverse delta method]\label{cor:finite_inverse_delta_boundary_main}\label{cor:finite-separated-root-rate-chart-main}
Work under \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}, and let
\(\widehat r_N\) be the all-complete-cycle inclusion estimator from
\Cref{def:cycle-index-inclusion-estimator-main}.  Suppose
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat r_N-r)\Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,\Sigma_r)
\]
with a covariance estimator \(\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}\) satisfying
the rate conclusion of \Cref{thm:finite-record-covariance-main}.  Assume the
population point lies in a stability neighbourhood where \(r_0\) and
\(|r_1/r_0-r_0|\) are bounded away from zero.  With \(\Phi\),
\(\widetilde\sigma_N\), and \(\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}\) defined by
\Cref{prop:finite-record-threshold-projection-rule-main},
\[
\sqrt N(\Phi(\widehat r_N)-\Phi(r))
\Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{\sigma}),
\qquad
\Sigma_{\sigma}=D\Phi(r)\Sigma_rD\Phi(r)^\top .
\]
The same limit holds with \(\Phi(\widehat r_N)\) replaced by
\(\widetilde\sigma_N\) at separated interior points of \(F\), and the sandwich
\(\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}\) is consistent there.  For the constrained D-MAP
population, \(\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps)\).  If \(\Delta_\sigma=(p-s)^2>0\), the roots are
recovered as an unordered pair and the finite-record rule may report the
unordered numerical rate set.  The ordered labelled-rate chart is not part of
this proposition; it requires the separate side-data hypotheses in
\Cref{cor:side-data-labelled-rate-wald-main}.
If \(\Delta_\sigma=0\), then
\(\widehat\Delta_{\sigma,N}=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), so the reporting rule of
\Cref{prop:finite-record-threshold-projection-rule-main} with
\(N^{1/2}\delta_N^2\to\infty\) withholds separated labelled rates with
probability tending to one.  The common-rate estimand is then handled by the
projected exchange-diagonal statement below.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the stated inverse stability neighbourhood, \(\Phi\) is \(C^1\).  The delta
method gives the displayed Gaussian limit for \(\Phi(\widehat r_N)\).  The
sandwich covariance inherits the same operator-norm rate because
\(D\Phi(\widehat r_N)\) is bounded and converges in probability to \(D\Phi(r)\)
on the denominator-retained event.  The identity \(\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps)\) is
exactly the quotient inverse calculation in
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}.  At separated roots,
\(\Phi(r)\) lies in the relative interior of \(F\), so
\Cref{prop:finite-record-threshold-projection-rule-main} makes projection
asymptotically inactive.  Since
\(\widehat\Delta_{\sigma,N}\to\Delta_\sigma>0\) and \(\delta_N^2\to0\), the
discriminant threshold is eventually passed.  The empirical projected roots
converge to the two distinct population roots in \((0,1)\), so the endpoint
threshold is eventually passed, but the proof stops at the unordered root set
because no statistic of the visible record chooses the branch label.  At
\(\Delta_\sigma=0\), the projected-coordinate
limit in \Cref{thm:finite-record-exchange-diagonal-main}
implies \(\widehat\Delta_{\sigma,N}=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), so the stated
threshold condition makes the no-label decision occur with probability tending
to one.
\end{proof}

\gamma(z)=-\frac{\log z}{\Delta\tau}.

\mathcal C_{\sigma,N}^{\rm diag}

Equivalently, the confidence set for the invariant ordered labelled-rate pair

\{(\gamma,\gamma):\gamma\in\mathcal C_{\gamma,N}^{\rm diag}\}.

For the theorem-level conservative report, define

\mathcal C_{\gamma,N}^{\rm diag,\varepsilon}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-rate-coverage-transfer-main}
\{\gamma_*\in\mathcal C_{\gamma,N}^{\rm diag}\}
=
\{\sigma_{z_*}\in\mathcal C_{\sigma,N}^{\rm diag}\}.

\begin{lemma}[Local projection at the exchange diagonal]\label{lem:local-projection-exchange-diagonal-main}
Let
\[
F=\{(x+y,xy):0\le x,y\le1\},\qquad \sigma_z=(2z,z^2),\quad 0<z<1.
\]
There is \(\varepsilon>0\) such that Euclidean projection onto \(F\) is
single-valued on \(B_\varepsilon(\sigma_z)\).  In local coordinates
\(v=\sigma_z+h/\sqrt N\), feasibility is
\[
h_2\le zh_1+\frac{h_1^2}{4\sqrt N}.
\]
Hence the scaled feasible sets converge locally to the half-space
\[
T_{\sigma_z}F=\{h:h_2\le zh_1\}.
\]
Moreover, for every fixed \(h\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
N^{1/2}\bigl(\Pi_F(\sigma_z+h/\sqrt N)-\sigma_z\bigr)
\longrightarrow
\Pi_{T_{\sigma_z}F}h.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(\delta>0\) so that \(2z-\delta>0\), \(2z+\delta<2\), and the other
box boundaries of \(F\) are outside \(B_\delta(\sigma_z)\).  By
\Cref{lem:feasible-set-computable-projection-main}, if
\((\xi_1,\xi_2)\in F\cap B_\delta(\sigma_z)\), then
\(\xi_1\) lies in a small neighbourhood of \(2z\).  On this neighbourhood
\(\ell_F(\xi_1)<\xi_2\) after shrinking \(\delta\), because
\(\ell_F(2z)<z^2\) for \(0<z<1\).  Thus the lower envelope and the endpoint
constraints are inactive locally, and feasibility is exactly the exchange
discriminant inequality
\[
F\cap B_\delta(\sigma_z)
=\{(\xi_1,\xi_2)\in B_\delta(\sigma_z):\xi_2\le \xi_1^2/4\}.
\]
Let \(\Gamma_z=\{(t,t^2/4):|t-2z|<\delta\}\).  This \(C^2\) arc has a tubular
neighbourhood on which nearest-point projection onto \(\Gamma_z\) is
single-valued; equivalently, the arc has positive local reach
\cite{Shapiro1987MetricProjection}.  Shapiro's differentiability result also
identifies the directional derivative of the local projection at the boundary;
for the hypograph-side inequality used here that derivative is Euclidean
projection onto the tangent half-space \(T_{\sigma_z}F\).  Shrinking \(\delta\)
if necessary, the
closed set \(F\setminus B_\delta(\sigma_z)\) has positive distance from a
smaller ball \(B_\varepsilon(\sigma_z)\).  A point of
\(B_\varepsilon(\sigma_z)\) below the local parabola is already in \(F\), while
a point above the local parabola has, inside the tubular neighbourhood, a
unique nearest point on \(\Gamma_z\).  The positive separation from
\(F\setminus B_\delta(\sigma_z)\) makes that local nearest point also the
global nearest point in \(F\).  This gives
\(\varepsilon>0\) such that the Euclidean projection onto the full, globally
nonconvex set \(F\) is single-valued on \(B_\varepsilon(\sigma_z)\).

For \(v=\sigma_z+h/\sqrt N\), the local feasibility condition is
\[
z^2+\frac{h_2}{\sqrt N}
\le
\frac{(2z+h_1/\sqrt N)^2}{4},
\]
which is exactly
\[
h_2\le zh_1+\frac{h_1^2}{4\sqrt N}.
\]
On every bounded set of \(h\)'s the last term converges uniformly to \(0\),
so the scaled feasible sets converge locally to
\(T_{\sigma_z}F=\{h:h_2\le zh_1\}\).
For fixed \(h\), write
\[
K_N=\sqrt N\{F-\sigma_z\}\cap B_R(0)
\]
with \(R>\|h\|+1\).  The preceding feasibility calculation gives
Painleve--Kuratowski convergence of \(K_N\) to
\(T_{\sigma_z}F\cap B_R(0)\).  Because \(T_{\sigma_z}F\) is closed and
convex, the Euclidean projection of \(h\) onto it is unique.  Equivalently,
Shapiro's boundary projection theorem gives Hadamard directional
differentiability of \(v\mapsto\Pi_F(v)\) at \(\sigma_z\), tangentially to the
local chart, with derivative \(h\mapsto\Pi_{T_{\sigma_z}F}h\).  The
single-valued local projection onto \(F\) and the positive separation from the
remote part of \(F\) therefore imply convergence of the rescaled nearest
points:
\[
N^{1/2}\bigl(\Pi_F(\sigma_z+h/\sqrt N)-\sigma_z\bigr)
\to \Pi_{T_{\sigma_z}F}h .
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Projection and root splitting at the exchange diagonal]\label{lem:exchange-diagonal-projection-main}
If \(y_N=\sigma_z+Z_N/\sqrt N+o_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) and
\(Z_N\Rightarrow Z\), then
\[
\sqrt N\{\Pi_F(y_N)-\sigma_z\}\Rightarrow \Pi_{T_{\sigma_z}F}(Z).
\]
Let \(d_z=(1,z)^\top\) be the tangent direction to the exchange diagonal and
put
\[
V=\Pi_{T_{\sigma_z}F}(Z),\qquad
\Delta(v)=4(zv_1-v_2).
\]
If \(\PP\{V\notin\operatorname{span}(d_z)\}=1\), then for the unordered roots
\(\widehat z_{1,N},\widehat z_{2,N}\) of the projected polynomial
\[
t^2-\{\Pi_F(y_N)\}_1t+\{\Pi_F(y_N)\}_2=0
\]
one has the \(N^{-1/4}\) splitting law
\[
N^{1/4}|\widehat z_{1,N}-\widehat z_{2,N}|
\Rightarrow \sqrt{\Delta(V)} .
\]
More generally this convergence holds with the possibly degenerate right-hand
side \(\sqrt{\Delta(V)}\); on the event
\(V\in\operatorname{span}(d_z)\), the first-order discriminant vanishes and no
nondegenerate \(N^{-1/4}\) separation is asserted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:local-projection-exchange-diagonal-main} gives local uniqueness and
Painleve-Kuratowski convergence of the scaled feasible sets to the closed
convex half-space \(T_{\sigma_z}F\).  Continuity of projection under this local
positive-reach limit, equivalently the directional derivative of the local
metric projection \cite{Shapiro1987MetricProjection}, gives the displayed weak
convergence by the extended continuous mapping theorem for directionally
differentiable maps; see also \cite{FangSantos2019DirectionalDifferentiability}
for the corresponding inference framework.  Write
\[
\Pi_F(y_N)=\sigma_z+\frac{V_N}{\sqrt N}+o_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}),
\qquad V_N\Rightarrow V .
\]
For \(\sigma=\sigma_z+v/\sqrt N\), the polynomial discriminant is
\[
\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2
=\left(2z+\frac{v_1}{\sqrt N}\right)^2
 -4\left(z^2+\frac{v_2}{\sqrt N}\right)
=\frac{4(zv_1-v_2)}{\sqrt N}+\frac{v_1^2}{N}.
\]
The projection puts the limit in the tangent half-space
\(T_{\sigma_z}F=\{v:v_2\le zv_1\}\), hence \(\Delta(V)\ge0\).  Since the
distance between the two roots of a monic quadratic equals the square root of
its discriminant,
\[
N^{1/4}|\widehat z_{1,N}-\widehat z_{2,N}|
=\left(4(zV_{N,1}-V_{N,2})+o_{\PP}(1)\right)^{1/2}
\Rightarrow \sqrt{\Delta(V)}
\]
by the continuous mapping theorem.  The limit is nonzero exactly off the
tangent line \(v_2=zv_1\), which is
\(\operatorname{span}(d_z)\).  Thus the \(N^{-1/4}\) root separation is a
conditional nondegenerate statement, not a generic assertion made on tangent
directions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exchange-diagonal self-dual inverse]\label{cor:critical_self_dual_inverse}
At the exchange diagonal \(p=s\), the visible quotient inverse returns the
self-dual unordered pair \(\{p,p\}\), and root splitting is governed by
\Cref{lem:exchange-diagonal-projection-main}.  This exchange-diagonal
self-duality is not, by itself, the exact-dependence critical point: along
\(p=s=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\),
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\) only at
\(\Gamma\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau=1\), equivalently
\(p=s=e^{-1}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Hidden-label exchange non-identifiability]\label{prop:hidden_label_exchange_nonidentifiability}
No statistic of one visible D-MAP record can distinguish the two separated
ordered points \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\) and
\((\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)\) without external labelling information.
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main} records the resulting reporting
convention.  This exchange
non-identifiability does not remove the diagonal common-rate estimand in
\Cref{lem:diagonal-common-rate-main}, where the two entries are equal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two parameter points induce the same finite-dimensional visible law by
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}; hence every visible-record
statistic has the same distribution under them.  This is the exchange
specialization inside the present constrained D-MAP(2) family, consistent with the
broader MAP2 lesson that a hidden arrival representation need not be uniquely
identified from the stationary observable arrival law
\cite{RamirezCoboLilloWiper2010MAP2Nonidentifiability}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fully studentized lag-two covariance interval}\label{subsec:studentized-lag-two-main}

This subsection isolates the only finite-record interval in the manuscript
that is fully studentized.  The first two lemmas are generic finite-lag CLT
and batch-covariance tools, reused here for the scalar lag-two observable.
The remaining results verify the lag-two reward identity, prove the positivity
of the scalar variance, and state the observable
\(\widehat\sigma^2_{\gamma_2,N}\)-based interval, with no report when fewer
than two complete separated lag-two batches are available,
\eqref{eq:lag-two-studentized-interval-main}.  Other displayed diagnostics in
the paper should be read as population formulas, consistency statements, or
projected/sandwich covariance statements unless a separate scalar studentizer
is explicitly supplied.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-lag regenerative CLT]\label{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main}\label{def:cycle-block-main}\label{lem:cycle-one-dep-main}\label{lem:renewal-reward-boundary-main}\label{lem:renewal-local-clt-main}\label{lem:deterministic-prefix-main}\label{lem:lag2-boundary-main}\label{thm:cycle-block-main}
Let \((A_n)\) be a stationary renewal indicator process with i.i.d. gaps
satisfying \(\PP(G\ge k)\le Ce^{-\eta k}\).  Let
\(Z_n=\psi(A_n,\ldots,A_{n+h_{\rm cal}})\in\RR^{d_{\rm rew}}\) be a bounded
centered local observable, with fixed reward dimension \(d_{\rm rew}\) and
fixed calendar look-ahead \(h_{\rm cal}\).  Let
\[
m_{\rm dep}:=q_*(0,h_{\rm cal})=\max\{h_{\rm cal}-1,0\},
\]
where \(q_*\) is the explicit local-window range from
\Cref{lem:local-window-regenerative-decomposition-main}.  Define the Palm
cycle reward by the measurable map
\[
Y_j:=F_{0,h_{\rm cal}}(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+m_{\rm dep}}).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
Y_j=f(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+m_{\rm dep}})\in\RR^{d_{\rm rew}},\qquad
\|f\|\le C_f\left(1+\sum_{i=0}^{m_{\rm dep}}G_{j+i}\right).
\]
Put \(\mu=\EE(G+1)\) and
\[
\mathcal T_{m_{\rm dep}}=
\Var(Y_0)+
\sum_{h=1}^{m_{\rm dep}}\{\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_h)+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_h,Y_0)\}.
\]
Then the exact calendar-time empirical vector average satisfies
\[
\sqrt N\left(\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Z_n\right)
=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Z_n
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\left(0,\frac{\mathcal T_{m_{\rm dep}}}{\mu}\right).
\]
In the scalar case this covariance reduces to \(\tau_{m_{\rm dep}}^2/\mu\),
where
\[
\tau_{m_{\rm dep}}^2=\Var(Y_0)+2\sum_{h=1}^{m_{\rm dep}}\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_h).
\]
The same conclusion holds after the finite-start burn-in in
\Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}.  In the constrained D-MAP
applications below, \((A_n)\) is the retained visible renewal record from that
standing assumption.  No continuous-comparator or growing-window perturbation
statement is used in this lemma.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:local-window-regenerative-decomposition-main} supplies the displayed
Palm reward \(Y_j=F_{0,h_{\rm cal}}(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+m_{\rm dep}})\), its
centering, fixed \(m_{\rm dep}\)-dependence, and the
\(O_{\PP}(\log N)\) endpoint replacement for the local calendar sum.
\Cref{prop:generic-complete-cycle-regenerative-clt-main} then gives the
deterministic-prefix CLT and the renewal random-index replacement, with
calendar-time covariance \(\mathcal T_{m_{\rm dep}}/\mu\).  The finite-start
burn-in case follows by applying
\Cref{lem:finite-hidden-start-path-contraction-main} to the retained
post-burn-in block.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite-lag separated-batch covariance]\label{thm:finite-lag-separated-batch-main}\label{lem:batch-var-random-count-main}
Under the hypotheses and notation of
\Cref{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main}, form all-complete-cycle batches of
cycle rewards \(Y_j\) with deterministic length
\[
b_N=\lfloor N^\beta\rfloor,\qquad 0<\beta<1,
\]
and leave \(m_{\rm dep}\) complete cycle rewards between consecutive batches.
Let \(K_N=\max\{k:\tau_k<N\}\), \(d_N=b_N+m_{\rm dep}\), and use only batch
indices for which every local calendar window entering the batched rewards is
observed.  With
\[
I_{\ell,N}=\{(\ell-1)d_N,\ldots,(\ell-1)d_N+b_N-1\},
\qquad
B_{\ell,N}=\sum_{j\in I_{\ell,N}}Y_j,
\]
and \(M_N\) the number of all-complete-cycle batches selected before calendar
time \(N\), with \(M_N=0\) when no such batch exists.  Define
\(\bar B_N=M_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}B_{\ell,N}\) on
\(\{M_N\ge1\}\), and set the auxiliary completed statistic
\[
\widehat\Sigma_N^{\rm batch,aux}
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{K_N}{N}\,
\frac1{b_N(M_N-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}(B_{\ell,N}-\bar B_N)(B_{\ell,N}-\bar B_N)^\top,
&M_N\ge2,\\[1.1ex]
0_{d_{\rm rew}\times d_{\rm rew}},
&M_N<2 .
\end{cases}
\]
Then \(M_N\ge2\) with probability tending to one and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-lag-separated-batch-rate-main}
\left\|\widehat\Sigma_N^{\rm batch,aux}
-\frac{\mathcal T_{m_{\rm dep}}}{\mu}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=O_{\PP}\left(b_N^{-1}+\sqrt{\frac{b_N}{N}}\right).
\end{equation}
Thus the auxiliary completed statistic estimates the calendar-time covariance
\(\mathcal T_{m_{\rm dep}}/\mu\) at the displayed rate.  The operational
finite-record report uses the \(M_N\ge2\) branch only; on \(\{M_N<2\}\) it
reports insufficient complete cycles.

In the constrained D-MAP applications below, \((A_n)\) is the retained visible
renewal record from \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}.  No
continuous-comparator or growing-window perturbation statement is used in this
lemma.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:local-window-regenerative-decomposition-main} identifies the
rewards as an \(m_{\rm dep}\)-dependent fixed-window function of the i.i.d.
gaps, and the exponential gap tail gives the moment hypothesis of
\Cref{lem:calendar-stopped-batch-cov-main}.  That appendix lemma gives the
displayed separated-batch rate, including the calendar-stopped batch count,
endpoint/look-ahead deletion, and empirical-mean subtraction.  Since
\(b_N=\lfloor N^\beta\rfloor\) with \(0<\beta<1\), the retained batch count
satisfies \(M_N\ge2\) with probability tending to one; the low-cycle
exception is covered by \Cref{lem:fixed-low-cycle-event-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Observed lag-two windows in the all-complete-cycle batches]\label{lem:all-cycle-lag-two-windows-main}
For lag-two rewards, using all-complete-cycle batches and leaving one complete
cycle after the last batched cycle ensures every term \(A_nA_{n+2}\) used by
the statistic is observed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(n\in[\tau_j,\tau_{j+1}-1]\), then \(A_{n+2}\) is determined by the
click pattern up to time \(\tau_{j+1}+1\).  This time lies no later than the
end of cycle \(j+1\): if \(G_j\ge1\), then \(\tau_{j+1}+1\le\tau_{j+2}\);
if \(G_j=0\), then the cycle \(j\) interval contains only \(n=\tau_j\), and
\(A_{\tau_j+2}\) is determined by whether \(G_{j+1}=0\).  Thus every lag-two
term assigned to cycle \(j\) is determined by \(G_j\) and \(G_{j+1}\).  The
all-complete-cycle convention uses only batched indices for which cycle
\(j+1\) is complete and, at the terminal edge, leaves one complete cycle after
the last batched cycle.  Hence no term \(A_nA_{n+2}\) in a used batch reaches
outside the observed record.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lag-two reward identity, centering, and one-gap dependence]\label{lem:lag-two-reward-main}
For \(Z_n=(A_n-\rho)(A_{n+2}-\rho)-\gamma_2\), the Palm cycle reward has the
explicit form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-cycle-reward-main}
Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1}),
\end{equation}
where
\[
f(g,h)=
\mathbf1_{\{g=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{g=0,\ h=0\}}
-\rho\bigl(1+\mathbf1_{\{g\ge1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{h=0\}}\bigr)
+(\rho^2-\gamma_2)(g+1).
\]
In particular \(\EE Y_j=0\), and \((Y_j)\) is \(1\)-dependent in the cycle
index.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work in Palm coordinates with \(\tau_j=0\).  The cycle \(j\) contains the
times \(0,1,\ldots,G_j\), the next click is at \(G_j+1\), and the following
click is at \(G_j+G_{j+1}+2\).  Hence
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{G_j}A_n=1,
\]
while the shifted count in the same cycle is
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{G_j}A_{n+2}
=\mathbf1_{\{G_j\ge1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_{j+1}=0\}}.
\]
The lag-two product count is nonzero only when the click at the cycle start
is followed by another click exactly two sampled steps later:
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{G_j}A_nA_{n+2}
=\mathbf1_{\{G_j=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0,\ G_{j+1}=0\}}.
\]
Summing
\((A_n-\rho)(A_{n+2}-\rho)-\gamma_2\) over the cycle therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y_j
&=\sum_{n=0}^{G_j}A_nA_{n+2}
-\rho\sum_{n=0}^{G_j}A_n
-\rho\sum_{n=0}^{G_j}A_{n+2}
+(\rho^2-\gamma_2)(G_j+1)  \\
&=f(G_j,G_{j+1}),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is \eqref{eq:lag-two-cycle-reward-main}.

The centering is not an additional assumption.  The stationary renewal record
has \(\EE[(A_0-\rho)(A_2-\rho)-\gamma_2]=0\), and Palm inversion gives
\[
\EE Y_j
=\EE L\,
\EE\bigl[(A_0-\rho)(A_2-\rho)-\gamma_2\bigr]
=0.
\]
Finally, \(Y_j\) is a function only of the adjacent pair
\((G_j,G_{j+1})\).  If \(|j-k|>1\), the pairs
\((G_j,G_{j+1})\) and \((G_k,G_{k+1})\) are disjoint functions of i.i.d. gaps,
so \(Y_j\) and \(Y_k\) are independent.  Thus the reward sequence is
\(1\)-dependent.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Hoeffding row--column decomposition and coboundary zero criterion]\label{lem:row-column-coboundary-criterion-main}
Let \(G_0,G_1,G_2\) be i.i.d. with a full-support law \(g\) on
\(\mathbb N_0\).  Let \(f:\mathbb N_0^2\to\RR\) have
\(\EE f(G_0,G_1)^2<\infty\), and put
\[
M=\EE f(G_0,G_1),\qquad
R_i=\EE\{f(i,G_1)\},\qquad
C_i=\EE\{f(G_0,i)\}.
\]
Then, with \(X=f(G_0,G_1)\), \(X'=f(G_1,G_2)\), and
\[
E_{ij}=f(i,j)-R_i-C_j+M,
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-column-variance-decomposition-main}
\EE(X-M)^2+2\EE\{(X-M)(X'-M)\}
=
\EE E_{G_0,G_1}^2+\EE(R_{G_0}+C_{G_0}-2M)^2 .
\end{equation}
Consequently the zero-variance coboundary criterion is the following: the
left-hand side is zero if and only if there is a sequence
\((u_i)_{i\ge0}\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-column-zero-coboundary-main}
f(i,j)-M=u_i-u_j,\qquad i,j\ge0 .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The residual has zero conditional mean in each coordinate:
\[
\EE\{E_{G_0,G_1}\mid G_0\}=0,\qquad
\EE\{E_{G_0,G_1}\mid G_1\}=0 .
\]
The Hoeffding row--column decomposition
\[
X-M=(R_{G_0}-M)+(C_{G_1}-M)+E_{G_0,G_1}
\]
is therefore orthogonal in \(L^2\).  Hence
\[
\EE(X-M)^2
=\EE(R_{G_0}-M)^2+\EE(C_{G_1}-M)^2+\EE E_{G_0,G_1}^2 .
\]
For the adjacent copy \(X'\), independence gives the only nonzero cross-term
after conditioning on the shared gap:
\[
\EE\{(X-M)(X'-M)\}
=\EE\{\EE(X-M\mid G_1)\EE(X'-M\mid G_1)\}
=\EE\{(C_{G_1}-M)(R_{G_1}-M)\}.
\]
Adding the preceding two displays proves
\eqref{eq:row-column-variance-decomposition-main}.

Both terms on the right of
\eqref{eq:row-column-variance-decomposition-main} are nonnegative.  Since
\(g_i>0\) for every \(i\), equality to zero is equivalent to
\[
E_{ij}=0,\qquad R_i+C_i=2M,\qquad i,j\ge0 .
\]
The first relation gives \(f(i,j)-M=(R_i-M)+(C_j-M)\).  The second gives
\(C_j-M=-(R_j-M)\).  Thus
\eqref{eq:row-column-zero-coboundary-main} holds with \(u_i=R_i-M\).
Conversely, if \eqref{eq:row-column-zero-coboundary-main} holds, then
\(R_i-M=u_i-\EE u_{G_0}\), \(C_i-M=\EE u_{G_0}-u_i\), and
\(E_{ij}=0\), so both square terms vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Explicit lag-two variance and finite-pair nondegeneracy certificate]\label{lem:lag-two-variance-nondegenerate-main}
Let \(g_k=\PP(G=k)\) be the full-support gap law from
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}, let \(\mu=\EE(G+1)=1/\rho\), and let
\[
f(i,j)=
\mathbf1_{\{i=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{i=0,\ j=0\}}
-\rho\bigl(1+\mathbf1_{\{i\ge1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{j=0\}}\bigr)
+(\rho^2-\gamma_2)(i+1),
\qquad i,j\ge0 .
\]
Equivalently, for independent gaps \(G_0,G_1,G_2\) with law \(g\),
the centered Palm reward for
\((A_n-\rho)(A_{n+2}-\rho)-\gamma_2\) is \(f(G_j,G_{j+1})\).  The
lag-two calendar-time variance in
\Cref{cor:boundary-reset-cycle-clt-main} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-probabilistic-variance-main}
\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2
=\frac{
\EE f(G_0,G_1)^2
+2\EE\{f(G_0,G_1)f(G_1,G_2)\}}
{\EE(G+1)} .
\end{equation}
It is also the absolutely convergent double/triple sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-explicit-variance-main}
\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2
=\frac1{\mu}\left[
\sum_{i,j\ge0}f(i,j)^2g_ig_j
+2\sum_{i,j,k\ge0}f(i,j)f(j,k)g_ig_jg_k
\right].
\end{equation}
Thus numerical verification of the studentizing variance is finite and
monotone: truncate the two sums to \(0\le i,j,k\le K\), bound the omitted tail
with any slack envelope \(g_k\le C_{\bar r}\bar r^k\),
\(\bar r\in(\max\{p,s\},1)\), from
\Cref{lem:killed-leakage-gap-exponential-moment-main} and the linear growth of
\(f\), and increase \(K\) until the tail bound is below the required tolerance.  The
closed nondegeneracy certificate below shows that this numerical recipe is a
check on arithmetic rather than an additional assumption.
Equivalently, with
\[
R_i=\sum_{j\ge0}f(i,j)g_j,\qquad
C_i=\sum_{j\ge0}f(j,i)g_j,\qquad
M=\sum_{i,j\ge0}f(i,j)g_ig_j,
\]
one has the sum-of-squares form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-variance-squares-main}
\mu\,\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2
=\sum_{i\ge0}g_i(R_i+C_i-2M)^2
+\sum_{i,j\ge0}g_ig_j\bigl(f(i,j)-R_i-C_j+M\bigr)^2 .
\end{equation}
The zero-variance criterion from \eqref{eq:row-column-zero-coboundary-main}
reduces nondegeneracy to checking that such a coboundary cannot hold.  The
finite certificate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-finite-nondegeneracy-certificate-main}
f(0,0)-f(0,1)=1-\rho,\qquad
f(1,0)-f(1,1)=-\rho
\end{equation}
will be compared after the zero-variance criterion is identified in the proof.
It excludes the only possible zero-variance coboundary.  Hence
the expression in \eqref{eq:lag-two-probabilistic-variance-main} is strictly
positive.
In particular \(\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2>0\) for every
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{lem:lag-two-reward-main}, the centered Palm reward is
\(Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1})\), where the gaps are i.i.d. with law \(g\).  The
exponential tail of \(g\) and the linear growth of \(f(i,j)\) in \(i\) give
absolute convergence of all displayed second-moment sums.  Since
\(\EE Y_j=0\) and the sequence is \(1\)-dependent,
\[
\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2
=\frac{\Var(Y_0)+2\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)}{\mu}
=\frac{\EE(Y_0^2)+2\EE(Y_0Y_1)}{\mu},
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:lag-two-probabilistic-variance-main} and
\eqref{eq:lag-two-explicit-variance-main} after expanding
\[
\EE(Y_0^2)=\sum_{i,j\ge0}f(i,j)^2g_ig_j,\qquad
\EE(Y_0Y_1)=\sum_{i,j,k\ge0}f(i,j)f(j,k)g_ig_jg_k .
\]

It remains to make the nondegeneracy explicit rather than implicit.  The
unconditional mean \(M=\EE f(G_0,G_1)\) is zero, because \(\EE Y_j=0\), but
we keep it in the displayed row--column formula.  Applying
\Cref{lem:row-column-coboundary-criterion-main} gives
\eqref{eq:lag-two-variance-squares-main}.  The same lemma also states the
only way the variance can vanish: because the gap law has full support, there
would have to be a sequence \((u_i)\) such that
\[
f(i,j)=u_i-u_j,\qquad i,j\ge0 .
\]
This coboundary form has a row-independent difference between any two fixed
columns:
\[
f(i,0)-f(i,1)=u_1-u_0,\qquad i\ge0 .
\]
For the lag-two reward this fails on the finite \(2\times2\) corner.  Directly
from the displayed formula for \(f\),
\[
f(0,0)-f(0,1)=1-\rho,\qquad
f(1,0)-f(1,1)=-\rho .
\]
These two values are unequal for \(0<\rho<1\).  Hence the zero-variance
coboundary case is excluded, and \(\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2>0\) throughout the
positive parameter domain.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Trimmed shifted-click centering]\label{lem:trimmed-shifted-click-centering-main}
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be a stationary renewal indicator process with
i.i.d. gaps \(G_j\) satisfying \(\PP(G\ge k)\le Ce^{-\eta k}\), click epochs
\((\tau_j)\), and click density \(\rho=\EE A_0\).  Fix
\(\ell\ge0\).  Let \(J_N^{(\ell)}\) be any all-complete-cycle index set
obtained from the complete observed cycles in \([0,N)\) by deleting only a
fixed number of endpoint cycles and by requiring that every term
\(A_{n+\ell}\), \(n\in[\tau_j,\tau_{j+1})\), is observed.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trimmed-shifted-click-centering-main}
\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_N^{(\ell)}}
\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}(A_{n+\ell}-\rho)
=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}) .
\end{equation}
Consequently, for any fixed finite set of shifts
\(\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), fixed coefficients \(c_\ell\), and any
all-complete-cycle index set on which all the shifted terms are observed and
which differs from the complete-cycle set by only fixed endpoint trimming,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trimmed-shifted-linear-combination-main}
\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_N}
\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
\sum_{\ell\in\Lambda}c_\ell(A_{n+\ell}-\rho)
=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}) .
\end{equation}
In particular the conclusion holds for the lag-two all-complete-cycle set
\(J_{2,N}\) with \(\ell=0\) and \(\ell=2\), and for their sum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{lem:local-window-regenerative-decomposition-main} to the bounded
local observable \(Z_n=A_{n+\ell}-\rho\), with \(r=0\) and the fixed
look-ahead \(\ell\).  Since the process is stationary,
\(\EE Z_0=0\).  Palm inversion therefore centers the assigned cycle reward
\[
W_j^{(\ell)}
=\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}(A_{n+\ell}-\rho)
\]
by the explicit local-window map with \(r=0\):
\(\EE W_j^{(\ell)}=0\).  The reward is a function of the fixed future gap
block \(F_{0,\ell}(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+q_\ell})\), where
\(q_\ell=\max\{\ell-1,0\}\).  Moreover
\(|W_j^{(\ell)}|\le L_j+\ell+1\), so the exponential tail of \(G_j\) gives
\(\EE |W_j^{(\ell)}|^2<\infty\).  Hence the centered sequence
\((W_j^{(\ell)})\) is \(q_\ell\)-dependent with finite variance, and its
partial sums over any deterministic \(m=O(N)\) consecutive cycles are
\(O_{\PP}(\sqrt N)\).

The renewal law gives \(K_N=O_{\PP}(N)\) for the number of complete cycles
intersecting \([0,N)\).  Replacing the deterministic \(O(N)\) prefix by the
calendar-stopped prefix \(K_N\), or by a subset that differs from it by only a
fixed number of endpoint and look-ahead cycles, changes the preceding partial
sum by \(O_{\PP}(\log N)\) by the endpoint bound in
\Cref{lem:local-window-regenerative-decomposition-main}.  Thus the numerator
in \eqref{eq:trimmed-shifted-click-centering-main} is \(O_{\PP}(\sqrt N)\),
and division by \(N\) proves the claim.
The linear-combination form follows by applying the preceding bound to each
fixed shift after replacing \(J_N\) by the corresponding shift-specific
observed all-complete-cycle set; the finitely many discrepancies between these
sets are endpoint cycles, hence contribute only \(O_{\PP}(\log N)\) before
division by \(N\).  A fixed finite linear combination of
\(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) terms is again \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).
\end{proof}



\begin{corollary}[Lag-two studentized scalar interval]\label{cor:finite-record-lag-two-studentized-main}\label{cor:boundary-reset-cycle-clt-main}\label{thm:sampled-finite-record-main}
Work under \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}.  Define the observed
complete-cycle lag-two covariance estimator by
\[
\widehat\gamma_{2,N}
=\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_{2,N}}\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
(A_n-\widehat\rho_N)(A_{n+2}-\widehat\rho_N),
\qquad
\widehat\rho_N=\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n,
\]
where \(J_{2,N}\) contains exactly those all-complete-cycle indices for which
the lag-two windows lie in the observed record.

The scalar studentizer used for this statistic is the following observable
separated-batch estimator.  Let
\[
b_N=\lfloor N^\beta\rfloor,\qquad 0<\beta<1,\qquad d_N=b_N+1,
\]
and let
\[
M_{2,N}=\max\{m\ge0:(m-1)d_N+b_N-1\in J_{2,N}\},
\qquad
I^{\gamma}_{\ell,N}
=\{(\ell-1)d_N,\ldots,(\ell-1)d_N+b_N-1\},
\]
with \(M_{2,N}=0\) if the displayed set is empty.  For
\(j\in J_{2,N}\), define the plug-in lag-two cycle reward
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-plugin-cycle-reward-main}
\widehat Y^{\gamma}_{j,N}
=
\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
\left\{(A_n-\widehat\rho_N)(A_{n+2}-\widehat\rho_N)
-\widehat\gamma_{2,N}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Put
\[
\widehat C^{\gamma}_{\ell,N}
=\sum_{j\in I^{\gamma}_{\ell,N}}\widehat Y^{\gamma}_{j,N},
\qquad
\bar{\widehat C}^{\gamma}_N
=M_{2,N}^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{2,N}}\widehat C^{\gamma}_{\ell,N}
\quad\text{on }\{M_{2,N}\ge1\}.
\]
The scalar lag-two variance estimator is reported only when at least two
complete separated lag-two batches are available.  For the proof of consistency
on a total probability space, use the auxiliary completed statistic
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-scalar-studentizer-main}
\widehat\sigma^{2,\rm aux}_{\gamma_2,N}
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{K_N}{N}\,
\frac1{b_N(M_{2,N}-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{2,N}}
\left(\widehat C^{\gamma}_{\ell,N}
-\bar{\widehat C}^{\gamma}_N\right)^2,
&M_{2,N}\ge2,\\[1.1ex]
0,&M_{2,N}<2 .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
On \(\{M_{2,N}<2\}\) the lag-two interval is not reported; the zero branch in
\(\widehat\sigma^{2,\rm aux}_{\gamma_2,N}\) is used only in the convergence
proof.  On \(\{M_{2,N}\ge2\}\), put
\(\widehat\sigma^2_{\gamma_2,N}:=
\widehat\sigma^{2,\rm aux}_{\gamma_2,N}\).
Then
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat\gamma_{2,N}-\gamma_2)
\Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2),
\]
where \(\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2\) is the explicit positive variance
\eqref{eq:lag-two-explicit-variance-main}, and
\[
\widehat\sigma^{2,\rm aux}_{\gamma_2,N}
\xrightarrow{\PP}\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2 .
\]
Consequently, for \(0<\alpha<1\), with
\(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) denoting the standard-normal \(1-\alpha/2\) quantile and
\(\widehat\sigma_{\gamma_2,N}=(\widehat\sigma^2_{\gamma_2,N})^{1/2}\),
the fixed-setting two-sided interval reported on \(\{M_{2,N}\ge2\}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-studentized-interval-main}
\left[
\widehat\gamma_{2,N}
-z_{1-\alpha/2}\frac{\widehat\sigma_{\gamma_2,N}}{\sqrt N},
\quad
\widehat\gamma_{2,N}
+z_{1-\alpha/2}\frac{\widehat\sigma_{\gamma_2,N}}{\sqrt N}
\right]
\end{equation}
has limiting coverage \(1-\alpha\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:lag-two-reward-main} identifies the complete-cycle reward for the
true-centered statistic as a centered \(1\)-dependent reward, and
\Cref{lem:all-cycle-lag-two-windows-main} verifies that every lag-two term
used by \(J_{2,N}\) is observed.  Thus the \(m_{\rm dep}=1\) case of
\Cref{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main} applies with
\[
\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2
=\frac{\Var(Y_0)+2\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)}{\EE(G+1)}.
\]
\Cref{lem:lag-two-variance-nondegenerate-main} identifies this quantity with
the double/triple sum \eqref{eq:lag-two-explicit-variance-main} and proves
that it is strictly positive on the full positive parameter domain.
Replacing \(\rho\) by \(\widehat\rho_N\) in the displayed estimator changes
the true-centered average by
\[
-(\widehat\rho_N-\rho)\frac1N
\sum_{j\in J_{2,N}}\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
\bigl((A_n-\rho)+(A_{n+2}-\rho)\bigr)
+(\widehat\rho_N-\rho)^2\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_{2,N}}L_j .
\]
The stationarity and exponential renewal tail give the ordinary record mean
\(\widehat\rho_N-\rho=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).  The trimmed bracket is exactly the
linear-combination estimate
\eqref{eq:trimmed-shifted-linear-combination-main} with
\(\Lambda=\{0,2\}\) and \(c_0=c_2=1\):
\[
\frac1N
\sum_{j\in J_{2,N}}\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
\bigl((A_n-\rho)+(A_{n+2}-\rho)\bigr)
=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}).
\]
Also \(N^{-1}\sum_{j\in J_{2,N}}L_j=O_{\PP}(1)\).  Therefore the two plug-in
terms are respectively \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1})\) and \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1})\), hence
\(o_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).  The CLT therefore has the same variance as the
true-centered reward.

It remains only to identify the displayed scalar studentizer with the
batch-estimated variance of that same limiting reward.  Let
\[
Y^\gamma_j
=\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
\{(A_n-\rho)(A_{n+2}-\rho)-\gamma_2\},
\qquad
C^\gamma_{\ell,N}=\sum_{j\in I^\gamma_{\ell,N}}Y^\gamma_j .
\]
Write
\(\bar C^\gamma_N=M_{2,N}^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{2,N}}C^\gamma_{\ell,N}\)
on \(\{M_{2,N}\ge1\}\).
The \(m_{\rm dep}=1\) case of
\Cref{thm:finite-lag-separated-batch-main} applied to
\((Y^\gamma_j)\) gives consistency of
\[
\frac{K_N}{N}\,
\frac1{b_N(M_{2,N}-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{2,N}}
\left(C^\gamma_{\ell,N}-\bar C^\gamma_N\right)^2
\]
for \(\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2\), with the same auxiliary zero completion on
\(\{M_{2,N}<2\}\), an event whose probability tends to zero and on which the
lag-two interval is not reported.

For the plug-in batch rewards, write
\[
\Delta_{\rho,N}=\widehat\rho_N-\rho,\qquad
\Delta_{\gamma,N}=\widehat\gamma_{2,N}-\gamma_2,
\]
and
\[
W_j=\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
\bigl\{(A_n-\rho)+(A_{n+2}-\rho)\bigr\}.
\]
Then, for every used cycle,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-plugin-cycle-difference-main}
\widehat Y^\gamma_{j,N}-Y^\gamma_j
=-\Delta_{\rho,N}W_j
+(\Delta_{\rho,N}^2-\Delta_{\gamma,N})L_j .
\end{equation}
The CLT already proved above gives
\(\Delta_{\gamma,N}=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), and the ordinary renewal mean gives
\(\Delta_{\rho,N}=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).  Since \(|W_j|\le2L_j+4\),
\Cref{lem:uniform-calendar-block-length-main} gives the same uniform
\(O_{\PP}(b_NN^{-1/2})\) block perturbation as
\eqref{eq:separated-batch-plugin-block-main}.  The plug-in covariance
perturbation bound \eqref{eq:separated-batch-plugin-covariance-main}, applied
in this scalar \(m=1\) setting, is \(o_{\PP}(1)\) because \(b_N/N\to0\).
Thus
\(\widehat\sigma^{2,\rm aux}_{\gamma_2,N}\to_{\PP}\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2\), and
the reported \(\widehat\sigma^2_{\gamma_2,N}\) has the same limit on the
retained event.
Since the limiting variance is positive by
\Cref{lem:lag-two-variance-nondegenerate-main}, Slutsky's theorem gives the
studentized statistic and hence the interval
\eqref{eq:lag-two-studentized-interval-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Side-data labelled-rate delta method and Wald statistic]\label{cor:side-data-labelled-rate-wald-main}
Work under the fixed finite-record experiment and reporting gate
\(\mathcal E_N\).  Suppose the quotient population point is separated:
\[
\Delta_\sigma=(p-s)^2>0,\qquad p,s\in(0,1),
\]
and suppose the visible-record estimator satisfies the inclusion CLT and
covariance rate used in \Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main}:
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat r_N-r)\Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,\Sigma_r),\qquad
\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=o_{\PP}(t_N)
\]
for the retained-rank threshold \(t_N\).  Add one of the following side-data
hypotheses, neither of which is a consequence of the visible record:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.35em]
\item A deterministic oracle branch map \(\mathcal B\) is fixed on a
neighbourhood of the unordered root set and orders the two roots as the
attempt and recovery branches.
\item A calibration or side-data estimator \(\widehat{\mathcal B}_N\) is used
only on an event \(\mathcal C_N\) such that
\[
\PP(\mathcal C_N)\to1,\qquad
\mathcal C_N\subseteq
\{\widehat{\mathcal B}_N=\mathcal B
\text{ on the retained separated-root neighbourhood}\}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

Then, on \(\mathcal E_N\) and on the corresponding side-data retained chart,
the ordered labelled-rate estimator
\[
(\widehat\Gamma_N,\widehat\kappa_{\mathrm r,N})
=\left(-\frac{\log\widehat p_N}{\Delta\tau},
       -\frac{\log\widehat s_N}{\Delta\tau}\right)
\]
satisfies
\[
\sqrt N\{(\widehat\Gamma_N,\widehat\kappa_{\mathrm r,N})
-(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\}
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa}),
\qquad
\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa}
=J_{\Gamma,\kappa}(r)\Sigma_rJ_{\Gamma,\kappa}(r)^\top .
\]
With
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa,N}
=\widehat J_{\Gamma,\kappa,N}\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}
\widehat J_{\Gamma,\kappa,N}^{\top},
\]
where \(\widehat J_{\Gamma,\kappa,N}\) is
\eqref{eq:inclusion-to-rate-jacobian-main} evaluated on the retained labelled
chart, the covariance is consistent at the same operator-norm scale.  If
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa,N}
-\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa}\|_{\operatorname{op}}=o_{\PP}(t_N),
\]
then the thresholded retained-rank Wald statistic converges to
\(\chi_k^2\), \(k=\operatorname{rank}\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa}\).  No labelled-rate
conclusion is asserted on \(\Delta_\sigma=0\), without an oracle map, or for an
estimated branch map outside the high-probability agreement event.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On a separated-root neighbourhood the oracle branch map fixes one \(C^1\)
ordering of the two roots, and
\Cref{lem:separated-root-rate-delta-main} gives the root and rate Jacobians.
The complete-cycle reporting gate has probability tending to one at the stable
population point, and the separated discriminant/end-point thresholds are
eventually retained.  The multivariate delta method therefore gives the
displayed Gaussian limit.  If an estimated map is used, the event
\(\mathcal C_N\) makes the estimated-map estimator identical to the oracle-map
estimator with probability tending to one, so the same limit follows by the
standard high-probability replacement argument.  The covariance consistency is
the same continuous-mapping argument applied to the plug-in Jacobian on the
retained separated chart.  Finally, Weyl's eigenvalue inequality retains
exactly the positive limiting covariance eigenvalues above the threshold
\(t_N\), and Slutsky's theorem gives the retained-rank \(\chi_k^2\) limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[What one constrained D-MAP fixed-setting long record estimates]\label{thm:one-record-estimates-main}
Under \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}, a single fixed-setting
record estimates only quantities of the stationary visible law: the inclusion
coordinates, the projected
symmetric coordinates \((p+s,ps)\), the fixed-setting lag-two covariance, and
their regenerative covariances under the assumptions above.  It does not label
two separated rates and does not identify any object outside the visible
stationary record law.  The branch-map convention is
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}; at \(\sigma=(2z,z^2)\) the common
numerical rate is the invariant exception described in
\Cref{lem:diagonal-common-rate-main}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The positive estimands are exactly the CLT targets in
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main,thm:sampled-finite-record-main}.
The non-identifiability statement is
\Cref{prop:hidden_label_exchange_nonidentifiability}.  The record analysis
starts from \(T_0,T_1\) and therefore supplies no estimator for any externally
defined scalar map or its inverse images.
\end{proof}
