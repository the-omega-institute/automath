\section{Four-block record theorem chain before response-rate calibration}\label{sec:records}

This section contains the four main theorem blocks of the article.  Propositions,
lemmas, corollaries, and remarks are retained where they make the proof
self-contained, but they are not independent theorem families.  Blocks I and II
are the sampled model/gap law and exact \(1\)-dependence diagnostics; Block III
is the renewal principal-minor encoding and quotient inverse; Block
IV is the fixed-record lag-two covariance CLT.  Conditional response-calibration
statements are deferred to the appendix.

\subsection{Block I: measurement protocol, sampled model, and gap law}
We formulate the readout layer as a classical sampled interval instrument. The field-facing UDW detector core supplies a stationary excitation intensity, while the counter contributes one hidden readiness bit and a binary ancilla that records only whether at least one ready-state excitation attempt occurred before the next sampling time. Each proper-time interval therefore uses a fresh ancilla and then coarse-grains the within-bin history to a single symbol $A_n\in\{0,1\}$. The hidden state passed to the next interval is again just ready $R$ or refractory $D$, so the sampled record is a two-state hidden Markov model.
Throughout this section \(\Gamma\) is the stipulated counter parameter.  The
only bridge to a continuous recovery comparison is the fixed-window
model-validity bound stated up front in
\Cref{prop:finite-window-continuous-comparator-main}, with the
finite-window-only restriction made formal in
\Cref{cor:finite-window-only-comparator-main}.

\begin{proposition}[Sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset instrument and labelled kernels]\label{prop:instrument-kernels-main}
Fix a sampling interval of length $\Delta\tau$ and a rate parameter $\Gamma>0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item If the hidden state at the beginning of the interval is $R$, let $N$ be a Poisson process of rate $\Gamma$ on $[0,\Delta\tau]$. The output ancilla reports
\[
A_n=\mathbf 1_{\{N(\Delta\tau)\ge1\}},
\]
and the next hidden state is $D$ when $A_n=1$ and $R$ when $A_n=0$.
\item If the hidden state at the beginning of the interval is $D$, let $S\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\kappa_{\mathrm r})$ be the recovery time, independent of $N$. When $S>\Delta\tau$, the detector stays refractory throughout the interval and $(A_n,H_{n+1})=(0,D)$. When $S=s\le\Delta\tau$, the detector is ready only on $(s,\Delta\tau]$, and the ancilla reports
\[
A_n=\mathbf 1_{\{N(\Delta\tau)-N(s)\ge1\}}.
\]
If $A_n=1$, the thresholded binary readout registers a single click for that interval and resets the next hidden state to $D$; if $A_n=0$, the next hidden state is $R$.
\end{enumerate}
We order the hidden states as $(R,D)$ and use row vectors for hidden
distributions.  Thus the labelled-kernel entry $(T_i)_{XY}$ is
$\PP(A_n=i,H_{n+1}=Y\mid H_n=X)$, matrix products are read in
chronological order, and a word $u=u_0\cdots u_{m-1}$ has
$T_u=T_{u_0}\cdots T_{u_{m-1}}$ and stationary cylinder probability
$\pi T_u\mathbf 1$. This is the convention used for every word
probability below; no determinant, trace, or cancellation identity is used to
define these sampled labelled kernels.
Repeating this interval instrument with fresh independent copies of $(N,S)$ at each sample produces a time-homogeneous hidden Markov chain on $\{R,D\}$ with output-labelled kernels
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t0-t1-main}
T_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
 p & 0 \\
 b & c
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
 0 & 1-p \\
 0 & a
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p-c-main}
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},
\qquad
c=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a-b-main}
a=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}s}\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-s)}\bigr)\,ds,
\qquad
b=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}s}e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-s)}\,ds.
\end{equation}
When $\kappa_{\mathrm r}\neq\Gamma$, these become
\[
a=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)-\Gamma(1-c)}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad
b=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(p-c)}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From the ready state, the interval contains no attempt with probability
$p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}$ and at least one attempt with probability
$q=1-p$, so
\[
\PP_R(A_n=0,H_{n+1}=R)=p,
\qquad
\PP_R(A_n=1,H_{n+1}=D)=q.
\]
This gives the first row of each labelled kernel.

From the refractory state, there are three disjoint possibilities. No
recovery before the sample time has probability
\[
\PP_D(S>\Delta\tau)=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}=c,
\]
which contributes the $(D,D)$ entry of $T_0$. If recovery occurs at
$s\in[0,\Delta\tau]$ and no attempt follows before the end of the bin, then
$A_n=0$ and $H_{n+1}=R$; integrating the conditional probability
$e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-s)}$ against the recovery density gives
\[
\PP_D(A_n=0,H_{n+1}=R)
=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}s}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-s)}\,ds=b.
\]
If recovery occurs at $s\le\Delta\tau$ and at least one attempt occurs in
$(s,\Delta\tau]$, then the thresholded ancilla outputs one visible click and
the post-click reset gives $H_{n+1}=D$. The corresponding probability is
\[
\PP_D(A_n=1,H_{n+1}=D)
=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}s}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-s)}\bigr)\,ds=a.
\]
These are exactly the second rows of $T_0$ and $T_1$. Because each interval
uses fresh independent copies of the Poisson attempt process, recovery clock,
and binary ancilla, the same labelled transition rule applies at every sample;
hence the sampled hidden process is time-homogeneous. The one-click-per-bin
readout is therefore not an extra postulate after the fact: it is built into
the thresholded interval instrument itself.
\end{proof}

This is the \emph{sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset}
instrument used throughout the paper. The point of the name is literal: a bin
that begins in $D$ allows one recovery opportunity before the sampling
boundary, but if a click occurs anywhere inside that bin then the hidden state
passed to the next bin is set to $D$. A second recovery after that within-bin
click is therefore not modelled inside the same bin. The model is not the
continuous-time alternating-renewal detector in which recovery and reclicking
may occur repeatedly before the boundary; it is a boundary-sampled coarse
graining with one ancilla bit and one post-bin hidden state per interval.
The continuous intra-bin comparator, the positive post-click leakage entries
that the sampled model kills, and the finite-window total-variation bound are
stated before the theorem chain in \Cref{subsec:model-validity-boundary}.  Thus
the exact renewal, determinant, inverse, and CLT statements below are theorems
about the sampled kernels, not about the comparator itself.  By
\Cref{cor:finite-window-only-comparator-main}, no stationary law, spectral
density, Fano limit, finite-record CLT, or other infinite-record conclusion is
transferred to the comparator unless a separate growing-window perturbation
theorem is supplied; none is used in this section.


\subsection{Three detector criteria and the Parry benchmark}\label{subsec:thermality-notions}

\begin{definition}[Detector criteria]\label{def:thermality-notions-main}
Three distinct detector criteria are used in this paper.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item \emph{Detailed-balance thermality} means
\[
\frac{\Gamma_+}{\Gamma_-}=e^{-\beta\Omega},
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\frac{\Gamma_-}{\Gamma_+}=e^{\beta\Omega},
\]
the standard rate-ratio criterion \cite{Unruh1976,BirDav1982}.
\item \emph{Gibbs thermalization} means convergence of the reduced detector state to $e^{-\beta H_{\mathrm{det}}}/Z$ \cite{GarayMartinMartinezRamon2016,JuarezAubryMoustos2022,PercheTherm2022}.
\item \emph{Parry benchmark} means that the stationary click record realizes the Parry measure on the golden-mean shift, the unique measure of maximal entropy for the dead-time grammar $11\notin\operatorname{supp}\mu$ \cite{Parry1964,LindMarcus1995}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The third criterion is what this paper studies. Neither detailed balance nor Gibbs thermalization implies the Parry benchmark, and the converse implication is not asserted. The physical content is that the detector click grammar is as disordered as the dead-time constraint permits.

\subsection{Ideal one-step dead time: standard benchmark proposition}

The ideal dead-time model serves only as a benchmark normalization. A click
suppresses the next one-step interval, so once this ideal counter law is
stipulated the visible record reduces immediately to a one-parameter Markov
chain on the golden-mean shift. Writing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p-beta-main}
p_\beta=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}, \qquad q_\beta=1-p_\beta,
\end{equation}
the sampled output is the stationary Markov chain on the golden-mean shift with transition matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:golden-mean-main}
P_\beta=
\begin{pmatrix}
 p_\beta & 1-p_\beta \\
 1 & 0
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The reference law on that shift is the Parry measure, namely the unique measure
of maximal entropy compatible with the grammar
$11\notin\operatorname{supp}\mu$ \cite{Parry1964,LindMarcus1995}. The
calculation below is the standard Perron--Frobenius/Parry calculation for this
stipulated golden-mean chain; it is included to name the clock value used later
as a comparison point.
\begin{proposition}[Standard Parry benchmark for the stipulated ideal counter]\label{prop:ideal-main}
For the ideal one-step blanking mode of the sampled classical counter, the sampled output realizes the Parry benchmark if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parry-threshold-main}
p_\beta=\varphi^{-1}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi,
\end{equation}
where $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ is the golden ratio. This threshold defines the
benchmark clock value for the stipulated ideal counter.  The
Parry/Perron--Frobenius calculation itself is used here only to fix the later
comparison rate and not as a new identifiability or response theorem.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After a click the counter is refractory for the whole next sampling interval, so $P_\beta(1,1)=0$ and the word $11$ is forbidden. After a zero the counter is ready at the next interval, and the Poisson no-excitation probability during one interval is $p_\beta=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}$. Thus
\[
P_\beta(0,0)=p_\beta,\qquad P_\beta(0,1)=1-p_\beta,\qquad P_\beta(1,0)=1,
\]
which is~\eqref{eq:golden-mean-main}.

Let
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
be the adjacency matrix of the golden-mean shift, with states ordered as $(0,1)$. Its Perron eigenvalue is $\varphi$, and a positive right Perron vector is $r=(\varphi,1)^\top$. The Parry transition matrix is
\[
P^{\mathrm P}_{ij}=\frac{A_{ij}r_j}{\varphi r_i},
\]
so
\[
P^{\mathrm P}(0,0)=\varphi^{-1},\qquad
P^{\mathrm P}(0,1)=\varphi^{-2},\qquad
P^{\mathrm P}(1,0)=1 .
\]
The counter law and the Parry law have the same support and each has only the single free transition probability out of state $0$. Therefore they coincide if and only if $p_\beta=\varphi^{-1}$. Since $p_\beta=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}$, this is equivalent to $\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{prop:ideal-main} is included only as the benchmark comparison point.
Once the ideal one-step blanking chain has been stipulated, the
Perron--Frobenius/Parry computation is the standard calculation for the
golden-mean shift \cite{Parry1964,LindMarcus1995}; its counter-specific content
is the identification of the clock selector
$p_\beta=\varphi^{-1}$, equivalently
$\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$.
The main mathematical work begins in the sampled boundary-reset recovery
model, where the zero-run law has full support on $\mathbb N_0$ and hence
every finite binary word has positive probability; the dependence threshold is
controlled by the hidden eigenmode rather than by a grammar reduction.

\subsection{Blocks I--II: renewal structure, exact \texorpdfstring{$1$}{1}-dependence, and diagnostics}

A sampled effective recovery model allows the detector to recover during the
bin before the sampling boundary, while still passing only one boundary state
to the next bin. This is the sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset
model described above. Let $\kappa_{\mathrm r}>0$ be the
recovery rate. Throughout this section, $\Gamma$ denotes the classical attempt
rate of the sampled counter. Only when the closure
$\Gamma=\Gamma_+$ is invoked later do we identify it with the stationary UDW
excitation rate $\Gamma_+$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p-c-recall-main}
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}, \qquad c=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a-b-recall-main}
a=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}s}\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-s)}\bigr)\,ds,
\qquad
b=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}s}e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-s)}\,ds.
\end{equation}
When $\kappa_{\mathrm r}\neq\Gamma$, these integrals evaluate to
$a=[\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)-\Gamma(1-c)]/(\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma)$
and
$b=\kappa_{\mathrm r}(p-c)/(\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma)$.
For brevity, later references to the ``boundary-reset recovery'' record always
mean this sampled one-recovery-opportunity model, not a continuous-time
alternating renewal detector with repeated recovery and reclicking inside one
sampling bin.
\begin{remark}[Notation convention for the record and response layers]\label{rem:notation-layers-main}
In the record layer of this section, $\Gamma$ is the classical attempt rate,
$p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}$ and $c=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}$ are
one-bin survival factors local to the sampled recovery model, and the hidden
eigenvalue is written $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$. In the response layer of
\Cref{sec:kms-shells}, response rates, inverse temperatures, and detector
couplings are introduced locally. Thus $p$, $c$, and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$ never denote response-side quantities, while bare
$\Gamma$ appears only before the closure $\Gamma=\Gamma_+$ is imposed.
\end{remark}
Put $q=1-p$. The aggregate hidden transition matrix is
\[
P=
\begin{pmatrix}
 p & q\\
 b & 1-b
\end{pmatrix},
\]
where $a+b=1-c$. By \Cref{prop:instrument-kernels-main}, the output-labelled kernels are exactly the matrices in \eqref{eq:t0-t1-main}.
The nontrivial eigenvalue of the two-state stochastic matrix $P$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda-hid-main}
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}-\Gamma e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}}
{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\end{equation}
with the equal-rate value understood by continuity as
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}(1-\Gamma\Delta\tau).
\]
This scalar is the only hidden mode available to transmit dependence after the deterministic post-click reset. \Cref{thm:recovery-main} below is the main record-theoretic result of this section: it identifies the unique memory-transition rate, proves exact $1$-dependence there, and records the hazard and covariance consequences. The intermediate lemmas isolate the ingredients of that theorem: regeneration, stationarity, covariance transfer through the hidden mode, the critical-rate analysis, the obstruction to finite Markov order, and the labelled-kernel factorization at the critical point.

\begin{lemma}[Regeneration and zero-run law]\label{lem:renewal-gap-main}
Every observed click resets the next sampling interval to the hidden state $D$.
Consequently the visible click record is a discrete-time renewal process in the
standard sense of i.i.d. inter-click zero runs, with finite-dimensional
point-process laws determined by the Palm renewal masses
\cite{CoxIsham1980,DaleyVereJones2003,Asmussen2003,MarzenCrutchfield2015Renewal}.
If $G$ is the number of consecutive zeros after a click and before the next click, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-main}
\PP(G=k)=g_k=e_D^{\top}T_0^kT_1\mathbf 1,
\qquad k\ge0,
\end{equation}
where $e_D=(0,1)^\top$ and $\mathbf 1=(1,1)^\top$. If $\kappa_{\mathrm r}\neq\Gamma$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-closed-main}
g_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-c)c^k}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad k\ge0.
\end{equation}
In particular $g_k>0$ for every $k$, so the sampled boundary-reset recovery record has full support on zero-run lengths.
This lemma supplies the renewal and positive gap-law input used for the full-support part of \Cref{thm:recovery-main}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After a click, the counter is refractory at the start of the next sampling interval independently of the earlier history. The hidden two-state chain is Markov and the post-click hidden state is deterministic, so click times are regeneration times. A gap of length $k$ consists of $k$ output-$0$ transitions followed by one output-$1$ transition, which gives~\eqref{eq:gap-main}.

For $\kappa_{\mathrm r}\neq\Gamma$, the matrix $T_0$ is lower triangular with distinct diagonal entries $p$ and $c$:
\[
T_0^k=
\begin{pmatrix}
p^k & 0\\[3pt]
b\,\dfrac{p^k-c^k}{p-c} & c^k
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since $T_1\mathbf 1=(1-p,a)^\top$, substitution gives
\[
g_k=(1-p)b\frac{p^k-c^k}{p-c}+ac^k .
\]
Using the closed forms
\[
b=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(p-c)}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad
a=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)-\Gamma(1-c)}
        {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}
\]
reduces this expression to~\eqref{eq:gap-closed-main}. Thus the gap law is the
direct lower-triangular labelled-kernel calculation
\[
g_k=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-c)c^k}
        {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
with no determinant or cancellation identity entering the derivation. Full
support is also visible directly from the kernel path: after a click the
detector may remain refractory through $k$ zero-labelled intervals and then
recover and click during the next one, an event with positive probability for
all positive parameters.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Support of finite binary words]\label{cor:word-support-main}
In the sampled boundary-reset recovery model with positive parameters, the
stationary binary record assigns positive probability to every finite binary
word. This corollary is the finite-word support input used in
\Cref{thm:recovery-main}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
w=0^{\ell_0}1\,0^{\ell_1}1\cdots 1\,0^{\ell_r},
\qquad \ell_0,\ell_r\ge0,\ \ell_1,\dots,\ell_{r-1}\ge0,
\]
be any finite binary word, allowing the case $r=0$ of an all-zero word. Since
$\PP(A_{-1}=1)=\rho>0$ in the stationary law, there is positive probability of
starting from a click at time $-1$. Conditional on that click, the successive
post-click zero-run lengths are i.i.d.\ with common law $(g_k)_{k\ge0}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:renewal-gap-main}. Therefore
\[
\PP\bigl(A_{-1}=1,\ G_0=\ell_0,\ \dots,\ G_{r-1}=\ell_{r-1},\ G_r\ge \ell_r\bigr)
=
\rho\, g_{\ell_0}\cdots g_{\ell_{r-1}}\PP(G\ge \ell_r)>0,
\]
because $g_k>0$ for every $k$. On this event the block
$(A_0,\dots,A_{|w|-1})$ is exactly $w$. Hence every finite binary word belongs
to the support of the stationary record.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Stationary boundary-reset recovery law]\label{lem:stationary-main}
The aggregate hidden chain has stationary row distribution
\[
\pi_R=\frac{b}{q+b},
\qquad
\pi_D=\frac{q}{q+b}.
\]
The stationary click density is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho-main}
\rho=\PP(A_0=1)
=\pi_R q+\pi_D a
=\frac{q(1-c)}{q+b}.
\end{equation}
Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho-bounds-main}
a<\rho<q=1-p .
\end{equation}
The hidden transition matrix $P$ has strictly positive entries and is therefore primitive. Its nontrivial eigenvalue $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b$ satisfies $|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|<1$.
This lemma supplies the stationary density, the inequality \(a<\rho\), and the
hidden-mode contraction used in \Cref{thm:recovery-main}; it is a record-layer
calculation for the sampled kernels of \Cref{prop:instrument-kernels-main}.
In particular the click-probability column used in later covariance formulas is
\(u=T_1\mathbf 1=(q,a)^\top\), not a response-side object.  The displayed
identities are the only stationary-law inputs used later in the covariance
phase calculation: \(\rho=\pi u\), \(a-\rho<0\), and the contraction of the
single hidden eigenmode.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The stationary balance equation is $\pi_R q=\pi_D b$, and normalization gives the displayed $\pi$. Since a refractory detector must first recover before it can click, its one-step click probability is strictly smaller than the ready-state click probability: indeed
\[
a=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}s}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-s)}\bigr)\,ds
< (1-e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau})\int_0^{\Delta\tau}
\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}s}\,ds
<q .
\]
Using $a+b=1-c$ gives~\eqref{eq:rho-main}. Moreover,
\[
\rho-a=\frac{b(q-a)}{q+b}>0,
\qquad
q-\rho=\frac{q(q-a)}{q+b}>0,
\]
which proves~\eqref{eq:rho-bounds-main}.
Finally, $p,q,b$ are positive and $1-b=c+a>0$, so every entry of $P$ is positive. Thus $P$ is primitive. The Perron root is $1$, and the only other eigenvalue of a primitive two-state stochastic matrix has modulus strictly less than one; here that eigenvalue is $p-b=\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Covariance transfer through the hidden mode]\label{lem:covariance-transfer-main}
Let $(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$ be the stationary boundary-reset recovery record, let $\rho=\PP(A_0=1)$, and let $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$ be the nontrivial eigenvalue in~\eqref{eq:lambda-hid-main}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cov-transfer-main}
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1 .
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)
=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1 .
\]
In particular the sign of every covariance beyond lag one is controlled only by the hidden eigenvalue $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$.
This lemma supplies the covariance formula used in \Cref{thm:recovery-main};
no response-side calibration is used.  The formula is a conditional hidden-state
calculation after the post-click reset to \(D\); it is not obtained from the
finite-principal-minor determinant representation in Block~III.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $q=1-p$ and
\[
P=
\begin{pmatrix}
p&q\\
b&1-b
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
u=T_1\mathbf 1=\binom{q}{a}.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:stationary-main}, the stationary row distribution is
\[
\pi=\left(\frac{b}{q+b},\frac{q}{q+b}\right),
\qquad
\rho=\pi u.
\]
Since a click-labelled transition always ends in the hidden state $D$,
conditioning on $A_0=1$ fixes the hidden state at the start of the next
interval. Therefore
\[
\PP(A_0=1,A_n=1)=\rho\,e_D^\top P^{n-1}u,\qquad n\ge1 .
\]
Using the identities in Lemma~\ref{lem:stationary-main},
\[
q-\rho=\frac{q(q-a)}{q+b},
\qquad
a-\rho=-\frac{b(q-a)}{q+b},
\]
so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:u-centered-main}
u-\rho\mathbf 1
=
\frac{q-a}{q+b}
\binom{q}{-b}.
\end{equation}
This displayed identity is the only centering identity used below: the
click-probability column is \(u=(q,a)^\top\), so the \(D\)-component is
\(a-\rho=-b(q-a)/(q+b)\), not an independent response-kernel entry.
Set
\[
w=\binom{q}{-b}.
\]
Then $\pi w=0$, so $w$ spans the one-dimensional zero-$\pi$-mean subspace.
Moreover
\[
Pw=
\begin{pmatrix}
pq-qb\\
bq-b+ b^2
\end{pmatrix}
=(p-b)\binom{q}{-b}
=\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}} w.
\]
Thus the only hidden mode away from stationarity is the eigenmode $w$. In
row-vector convention this is the two-state spectral decomposition
\[
P^r z=\rho_z\mathbf 1+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^r(z-\rho_z\mathbf 1),
\qquad
\rho_z:=\pi z,
\]
for every column vector $z$ and every $r\ge0$; indeed
$z-\rho_z\mathbf 1$ lies in the one-dimensional zero-$\pi$-mean subspace
spanned by $w$. Applying this to $z=u$ and using
\eqref{eq:u-centered-main} gives
\[
P^{n-1}u=\rho\mathbf 1+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}(u-\rho\mathbf 1).
\]
Taking the $D$ component,
\[
e_D^\top P^{n-1}u
=\rho+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}(a-\rho),
\]
because the $D$ component of $u-\rho\mathbf 1$ is $a-\rho$. Hence
\[
\PP(A_0=1,A_n=1)
=\rho\bigl[\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}\bigr].
\]
Subtracting $\rho^2$ yields~\eqref{eq:cov-transfer-main}.
\end{proof}

The boundary-reset recovery argument has four logically separate parts, and we
keep them separate because they answer different possible objections. First,
the deterministic post-click reset gives a renewal process, so waiting-time
hazards are legitimate record observables. Second, covariance beyond lag one
can only be carried by the single hidden eigenmode in
Lemma~\ref{lem:covariance-transfer-main}. Third, the hazard is strictly
increasing, which rules out finite visible Markov order; this remains true even
at the memory threshold. Fourth, exact $1$-dependence at the threshold is not
inferred from vanishing covariances. It follows from a stronger
labelled-kernel factorization when the hidden transition matrix loses its
nontrivial eigenmode.

From the probability side, the relevant comparison theorem family here is exact
finite dependence rather than finite-state Markov order. Classical $m$-dependent limit
theory begins with \cite{HoeffdingRobbins1948,Diananda1955}, while
Holroyd--Liggett \cite{HolroydLiggett2016} give natural stationary finitely
dependent processes that lie outside block-factor and finite-state Markov-chain
templates. The present theorem is narrower and explicit: it identifies a
sampled dead-time hidden-Markov/renewal law whose exact-$1$-dependence point is
computable and is reached without flattening the renewal hazard.

\begin{lemma}[Critical-rate uniqueness and covariance phase]\label{lem:critical-rate-main}
For fixed $\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0$, the equation $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}(\Gamma)=0$, with $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$ defined by~\eqref{eq:lambda-hid-main}, has exactly one positive solution. This solution, denoted $\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$, is characterized by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-c-main}
\Delta\tau=
\frac{\log\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\log\Gamma_c}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma_c},
\end{equation}
with the quotient interpreted by continuity when $\Gamma_c=\kappa_{\mathrm r}$. Moreover
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}(\Gamma)>0\quad\text{for }0<\Gamma<\Gamma_c,
\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}(\Gamma)<0\quad\text{for }\Gamma>\Gamma_c .
\]
This lemma supplies the unique critical point and covariance-phase input used
in \Cref{thm:recovery-main}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $s=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau$ and $x=\Gamma/\kappa_{\mathrm r}$. Away from $x=1$,
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}(\Gamma)
=
\frac{e^{-s}-x e^{-sx}}{1-x}.
\]
Thus $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0$ is equivalent to
\[
H_s(x):=\log x-s(x-1)=0.
\]
The root $x=1$ is the removable root introduced by multiplying through the quotient; in the equal-rate limit $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=e^{-s}(1-s)$, so $x=1$ is critical only when $s=1$.

We now locate the non-removable root and the sign of $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$. Since
\[
e^{-s}-x e^{-sx}
=e^{-s}\bigl(1-e^{H_s(x)}\bigr),
\]
the numerator has the opposite sign from $H_s(x)$. Also
$H_s'(x)=x^{-1}-s$ and $H_s''(x)=-x^{-2}<0$.
If $0<s<1$, then $H_s$ is strictly increasing on $(0,1]$, so $H_s<0$ on $(0,1)$. On $(1,\infty)$ it first becomes positive and, by strict concavity and $H_s(x)\to-\infty$ as $x\to\infty$, crosses zero exactly once at a point $x_c>1$. Therefore $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}>0$ for $0<x<x_c$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}<0$ for $x>x_c$.

If $s>1$, then $H_s$ is strictly decreasing on $[1,\infty)$, so $H_s<0$ for $x>1$. On $(0,1)$ the maximum occurs at $x=1/s$, where $H_s(1/s)=s-1-\log s>0$, while $H_s(x)\to-\infty$ as $x\downarrow0$; strict concavity gives exactly one zero $x_c\in(0,1)$. Again the quotient formula shows $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}>0$ for $0<x<x_c$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}<0$ for $x>x_c$. Finally, when $s=1$, the inequality $\log x\le x-1$ gives $H_1(x)\le0$ with equality only at $x=1$, and the equal-rate limit gives the single critical point $x_c=1$. Rewriting $H_s(x_c)=0$ in the original variables gives~\eqref{eq:gamma-c-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Hazard obstruction to finite symbolic memory]\label{lem:hazard-obstruction-main}
Let a stationary binary renewal process have full support on zero-run lengths and hazard sequence
\[
h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k),\qquad k\ge0,
\]
after a click. If the process is an $r$-step Markov chain for some finite $r$, then $h_k$ is constant for all $k\ge r$. Consequently a strictly monotone hazard sequence excludes every finite Markov order.
This lemma is used in \Cref{thm:recovery-main} only after strict hazard
monotonicity has been proved for the concrete gap law.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Condition on a past ending with a click followed by $k$ zeros. For $k\ge r$, the last $r$ observed symbols are all zeros, independently of the value of $k$. In an $r$-step Markov chain the next-click probability is therefore the same for all such histories. Since the renewal law has full support on zero-run lengths, these conditioning events have positive probability. That common next-click probability is exactly $h_k$, so $h_k$ must be eventually constant. A strictly monotone hazard cannot have this property.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Kernel factorization at the memory threshold]\label{lem:kernel-factorization-main}
At $\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$, let $\pi$ be the stationary row distribution of the aggregate hidden transition matrix $P=T_0+T_1$. Then
\[
P=\mathbf 1\,\pi .
\]
Consequently, for any two finite words $u$ and $v$ supported on time blocks separated by at least one unobserved sample,
\[
\PP(A_{-m}^{0}=u,\ A_2^{2+\ell}=v)
=
\PP(A_{-m}^{0}=u)\,\PP(A_2^{2+\ell}=v).
\]
Thus the visible output process is exact $1$-dependent at the memory threshold.
This lemma supplies the positive-direction exact \(1\)-dependence input in
\Cref{thm:recovery-main}; it is a labelled-kernel factorization, not a
covariance-cancellation argument.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{def:record-benchmarks-main}, exact $1$-dependence compares two blocks with one unobserved sample between them; it does not condition on that intermediate sample. We use the row-vector convention of \Cref{prop:instrument-kernels-main}: if $T_u$ is the chronological product of labelled kernels for a word $u$, then its cylinder probability from stationarity is $\pi T_u\mathbf 1$.

The condition $\Gamma=\Gamma_c$ is equivalent to $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b=0$. Thus $b=p$ and, since $q=1-p$, the aggregate transition matrix becomes
\[
P=
\begin{pmatrix}
p&q\\
p&q
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its stationary distribution is therefore $\pi=(p,q)$, and $P=\mathbf 1\pi$. Equivalently, the two-state stochastic matrix has eigenvalues $1$ and $0$, so the hidden state is resampled from stationarity after one unobserved interval. For output-labelled kernels, summing over the unobserved sample at time $1$ inserts the aggregate matrix $P=T_0+T_1$. Hence the probability of a past word $u$ and a future word $v$ separated by that unobserved sample has the form
\[
\pi T_u\,P\,T_v\mathbf 1
=
\pi T_u\,\mathbf 1\,\pi T_v\mathbf 1
=
\PP(A_{-m}^{0}=u)\,\PP(A_2^{2+\ell}=v),
\]
where $T_u$ and $T_v$ denote the ordered products of the labelled kernels for the two words. If the separation contains more than one unobserved sample, the intervening factor is $P^j$ with $j\ge1$; since $P=\mathbf 1\pi$ implies $P^j=P$, the same factorization applies. Cylinder events generate the corresponding past and future sigma algebras, so this factorization gives exact $1$-dependence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact \texorpdfstring{$1$}{1}-dependence criterion]\label{lem:one-dep-criterion-main}
For positive boundary-reset recovery parameters, the stationary visible record is exact $1$-dependent if and only if $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0$. Equivalently,
\[
\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel-factorization-main} gives exact $1$-dependence by labelled-kernel factorization. Conversely suppose $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\neq0$. Lemma~\ref{lem:covariance-transfer-main} gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}} .
\]
Here $\rho>0$ and $a-\rho<0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:stationary-main}, so the displayed covariance is nonzero. Exact $1$-dependence would make $A_0$ and $A_2$ independent and hence uncorrelated, a contradiction. Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-rate-main} identifies the unique positive solution of $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0$ as $\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$.
\end{proof}

At the critical rate $\Gamma=\Gamma_c$, exact $1$-dependence and nonconstant
renewal hazard coexist in a concrete way. The factorization in
Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel-factorization-main} gives
\[
\PP(A_0=1,\ A_2=1)=\PP(A_0=1)\PP(A_2=1),
\]
so the click at time $0$ is independent of the click two samples later once
one unobserved sample separates the blocks. But the renewal hazard still keeps
track of how many zeros have elapsed since the last click:
\[
\PP(A_{k+1}=1\mid A_0=1,\ A_1=\cdots=A_k=0)=h_k,
\]
and \Cref{lem:survival-hazard-main} shows that $h_{k+1}>h_k$. Thus exact
$1$-dependence removes dependence across a skipped sample, whereas finite
Markov order would require the next-click probability after a long zero run to
stabilize, which the strictly increasing hazard forbids even at
$\Gamma=\Gamma_c$.

The converse direction in Lemma~\ref{lem:one-dep-criterion-main} uses covariance only as an obstruction: if the process were exact $1$-dependent, then $A_0$ and $A_2$ would be independent and hence uncorrelated. Independence at the critical rate itself is supplied by the rank-one labelled-kernel factorization, not by covariance cancellation alone.

\begin{lemma}[Survival tails and hazard monotonicity]\label{lem:survival-hazard-main}
For the boundary-reset recovery renewal law, put
\[
S_k=\PP(G\ge k),\qquad h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k)=\frac{g_k}{S_k}.
\]
If $\kappa_{\mathrm r}\neq\Gamma$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:survival-tail-main}
S_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma c^k}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad k\ge0,
\end{equation}
and
\[
h_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-c)c^k}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma c^k}.
\]
Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hazard-increment-main}
h_{k+1}-h_k
=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Gamma(p-c)^2p^kc^k}
{(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma c^k)
(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^{k+1}-\Gamma c^{k+1})}
>0 .
\end{equation}
If $\kappa_{\mathrm r}=\Gamma$, writing $\alpha=\Gamma\Delta\tau$ and
$p=e^{-\alpha}$ gives
\[
S_k=p^k(1+\alpha k),
\qquad
(h_{k+1}-h_k)=\frac{\alpha^2p}{(1+\alpha k)(1+\alpha(k+1))}>0 .
\]
Thus the click hazard is strictly increasing for all positive boundary-reset
recovery parameters.
Together with \Cref{lem:hazard-obstruction-main}, this lemma supplies the
strict-hazard and nonfinite-visible-Markov-order input in
\Cref{thm:recovery-main}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Assume first that $\kappa_{\mathrm r}\neq\Gamma$. Summing the closed gap law in
Lemma~\ref{lem:renewal-gap-main} over $j\ge k$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_k
&=\sum_{j\ge k}
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^j-\Gamma(1-c)c^j}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}  \\
&=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k/(1-p)
      -\Gamma(1-c)c^k/(1-c)}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}
=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma c^k}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $S_k$ is a survival probability, $S_k>0$ for every $k$. Hence
$\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma c^k$ is nonzero and has the sign of
$\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma$. Dividing $g_k$ by $S_k$ gives
\[
h_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-c)c^k}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma c^k}.
\]
For monotonicity it is cleaner to use the survival ratio:
\[
h_k=1-\frac{S_{k+1}}{S_k}.
\]
Therefore
\[
h_{k+1}-h_k
=\frac{S_{k+1}^2-S_kS_{k+2}}{S_kS_{k+1}}.
\]
Substitution of~\eqref{eq:survival-tail-main} gives the exact determinant
\[
S_{k+1}^2-S_kS_{k+2}
=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Gamma p^kc^k(p-c)^2}
     {(\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma)^2}.
\]
The numerator is strictly positive because $p\neq c$ when
$\kappa_{\mathrm r}\neq\Gamma$, and $S_kS_{k+1}>0$. Cancelling the common
factor $(\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma)^2$ yields
\eqref{eq:hazard-increment-main}, which is strictly positive.

At equal rates, take the limit $\kappa_{\mathrm r}\to\Gamma$ in
\eqref{eq:survival-tail-main}. With $\alpha=\Gamma\Delta\tau$ this gives
$S_k=p^k(1+\alpha k)$. Then
\[
h_k=1-\frac{p(1+\alpha(k+1))}{1+\alpha k},
\]
and direct subtraction gives the displayed positive increment.
\end{proof}

The following theorem is the accepted record-side result of the paper: a
classical sampled-record theorem for the hidden Markov law fixed by the
interval instrument above. The proof uses exactly the local renewal,
stationary-law, covariance-transfer, hazard, and finite-dependence inputs
proved in
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main,lem:stationary-main,lem:covariance-transfer-main,lem:critical-rate-main,lem:survival-hazard-main,lem:hazard-obstruction-main,lem:kernel-factorization-main,lem:one-dep-criterion-main}.
In particular, the covariance phase is fixed by
Lemma~\ref{lem:covariance-transfer-main}: a click resets the next hidden state
to $D$, so the lag $n$ covariance carries
$\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}$, including the lag-one value
$\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^0=1$.  Thus the theorem uses
\Cref{lem:stationary-main} only for \(\rho=\pi u\), \(a-\rho<0\), and the
hidden-mode eigenspace.  Concretely, with
\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}p&q\\ b&1-b\end{pmatrix},\qquad
u=T_1\mathbf 1=\binom{q}{a},\qquad
w=\binom{q}{-b},
\]
one has \(Pw=(p-b)w=\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}w\) and
\[
u-\rho\mathbf 1=\frac{q-a}{q+b}\binom{q}{-b}.
\]
These are the exact hidden-mode identities behind the covariance transfer.
\Cref{lem:covariance-transfer-main} is used only for the
conditional identity
\[
\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)
=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}.
\]

This convention is part of the theorem statement.  Before applying the theorem

\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}=\Gamma\).  In particular
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}
-\Gamma e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}}
{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}
\quad\text{means}\quad
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}(1-\Gamma\Delta\tau)
\quad\text{on }p=c .
\]
At the critical point \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), all occurrences of
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^m\) use ordinary integer powers after this continuous

case \(m=0\) even when \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\).  Thus the lag-one
covariance uses \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^0=1\), while critical covariances at

\begin{theorem}[Classical sampled-record theorem for the one-recovery-opportunity hidden Markov model]\label{thm:recovery-main}
For the sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset kernels
\eqref{eq:t0-t1-main}, with $\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0$, the
stationary visible process is a full-support renewal process. Its zero-run
hazard is strictly increasing, its covariance profile is
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1,
\]
and it is exact $1$-dependent if and only if
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}
-\Gamma e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}}
{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}
=0,
\]
equivalently $\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$. More
explicitly, this theorem is for the sampled one-recovery-opportunity
boundary-reset kernels of \Cref{prop:instrument-kernels-main}, not the
continuous intra-bin recovery kernels of
\Cref{prop:continuous-comparator-main}.  The fixed-window comparison in
\Cref{prop:continuous-comparator-main} and
\Cref{cor:finite-window-only-comparator-main} is not an input to any
stationary, spectral, or CLT conclusion in this theorem. With the record-layer notation from
Table~\ref{tab:notation-global}, the sampled boundary-reset record satisfies
the following, using the boxed equal-rate and critical conventions above.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.3em]
\item The zero-run length law is the renewal law of
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}, hence has full support on $\mathbb N_0$ and
\Cref{cor:word-support-main} gives positive probability to every finite binary
word.
\item If $\kappa_{\mathrm r}\neq\Gamma$, then the click hazard after $k$ zeros is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hazard-main}
h_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-c)c^k}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma c^k},
\qquad k\ge0.
\end{equation}
If $\kappa_{\mathrm r}=\Gamma$, write $\alpha=\Gamma\Delta\tau$ and
$p=e^{-\alpha}$. Then the equal-rate survival tail and click hazard are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equal-rate-survival-hazard-main}
\PP(G\ge k)=p^k(1+\alpha k),
\qquad
h_k=
\frac{(1-p)+\alpha(k(1-p)-p)}{1+\alpha k},
\qquad k\ge0.
\end{equation}
These equal-rate formulas are the continuous extensions of the corresponding
unequal-rate survival and hazard formulas.
In both cases $h_{k+1}>h_k$ for every $k$, so the visible process is not an
$r$-step Markov chain for any finite $r$.
\item Let $\rho=\PP(A_0=1)$ and let $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$ be the hidden
eigenvalue from \eqref{eq:lambda-hid-main}. Then
\[
\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)
=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1,
\]
because a click at time \(0\) resets the next hidden state to \(D\), and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cov-main}
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1.
\end{equation}
Thus every covariance beyond lag one is carried by the single hidden mode
$\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$.
\item There is a unique critical rate
$\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)>0$, characterized by
\eqref{eq:gamma-c-main}, and the sampled record is exact $1$-dependent if and
only if $\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$. At the threshold,
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=0\quad(n\ge2),
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_1)<0.
\]
For $0<\Gamma<\Gamma_c$, all lag-$n$ covariances are negative; for
$\Gamma>\Gamma_c$, the lag-one covariance remains negative and the signs for
$n\ge2$ alternate with the lag.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Item~(i) is Lemma~\ref{lem:renewal-gap-main} together with
\Cref{cor:word-support-main}. That is where the explicit gap law, its closed
form, and the full-support statement are proved.

For item~(ii), Lemma~\ref{lem:survival-hazard-main} gives
\eqref{eq:hazard-main} when $\kappa_{\mathrm r}\neq\Gamma$, gives the equal-rate
formula \eqref{eq:equal-rate-survival-hazard-main} by continuity, and proves
the strict increment $h_{k+1}-h_k>0$ in both regimes. Because the gap law has
full support, \Cref{lem:hazard-obstruction-main} then excludes every finite
visible Markov order.

For item~(iii), the covariance transfer is the hidden-mode calculation in
\Cref{lem:covariance-transfer-main}.  To make the normalization explicit, put
\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}p&q\\ b&1-b\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
u=T_1\mathbf 1=\binom q a,
\qquad
\pi=\left(\frac b{q+b},\frac q{q+b}\right).
\]
Then \(\rho=\pi u=q(1-c)/(q+b)\), and
\[
q-\rho=\frac{q(q-a)}{q+b},
\qquad
a-\rho=-\frac{b(q-a)}{q+b},
\qquad
u-\rho\mathbf 1
=\frac{q-a}{q+b}\binom q{-b}.
\]
Moreover
\[
P\binom q{-b}
=
\binom{pq-qb}{bq-b(1-b)}
=(p-b)\binom q{-b}
=\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\binom q{-b}.
\]
Thus
\[
P^{n-1}u
=
\rho\mathbf 1
+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}(u-\rho\mathbf 1).
\]
Since a click at time \(0\) resets the hidden state at the next interval to
\(D\), the \(D\)-component gives
\[
\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)
=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\PP(A_0=1,A_n=1)
=\rho\bigl[\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}\bigr],
\qquad n\ge1,
\]
so subtracting $\rho^2$ yields the displayed covariance formula. By
\Cref{lem:stationary-main},
\[
\rho>0,
\qquad
a-\rho=-\frac{b(q-a)}{q+b}<0,
\]
hence every lag-$n$ covariance with $n\ge2$ has the sign of
$-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}$.
This is a conditional hidden-state calculation after the post-click reset to
\(D\); it is not inferred from the finite-principal-minor bookkeeping of
Block~III.

For item~(iv), \Cref{lem:critical-rate-main} gives the unique positive zero
$\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$ of $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$ and the
sign change of $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$ across that point. Since
$\rho(a-\rho)<0$, the lag-one covariance is always negative, while the sign of
every covariance with $n\ge2$ is determined by the sign of
$\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}$. Therefore the lag-$n$ covariances vanish for
$n\ge2$ at $\Gamma=\Gamma_c$, remain negative for $0<\Gamma<\Gamma_c$, and
alternate in sign for $\Gamma>\Gamma_c$.

It remains to identify the exact-$1$-dependence point itself. If
$\Gamma=\Gamma_c$, then $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0$, so
\Cref{lem:kernel-factorization-main} gives the rank-one identity
$P=\mathbf 1\pi$ and hence exact $1$-dependence by labelled-kernel
factorization. Conversely, if the visible process were exact $1$-dependent,
then $A_0$ and $A_2$ would be independent and therefore uncorrelated. Applying
\eqref{eq:cov-main} with $n=2$ gives
\[
0=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}.
\]
Because $\rho(a-\rho)\neq0$, one must have $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0$, hence
$\Gamma=\Gamma_c$ by \Cref{lem:critical-rate-main}. Thus covariance is used
only for the converse obstruction; exact independence at the threshold comes
from the rank-one kernel factorization, not from covariance cancellation alone.

The proof dependencies are therefore exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{full\ support}
&\leftarrow
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}+\Cref{cor:word-support-main},\\
\mathrm{stationary\ density\ and\ covariance}
&\leftarrow
\Cref{lem:stationary-main}+\Cref{lem:covariance-transfer-main},\\
\mathrm{unique\ critical\ point\ and\ covariance\ phase}
&\leftarrow
\Cref{lem:critical-rate-main},\\
\mathrm{strict\ hazard\ monotonicity\ and\ nonfinite\ Markov\ order}
&\leftarrow
\Cref{lem:survival-hazard-main}+\Cref{lem:hazard-obstruction-main},\\
\mathrm{exact\ }1\mathrm{\text{-}dependence\ at\ the\ threshold}
&\leftarrow
\Cref{lem:kernel-factorization-main},\\
\mathrm{only\text{-}if\ criterion}
&\leftarrow
\Cref{lem:one-dep-criterion-main}.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

The memory-transition threshold is therefore not a softened version of the
ideal Parry point, and it is not a model-free trajectory-memory or weak-memory
inference procedure of the kind studied in recent \emph{J. Phys. A} work
\cite{VollmarEtAl2024Memory,MeyerBrandner2026WeakMemory}. The ideal point is a
standard maximum-entropy benchmark for a restricted grammar; the sampled
boundary-reset recovery point is the theorem-level result, namely an exact
dependence transition inside one explicit two-state sampled HMM/renewal class
whose zero-run law has full support and hence supports every finite binary
word. The essential feature is the hidden-eigenvalue criterion: the nontrivial
hidden mode $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$ vanishes precisely at the closed critical
rate $\Gamma_c$, labelled kernels factor through stationarity at that point,
and away from it the lag-two covariance obstructs exact $1$-dependence. The
same recovery mechanism is incompatible both with the Parry support constraint
and with a Bernoulli full-support benchmark, as made explicit below.  Any
record-side plots are only deterministic evaluations of these closed formulas
and are kept with the supplementary numerical bookkeeping rather than used as
evidence in the theorem chain.

\begin{remark}[No exact Parry or Bernoulli law at positive sampling interval]\label{cor:no-exact-main}
For every positive choice of
$\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau$, the sampled boundary-reset recovery
model does not realize the Parry benchmark of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ideal-main}. The same stationary record is also never
Bernoulli.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
The Parry law on the golden-mean shift assigns zero probability to the word
$11$. In the sampled boundary-reset recovery detector, however,
\[
\PP(A_{n+1}=1\mid A_n=1)=a>0,
\]
so $\PP(A_n=1,A_{n+1}=1)=\rho a>0$. The support already differs from the
Parry support. If one instead compares the full-support record with a Bernoulli
maximum-entropy law at fixed click density, the zero-run hazard would be
constant. \Cref{thm:recovery-main} gives a strictly increasing hazard for
every positive parameter choice in the sampled boundary-reset recovery model,
excluding that possibility as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Equal-rate limit and Lambert $W$ expression for $\Gamma_c$]\label{rem:edge-cases}
In the equal-rate limit $\kappa_{\mathrm r}\to\Gamma$, the closed gap formula~\eqref{eq:gap-closed-main} is $0/0$; l'H\^opital's rule in $\kappa_{\mathrm r}$ yields
\[
g_k\big|_{\kappa_{\mathrm r}=\Gamma}
=
p^k\bigl[(1-p)+\Gamma\Delta\tau\bigl(k(1-p)-p\bigr)\bigr],
\]
which is a continuous function of $(\Gamma,\Delta\tau)$. The equal-rate
survival and hazard formulas are stated in
\Cref{thm:recovery-main,eq:equal-rate-survival-hazard-main}. A direct
subtraction there gives
\[
(h_{k+1}-h_k)=\frac{\alpha^2p}{(1+\alpha k)(1+\alpha(k+1))}>0 .
\]
Thus the equal-rate case retains the same strictly increasing hazard and the same obstruction to finite symbolic memory. The formulas for $a$, $b$, and $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$ extend analogously to $\kappa_{\mathrm r}=\Gamma$.

The critical rate $\Gamma_c$ is defined implicitly by~\eqref{eq:gamma-c-main}. Setting $s=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau$ and $x=\Gamma_c/\kappa_{\mathrm r}$, that equation is equivalent, for $x\neq1$, to
\[
\log x=s(x-1),
\qquad\text{or equivalently}\qquad
x e^{-sx}=e^{-s}.
\]
Thus
\[
(-sx)e^{-sx}=-s e^{-s},
\]
and the nontrivial solution is
\[
\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)
=
-\frac{1}{\Delta\tau}\,
W_k\!\left(-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\,e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\right),
\]
where
\[
k=
\begin{cases}
-1, & 0<s<1,\\
0, & s>1.
\end{cases}
\]
The excluded branch gives $W(-s e^{-s})=-s$, hence the removable root $x=1$, which solves the multiplied equation but not the original quotient equation unless $s=1$. At $s=1$ both real branches meet at $W(-e^{-1})=-1$ and the unique solution is $\Gamma_c=\kappa_{\mathrm r}$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Soft approach to the ideal grammar}

The obstruction in Remark~\ref{cor:no-exact-main} is exact, but the sampled
boundary-reset recovery record still approaches the ideal grammar in a
controlled small-$\Delta\tau$ regime. This is the sense in which this sampled
boundary-reset law yields a \emph{soft} dead time.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-rate soft grammar and exact-clock singularity]\label{prop:soft-main}
In the sampled boundary-reset recovery model, for fixed positive $\Gamma$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm r}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:soft-main}
\PP(A_{n+1}=1\mid A_n=1)=a
=\frac12\,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Gamma(\Delta\tau)^2+O((\Delta\tau)^3)
\qquad (\Delta\tau\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
For a fixed window length $m\ge2$, let
\[
V_m=\{(a_0,\dots,a_{m-1})\in\{0,1\}^m:\exists\,k\le m-2,\ a_ka_{k+1}=11\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window-main}
\PP\!\bigl((A_0,\dots,A_{m-1})\in V_m\bigr)
\le
\frac12\,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Gamma(\Delta\tau)^2\,(m-1)
+O\!\bigl(m(\Delta\tau)^3\bigr).
\end{equation}
Thus, for every fixed $m$, the sampled boundary-reset recovery record assigns
vanishing probability to the inadmissible length-$m$ cylinders as
$\Delta\tau\downarrow0$ at fixed physical rates. This is only a fixed-$m$
cylinder estimate from a union bound; no process-level convergence to the
golden-mean law, in any topology or under any growing-window scaling, is
asserted here, and the proposition should not be read as a full-record Parry
limit theorem.

By contrast, along the exact-clock Parry scaling
$\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$ with fixed $\kappa_{\mathrm r}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:soft-exact-clock-main}
\PP(A_{n+1}=1\mid A_n=1)
=
\kappa_{\mathrm r}C_\varphi\,\Delta\tau+O((\Delta\tau)^2),
\qquad
C_\varphi:=1-\frac{1-\varphi^{-1}}{\log\varphi}>0.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\[
\PP\!\bigl((A_0,\dots,A_{m-1})\in V_m\bigr)
\le
\kappa_{\mathrm r}C_\varphi(m-1)\Delta\tau
+O\!\bigl(m(\Delta\tau)^2\bigr)
\]
for fixed $m$. Thus the quadratic fixed-rate suppression of adjacent clicks is
not uniform across the exact-clock scaling that produces the ideal Parry
benchmark in the one-step blanking model.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After a click, the next sampling interval starts in the refractory state. A consecutive click can occur only if recovery happens at some time $s\in[0,\Delta\tau]$ and an excitation attempt occurs in the remaining time $\Delta\tau-s$. Therefore
\[
\PP(A_{n+1}=1\mid A_n=1)=a
=
\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}s}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-s)}\bigr)\,ds.
\]
For fixed $\Gamma$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm r}$, uniformly in $0\le s\le\Delta\tau$,
\[
e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}s}=1+O(\Delta\tau),
\qquad
1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-s)}
=\Gamma(\Delta\tau-s)+O((\Delta\tau)^2).
\]
Substitution gives
\[
a=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Gamma\int_0^{\Delta\tau}(\Delta\tau-s)\,ds
+O((\Delta\tau)^3)
=\frac12\,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Gamma(\Delta\tau)^2+O((\Delta\tau)^3),
\]
which proves~\eqref{eq:soft-main}. For the window estimate, use the union bound and stationarity:
\[
\PP(V_m)
\le
\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\PP(A_k=1,A_{k+1}=1)
=
(m-1)\rho a
\le (m-1)a .
\]
Inserting the expansion for $a$ gives~\eqref{eq:window-main}.

For the exact-clock comparison, set $L=\log\varphi$ and impose
$\Gamma=L/\Delta\tau$. The same integral formula for $a$ gives, after the
change of variables $s=u\Delta\tau$,
\[
a
=
\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau
\int_0^1 e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau u}
\bigl(1-e^{-L(1-u)}\bigr)\,du .
\]
Since $\kappa_{\mathrm r}$ is fixed,
$e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau u}=1+O(\Delta\tau)$ uniformly on
$0\le u\le1$, and therefore
\[
a=
\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau
\int_0^1 \bigl(1-e^{-L(1-u)}\bigr)\,du
+O((\Delta\tau)^2).
\]
The integral equals
\[
1-\int_0^1 e^{-L(1-u)}\,du
=
1-\frac{1-e^{-L}}{L}
=
1-\frac{1-\varphi^{-1}}{\log\varphi}
=C_\varphi.
\]
It is positive because the integrand is nonnegative and not identically zero.
This proves~\eqref{eq:soft-exact-clock-main}. The exact-clock window estimate
again follows from the same stationary union bound
$\PP(V_m)\le(m-1)a$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:soft-main} explains why the ideal grammar is a singular
but physically meaningful finite-window comparison. It is specifically a
fixed-rate short-bin estimate: $\Gamma$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm r}$ are held fixed
while $\Delta\tau\downarrow0$, and the window length $m$ is fixed before the
limit is taken. It should not be confused with the exact-clock scaling
$\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$, where the excitation rate grows like
$\Delta\tau^{-1}$ and, for fixed $\kappa_{\mathrm r}$, the adjacent-click
probability is only $O(\Delta\tau)$. Nor is it a statement that the full
stationary process converges to the Parry measure or to any other
golden-mean-shift law. At fixed finite window length and fixed physical rates,
inadmissible words become quadratically rare. Yet the Parry benchmark is still
excluded at any positive sampling interval: the word $11$ has positive
probability, and the hazard remains strictly increasing, so the zero-run length
is genuinely remembered.

\subsection{Spectral density and classical sub-Poisson count noise}

The boundary-reset recovery renewal law has a second operational consequence
directly relevant to finite counting data. This is a classical discrete-time
count-statistics statement for the already specified sampled renewal record.
For this binary record we use the
discrete normalized conditional intensity
\[
g^{(2)}(n)
:=
\frac{\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)}{\rho}
=
\frac{u_n}{\rho}
=
1+\frac{a-\rho}{\rho}\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1,
\]
as the record-level analogue of a normalized second-order click statistic.
Thus \(g^{(2)}(1)=a/\rho<1\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:stationary-main}; this is
exactly the adjacent-click suppression already contained in the renewal law.
The longer-scale question is whether this local suppression is accompanied by
sub-Poisson coarse counts.  The long-window Fano factor will be
\[
F=\frac{f(0)}{\rho}
=1-\rho+\frac{2(a-\rho)}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}<1.
\]
Define the spectral density
\[
f(\theta)=\rho(1-\rho)+2\sum_{n\ge1}\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)\cos(n\theta),
\qquad \theta\in[-\pi,\pi].
\]
Since the covariance sequence is explicit, so are the low-frequency noise properties.

This count-noise conclusion is not automatic.  After the present classical
sampled boundary-reset record has been fixed, the one-mode covariance tail
gives a closed Herglotz spectral density and an exact asymptotic Fano factor.
Both are infinite-record consequences of the sampled stationary renewal law
alone, not consequences of the fixed-window continuous-comparator estimate in
\Cref{prop:finite-window-continuous-comparator-main}.
The sampled boundary-reset recovery law suppresses immediate reclicks, but it
could in principle still generate longer-range positive correlations and
enhanced low-frequency noise.  The explicit covariance formula shows that the
opposite happens: the reset mechanism suppresses low-frequency fluctuations
throughout the parameter range.  Applied photon-counting dead-time noise models
are comparison points \cite{KirchhoffEtAl2025Deadtime}; no correction estimator
is constructed here.  Waiting-time and Zeno results for continuously monitored
quantum systems are likewise distinct measurement-level results
\cite{WallsEtAl2024StochasticTrajectoriesZeno}.

\begin{proposition}[Spectral density and classical sub-Poisson count noise for the sampled renewal record]\label{prop:spectrum-main}
For the stationary boundary-reset recovery record,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrum-main}
f(\theta)=
\rho(1-\rho)
+
2\rho(a-\rho)\,
\frac{\cos\theta-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}{1-2\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\cos\theta+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^2},
\end{equation}
and $f$ is strictly increasing on $[0,\pi]$. If
\[
N_N=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n,
\]
and \(G\) denotes one zero-run gap from Lemma~\ref{lem:renewal-gap-main}, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fano-main}
0
<
F:=
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Var(N_N)}{\EE N_N}
=
\frac{f(0)}{\rho}
=
\frac{\Var(G+1)}{\EE(G+1)^2}
=
1-\rho+\frac{2(a-\rho)}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}
<1.
\end{equation}
In particular \(f(0)>0\), and the boundary-reset recovery record satisfies
\(0<F<1\) throughout the parameter range. Together with
\(g^{(2)}(1)=a/\rho<1\), this is the
precise sense in which the sampled renewal record has classical
discrete-time antibunching/sub-Poisson count noise.
The strict lower bound is the renewal-count identity
\(F=\Var(G+1)/\EE(G+1)^2\) plus the full support of the gap law.
This proposition is only the covariance-tail consequence of
\Cref{thm:recovery-main}; it introduces no separate renewal spectral theorem,
dead-time-correction estimator, detector-response statement, or response
level-set statement.  It also does not assert the same spectral density or Fano
limit for the continuous intra-bin comparator; by
\Cref{cor:finite-window-only-comparator-main}, such a transfer would require a
separate perturbation theorem, including control of the growing record length.
Classical renewal-noise examples such as fractal renewal processes
\cite{LowenTeich1993FractalRenewal} are useful comparators: here the proved
exponential covariance tail gives strict low-frequency suppression rather than
\(1/f\)-type behavior.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The spectral-density convention here is the Herglotz covariance convention for
the stationary discrete-time binary process: a summable covariance sequence
$(\gamma_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$ has density
$f(\theta)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\gamma_n e^{in\theta}$ with
$\gamma_n=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)$. No renewal spectral formula is imported
at this step; the required covariance sequence is the one already proved in the
main recovery theorem.
By \Cref{thm:recovery-main},
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},\qquad n\ge1.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:stationary-main}, $|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|<1$. Therefore the
covariance sequence is absolutely summable, uniformly enough to justify the
following termwise summation of the Fourier series. Hence
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}e^{in\theta}
=\frac{e^{i\theta}}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}} e^{i\theta}},
\]
and taking real parts gives
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}\cos(n\theta)
=
\frac{\cos\theta-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}{1-2\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\cos\theta+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^2},
\]
which proves~\eqref{eq:spectrum-main}. To prove monotonicity, set $x=\cos\theta$ and
\[
R_{\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}(x)=\frac{x-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}{1-2\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}} x+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^2}.
\]
Then
\[
R_{\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}'(x)=
\frac{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^2}{(1-2\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}} x+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^2)^2}>0.
\]
Since $a-\rho<0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:stationary-main}, $f$ is strictly decreasing as a function of $x$. Because $\cos\theta$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$, $f(\theta)$ is strictly increasing on that interval.

For the counting variance, stationarity and the exact finite sum identity give
\[
\Var(N_N)=N\rho(1-\rho)+2\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}(N-n)\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n).
\]
Divide by $\EE N_N=N\rho$ and let $N\to\infty$. Since
$\sum_{n\ge1}n|\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)|<\infty$, dominated convergence for
the weights $(1-n/N)\mathbf 1_{n<N}$ yields
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Var(N_N)}{\EE N_N}
=
1-\rho+
2\sum_{n\ge1}(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}
=
1-\rho+\frac{2(a-\rho)}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}.
\]
Evaluating~\eqref{eq:spectrum-main} at $\theta=0$ shows that this limit is
$f(0)/\rho$.

It remains to record why the lower bound is strict, not merely
nonnegative. Let \(G\) be one inter-click zero-run length from the stationary
renewal law of Lemma~\ref{lem:renewal-gap-main}, and write \(L=G+1\) for the
interarrival time between consecutive clicks.  Then \(\mu=\EE L=1/\rho\) and
\(\sigma_L^2=\Var L<\infty\).  For the ordinary delayed renewal process with
i.i.d. complete interarrival times \(L_1,L_2,\ldots\), put
\(S_k=L_1+\cdots+L_k\) and
\[
M_N=\max\{k:S_k\le N\}.
\]
The renewal CLT gives
\[
\frac{S_k-k\mu}{\sqrt{k}}\Rightarrow N(0,\sigma_L^2),
\]
and the standard inversion of this CLT at time \(N\) gives
\[
\frac{M_N-N/\mu}{\sqrt{N}}
\Rightarrow
N\!\left(0,\frac{\sigma_L^2}{\mu^3}\right).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Var M_N}{N}
=
\frac{\sigma_L^2}{\mu^3}.
\]
The stationary renewal count \(N_N=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n\) differs from \(M_N\)
only by the initial size-biased residual cycle and the terminal incomplete
cycle.  These boundary contributions have uniformly bounded second moments
because the present gap law has an exponential tail, so they do not change the
variance per unit time.  Hence the stationary renewal-count variance identity
is
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Var N_N}{N}
=
\frac{\Var L}{(\EE L)^3}.
\]
Dividing by the stationary mean density
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\EE N_N}{N}=\rho=\frac1{\EE L}
\]
gives the Fano identity
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Var(N_N)}{\EE N_N}
=
\frac{\Var L}{(\EE L)^2}
=
\frac{\Var(G+1)}{\EE(G+1)^2}.
\]
Here \(0<\EE L<\infty\), because the gap law is a finite exponential-tail law,
and \(\Var L>0\), because Lemma~\ref{lem:renewal-gap-main} gives full support
on \(\mathbb N_0\), so the interarrival law is not concentrated at one value.
Thus \(F>0\). Since \(\rho>0\), this also gives \(f(0)=\rho F>0\). The
denominator in the covariance expression is positive and \(a-\rho<0\), so
\[
F=1-\rho+\frac{2(a-\rho)}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}<1-\rho<1.
\]
Combining the renewal-count identity with the covariance calculation proves
\(0<F<1\).
\end{proof}

At the population level, the memory-transition crossing has several equivalent
record-side signatures besides the sign of one covariance.  As the fixed
record-side parameter \(\Gamma\) is varied inside the already specified
boundary-reset model, the lag profile changes phase at exact \(1\)-dependence,
while the whole renewal class keeps \(g^{(2)}(1)<1\), strictly increasing
spectral density on \([0,\pi]\), and a sub-Poisson Fano limit.  These are
closed-form properties of the sampled renewal law.  The finite-record theorem
below estimates only the fixed-setting value of \(\gamma_2\) and its variance;
it is not a scan theorem.  The deterministic plots of \(h_k\), the covariance
profile, \(f(\theta)\), and \(F=f(0)/\rho\) are supplementary formula checks
only; they are not simulations, fits, or evidence for the theorem statements.


\subsection{Block III: renewal principal-minor encoding of inclusions}\label{subsec:boundary-reset-finite-inclusion-record}

The covariance and Fano formulas above are second-cumulant consequences of the
renewal law.  The determinant calculation in this block is a renewal
principal-minor encoding: a finite algebraic representative for
stationary-renewal inclusion products whose probabilities have already been
constructed.
For a stationary renewal click set \(\varXi\) with intensity \(\rho\) and Palm
renewal masses \(u_n=\PP(n\in\varXi\mid0\in\varXi)\), the inclusion probability
of ordered sites is
\[
\PP(\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\subset\varXi)
=\rho\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}.
\]
The sampled boundary-reset model enters this block only by supplying the
explicit scalar sequence
\(u_n=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}\).  Everything below is a
finite algebraic encoding of that renewal product
\cite{CoxIsham1980,DaleyVereJones2003,Asmussen2003}.

\begin{proposition}[Finite LU principal-minor identity]\label{prop:renewal-principal-minor-lu-main}\label{lem:renewal-principal-minor-main}
Let \(\rho>0\) and let \(u_n\) be any scalar sequence indexed by \(n\ge1\).
Define \(K_u=K_u^{(\rho)}\) by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renewal-principal-minor-kernel-main}
K_u(i,j)=
\begin{cases}
\rho, & j\le i,\\[0.4ex]
\rho-u_{j-i}, & j>i .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
For \(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\), set
\(K_B=[K_u(i_r,i_s)]_{r,s=1}^m\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renewal-principal-minor-product-main}
\det K_B=\rho\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Not a DPP theorem]\label{rem:finite-determinant-language-main}\label{lem:principal_minor_gauge_nonuniqueness_main}
\Cref{prop:renewal-principal-minor-lu-main} is only a finite LU identity and,
when \(u_n\) is a Palm renewal mass sequence, an algebraic representative for
inclusion products of a renewal law that has already been constructed.  Its
admissibility is inherited from those renewal probabilities and finite
inclusion--exclusion, not proved from separate kernel conditions.  On any
finite \(I\), diagonal conjugation \(K_I\mapsto D_IK_ID_I^{-1}\) leaves all
principal minors unchanged, so the representative is noncanonical.  No
Hermitian positive-contraction kernel, canonical operator kernel, negative
association, hyperuniformity, DPP sampling theorem, nonsymmetric-DPP
well-definedness theorem, or Fredholm theorem is claimed.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{prop:renewal-principal-minor-lu-main}]
This is one LU calculation.  Put
\[
K_B=\rho L+U,\qquad
L_{rs}=\mathbf 1_{\{s\le r\}},\qquad
U_{rs}=(\rho-u_{i_s-i_r})\mathbf 1_{\{s>r\}}.
\]
Since \(L^{-1}\) has diagonal \(1\) and subdiagonal \(-1\),
\[
K_B=L(\rho I+L^{-1}U),
\]
and \(\rho I+L^{-1}U\) is upper triangular with diagonal entries
\[
\rho,\quad u_{i_2-i_1},\quad \ldots,\quad u_{i_m-i_{m-1}}.
\]
As \(\det L=1\),
\[
\det K_B=\rho\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}.
\]
This proves the determinant identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Renewal inclusion and finite word law]\label{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}
Let \(\varXi\) be a stationary renewal process on \(\mathbb Z\) with intensity
\(\rho>0\) and Palm renewal masses
\[
u_n=\PP(n\in\varXi\mid0\in\varXi),\qquad n\ge1 .
\]
For finite nonempty \(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renewal-inclusion-product-main}
\PP(B\subset\varXi)=\rho\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}
=\det K_B,
\end{equation}
where \(K_B=[K_u(i_r,i_s)]_{r,s=1}^m\) is the finite matrix from
\Cref{prop:renewal-principal-minor-lu-main}.  For \(B=\varnothing\), the empty
determinant is \(1\).  For every finite \(I\subset\mathbb Z\), exact
configuration probabilities are recovered by finite inclusion--exclusion:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-exact-configuration-probabilities-main}
P_I(\varXi\cap I=S)
=\sum_{C\subset I\setminus S}(-1)^{|C|}\det K_{S\cup C},
\qquad S\subset I .
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For \(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\), stationarity gives
\(\PP(i_1\in\varXi)=\rho\).  After conditioning on \(i_r\in\varXi\), the future
regenerates, so the probability of a click at the next specified time
\(i_{r+1}\), with any number of unspecified intervening clicks allowed, is
\(u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}\).  Successive conditioning gives the renewal inclusion
product, and \Cref{prop:renewal-principal-minor-lu-main} identifies that product
with \(\det K_B\).

The exact configuration formula is the finite inclusion--exclusion identity
\[
\mathbf 1_{\{\varXi\cap I=S\}}
=
\sum_{C\subset I\setminus S}(-1)^{|C|}
\mathbf 1_{\{S\cup C\subset\varXi\}}.
\]
Substituting the already proved inclusion determinants gives
\eqref{eq:finite-exact-configuration-probabilities-main}.  These probabilities
are the probabilities of the stationary renewal process, so their
nonnegativity and consistency come from the renewal law itself.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Boundary-reset specialization and finite word law.}
\phantomsection\label{cor:boundary_reset_toeplitz_renewal_specialization}\label{cor:finite_window_principal_minor_word_law}\label{prop:finite_inclusion_tail_phase}
For the stationary sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset model, put
\[
\varXi=\{n\in\mathbb Z:A_n=1\},\qquad
d=\rho-a,\qquad \ell=\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}},
\]
where \(a=\PP(A_1=1\mid A_0=1)\), the equal-rate value of
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\) is understood by continuous extension as in
\eqref{eq:lambda-hid-main}, and \(\ell^0=1\), including when \(\ell=0\).
Then \(d>0\) and, for \(n\ge1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-inclusion-u-n-main}
u_n=\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)
=\rho+(a-\rho)\ell^{n-1}
=\rho-d\ell^{n-1}.
\end{equation}
Substitution in
\Cref{prop:renewal-principal-minor-lu-main,cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}
gives the Toeplitz finite-principal-minor representative
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-reset-toeplitz-kernel-main}
K_\Gamma(i,j)=
\begin{cases}
\rho, & j\le i,\\[0.4ex]
d\ell^{j-i-1}, & j>i ,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and therefore, for \(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-reset-inclusion-product-main}
\PP(B\subset\varXi)
=\det\bigl[K_\Gamma(i_r,i_s)\bigr]_{r,s=1}^m
=\rho\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}
\Bigl[\rho+(a-\rho)\ell^{i_{r+1}-i_r-1}\Bigr].
\end{equation}
The strict-upper entries have the single transfer tail
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-inclusion-upper-transfer-tail-main}
K_\Gamma(i,j)=d\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{j-i-1}\qquad (j>i).
\end{equation}
For every finite \(I\subset\mathbb Z\), if
\(N_I=|\varXi\cap I|\) and \(K_{\Gamma,I}\) is the finite restriction of
\(K_\Gamma\) to \(I\times I\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-window-count-generating-main}
\EE[t^{N_I}]
=\det\bigl(I_I+(t-1)K_{\Gamma,I}\bigr).
\end{equation}
For disjoint finite sets \(S,T\subset\mathbb Z\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-word-inclusion-exclusion-main}
\PP(A_i=1\ \forall i\in S,\ A_j=0\ \forall j\in T)
=\sum_{C\subset T}(-1)^{|C|}\det K_{\Gamma,S\cup C},
\end{equation}
where the determinant is the principal determinant indexed by \(S\cup C\),
ordered increasingly, and the empty determinant is \(1\).

Indeed, Theorem~\ref{thm:recovery-main} and Lemma~\ref{lem:renewal-gap-main} give a
stationary renewal click set.  The inequality \(d>0\) is \(a<\rho\) from
Lemma~\ref{lem:stationary-main}.  Conditioning on \(A_0=1\) fixes the hidden
state at the start of the next interval to be refractory, and the one-mode
calculation from Lemma~\ref{lem:covariance-transfer-main} gives
\[
u_n=e_D^\top P^{n-1}\binom{q}{a}
=\rho+\ell^{n-1}(a-\rho).
\]
Every specified click resets the next interval to the same refractory hidden
state, so the renewal product in
\Cref{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}
applies.  Substituting this \(u_n\) into \(K_u\) gives \(K_\Gamma\), its
transfer tail, and the product formula
\eqref{eq:boundary-reset-inclusion-product-main}.

The count identity is the finite product expansion
\[
t^{N_I}=\prod_{i\in I}\bigl(1+(t-1)\mathbf 1_{\{i\in\varXi\}}\bigr)
\]
together with the principal-minor expansion of
\(\det(I_I+(t-1)K_{\Gamma,I})\).  The word law is the same finite
inclusion--exclusion calculation as in
\Cref{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}, with \(I=S\cup T\).

\paragraph{Equivalence to the renewal product.}
\Cref{prop:renewal-principal-minor-lu-main} is used only through the finite LU
identity
\[
\det[K_\Gamma(i,j)]_{i,j\in B}
=\rho\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}.
\]
Consequently every finite-window count or word formula above can be derived
directly from the renewal product followed by finite inclusion--exclusion.  The
determinant notation is a compact bookkeeping device for those algebraic
expansions.

\paragraph{Critical finite-inclusion collapse and memory package.}
\phantomsection\label{lem:critical-cluster-factorization-main}\label{cor:critical_finite_inclusion_memory_phase}\label{cor:critical_principal_minor_nonmarkov_fullsupport}
Fix \(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\) and put
\(\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\).  Then
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\).  With
\(\rho_c=\rho(\Gamma_c)\), \(a_c=a(\Gamma_c)\), and \(d_c=\rho_c-a_c\), the
Toeplitz representative above
collapses to upper range one:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-toeplitz-range-one-main}
K_c(i,j)=
\begin{cases}
\rho_c, & j\le i,\\[0.4ex]
d_c, & j=i+1,\\[0.4ex]
0, & j\ge i+2 .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The same critical law is also encoded, at the level of finite principal minors,
by the Hessenberg representative
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-hessenberg-kernel-main}
K_c^H(i,j)=
\begin{cases}
-1, & j=i-1,\\[0.4ex]
\rho_c(-d_c)^{j-i}, & j\ge i,\\[0.4ex]
0, & j\le i-2 .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
If \(B\subset\mathbb Z\) is finite and its nonempty case decomposes into
maximal contiguous clusters
\[
B=C_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_J,\qquad
C_\alpha=\{s_\alpha,s_\alpha+1,\ldots,s_\alpha+m_\alpha-1\},
\]
then, with rows and columns ordered increasingly and with the empty product
convention for \(B=\varnothing\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-cluster-factorization-main}
\det [K_c(i,j)]_{i,j\in B}
=
\det [K_c^H(i,j)]_{i,j\in B}
=
\prod_{\alpha=1}^J\rho_c a_c^{m_\alpha-1}
=
\rho_c^J a_c^{|B|-J}.
\end{equation}
At \(\Gamma_c\), the sampled boundary-reset record is therefore a full-support
renewal process, exact \(1\)-dependent, represented by the two explicit
finite-principal-minor matrices \(K_c\) and \(K_c^H\), strictly non-Markov of
every finite visible order by its increasing zero-run hazard, and neither
Bernoulli nor the Parry golden-mean law.

Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-rate-main} identifies \(\Gamma_c\) as the unique positive
zero of \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\), so \eqref{eq:critical-toeplitz-range-one-main}
is \eqref{eq:boundary-reset-toeplitz-kernel-main} with \(\ell=0\).  Then
\eqref{eq:finite-inclusion-u-n-main} gives \(u_1=a_c\) and \(u_n=\rho_c\) for
\(n\ge2\).  The renewal product in
\Cref{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main} immediately gives the cluster
product:
inside a contiguous cluster each adjacent specified gap contributes \(a_c\),
and each gap between distinct clusters contributes \(\rho_c\).

It remains only to check that the two displayed critical matrices have that
same finite product as their principal determinant.  For one contiguous cluster
\(C=\{s,\ldots,s+m-1\}\), the \(K_c\) block has \(\rho_c\) on and below the
diagonal, \(d_c\) on the first superdiagonal, and zero above it.  The column
operations \(C_r\leftarrow C_r-C_{r+1}\), \(r=1,\ldots,m-1\), make the block
upper triangular with diagonal entries \(a_c,\ldots,a_c,\rho_c\).  The
\(K_c^H\) block has diagonal entries \(\rho_c\), subdiagonal entries \(-1\),
and strict upper entries \(\rho_c(-d_c)^{j-i}\).  Processing rows downward by
adding \(d_c\) times the next unaltered row cancels the strict upper entries in
rows \(1,\ldots,m-1\) and changes their diagonal entries to
\(\rho_c-d_c=a_c\), leaving the last diagonal entry \(\rho_c\).  Both cluster
blocks therefore have determinant \(\rho_c a_c^{m-1}\).

For arbitrary \(B\), the \(K_c\) matrix is block lower triangular across the
ordered clusters because strict-upper entries vanish at gaps at least two, and
the \(K_c^H\) matrix is block upper triangular because entries below the
subdiagonal vanish across such gaps.  Multiplying the cluster determinants gives
\eqref{eq:critical-cluster-factorization-main}.  Full support is
Corollary~\ref{cor:word-support-main}; exact \(1\)-dependence is
Theorem~\ref{thm:recovery-main}; non-Markovness follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:survival-hazard-main} and Lemma~\ref{lem:hazard-obstruction-main};
and Remark~\ref{cor:no-exact-main} excludes the Bernoulli and Parry laws.


\subsection{Block III continued: exact quotient inverse and rate-duality of the sampled record}\label{subsec:exact-inverse-rate-duality}

The finite inclusion formulas above are not only forward formulas.  At fixed
sampling interval they also give an exact inverse for the visible family, up to
the intrinsic exchange of the two hidden survival factors.

This is an identifiability statement in the classical sense of functions of
finite Markov chains.  The relevant lineage starts with the
Blackwell--Koopmans and Gilbert identifiability problem for observed functions
of finite Markov chains, and continues through Dharmadhikari's realization
criteria and Heller's finite-process characterization
\cite{BlackwellKoopmans1957,Gilbert1959,Dharmadhikari1963Functions,Dharmadhikari1963Sufficient,Heller1965}.
Recent model-free memory and weak-memory work addresses broader inference or
memory-reduction questions \cite{VollmarEtAl2024Memory,MeyerBrandner2026WeakMemory}.
The theorem below is a fixed-family quotient inverse: the hidden state space,
sampling rule, and two-parameter labelled kernel family \eqref{eq:t0-t1-main}
are fixed in advance, and the visible process recovers only the unordered
survival pair.  Its increment is the explicit three-inclusion quotient inverse
through the symmetric coordinates \(p+c,pc\), together with the sharp
\(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) label non-identifiability for this
fixed sampled family.

\begin{theorem}[Visible-law quotient identifiability]\label{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}
Fix \(\Delta\tau>0\), and write \(\mu_{p,c}\) for the stationary visible law
of the sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset record with survival
factors
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},
\qquad
c=e^{-\kappa\Delta\tau}.
\]
The map
\[
(p,c)\in(0,1)^2\longmapsto \mu_{p,c}
\]
factors through the quotient \((p,c)\sim(c,p)\) and is injective on that
quotient.  Equivalently, in labelled rate notation,
\[
\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa}=\mu_{\Gamma',\kappa'}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa\Delta\tau}\}
=
\{e^{-\Gamma'\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa'\Delta\tau}\}.
\]
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:visible-law-exchange-main}
\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa}=\mu_{\kappa,\Gamma}.
\end{equation}
Thus a visible sampled record identifies only the unordered survival set
\[
\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa\Delta\tau}\},
\]
not the ordered pair \((\Gamma,\kappa)\). Equivalently, the labelled rate map
is exactly two-to-one off the diagonal \(\Gamma=\kappa\), and becomes
one-to-one after quotienting by the exchange \(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa\).
The quotient inverse is explicit: from the visible law define
\[
\rho=\PP(A_0=1),
\qquad
a=\PP(A_1=1\mid A_0=1),
\qquad
u_2=\PP(A_2=1\mid A_0=1),
\]
and
\[
\lambda=\frac{u_2-\rho}{a-\rho}.
\]
Set
\[
s_1=p+c=1-a+\lambda,
\qquad
s_2=pc=\rho(1-\lambda)-a+\lambda.
\]
Then \(\{p,c\}\) is the root set of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-quadratic-main}
z^2-s_1z+s_2=0,
\end{equation}
and the unordered rate pair is
\[
\left\{-\Delta\tau^{-1}\log p,
-\Delta\tau^{-1}\log c\right\}.
\]
This is visible-law quotient identifiability for this fixed
finite-Markov-function family.  The inverse is restricted to the feasible
symmetric-coordinate image
\begin{equation}\label{eq:population-feasible-symmetric-coordinate-region-main}
\mathcal F
=
\left\{
(s_1,s_2):
0<s_2<1,\quad
0<s_1<2,\quad
\Delta=s_1^2-4s_2\ge0,\quad
0<\frac{s_1\pm\sqrt{\Delta}}2<1
\right\},
\end{equation}
where both signs are required to satisfy the displayed root inequality.
Equivalently,
\(\mathcal F=\{(z_1+z_2,z_1z_2):z_1,z_2\in(0,1)\}\), including the exchange
diagonal.  Finite-record root or rate charts later are used only after this
feasibility check and, for labelled root coordinates, a prespecified
separated-root event.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All quantities in the first line of the inverse are finite-dimensional visible
probabilities.  By \Cref{lem:stationary-main}, \(a<\rho\), so the denominator
in the definition of \(\lambda\) is nonzero.  The renewal inclusion product,
or equivalently its finite LU representative from
\Cref{prop:renewal-principal-minor-lu-main}, gives through the
covariance transfer formula \eqref{eq:cov-transfer-main},
\[
\PP(A_0=1,A_2=1)
=\rho\bigl[\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\bigr].
\]
Hence \(u_2=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\), and the visible ratio above
recovers \(\lambda=\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\).

Put \(q=1-p\).  The labelled-kernel identities in
\Cref{prop:instrument-kernels-main,lem:stationary-main} give
\[
b=p-\lambda,
\qquad
a+b=1-c,
\qquad
q+b=1-\lambda,
\qquad
\rho=\frac{q(1-c)}{q+b}.
\]
The first two identities imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-sum-main}
p+c=1-a+\lambda.
\end{equation}
The last two identities imply
\[
\rho(1-\lambda)=q(1-c)=(1-p)(1-c).
\]
Combining this with \eqref{eq:inverse-sum-main} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-product-main}
pc=\rho(1-\lambda)-a+\lambda.
\end{equation}
Thus the visible law recovers the elementary symmetric functions \(p+c\) and
\(pc\), so \(p\) and \(c\) are the two roots of
\eqref{eq:inverse-quadratic-main}.  Since they came from positive rates, both
roots lie in \((0,1)\), and the unordered rate pair follows by applying
\(-\Delta\tau^{-1}\log\) to the two roots.

It remains to prove the converse and the claimed exchange invariance.  Let
\(L_p=\log p\) and \(L_c=\log c\).  For \(p\ne c\), the forward coordinates can
be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-forward-coordinates-main}
\lambda
=\frac{pL_p-cL_c}{L_p-L_c},
\qquad
a=
\frac{(1-c)L_p-(1-p)L_c}{L_p-L_c},
\qquad
\rho=\frac{(1-p)(1-c)}{1-\lambda}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the first identity is \(\lambda=p-b\) after substituting
\(\Gamma=-\Delta\tau^{-1}L_p\) and
\(\kappa=-\Delta\tau^{-1}L_c\) in the formula for \(b\), the second is
\(a=1-c-b\), and the third is the stationary density formula with
\(q+b=1-\lambda\).  Each expression in
\eqref{eq:symmetric-forward-coordinates-main} is unchanged by exchanging
\(p\) and \(c\).  On the diagonal, the continuous limits are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-forward-diagonal-main}
\lambda=p(1+\log p),
\qquad
a=1-p+p\log p,
\qquad
\rho=\frac{(1-p)^2}{1-\lambda},
\end{equation}
which are the same limits obtained from the equal-rate kernels.

Therefore the renewal determinant \(K_\Gamma\) obtained from
\Cref{prop:renewal-principal-minor-lu-main}, and hence every finite
inclusion, count, and word probability given by
\eqref{eq:finite-window-count-generating-main} and
\eqref{eq:finite-word-inclusion-exclusion-main}, depends on the labelled rates only
through the unordered pair \(\{p,c\}\).  Equal unordered survival pairs give the
same visible law.  Conversely, equal visible laws give the same
\(\rho,a,u_2\), hence the same \(\lambda\), the same quadratic
\eqref{eq:inverse-quadratic-main}, and the same unordered pair \(\{p,c\}\).
The map from \((\Gamma,\kappa)\) to \((p,c)\) is one-to-one in labelled
coordinates, so only the exchange \(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa\) remains, with
no exchange ambiguity on the diagonal.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Three inclusion probabilities are complete quotient coordinates]\label{cor:three_inclusion_complete_coordinates}
For fixed \(\Delta\tau>0\), the three inclusion probabilities
\[
r_0=\PP(A_0=1),
\qquad
r_1=\PP(A_0=1,A_1=1),
\qquad
r_2=\PP(A_0=1,A_2=1)
\]
form complete coordinates for the quotient visible-law family.  Specifically,
\[
a=\frac{r_1}{r_0},
\qquad
u_2=\frac{r_2}{r_0},
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{u_2-r_0}{a-r_0},
\]
and the two roots of \eqref{eq:inverse-quadratic-main}, with \(\rho=r_0\), are
the unordered survival factors \(\{p,c\}\).  Thus the unordered rates
\(\{-\Delta\tau^{-1}\log p,-\Delta\tau^{-1}\log c\}\) are reconstructed from
\((r_0,r_1,r_2)\), and once these are known
\Cref{prop:renewal-principal-minor-lu-main} and
\eqref{eq:finite-window-count-generating-main} and
\eqref{eq:finite-word-inclusion-exclusion-main}
reconstruct every finite inclusion probability, count law, and finite word
probability.
This corollary is a population quotient-coordinate statement.  The finite-record
vector CLT and feasible separated-batch covariance estimator for
\((\widehat r_{0,N},\widehat r_{1,N},\widehat r_{2,N})\) are supplied in
\Cref{prop:three-coordinate-vector-batch-main}, and the delta map is applied
only after that covariance input is available.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Stationarity and positivity give \(r_0=\rho>0\).  By definition,
\(r_1=\PP(A_0=1)\PP(A_1=1\mid A_0=1)=r_0a\) and
\(r_2=\PP(A_0=1)\PP(A_2=1\mid A_0=1)=r_0u_2\).  Therefore the displayed
formulas recover \(a\), \(u_2\), and \(\lambda\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}
then recovers the unordered survival factors and rates.  Substituting those
coordinates into the renewal determinant and finite-window count formulas gives
all finite inclusion, count, and word probabilities, so no
further visible coordinate is needed on the quotient family.
\end{proof}

For the finite-record results below, fix \(N\ge3\) and a record
\((A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1})\).  Define the empirical three-inclusion coordinate
vector by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-three-inclusion-vector-main}
\widehat r_N=(\widehat r_{0,N},\widehat r_{1,N},\widehat r_{2,N})^\top,
\qquad
\widehat r_{0,N}=\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}A_j,
\end{equation}
\[
\widehat r_{1,N}=\frac1{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}A_jA_{j+1},
\qquad
\widehat r_{2,N}=\frac1{N-2}\sum_{j=0}^{N-3}A_jA_{j+2}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Finite-record covariance estimator for the three quotient coordinates]\label{prop:three-coordinate-vector-batch-main}
Fix \(\Delta\tau>0\), and let the sampled boundary-reset record be stationary
with survival factors \(p,c\in(0,1)\).  Let
\[
r=(r_0,r_1,r_2)^\top
\]
be the three inclusion coordinates in
\Cref{cor:three_inclusion_complete_coordinates}.  In the stationary two-sided
record define the centered local vector
\[
X_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_n-r_0\\
A_nA_{n+1}-r_1\\
A_nA_{n+2}-r_2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In the Palm law started from a click at time \(0\), let
\[
0=T_0<T_1<T_2<\cdots,\qquad L_j=T_{j+1}-T_j,
\]
and set the vector cycle rewards
\[
V_j=\sum_{n=T_j}^{T_{j+1}-1}X_n,\qquad j\ge1.
\]
Then \((V_j)_{j\ge1}\) is stationary and \(1\)-dependent, has finite moments
of every order, and
\[
\EE V_1=0.
\]
Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-coordinate-covariance-main}
\Sigma_r=
\frac{
\Var(V_1)+\operatorname{Cov}(V_1,V_2)
+\operatorname{Cov}(V_2,V_1)}
{\EE L_1},
\end{equation}
where vector covariances are matrices,
\(\operatorname{Cov}(U,W)=\EE[UW^\top]-\EE U\,\EE W^\top\).  For the empirical
coordinate vector
\[
\widehat r_N=(\widehat r_{0,N},\widehat r_{1,N},\widehat r_{2,N})^\top
\]
defined in \eqref{eq:empirical-three-inclusion-vector-main},
\[
\sqrt N\,(\widehat r_N-r)\Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,\Sigma_r).
\]

Moreover \(\Sigma_r\) has a feasible all-complete-cycle separated-batch
estimator from the same record.  Let
\[
0\le \tau_1<\cdots<\tau_{J_N}\le N-1
\]
be the observed click times, choose \(b_N\in\mathbb N\) with
\[
b_N\to\infty,\qquad b_N/N\to0,
\]
and set
\[
M_N^{\mathrm{all}}=
\left\lfloor\frac{J_N-1}{b_N+1}\right\rfloor,\qquad
R_N^{\mathrm{all}}=
\begin{cases}
M_N^{\mathrm{all}}b_N+(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}-1),&M_N^{\mathrm{all}}\ge1,\\
0,&M_N^{\mathrm{all}}=0.
\end{cases}
\]
On \(\{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}<2\}\), set
\(\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}=0\).  On
\(\{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}\ge2\}\), define
\[
\bar L_N^{\mathrm{all}}
=
\frac1{R_N^{\mathrm{all}}}\sum_{j=1}^{R_N^{\mathrm{all}}}
(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_j),
\]
\[
\widehat X_{n,N}=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_n-\widehat r_{0,N}\\
A_nA_{n+1}-\widehat r_{1,N}\\
A_nA_{n+2}-\widehat r_{2,N}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\widehat V_{j,N}^{\mathrm{all}}
=
\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\widehat X_{n,N},
\quad
1\le j\le R_N^{\mathrm{all}}.
\]
The definition of \(R_N^{\mathrm{all}}\) leaves at least one later complete
cycle after the last batched cycle, so every displayed lag-two term is observed.
Define the separated vector batch sums
\[
\widehat C_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}
=
\sum_{j=(r-1)(b_N+1)+1}^{(r-1)(b_N+1)+b_N}
\widehat V_{j,N}^{\mathrm{all}},
\qquad 1\le r\le M_N^{\mathrm{all}},
\]
their mean
\[
\bar C_N^{\mathrm{all}}
=
\frac1{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}}\sum_{r=1}^{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}}
\widehat C_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}},
\]
and the matrix estimator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-coordinate-batch-estimator-main}
\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}
=
\frac{1}{\bar L_N^{\mathrm{all}}\,b_N\,(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}-1)}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}}
(\widehat C_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}-\bar C_N^{\mathrm{all}})
(\widehat C_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}-\bar C_N^{\mathrm{all}})^\top .
\end{equation}
Then
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}\to\Sigma_r
\qquad\text{in probability.}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any fixed \(t\in\RR^3\), the scalar observable
\[
t^\top X_n
=t_0(A_n-r_0)+t_1(A_nA_{n+1}-r_1)+t_2(A_nA_{n+2}-r_2)
\]
is bounded, local, and centered in the stationary law.  Its Palm cycle sum is
\(t^\top V_j\).  The terminal lag-two inspection across the next click makes
this cycle sum depend only on the adjacent zero-run pair \((G_j,G_{j+1})\),
with linear growth in that pair.  Hence
\Cref{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main} applies to \(t^\top X_n\).  The Cramer--Wold
device gives a vector CLT for the \(N^{-1}\)-averages of
\((A_n,A_nA_{n+1},A_nA_{n+2})^\top\), with covariance matrix
\eqref{eq:three-coordinate-covariance-main}.  Replacing these common
denominators by \(N,N-1,N-2\) changes the three coordinates by \(O(N^{-1})\),
so the same CLT holds for \(\widehat r_N\).

It remains to prove consistency of the matrix estimator.  First replace
\(\widehat X_{n,N}\) in the definition of
\(\widehat V_{j,N}^{\mathrm{all}}\) by the population-centered \(X_n\), and
call the resulting oracle batch sums \(C_{r,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}\).  The
vector rewards \(V_j\) are stationary, mean zero, and \(1\)-dependent, and each
coordinate has linear growth in the adjacent gap pair \((G_j,G_{j+1})\).  The
matrix form of \Cref{lem:separated-batch-qdependent-main}, with \(d=3\) and
\(q=1\), therefore gives directly
\[
\frac{1}{\bar L_N^{\mathrm{all}}\,b_N\,(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}-1)}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}}
(C_{r,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}-\bar C_N^{\circ,\mathrm{all}})
(C_{r,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}-\bar C_N^{\circ,\mathrm{all}})^\top
\to
\Sigma_r
\]
in probability.  This invocation includes the fourth-moment triangular-array
bound for the separated vector batches, the renewal law of large numbers for
the random number \(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}\) of all-complete-cycle batches, and the
deletion of the stationary initial and terminal incomplete cycles together
with the terminal unbatched remainder.

For plug-in centering, the vector CLT already proved gives
\[
\delta_N:=\|\widehat r_N-r\|=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}).
\]
Since every coordinate of \(X_n-\widehat X_{n,N}\) is bounded in absolute value
by \(\delta_N\), there is a deterministic constant \(C\) such that the batch
perturbations
\[
\Delta_{r,N}:=\widehat C_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}-C_{r,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}
\]
satisfy
\[
\|\Delta_{r,N}\|
\le
C\delta_N
\sum_{j=(r-1)(b_N+1)+1}^{(r-1)(b_N+1)+b_N}
(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_j).
\]
The plug-in perturbation part of
\Cref{lem:separated-batch-qdependent-main} gives
\[
\frac1{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}b_N}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}}\|\Delta_{r,N}\|^2
=O_{\PP}(\delta_N^2b_N)
=O_{\PP}(b_N/N)
=o_{\PP}(1).
\]
After centering the batches, Cauchy--Schwarz shows that the difference between
the plug-in and oracle matrix estimators is \(o_{\PP}(1)\) entrywise.  This
proves \eqref{eq:three-coordinate-batch-estimator-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Concrete rate for the complete-cycle covariance estimator]\label{lem:three-coordinate-batch-rate-main}
In the setting of
\Cref{prop:three-coordinate-vector-batch-main}, choose
\[
b_N=\max\{1,\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\}.
\]
Then the feasible separated-batch covariance estimator satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-coordinate-batch-rate-main}
\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/3}).
\end{equation}
Consequently, at every population point where the map \(\Phi\) is \(C^1\),
the symmetric-coordinate covariance estimator used in
\Cref{cor:finite_inverse_delta_boundary_main} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-coordinate-batch-rate-main}
\|\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}-\Sigma_s\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/3}).
\end{equation}
Thus every deterministic tolerance \(t_N\downarrow0\) with
\[
N^{-1/3}/t_N\to0
\]
is rate-certified for the rank rule in
\eqref{eq:rank-tolerance-condition-main}; for example \(t_N=N^{-1/4}\) is an
admissible deterministic choice.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the notation from the proof of
\Cref{prop:three-coordinate-vector-batch-main}.  For oracle batch sums set
\[
X_{r,N}=b_N^{-1/2}C_{r,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}} .
\]
The separated batches are independent after the one omitted cycle, and the
fourth-moment estimate in
\Cref{lem:separated-batch-qdependent-main} gives
\(\sup_N\EE\|X_{1,N}\|^4<\infty\).  Therefore each matrix entry of the sample
second moment has stochastic error \(O_{\PP}((M_N^{\mathrm{all}})^{-1/2})\).
The finite-block covariance bias is the usual missing-lag edge term:
\[
\frac1{b_N}\EE C_{1,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}
(C_{1,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}})^\top-\Gamma_Y
=O(b_N^{-1}),
\]
with \(\Gamma_Y\) the cycle-reward long-run covariance matrix.  Also
\(\bar L_N^{\mathrm{all}}-\EE L_1=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).  Since
\[
M_N^{\mathrm{all}}\asymp_{\PP}N/b_N,
\]
the oracle normalized matrix estimator has error
\[
O_{\PP}\bigl((N/b_N)^{-1/2}\bigr)+O(b_N^{-1})+O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}).
\]
For \(b_N=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\) this is \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/3})\).

The plug-in centering perturbation in the proof of
\Cref{prop:three-coordinate-vector-batch-main} has
\(\delta_N=\|\widehat r_N-r\|=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) and obeys
\[
\frac1{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}b_N}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}}\|\Delta_{r,N}\|^2
=O_{\PP}(b_N/N).
\]
The cross term between oracle and plug-in batch sums is therefore
\(O_{\PP}((b_N/N)^{1/2})=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/3})\) after the same normalization, and
the pure plug-in quadratic term is \(O_{\PP}(b_N/N)=O_{\PP}(N^{-2/3})\).
This proves \eqref{eq:three-coordinate-batch-rate-main}.

At a \(C^1\) point of \(\Phi\),
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}
=D\Phi(\widehat r_N)\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}
D\Phi(\widehat r_N)^\top
\]
as in the symmetric-coordinate delta method.
Since \(D\Phi(\widehat r_N)-D\Phi(r)=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) and
\(\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}=O_{\PP}(1)\), the Jacobian plug-in
contributes only \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), while
\eqref{eq:three-coordinate-batch-rate-main} contributes \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/3})\).
This proves \eqref{eq:symmetric-coordinate-batch-rate-main}.  Dividing that
rate by any deterministic \(t_N\) satisfying \(N^{-1/3}/t_N\to0\) gives
\eqref{eq:rank-tolerance-condition-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Separated roots for finite-record root and rate inference]\label{ass:separated-roots-main}
For ordinary root-coordinate and rate-coordinate inference from a finite
record, the population survival factors are assumed separated from the exchange
diagonal: there is a constant \(\eta>0\) such that
\[
|p-c|\ge \eta,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\Delta_s=(p-c)^2\ge \eta^2 .
\]
This is an inferential regularity hypothesis only.  It is not needed for the
population quotient inverse in
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality,cor:three_inclusion_complete_coordinates},
and it is not needed for the finite-record delta method in the symmetric
coordinates \((s_1,s_2)\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-record projected symmetric-coordinate inverse]\label{cor:finite_inverse_delta_boundary_main}
Fix \(\Delta\tau>0\), and let the sampled boundary-reset record be stationary
with survival factors \(p,c\in(0,1)\).  From one record
\((A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1})\), use the empirical inclusion vector
\(\widehat r_N\) in \eqref{eq:empirical-three-inclusion-vector-main}.
Then, for some positive semidefinite matrix \(\Sigma_r\),
\[
\sqrt N\,(\widehat r_N-r)
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_r),
\qquad
r=(r_0,r_1,r_2).
\]
Here \(\Sigma_r\) is the regenerative covariance matrix in
\eqref{eq:three-coordinate-covariance-main}, and the feasible separated-batch
matrix estimator
\(\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}\) in
\eqref{eq:three-coordinate-batch-estimator-main} is consistent for it.
On the open set \(r_0>0\) and \(r_1/r_0\ne r_0\), put
\[
a(r)=\frac{r_1}{r_0},\qquad
u_2(r)=\frac{r_2}{r_0},\qquad
\lambda(r)=\frac{u_2(r)-r_0}{a(r)-r_0},
\]
and define the symmetric-coordinate map
\[
\Phi(r)=(s_1(r),s_2(r))
\]
by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-inverse-delta-map-main}
s_1(r)=1-a(r)+\lambda(r),
\qquad
s_2(r)=r_0\bigl(1-\lambda(r)\bigr)-a(r)+\lambda(r).
\end{equation}
At the population value, \(s_1=p+c\) and \(s_2=pc\).  The finite-record
estimator used for inference is the projected feasible symmetric-coordinate
estimator, not the raw value \(\Phi(\widehat r_N)\).  Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-feasible-image-main}
F
=
\{(x+y,xy):0\le x\le1,\ 0\le y\le1\}
=
\left\{
(s_1,s_2):
\Delta=s_1^2-4s_2\ge0,\quad
0\le\frac{s_1\pm\sqrt\Delta}{2}\le1
\right\}.
\end{equation}
This compact set is the closed symmetric-coordinate image of the two survival
factors.  The strict sampled-counter model has \(0<p,c<1\).  Its
two-dimensional feasible interior is the separated-root part
\[
F^{\circ}_{\rm sep}
=
\{(x+y,xy):0<x<1,\ 0<y<1,\ x\ne y\},
\]
whereas the exchange diagonal is
\[
F_{\rm ex}
=
\{(2z,z^2):0<z<1\}.
\]
For \(y\in\mathbb R^2\), define the canonical Euclidean projection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-symmetric-projection-main}
\Pi_F(y)
=
\operatorname*{lex\,min}
\arg\min_{v\in F}\|y-v\|_2 ,
\end{equation}
where the lexicographic rule is used only to break possible ties among nearest
points.  On the event that \(\widehat r_N\) lies in the displayed open set of
\(\Phi\), set \(\widehat s_N^{\rm raw}=\Phi(\widehat r_N)\); off that event, set
\(\widehat s_N^{\rm raw}\) equal to any fixed deterministic vector in
\(\mathbb R^2\).  The reported symmetric-coordinate estimator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-symmetric-estimator-main}
\widetilde s_N:=\Pi_F(\widehat s_N^{\rm raw}).
\end{equation}
On the event whose probability tends to one, the raw delta estimate satisfies
\[
\widehat s_N^{\rm raw}=\Phi(\widehat r_N),\qquad
s:=\Phi(r),\qquad
\Sigma_s:=D\Phi(r)\Sigma_rD\Phi(r)^\top
\]
and the symmetric-coordinate central limit theorem
\[
\sqrt N\,(\widehat s_N^{\rm raw}-s)
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_s).
\]
If \(s\in F^{\circ}_{\rm sep}\), then
\[
\PP(\widetilde s_N=\widehat s_N^{\rm raw})\to1,
\qquad
\sqrt N\,(\widetilde s_N-s)
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_s).
\]
The corresponding feasible covariance estimator is
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}
=
D\Phi(\widehat r_N)\,
\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}\,
D\Phi(\widehat r_N)^\top
\]
on the event that \(\widehat r_N\) lies in the displayed open set, with any
fixed positive semidefinite fallback value off that event.  It converges in
probability to \(\Sigma_s\).
The feasible two-dimensional Wald statement proved from the separated-batch
estimator is the full-rank interior statement.  If \(s\in F^{\circ}_{\rm sep}\)
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-coordinate-full-rank-assumption-main}
\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_s)\ge\sigma_0>0,
\end{equation}
then Weyl's inequality and
\(\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}\to\Sigma_s\) in probability give
\[
\PP\!\left(
\lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Sigma_{s,N})\ge\sigma_0/2
\right)\to1.
\]
On that event \(\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}\) is invertible, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-coordinate-full-rank-wald-main}
N(\widetilde s_N-s)^\top
\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}^{-1}
(\widetilde s_N-s)
\Rightarrow\chi^2_2 .
\end{equation}
The corresponding feasible symmetric-coordinate confidence region is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-coordinate-full-rank-confidence-region-main}
\mathcal C_{s,N}^{\operatorname{full}}(1-\alpha)
=
\left\{
s'\in\mathcal F:
N(\widetilde s_N-s')^\top
\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}^{-1}
(\widetilde s_N-s')
\le \chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}
\right\},
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal F=F\).  Since the true \(s\) is in
\(F^{\circ}_{\rm sep}\), replacing \(F\) by the strict image
\(\{(x+y,xy):0<x,y<1\}\) gives the same asymptotic coverage.

If the full-rank condition is not imposed but \(s\in F^{\circ}_{\rm sep}\),
the unconditional conclusion is only the following population range statement.
Let \(k_s=\operatorname{rank}\Sigma_s\).  If \(k_s>0\), assume the nonzero
spectrum is separated from zero:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-coordinate-rank-gap-main}
\lambda_{k_s}(\Sigma_s)\ge\sigma_0>0,
\end{equation}
and let \(Q_s\in\mathbb R^{2\times k_s}\) have orthonormal columns spanning
\(\operatorname{range}\Sigma_s\).  Put
\(\Sigma_{s,+}=Q_s^\top\Sigma_sQ_s\).  Then \(\Sigma_{s,+}\) is nonsingular and
the population range-Wald statistic satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-coordinate-rank-adaptive-wald-main}
N(Q_s^\top(\widetilde s_N-s))^\top
\Sigma_{s,+}^{-1}
Q_s^\top(\widetilde s_N-s)
\Rightarrow\chi^2_{k_s}.
\end{equation}
If \(k_s=0\), then \(\sqrt N(\widetilde s_N-s)\Rightarrow0\), and no positive
dimensional Wald ellipsoid is asserted.

The separated-batch proof above gives consistency of
\(\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}\), and
\Cref{lem:three-coordinate-batch-rate-main} gives the explicit rate
\(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/3})\) when
\(b_N=\max\{1,\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\}\).  Thus the rank selector is feasible
only after such a deterministic batch rule and a deterministic spectral
tolerance have been fixed in advance.  More generally, a rank-adaptive
implementation is a rate-certified statement: before inspecting the record one
must have an external deterministic spectral tolerance \(t_N\downarrow0\) and
a proof, simulation-calibrated certificate, or other design-specific bound
guaranteeing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-tolerance-condition-main}
\frac{\|\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}-\Sigma_s\|_{\operatorname{op}}}{t_N}
\xrightarrow{\PP}0 .
\end{equation}
For the concrete complete-cycle rule above, \(t_N=N^{-1/4}\) is one admissible
choice.  In all cases the sequence \(t_N\) is a rate-certificate input fixed
before seeing the data; it is not chosen from the realized eigenvalue plot and
is not supplied by mere consistency of the separated-batch covariance
estimator.  If such a certificate is available,
let \(\widehat Q_{s,N}\) contain the
eigenvectors of \(\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}\) with eigenvalues larger than \(t_N\),
let \(\widehat k_{s,N}\) be their number, and set
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{s,+,N}
=\widehat Q_{s,N}^{\top}\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}\widehat Q_{s,N}.
\]
Then
\[
\widehat k_{s,N}\xrightarrow{\PP}k_s,
\qquad
\widehat Q_{s,N}\widehat Q_{s,N}^{\top}
\xrightarrow{\PP}
Q_sQ_s^\top
\]
in operator norm, with the zero projector understood when \(k_s=0\).  If
\(k_s>0\), the certified rank-adaptive Wald statistic
\[
N\bigl(\widehat Q_{s,N}^{\top}(\widetilde s_N-s)\bigr)^\top
\widehat\Sigma_{s,+,N}^{-1}
\widehat Q_{s,N}^{\top}(\widetilde s_N-s)
\]
converges to \(\chi^2_{k_s}\).  The corresponding certified confidence set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-coordinate-rank-adaptive-confidence-region-main}
\mathcal C_{s,N}^{\operatorname{rank}}(1-\alpha)
=
\left\{
s'\in\mathcal F:
N\bigl(\widehat Q_{s,N}^{\top}(\widetilde s_N-s')\bigr)^\top
\widehat\Sigma_{s,+,N}^{-1}
\widehat Q_{s,N}^{\top}(\widetilde s_N-s')
\le \chi^2_{\widehat k_{s,N},1-\alpha}
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for \(k_s>0\),
\[
\PP\!\left(s\in\mathcal C_{s,N}^{\operatorname{rank}}(1-\alpha)\right)
\to1-\alpha .
\]
If \(\widehat k_{s,N}=0\), the certified rank-adaptive procedure reports the degenerate
zero-rank case rather than a positive-dimensional ellipsoid; when \(k_s=0\),
this occurs with probability tending to one.  The label-free root-set image of
the certified symmetric-coordinate confidence set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unordered-root-confidence-region-main}
C_{\{p,c\},N}^{\operatorname{rank}}(1-\alpha)
=
\left\{
\left\{
\frac{s_1+\sqrt{s_1^2-4s_2}}2,\,
\frac{s_1-\sqrt{s_1^2-4s_2}}2
\right\}:
(s_1,s_2)\in\mathcal C_{s,N}^{\operatorname{rank}}(1-\alpha)
\right\}.
\end{equation}
This is a feasible root-set image, not a labelled root covariance statement at
the exchange diagonal.  The data-feasible rank-adaptive confidence set is
asserted only under the deterministic rate certificate
\eqref{eq:rank-tolerance-condition-main}; in particular,
\Cref{lem:three-coordinate-batch-rate-main} supplies such a certificate for
\(b_N=\max\{1,\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\}\) and \(t_N=N^{-1/4}\).  Without a
deterministic certificate of this kind, the paper asserts only the full-rank
feasible Wald region and the population range-Wald limit
\eqref{eq:symmetric-coordinate-rank-adaptive-wald-main}, not a data-chosen
rank-adaptive confidence set.  In the special case \(k_s=2\), any certified
rank-adaptive statement reduces to the full-rank Wald ellipsoid above.

If \Cref{ass:separated-roots-main} holds, then
\[
\Delta_s:=s_1^2-4s_2=(p-c)^2\ge\eta^2>0,
\]
and an ordinary ordered-root covariance calculation should be reported only
after a feasibility-and-separation event has been fixed before inspecting the
record.  For example, with the separation constant \(\eta\) fixed in
\Cref{ass:separated-roots-main}, one may use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-separated-root-event-main}
\mathcal E_{N,\eta}^{\operatorname{root}}
=
\left\{
\widetilde s_N\in F,\quad
\widehat\Delta_{s,N}
:=
\widetilde s_{1,N}^{\,2}-4\widetilde s_{2,N}
\ge \eta^2/4
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, one may replace \(\eta^2/4\) by any other positive margin chosen
in advance.  On this event, with probability tending to one under the
separated-root hypothesis, the ordered-by-size root chart
\[
\Psi(s_1,s_2)=
\left(
\frac{s_1+\sqrt{s_1^2-4s_2}}2,\,
\frac{s_1-\sqrt{s_1^2-4s_2}}2
\right)
\]
is \(C^1\) near \(\Phi(r)\).  Hence the root estimators
\[
(\widehat z_{+,N},\widehat z_{-,N})
=\Psi(\widetilde s_N)
\]
satisfy the ordinary delta-method limit
\[
\sqrt N
\begin{pmatrix}
\widehat z_{+,N}-z_+\\
\widehat z_{-,N}-z_-
\end{pmatrix}
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(
0,\,
D\Psi(\Phi(r))D\Phi(r)\Sigma_rD\Phi(r)^\top
D\Psi(\Phi(r))^\top
\right),
\]
where \(\{z_+,z_-\}=\{p,c\}\) and \(z_+>z_-\).  Applying
\(-\Delta\tau^{-1}\log\) to these roots gives the corresponding unordered
rate estimates.  The additional rate-map Jacobian is
\[
\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
-\frac1{\Delta\tau z_+},
-\frac1{\Delta\tau z_-}
\right).
\]
Off the diagonal, feasible root and rate covariance estimators are obtained by
substituting \(\widehat r_N\) and
\(\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}\) in the two displayed Jacobian
sandwiches.  They are consistent on every separated-root subregion with
\(\Delta_s\ge\eta^2>0\).

On the exchange diagonal \(p=c=z\in(0,1)\), the discriminant vanishes:
\(\Delta_s=0\), and the preceding interior first-order equivalence is not
asserted.  The projected estimator is still canonical and feasible.  If
\(Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_s)\), then the local tangent cone of \(F\) at
\((2z,z^2)\) is the half-space
\[
T_zF=\{(h_1,h_2):h_2\le z h_1\},
\]
and the projected estimator has the boundary local limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exchange-diagonal-projection-limit-main}
\sqrt N\,(\widetilde s_N-s)
\Rightarrow
\Pi_{T_zF}(Z),
\end{equation}
where \(\Pi_{T_zF}\) is Euclidean projection onto this closed half-space.
Thus \(\widetilde s_N\in F\) for every record and
\(\|\widetilde s_N-s\|=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), but the limit in
\eqref{eq:exchange-diagonal-projection-limit-main} is generally not the
unconstrained Gaussian \(Z\).  The map
\((s_1,s_2)\mapsto\{z:z^2-s_1z+s_2=0\}\) is not differentiable in ordinary
Euclidean root coordinates at this point, and the above root delta method does
not apply.  Near the discriminant locus the root Jacobian contains powers of
\(\Delta_s^{-1/2}=|p-c|^{-1}\), so finite-record root intervals are unstable
as the two survival factors approach one another.  More precisely, if
\(H=\Pi_{T_zF}(Z)\), then the unordered roots of the projected
symmetric-coordinate polynomial obey
\[
z_{\pm,N}
=
z+\frac{H_1}{2\sqrt N}
\pm N^{-1/4}\sqrt{(zH_1-H_2)_+}
{}+
o_{\PP}(N^{-1/4}).
\]
The diagonal therefore has a boundary projection law in symmetric coordinates
and a nonstandard root-splitting scale, not a labelled root/rate covariance
matrix.  Finally, even off the
diagonal, the chart \((z_+,z_-)\) labels roots only by numerical order; assigning
one root to the attempt rate and the other to the recovery rate requires
external information, exactly as in
\Cref{prop:hidden_label_exchange_nonidentifiability}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The sampled law is a stationary renewal process with an exponentially decaying
zero-run tail.  For any fixed \(t\in\RR^3\), the scalar observable
\[
t_0A_j+t_1A_jA_{j+1}+t_2A_jA_{j+2}
\]
is bounded and local.  Its Palm cycle sum depends only on adjacent zero-run
lengths and has linear growth in those lengths.  Applying
\Cref{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main} to this observable, and using that replacing
\(N\) by \(N-1\) or \(N-2\) in the empirical denominators changes the statistic
by \(o_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), gives a one-dimensional CLT for every linear
combination of \(\widehat r_N-r\).  The Cramer--Wold device gives the displayed
joint CLT with the covariance matrix \(\Sigma_r\) identified in
\Cref{prop:three-coordinate-vector-batch-main}.  The same proposition gives
the separated-batch consistency of
\(\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}\).

The inequalities \(r_0>0\) and \(r_1/r_0\ne r_0\) hold at the population value:
the first is stationarity with positive click density, and the second is
\(a\ne\rho\) from \Cref{lem:stationary-main}.  Thus \(\Phi\) is \(C^1\) in a
neighbourhood of the population inclusion vector, and the multivariate delta
method gives the symmetric-coordinate limit.  The identities
\(s_1=p+c\) and \(s_2=pc\) are exactly
\eqref{eq:inverse-sum-main} and \eqref{eq:inverse-product-main}.
Since \(\widehat r_N\to r\) in probability, the event that
\(\widehat r_N\) lies in the open set has probability tending to one; the
covariance-estimator consistency for \(\Phi(\widehat r_N)\) follows from the
continuity of \(D\Phi\) and Slutsky's theorem.

The set \(F\) in \eqref{eq:symmetric-feasible-image-main} is compact, so the
nearest-point set in \eqref{eq:canonical-symmetric-projection-main} is always
nonempty, and the lexicographic tie-break makes \(\Pi_F\) single-valued.  If
\(s\in F^{\circ}_{\rm sep}\), then \(s\) has a positive Euclidean distance
from the complement of the feasible chart used by the root map.  Since
\(\widehat s_N^{\rm raw}\to s\) in probability, the raw estimator belongs to
that same open feasible neighbourhood with probability tending to one.  On
this event \(\Pi_F(\widehat s_N^{\rm raw})=\widehat s_N^{\rm raw}\), which
proves the first-order equivalence and transfers the raw symmetric-coordinate
CLT to \(\widetilde s_N\).

At an exchange-diagonal point \(s=(2z,z^2)\), a perturbation
\((2z,z^2)+t(h_1,h_2)\) is feasible to first order exactly when its
discriminant satisfies
\[
(2z+th_1)^2-4(z^2+th_2)
=4t(zh_1-h_2)+O(t^2)\ge0
\]
for small positive \(t\).  Hence the closed Bouligand tangent cone of \(F\) at
\(s\) is the half-space \(T_zF=\{h_2\le zh_1\}\).  The standard local limit theorem for metric
projections onto a closed set with this tangent cone gives
\[
\sqrt N(\Pi_F(s+Z_N/\sqrt N)-s)\Rightarrow\Pi_{T_zF}(Z)
\]
whenever \(Z_N\Rightarrow Z\); applying this with
\(Z_N=\sqrt N(\widehat s_N^{\rm raw}-s)\) gives
\eqref{eq:exchange-diagonal-projection-limit-main}.  The displayed
root-splitting expansion follows from
\[
s_{1,N}=2z+H_1/\sqrt N+o_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}),\qquad
s_{2,N}=z^2+H_2/\sqrt N+o_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}),
\]
because the discriminant is
\[
s_{1,N}^2-4s_{2,N}
=4(zH_1-H_2)N^{-1/2}+o_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}),
\]
and feasibility of the projection makes the leading positive part
\((zH_1-H_2)_+\) the relevant term.

Under the full-rank assumption
\(\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_s)\ge\sigma_0>0\), Weyl's inequality gives
\[
\lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Sigma_{s,N})
\ge
\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_s)
-\|\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}-\Sigma_s\|_{\operatorname{op}} .
\]
Since \(\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}\to\Sigma_s\) in probability, the right-hand side
is at least \(\sigma_0/2\) with probability tending to one.  Thus the feasible
inverse exists with probability tending to one, and Slutsky's theorem applied
to the two-dimensional normal limit gives
\eqref{eq:symmetric-coordinate-full-rank-wald-main}.

Let \(k_s=\operatorname{rank}\Sigma_s\).  If \(k_s>0\), projection onto the
range of \(\Sigma_s\) gives a \(k_s\)-dimensional nonsingular normal limit with
covariance \(\Sigma_{s,+}=Q_s^\top\Sigma_sQ_s\), so the ordinary Wald theorem
in that range gives
\eqref{eq:symmetric-coordinate-rank-adaptive-wald-main}.  If \(k_s=0\), the
limit covariance is zero and the displayed CLT degenerates to
\(\sqrt N(\widetilde s_N-s)\Rightarrow0\).

It remains only for the conditional rank-adaptive clause to justify that a
spectral rule with the certified tolerance estimates the rank and range.  Let the eigenvalues
of \(\Sigma_s\) be
\[
\lambda_1(\Sigma_s)\ge\lambda_2(\Sigma_s)\ge0,
\]
with the convention that the positive eigenvalues are absent when \(k_s=0\).
For \(k_s>0\), the nonzero-eigenvalue separation gives
\(\lambda_{k_s}(\Sigma_s)\ge\sigma_0>0\).  Weyl's inequality gives, for
\(j=1,2\),
\[
\bigl|\lambda_j(\widehat\Sigma_{s,N})-\lambda_j(\Sigma_s)\bigr|
\le
\|\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}-\Sigma_s\|_{\operatorname{op}}.
\]
Because \(t_N\downarrow0\) and
\(\|\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}-\Sigma_s\|_{\operatorname{op}}/t_N\to0\) in
probability, every positive population eigenvalue is eventually larger than
\(t_N\) with probability tending to one, while every zero population eigenvalue
is eventually below \(t_N\) with probability tending to one.
Thus
\[
\widehat k_{s,N}\to k_s
\qquad\text{in probability.}
\]
On this event, if \(0<k_s<2\), the spectral gap between the retained and
discarded eigenspaces is asymptotically bounded below by
\(\lambda_{k_s}(\Sigma_s)/2\).  The Davis--Kahan sin-theta theorem therefore
gives
\[
\|\widehat Q_{s,N}\widehat Q_{s,N}^{\top}-Q_sQ_s^\top\|_{\operatorname{op}}
\le
\frac{2\|\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}-\Sigma_s\|_{\operatorname{op}}}
     {\lambda_{k_s}(\Sigma_s)}
+o_{\PP}(1)
\to0
\]
in probability.  For \(k_s=2\) both projectors are the identity with
probability tending to one, and for \(k_s=0\) both are the zero projector with
probability tending to one, so the same projector-consistency conclusion holds
in all ranks.

When \(k_s=1\), rotate the empirical eigenvector by its sign, if necessary, so
that \(\widehat Q_{s,N}^\top Q_s\to1\) in probability; the Wald quadratic is
unchanged by this sign choice.  The projector consistency and the fixed CLT
then imply
\[
\widehat Q_{s,N}^{\top}\sqrt N(\widetilde s_N-s)
=
Q_s^\top\sqrt N(\widetilde s_N-s)+o_{\PP}(1)
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{s,+}).
\]
Also
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{s,+,N}
=\widehat Q_{s,N}^{\top}\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}\widehat Q_{s,N}
\to
Q_s^\top\Sigma_sQ_s=\Sigma_{s,+}
\]
in probability, and the scalar limit \(\Sigma_{s,+}\) is positive.  Slutsky's
theorem gives the certified rank-one Wald limit.  When \(k_s=2\), the rank rule
retains the full two-dimensional space with probability tending to one, and
the statistic equals
\[
N(\widetilde s_N-s)^\top\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}^{-1}(\widetilde s_N-s)
\]
on that event; ordinary Slutsky's theorem with
\(\widehat\Sigma_{s,N}\to\Sigma_s\) and \(\Sigma_s\) nonsingular gives
\eqref{eq:symmetric-coordinate-full-rank-wald-main}.  These two cases prove
the certified rank-adaptive Wald limit and hence the stated coverage of
\(\mathcal C_{s,N}^{\operatorname{rank}}(1-\alpha)\).  Intersecting the
ellipsoid with \(\mathcal F\) enforces the exact polynomial image
of two survival factors in \([0,1]\).  In the present strict record model the
population roots lie in \((0,1)\).  The unordered root region is then the
algebraic image of that feasible symmetric-coordinate region.

Under \Cref{ass:separated-roots-main}, the discriminant
\(\Delta_s=(p-c)^2\) is bounded away from zero at the population point.  Hence
the ordered root map \(\Psi\) is \(C^1\) on a neighbourhood of \(\Phi(r)\), and
with probability tending to one the empirical symmetric-coordinate vector lies
in that neighbourhood.  A second application of the delta method gives the root
limit.  The log-rate limit is the delta method applied once more to
\(z\mapsto-\Delta\tau^{-1}\log z\); since \(z_\pm\in(0,1)\), the logarithm is
smooth at both population roots.  If \(p=c\), then \(\Delta_s=0\) and
\(\sqrt{s_1^2-4s_2}\) is not differentiable at the population point, so the
ordinary Euclidean root-coordinate delta method has no valid derivative there.
The explicit derivative for
\[
z_\pm=\frac{s_1\pm\sqrt{\Delta_s}}2
\]
contains the factors
\[
\frac{\partial z_\pm}{\partial s_1}
=\frac12\pm\frac{s_1}{2\sqrt{\Delta_s}},
\qquad
\frac{\partial z_\pm}{\partial s_2}
=\mp\frac1{\sqrt{\Delta_s}},
\]
which also records the finite-record instability as the diagonal is approached.
The final label statement follows from the exchange invariance in
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality,prop:hidden_label_exchange_nonidentifiability}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Critical self-dual inverse]\label{cor:critical_self_dual_inverse}
The exact-\(1\)-dependence transition is the label-free self-dual level
\(\lambda=0\).  Off the diagonal, this is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-dual-critical-main}
p\log p=c\log c,
\end{equation}
and the continuous diagonal interpretation gives the single diagonal critical
point \(p=c=e^{-1}\).  Off the diagonal, the two survival factors lie on
opposite sides of \(e^{-1}\).  If
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) is externally known, this reduces to the unique labelled
rate \(\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) of
\Cref{lem:critical-rate-main}.  This labelled notation is meaningful only after
detector calibration, hardware recovery measurement, or another external
experimental protocol has supplied the recovery label.  If
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) is not externally labelled, then
\eqref{eq:visible-law-exchange-main} applies and one visible sampled record
identifies only the unordered critical rate pair; the critical statement must
be read on the quotient by
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\sim(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{lem:one-dep-criterion-main}, exact \(1\)-dependence occurs exactly
when \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\).  \Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality} identifies the visible
\(\lambda\) with \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\), so the condition is label-free.
For \(p\ne c\), the first formula in
\eqref{eq:symmetric-forward-coordinates-main} gives
\(\lambda=0\) if and only if \(p\log p=c\log c\).  On the diagonal,
\eqref{eq:symmetric-forward-diagonal-main} gives
\(\lambda=p(1+\log p)\), hence the unique diagonal critical point is
\(p=e^{-1}\).

The function \(f(x)=x\log x\) decreases on \((0,e^{-1})\), increases on
\((e^{-1},1)\), and has its unique minimum at \(e^{-1}\).  Therefore two
distinct solutions of \(f(p)=f(c)\) must lie on opposite sides of \(e^{-1}\).
When the recovery rate is externally labelled, \(c=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\)
is fixed and \Cref{lem:critical-rate-main} supplies the unique labelled
attempt rate \(\Gamma_c\).  Without that external label, the visible inverse
recovers only the unordered pair of survival factors, and therefore only the
unordered pair of critical rates.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Hidden-label exchange non-identifiability]\label{prop:hidden_label_exchange_nonidentifiability}
Except on the diagonal \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), the two labelled HMMs
obtained by exchanging \(\Gamma\) and \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) have different
hidden labelled kernels \(T_0,T_1\), but exactly the same visible
finite-dimensional law.  Thus the hidden labelled kernel is not identifiable
from one visible record, even though the visible law identifies the unordered
survival pair and all finite word laws:
\[
\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma},
\qquad
\text{visible data identify only }
\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}.
\]
This is the local non-identifiability qualification for the quotient inverse.
The conditional response use of a labelled memory target is stated only in the
A0 and regular-level-set paragraphs of \Cref{sec:kms-shells}.
Finite-record covariance estimation for the three inverse coordinates is
instead stated in
\Cref{prop:three-coordinate-vector-batch-main,cor:finite_inverse_delta_boundary_main}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The labelled kernels in \eqref{eq:t0-t1-main} contain the entries
\(p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}\), \(c=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\), and the
refractory-to-ready no-click probability \(b\).  If
\(\Gamma\ne\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), then \(p\ne c\), so exchanging the two rates
interchanges the two survival factors and changes at least the \((R,R)\) and
\((D,D)\) entries of \(T_0\).  Hence the hidden labelled kernels are different.

By \Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}, however, the full visible law
depends only on the unordered pair \(\{p,c\}\).  The exchanged HMM therefore has
the same finite inclusion probabilities, and by
\eqref{eq:finite-window-count-generating-main} and
\eqref{eq:finite-word-inclusion-exclusion-main}
the same finite count and word
probabilities.  This separates hidden-kernel identifiability from
visible-record identifiability: the visible record determines the quotient
coordinates completely, but it cannot label which recovered rate was the
attempt rate and which was the recovery rate without external calibration.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Block IV: fixed-record observability and lag-two covariance CLT}\label{subsec:finite-sample}
This subsection proves the fixed-record finite-sample result used later: for
the sampled boundary-reset law, one stationary record estimates
\[
\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)
\]
and supplies a studentized interval using the all-complete-cycle separated-batch
estimator.

This is adjacent to recent model-free trajectory-memory and weak-memory
inference work
\cite{VollmarEtAl2024Memory,MeyerBrandner2026WeakMemory}, but here the record
law, detector setting, and estimator are fixed in advance.

For a record $(A_0,\dots,A_{N-1})$, set $\widehat\rho=N^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}A_j$. Let $C_k^{=}$ be the number of completed inter-click gaps with exactly $k$ zeros, and let $C_k^{\ge}$ be the number with at least $k$ zeros; when $C_k^{\ge}>0$, put
\[
\widehat h_k=\frac{C_k^{=}}{C_k^{\ge}} .
\]
For a fixed lag $n<N$, use
\[
\widehat\gamma_n=
\frac{1}{N-n}\sum_{j=0}^{N-n-1}
(A_j-\widehat\rho)(A_{j+n}-\widehat\rho).
\]
For a block size $B$, split the record into $M=\lfloor N/B\rfloor$ disjoint blocks and write $N_B^{(m)}=\sum_{j=(m-1)B}^{mB-1}A_j$; the empirical block Fano factor is the sample variance of the $N_B^{(m)}$ divided by their sample mean.

For the lag-two covariance, the natural click-to-click reward is not an i.i.d.\
one-cycle observable: the terminal lag-two window in one cycle uses the first
symbol of the next cycle. The correct finite-record construction is therefore a
cycle decomposition with a stationary $1$-dependent reward sequence and a
batching step that removes this one-step overlap.

The variance-estimation device is a specialized batch-means construction, not a
new general simulation-output theorem.  Classical steady-state output analysis
uses batch means, overlapping batch means, spaced batch means, and standardized
time-series estimators to estimate the time-average variance constant, with
both the batch length and the number of batches diverging; see, for example,
Glynn--Whitt on asymptotic-variance estimation by batch means, Damerdji on
strong and mean-square consistency of batch-means-type variance estimators, and
subsequent large-sample and survey treatments
\cite{GlynnWhitt1991BatchMeans,Damerdji1994StrongConsistency,Damerdji1995MeanSquareConsistency,ChienGoldsmanMelamed1997BatchMeans,AlexopoulosGoldsman2004Batch}.
What is paper-specific here is the regenerative geometry of the sampled
counter.  The regeneration times are observed clicks, the reward attached to a
click-to-click cycle is the complete lag-two covariance contribution for that
cycle, and adjacent rewards are exactly \(1\)-dependent because a terminal
lag-two window reaches into the next cycle.  The separated-batch estimator
therefore deliberately omits one complete-cycle reward between neighboring
batches; this omission kills the one-dependence before the usual batch-variance
argument is applied.  The remaining technical work below is only the
finite-record bookkeeping for this model: plug-in centering stability and
transfer from the deterministic Palm proof device to the observed
all-complete-cycle random batch count.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-record dependency map and narrow diagnostic closure]\label{prop:finite-record-map-main}
For the stationary boundary-reset record of \Cref{thm:recovery-main}, the
finite-record results in \Cref{subsec:finite-sample} have one statistical
output: the fixed-setting lag-two diagnostic
\[
\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2),
\]
with studentization justified for the sampled law by
\Cref{lem:boundary-reset-tau-positive-main}.  The implemented variance estimate
is the all-complete-cycle estimator \eqref{eq:cycle-block-est-all-main}; the
finite-lag theorem in Appendix~\ref{app:renewal-reward-proof-package} and the
deterministic-prefix estimator are proof machinery.  Scan and response uses of
\(\gamma_2\) require separate inputs.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main} is the single reusable probability
input.  Its \(q=1\) specialization reduces the empirical lag-two covariance to
a stationary finite-lag renewal-reward CLT and gives the all-complete-cycle
separated-batch variance estimator, including random complete-cycle counts and
plug-in centering.  \Cref{lem:boundary-reset-tau-positive-main} then proves
that the resulting sampled-law variance is strictly positive.  These two
inputs give the sampled-model theorem below for \(\widehat\gamma_2\) and no
other parameter.
\Cref{prop:finite-diagnostics-main} then translates the population value of
  \(\gamma_2\) inside the already proved boundary-reset model: its sign detects
  the side of the memory transition, while a supplied one-parameter population
  curve gives only the displayed deterministic derivative identity.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof-package location.}
The generic regenerative and batch-means proof package for Block~IV is the
single finite-lag theorem in
Appendix~\ref{app:renewal-reward-proof-package},
\Cref{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main}.  Its preliminary

\begin{lemma}[Finite-start burn-in transfer for the primitive hidden chain]\label{lem:finite-start-burnin-main}
Fix positive sampled boundary-reset parameters and let
\[
P=T_0+T_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
p&q\\
b&1-b
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad q=1-p,
\]
with stationary hidden row distribution \(\pi\) and nontrivial eigenvalue
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b\).  Let \(\mathsf P_\alpha\) denote the one-sided
sampled hidden Markov law started from an arbitrary hidden distribution
\(\alpha\) at time \(0\), and let \(\mathsf P_\pi\) denote the corresponding
stationary one-sided law.  For every burn-in length \(L\ge0\) and every suffix
length \(m\ge1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-start-suffix-tv-main}
\left\|
\mathsf P_\alpha\{(A_L,\ldots,A_{L+m-1})\in\cdot\}
-
\mathsf P_\pi\{(A_0,\ldots,A_{m-1})\in\cdot\}
\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\|\alpha P^L-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|^L .
\end{equation}
Consequently, if \(L_N\to\infty\) and
\[
L_N=o(\sqrt N),
\qquad
n_N=N-L_N,
\]
then any statistic \(S_{n_N}\) computed only from the post-burn-in suffix
\[
(A_{L_N},\ldots,A_{N-1})
\]
has the following transfer property: if the stationary length-\(n_N\) version
of the statistic converges in distribution to a law \(\mathcal L\), then the
post-burn-in finite-start version also converges in distribution to
\(\mathcal L\).  The same implication holds for convergence in probability to
constants.  In particular the joint CLTs and separated-batch covariance
consistency statements in
\Cref{prop:three-coordinate-vector-batch-main,thm:sampled-finite-record-main}
transfer to post-burn-in finite-start records with the same limiting covariance
matrices.  The normalizations \(\sqrt{n_N}\) and \(\sqrt N\) are equivalent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because \(P\) is a primitive two-state stochastic matrix,
\Cref{lem:stationary-main} gives \(|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|<1\).  On the
one-dimensional subspace of row vectors with total mass zero, right
multiplication by \(P\) is multiplication by \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\).
Hence
\[
\alpha P^L-\pi=(\alpha-\pi)P^L
=\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^L(\alpha-\pi),
\]
and therefore
\[
\|\alpha P^L-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
=|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|^L\|\alpha-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le |\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|^L .
\]

Condition on the hidden state at time \(L\).  The law of the future visible
suffix of length \(m\) is obtained by applying the same Markov kernel
\[
K_m(h,\cdot)
=\mathsf P\{(A_0,\ldots,A_{m-1})\in\cdot\mid H_0=h\},
\qquad h\in\{R,D\},
\]
to the hidden distribution at the suffix start.  Total variation distance
contracts under Markov kernels, so the visible suffix distance is bounded by
\(\|\alpha P^L-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:finite-start-suffix-tv-main}.  The bound is uniform in the suffix
length \(m\).

Now let \(L=L_N\) and \(m=n_N\).  Since \(L_N\to\infty\),
\(|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|^{L_N}\to0\), so the laws of all post-burn-in
statistics and their stationary length-\(n_N\) counterparts are contiguous in
total variation in the elementary sense that expectations of bounded
test functions differ by \(o(1)\).  Hence every stationary weak limit transfers
to the finite-start suffix.  The condition \(L_N=o(\sqrt N)\) implies
\(n_N/N\to1\), and therefore
\(\sqrt N/\sqrt{n_N}\to1\); Slutsky's theorem makes the two root normalizations
interchangeable.  This proves the CLT and covariance-estimator transfer.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Observed lag-two windows in the all-complete-cycle batches]\label{lem:all-cycle-lag-two-windows-main}
Let
\[
0\le \tau_1<\cdots<\tau_{J_N}\le N-1
\]
be the observed click times in a record of length \(N\).  For an integer
\(b_N\ge1\), set
\[
M_N^{\mathrm{all}}
=\left\lfloor\frac{J_N-1}{b_N+1}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
R_N^{\mathrm{all}}=
\begin{cases}
M_N^{\mathrm{all}}b_N+(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}-1),& M_N^{\mathrm{all}}\ge1,\\
0,& M_N^{\mathrm{all}}=0.
\end{cases}
\]
with the convention \(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}=R_N^{\mathrm{all}}=0\) when
\(J_N=0\).
On \(\{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}\ge1\}\),
\[
R_N^{\mathrm{all}}\le J_N-2.
\]
Consequently, for every cycle index \(j\le R_N^{\mathrm{all}}\) and every
\(n\in\{\tau_j,\ldots,\tau_{j+1}-1\}\), the lag-two window used in
\(\widehat Z_{n,N}\) is observed:
\[
n+2\le N-1.
\]
More explicitly, the potentially problematic terminal term has
\(n=\tau_{j+1}-1\) and inspects \(A_{\tau_{j+1}+1}\).  Since
\(j+2\le J_N\), the following complete cycle and its closing click
\(\tau_{j+2}\) are observed, and
\[
\tau_{j+1}+1\le \tau_{j+2}\le N-1.
\]
In particular every summand appearing in the batched rewards
\(\widehat Y_{j,N}^{\mathrm{all}}\), and hence in
\(\widehat B_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}\), is a function only of the recorded symbols
\(A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}\ge1\), then
\[
R_N^{\mathrm{all}}
=M_N^{\mathrm{all}}(b_N+1)-1.
\]
The definition of the floor gives
\[
M_N^{\mathrm{all}}(b_N+1)\le J_N-1,
\]
and therefore \(R_N^{\mathrm{all}}\le J_N-2\).  Now fix
\(j\le R_N^{\mathrm{all}}\) and
\(n\in\{\tau_j,\ldots,\tau_{j+1}-1\}\).  The preceding inequality gives
\(j+1\le J_N-1\) and \(j+2\le J_N\).  In particular the complete cycle after
\(\tau_{j+1}\) is still part of the observed click list.  Since the click times
are strictly increasing integers and \(\tau_{J_N}\le N-1\),
\[
\tau_{j+1}+1\le \tau_{j+2}\le \tau_{J_N}\le N-1.
\]
Hence
\[
n+2\le(\tau_{j+1}-1)+2=\tau_{j+1}+1\le N-1,
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Compact all-complete-cycle renewal-reward input]\label{thm:compact-all-cycle-renewal-reward-main}
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be a stationary binary renewal record whose click
times are regeneration times.  This is the stationary-window probability input;
finite-start sampled boundary-reset records are first reduced to this case by
the burn-in transfer in \Cref{lem:finite-start-burnin-main}.  In the Palm law
started from a click at time \(0\), write
\[
0=T_0<T_1<T_2<\cdots,\qquad L_j=T_{j+1}-T_j,\qquad G_j=L_j-1,
\]
and assume
\[
\PP(G_1\ge k)\le C e^{-\eta k}\qquad(k\ge0).
\]
The observed finite record used by the estimator is indexed by its observed
click times \(0\le\tau_1<\cdots<\tau_{J_N}\le N-1\); every reward included
below is required to be evaluable from \(A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\).  For the
sampled lag-two theorem this observable indexing condition is exactly
\Cref{lem:all-cycle-lag-two-windows-main}.
Let \((Z_n)\) be a bounded stationary local observable with
\(\EE_{\mathrm{stat}}Z_0=0\).  Suppose that, for a fixed integer \(q\ge0\), its
Palm cycle rewards
\[
Y_j=\sum_{n=T_j}^{T_{j+1}-1}Z_n
\]
have the finite-lag representation
\[
Y_j=f(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+q}),\qquad
|f(g_0,\ldots,g_q)|\le C_f\Bigl(1+\sum_{i=0}^q g_i\Bigr).
\]
Put
\[
\mu_L=\EE L_1,\qquad
\tau_q^2=\Var(Y_1)+2\sum_{h=1}^q\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_{1+h}).
\]
Then
\[
\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Z_n
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{\tau_q^2}{\mu_L}\right).
\]
Moreover the all-complete-cycle separated-batch estimator is consistent under
the following fully observed construction.  Let
\[
0\le \tau_1<\cdots<\tau_{J_N}\le N-1
\]
be the observed click times, choose a deterministic batch size
\[
b_N\to\infty,\qquad b_N/N\to0,
\]
and set, with \(b_N+q=b_N\) when \(q=0\),
\[
M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}
=\left\lfloor\frac{J_N-1}{b_N+q}\right\rfloor,\qquad
R_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}
=M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}b_N+(M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}-1)q,
\]
with both quantities interpreted as \(0\) if the displayed batch count is
\(<1\).  The prefix before the first observed click, the suffix after the last
observed click, and the terminal remainder of fewer than \(b_N+q\) complete
cycles are omitted.  For \(1\le r\le M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}\), form separated
batch sums from \(b_N\) included rewards and omit \(q\) rewards between
neighboring batches:
\[
B_{r,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}
=
\sum_{j=(r-1)(b_N+q)+1}^{(r-1)(b_N+q)+b_N}
Y_j^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}.
\]
Assume the local windows needed to evaluate every summand in the included
rewards are contained in \(A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\).  If feasible rewards
\(\widehat Y_{j,N}\) obey the plug-in perturbation bound
\[
|\widehat Y_{j,N}-Y_{j,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}|
\le C\,\delta_N\,(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_j),
\qquad
\delta_N=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}),
\]
uniformly over the batched cycle indices, and if
\(\widehat B_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}\), \(\bar B_N^{\mathrm{all}}\), and
\(\bar L_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}\) are formed from the same separated complete
cycles, then
\[
\frac{1}{\bar L_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}\,b_N\,(M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}-1)}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}}
\bigl(\widehat B_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}-\bar B_N^{\mathrm{all}}\bigr)^2
\to
\frac{\tau_q^2}{\mu_L}
\qquad\text{in probability}.
\]
The estimator may be defined arbitrarily on
\(\{M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}<2\}\), an event whose probability tends to zero.
In the lag-two covariance theorem below, \(q=1\) and
\Cref{lem:all-cycle-lag-two-windows-main} verifies the displayed fully
observed-window assumption.  There the cycle reward is derived by writing the
linearized empirical lag-two covariance as
\[
Z_n=(A_nA_{n+2}-\mu_2)-2\rho(A_n-\rho)
\]
and summing this \(Z_n\) over complete click-to-click cycles; the only
cross-cycle contribution is the terminal \(A_nA_{n+2}\) term, which is why one
cycle reward is omitted between neighboring batches when \(q=1\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the all-complete-cycle part of
\Cref{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main}, specialized to the notation used in
Block~IV.  More explicitly, its preliminary
\Cref{lem:renewal-index-prelim-main} proves that stationary initial and
terminal boundary cycles are size-biased and negligible at root-\(N\) scale,
that the Palm cycle CLT survives the random renewal index by applying
Anscombe's theorem on the finitely many residue classes of the \(q\)-dependent
sequence, and that the random number of all-complete-cycle batches satisfies
the required renewal law of large numbers.  The following separated-batch
lemma in the appendix then proves the triangular-array variance law and the
plug-in perturbation estimate.  The additional observed-window sentence is not
a probabilistic hypothesis: it is the finite-record measurability requirement
that the rewards used by the feasible estimator are functions of the observed
record.  In the sampled lag-two application this requirement is discharged by
\Cref{lem:all-cycle-lag-two-windows-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Self-contained fixed-setting lag-two covariance CLT]\label{cor:boundary-reset-cycle-clt-main}\label{thm:sampled-finite-record-main}
Fix one detector setting
\[
\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0
\]
in the sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset kernels
\eqref{eq:t0-t1-main}, and let
$(A_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}$ be the stationary visible record for this fixed setting.
If the physical record is started from an
arbitrary hidden distribution rather than the stationary hidden law, the
post-burn-in version below applies after discarding \(L_N\to\infty\) samples
with \(L_N=o(\sqrt N)\).  Define
\[
\rho=\EE A_0,
\qquad
\mu_2=\EE(A_0A_2),
\qquad
\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)=\mu_2-\rho^2,
\]
and the centered lag-two linearization
\[
Z_j=(A_jA_{j+2}-\mu_2)-2\rho(A_j-\rho).
\]
Let \(T_0<T_1<T_2<\cdots\) be successive click times in the Palm version of the
record, put
\[
L_j=T_{j+1}-T_j,\qquad G_j=L_j-1,\qquad
Y_j=\sum_{n=T_j}^{T_{j+1}-1}Z_n,
\]
and let \(G\) denote a generic copy of the post-click zero-run length from
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}. Put
\[
\pi_g=\PP(G=g),
\qquad
m_1=\EE G,
\qquad
m_2=\EE G^2,
\]
so that \(\EE(G+1)=\EE L_0=\rho^{-1}\). With
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\) as in \eqref{eq:lambda-hid-main},
\Cref{thm:recovery-main} gives
\[
\gamma_2
=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}},
\]
where \(a=\PP(A_1=1\mid A_0=1)\). Define
\[
\alpha=2\rho^2-\mu_2,
\qquad
\beta=\alpha-2\rho,
\]
and define the explicit sampled-law cycle variance by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau2-boundary-reset-main}
\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2
=
\alpha^2m_2
+2\alpha\beta m_1
+\beta^2
+(2\alpha+2\beta+1)\pi_1
+(4\beta+1)\pi_0^2
+2\pi_0^3.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2=\Var(Y_1)+2\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_2),
\]
where the covariance is taken under the Palm cycle law and the reward \(Y_j\)
is the Palm cycle reward defined above.  Its explicit gap form is derived in
the proof below.  For one observed record
\(A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\), define the full-record centering constants and the
fixed-lag empirical covariance by
\[
\widehat\rho_N=\frac1N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}A_j,
\qquad
\widehat\mu_{2,N}=\frac1{N-2}\sum_{j=0}^{N-3}A_jA_{j+2},
\qquad
\widehat\gamma_2
=
\frac{1}{N-2}\sum_{j=0}^{N-3}
(A_j-\widehat\rho_N)(A_{j+2}-\widehat\rho_N),
\]
for \(N\ge3\).  The variance estimator uses only complete click-to-click cycles
inside the observed record.  Let
\[
0\le \tau_1<\cdots<\tau_{J_N}\le N-1
\]
be the observed click times, choose the deterministic batch size
\[
b_N\in\mathbb N,\qquad
b_N\to\infty,\qquad b_N/N\to0,
\]
for example \(b_N=\max\{1,\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\}\),
and set
\[
M_N^{\mathrm{all}}
=\left\lfloor\frac{J_N-1}{b_N+1}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
R_N^{\mathrm{all}}=
\begin{cases}
M_N^{\mathrm{all}}b_N+(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}-1),& M_N^{\mathrm{all}}\ge1,\\
0,& M_N^{\mathrm{all}}=0.
\end{cases}
\]
On the event \(J_N=0\), interpret \(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}=R_N^{\mathrm{all}}=0\).
The prefix before the first observed click, the suffix after the last observed
click, and the terminal remainder of fewer than \(b_N+1\) complete cycles are
boundary pieces; they are not batched.  If \(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}<2\), put
\(\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}=0\).  On
\(\{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}\ge2\}\), for \(1\le j\le R_N^{\mathrm{all}}\) define
\[
L_{j,N}^{\mathrm{all}}=\tau_{j+1}-\tau_j,
\qquad
\bar L_N^{\mathrm{all}}
=\frac1{R_N^{\mathrm{all}}}\sum_{j=1}^{R_N^{\mathrm{all}}}
L_{j,N}^{\mathrm{all}},
\]
\[
\widehat Z_{n,N}
=(A_nA_{n+2}-\widehat\mu_{2,N})-2\widehat\rho_N(A_n-\widehat\rho_N),
\qquad
\widehat Y_{j,N}^{\mathrm{all}}
=\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}\widehat Z_{n,N},
\]
where \Cref{lem:all-cycle-lag-two-windows-main} ensures that even the terminal
lag-two term in each batched complete cycle lies in the observed record.  Thus
every batched \(\widehat Y_{j,N}^{\mathrm{all}}\) is formed only from recorded
symbols \(A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\), with no unobserved terminal window.  Define
the separated batch sums, omitting one cycle reward between adjacent batches, by
\[
\widehat B_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}
=
\sum_{j=(r-1)(b_N+1)+1}^{(r-1)(b_N+1)+b_N}
\widehat Y_{j,N}^{\mathrm{all}},
\qquad
1\le r\le M_N^{\mathrm{all}},
\]
\[
\bar B_N^{\mathrm{all}}
=
\frac1{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}}\widehat B_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}},
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sampled-clt-all-cycle-estimator-self-contained-main}
\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}
=
\frac{1}{\bar L_N^{\mathrm{all}}\,b_N\,(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}-1)}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}}
\bigl(\widehat B_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}-\bar B_N^{\mathrm{all}}\bigr)^2 .
\end{equation}
This is the same all-complete-cycle estimator as
\eqref{eq:cycle-block-est-all-main}. Then
\[
\sqrt N\,(\widehat\gamma_2-\gamma_2)
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(0,
\frac{\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2}{\EE(G+1)}\right).
\]
\(\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}\) is consistent for the displayed
variance. Moreover, by
\Cref{lem:boundary-reset-tau-positive-main},
\[
\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2>0.
\]
Consequently
\[
\PP\!\left(\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}>0\right)\to1,
\qquad
\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all}}
:=\sqrt{\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}}
\to
\left(\frac{\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2}{\EE(G+1)}\right)^{1/2},
\]
and
\[
\frac{\sqrt N\,(\widehat\gamma_2-\gamma_2)}
{\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all}}}
\Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,1),
\]
and the usual fixed-setting Wald interval for \(\gamma_2\) is justified for all
positive sampled-law parameters.  The closed-form and interval formulas in
\Cref{rem:lag-two-nondeg-main} are optional numerical certificates for concrete
detector settings, not additional hypotheses for studentization in this
sampled-law corollary.
The all-complete-cycle variance consistency uses only
\(b_N\to\infty\) and \(b_N/N\to0\); the displayed \(N^{1/3}\) choice is a
convenient example and is not a hidden growth assumption in the plug-in
centering step.
Combined with \Cref{thm:recovery-main}, the mean being estimated is zero if and
only if
\[
\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau),
\]
while away from that rate its sign is the sign prescribed by the hidden-mode
covariance phase in \Cref{thm:recovery-main}. Thus a one-record Wald diagnostic
for the fixed-setting lag-two covariance is theorem-level justified throughout
the sampled-law parameter space.

The same CLT and studentized limit hold for a finite-start record.  More
precisely, start the hidden chain at any distribution \(\alpha\), choose
\[
L_N\to\infty,\qquad L_N=o(\sqrt N),\qquad n_N=N-L_N,
\]
discard \(A_0,\ldots,A_{L_N-1}\), and apply the displayed definitions to the
suffix
\[
A_{L_N},\ldots,A_{N-1}
\]
with \(n_N\) in place of \(N\) and suffix-relative indices.  Then
\[
\sqrt{n_N}\,
(\widehat\gamma_{2,N}^{\operatorname{burn}}-\gamma_2)
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(0,
\frac{\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2}{\EE(G+1)}\right),
\]
the corresponding all-complete-cycle variance estimator is consistent for the
same variance, and the studentized statistic converges to
\(\mathcal N(0,1)\).  Since \(n_N/N\to1\), the same statements hold with
\(\sqrt N\) replacing \(\sqrt{n_N}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{thm:recovery-main} supplies the renewal input required by
\Cref{thm:compact-all-cycle-renewal-reward-main}: the sampled boundary-reset visible
process is stationary, its observed click times are regeneration times by
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}, and the zero-run law is exactly
$(\pi_g)_{g\ge0}$. Its tail is exponential. Indeed, when
$\kappa_{\mathrm r}\neq\Gamma$, \eqref{eq:gap-closed-main} and
$0<p,c<1$ bound $\PP(G=k)$ by a constant times
$\max\{p,c\}^k$; in the equal-rate case
\eqref{eq:equal-rate-survival-hazard-main} gives
$\PP(G\ge k)=p^k(1+\Gamma\Delta\tau\,k)$, which is again exponentially bounded.

The covariance identity in \Cref{thm:recovery-main} gives
$\gamma_2=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$ and hence
$\mu_2=\rho^2+\gamma_2$.  It remains to identify the \(q=1\) reward required
by the compact theorem.  On the Palm cycle beginning at \(T_j\), the observed
word is
\[
A_{T_j}\cdots A_{T_{j+1}}
=1\,0^{G_j}\,1 .
\]
Inside the half-open cycle \(\{T_j,\ldots,T_{j+1}-1\}\), there are \(G_j+1\)
indices.  Summing the centered linear part gives
\[
\sum_{n=T_j}^{T_{j+1}-1}\{-\mu_2-2\rho(A_n-\rho)\}
=-\mu_2(G_j+1)-2\rho(1-\rho(G_j+1))
=\alpha G_j+\beta ,
\]
where \(\alpha=2\rho^2-\mu_2\) and \(\beta=\alpha-2\rho\).  The remaining
indicator \(\sum A_nA_{n+2}\) has exactly two possible contributions.  First,
the pattern \(101\) across the next click occurs only when \(G_j=1\),
contributing \(\mathbf 1_{\{G_j=1\}}\).  Second, if \(G_j=0\), the window
starting at \(T_j\) crosses two consecutive click intervals and contributes
only when the next gap is also zero, equivalently \(G_j=0\) and
\(G_{j+1}=0\), giving \(\mathbf 1_{\{G_j=0,G_{j+1}=0\}}\).  Thus
\[
Y_j=\alpha G_j+\beta+\mathbf 1_{\{G_j=1\}}
    +\mathbf 1_{\{G_j=0,\ G_{j+1}=0\}}
\]
depends only on \((G_j,G_{j+1})\) and has linear growth.
For completeness we spell out the variance calculation.  In a cycle beginning
with a click and followed by \(G_j\) zeros,
\[
Y_j=\sum_{n=T_j}^{T_{j+1}-1}Z_n,
\qquad
Z_n=(A_nA_{n+2}-\mu_2)-2\rho(A_n-\rho).
\]
Since
\[
Z_n=A_nA_{n+2}-2\rho A_n+(2\rho^2-\mu_2),
\]
and since only the first symbol in the half-open cycle is a click while all
later symbols in the cycle are zeros, the constants
\(\alpha=2\rho^2-\mu_2\) and \(\beta=\alpha-2\rho\) give the displayed
cycle reward.  The term \(\mathbf 1_{\{G_j=1\}}\) is the within-cycle
\(101\) contribution, and
\(\mathbf 1_{\{G_j=0,\ G_{j+1}=0\}}\) is the only lag-two product that crosses
two consecutive cycle boundaries.

Let \(G,H,H'\) be independent copies of the post-click zero-run gap.  Then
\[
Y_1=\alpha G+\beta+\mathbf 1_{\{G=1\}}
    +\mathbf 1_{\{G=0,H=0\}},
\qquad
Y_2=\alpha H+\beta+\mathbf 1_{\{H=1\}}
    +\mathbf 1_{\{H=0,H'=0\}}.
\]
Using \(\pi_g=\PP(G=g)\), \(m_1=\EE G\), \(m_2=\EE G^2\), and
\(\EE Y_1=0\), expansion gives
\[
\EE(Y_1^2)
=
\alpha^2m_2+2\alpha\beta m_1+\beta^2
+(2\alpha+2\beta+1)\pi_1+(2\beta+1)\pi_0^2.
\]
The adjacent rewards share only the middle gap \(H\), and the same expansion
gives
\[
\EE(Y_1Y_2)=\beta\pi_0^2+\pi_0^3.
\]
Therefore
\[
\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2
=\EE(Y_1^2)+2\EE(Y_1Y_2)
=
\alpha^2m_2+2\alpha\beta m_1+\beta^2
+(2\alpha+2\beta+1)\pi_1+(4\beta+1)\pi_0^2+2\pi_0^3,
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:tau2-boundary-reset-main}.
The full-record plug-in centering satisfies the theorem's perturbation condition:
the regenerative CLT applied to the bounded observables \(A_n\) and
\(A_nA_{n+2}\) gives
\[
|\widehat\rho_N-\rho|+|\widehat\mu_{2,N}-\mu_2|=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}),
\]
and boundedness of the binary record gives
\[
|\widehat Y_{j,N}^{\mathrm{all}}-Y_{j,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}|
\le C(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_j)
\bigl(|\widehat\rho_N-\rho|+|\widehat\mu_{2,N}-\mu_2|\bigr).
\]
\Cref{lem:all-cycle-lag-two-windows-main} verifies that all terminal lag-two
windows in the all-complete-cycle batches are inside the observed record.
Thus \Cref{thm:compact-all-cycle-renewal-reward-main} gives both the CLT for
\(\widehat\gamma_2\) and consistency of the displayed all-complete-cycle
variance estimator under the sole batch assumptions \(b_N\to\infty\) and
\(b_N/N\to0\).  \Cref{lem:boundary-reset-tau-positive-main} supplies the
strict positivity needed for the studentized limit. Finally, the equivalence
$\gamma_2=0\iff\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\iff
\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$ and the sign phase away from the
critical rate are precisely the covariance conclusions of
\Cref{thm:recovery-main}. This proves the claimed fixed-setting application of
the generic cycle CLT to the sampled boundary-reset law.

For a finite-start record, \Cref{lem:finite-start-burnin-main} compares the
entire post-burn-in suffix law with the stationary one-sided suffix law in
total variation by at most \(|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|^{L_N}\).  The lag-two
covariance statistic and the all-complete-cycle variance estimator are
measurable functions of that suffix.  Therefore the stationary CLT, variance
consistency, positivity-in-probability, and studentized limit just proved
transfer to the finite-start suffix.  The condition \(L_N=o(\sqrt N)\) gives
\(\sqrt N/\sqrt{n_N}\to1\), so changing the root normalization from
\(\sqrt{n_N}\) to \(\sqrt N\) is another Slutsky step.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-record variance estimator as a specialization of the finite-lag theorem]\label{prop:finite-record-variance-estimator-main}\label{prop:cycle-block-est-all-main}
Under the assumptions of the \(q=1\) lag-two specialization in
\Cref{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main}, let
\(\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}\) be the feasible
all-complete-cycle separated-batch estimator in
\eqref{eq:cycle-block-est-all-main}.  If \(b_N\to\infty\) and \(b_N/N\to0\),
then
\[
\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}
\to
\sigma_2^2
:=
\frac{\Var(Y_1)+2\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_2)}{\EE L_1}
\qquad\text{in probability}.
\]
If \(\sigma_2^2>0\), then
\[
\PP\!\left(\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}>0\right)\to1,
\qquad
\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all}}
:=\sqrt{\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}}
\to\sigma_2
\qquad\text{in probability}.
\]
For the paper's finite-record inference, the implemented estimator is the
observed all-complete-cycle estimator, with one complete-cycle reward omitted
between neighboring batches to remove the \(1\)-dependence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is exactly the separated-batch conclusion of
\Cref{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main} with \(q=1\).  In that specialization
the oracle separated batches are independent after the omitted reward, the
random complete-cycle count satisfies the renewal law of large numbers, and the
plug-in centering perturbation is \(o_{\PP}(1)\) because the centering error is
\(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) and \(b_N/N\to0\).  Positivity transfers to the square
root by the continuous mapping theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Closed-form and interval evaluation of the sampled-law variance]\label{rem:lag-two-nondeg-main}
The finite-lag theorem, \Cref{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main}, reduces the studentization hypothesis to the
finite quantity \eqref{eq:tau2-explicit-main}. The needed inputs are only
\[
\rho,\qquad \mu_2,\qquad \pi_0,\qquad \pi_1,\qquad m_1=\sum_{g\ge0}g\,\pi_g,
\qquad m_2=\sum_{g\ge0}g^2\pi_g,
\]
and all are computable from the explicit zero-run law of
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}. For $\kappa_{\mathrm r}\neq\Gamma$, with
$p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}$, $c=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}$, and
$q=1-p$,
\[
\pi_0=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)-\Gamma(1-c)}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad
\pi_1=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p-\Gamma(1-c)c}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
\[
m_1=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p/(1-p)-\Gamma c/(1-c)}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad
m_2=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p(1+p)/(1-p)^2-\Gamma c(1+c)/(1-c)^2}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}.
\]
The remaining quantities are
\[
b=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(p-c)}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad
\rho=\frac{q(1-c)}{q+b},
\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b,
\]
\[
\mu_2=\rho^2+\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}},
\qquad
a=\pi_0,\qquad
\alpha=2\rho^2-\mu_2,\qquad
\beta=\alpha-2\rho .
\]
At equal rates, write $\eta=\Gamma\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau$ and
$p=e^{-\eta}$. Then
\[
\pi_0=(1-p)-\eta p,
\qquad
\pi_1=p\bigl((1-p)+\eta(1-2p)\bigr),
\]
\[
m_1=\frac{p}{1-p}+\frac{\eta p}{(1-p)^2},
\qquad
m_2=
\frac{p(1+p)}{(1-p)^2}
+\frac{\eta p(1+3p)}{(1-p)^3},
\]
and
\[
b=\eta p,\qquad
\rho=\frac{(1-p)^2}{(1-p)+\eta p},\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p(1-\eta),
\]
with $\mu_2,\alpha,\beta$ then obtained from the same displayed formulas.

These identities give a finite interval certificate for any fixed detector
setting. Use outward-rounded interval arithmetic for the input box
$I_\Gamma\times I_\kappa\times I_{\Delta\tau}\subset(0,\infty)^3$. If
$0\notin I_\kappa-I_\Gamma$, evaluate the distinct-rate formulas above with
interval operations and the interval exponential, obtaining interval enclosures
\[
I_\rho,\ I_{\mu_2},\ I_{\pi_0},\ I_{\pi_1},\ I_{m_1},\ I_{m_2},
\qquad
I_\alpha=2I_\rho^2-I_{\mu_2},\quad
I_\beta=I_\alpha-2I_\rho .
\]
Then enclose the sampled-law cycle variance by
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_{\tau,\mathrm{br}}
={}&
I_\alpha^2 I_{m_2}
+2I_\alpha I_\beta I_{m_1}
+I_\beta^2
+(2I_\alpha+2I_\beta+1)I_{\pi_1}  \\
&\quad
+(4I_\beta+1)I_{\pi_0}^2
+2I_{\pi_0}^3 .
\end{aligned}
\]
If the detector setting lies on the diagonal $\kappa_{\mathrm r}=\Gamma$, use
the equal-rate formulas with an outward-rounded interval $I_\eta$ for the common
product $\Gamma\Delta\tau$ instead. A box that straddles the diagonal may be
split into subboxes or evaluated from the defining integrals for $a,b$ and the
finite geometric sums with interval remainder bounds. In every case,
\[
\inf I_{\tau,\mathrm{br}}>0
\]
is a certificate that \(\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2>0\) on the box, and therefore the
interval computation has numerically certified what
\Cref{lem:boundary-reset-tau-positive-main} proves analytically. If the
enclosure contains zero, the computation is only too coarse; it is not a
failure of the theorem-level studentization condition for the sampled law.

Equivalently, one may evaluate $m_1$ or $m_2$ by truncating at $K$. The tail
error is rigorously controlled by
\[
0\le m_1-\sum_{g=0}^K g\,\pi_g\le \sum_{g>K} g\,\pi_g,
\qquad
0\le m_2-\sum_{g=0}^K g^2\,\pi_g\le \sum_{g>K} g^2\,\pi_g.
\]
Those tail sums are again explicit from the same gap law, so
\eqref{eq:tau2-explicit-main} can also be enclosed by a finite
truncation-and-tail computation before reporting a Wald interval.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-record diagnostics]\label{prop:finite-diagnostics-main}
Fix positive boundary-reset recovery parameters and consider the stationary
record generated by the kernels in~\eqref{eq:t0-t1-main}. For each fixed $k$,
$n$, and $B$, the estimators $\widehat h_k$, $\widehat\gamma_n$, and the
empirical block Fano factor converge almost surely to the corresponding
population quantities as $N\to\infty$; for $\widehat h_k$, the denominator is
eventually nonzero almost surely because the zero-run law has full support.
If the hidden chain is instead started from an arbitrary initial distribution,
discard any deterministic burn-in \(L_N\to\infty\) with \(L_N=o(\sqrt N)\) and
compute the same fixed-lag and all-complete-cycle lag-two statistics on the
suffix \(A_{L_N},\ldots,A_{N-1}\).  The post-burn-in lag-two CLT,
studentized interval, and consistency statements below are then unchanged by
\Cref{lem:finite-start-burnin-main}; the population quantities remain those of
the stationary law for the fixed detector setting.
Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item The sign of $\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)$ is the opposite of the sign of $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$, and $\gamma_2=0$ if and only if $\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$.
\item The zero of the fixed-design population diagnostic is simple as a
function of the attempt rate \(\Gamma\) with
\((\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) held fixed:
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial\Gamma}
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}(\Gamma_c)\neq0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial}{\partial\Gamma}
\gamma_2(\Gamma_c)
=
\rho_c(a_c-\rho_c)
\frac{\partial}{\partial\Gamma}\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}(\Gamma_c)
\neq0 .
\]
Here \(\rho_c=\rho(\Gamma_c)>0\), \(a_c=a(\Gamma_c)\), and
\(a_c-\rho_c<0\).  This is only a population derivative inside the single
sampled-counter family.  It is not a scan theorem, contains no neighboring
setting data, and gives no estimator or confidence set for a crossing point.
\item The sampled-law positivity lemma
\Cref{lem:boundary-reset-tau-positive-main} verifies the nondegeneracy
criterion \eqref{eq:tau2-explicit-main} from the proof package; equivalently,
\[
\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sigma_2^2>0.
\]
Therefore the sampled finite-record theorem
\Cref{thm:sampled-finite-record-main} yields an asymptotic two-sided
$(1-\alpha)$ confidence interval for $\gamma_2$:
\[
\widehat\gamma_2\pm
z_{1-\alpha/2}\frac{\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all}}}{\sqrt N}.
\]
For a finite-start record, the same display is read after replacing
\(N\), \(\widehat\gamma_2\), and
\(\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all}}\) by their post-burn-in suffix versions
with \(n_N=N-L_N\); equivalently one may keep \(\sqrt N\) because
\(n_N/N\to1\).
This interval is a fixed-setting statement: it applies to $\gamma_2$ at the
chosen detector design.  A scan-root theorem would be a different theorem.  It
would require, at minimum, a design such as
\[
s_i=s_*+h_Nx_i,\qquad h_N\to0,
\]
independent records of lengths \(N_i\), a joint CLT for the estimators at those
settings, a local expansion
\[
\gamma_2(s)=\gamma_2'(s_*)(s-s_*)+O((s-s_*)^2),
\qquad \gamma_2'(s_*)\ne0,
\]
and balance conditions for the chosen local estimator, for example
\[
N_{\rm eff}^{1/2}h_N^2\to0,
\qquad
N_{\rm eff}^{1/2}h_N\to\infty
\]
in the standard local-linear scaling.  No such scan design, slope-estimation
rule, or joint scan CLT is assumed or proved here.  Hence no crossing-point
estimator, response-level-set confidence set, or spatial-location confidence
set follows.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The click times are regeneration times by Lemma~\ref{lem:renewal-gap-main}, and the zero-run law has exponential tails because it is a finite linear combination of $p^k$ and $c^k$ with $0<p,c<1$; the equal-rate limit has the same exponential tail with a polynomial prefactor. Standard renewal-reward strong laws therefore give almost-sure convergence of fixed-gap empirical hazards, fixed-lag covariance estimators, and fixed-block counting statistics \cite{Asmussen2003}. For $\widehat h_k$, the completed gaps are i.i.d.; since $\PP(G\ge k)>0$, the denominator count diverges almost surely.

For the sign statement, \Cref{thm:recovery-main} gives
\[
\gamma_2=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}} .
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:stationary-main} gives $\rho>0$ and $a-\rho<0$, so $\gamma_2$ has the opposite sign from $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-rate-main} says that $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$ has the unique zero $\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$ and changes sign there, proving (i).

For (ii), write $s_0=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau$ and
$x=\Gamma/\kappa_{\mathrm r}$. Away from $x=1$,
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}(\Gamma)=\frac{e^{-s_0}\bigl(1-e^{H_{s_0}(x)}\bigr)}{1-x},
\qquad
H_{s_0}(x)=\log x-s_0(x-1).
\]
If $s_0\neq1$, then the critical point $x_c=\Gamma_c/\kappa_{\mathrm r}$ is not $1$, and it cannot satisfy $H_{s_0}'(x_c)=0$: otherwise $x_c=1/s_0$, but then
\[
H_{s_0}(1/s_0)=s_0-1-\log s_0>0,
\]
contradicting $H_{s_0}(x_c)=0$. Hence $H_{s_0}'(x_c)\neq0$, so differentiation of the displayed quotient gives
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}'(\Gamma_c)=
-\frac{e^{-s_0}H_{s_0}'(x_c)}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-x_c)}
\neq0.
\]
If $s_0=1$, then $\Gamma_c=\kappa_{\mathrm r}$. Here
$s_0=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau$ is held fixed while $\Gamma$ varies, so this
derivative is not the derivative of the diagonal equal-rate family
$\Gamma\mapsto e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}(1-\Gamma\Delta\tau)$. Instead one must
differentiate the fixed-$(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$ quotient at $x=1$:
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}(\Gamma)
=\frac{e^{-1}-x e^{-x}}{1-x},
\qquad
x=\frac{\Gamma}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}}.
\]
Writing $x=1+\delta$ gives
\[
x e^{-x}
=e^{-1}(1+\delta)e^{-\delta}
=e^{-1}\left(1-\frac{\delta^2}{2}+O(\delta^3)\right),
\]
hence
\[
e^{-1}-x e^{-x}
=\frac{e^{-1}}{2}\delta^2+O(\delta^3).
\]
Since $1-x=-\delta$, we obtain
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}(\Gamma)
=-\frac{e^{-1}}{2}\left(\frac{\Gamma}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}}-1\right)
+O\!\left(\left(\frac{\Gamma}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}}-1\right)^2\right),
\]
so
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}'(\Gamma_c)
=-\frac{e^{-1}}{2\kappa_{\mathrm r}}\neq0.
\]
Thus, when $s_0=1$ and $\Gamma$ increases through
$\Gamma_c=\kappa_{\mathrm r}$, the hidden mode decreases through zero:
$\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}>0$ for $\Gamma<\Gamma_c$ and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}<0$ for $\Gamma>\Gamma_c$. Since
$\gamma_2=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$ with $\rho(a-\rho)<0$, the
lag-two covariance increases through zero instead:
$\gamma_2<0$ for $\Gamma<\Gamma_c$ and $\gamma_2>0$ for $\Gamma>\Gamma_c$.
Therefore $\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}$ has a simple zero at $\Gamma_c$ for every
fixed $(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$.
The derivative of
\(\gamma_2(\Gamma)=\rho(\Gamma)(a(\Gamma)-\rho(\Gamma))
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}(\Gamma)\) at \(\Gamma_c\) is therefore just the
prefactor times \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}'(\Gamma_c)\), because the derivative
of the prefactor is multiplied by
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}(\Gamma_c)=0\).  Since
\(\rho_c>0\), \(a_c-\rho_c<0\), and
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}'(\Gamma_c)\neq0\), this derivative is nonzero.  This
proves the fixed-family population simplicity statement in part~(ii).
Item (iii) is the fixed-setting studentized confidence interval from
\Cref{thm:sampled-finite-record-main}.  The explicit positivity check
\eqref{eq:tau2-explicit-main} is supplied analytically for the sampled law by
\Cref{lem:boundary-reset-tau-positive-main}.

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[What one fixed record estimates]\label{thm:one-record-estimates-main}
Fix one sampled boundary-reset detector design
\[
(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\in(0,\infty)^3
\]
and observe one click record \((A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1})\) from that design.  If the
hidden chain is stationary, use the full record.  If it is started from an
arbitrary initial hidden distribution, first choose and discard a deterministic
burn-in
\[
L_N\to\infty,\qquad L_N=o(\sqrt N),
\qquad n_N=N-L_N,
\]
and use the suffix \(A_{L_N},\ldots,A_{N-1}\).  Form the centered lag-two
covariance \(\widehat\gamma_2\) and the all-complete-cycle separated batch
estimator \(\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}\) exactly as in
\Cref{thm:sampled-finite-record-main}, with \(N\) replaced by \(n_N\) in the
finite-start suffix case.  In the stationary full-record case one may take
\[
b_N=\max\{1,\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\},
\qquad b_N\to\infty,\qquad b_N/N\to0,
\]
and in the finite-start suffix case the same formula is used with \(n_N\) in
place of \(N\).
Only complete click-to-click cycles are batched, one cycle reward is omitted
between adjacent batches, and the initial prefix, terminal suffix, and final
incomplete batch of fewer than \(b_N+1\) complete cycles are boundary remainders.
The observed-window indexing needed for these batched rewards is exactly
\Cref{lem:all-cycle-lag-two-windows-main}: it proves
\(R_N^{\mathrm{all}}\le J_N-2\), hence every terminal lag-two term in a batched
cycle lies inside \(A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\).
The finite-record CLT estimates only this fixed-setting \(\gamma_2\). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item The resulting CLT estimates only the fixed-setting covariance
\[
\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)
\]
and its asymptotic variance
\[
\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2/\EE(G+1),
\]
where \(G\) is the post-click zero-run length. Its studentized interval is a
confidence interval for this fixed-setting $\gamma_2$ and for no other quantity.
In the sampled boundary-reset law studied here, nondegeneracy is discharged
analytically by \Cref{lem:boundary-reset-tau-positive-main}, so
\(\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}>0\) with probability tending to one.
For finite-start records the same statement applies to the burn-in suffix by
\Cref{lem:finite-start-burnin-main}; \(n_N/N\to1\) makes the choice of
\(\sqrt{n_N}\) or \(\sqrt N\) asymptotically immaterial.
\item \Cref{prop:finite-diagnostics-main}(ii) adds only a fixed-family
population simplicity statement for the zero of \(\gamma_2\) as a function of
\(\Gamma\).  It is not a scan-data theorem and contains no crossing-point
estimator.  In particular, it assumes no design
\(s_i=s_*+h_Nx_i\), no independent neighboring-setting records, no local-linear
root estimator, no scan balance conditions such as
\(N_{\rm eff}^{1/2}h_N^2\to0\) and
\(N_{\rm eff}^{1/2}h_N\to\infty\), and no joint CLT across settings.
\item Response-locus, denominator-normalized, and ratio-data asymptotics
require separate calibrated response or ratio-data inputs.
\item The visible-law quotient inverse is a separate record-side calculation:
\Cref{prop:three-coordinate-vector-batch-main,cor:finite_inverse_delta_boundary_main}
give a regenerative covariance estimator and delta method for the unordered
survival factors \(\{p,c\}\), off the diagonal \(p=c\).  That statement still
does not label one root as the attempt rate.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Items~(i)--(iii) are exactly the separation of roles established by
\Cref{thm:sampled-finite-record-main,prop:finite-diagnostics-main}.
Item~(iv) is the standing scope condition used to keep the single-record CLT
separate from any external ratio-data or scan-data theorem.
\end{proof}

For the memory-transition readout, the primary one-record diagnostic is
therefore the sign of $\widehat\gamma_2$. In the model,
\[
\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}},
\]
with $\rho>0$ and $a-\rho<0$. Thus the lag-one covariance
$\gamma_1=\rho(a-\rho)$ remains negative, and
\Cref{prop:finite-diagnostics-main,thm:one-record-estimates-main} make the
lag-two zero the finite-record diagnostic for the memory threshold. The
fixed-setting confidence interval for $\gamma_2$ is available throughout the
positive sampled-law parameter space because
\Cref{lem:boundary-reset-tau-positive-main} proves
\(\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2>0\) analytically.

The same data should also pass two consistency checks: the empirical hazards
$\widehat h_k$ should increase over the reliable range of observed gaps, and
the lag profile should be compatible with the one-mode form
$\widehat\gamma_n\approx \widehat\gamma_1\widehat\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}$ away from
sampling noise. These checks are not additional response-level equations; they test
whether the sampled boundary-reset recovery readout model is the right
effective description of the observed record.

\Cref{thm:sampled-finite-record-main} supplies the lag-two variance estimate,
and \Cref{lem:boundary-reset-tau-positive-main} supplies the sampled-law
positivity needed for studentization. \Cref{rem:lag-two-nondeg-main} only
explains how to compute or enclose that finite quantity in practice. Other
design-specific observables still require the corresponding regenerative
variance estimate or a Monte Carlo calibration, and no universal sample-size
bound follows from the closed-form law alone.

For the ideal-benchmark response locus in the ideal-blanking mode, the
finite-sample test is different because the benchmark is a Markov law on a
restricted support.

\begin{proposition}[Exact-clock finite-record diagnostic]\label{prop:ideal-diagnostics-main}
Assume the ideal one-step blanking model of Proposition~\ref{prop:ideal-main}, with transition parameter $p\in(0,1)$ and $q=1-p$. Then
\[
\PP(A_j=1,A_{j+1}=1)=0\qquad(j\in\mathbb Z).
\]
For $N\ge2$, define
\[
\widehat p_N=
\frac{\#\{0\le j\le N-2:(A_j,A_{j+1})=(0,0)\}}
{\#\{0\le j\le N-2:A_j=0\}}
\]
whenever the denominator is nonzero. Then the denominator is eventually positive almost surely,
\[
\widehat p_N\to p
\qquad\text{almost surely,}
\]
and
\[
\sqrt N\,(\widehat p_N-p)
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\sigma_p^2),
\qquad
\sigma_p^2=p(1-p)(2-p).
\]
Hence the plug-in estimate
\[
\widehat\sigma_{p,N}^2=\widehat p_N(1-\widehat p_N)(2-\widehat p_N)
\]
is consistent, and under the ideal-support model an asymptotic two-sided $(1-\alpha)$ confidence interval for $p$ is
\[
\widehat p_N\pm
z_{1-\alpha/2}\frac{\widehat\sigma_{p,N}}{\sqrt N}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The support statement is immediate from the ideal blanking rule: after a click
the next sample is deterministically $0$.

Write
\[
C_N=\#\{0\le j\le N-2:A_j=0\},
\qquad
D_N=\#\{0\le j\le N-2:(A_j,A_{j+1})=(0,0)\},
\]
so that $\widehat p_N=D_N/C_N$ whenever $C_N>0$.

Let $\cdots<T_0<T_1<T_2<\cdots$ be the successive click times, indexed so that
$T_0<0\le T_1$. Under the ideal model the inter-click zero counts
\[
K_r=T_{r+1}-T_r-1
\]
are i.i.d.\ with
\[
\PP(K_r=k)=p^{k-1}q,
\qquad k\ge1,
\]
because after the forced post-click zero, each further zero is followed by
another zero with probability $p$ and by a click with probability $q$. For one
complete inter-click cycle, let
\[
U_r=K_r,
\qquad
V_r=K_r-1,
\qquad
L_r=K_r+1.
\]
Then $U_r$ is the number of zero-start transitions in the cycle, $V_r$ is the
number of $(0,0)$ transitions, and $L_r$ is the cycle length in samples. Their
moments are
\[
\EE U_r=\frac1q,
\qquad
\EE V_r=\frac{p}{q},
\qquad
\EE L_r=\frac{2-p}{q},
\]
and for the centered reward
\[
W_r:=V_r-pU_r=(1-p)K_r-1=qK_r-1
\]
one has
\[
\EE W_r=0,
\qquad
\Var(W_r)=q^2\Var(K_r)=p.
\]

Let
\[
M_N=
\begin{cases}
\max\{r\ge1:T_r\le N-1\},& T_1\le N-1,\\
0,& T_1>N-1.
\end{cases}
\]
Then the complete cycles fully contained in $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ are exactly
$r=1,\dots,M_N-1$. Hence there are remainder terms $R_N^{(U)}$ and
$R_N^{(V)}$ such that
\[
C_N=\sum_{r=1}^{M_N-1}U_r+R_N^{(U)},
\qquad
D_N=\sum_{r=1}^{M_N-1}V_r+R_N^{(V)}.
\]
The initial incomplete segment $[0,T_1-1]$ contributes at most $T_1$ zero-start
or $(0,0)$ transitions, and, when $M_N\ge1$, the terminal incomplete segment
inside the cycle that starts at $T_{M_N}$ contributes at most $L_{M_N}$ such
transitions. Thus
\[
|R_N^{(U)}|+|R_N^{(V)}|
\le 2B_N,
\qquad
B_N:=T_1+\mathbf 1_{\{M_N\ge1\}}L_{M_N}.
\]

The boundary variable $B_N$ has a uniform exponential tail. Indeed, if
\[
\pi_0=\PP(A_0=0)=\frac{1}{2-p},
\]
then for every $m\ge1$,
\[
\PP(T_1\ge m)=\PP(A_0=\cdots=A_{m-1}=0)=\pi_0 p^{m-1}.
\]
The terminal incomplete segment is different: it lies in the stationary cycle
straddling the deterministic time $N-1$, so its law is size-biased rather than
Palm-distributed. Let $L^\ast$ denote the length of the stationary cycle
containing the origin. Then
\[
\PP(L_r=\ell)=q p^{\ell-2},
\qquad \ell\ge2,
\qquad
\EE L_r=\frac{2-p}{q},
\]
so
\[
\PP(L^\ast=\ell)
=
\frac{\ell\,\PP(L_r=\ell)}{\EE L_r}
=
\frac{\ell q^2}{2-p}\,p^{\ell-2},
\qquad \ell\ge2,
\]
and therefore, for every $m\ge2$,
\[
\PP(L^\ast\ge m)
=
\frac{\EE[L_r\mathbf 1_{\{L_r\ge m\}}]}{\EE L_r}
=
\frac{q^2}{2-p}\sum_{\ell=m}^\infty \ell p^{\ell-2}
\]
The geometric-series identity
\[
\sum_{\ell=m}^\infty \ell p^{\ell-2}
=
p^{m-2}\frac{qm+p}{q^2}
\]
therefore gives the exact size-biased tail formula
\[
\PP(L^\ast\ge m)
=
p^{m-2}\frac{(1-p)m+p}{2-p}.
\]
In particular, for every
$\eta<-\log p$ there is a constant $C_\eta<\infty$ such that
\[
\PP(L^\ast\ge m)\le C_\eta e^{-\eta m}
\qquad(m\ge2).
\]
Now $T_1\le L^\ast$, and by stationarity the cycle containing $N-1$ has the
same law as $L^\ast$; on $\{M_N\ge1\}$ its length is exactly
$L_{M_N}=T_{M_N+1}-T_{M_N}$. Hence, uniformly in $N$,
\[
\PP(\mathbf 1_{\{M_N\ge1\}}L_{M_N}\ge m)\le \PP(L^\ast\ge m)
\le C_\eta e^{-\eta m}
\qquad(m\ge2).
\]
Therefore there exist constants $C,\eta>0$, independent of $N$, such that
\[
\PP(B_N>x)\le C e^{-\eta x}
\qquad(x\ge0).
\]
By Borel--Cantelli, $B_N=O(\log N)$ almost surely eventually; in particular
$B_N=o_{\PP}(\sqrt N)$.

Since $U_r$, $V_r$, and $L_r$ have finite means, the renewal strong law gives
\[
\frac{M_N}{N}\to\frac{1}{\EE L_r},
\qquad
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{r=1}^{M_N-1}U_r\to\frac{\EE U_r}{\EE L_r},
\qquad
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{r=1}^{M_N-1}V_r\to\frac{\EE V_r}{\EE L_r}
\qquad\text{almost surely.}
\]
Using the remainder bound above,
\[
\frac{C_N}{N}\to\frac{\EE U_r}{\EE L_r}=\frac{1}{2-p}>0,
\qquad
\frac{D_N}{N}\to\frac{\EE V_r}{\EE L_r}=\frac{p}{2-p}
\qquad\text{almost surely,}
\]
so the denominator is eventually positive almost surely and
\[
\widehat p_N=\frac{D_N/N}{C_N/N}\to p
\qquad\text{almost surely.}
\]

For the fluctuation scale,
\[
D_N-pC_N=\sum_{r=1}^{M_N-1}W_r+R_N^{(W)},
\qquad
R_N^{(W)}:=R_N^{(V)}-pR_N^{(U)},
\]
and hence $|R_N^{(W)}|\le 2(1+p)B_N=o_{\PP}(\sqrt N)$. The regenerative CLT for
the centered cycle reward $W_r$ now gives \cite{Asmussen2003}
\[
\frac{1}{\sqrt N}\sum_{r=1}^{M_N-1}W_r
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{\Var(W_r)}{\EE L_r}\right)
=
\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{p}{\EE L_r}\right).
\]
Since $C_N/N\to \EE U_r/\EE L_r$ almost surely, Slutsky's theorem yields
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat p_N-p)
=
\frac{N^{-1/2}(D_N-pC_N)}{C_N/N}
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(0,\,
\frac{\EE L_r\,\Var(W_r)}{(\EE U_r)^2}\right).
\]
Substituting the displayed moments gives
\[
\frac{\EE L_r\,\Var(W_r)}{(\EE U_r)^2}
=
\frac{(2-p)q^{-1}\cdot p}{q^{-2}}
=
p\,q(2-p)
=
p(1-p)(2-p),
\]
which is the stated variance. Consistency of $\widehat\sigma_{p,N}^2$ follows
from $\widehat p_N\to p$ almost surely.
\end{proof}

In practice the exact-clock test is therefore two-stage: first verify that adjacent clicks are absent up to the experimental tolerance, and then test the benchmark value $p=\varphi^{-1}$ using Proposition~\ref{prop:ideal-diagnostics-main}. This exact-clock diagnostic should not be mixed with the sampled boundary-reset recovery one: the former tests a support-and-transition benchmark, while the latter tests an exact-dependence crossing inside a renewal class whose zero-run law has full support and hence supports every finite binary word. Any design calculation for the exact-clock benchmark must use the variance $\sigma_p^2$ above together with the desired confidence level and power; no universal sample-size rule is asserted here.
