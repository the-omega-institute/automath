\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Theorems~F and G make the collision experiment, rather than the order-two
quotient, the mathematical centre of the article.  For an isolated repeated
rate in a fixed-order sampled generalized-Erlang renewal, the stationary
binary record has a uniform Gaussian half-space experiment in the squared
separation coordinate.  The sampled fourth-order pole proves uniform
nuisance-adjusted information, the residualized score attains the one-sided
power envelope, and the finite-tail recurrence estimator and contiguity
argument match at \(N^{-1/4}\).  The two-state result is the prerequisite
special case, not the endpoint of the extension.

At the field level, \Cref{thm:stationary-renewal-palm-equivalence-main} is
logically prior to that application.  It equates the local stationary-window
experiment with an undershot fixed-size Palm-interarrival experiment in both
deficiency directions, transferring bounded-loss decision theory and
semiparametric efficiency rather than only one Gaussian likelihood expansion.

The rank-one marked reset turns the visible output of the constrained D-MAP
into a renewal process.  Here the restart law is the point mass at \(D\), but
a fixed nondegenerate restart law would regenerate as well.  This structure
is the source of both the explicit record law and the low-dimensional quotient
inverse.  Arbitrary leakage into the ready state no longer guarantees renewal;
the proof fails when the normalized post-click law depends on the pre-click
state.  Rank-one leakage with mass on both states remains regenerative.

The visible law determines the unordered survival pair, not the hidden labels.
The two-state fibre result makes that distinction concrete.  Away from the
exchange diagonal, weakly equivalent stochastic realizations in the
killed-reset cone form a closed arc with the two triangular rate orderings as
endpoints.  On the diagonal the arc collapses.  In higher state dimension the
\((n-1)^2\) orbit calculation applies to the minimal interior stratum and is
not a statement about the global dimension of every visible fibre.

The lettered two-state inferential statements A--E remain downstream of the
population algebra.  Theorem~C estimates the three inclusion coordinates;
Theorem~D checks the complete visible law under its stated null and alternative
classes; Theorem~E transports Theorem~C through the stable quotient chart of
Theorem~B.  Sorted roots and log-rates are ordinary delta-method consequences.
The physical-image test is a separate finite-dimensional diagnostic.  The
abstract semiparametric, moving-dimensional coupling, complete-Palm-gap split,
and projection calculations are supplementary and are not used to replace the
stationary likelihood proof in Theorems~F--G.

\section{Comparison with prior representation and tangent results}
\label{sec:theorem-led-comparison}

\Cref{tab:priority-comparison-main} states the comparison at the formula level.
The middle column identifies the specialization actually used; the last column
states only what remains after that specialization.  In particular, the table
does not treat a change of notation or a standard delta method as a
contribution.

Two statistical resolution comparators are especially close to the visible
\(N^{-1/4}\) rate but do not generate it for this experiment.
Batenkov--Goldman--Yomdin obtain cluster-size-dependent minimax location and
amplitude errors for point sources observed through bandlimited spectral data
with bounded deterministic error
\cite{BatenkovGoldmanYomdin2021SuperResolution}.  Kulaitis--Munk--Werner pose
one blurred source against two equal-intensity sources and derive minimax
detection boundaries under Poisson, variance-stabilized Gaussian, and
homogeneous Gaussian microscopy models
\cite{KulaitisMunkWerner2021Resolution}.  Their Poisson and
variance-stabilized Gaussian separation is proportional to the inverse fourth
root of illumination.  The common fourth-root behavior reflects quadratic
loss of first-order separation information, but the observation operators,
noise laws, parameter classes, and losses differ from the stationary renewal
record.  Neither super-resolution minimax bound transfers to this paper.

To our knowledge, no previous result gives the stationary finite-window LAN
experiment at an isolated rate collision for a sampled generalized-Erlang
renewal record, together with sampled pole-order information nondegeneracy and
matching \(N^{-1/4}\) unordered-rate recovery.  The boundary Gaussian
experiment, singular-rate phenomenon, confluent Prony algebra, and phase-type
representation ingredients each have substantial antecedents cited below;
the contribution is their model-specific stationary-record synthesis and
proof.

The general renewal equivalence theorem removes the stationary-window and
random-cycle-count difficulty, but it does not broaden the singular geometry.
Theorem~G remains a fixed-serial-order application with one isolated real
double pole, all other poles simple and uniformly separated, a known sampling
interval, and a special positive realization in which every phase is
traversed.  The paper does not characterize when a general phase-type or
matrix-exponential renewal law has a quadratic identifiable collision
coordinate, what happens under residue cancellation, how positivity
restricts root splitting, or which minimax rates occur for multiple,
defective, or complex pole clusters.  Those questions require
positive-realization and representation geometry in addition to rational
pole algebra.  Thus the stationary-renewal experiment transport is broad,
while the singular application deliberately remains confined to the serial
generalized-Erlang family.  General acyclic phase-type and
matrix-exponential models can add positive-realization constraints, pole
cancellation, non-IFR tails, and parameter-dependent hidden paths; treating
them would be a separate representation-and-singularity project.  Likewise,
Markov-renewal and generic MAP observations require a different likelihood
argument because their observed cycles are not i.i.d. Palm interarrivals.

\subsection{Sampled-counter interpretation}

Continuous dead-time and exponential-recovery detector models motivate the
two-state parametrization
\cite{YuFessler2000,SarbaziSafariHaas2018LongDeadTime,
KrauseWalenta2025SPADRecovery}.  They do not provide the theorem object here:
continuous within-bin recovery after a click can give a marked transition into
the ready state, whereas the present D-MAP latches every click in the dead
state until the boundary.  The sampled counter is therefore an interpretation
of the constrained kernel, not an independently analysed physical system.
A different within-bin protocol would require an explicit check that its
marked kernel retains rank one before the renewal arguments could be used.
