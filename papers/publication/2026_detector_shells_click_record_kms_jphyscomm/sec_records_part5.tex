\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed formula for \(U_j\) is the only model-specific input.  It is the
adjacent-gap form of the three inclusion rewards: a half-open Palm cycle
contains the click at its left endpoint, a lag-one inclusion occurs exactly on
\(G_j=0\), and a lag-two inclusion occurs on \(G_j=1\) or on two consecutive
zero gaps.  Palm inversion for these local windows gives
\(\EE U_j^{(m)}=r_m\EE L_j\), hence \(\EE Y_j=0\).  Because
\(Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1})\) and the gaps are i.i.d., \((Y_j)\) is \(1\)-dependent,
and the exponential gap moment from
\Cref{lem:killed-leakage-gap-exponential-moment-main} gives the fourth moments
required by the master covariance theorem.

The concrete instantiation of
\Cref{thm:main-calendar-stopped-separated-batch-plugin} is
\[
d=3,\qquad T_1=T_1^{(r)},\qquad
\Delta_N=\Rinch_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-\Rinc .
\]
The common complete-cycle set
\(J_N=J_{0,N}\cap J_{1,N}\cap J_{2,N}\) and Algorithm~1's requirement that
every batch be contained in \(J_N\) are precisely the stopped condition
\((M-1)(b_N+1)+b_N\le K_N-1\).  Finally,
\Cref{lem:three-inclusion-clt-reduction-main} gives the plug-in scale
\(\Delta_N=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), and
\Cref{lem:finite-record-reporting-gate-main} gives
\(\PP(\mathcal E_N^c)\to0\).  Applying the master theorem with these
identifications proves the displayed rate for the reported covariance object,
including the empirical batch mean, the multiplier \(K_N/N\), the random
calendar-stopped batch count, and the plug-in rewards.  The auxiliary zero
completion has the same probability rate because the withholding event has
vanishing probability.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Off-diagonal gap-law formula for the inclusion covariance]\label{lem:off-diagonal-inclusion-covariance-gap-sums-main}
Work under the positive constrained D-MAP record model away from the exchange
diagonal, so that the two survival roots \(p\ne s\) are distinct.  Let
\[
g_k=\PP(G=k),\qquad k\ge0,
\]
be the Palm gap law, equivalently the off-diagonal closed law
\eqref{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main}.  Put
\[
\mu=\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)g_k,\qquad
r=\frac1\mu(1,g_0,g_1+g_0^2)^\top .
\]
For \(i,j\ge0\), define the two-gap reward kernel
\[
u(i,j)=
\left(1,\mathbf1_{\{i=0\}},
\mathbf1_{\{i=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{i=0,j=0\}}\right)^\top,
\qquad
y(i,j)=u(i,j)-r(i+1).
\]
Finally set
\[
\bar y(j):=\sum_{k\ge0}y(j,k)g_k
=
\left(
1-r_0(j+1),\
\mathbf1_{\{j=0\}}-r_1(j+1),\
\mathbf1_{\{j=1\}}+g_0\mathbf1_{\{j=0\}}-r_2(j+1)
\right)^\top .
\]
Then the cycle-time long-run covariance in
\Cref{prop:exact-inclusion-separated-batch-covariance-main} has the explicit
gap-law representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:off-diagonal-inclusion-covariance-gap-sums-main}
T_1^{(r)}=V_g+C_g+C_g^\top ,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
V_g&=\sum_{i,j\ge0} y(i,j)y(i,j)^\top g_i g_j,
\label{eq:off-diagonal-inclusion-var-gap-sum-main}\\
C_g&=\sum_{i,j\ge0} y(i,j)\bar y(j)^\top g_i g_j .
\label{eq:off-diagonal-inclusion-adjacent-gap-sum-main}
\end{align}
Equivalently, entry by entry,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:off-diagonal-inclusion-entry-gap-sum-main}
[T_1^{(r)}]_{\alpha\beta}
=\sum_{i,j\ge0}y_\alpha(i,j)y_\beta(i,j)g_i g_j
+\sum_{i,j\ge0}
\{y_\alpha(i,j)\bar y_\beta(j)+\bar y_\alpha(j)y_\beta(i,j)\}g_i g_j .
\end{equation}
All finite-dimensional sums in
\eqref{eq:off-diagonal-inclusion-var-gap-sum-main}--\eqref{eq:off-diagonal-inclusion-entry-gap-sum-main}
are absolutely convergent.  Since the off-diagonal law
\eqref{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main} is a finite linear combination of
\(p^k\) and \(s^k\), these finite and double sums reduce, if desired, to
ordinary geometric moment sums; no Monte Carlo or covariance-tail truncation is
part of the definition.  The retained-record covariance in Theorem~C is
\[
\Sigma_r=\frac{T_1^{(r)}}{\mu}.
\]
Equivalently, let \(G_0,G_1,G_2\) be i.i.d. with law \(g\), put
\(L_i=G_i+1\), and set
\[
A=\EE\{u(G_0,G_1)u(G_0,G_1)^\top\},\qquad
B=\EE\{u(G_0,G_1)u(G_1,G_2)^\top\},
\]
\[
e=\EE\{L_0u(G_0,G_1)\},\qquad
d=\EE\{L_1u(G_0,G_1)\},\qquad
\nu=\EE L_0^2 .
\]
Then the same covariance is the reproducible matrix formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:off-diagonal-inclusion-covariance-ABednu-main}
\Sigma_r
=\frac1\mu\{A-er^\top-re^\top+\nu rr^\top
+B-dr^\top+B^\top-rd^\top\}.
\end{equation}
In summation form,
\[
A=\sum_{i,j\ge0}u(i,j)u(i,j)^\top g_i g_j,\qquad
B=\sum_{i,j,k\ge0}u(i,j)u(j,k)^\top g_i g_j g_k,
\]
\[
e=\sum_{i,j\ge0}(i+1)u(i,j)g_i g_j,\qquad
d=\sum_{i,j\ge0}(j+1)u(i,j)g_i g_j,\qquad
\nu=\sum_{i\ge0}(i+1)^2g_i .
\]
These sums also converge absolutely by the exponential gap tail.  Thus the
off-diagonal covariance is determined for every positive
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\rm r},\Delta\tau)\) away from the exchange diagonal by
gap-law sums alone.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The complete-cycle reward identities in
\eqref{eq:inclusion-cycle-reward-identities-main} give
\[
Y_j=y(G_j,G_{j+1}).
\]
The Palm inversion identity used in
\Cref{prop:exact-inclusion-separated-batch-covariance-main} gives
\(\EE Y_j=0\), and the displayed formula for \(r\) follows from
\[
\EE U_j=(1,g_0,g_1+g_0^2)^\top .
\]
Because the gaps are i.i.d.,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y_0)=\EE(Y_0Y_0^\top)
=\sum_{i,j\ge0}y(i,j)y(i,j)^\top g_i g_j=V_g .
\]
For the adjacent covariance,
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)=\EE(Y_0Y_1^\top)
=\sum_{i,j,k\ge0}y(i,j)y(j,k)^\top g_i g_jg_k .
\]
Summing first over the independent future gap \(k\) gives
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}y(j,k)g_k=\bar y(j),
\]
with the displayed closed expression; hence
\(\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)=C_g\).  Transposition gives
\(\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0)=C_g^\top\), which proves
\eqref{eq:off-diagonal-inclusion-covariance-gap-sums-main}.

The \(A,B,e,d,\nu\) display is the same identity expanded before centering.
Indeed,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y_0)
=\EE\{(u(G_0,G_1)-rL_0)(u(G_0,G_1)-rL_0)^\top\}
=A-er^\top-re^\top+\nu rr^\top .
\]
Also
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)
&=\EE\{(u(G_0,G_1)-rL_0)(u(G_1,G_2)-rL_1)^\top\}\\
&=B-dr^\top-r\,\EE\{L_0u(G_1,G_2)^\top\}
  +rr^\top\EE(L_0L_1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(G_0\) is independent of \((G_1,G_2)\),
\(\EE\{L_0u(G_1,G_2)\}=\mu\,\EE u(G_1,G_2)=\mu^2r\), and
\(\EE(L_0L_1)=\mu^2\).  These last two terms cancel, so
\(\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)=B-dr^\top\), and transposition gives
\(\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0)=B^\top-rd^\top\).  Dividing
\(T_1^{(r)}\) by \(\mu\) proves
\eqref{eq:off-diagonal-inclusion-covariance-ABednu-main}.

The exponential gap bound in
\Cref{lem:killed-leakage-gap-exponential-moment-main} and the linear growth
of \(y(i,j)\) and \(\bar y(j)\) imply absolute convergence of all displayed
sums.  Finally, \eqref{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main} expresses \(g_k\) as a
finite linear combination of geometric sequences, so the required moments are
finite combinations of the usual geometric derivative sums.
\end{proof}

consequence used by the main inferential theorems.

\begin{proposition}[D-MAP specialization of separated-batch covariance]\label{thm:finite-record-covariance-main}
Work under \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main} and the deterministic
threshold protocol \ref{fr:protocol-main} at a population point in the stable
quotient chart \(r_0>0\), \(\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}=r_1-r_0^2\ne0\).
Let \(U_j\), \(Y_j\), \(T_1^{(r)}\), and
\(\Sigma_r=T_1^{(r)}/\mu\) be the adjacent-gap reward and calendar-time
covariance from
\Cref{prop:exact-inclusion-separated-batch-covariance-main}.  Away from the
exchange diagonal, \(T_1^{(r)}\) is the explicit finite-gap-law expression
in \Cref{lem:off-diagonal-inclusion-covariance-gap-sums-main}; on the
exchange diagonal, the corresponding computable closed formula is
\Cref{prop:computable-diagonal-covariance-main}.  Algorithm~1
forms the separated all-complete-cycle plug-in covariance estimator
\(\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}\) and reports it only on \(\mathcal E_N\).  For
probability limits use the auxiliary total-space completion
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\rm tot}
:=\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_N}\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}
+\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_N^c}0_{3\times3}.
\]
With a deterministic separated batch length
\(b_N=\lfloor N^\beta\rfloor\), \(0<\beta<1\), this completed matrix satisfies
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\rm tot}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=
O_{\PP}\left(b_N^{-1}+\sqrt{\frac{b_N}{N}}\right)
=
O_{\PP}\left(N^{-\beta}+N^{-(1-\beta)/2}\right).
\]
With
\[
\omega_\beta:=\min\{\beta,(1-\beta)/2\},
\]
any rank-adaptive deterministic threshold \(t_N\) must satisfy
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\rm tot}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=o_{\PP}(t_N).
\]
A concrete admissible choice is
\[
t_N=N^{-\alpha},
\qquad
0<\alpha<\omega_\beta;
\]
for the balanced choice \(b_N=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\), this becomes any
\(0<\alpha<1/3\), for instance \(t_N=N^{-1/4}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The D-MAP reward identity and the scale
\(\Sigma_r=T_1^{(r)}/\mu\) are those of
\Cref{prop:exact-inclusion-separated-batch-covariance-main}.  That proposition
instantiates the master adjacent-gap theorem
\Cref{thm:main-calendar-stopped-separated-batch-plugin} with
\(Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1})=U_j-\Rinc L_j\) and
\(\Delta_N=\Rinch_N^{\mathrm{cyc}}-\Rinc=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).  Therefore
Algorithm~1 gives
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\rm tot}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=O_{\PP}\left(b_N^{-1}+\sqrt{\frac{b_N}{N}}\right).
\]
Substituting \(b_N=\lfloor N^\beta\rfloor\), \(0<\beta<1\), gives
\[
O_{\PP}\left(N^{-\beta}+N^{-(1-\beta)/2}\right).
\]
The threshold claim is immediate from Weyl's eigenvalue inequality: every
positive limiting eigenvalue stays above \(t_N\), and every zero limiting
eigenvalue falls below \(t_N\), with probability tending to one whenever
\(\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\rm tot}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=o_{\PP}(t_N)\).
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Conditional projected-Gaussian law at a moving half-space]\label{lem:projected-gaussian-bootstrap-halfspace-main}
For \(t\in(0,1)\), write
\[
H_t=\{h\in\RR^2:h_2\le th_1\}.
\]
Let \(z_N\to z\in(0,1)\) in probability and let
\(\widehat\Sigma_N\) be a symmetric nonnegative definite covariance estimator
such that
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_N-\Sigma\|_{\operatorname{op}}=o_{\PP}(t_N),
\qquad t_N\downarrow0,
\]
for a fixed nonnegative definite \(2\times2\) matrix \(\Sigma\).  Let
\[
\widehat\Sigma_N^+
=\sum_{\widehat\lambda_{i,N}>t_N}
\widehat\lambda_{i,N}^{-1}
\widehat v_{i,N}\widehat v_{i,N}^{\top}
\]
be the thresholded Moore--Penrose inverse.  Suppose that
\[
X_N\Rightarrow X:=\Pi_{H_z}(Z),
\qquad Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma).
\]
This is the local constrained-estimation limit: the Gaussian perturbation is
projected onto the tangent half-space before the quadratic statistic is formed.
It is the same projected-normal structure that appears in constrained
\(M\)-estimation limits \cite{Geyer1994ConstrainedM}.  The simulation below is
therefore not an ordinary nonparametric or parametric bootstrap from the
record; it is the conditional plug-in projected Gaussian law, with retained
empirical rank.
Define
\[
T_N=X_N^\top\widehat\Sigma_N^+X_N
\]
and, conditionally on the observed record,
\[
Z_N^\ast\sim \mathcal N(0,\widehat\Sigma_N),\qquad
X_N^\ast=\Pi_{H_{z_N}}(Z_N^\ast),\qquad
T_N^\ast=(X_N^\ast)^\top\widehat\Sigma_N^+X_N^\ast .
\]
Then
\[
T_N\Rightarrow T:=X^\top\Sigma^+X,
\]
and the conditional law of \(T_N^\ast\) converges weakly in probability to
the law of \(T\).  Consequently, if \(c_{1-\alpha,N}^{\ast,-}\) is the lower
conditional \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(T_N^\ast\) and the limiting quantile
\[
c_{1-\alpha}:=\inf\{c:\PP(T\le c)\ge1-\alpha\}
\]
is a continuity point of the distribution function of \(T\), then
\[
c_{1-\alpha,N}^{\ast,-}\to c_{1-\alpha}
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]
If in addition \(\PP(T\le c_{1-\alpha})=1-\alpha\), then
\[
\PP\{T_N\le c_{1-\alpha,N}^{\ast,-}\}\to1-\alpha .
\]
If \(\Sigma=0\), then the retained-rank inverse is the zero matrix with
probability tending to one; consequently \(T_N=0\) and \(T_N^\ast=0\) with
probability tending to one, and the fixed positive-buffer lower-coverage
conclusion follows from
\Cref{lem:buffered-random-quantile-coverage-main}.

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Weyl's inequality and the condition
\(\|\widehat\Sigma_N-\Sigma\|_{\operatorname{op}}=o_{\PP}(t_N)\) imply that,
with probability tending to one, every positive eigenvalue of \(\Sigma\) is
retained and every zero eigenvalue is removed by the threshold.  On that event
the retained spectral projection converges to the range projection of
\(\Sigma\), and
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_N^+-\Sigma^+\|_{\operatorname{op}}\to0
\]
in probability.  Slutsky's theorem therefore gives
\[
X_N^\top\widehat\Sigma_N^+X_N
\Rightarrow X^\top\Sigma^+X .
\]
In the special case \(\Sigma=0\), the same eigenvalue bound says
\[
\PP\{\|\widehat\Sigma_N\|_{\operatorname{op}}\le t_N\}\to1,
\]
so the thresholded retained-rank inverse is exactly zero with probability
tending to one.  Hence \(T_N=0\) and, conditionally, \(T_N^\ast=0\) on that
high-probability event.  The limiting distribution is the point mass at zero,
whose lower \(q\)-quantile is zero for every \(0<q<1\); the positive-buffer
coverage statement is therefore the buffered random-quantile conclusion.

The remaining issue is conditional Gaussian convergence after the moving
projection.  The half-space projection has the explicit formula
\[
\Pi_{H_t}(u)
=u-\frac{(u_2-tu_1)_+}{1+t^2}\binom{-t}{1}.
\]
Thus \((t,u)\mapsto\Pi_{H_t}(u)\) is continuous, uniformly for \(t\) in compact
subintervals of \((0,1)\) and \(u\) in bounded sets.  Since
\(\widehat\Sigma_N\to\Sigma\) in probability, the conditional Gaussian laws
\(\mathcal N(0,\widehat\Sigma_N)\) converge weakly in probability to
\(\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)\).  Combining this Gaussian continuity with
\(z_N\to z\) and \(\widehat\Sigma_N^+\to\Sigma^+\) shows that, for every
bounded continuous \(\varphi:\RR\to\RR\),
\[
\EE^\ast\varphi(T_N^\ast)
\to
\EE\varphi\!\left((\Pi_{H_z}Z)^\top\Sigma^+\Pi_{H_z}Z\right)
\]
in probability, where \(\EE^\ast\) denotes conditional expectation given the
record.  This is conditional weak convergence in probability.  The quantile
conclusion is the standard consequence of weak convergence at a continuity
point of the limiting distribution function; the final coverage statement
then follows from the convergence of \(T_N\) and the random quantile
\(c_{1-\alpha,N}^{\ast,-}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Buffered random-quantile lower coverage]\label{lem:buffered-random-quantile-coverage-main}
Let \(T_N\Rightarrow T\), and let \(F_N^\ast\) be random conditional
distribution functions such that
\[
F_N^\ast(t)\to F(t):=\PP(T\le t)
\qquad\text{in probability}
\]
at every continuity point \(t\) of \(F\).  Fix \(0<q<1\), and define the
lower random conditional \(q\)-quantile and the limiting lower \(q\)-quantile
by
\[
c_N^-=\inf\{t:F_N^\ast(t)\ge q\},
\qquad
c^-=\inf\{t:F(t)\ge q\}.
\]
Assume \(c^-\) is finite.  Then, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\PP\{T_N\le c_N^-+\varepsilon\}\ge q .
\]
No continuity of \(F\) at \(c^-\), and no equality \(F(c^-)=q\), is required.
In particular, this lemma applies to the diagonal projected-Gaussian
zero-quantile alternatives after replacing \(q\) by \(1-\alpha\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First the random lower quantile cannot fall a fixed distance below the
limiting lower quantile.  Fix \(\delta>0\).  By the definition of \(c^-\),
\(F(c^--\delta)<q\).  Choose a continuity point
\[
x\in(c^--\delta,c^-)
\]
with \(F(x)<q\); distribution functions have at most countably many
discontinuities, so such a point is available after decreasing \(x\) slightly
if needed.  The pointwise conditional convergence gives
\[
F_N^\ast(x)\to F(x)
\qquad\text{in probability}.
\]
For any distribution function \(G\), the event
\(\inf\{t:G(t)\ge q\}\le x\) implies \(G(x)\ge q\).  Hence
\[
\PP\{c_N^-\le x\}
\le
\PP\{F_N^\ast(x)\ge q\}\to0,
\]
and \(c_N^->x\) with probability tending to one.

Choose \(x>c^- -\varepsilon\), and then choose a continuity point
\[
y\in(c^-,x+\varepsilon).
\]
This is possible because \(x+\varepsilon>c^-\).  On the event
\(\{c_N^->x\}\), the inclusion
\[
\{T_N\le y\}\subseteq\{T_N\le c_N^-+\varepsilon\}
\]
holds.  Therefore
\[
\liminf_N\PP\{T_N\le c_N^-+\varepsilon\}
\ge
\liminf_N\PP\{T_N\le y\}.
\]
Weak convergence at the continuity point \(y\) gives
\[
\lim_N\PP\{T_N\le y\}=F(y).
\]
Finally \(y>c^-\) implies \(F(y)\ge q\) by the lower-quantile definition.
The displayed lower bound follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional Monte Carlo diagnostic and auxiliary domination]\label{lem:conditional-monte-carlo-quantile-main}
Fix \(0<z<1\) and condition on the observed record.  Let
\[
T_N^\ast(z)=
\Pi_{H_z}(Z_N^\ast)^\top\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}^{+}
\Pi_{H_z}(Z_N^\ast),
\qquad
Z_N^\ast\mid A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\sim
\mathcal N(0,\widehat\Sigma_{\sigma,N}),
\]
where \(H_z=\{h:h_2\le zh_1\}\), and let
\[
F_N^\ast(t;z)=\PP^\ast\{T_N^\ast(z)\le t\}
\]
be its conditional distribution function.  If
\(T_{N,1}^\ast(z),\ldots,T_{N,R}^\ast(z)\) are conditionally independent draws
from this law and
\[
\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast(t;z)
=\frac1R\sum_{m=1}^R\mathbf1_{\{T_{N,m}^\ast(z)\le t\}},
\]
then for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\PP^\ast\!\left(
\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}
\left|\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast(t;z)-F_N^\ast(t;z)\right|>\varepsilon
\right)
\le 2e^{-2R\varepsilon^2}.
\]
Equivalently, for every \(0<\delta<1\), with conditional probability at least
\(1-\delta\),
\[
\sup_t|\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast(t;z)-F_N^\ast(t;z)|
\le
\varepsilon_{R,\delta}:=
\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{2R}} .
\]
Let \(q_\alpha=1-\alpha\), let
\[
c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)
=\inf\{t:F_N^\ast(t;z)\ge q_\alpha\},
\qquad
\widehat c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N,R}(z)
=T_{N,(\lceil q_\alpha R\rceil)}^\ast(z),
\]
where the draws are sorted increasingly.  If \(c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)\) is a
continuity point of \(F_N^\ast(\cdot;z)\), then
\[
\widehat c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N,R}(z)
\longrightarrow c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)
\qquad\text{in conditional probability as }R\to\infty .
\]
If the conditional law has an atom or a flat stretch at the lower quantile,
the raw order statistic still estimates a lower conditional quantile in the
usual empirical-quantile sense, but the raw order statistic at level
\(q_\alpha\) is not by itself a one-sided coverage certificate.  A DKW
certificate is obtained by spending probability in the distribution-function
scale.  If
\[
\varepsilon_{R,\delta}<\alpha,\qquad
q_{\alpha,\delta,R}^{+}:=q_\alpha+\varepsilon_{R,\delta}<1,
\]
put \(\widehat c_R=\widehat c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N,R}(z)\) and define
\[
d^{\mathrm{DKW}}_{R,\delta,N}(z)
:=
\inf\left\{d\ge0:
\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast(\widehat c_R+d;z)-\varepsilon_{R,\delta}
\ge 1-\alpha\right\}.
\]
Equivalently, since the empirical distribution is a step function, the
inflated lower empirical quantile is
\[
\widehat c^{\mathrm{DKW},-}_{1-\alpha,N,R,\delta}(z)
:=T_{N,(\lceil q_{\alpha,\delta,R}^{+}R\rceil)}^\ast(z),
\]
and \(\widehat c_R+d^{\mathrm{DKW}}_{R,\delta,N}(z)
=\widehat c^{\mathrm{DKW},-}_{1-\alpha,N,R,\delta}(z)\).
Then, conditionally on the observed record,
\[
\PP^\ast\!\left\{
\widehat c_R+d^{\mathrm{DKW}}_{R,\delta,N}(z)
\ge c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)\right\}\ge1-\delta .
\]
Equivalently, the inflated simulated critical value is the empirical lower
\((q_\alpha+\varepsilon_{R,\delta})\)-quantile.  This conclusion is
distribution-free and does not require continuity or a positive density at the
ideal conditional quantile.

For the record-probability consequence, fix the true diagonal coordinate
\(z_0\).  Throughout this paragraph \(\PP\) is the fixed-population law of the
observed retained record and all record-level gates, whereas \(\PP^\ast\) is
only the conditional law of the auxiliary Gaussian simulation after that
record has been observed.  The DKW step below is not a finite-sample
statement about record coverage; it is a conditional one-sided domination
statement for the simulated projected-Gaussian critical value.  Let
\(G_N=G_{\mathrm{cert},N}(z_0)\) be the record-level reporting event on which
the population branch, ideal-quantile enclosure, and numerical recipe are
certified.  A finite report may set \(G_N=1\) only when the manuscript or
attached transcript contains the branch certificate and the ideal conditional
quantile enclosure used by the reported inequality; otherwise the event is
declared false for coverage-certification purposes.  Let
\[
H_N=G_{\mathrm{MC},N}(z_0)
=\{\widehat c^-_{1-\alpha,N,R}(z_0)
+d_{R,\delta_N^{\rm MC},N}(z_0)\ge c^-_{1-\alpha,N}(z_0)\}
\]
be the auxiliary one-sided Monte Carlo domination event.  For each realized
record, \(H_N\) is an event in the auxiliary simulation space; its probability
is measured by \(\PP^\ast(\,\cdot\,)\), not by \(\PP\).  The event \(H_N\) is
part of the report only through a displayed one-sided certificate, for example
the DKW event and inflation in the preceding display.  Suppose
\[
\PP(G_N)\to1,\qquad \delta_N^{\rm MC}\to0,
\]
and on \(G_N\),
\[
\PP^\ast\{\widehat c^-_{1-\alpha,N,R}(z_0)
+d_{R,\delta_N^{\rm MC},N}(z_0)\ge c^-_{1-\alpha,N}(z_0)\}
\ge1-\delta_N^{\rm MC} .
\]
Define the auxiliary all-space fixed-candidate ideal and simulated diagonal
acceptance events by
\[
\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{aux}}(z_0)
:=\{Q_N(z_0)\le c^-_{1-\alpha,N}(z_0)\},
\]
and
\[
\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{MC,aux}}(z_0)
:=\{Q_N(z_0)\le
\widehat c^-_{1-\alpha,N,R}(z_0)+d_{R,\delta_N^{\rm MC},N}(z_0)\}.
\]
The operational reported events are
\[
\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{rep}}(z_0)
:=\mathcal E_N\cap\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{aux}}(z_0),
\qquad
\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{MC,rep}}(z_0)
:=\mathcal E_N\cap\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{MC,aux}}(z_0).
\]
On \(\mathcal E_N^c\) the finite-record procedure withholds the diagonal
report, so no diagonal finite-record report is formed there.
Then
\[
\PP\{\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{MC,rep}}(z_0)\}
\ge
\PP\{\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{aux}}(z_0)\}
-\delta_N^{\rm MC}-\PP(G_N^c)-\PP(\mathcal E_N^c)-o(1).
\]
Therefore, if the ideal event has limiting coverage \(1-\alpha\), the simulated
reported event has
\[
\liminf_N
\PP\{\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{MC,rep}}(z_0)\}
\ge1-\alpha .
\]
Without such an explicit event \(G_{\mathrm{MC},N}(z_0)\) and one-sided
inflation \(d_{R,\delta_N^{\rm MC},N}(z_0)\), finite simulation remains only a
numerical diagnostic for the ideal conditional projected-Gaussian law.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on the record, the variables
\(T_{N,1}^\ast(z),\ldots,T_{N,R}^\ast(z)\) are independent real-valued draws
with distribution function \(F_N^\ast(\cdot;z)\).  The displayed exponential
bound is therefore the Dvoretzky--Kiefer--Wolfowitz inequality, in Massart's
sharp-constant form, applied under \(\PP^\ast\) \cite{Massart1990DKW}.

Let
\[
\Delta_{N,R}(z)=
\sup_t|\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast(t;z)-F_N^\ast(t;z)|.
\]
The DKW bound gives \(\Delta_{N,R}(z)=O_{\PP^\ast}(R^{-1/2})\).  At a
continuity point of the conditional quantile, the Glivenko--Cantelli
consequence of this bound gives the standard empirical-quantile consistency:
for every \(\eta>0\), after decreasing \(\eta\) if necessary on the left,
\[
F_N^\ast(c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)-\eta;z)<q_\alpha
\le F_N^\ast(c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)+\eta;z).
\]
Uniform convergence of \(\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast\) to \(F_N^\ast\) then forces
the order statistic into this \(\eta\)-neighbourhood with conditional
probability tending to one.  At a discontinuity or flat quantile the same DKW
bound controls the simulated distribution function but the uninflated
\(q_\alpha\)-order statistic need not dominate the ideal lower quantile.

The DKW-inflated statistic gives a one-sided certificate without any local
regularity.  On the event
\[
B_{R,\delta,N}(z)=
\left\{\Delta_{N,R}(z)\le\varepsilon_{R,\delta}\right\},
\]
we have, with
\[
\widehat c^{\mathrm{DKW},-}_{1-\alpha,N,R,\delta}(z)
=T_{N,(\lceil(q_\alpha+\varepsilon_{R,\delta})R\rceil)}^\ast(z),
\]
that
\[
\widehat F_{N,R}^\ast
\bigl(\widehat c^{\mathrm{DKW},-}_{1-\alpha,N,R,\delta}(z);z\bigr)
\ge q_\alpha+\varepsilon_{R,\delta}.
\]
Therefore
\[
F_N^\ast
\bigl(\widehat c^{\mathrm{DKW},-}_{1-\alpha,N,R,\delta}(z);z\bigr)
\ge q_\alpha .
\]
By the definition of the lower quantile,
\[
c^{-}_{1-\alpha,N}(z)
\le
\widehat c^{\mathrm{DKW},-}_{1-\alpha,N,R,\delta}(z)
=\widehat c_R
+d^{\mathrm{DKW}}_{R,\delta,N}(z).
\]
Since the DKW inequality gives \(\PP^\ast(B_{R,\delta,N}(z))\ge1-\delta\),
the stated DKW domination bound follows.  The only deterministic size
condition needed for this construction is
\(\varepsilon_{R,\delta}<\alpha\), which ensures that the empirical
\((q_\alpha+\varepsilon_{R,\delta})\)-quantile is below level one.

It remains to prove the coverage-transfer inequality.
On \(G_N\cap H_N\), the auxiliary ideal fixed-candidate event is contained in the
simulated event, because the simulated critical value is at least as large as
the ideal one.  Intersecting this inclusion with the reporting gate
\(\mathcal E_N\) and then taking conditional expectation over the auxiliary
Monte Carlo draws gives
\[
\PP\{\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{MC,rep}}(z_0)\}
\ge
\PP\{\mathcal E_N\cap\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{aux}}(z_0)\cap G_N\}
-\EE\{1_{G_N}\PP^\ast(H_N^c)\}.
\]
The assumed conditional bound on \(G_N\) makes the last term at most
\(\delta_N^{\rm MC}\).  Since
\[
\PP\{\mathcal E_N\cap\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{aux}}(z_0)\cap G_N\}
\ge
\PP\{\mathcal A_N^{\mathrm{aux}}(z_0)\}
