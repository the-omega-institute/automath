h_k=\frac{g_k}{S_k}=1-\frac{S_{k+1}}{S_k}.
\]
This hazard formula is a population diagnostic.  Substitution yields the
theorem's hazard formula and the increment identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hazard-increment-theorem-main}
h_{k+1}-h_k
=
\frac{
  \Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}\,
  (p-s)^2\,p^k s^k
}{
  \bigl(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k\bigr)
  \bigl(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^{k+1}-\Gamma s^{k+1}\bigr)
}.
\end{equation}
The numerator in \eqref{eq:hazard-increment-theorem-main} is positive because
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}>0\), \(p,s\in(0,1)\), and \((p-s)^2>0\) off the
diagonal.  The denominator factors satisfy
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^m-\Gamma s^m
=(\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma)S_m,\qquad m=k,k+1.
\]
Both denominator factors have sign \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma\), so their
product is positive.  Equivalently,
\[
\bigl(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k\bigr)
\bigl(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^{k+1}-\Gamma s^{k+1}\bigr)
=(\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma)^2S_kS_{k+1}>0.
\]
Thus \(h_{k+1}>h_k\) off the diagonal.  On the equal-rate diagonal,
\eqref{eq:equal-rate-dictionary-main} gives
\[
h_k=1-p-\frac{yp}{1+yk},\qquad
h_{k+1}-h_k=
\frac{y^2p}{(1+yk)(1+y(k+1))}>0,
\]
where \(y=\Gamma\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\).  Hence the
zero-run hazard is strictly increasing in all positive parameter cases.

It remains to verify the covariance and dependence assertions.  Let
\(P=T_0+T_1\) and \(u=T_1\mathbf1=(1-p,a)^\top\).  By
\Cref{lem:stationary-main}, the stationary hidden law is \(\pi\), the click
density is \(\rho=\pi u\), and \(a-\rho\ne0\).  The two eigenvalues of \(P\)
are \(1\) and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b\).  The centered click vector lies
in the hidden eigenspace:
\[
u-\rho\mathbf1=\frac{1-p-a}{1-p+b}\binom{1-p}{-b},
\qquad
P^{n-1}u=\rho\mathbf1+
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}(u-\rho\mathbf1).
\]
Conditioning on \(A_0=1\) again leaves the next hidden state equal to \(D\);
therefore
\[
\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)
=e_D^\top P^{n-1}u
=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}.
\]
Multiplying by \(\PP(A_0=1)=\rho\) and subtracting \(\rho^2\) gives
\[
\gamma_n=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},\qquad n\ge1 .
\]

If \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), then \(\det P=0\).  Since \(P\) is stochastic,
both rows of \(P\) equal the stationary row \(\pi\), so \(P=\mathbf1\pi\).
Summing over one unobserved sample between two visible blocks inserts this
rank-one matrix and factorizes the two cylinder probabilities, as in
\Cref{lem:kernel-factorization-main}; hence the visible record is
\(1\)-dependent.  Conversely, \(1\)-dependence makes \(A_0\) and \(A_2\)
independent, so \(0=\gamma_2=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\).  The
prefactor is nonzero by \Cref{lem:stationary-main}, and hence
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\).

Finally, suppose the renewal record were an \(r\)-step Markov chain.  Because
all finite zero runs have positive probability, the conditional click
probability after a run of \(k\) zeros would depend only on the final \(r\)
zeros once \(k\ge r\).  Thus \(h_k\) would be eventually constant.  The strict
increase proved above rules this out for every \(r\), so the visible process
is not a finite-order Markov chain.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Threshold finite-window count variance]\label{prop:threshold-finite-window-count-main}
Fix the constrained D-MAP kernels of
\Cref{prop:instrument-kernels-main} in stationarity and suppose that the
hidden transfer mode is at the exact-dependence threshold
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\).  For
\[
N_m=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}A_n,\qquad m\ge1,
\]
one has the exact finite-window identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threshold-finite-window-count-main}
\Var(N_m)
=m\rho(1-\rho)+2(m-1)\rho(a-\rho).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the finite-window variance is the one-dependent covariance
sum with only the adjacent covariance retained.

The paper uses only \eqref{eq:threshold-finite-window-count-main}.  Higher
finite-window count laws at the threshold are obtained from the ordinary
probability generating polynomial
\[
P_m(z)=\EE z^{N_m}
=\sum_{u\in\{0,1\}^m}z^{u_0+\cdots+u_{m-1}}
  \,\PP(A_0=u_0,\ldots,A_{m-1}=u_{m-1}),
\]
with the cylinder probabilities evaluated by the renewal inclusion product
of \Cref{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}.  This is the finite-window
specialization of the standard \(1\)-dependent count generating-function
bookkeeping; no separate closed formula for every higher count law is used in
the sequel.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Stationarity gives the exact variance decomposition
\[
\Var(N_m)
=m\gamma_0+2\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}(m-h)\gamma_h,
\qquad
\gamma_h=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_h).
\]
Here \(\gamma_0=\rho(1-\rho)\).  The covariance tail proved in
\Cref{thm:main-record-wrapper} is
\[
\gamma_h=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,h-1},
\qquad h\ge1.
\]
At \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), exact \(1\)-dependence is
\Cref{lem:one-dep-criterion-main} and the displayed tail leaves
\(\gamma_1=\rho(a-\rho)\) and \(\gamma_h=0\) for every \(h\ge2\).
The strict inequality \(\gamma_1<0\), proved in
\Cref{lem:stationary-main}, excludes \(0\)-dependence.
Substitution
in the stationary variance sum gives
\[
\Var(N_m)
=m\rho(1-\rho)+2(m-1)\rho(a-\rho),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:threshold-finite-window-count-main}.

For the final assertion, the polynomial \(P_m\) is just the definition of the
finite-window count distribution.  At the threshold the law is \(1\)-dependent
by \Cref{lem:one-dep-criterion-main}; the same finite cylinder probabilities
are explicitly determined from renewal inclusion products in
\Cref{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}.  Taking coefficients of \(P_m\) therefore
recovers the count probabilities.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Discrete renewal count-variance identity]\label{lem:discrete-renewal-fano-main}
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be a stationary binary renewal process on the
sample lattice, with click epochs separated by i.i.d. integer cycle lengths
\(L_j\ge1\), mean \(\mu=\EE L_j<\infty\), variance
\(\sigma_L^2=\Var(L_j)<\infty\), and exponentially decaying tails.  For
\(N_m=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}A_n\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discrete-renewal-fano-identity-main}
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\Var(N_m)}{\EE N_m}
=\frac{\sigma_L^2}{\mu^2}.
\end{equation}
If, in addition, the autocovariances are summable and \(f(0)\) denotes the
zero-frequency spectral density
\(\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_0)+2\sum_{h\ge1}\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_h)\),
then the same limit equals \(f(0)/\rho\), where \(\rho=\EE A_0=1/\mu\).
Throughout this paper \(f\) uses this covariance-series convention:
\[
f(\theta)=\gamma_0+2\sum_{h\ge1}\gamma_h\cos(h\theta),
\qquad
\gamma_h=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_h).
\]
There is no \(2\pi\) normalization in \(f\); with the Fourier-transform
convention \(S(\theta)=(2\pi)^{-1}\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}\gamma_he^{-ih\theta}\),
one has \(f(\theta)=2\pi S(\theta)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work first in Palm coordinates with a click at the left endpoint and write
\(S_k=L_1+\cdots+L_k\) and
\(K_m=\max\{k:S_k\le m\}\).  The stationary count \(N_m\) differs from the
corresponding Palm count only through the initial delayed cycle and the
terminal backward-recurrence (age) cycle.  The stationary endpoint cycle has the size-biased
cycle tail, which is still exponentially decaying, and the maximum of the
complete cycles intersecting \([0,m)\) is \(O_{\PP}(\log m)\).  Hence this
endpoint replacement changes \(m^{-1/2}(N_m-\EE N_m)\) by \(o_{\PP}(1)\), and
the squared normalized family is uniformly integrable.

At the renewal stopping index,
\[
S_{K_m}\le m<S_{K_m+1}.
\]
Thus, with \(W_j=L_j-\mu\),
\[
\mu\left(K_m-\frac m\mu\right)
=-\sum_{j=1}^{K_m}W_j+O_{\PP}(\log m).
\]
Since \(K_m/m\to1/\mu\) and the partial sums of the centered i.i.d. sequence
\((W_j)\) satisfy the usual random-time-change estimate,
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{K_m}W_j
=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor m/\mu\rfloor}W_j+o_{\PP}(\sqrt m).
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{K_m-m/\mu}{\sqrt m}
=-\frac1\mu\,
\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor m/\mu\rfloor}W_j}{\sqrt m}
+o_{\PP}(1),
\]
and uniform integrability yields
\[
\frac{\Var(N_m)}m
\longrightarrow
\frac{\sigma_L^2}{\mu^3}.
\]
Stationarity gives \(\EE N_m=m\rho=m/\mu\), which proves
\eqref{eq:discrete-renewal-fano-identity-main}.

For the spectral form, stationarity gives the exact sampled-window identity
\[
\Var(N_m)=
m\gamma_0+2\sum_{h=1}^{m-1}(m-h)\gamma_h,\qquad
\gamma_h=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_h).
\]
Summability of \((\gamma_h)\) implies
\[
\frac{\Var(N_m)}m\longrightarrow
\gamma_0+2\sum_{h\ge1}\gamma_h=f(0).
\]
Dividing by \(\EE N_m/m=\rho\) gives the equivalent limit \(f(0)/\rho\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Renewal generating-function expansion at one]\label{lem:renewal-gf-expansion-main}
Let \(L\ge1\) have finite third moment, put \(\mu=\EE L\),
\(\rho=1/\mu\), and let
\(u_0=1,\ u_n=\PP(n\ \text{is a renewal epoch}\mid0\ \text{is a renewal epoch})\).
If \(\sum_{n\ge0}|u_n-\rho|<\infty\), then
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}u_nz^n=\frac{1}{1-\EE z^L}
=\frac{\rho}{1-z}
 + \frac{\EE\{L(L-1)\}}{2\mu^2}+O(1-z)
\qquad (z\uparrow1),
\]
and consequently
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}(u_n-\rho)
=\frac{\EE\{L(L-1)\}}{2\mu^2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first identity is the renewal equation in generating-function form.  With
\(t=1-z\),
\[
\EE z^L
=1-\mu t+\frac{\EE\{L(L-1)\}}2t^2+O(t^3),
\]
so
\[
\frac1{1-\EE z^L}
=\frac1{\mu t}
 +\frac{\EE\{L(L-1)\}}{2\mu^2}+O(t).
\]
Since \(\rho=1/\mu\), subtracting \(\rho/(1-z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\rho z^n\) and
letting \(z\uparrow1\) gives the stated sum by absolute convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral density and Fano limit for the constrained D-MAP renewal record]\label{prop:spectrum-main}
Fix one positive constrained D-MAP setting
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\), let \(T_0,T_1\) be the
killed-leakage labelled kernels in \eqref{eq:t0-t1-main} with
\(p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}\), \(s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\), and
the lower-row weights \(a,b\) in \eqref{eq:a-b-main}.  Thus
\(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\), \(P=T_0+T_1\) is stochastic, \(0<p,s<1\),
\(a>0\), \(b>0\), and \(a+b=1-s\).  Let
\((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be the stationary visible binary process generated
by these kernels, put \(\rho=\PP(A_0=1)\) and
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b\), and interpret equal-rate expressions by
continuous limits when \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).  The stationary
covariance spectral density is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrum-main}
f(\theta)=
\rho(1-\rho)+
2\rho(a-\rho)
\frac{\cos\theta-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}
     {1-2\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\cos\theta+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^2}.
\end{equation}
This is the covariance-series normalization
\[
f(\theta)=\gamma_0+2\sum_{n\ge1}\gamma_n\cos(n\theta),
\qquad
\gamma_n=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n),
\]
so no \(2\pi\) factor is included in \(f\).
The displayed \(f(\theta)\) is a population spectral diagnostic.
If \(N_m=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}A_n\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fano-main}
0\le
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\Var(N_m)}{\EE N_m}
=\frac{\Var(G+1)}{\bigl(\EE(G+1)\bigr)^2}
=1-\rho+\frac{2(a-\rho)}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}
<1,
\end{equation}
with \(G\) distributed as in \Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}.
This Fano display has the same population status: finite-record uncertainty
for quotient coordinates is handled later by sandwich/projection statements.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Insert the covariance tail from \Cref{lem:covariance-transfer-main} into the Herglotz
Fourier series:
\[
f(\theta)
=\rho(1-\rho)
+2\rho(a-\rho)\sum_{n\ge1}
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}\cos(n\theta).
\]
The exponent is \(n-1\), not \(n\), because the covariance tail begins with
\(\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_1)=\rho(a-\rho)\).  Thus the relevant geometric
series is the shifted series below; using
\(\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{n}e^{in\theta}\) would insert an
extraneous factor of \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\) and give the wrong numerator.
Since \(|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|<1\),
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}e^{in\theta}
=
\frac{e^{i\theta}}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}e^{i\theta}},
\]
and taking real parts gives
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}\cos(n\theta)
=
\frac{\cos\theta-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}
       {1-2\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\cos\theta+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^2}.
\]
This includes the exact \(1\)-dependent case
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), where only the adjacent covariance contributes.
For this discrete count convention,
\Cref{lem:discrete-renewal-fano-main} gives, with \(L=G+1\),
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\Var(N_m)}{\EE N_m}
=\frac{\Var(L)}{(\EE L)^2}.
\]
The cycle moments can be read directly from the renewal-mass tail.  Let
\(u_n=\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)\).  In the present D-MAP renewal law,
\[
u_n=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1,
\]
so \(\sum_{n\ge1}(u_n-\rho)=(a-\rho)/(1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}})\).  The
renewal generating-function expansion in
\Cref{lem:renewal-gf-expansion-main},
\[
\frac{1}{1-\EE z^L}
=\sum_{n\ge0}u_nz^n,
\]
gives
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}(u_n-\rho)
=\frac{\EE\{L(L-1)\}}{2(\EE L)^2}.
\]
Since \(u_0=1\) and \(\rho=1/\EE L\), this yields the explicit cycle-moment
display
\[
\EE L=\frac1\rho,\qquad
\EE L^2
=\frac{2-\rho+\displaystyle\frac{2(a-\rho)}
              {1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}}{\rho^2},
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{\Var(L)}{(\EE L)^2}
=\rho^2\EE L^2-1
=1-\rho+\frac{2(a-\rho)}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}.
\]
The same discrete-renewal identity also identifies this cycle-variance
quotient with \(f(0)/\rho\).  Equivalently, evaluating the displayed spectral
density at \(\theta=0\) gives the same expression:
\[
\frac{\Var(G+1)}{\bigl(\EE(G+1)\bigr)^2}
=\frac{f(0)}{\rho}
=1-\rho+\frac{2(a-\rho)}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}.
\]
The lower bound in \eqref{eq:fano-main} is therefore inherited from the
nonnegative variance quotient, not from the sign of the closed algebraic
correction.  The upper bound is direct: \(\rho>0\), \(a-\rho<0\) by
\Cref{lem:stationary-main}, and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}<1\) by
\Cref{lem:hidden-mode-contraction-main}.  Hence
\[
\frac{f(0)}{\rho}-1
=-\rho+\frac{2(a-\rho)}{1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}}<0.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Population lag-two diagnostic and scope]\label{rem:diagnostics-consistency-only-main}
For the fixed constrained D-MAP kernels in
\eqref{eq:t0-t1-main}, the covariance paragraph in the proof of

\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}} .
\]
The prefactor is nonzero by \Cref{lem:stationary-main}.  Thus the population

\end{remark}

\subsection{Renewal inclusion products and quotient inverse}

In Markovian-arrival-process language, the pair \((T_0,T_1)\) in
\eqref{eq:t0-t1-main} is a constrained two-state discrete-time MAP:
\(T_0+T_1\) is stochastic, \(T_0\) records a no-arrival transition, and
\(T_1\) records a one-arrival transition.  This places the model inside the
MAP/D-MAP family introduced and developed for correlated arrival streams
\cite{Neuts1979MAP,Lucantoni1991BMAP,AlfaNeuts1995DMAP,
Chakravarthy2010MAPSurvey,AsanjaraniNazarathy2022MAP}.  The closest
representation comparators are the D-MAP construction of
\cite{AlfaNeuts1995DMAP} and the MAP/non-renewal arrival antecedents of
\cite{LucantoniMeierHellsternNeuts1990Vacations}.  The relevant
canonical-form and weak-equivalence results are
\cite{Ryden1996Aggregated,BodrogHeindlHorvathTelek2008MAP2Canonical,
RamirezCoboLilloWiper2010MAP2Nonidentifiability,
RamirezCoboLillo2012WeakEquivalentMAP}, which show that hidden MAP and
phase-type representations are generally nonunique and must be quotiented or
put in a specified representation chart.  The theorem below proves a different
and narrower statement for the sampled killed-leakage D-MAP(2) subfamily:
three visible inclusion probabilities reduce the renewal law to the quotient
coordinates \((p+s,ps)\), so the visible law identifies the unordered pair
\(\{p,s\}\) and no ordered branch label beyond it.

\begin{corollary}[Renewal inclusion and finite word law]\label{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}
For a stationary renewal click set \(\varXi\) with intensity \(\rho\) and Palm
renewal masses \(u_n=\PP(n\in\varXi\mid0\in\varXi)\), every nonempty finite
ordered set \(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renewal-inclusion-product-main}
\PP(B\subset\varXi)
=\rho\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}.
\end{equation}
For the empty set this inclusion probability is \(1\).  Exact finite
configuration probabilities follow by finite inclusion-exclusion:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-exact-configuration-probabilities-main}
\PP(\varXi\cap I=S)
=\sum_{C\subset I\setminus S}(-1)^{|C|}
\PP(S\cup C\subset\varXi).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Condition on the leftmost click in \(B\).  Under the Palm law at that click,
the future click process renews, so the conditional probability of the
successive gaps \(i_{r+1}-i_r\) being occupied is
\(\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}\).  Multiplying by the stationary click
intensity \(\rho\) gives \eqref{eq:renewal-inclusion-product-main}.  The
finite word formula is the ordinary inclusion-exclusion identity over the
unprescribed click sites \(I\setminus S\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Constrained D-MAP specialization of the renewal product notation]\label{lem:constrained-dmap-renewal-product-specialization-main}
Fix one positive constrained D-MAP setting
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\), and let
\((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be the stationary visible binary process generated
by the killed-leakage kernels \eqref{eq:t0-t1-main} with
\(p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}\), \(s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\), the
positive \(a,b\) from \eqref{eq:a-b-main}, \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\), and
continuous equal-rate interpretation on \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-inclusion-u-n-main}
u_n=\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)
=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1.
\end{equation}
Thus the constrained D-MAP renewal inclusion products are obtained from
\eqref{eq:renewal-inclusion-product-main} by this \(u_n\), and finite word
laws are given by
\eqref{eq:finite-exact-configuration-probabilities-main}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The formula for \(u_n\) is \Cref{lem:covariance-transfer-main}.  Insert that
Palm renewal mass into \Cref{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Symmetric-coordinate algebra for the quotient inverse]\label{lem:quotient-inverse-symmetric-algebra-main}
Let \(p\in(0,1)\), put \(q=1-p\), and let the constrained D-MAP parameters satisfy
\[
a+b=1-s,\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b,\qquad
\rho=\frac{q(1-s)}{q+b}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-inverse-s1-algebra-main}
1-a+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p+s
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-inverse-s2-algebra-main}
\rho(1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}})-a+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=ps .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(a+b=1-s\), we have \(a=1-s-b\).  Therefore
\[
1-a+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=1-(1-s-b)+(p-b)
=p+s,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:quotient-inverse-s1-algebra-main}.  For the second
identity, first note that
\[
1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=1-p+b=q+b.
\]
Thus
\[
\rho(1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}})
=\frac{q(1-s)}{q+b}\,(q+b)
=q(1-s).
\]
Combining this with \(a=1-s-b\) and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b\) gives
\[
\rho(1-\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}})-a+\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=q(1-s)-(1-s-b)+(p-b)
=q(1-s)+p+s-1.
\]
Because \(p+q=1\), the last expression is
\[
q(1-s)+p+s-1
=s-qs
=ps,
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exchange symmetry in dimensionless survival coordinates]\label{lem:gap-law-exchange-symmetry-main}
Put
\[
x=-\log p=\Gamma\Delta\tau,\qquad
y=-\log s=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau,
\qquad p=e^{-x},\qquad s=e^{-y}.
\]
For \(x\ne y\), the zero-run law in \eqref{eq:gap-closed-main} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main}
g_k=
\frac{y(1-p)p^k-x(1-s)s^k}{y-x},
\qquad k\ge0.
\end{equation}
The hidden eigenvalue in \eqref{eq:lambda-hid-main} is, in the same
coordinates,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-lambda-hid-main}
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=\frac{y s-x p}{y-x}
=\frac{y e^{-y}-x e^{-x}}{y-x}.
\end{equation}
Both \eqref{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main} and
\eqref{eq:symmetric-lambda-hid-main} are invariant under the simultaneous
exchange \((x,p)\leftrightarrow(y,s)\).  The equal-rate laws are their
continuous limits.  Consequently the stationary visible renewal law is
unchanged by exchanging \(\Gamma\) and \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Multiplying numerator and denominator in \eqref{eq:gap-closed-main} by
\(\Delta\tau\) gives \eqref{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main}.  Similarly,
\eqref{eq:lambda-hid-main} becomes
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}s-\Gamma p}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}
=\frac{y s-x p}{y-x}.
\]
Under \((x,p)\leftrightarrow(y,s)\), the numerator
\[
y(1-p)p^k-x(1-s)s^k
\]
and the denominator \(y-x\) both change sign, so \(g_k\) is unchanged.  The
same sign cancellation gives
\[
y s-x p\longmapsto x p-y s=-(y s-x p),
\qquad
y-x\longmapsto x-y=-(y-x),
\]
and hence \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\) is unchanged.  The diagonal formulas
follow by taking the continuous limits \(x\to y\).  Since
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main} proves that the visible law is the stationary
renewal law with gap probabilities \(g_k\), the unordered pair
\(\{(x,p),(y,s)\}\) determines the whole gap distribution.  It also determines
the stationary renewal intensity \(1/\EE(G+1)\).  Therefore the stationary
renewal measures generated before and after the exchange have the same
finite-dimensional visible distributions.
\end{proof}

\begin{convention}\label{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}
\textbf{Identifiable estimands and branch-map convention.}
From one stationary visible record the identifiable objects are the inclusion
vector \((r_0,r_1,r_2)\), the quotient
\(\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps)\) on
\(\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}=r_1-r_0^2\ne0\), the unordered survival-factor pair
\(\{p,s\}\), and visible functionals such as
\(\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)\).  These are quotient-level objects:
the visible inverse does not order the two roots and does not by itself
produce labelled attempt/recovery parameters.  When the sampling interval is
a known design interval, the unordered numerical rate set
\[
\left\{-\frac{\log p}{\Delta\tau},
       -\frac{\log s}{\Delta\tau}\right\}
\]
is visible-law identifiable, but the ordered vector
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\) is not.  If \(\Delta\tau\) is not known as a
design interval, the same quotient identifies only
\[
\{-\log p,-\log s\}
=\{\Gamma\Delta\tau,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\},
\]
not numerical rates per unit time.  A separated ordered labelled-rate vector
may be reported only after an additional side-data branch map
\(\mathcal B\), not contained in the visible record, assigns the two roots to
the attempt and recovery labels on a separated chart
\(\Delta_\sigma=(p-s)^2>0\).  Any theorem using such a vector is a
conditional side-data theorem: it first applies the quotient inverse
\(\Phi\), then applies the externally supplied branch map.  Theorem statements
condition on a deterministic branch map; estimated branch maps are reduced to
this case in \Cref{app:branch-map-oracle-reduction-main}.  On the exchange
diagonal \((2z,z^2)\), the invariant visible-law estimand is the common
dimensionless product \(-\log z\), and only for known \(\Delta\tau\) the common
numerical rate \(-\log z/\Delta\tau\), not a separated ordered chart.
\end{convention}

\begin{proposition}[Detailed form of Theorem~B: quotient inverse]\label{prop:toeplitz-image-inverse-duality-detailed}
Work under the constrained D-MAP record model of

r_0=\rho,\qquad r_1=\rho a,\qquad r_2=\rho u_2 .
\]

\begin{equation}\label{eq:population-inverse-domain-main}
r_0>0,\qquad \Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}:=r_1-r_0^2\ne0 .
\end{equation}
On this domain put
\[
a=\frac{r_1}{r_0},\qquad
\lambda=\frac{r_2-r_0^2}{r_1-r_0^2}.
\]
\[

\left( 1-a+\lambda,\,
r_0(1-\lambda)-a+\lambda \right).}
\]

\[

\]

\[

\]

\[

       -\frac{\log s}{\Delta\tau}\right\}

\]

\[
\{-\log p,-\log s\}
=\{\Gamma\Delta\tau,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\}.
\]
Its root discriminant is
\[
\Delta_\sigma:=\Phi_1^2-4\Phi_2=(p-s)^2 .
\]
Within this constrained D-MAP(2) subfamily, the population inverse is
the quotient map
\[
r\longmapsto (p+s,ps).
\]

\]
