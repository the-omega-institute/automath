\begin{abstract}
This paper proves exact record laws for a stipulated two-state sampled renewal
HMM.  Time is sampled in intervals of length \(\Delta\tau\), the hidden state
is ready or recovering, the sampled post-event boundary convention sets the
return-to-ready entries of the event kernel to zero, and the visible record is a
stationary binary sequence.  The ideal one-step blanking model is retained only
as the standard Parry benchmark \(\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi\).  The main
killed-leakage sampled-boundary theorem treats the one-recovery-opportunity HMM
with attempt rate \(\Gamma\) and recovery rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\): its
zero-run law has full support on \(\mathbb N_0\), its zero-run hazard is
strictly increasing, and its binary-record covariance has the hidden-eigenvalue
tail
\(\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{n-1}\).
The visible record is exact \(1\)-dependent exactly at the unique rate
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) where
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\).

A stationary-renewal lemma first constructs click-set probabilities from
renewal products and finite inclusion--exclusion.  After those probabilities
are known, the same finite algebra can be written as an LU determinant
shorthand for that product; the boundary-reset law specializes the inclusion
product to \(\rho\prod_r u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}\).  The same renewal record law has
a quotient inverse: on the inverse domain \(D=r_1-r_0^2\ne0\), three inclusion
probabilities recover the symmetric coordinate vector
\(\sigma_{\rm sym}=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)=(p+s,ps)\), and hence the unordered
survival-factor pair
\(\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}\).  The
discriminant
\(\Delta_\sigma=\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2=(p-s)^2\) controls labelled recovery:
when \(\Delta_\sigma>0\), labelled roots and labelled rates are available only
after an external branch label chooses which root is the attempt survival
factor and which is the recovery survival factor; when
\(\Delta_\sigma=0\), the labelled-rate chart is singular.  Thus
\(\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}\), and one
visible sampled record identifies only the exchange quotient.  The finite-record
part proves a fixed-setting lag-two covariance CLT and a complete-cycle
variance estimator for \(\gamma_2\) in this sampled HMM law.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} classical sampled HMM, renewal process, exact \(1\)-dependence, renewal inclusion products, finite-record diagnostics

\section{Introduction}

This paper is about the record law of a two-state sampled renewal HMM.  The
model has an internal attempt rate \(\Gamma\), one hidden readiness bit, and a
binary output recording whether the sampling interval contains an event.  Its
visible stationary process has grammar, waiting-time, dependence, covariance,
and counting-noise observables that are invisible in a rate-only summary.  The
paper-level object is therefore the sampled record itself: one stipulated
interval law, its stationary binary distribution, its dependence threshold, its
renewal inclusion products, its quotient inverse, and its fixed-setting
long-record lag-two covariance estimator.

The scalar rate \(\Gamma\) is an internal parameter of the sampled counter.
All main-text claims below concern the induced binary record law.

The main text below is organized around one record-law theorem, followed by
supporting result groups and the theorem-by-theorem comparison in
\Cref{sec:theorem-led-comparison}.  The
words event, click, recovery, reset, and count are names for variables and
transitions in this stipulated binary record model.  They do not add any
device-level assumptions beyond the sampled kernels displayed below.

\subsection{Main two-state sampled-renewal HMM theorem}\label{subsec:main-theorem-boundary}\label{subsec:scope-nonclaims}

For fixed
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\in(0,\infty)^3\) and the sampled
boundary-reset kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix},
\]
the visible click process is a stationary renewal process with gap law \(g_k\),
increasing hazard \(h_k\), covariance
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1,
\]
and exact \(1\)-dependence if and only if
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), equivalently
\(\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\).  This is the
sole theorem-level statement of the record law.  Every renewal, covariance,
LU-determinant shorthand, inverse, and fixed-setting long-record CLT statement
in the main text is a supporting consequence of \(T_0,T_1\) and the associated
stationary hidden Markov law.

The main record theorem and its supporting result groups are listed in
\Cref{tab:four-main-blocks}.  Lemmas, propositions, corollaries, and proof
devices are subordinated to one of these groups.  In particular, the generic
cycle-reward lemmas support the fixed-setting inference group.

\subsection{Sampled boundary-reset convention}\label{subsec:model-validity-boundary}

The record analysis begins from the following sampled boundary convention.

\begin{assumption}[Killed-leakage sampled-boundary regime]\label{ass:killed-leakage-boundary-reset-main}
The record results are stated for the sampled boundary-reset kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Equivalently, a bin that contains a click passes the refractory state \(D\) to
the next sampling boundary:
\[
T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0 .
\]
Thus every stationary, spectral, Fano-factor, inverse, and CLT theorem below is
about these sampled kernels.
\end{assumption}

\section{Classical sampled-counter setting and record-level observables}\label{sec:operational}

We first consider only the sampled readout law. Proper time is divided into
intervals of length $\Delta\tau$, a classical counter carries a hidden readiness
state, and the visible output is the stationary two-sided binary process
\[
(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z},
\]
where \(A_n=1\) denotes a click in the \(n\)th sampling interval of the
stationary extension.  All Palm, covariance, renewal, and independence
statements below refer to this two-sided stationary law.  A finite observed
record of length \(N\) is only the restriction
\[
A^{(N)}=(A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_{N-1}),
\]
of the stationary process.  The only nonstationary finite-start convention
used below is the post-burn-in retained-length convention: generate
\(L_N+N\) intervals from an arbitrary hidden initial law, discard the first
\(L_N\), and reindex the retained word as
\((A_{L_N},\ldots,A_{L_N+N-1})\), so \(N\) is always the retained sample size.
Compressing such a finite record to the empirical click
frequency recovers only the one-point observable
\[
\widehat\rho_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n.
\]
The full record, however, carries more. In particular, one may ask:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item what words belong to the support of the stationary law;
\item how the zero-run lengths between clicks are distributed;
\item whether the click hazard after a long run of zeros remembers the run length;
\item how the low-lag covariance sequence behaves;
\item what counting noise remains after coarse time averaging.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{definition}[Record benchmarks]\label{def:record-benchmarks-main}
For a stationary binary record $(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$, a \emph{zero-run length} is the number $G$ of consecutive zeros following a click and preceding the next click. If the zero-run law has full support, its hazard is
\[
h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k),\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
The ideal record is on the \emph{Parry benchmark} when its stationary law is the Parry measure on the golden-mean shift, i.e. the unique measure of maximal entropy subject to the grammar constraint $11\notin\operatorname{supp}\mu$. A stationary record is \emph{exact $1$-dependent} when, for every $m$, the sigma algebras generated by $\{A_j:j\le m\}$ and $\{A_j:j\ge m+2\}$ are independent.
Equivalently, one leaves the intermediate sample at time $m+1$ unobserved: a block ending at time $m$ is tested for independence from a block beginning at time $m+2$, not conditioned on the middle sample.
\end{definition}

These observables are not themselves rate observables, yet in the explicit
readout models below they reduce to sharp thresholds in the internal counter
rate $\Gamma$.

It is useful to keep the record construction in two logically separate steps.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item A counter model determines a stationary word law and a record-level benchmark: the Parry law in the ideal case, exact $1$-dependence in the sampled boundary-reset case.
\item For the two models studied here, that benchmark is first equivalent to a scalar internal counter-rate condition, respectively $\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$ and $\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$.  The memory condition is labelled only after the recovery rate has been independently identified: the inverse theorem gives
\[
\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}
\quad\text{and hence only}\quad
\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}.
\]
Without an external label the memory statement is made on the quotient by
$(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\sim(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)$.
\end{enumerate}
The two counter readout regimes studied in this work have intrinsically
different record thresholds. The ideal model imposes a hard symbolic grammar, so its
distinguished value is the entropy benchmark on the golden-mean shift. The
sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset model instead produces a
full-support renewal law, so its distinguished value is the exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold where the hidden eigenmode vanishes.

\begin{theorem}[Main two-state killed-leakage sampled-renewal HMM theorem]\label{thm:main-record-wrapper}
Fix one positive sampled-boundary setting
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\), set
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},
\]
and define
\[
a=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\bigr)\,dr,
\qquad
b=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr .
\]
Let
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
These are the killed-leakage labelled kernels: a click sends the next
boundary state to \(D\), so \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\).  The row sum
\(P=T_0+T_1\) is stochastic, \(0<p,s<1\), \(a>0\), \(b>0\), and
\(a+b=1-s\).
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be the stationary visible binary process
generated by these labelled kernels, in the convention
\((T_i)_{XY}=\PP(A_n=i,H_{n+1}=Y\mid H_n=X)\).  Define
\[
\rho=\PP(A_0=1),\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b,
\]
with every expression containing \((\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma)^{-1}\)
interpreted by its continuous equal-rate limit, in particular
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=e^{-y}(1-y)\) when
\(y=\Gamma\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\).  On this equal-rate
diagonal, with \(p=s=e^{-y}\), the dictionary used in this theorem is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-wrapper-equal-rate-dictionary}
\begin{aligned}
a&=1-(1+y)p,&
b&=yp,\\
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}&=p-b=(1-y)p,&
S_k&=\PP(G\ge k)=p^k(1+yk),\\
g_k&=p^k\bigl((1-p)(1+yk)-yp\bigr),&
h_k&=\frac{(1-p)+y(k(1-p)-p)}{1+yk},
\qquad k\ge0 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Every covariance, finite-record CLT, batch-covariance, and inverse-delta
assertion below uses this same continuous substitution on the equal-rate
diagonal; there is no separate or arbitrary fallback law.  Then \((A_n)\) is a
stationary full-support renewal process.  Its zero-run law is
\[
g_k=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1,\qquad k\ge0.
\]
For \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\ne\Gamma\),
\[
g_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-s)s^k}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
while at \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}=\Gamma\), writing
\(y=\Gamma\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\) and \(p=e^{-y}\),
\[
g_k=p^k\bigl((1-p)(1+yk)-yp\bigr),\qquad k\ge0.
\]
Its zero-run hazard is strictly
increasing and is given by \eqref{eq:hazard-main}, with the equal-rate
convention \eqref{eq:equal-rate-survival-hazard-main}.  Its covariance is
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)
=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1,
\]
and the visible record is exact \(1\)-dependent iff
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), equivalently
\(\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) under the
non-removable equal-rate convention of
\eqref{eq:gamma-crit-piecewise-main}.  In all parameter cases the visible
record is not a finite-order Markov chain.
\end{theorem}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The killed-leakage sampled-boundary theorem is the main mathematical result:
one classical sampled counter law produces a full-support renewal record with
strictly increasing hazard, suppressed adjacent-click conditional probability,
classical sub-Poisson count noise, a unique exact \(1\)-dependence threshold at
the zero of the hidden eigenmode, renewal inclusion products with optional
LU determinant shorthand, a closed Fano law, and a three-inclusion inverse with
\(D=r_1-r_0^2\ne0\), \(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) exchange
duality, and no labelled-rate chart at \(p=s\). The ideal Parry
point remains the benchmark comparison rather than the main theorem.
All items in this list are sampled-record statements.  The appendix
continuous-recovery comparator is confined to fixed finite cylinders and does
not supply a stationary or asymptotic-transfer interpretation for these
formulas.

The theorem-family comparison in \Cref{tab:main-jpa-comparison} records how
these sampled-counter results differ from HMM record laws,
finite-dependence theory, regenerative CLTs, trajectory models, and
memory-inference results.

\section{Fixed-Window Comparator for Killed-Leakage Sampling}\label{app:fixed-window-comparator}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-window comparison of killed-leakage sampled-boundary and continuous intra-bin recovery]\label{prop:model-validity-boundary-main}\label{prop:continuous-comparator-main}\label{prop:short-bin-boundary-reset-main}\label{prop:finite-window-continuous-comparator-main}
Put
\[
x=\Gamma\Delta\tau,\qquad y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau,\qquad
p=e^{-x},\qquad s=e^{-y},
\]
and
\[
b=\int_0^1 y e^{-yt}e^{-x(1-t)}\,dt,\qquad
a=\int_0^1 y e^{-yt}\bigl(1-e^{-x(1-t)}\bigr)\,dt .
\]
Let \(T_0,T_1\) be the sampled boundary-reset kernels in
\Cref{ass:killed-leakage-boundary-reset-main}.  Let
\(\widetilde T_0,\widetilde T_1\) be the continuous intra-bin comparator:
within one bin the hidden state evolves as
\[
R \xrightarrow{\ \Gamma\ } D,\qquad
D \xrightarrow{\ \kappa_{\mathrm r}\ } R,
\]
and the visible output records only whether at least one click occurred before
the boundary.  Then
\[
\widetilde T_0=T_0,\qquad
\widetilde T_1-T_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
\delta_R&-\delta_R\\
\delta_D&-\delta_D
\end{pmatrix},
\]
where
\[
\delta_R=
\frac{y(1-e^{-x})-x e^{-x}(1-e^{-y})}{x+y},
\qquad
\delta_D=
\frac{y}{x+y}\bigl(1-e^{-(x+y)}\bigr)-b.
\]
Moreover \(\delta_R>0\) and \(\delta_D>0\), whereas the sampled kernels set the
same two leakage entries to zero.

Let
\[
\eta_{x,y}:=\max\{\delta_R,\delta_D\}.
\]
For every initial hidden law \(\alpha\), every fixed word length \(m\ge1\), let
\(\mathsf P_{\alpha}^{(m)}\) and
\(\widetilde{\mathsf P}_{\alpha}^{(m)}\) be the two output laws on
\(\{0,1\}^m\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-window-tv-comparator-main}
\bigl\|\mathsf P_{\alpha}^{(m)}
-\widetilde{\mathsf P}_{\alpha}^{(m)}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le m\,\eta_{x,y}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every bounded function
\(F:\{0,1\}^m\to\mathbb R\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-window-test-function-comparator-main}
\left|
\EE_{\rm sampled}F(A_0,\ldots,A_{m-1})
-
\EE_{\rm cont}F(A_0,\ldots,A_{m-1})
\right|
\le
2\|F\|_\infty\,m\,\eta_{x,y}.
\end{equation}
In the dimensionless small-bin regime \(x=\Gamma\Delta\tau\to0\) and
\(y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\to0\), with \(m\) fixed,
\[
\delta_R=\frac12xy+O((x+y)^3),
\qquad
\delta_D=\frac16xy^2+O((x+y)^4),
\]
where the constants in the two \(O\)-terms are uniform for
\(0\le x,y\le\varepsilon\), for each sufficiently small fixed
\(\varepsilon>0\).
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-window-tv-shortbin-main}
\bigl\|\mathsf P_{\alpha}^{(m)}
-\widetilde{\mathsf P}_{\alpha}^{(m)}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\frac12mxy+O_m((x+y)^3).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The no-click kernel is unchanged, because no reset can occur before the first
recorded click.  For the hidden continuous-time generator \(Q\) with rates
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\),
\[
e^{Q\Delta\tau}
=\frac1{x+y}
\begin{pmatrix}
y+x e^{-(x+y)}&x(1-e^{-(x+y)})\\
y(1-e^{-(x+y)})&x+y e^{-(x+y)}
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and subtracting \(T_0\) and \(T_1\) gives the displayed correction.  Its two
positive entries are exactly the paths suppressed by the sampled
boundary-reset convention: click-then-recover within the same bin from \(R\),
and recover-click-recover within the same bin from \(D\).  Thus the one-step
total-variation distance between the two labelled laws on
\(\{0,1\}\times\{R,D\}\) is at most \(\eta_{x,y}\).  A stepwise maximal coupling
and the union bound give \eqref{eq:finite-window-tv-comparator-main}; the
bounded-test-function estimate is the total-variation dual inequality.
Taylor expansion of the displayed entire integral formulas gives the stated
uniform small-bin expansions, and substituting them into
\eqref{eq:finite-window-tv-comparator-main} gives
\eqref{eq:finite-window-tv-shortbin-main}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Warning.}
\Cref{prop:finite-window-continuous-comparator-main} is only a fixed-cylinder
bound.  If the window grows, the displayed estimate is informative only under
an additional condition such as \(m_N\eta_{x,y}\to0\), and for a full
\(N\)-bin word this becomes \(N\eta_{x,y}\to0\).  That law-level condition is
not a stationary, spectral, inverse, Fano-factor, or CLT perturbation theorem:
the main stationary and finite-record theorems remain statements about the
sampled kernels \(T_0,T_1\) in
\Cref{ass:killed-leakage-boundary-reset-main}, and no continuous-comparator
long-record transfer is asserted.
