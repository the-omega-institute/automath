\begin{abstract}
We study a classical sampled counter whose visible binary law is fixed at the
readout level. Its internal attempt rate is a parameter \(\Gamma\); only after
the record law is proved do we impose the conditional calibration
\(\Gamma=R_\Omega(x)=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)\) of Assumption~A0. No UDW measurement
model is derived. The main contribution is the exact analysis of the sampled
one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset record, with the ideal dead-time Parry
point retained only as the standard benchmark
\(\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi\).

For the sampled boundary-reset rule, the zero-run law has full support on
\(\mathbb N_0\), the zero-run hazard is strictly increasing, and the visible
record becomes exact \(1\)-dependent exactly at the unique rate
\(\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) where the hidden eigenvalue
vanishes. A stationary-renewal principal-minor lemma gives the finite-inclusion
formula, and the boundary-reset law specializes it to an explicit
non-Hermitian Toeplitz kernel with geometric upper tail. This is only an
algebraic family of finite principal-minor identities: it does not give a
Hermitian positive-contraction kernel, negative association, or a fermionic
interpretation. The Toeplitz record law also has an exact inverse: three
inclusion probabilities recover the unordered attempt/recovery rate pair,
revealing a \(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) exchange duality and
labelled-rate non-identifiability from one visible record. Relative to true
continuous intra-bin recovery, the sampled click kernel removes the positive
post-click return-to-ready leakage, whose leading orders are
\(\frac12\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}(\Delta\tau)^2\) from an initially ready bin
and \(\frac16\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}^2(\Delta\tau)^3\) from an initially
refractory bin.

The response section is the corresponding fixed-profile level-set theorem.
For a supplied positive \(C^1\) response function
\[
R_\Omega(x)=\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2F_x(\Omega)
=\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2
\frac{\mathcal S_x(\Omega)}{e^{\beta_\infty N(x)\Omega}-1},
\]
the record-selected targets
\(\gamma_\varphi=\log\varphi/\Delta\tau\) and
\(\gamma_{\mathrm{mem}}=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) define the
response loci \(R_\Omega^{-1}(\gamma_\varphi)\) and
\(R_\Omega^{-1}(\gamma_{\mathrm{mem}})\). They are called shells only at
regular level-set points after the target is in the range of \(R_\Omega\).
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} sampled counter, hidden Markov model, Toeplitz principal-minor formula, finite-record diagnostics, conditional response calibration

\section{Introduction}

This paper is about the record law of a classical sampled counter. The counter
has an internal attempt rate \(\Gamma\), one hidden readiness bit, and a binary
output recording whether the sampling interval contains a click. Its visible
process has grammar, waiting-time, dependence, covariance, and counting-noise
observables that are invisible in a rate-only summary. Transition rates,
detailed balance, and Tolman redshift remain the standard observables used to
characterize Unruh and Hawking thermality
\cite{Tolman1930,TolmanEhrenfest1930,Unruh1976,DeWitt1979,BirDav1982,Takagi1986,CrispinoHiguchiMatsas2008,LoukoSatz2006,LoukoSatz2008},
but here they enter only after the record theorem has been proved. The
paper-level object is a \emph{classical sampled counter with conditional
response calibration}: one sampled interval instrument whose attempt rate is
later identified with a supplied stationary excitation profile only through the
explicit A0 closure \(\Gamma=R_\Omega(x)=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)\). The sampled
counter is specified first; Assumption~A0 names the closure, and the
fixed-response theorem applies the target-range and regularity checks.

This problem sits near hidden-Markov process theory, finite-dependence
asymptotics, trajectory memory inference, dead-time counting, stationary UDW
response theory, detector regularization, and detector-based QFT measurement
theory, but it does not make the same claims as any of those literatures. The
stochastic comparison classes matter for the sampled-record theorems; the
detector and AQFT comparison classes matter only as boundary references for the
conditional response-locus interpretation. \Cref{tab:main-jpa-comparison} is
placed before the theorem summary because the novelty split is theorem-family
by theorem-family rather than a single global claim.

The answer has two layers. The first layer is record theory, with the new
mathematics concentrated in the sampled boundary-reset law. In the ideal
one-step dead-time mode the record is the stipulated golden-mean Markov chain,
and the standard Parry calculation gives the benchmark clock value
$\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$. In the sampled one-recovery-opportunity
boundary-reset mode the visible process is instead a full-support renewal law.
Its zero-run hazard is strictly increasing, every finite binary word remains
admissible, and the visible record becomes exact $1$-dependent exactly when
the hidden eigenvalue vanishes, at the unique rate
$\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$. A general renewal
principal-minor lemma gives the finite-inclusion determinant identity, and the
boundary-reset law supplies the explicit non-Hermitian Toeplitz specialization
with geometric strict-upper tail; this is not a Hermitian positive-contraction
DPP theorem. At
$\Gamma_c$ its transfer parameter vanishes, giving exact $1$-dependence
without finite visible Markov order. The Toeplitz record law also has an exact
inverse: three inclusion probabilities recover the unordered attempt/recovery
rate pair, revealing a $\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}$ exchange
duality and a labelled-rate non-identifiability from one visible record. The
memory threshold is therefore not an entropy benchmark but an exact dependence
transition inside a renewal family that still supports the full binary
grammar.

After these two record thresholds are fixed, Section~4 keeps only the short
fixed-response theorem for the calibrated stationary response profile
\[
R_\Omega(x)=\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\frac{\mathcal S_x(\Omega)}{e^{\beta_\infty N(x)\Omega}-1}.
\]
For one calibrated response function, the ideal and memory target equations
define two response loci, and the theorem explains that they are called shells
only after the range check and the regularity condition \(dR_\Omega\neq 0\) are
verified. The longer homogeneous quotient, near-horizon,
fixed-radius inverse/sensitivity, and admissibility statements are not part of
the main article; they are split-candidate conditional response-model algebra
requiring their own stable-system, external-data, and comparison assumptions,
rather than new detector-dynamics theorems in this paper.
The finite-record theorem on the record side estimates only the fixed-setting
covariance $\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)$ and its variance; for the
sampled boundary-reset law, a coboundary argument proves the cycle variance is
strictly positive for all positive parameters, so the fixed-setting
studentized Wald interval is not conditional on a numerical positivity check.
\Cref{tab:main-jpa-comparison} records the adjacent output-statistics,
trajectory-memory, one-dependent determinantal, DPP, and AQFT measurement
comparison classes
\cite{Glauber1963,MandelWolf1995,SrinivasDavies1981,Carmichael1993Book,WisemanMilburn2009,BarchielliGregoratti2009,Sorkin1993,FewsterVerch2020,Fewster2020,PoloGomezEtAl2022,PranziniKeskiVakkuri2025AQFT,Oeckl2025CausalMeasurement}.

Dead time and recovery are also physically natural. In nuclear and photon counting, refractory effects have been central since the earliest pulse detectors and remain standard in modern single-photon models \cite{Muller1973,CantorTeich1975,Hadfield2009,EisamanEtAl2011}. The observables used here---grammar, hazard, dependence class, covariance tail, and low-frequency noise---therefore belong to the standard counting-statistics fingerprint of a sampled detector output. Their role is not to replace the local rate, but to show that keeping the full record produces exact thresholds that can later be lifted to response-model level sets.

The paper is organized accordingly. \Cref{sec:operational} and
\Cref{sec:records} define the record-level observables, record the standard
exact-clock Parry benchmark for the ideal counter, and carry the main
sampled-boundary-reset theorem chain for the memory-transition threshold and
its operational signatures.
\Cref{sec:kms-shells} then states Assumption~A0 and the short fixed-response
theorem for one stationary response function. Longer homogeneous,
near-horizon, fixed-radius, Schwarzschild, external-ratio, and residual-graph
developments are outside the submitted theorem chain.
To reduce the symbol load before the technical record and response
calculations, Tables~\ref{tab:notation-record}--\ref{tab:notation-response}
split the recurring notation into record and response layers for
Sections~\ref{sec:records} and~\ref{sec:kms-shells}.

\section{Classical sampled-counter setting and record-level observables}\label{sec:operational}

We first consider only the sampled readout law. Proper time is divided into
intervals of length $\Delta\tau$, a classical counter carries a hidden readiness
state, and the visible output is a stationary binary process
\[
A_0,A_1,A_2,\dots \in \{0,1\},
\]
where $A_n=1$ denotes a click in the $n$th sampling interval. The word
``detector'' in Sections~\ref{sec:operational}--\ref{sec:records} refers to
this classical readout mode unless the later calibration closure
$\Gamma=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)$ is explicitly invoked. Compressing the record to the
empirical click frequency recovers only the one-point observable
\[
\widehat\rho_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n.
\]
The full record, however, carries more. In particular, one may ask:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item what words belong to the support of the stationary law;
\item how the zero-run lengths between clicks are distributed;
\item whether the click hazard after a long run of zeros remembers the run length;
\item how the low-lag covariance profile behaves;
\item what counting noise remains after coarse time averaging.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{definition}[Record benchmarks]\label{def:record-benchmarks-main}
For a stationary binary record $(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$, a \emph{zero-run length} is the number $G$ of consecutive zeros following a click and preceding the next click. If the zero-run law has full support, its hazard is
\[
h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k),\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
The ideal record is on the \emph{Parry benchmark} when its stationary law is the Parry measure on the golden-mean shift, i.e. the unique measure of maximal entropy subject to the grammar constraint $11\notin\operatorname{supp}\mu$. A stationary record is \emph{exact $1$-dependent} when, for every $m$, the sigma algebras generated by $\{A_j:j\le m\}$ and $\{A_j:j\ge m+2\}$ are independent.
Equivalently, one leaves the intermediate sample at time $m+1$ unobserved: a block ending at time $m$ is tested for independence from a block beginning at time $m+2$, not conditioned on the middle sample.
\end{definition}

These observables are not themselves rate observables, yet in the explicit
readout models below they reduce to sharp thresholds in the internal counter
rate $\Gamma$. That reduction is important: response geometry enters only after
the stochastic-record benchmark has already fixed the target rate and the
separate calibration closure has been imposed.

It is useful to keep the construction in three logically separate steps.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item A counter model determines a stationary word law and a record-level benchmark: the Parry law in the ideal case, exact $1$-dependence in the sampled boundary-reset case.
\item For the two models studied here, that benchmark is first equivalent to a scalar internal counter-rate condition, respectively $\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$ and $\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$. Under the paper's calibration closure $\Gamma=\Gamma_+$, these become $\Gamma_+\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$ and $\Gamma_+=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$.
\item Substitution of the specified static KMS response then turns those rate conditions into level-set equations for the detector response.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the response loci are model level sets whose constants are selected by
full-record statistics; detailed balance supplies only the thermal rate ratio.

The two detector regimes studied in this work are illustrated schematically in \cref{fig:scheme}. The ideal model imposes a hard symbolic grammar, while the sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset model produces a renewal law whose zero-run distribution has full support and hence allows every finite binary word. The thresholds associated with the two regimes are therefore intrinsically different: one is entropic and grammar-based, the other is correlation-based.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The sampled boundary-reset theorem is the main mathematical result: one
classical sampled counter law produces a full-support renewal record with
strictly increasing hazard, universal antibunching, a unique exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold at the zero of the hidden eigenmode, a
non-Hermitian Toeplitz principal-minor count formula, a closed Fano law, and a
three-inclusion inverse with
\(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) exchange duality. The ideal Parry
point remains the benchmark comparison rather than the main theorem.

The response theorem is the fixed-profile consequence of those record-selected
target rates under A0. It turns the two scalar rates into level sets of one
supplied \(C^1\) response function and calls them shells only after the range
and regularity checks. The theorem-family comparison in
\Cref{tab:main-jpa-comparison} records how this sampled-counter theorem chain
differs from record-HMM, one-dependent/regenerative determinantal, DPP,
stochastic-trajectory, memory-inference, and UDW/AQFT detector results.
