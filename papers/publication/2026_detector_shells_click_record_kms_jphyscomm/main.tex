\begin{abstract}
This paper proves exact laws for a stipulated classical sampled HMM renewal
process.  Time is sampled in intervals of length \(\Delta\tau\), the hidden
state records readiness or recovery, and the visible record is a binary
sequence.  The theorem chain is entirely record-level.  The ideal one-step
blanking model is retained only as the standard Parry benchmark
\(\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi\). The main sampled boundary-reset theorem
treats the one-recovery-opportunity counter with attempt rate \(\Gamma\) and
recovery rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\): its zero-run law has full support on
\(\mathbb N_0\), its zero-run hazard is strictly increasing, and its click
covariance has the hidden-eigenvalue tail
\(\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{n-1}\).
The visible record is exact \(1\)-dependent exactly at the unique rate
\(\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) where
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\).

A stationary-renewal lemma gives a renewal principal-minor encoding for the
click set, and the boundary-reset law specializes it to an explicit
Toeplitz matrix with
\(\det K_B=\rho\prod_r u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}\). The same principal-minor record law has a quotient inverse:
three inclusion probabilities recover the symmetric coordinates, and hence at
the population level the unordered survival-factor pair
\(\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}\), equivalently
\(\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}\). Thus one
visible sampled record does not label which recovered rate is the recovery
rate. Finite-record inverse inference is stated for the projected feasible
symmetric coordinates \((s_1,s_2)\); ordinary root and rate covariance
estimates require a separated-root hypothesis away from the exchange diagonal
\(e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\). The finite-record
part then proves a fixed-setting lag-two covariance CLT and a complete-cycle
variance estimator for \(\gamma_2\) in this sampled-counter law.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} classical sampled HMM, renewal process, exact \(1\)-dependence, renewal principal-minor encoding, finite-record diagnostics

\section{Introduction}

This paper is about the record law of a classical sampled HMM.  The model has
an internal attempt rate \(\Gamma\), one hidden readiness bit, and a binary
output recording whether the sampling interval contains a click. Its visible
process has grammar, waiting-time, dependence, covariance, and counting-noise
observables that are invisible in a rate-only summary. The paper-level object
is therefore the sampled record itself: one stipulated interval instrument, its
stationary binary law, its dependence threshold, its renewal-product
principal-minor encoding, its quotient inverse, and its fixed-record lag-two
covariance estimator.

Transition rates, detailed balance, and Tolman redshift remain the standard
observables used to characterize Unruh and Hawking thermality
\cite{Tolman1930,TolmanEhrenfest1930,Unruh1976,DeWitt1979,BirDav1982,Takagi1986,CrispinoHiguchiMatsas2008,LoukoSatz2006,LoukoSatz2008},
but they are not inputs to the sampled-counter theorem. They enter only in the
external calibration section, after the classical record law has already
selected scalar target rates.

The main text below is organized as four record-side theorem blocks, followed by
the theorem-by-theorem comparison in \Cref{sec:theorem-led-comparison}.

\subsection{Main theorem and scope boundary}\label{subsec:main-theorem-boundary}\label{subsec:scope-nonclaims}

The theorem boundary is part of the statement of the paper and will not be
repeated inside each theorem block.  The main theorem is a theorem about the
following two labelled stochastic kernels and their visible binary process.
For fixed
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\in(0,\infty)^3\) and the sampled
boundary-reset kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&c\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix},
\]
the visible click process is a stationary renewal process with gap law \(g_k\),
increasing hazard \(h_k\), covariance
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1,
\]
and exact \(1\)-dependence if and only if
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), equivalently
\(\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\).  This is the main record
theorem.  Every renewal, covariance, finite-principal-minor, inverse, and
fixed-record CLT statement in the main theorem chain is a consequence of
\(T_0,T_1\) and the associated stationary hidden Markov law.

Second, \(\Gamma\) is a record-side parameter until a separate calibration
supplies one positive \(C^1\) response profile and an external
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) label.  Only then may the scalar record condition be
translated into
\[
\Gamma=R_\Omega(x),\qquad
R_\Omega(x)=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau).
\]
The regular-level-set theorem then supplies only a local codimension-one
hypersurface at regular points of that supplied function.  The theorem chain
does not use any microscopic detector dynamics, labelled hidden-kernel
recovery, scan-root design, response-level inference, or determinant-kernel
admissibility theorem.

The submitted theorem chain has four main blocks, listed in
\Cref{tab:four-main-blocks}.  Lemmas, corollaries, and proof devices are
subordinated to one of these blocks.  In particular, the generic cycle-reward
lemmas prove Block~IV.  The appendix calibration note is a conditional
translation of record-selected constants for an externally supplied scalar
profile, not a fifth main theorem family.

\subsection{Sampled boundary-reset convention}\label{subsec:model-validity-boundary}

The record theorem chain begins from the following sampled boundary convention;
\Cref{app:fixed-window-comparator} records only a fixed-window continuous
intra-bin comparison.

\begin{assumption}[Killed-leakage sampled-boundary regime]\label{ass:killed-leakage-boundary-reset-main}
The record theorem chain is stated for the sampled boundary-reset kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&c\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Equivalently, a bin that contains a click passes the refractory state \(D\) to
the next sampling boundary:
\[
T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0 .
\]
The positive post-click return-to-ready leakage present under true continuous
intra-bin recovery is therefore killed by the sampled boundary convention.  This
is a theorem-level model assumption, not a consequence of a continuous detector
evolution.
\end{assumption}

\section{Classical sampled-counter setting and record-level observables}\label{sec:operational}

We first consider only the sampled readout law. Proper time is divided into
intervals of length $\Delta\tau$, a classical counter carries a hidden readiness
state, and the visible output is a stationary binary process
\[
A_0,A_1,A_2,\dots \in \{0,1\},
\]
where $A_n=1$ denotes a click in the $n$th sampling interval. Compressing the record to the
empirical click frequency recovers only the one-point observable
\[
\widehat\rho_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n.
\]
The full record, however, carries more. In particular, one may ask:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item what words belong to the support of the stationary law;
\item how the zero-run lengths between clicks are distributed;
\item whether the click hazard after a long run of zeros remembers the run length;
\item how the low-lag covariance profile behaves;
\item what counting noise remains after coarse time averaging.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{definition}[Record benchmarks]\label{def:record-benchmarks-main}
For a stationary binary record $(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$, a \emph{zero-run length} is the number $G$ of consecutive zeros following a click and preceding the next click. If the zero-run law has full support, its hazard is
\[
h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k),\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
The ideal record is on the \emph{Parry benchmark} when its stationary law is the Parry measure on the golden-mean shift, i.e. the unique measure of maximal entropy subject to the grammar constraint $11\notin\operatorname{supp}\mu$. A stationary record is \emph{exact $1$-dependent} when, for every $m$, the sigma algebras generated by $\{A_j:j\le m\}$ and $\{A_j:j\ge m+2\}$ are independent.
Equivalently, one leaves the intermediate sample at time $m+1$ unobserved: a block ending at time $m$ is tested for independence from a block beginning at time $m+2$, not conditioned on the middle sample.
\end{definition}

These observables are not themselves rate observables, yet in the explicit
readout models below they reduce to sharp thresholds in the internal counter
rate $\Gamma$.

It is useful to keep the record construction in two logically separate steps.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item A counter model determines a stationary word law and a record-level benchmark: the Parry law in the ideal case, exact $1$-dependence in the sampled boundary-reset case.
\item For the two models studied here, that benchmark is first equivalent to a scalar internal counter-rate condition, respectively $\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$ and $\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$.  The memory condition is labelled only after the recovery rate has been independently identified: the inverse theorem gives
\[
\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}
\quad\text{and hence only}\quad
\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}.
\]
Without an external label the memory statement is made on the quotient by
$(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\sim(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)$.
\end{enumerate}
After these record statements are complete, the optional calibration note may
evaluate a supplied scalar profile \(R_\Omega\) at the selected constants.

The two detector regimes studied in this work have intrinsically different
record thresholds. The ideal model imposes a hard symbolic grammar, so its
distinguished value is the entropy benchmark on the golden-mean shift. The
sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset model instead produces a
full-support renewal law, so its distinguished value is the exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold where the hidden eigenmode vanishes.

\begin{theorem}[Main record theorem]\label{thm:main-record-wrapper}
Fix \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\in(0,\infty)^3\) and the sampled
boundary-reset kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&c\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then the visible process is a stationary renewal process with gap law \(g_k\),
increasing hazard \(h_k\), covariance
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)
=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1,
\]
and exact \(1\)-dependence iff \(\lambda_{\rm hid}=0\).
\end{theorem}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The sampled boundary-reset theorem is the main mathematical result: one
classical sampled counter law produces a full-support renewal record with
strictly increasing hazard, suppressed adjacent-click conditional probability,
classical sub-Poisson count noise, a unique exact \(1\)-dependence threshold at
the zero of the hidden eigenmode, a Toeplitz renewal
finite-principal-minor encoding, a closed Fano law, and a three-inclusion inverse with
\(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) exchange duality. The ideal Parry
point remains the benchmark comparison rather than the main theorem.

The theorem-family comparison in \Cref{tab:main-jpa-comparison} records how
this sampled-counter theorem chain differs from HMM record laws,
one-dependent determinant formulas, regenerative CLTs, trajectory models,
memory-inference results, and UDW or AQFT detector theorems.

\section{Fixed-Window Sampled-Reset Comparator}\label{app:fixed-window-comparator}

This appendix records the fixed-cylinder comparison between the sampled
boundary-reset kernels and a continuous intra-bin hidden recovery model.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-window comparison with continuous intra-bin recovery]\label{prop:model-validity-boundary-main}\label{prop:continuous-comparator-main}\label{prop:short-bin-boundary-reset-main}\label{prop:finite-window-continuous-comparator-main}
This appendix proposition is the precise regime in which the killed-leakage
sampled approximation is used: the dimensionless bin parameters
\(x=\Gamma\Delta\tau\) and \(y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\) are small, and
the comparison is only for a fixed finite output window.  The conclusion below
is a cylinder-law estimate; it is not a stationary-law, spectral, or
infinite-record approximation theorem, nor a Fano-factor or CLT transfer.
Put
\[
x=\Gamma\Delta\tau,\qquad y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau,\qquad
p=e^{-x},\qquad c=e^{-y},
\]
and
\[
b=\int_0^1 y e^{-yt}e^{-x(1-t)}\,dt,\qquad
a=\int_0^1 y e^{-yt}\bigl(1-e^{-x(1-t)}\bigr)\,dt .
\]
Let \(T_0,T_1\) be the sampled boundary-reset kernels in
\Cref{ass:killed-leakage-boundary-reset-main}.  Let
\(\widetilde T_0,\widetilde T_1\) be the comparator kernels obtained by
letting the hidden state evolve continuously during a bin as
\[
R \xrightarrow{\ \Gamma\ } D,\qquad
D \xrightarrow{\ \kappa_{\mathrm r}\ } R,
\]
while the visible ancilla still records only whether at least one click occurred
before the boundary.  Then
\[
\widetilde T_0=T_0,\qquad
\widetilde T_1-T_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
\delta_R&-\delta_R\\
\delta_D&-\delta_D
\end{pmatrix},
\]
where
\[
\delta_R=
\frac{y(1-e^{-x})-x e^{-x}(1-e^{-y})}{x+y},
\qquad
\delta_D=
\frac{y}{x+y}\bigl(1-e^{-(x+y)}\bigr)-b.
\]
Moreover \(\delta_R>0\) and \(\delta_D>0\), whereas the sampled kernels set the
same two leakage entries to zero.

Let
\[
\eta_{x,y}:=\max\{\delta_R,\delta_D\}.
\]
For every initial hidden law \(\alpha\) and every word length \(m\ge1\), let
\(\mathsf P_{\alpha}^{(m)}\) and
\(\widetilde{\mathsf P}_{\alpha}^{(m)}\) be the two output laws on
\(\{0,1\}^m\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-window-tv-comparator-main}
\bigl\|\mathsf P_{\alpha}^{(m)}
-\widetilde{\mathsf P}_{\alpha}^{(m)}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le m\,\eta_{x,y}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every bounded function
\(F:\{0,1\}^m\to\mathbb R\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-window-test-function-comparator-main}
\left|
\EE_{\rm sampled}F(A_0,\ldots,A_{m-1})
-
\EE_{\rm cont}F(A_0,\ldots,A_{m-1})
\right|
\le
2\|F\|_\infty\,m\,\eta_{x,y}.
\end{equation}
Every fixed cylinder probability differs by at most \(m\eta_{x,y}\).
In the dimensionless small-bin regime \(x=\Gamma\Delta\tau\to0\) and
\(y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\to0\), with \(m\) fixed,
\[
\delta_R=\frac12xy+O((x+y)^3),
\qquad
\delta_D=\frac16xy^2+O((x+y)^4),
\]
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-window-tv-shortbin-main}
\bigl\|\mathsf P_{\alpha}^{(m)}
-\widetilde{\mathsf P}_{\alpha}^{(m)}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\frac12mxy+O_m((x+y)^3).
\end{equation}
If \(\Gamma\) is later supplied by the response-side closure
\(\Gamma=R_\Omega(x_{\mathrm{sp}})\), the same estimate is read at that fixed
calibrated rate.
\end{proposition}

The bound in \Cref{prop:finite-window-continuous-comparator-main} is a
fixed-window comparison.  Growing-window conclusions require additional
assumptions, such as \(m_N\eta_{x,y}\to0\), and a corresponding perturbation
proof.

\begin{proof}
The no-click event occurs before any reset can matter, so the comparator and
the sampled model have the same output-\(0\) kernel.  The continuous hidden
boundary transition matrix is
\[
e^{Q\Delta\tau}
=\frac1{x+y}
\begin{pmatrix}
y+x e^{-(x+y)}&x(1-e^{-(x+y)})\\
y(1-e^{-(x+y)})&x+y e^{-(x+y)}
\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(Q\) is the two-state generator with rates
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).  Subtracting \(T_0\) gives
\(\widetilde T_1\), and subtracting the sampled \(T_1\) gives the displayed
rank-one correction.  The entries \(\delta_R,\delta_D\) are strictly positive:
from \(R\), there is positive probability of a click followed by recovery
before the boundary; from \(D\), there is positive probability of recovery,
click, and a second recovery before the boundary.

For a fixed current hidden state, the one-step total variation distance between
the two labelled laws on \(\{0,1\}\times\{R,D\}\) is at most
\(\eta_{x,y}\).  Coupling the two chains step-by-step by maximal one-step
couplings, the visible words can differ during the first \(m\) bins only if at
least one of the \(m\) labelled transitions disagrees.  The union bound gives
\eqref{eq:finite-window-tv-comparator-main}.  The test-function bound
\eqref{eq:finite-window-test-function-comparator-main} is the standard
total-variation dual inequality, and the cylinder bound follows by applying
\eqref{eq:finite-window-tv-comparator-main} to a cylinder indicator.  Taylor
expansion of the displayed dimensionless formulas gives the two leakage
expansions and then
\eqref{eq:finite-window-tv-shortbin-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-window comparator scope]\label{cor:finite-window-only-comparator-main}
\Cref{prop:finite-window-continuous-comparator-main} has only the following
content.  For each fixed \(m\), fixed initial hidden law \(\alpha\), and bounded
test function on \(\{0,1\}^m\), the sampled boundary-reset output law and the
continuous intra-bin comparator output law differ by the total-variation and
expectation bounds
\[
\bigl\|\mathsf P_{\alpha}^{(m)}
-\widetilde{\mathsf P}_{\alpha}^{(m)}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le m\eta_{x,y},
\qquad
\left|\EE_{\rm sampled}F-\EE_{\rm cont}F\right|
\le2\|F\|_\infty m\eta_{x,y}.
\]
Consequently every use of the proposition is confined to fixed cylinders and
fixed-window bounded observables; the later stationary, spectral, Fano-factor,
and CLT statements remain statements about the sampled kernels \(T_0,T_1\).

For a growing record window \(m_N\to\infty\), even cylinder-level comparison
requires at least the additional scaling condition
\[
m_N\eta_{x,y}\to0.
\]
For a full record of length \(N\), this reads \(N\eta_{x,y}\to0\).
Stationary spectra, long-window count variances, and CLTs would still require
their own perturbation theorem after such a scaling hypothesis is imposed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first display repeats the bounds in
\Cref{prop:finite-window-continuous-comparator-main}.  If \(m\) is fixed, the
right-hand side tends to zero in the small-bin regime.  If \(m=m_N\) grows, the
same bound is informative only under \(m_N\eta_{x,y}\to0\).  The remaining
claims follow because stationary laws, spectral densities, Fano limits, and
CLTs depend on limits in the window length or record length, not on a fixed
cylinder functional.
\end{proof}

\section{Renewal--Reward Proof Package for Block IV}\label{app:renewal-reward-proof-package}

This appendix contains one reusable finite-lag renewal-reward theorem.  It
combines the renewal CLT, stationary-window boundary control, random complete
cycle counts, plug-in centering, and separated-batch variance consistency used
by the finite-record theorem.  The result is proof machinery for the sampled
boundary-reset theorem, not an additional statistical output of the paper.  The
regenerative CLT input is classical: finite-dependent CLTs go back to
Hoeffding--Robbins and Diananda
\cite{HoeffdingRobbins1948,Diananda1955}, while regenerative-increment CLTs
and their renewal random-index form are standard in the Serfozo/Janson
formulation \cite{Serfozo2009,Janson2024RenewalCLT}.  The proof below records
only the hypotheses needed here, because the sampled counter has bounded local
observables and exponentially-tailed cycles.

\begin{lemma}[Regenerative \(q\)-dependent reward CLT]\label{lem:regenerative-qdependent-clt-main}
Let \(L_1,L_2,\ldots\) be i.i.d. positive integer-valued cycle lengths with
\[
\mu_L=\EE L_1\in(0,\infty),
\qquad
\EE L_1^{2+\delta}<\infty
\]
for some \(\delta>0\).  Let
\[
Y_j=f(L_j,\ldots,L_{j+q})
\]
be a centered, stationary, \(\RR^d\)-valued, \(q\)-dependent reward sequence
with \(\EE\|Y_1\|^{2+\delta}<\infty\).  Put
\[
\Gamma_Y=
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_1)
+\sum_{h=1}^q
\left\{
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_{1+h})
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{1+h},Y_1)
\right\}.
\]
If
\[
K_N=\max\left\{k:\sum_{j=1}^kL_j\le N\right\},
\]
then
\[
N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}Y_j
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Gamma_Y/\mu_L).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each \(u\in\RR^d\), the scalar sequence \(u^\top Y_j\) is centered,
stationary, \(q\)-dependent, and has a \(2+\delta\) moment.  The classical
\(m\)-dependent central limit theorem
\cite{HoeffdingRobbins1948,Diananda1955}, applied by Cramer--Wold, gives
\[
m^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}Y_j
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Gamma_Y).
\]
The renewal strong law gives \(K_N/N\to1/\mu_L\).  Anscombe's theorem
\cite{Anscombe1952}, in its standard finite-dependent random-index form,
therefore transfers the deterministic-index CLT to the renewal index:
\[
N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}Y_j
=
\sqrt{\frac{K_N}{N}}\,
K_N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}Y_j
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Gamma_Y/\mu_L).
\]
Equivalently, this is the renewal-increment CLT formulation used in
\cite{Serfozo2009,Janson2024RenewalCLT}.  The exponential-tail assumptions
used later in this paper are stronger than the displayed moment hypotheses and
therefore automatically supply the conditions of the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Boundary-cycle tails, \(q\)-dependent regenerative CLT transfer, and random batch counts]\label{lem:renewal-index-prelim-main}
Let \(L_1,L_2,\ldots\) be i.i.d. positive integer-valued cycle lengths with
\[
\mu_L=\EE L_1\in(0,\infty),
\qquad
\PP(L_1\ge k)\le C e^{-\eta k}\quad(k\ge1).
\]
Then the following three facts hold.

\emph{(i) Stationary boundary cycles.}  In the stationary renewal version, the
cycle containing a deterministic time has the size-biased law
\[
\PP(L^\star=\ell)=\frac{\ell\,\PP(L_1=\ell)}{\mu_L},
\qquad \ell\ge1.
\]
If \(A^\star\) and \(R^\star\) are the age and residual life inside this
stationary cycle, then
\[
A^\star+R^\star+1=L^\star
\]
and, for some constants \(C',\eta'>0\),
\[
\PP(L^\star\ge k)+\PP(A^\star\ge k)+\PP(R^\star\ge k)
\le C'e^{-\eta' k}.
\]
Consequently any bounded local reward supported in the initial incomplete
cycle or terminal incomplete cycle is \(O_{\PP}(1)=o_{\PP}(\sqrt N)\).

\emph{(ii) Random-index transfer for \(q\)-dependent cycle rewards.}  Let
\((Y_j)_{j\ge1}\) be a mean-zero \(\RR^d\)-valued stationary \(q\)-dependent
sequence with \(\EE\|Y_1\|^{2+\delta}<\infty\) for some \(\delta>0\), and set
\[
\Gamma_Y=
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_1)
+\sum_{h=1}^q
\left\{
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_{1+h})
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{1+h},Y_1)
\right\}.
\]
If \(S_m=\sum_{j=1}^mY_j\) and integer-valued random indices \(K_N\) satisfy
\[
\frac{K_N}{N}\to\theta\in(0,\infty)
\qquad\text{in probability},
\]
then
\[
\frac{S_{K_N}}{\sqrt N}
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\theta\Gamma_Y).
\]
Equivalently, the pair
\[
\left(
N^{-1/2}S_{K_N},\ N^{-1}K_N
\right)
\]
has the same Gaussian first-coordinate limit as the deterministic-index
\(q\)-dependent CLT evaluated at \(\lfloor\theta N\rfloor\).  The statement is
used below only in this form.
In particular, for the renewal count
\[
K_N=\max\left\{k:\sum_{j=1}^kL_j\le N\right\},
\]
one has \(K_N/N\to1/\mu_L\), and the limit covariance is
\(\Gamma_Y/\mu_L\).  This is exactly
\Cref{lem:regenerative-qdependent-clt-main}; the more general random-index
form with \(K_N/N\to\theta\) follows by applying the same lemma after replacing
the time scale \(N\) by \(N/\theta\).

\emph{(iii) Random complete-cycle batch law of large numbers.}  Let
\[
b_N\to\infty,\qquad b_N/N\to0,
\]
and, for fixed \(q\ge0\), define
\[
M_N=\left\lfloor\frac{K_N}{b_N+q}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
R_N=M_Nb_N+(M_N-1)q
\]
with the evident convention \(b_N+q=b_N\) when \(q=0\) and \(R_N=0\) if
\(M_N=0\).  Then
\[
\frac{M_Nb_N}{N}\to\frac1{\mu_L},
\qquad
\frac{R_N}{N}\to\frac1{\mu_L},
\qquad
\frac1{R_N}\sum_{j=1}^{R_N}L_j\to\mu_L
\]
in probability on \(\{R_N\ge1\}\), and
\(\PP(M_N<2)\to0\).  The same conclusions hold for the all-complete-cycle
count extracted from a stationary window after deleting the initial incomplete
cycle, the terminal incomplete cycle, and the final remainder of fewer than
\(b_N+q\) complete cycles.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For (i), the stationary renewal construction chooses the containing cycle with
probability proportional to its length, which gives the displayed size-biased
law.  Since
\[
\PP(L^\star\ge k)
=\frac1{\mu_L}\sum_{\ell\ge k}\ell\,\PP(L_1=\ell)
\le \frac{C_1}{\mu_L}\sum_{\ell\ge k}\ell e^{-\eta \ell},
\]
the right side is bounded by \(C'e^{-\eta' k}\) for any fixed
\(\eta'<\eta\), after increasing \(C'\).  The age and residual life are bounded
by \(L^\star\).  Hence a bounded local observable accumulated over either
boundary cycle is stochastically dominated by a constant times \(L^\star\),
which is tight and therefore \(o_{\PP}(\sqrt N)\).

For (ii), \Cref{lem:regenerative-qdependent-clt-main} gives the renewal-count
case with covariance \(\Gamma_Y/\mu_L\).  If \(K_N/N\to\theta\) for a general
integer-valued random index, the same finite-dependent CLT and Anscombe
transfer used in that lemma give the displayed covariance \(\theta\Gamma_Y\).
This part of the argument is an imported \(m\)-dependent/regenerative CLT
step; the paper-specific work below is only to verify finite dependence,
centering, and moment bounds for the sampled-counter rewards.

For (iii), the renewal strong law gives \(K_N/N\to1/\mu_L\).  Since
\((b_N+q)/b_N\to1\) and \(b_N=o(N)\),
\[
\frac{M_Nb_N}{N}
=
\frac{K_N}{N}\,
\frac{M_Nb_N}{K_N}
\to\frac1{\mu_L},
\qquad
\frac{R_N}{N}-\frac{M_Nb_N}{N}
=O_{\PP}\!\left(\frac{M_Nq}{N}\right)=O_{\PP}\!\left(\frac1{b_N}\right)\to0.
\]
Also \(M_N\to\infty\) in probability, hence
\(\PP(M_N<2)\to0\).  On \(\{R_N\to\infty\}\), the ordinary strong law for
cycle lengths evaluated at the random index \(R_N\) gives
\(R_N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{R_N}L_j\to\mu_L\) in probability.  In a stationary
window, part (i) makes the deleted initial and terminal incomplete cycles
tight, while the deterministic terminal batch remainder contains fewer than
\(b_N+q=o(N)\) complete cycles.  These deletions perturb \(K_N\), \(M_N\), and
\(R_N\) only by \(o_{\PP}(N)\) cycle indices, so the same limits hold.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Separated-batch consistency for random all-cycle counts]\label{lem:separated-batch-qdependent-main}
Fix \(d\ge1\) and \(q\ge0\).  Let \(L_1,L_2,\ldots\) be i.i.d. positive
integer-valued cycle lengths with
\[
\mu_L=\EE L_1\in(0,\infty),
\qquad
\PP(L_1\ge k)\le C e^{-\eta k}\quad(k\ge1).
\]
Let \((Y_j)_{j\ge1}\) be a mean-zero \(\RR^d\)-valued stationary
\(q\)-dependent cycle-reward sequence of the form
\[
Y_j=\psi(L_j,\ldots,L_{j+q}),\qquad
\|Y_j\|\le C_\psi\Bigl(1+\sum_{\ell=0}^q L_{j+\ell}\Bigr).
\]
Set
\[
\Gamma_Y=
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_1)
+\sum_{h=1}^q\left\{
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_{1+h})
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{1+h},Y_1)
\right\},
\]
where the covariance is interpreted by stationarity.  Let \(b_N\to\infty\) and
\(b_N/N\to0\), and let \(K_N=\max\{k:\sum_{j=1}^k L_j\le N\}\).  Define
\[
M_N=
\begin{cases}
\left\lfloor K_N/b_N\right\rfloor,&q=0,\\[3pt]
\left\lfloor K_N/(b_N+q)\right\rfloor,&q\ge1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
s_{r,N}=(r-1)(b_N+q)+1,
\]
with the convention \(s_{r,N}=(r-1)b_N+1\) when \(q=0\).  For \(1\le r\le M_N\)
put
\[
B_{r,N}^{\circ}=\sum_{j=s_{r,N}}^{s_{r,N}+b_N-1}Y_j,\qquad
\bar B_N^{\circ}=\frac1{M_N}\sum_{r=1}^{M_N}B_{r,N}^{\circ},
\]
and
\[
R_N=M_Nb_N+(M_N-1)q,\qquad
\bar L_N=\frac1{R_N}\sum_{j=1}^{R_N}L_j
\]
on \(\{M_N\ge2\}\).  Then \(M_N\to\infty\) in probability,
\(\bar L_N\to\mu_L\) in probability, and
\[
\frac{1}{\bar L_N\,b_N\,(M_N-1)}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_N}
(B_{r,N}^{\circ}-\bar B_N^{\circ})
(B_{r,N}^{\circ}-\bar B_N^{\circ})^\top
\longrightarrow
\frac{\Gamma_Y}{\mu_L}
\qquad\text{in probability}.
\]
The displayed estimator is interpreted arbitrarily on \(\{M_N<2\}\), an event
whose probability tends to zero.
The same conclusion holds for a stationary renewal window after deleting the
initial incomplete cycle, the terminal incomplete cycle, and the terminal
remainder of fewer than \(b_N+q\) complete cycles.

Moreover, suppose feasible rewards \(\widehat Y_{j,N}\) satisfy, uniformly over
the batched cycle indices,
\[
\|\widehat Y_{j,N}-Y_j\|\le C\,\delta_N L_j,
\qquad
\delta_N=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}).
\]
If \(\widehat B_{r,N}\) and \(\bar{\widehat B}_N\) are formed from
\(\widehat Y_{j,N}\) with the same separated batches, then replacing
\(B_{r,N}^{\circ}\) by \(\widehat B_{r,N}\) in the displayed matrix estimator
does not change its probability limit.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The exponential tail gives moments of all orders for \(L_1\), and the linear
growth assumption gives \(\EE\|Y_1\|^4<\infty\).  For a deterministic batch
length \(b\), the separated batch sums
\[
B_{r,b}^{\circ}=\sum_{j=(r-1)(b+q)+1}^{(r-1)(b+q)+b}Y_j
\]
are independent in \(r\), because the \(q\) omitted rewards separate the
supporting cycle-length variables of neighboring included blocks.  For \(q=0\)
there is no omission and the rewards are independent.  They are identically
distributed and mean zero.  Their covariance satisfies
\[
\frac1b\EE B_{1,b}^{\circ}(B_{1,b}^{\circ})^\top
=
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_1)
+\sum_{h=1}^q\left(1-\frac{h}{b}\right)
\left\{\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_{1+h})
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{1+h},Y_1)\right\}
\longrightarrow \Gamma_Y .
\]
Also,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:batch-fourth-main}
\EE\|B_{1,b}^{\circ}\|^4\le C b^2 .
\end{equation}
Indeed, split the \(q\)-dependent sequence into \(q+1\) residue classes; each
class is a sum of independent variables with finite fourth moment, so the
fourth moment of each class sum is \(O(b^2)\), and Minkowski's inequality gives
\eqref{eq:batch-fourth-main}.

Let \(X_{r,N}=b_N^{-1/2}B_{r,b_N}^{\circ}\).  The preceding bounds imply
\[
\sup_N \EE\|X_{1,N}\|^4<\infty,\qquad
\EE X_{1,N}X_{1,N}^\top\to\Gamma_Y .
\]
For deterministic \(m_N\to\infty\), the ordinary triangular-array weak law for
independent rows gives
\[
\frac1{m_N}\sum_{r=1}^{m_N}X_{r,N}X_{r,N}^\top
\to\Gamma_Y,\qquad
\frac1{m_N}\sum_{r=1}^{m_N}X_{r,N}\to0
\]
in probability.  The same convergence is uniform over deterministic indices in
any relative window \(m\in[m_N(1-\varepsilon),m_N(1+\varepsilon)]\).  For
example, for each matrix entry,
Kolmogorov's maximal inequality applied to the independent centered variables
\((X_{r,N}X_{r,N}^\top-\EE X_{1,N}X_{1,N}^\top)_{ab}\) gives
\[
\PP\!\left(
\max_{m_-\le m\le m_+}
\left|
\frac1m\sum_{r=1}^{m}
(X_{r,N}X_{r,N}^\top-\EE X_{1,N}X_{1,N}^\top)_{ab}
\right|>\varepsilon
\right)
\le
\frac{C\,m_+}{\varepsilon^2 m_-^2},
\]
which tends to zero when \(m_-/m_N\) and \(m_+/m_N\) stay bounded away from
\(0\) and infinity.  The vector average is handled identically using the
second-moment bound.  Hence the centered sample covariance of the first \(m\)
separated batches converges uniformly in that relative window to \(\Gamma_Y\).

The renewal strong law gives \(K_N/N\to1/\mu_L\), and therefore
\[
M_N\sim \frac{N}{\mu_L(b_N+q)}
\]
with the evident interpretation \(b_N+q=b_N\) when \(q=0\).  Since
\(b_N=o(N)\), \(M_N\to\infty\), and with probability tending to one \(M_N\)
lies in one of the deterministic relative windows just considered.  The
uniform deterministic-index convergence therefore applies at the random index
\(M_N\), even though \(M_N\) is a function of the same cycle lengths.  The
ordinary strong law for the cycle lengths, again evaluated at the random index
\(R_N\to\infty\), gives \(\bar L_N\to\mu_L\).  This proves the oracle matrix
estimator.

For a stationary renewal window, the first incomplete cycle is a size-biased
cycle and the residual time to the first observed renewal has an exponential
tail by \Cref{lem:renewal-index-prelim-main}(i).  After that first observed
renewal the subsequent complete cycles have the Palm law used above.  The
initial and terminal incomplete cycles are tight, and the terminal unbatched
remainder contains fewer than \(b_N+q\) complete cycles.  By
\Cref{lem:renewal-index-prelim-main}(iii), these deletions change neither the
complete-cycle renewal law of large numbers nor the random relative position
of \(M_N\).  The same proof therefore applies to all-complete-cycle batches
extracted from a stationary record.

It remains to check plug-in centering.  Put
\[
\Delta_{r,N}=\widehat B_{r,N}-B_{r,N}^{\circ}.
\]
The displayed perturbation hypothesis gives
\[
\|\Delta_{r,N}\|
\le C\delta_N\sum_{j=s_{r,N}}^{s_{r,N}+b_N-1}L_j .
\]
The same fourth-moment argument used above, now for the positive cycle lengths,
combined with the uniform relative-window argument at the random index \(M_N\),
yields
\[
\frac1{M_Nb_N}\sum_{r=1}^{M_N}\|\Delta_{r,N}\|^2
=O_{\PP}(\delta_N^2 b_N)=O_{\PP}(b_N/N)=o_{\PP}(1).
\]
After subtracting the batch means this bound can only increase by a fixed
factor.  The oracle normalized second moment is \(O_{\PP}(1)\), so
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\frac1{M_Nb_N}\sum_{r=1}^{M_N}
\|(B_{r,N}^{\circ}-\bar B_N^{\circ})
(\Delta_{r,N}-\bar\Delta_N)^\top\|=o_{\PP}(1),
\]
and the quadratic term in \(\Delta_{r,N}-\bar\Delta_N\) is \(o_{\PP}(1)\) as
well.  Multiplication by \(\bar L_N^{-1}\to\mu_L^{-1}\) proves the plug-in
version.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-lag renewal-reward CLT and separated-batch variance]\label{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main}\label{def:cycle-block-main}\label{lem:cycle-one-dep-main}\label{lem:renewal-reward-boundary-main}\label{lem:renewal-local-clt-main}\label{lem:batch-var-random-count-main}\label{lem:deterministic-prefix-main}\label{lem:lag2-boundary-main}\label{thm:cycle-block-main}
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be a stationary binary renewal record whose click
times are regeneration times.  In the Palm law started from a click at time
\(0\), write
\[
0=T_0<T_1<T_2<\cdots,\qquad L_j=T_{j+1}-T_j,\qquad G_j=L_j-1,
\]
and assume the i.i.d. gaps have an exponential tail:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-tail-main}
\PP(G_1\ge k)\le C e^{-\eta k}\qquad(k\ge0).
\end{equation}
Let \(q\ge0\) be fixed, and let \((Z_n)\) be a bounded stationary local
observable with \(\EE_{\mathrm{stat}} Z_0=0\).  Suppose its Palm cycle rewards
\[
Y_j=\sum_{n=T_j}^{T_{j+1}-1}Z_n
\]
have a finite-lag gap representation
\[
Y_j=f(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+q}),\qquad
|f(g_0,\ldots,g_q)|\le C_f\Bigl(1+\sum_{i=0}^q g_i\Bigr).
\]
Then \((Y_j)\) is stationary \(q\)-dependent, has moments of every order, and
with
\[
\mu_L=\EE L_1,\qquad
\tau_q^2=\Var(Y_1)+2\sum_{h=1}^q\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_{1+h}),
\]
the stationary-window renewal-reward CLT is
\[
\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} Z_n
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{\tau_q^2}{\mu_L}\right).
\]
The boundary between the stationary window and the complete Palm cycles is
\(o_{\PP}(\sqrt N)\); equivalently the initial incomplete cycle and the terminal
incomplete cycle do not contribute at root-\(N\) scale.

The same theorem gives the all-complete-cycle separated-batch variance
estimator.  Let \(0\le\tau_1<\cdots<\tau_{J_N}\le N-1\) be the observed click
times, choose \(b_N\to\infty\) with \(b_N/N\to0\), and for \(q\ge1\) put
\[
M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}
=\left\lfloor\frac{J_N-1}{b_N+q}\right\rfloor,\qquad
R_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}
=M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}b_N+(M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}-1)q,
\]
with value \(0\) when \(M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}=0\).  For \(q=0\), use the same
formulas with \(b_N+q=b_N\) and no omitted cycle.  Form batch sums from \(b_N\)
successive rewards and omit \(q\) rewards between neighboring batches.  If the
oracle population-centered rewards are used, then
\[
\frac{1}{\bar L_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}\,b_N\,(M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}-1)}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}}
\bigl(B_{r,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}-\bar B_N^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}\bigr)^2
\to
\frac{\tau_q^2}{\mu_L}
\qquad\text{in probability}.
\]
The same limit holds for plug-in rewards \(\widehat Y_{j,N}\) whenever the
plug-in centering error satisfies \(\delta_N=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) and, uniformly
over batched cycles,
\[
|\widehat Y_{j,N}-Y_{j,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}|
\le C\,\delta_N\,(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_j).
\]

For the lag-two covariance statistic used in Block~IV, take \(q=1\),
\[
Z_n=(A_nA_{n+2}-\mu_2)-2\rho(A_n-\rho),
\qquad
\rho=\EE A_0,\quad \mu_2=\EE(A_0A_2).
\]
Then
\[
\widehat\gamma_2-\gamma_2
=
\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Z_n+O_{\PP}(N^{-1}),
\qquad
\gamma_2=\mu_2-\rho^2,
\]
where
\[
\widehat\gamma_2
=\frac1{N-2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-3}
(A_n-\widehat\rho_N)(A_{n+2}-\widehat\rho_N).
\]
Thus
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat\gamma_2-\gamma_2)
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{\tau_1^2}{\mu_L}\right).
\]
If \(G,H,H'\) are i.i.d. copies of the zero-run gap and
\[
\pi_g=\PP(G=g),\qquad m_1=\EE G,\qquad m_2=\EE G^2,
\]
\[
\alpha=2\rho^2-\mu_2,\qquad \beta=\alpha-2\rho,
\]
then the lag-two cycle reward has the explicit form
\[
Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1}),\qquad
f(g,h)=\alpha g+\beta+\mathbf 1_{\{g=1\}}+\mathbf 1_{\{g=0,h=0\}},
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau2-explicit-main}
\tau_1^2
=
\alpha^2m_2
+2\alpha\beta m_1
+\beta^2
+(2\alpha+2\beta+1)\pi_1
+(4\beta+1)\pi_0^2
+2\pi_0^3 .
\end{equation}
The all-cycle estimator specialized to \(q=1\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycle-block-est-all-main}
\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}
=
\frac{1}{\bar L_N^{\mathrm{all}}\,b_N\,(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}-1)}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}}
\bigl(\widehat B_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}-\bar B_N^{\mathrm{all}}\bigr)^2 ,
\end{equation}
where
\[
M_N^{\mathrm{all}}
=\left\lfloor\frac{J_N-1}{b_N+1}\right\rfloor,\qquad
R_N^{\mathrm{all}}=M_N^{\mathrm{all}}b_N+(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}-1),
\]
and it converges in probability to \(\tau_1^2/\mu_L\).  If this variance is
positive, the corresponding studentized statistic converges to
\(\mathcal N(0,1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cycle-reward dependence assertion is immediate from the i.i.d. gap
sequence: \(Y_j\) depends on \(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+q}\), so rewards separated by
more than \(q\) cycle indices are independent.  The exponential tail and the
linear growth bound give all moments.  The finite-dependent CLT cited in
\Cref{lem:renewal-index-prelim-main}(ii) gives
\[
m^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^mY_j\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,\tau_q^2).
\]
\Cref{lem:renewal-index-prelim-main}(ii) is also the needed random-index step:
it applies the Anscombe transfer for \(q\)-dependent partial sums and evaluates
the sum at the renewal count \(K_N\).  Since the renewal strong law gives
\(K_N/N\to1/\mu_L\), this contributes the covariance factor \(\mu_L^{-1}\).

It remains only to compare the stationary fixed window with the complete-cycle
sum.  The part before the first observed click and the part after the last
complete cycle are bounded by a constant times the lengths of the two boundary
cycles.  Under the stationary renewal law these lengths are dominated by the
size-biased Palm cycle length.  Its exponential tail and the
\(o_{\PP}(\sqrt N)\) boundary deletion are exactly
\Cref{lem:renewal-index-prelim-main}(i).  This proves the CLT.

The batch statement is the scalar \(d=1\) case of
\Cref{lem:separated-batch-qdependent-main}.  Its hypotheses hold because the
cycle rewards are stationary, \(q\)-dependent, mean zero, and have the displayed
linear growth in exponentially-tailed cycle lengths.  The lemma proves the
needed fourth-moment triangular-array bound for the independent separated
batches, transfers the deterministic-index sample-variance law to the random
all-complete-cycle count \(M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}\), deletes the stationary
initial and terminal incomplete cycles and the terminal unbatched remainder,
and gives the stated plug-in stability under the displayed perturbation
condition.  Its scalar covariance \(\Gamma_Y\) is exactly
\(\tau_q^2=\Var(Y_1)+2\sum_{h=1}^q\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_{1+h})\), and its
normalizing factor \(\bar L_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}\) converges to \(\mu_L\).
The complete-cycle count and mean-cycle-length limits used in this last
sentence are the random batch-count law of large numbers in
\Cref{lem:renewal-index-prelim-main}(iii).

For the lag-two specialization, expand the empirical covariance and compare
the denominators \(N\) and \(N-2\):
\[
\widehat\gamma_2-\gamma_2
=
\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Z_n+O_{\PP}(N^{-1}).
\]
The lag-two cycle identity is the standalone reward calculation in
\Cref{lem:lag-two-reward-main}.  It gives the displayed adjacent-gap formula
for \(f(g,h)\), the centering identity \(\EE Y_1=0\), exact
\(1\)-dependence through the shared next gap, and
\[
\tau_1^2=\Var(Y_1)+2\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_2),
\]
with the explicit value \eqref{eq:tau2-explicit-main}.  The \(q=1\) batch formula is
\eqref{eq:cycle-block-est-all-main}; positivity plus Slutsky's theorem gives
the studentized limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Positivity of the sampled-law lag-two cycle variance]\label{lem:boundary-reset-tau-positive-main}
For the stationary sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset record with
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\), the explicit cycle variance in
\eqref{eq:tau2-boundary-reset-main} is strictly positive:
\[
\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2>0 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(G_1,G_2,\ldots\) be the i.i.d.\ post-click zero-run lengths from
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}.  The explicit zero-run law has full support:
\(\PP(G=g)>0\) for every \(g\ge0\).  In the proof of
\Cref{thm:cycle-block-main}, the centered lag-two cycle reward has the form
\[
Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1}),\qquad
f(g,h)=c(g)+\mathbf 1_{\{g=0,h=0\}},
\]
where \(c(g)=\alpha g+\beta+\mathbf 1_{\{g=1\}}\), and
\[
\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2=\Var(Y_1)+2\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_2).
\]
For such a stationary \(1\)-dependent sequence, zero long-run variance forces
a coboundary.  To see this explicitly, take the Hoeffding decomposition of
\(f(G,H)\) for two independent gap variables \(G,H\):
\[
f(g,h)=A(g)+B(h)+R(g,h),
\]
where \(\EE A(G)=\EE B(G)=0\), \(\EE[R(G,h)]=0\) for every \(h\), and
\(\EE[R(g,H)]=0\) for every \(g\).  The mean term is absent because
\(\EE Y_j=0\).  For \(S_n=\sum_{j=1}^n f(G_j,G_{j+1})\),
\[
S_n=A(G_1)+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\bigl(A+B\bigr)(G_j)+B(G_{n+1})
     +\sum_{j=1}^{n} R(G_j,G_{j+1}).
\]
The two sums on the right are orthogonal to each other and to the boundary
terms, and the degenerate variables \(R(G_j,G_{j+1})\) are themselves
uncorrelated at distinct lags.  Hence
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\Var(S_n)
=\Var\bigl((A+B)(G)\bigr)+\EE R(G,H)^2 .
\]
Thus \(\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2=0\) implies \(R=0\) and \(A+B=0\) almost surely.
On the full support this is exactly the telescoping form
\[
f(g,h)=u(h)-u(g)
\]
with \(u=B=-A\).

This alternative is impossible here.  The pairs \((0,0)\) and \((0,1)\) both
have positive probability, and the displayed formula for \(f\) gives
\[
f(0,0)=\beta+1,\qquad f(0,1)=\beta .
\]
A coboundary representation would therefore imply
\[
0=f(0,0)=\beta+1,\qquad u(1)-u(0)=f(0,1)=\beta=-1 .
\]
On the other hand, for every \(g\ge2\) and every \(h\ge0\),
\[
f(g,h)=\alpha g+\beta
\]
is independent of \(h\).  The same representation \(f(g,h)=u(h)-u(g)\) would
make \(u(h)-u(g)\) independent of \(h\), so \(u\) is constant on
\(\mathbb N_0\).  This contradicts \(u(1)-u(0)=-1\).  Therefore the coboundary
case is excluded, and \(\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2>0\).
\end{proof}
