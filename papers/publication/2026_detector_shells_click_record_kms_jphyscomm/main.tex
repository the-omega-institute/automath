\begin{abstract}
We study the visible binary output of a boundary-reset sampled counter whose
click and no-click kernels form a two-state killed-leakage D-MAP.  The physical
input is an attempt clock with rate \(\Gamma\), a recovery clock with rate
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), and a sampling interval \(\Delta\tau\); the
killed-leakage convention is a structural part of the model, not a generic
property of two-state D-MAPs.

For this fixed subfamily the click record is a full-support stationary renewal
process.  We derive its Palm gap law, show that the zero-run hazard is strictly
increasing, compute the one-mode covariance tail, locate the exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold, and construct all finite word probabilities by
renewal products and finite inclusion--exclusion.  The visible law also admits
a quotient inversion: three inclusion probabilities determine the symmetric
survival-factor coordinates and hence the unordered survival-factor pair.  An
ordered labelled rate pair is not identified by one visible record unless an
external separated-root branch label is supplied.
For minimal \(n\)-state killed-reset D-MAPs, we prove the sharp boundary:
a declared hidden subclass is identifiable exactly when each
reset-preserving similarity orbit meets its Markovian cone slice in one
declared structural-equivalence class. The pure serial subclass satisfies
this criterion modulo rate permutation for every
positive rate multiset, including repeated sampled poles, whereas the
unrestricted class has continuum fibres at every minimal interior point.

We then give fixed-setting retained-record asymptotics for these visible
coordinates.  Under the stated sampled-counter model and pre-specified
reporting gates, complete-cycle estimators satisfy an inclusion-coordinate CLT,
the quotient inverse has the corresponding delta method, and the exchange
diagonal has a projected Gaussian limit.  The only diagonal candidate with a
printed branch certificate here is \(z_0=1/2\).  The manuscript does not print
a record-specific critical-value bracket or a realized statistic interval, and
therefore does not execute a finite-record accept/reject decision.  These
claims are conditional on the model contract; diagnostic pass or fail outcomes
are not used to create model-selection-valid confidence coverage.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} constrained D-MAP, phase-type identifiability,
similarity orbit, confluent Prony method, renewal process, exact
\(1\)-dependence

\section{Introduction}

This paper is about a constrained two-state discrete-time Markovian-arrival
process (D-MAP) and the stationary binary renewal record it generates.  The
physical input is deliberately modest: a classical sampled counter with two
internal states, \(R\) for ready and \(D\) for dead/recovering.  During one
sampling interval of length \(\Delta\tau\), a ready counter attempts to click
with exponential rate \(\Gamma\), and a dead counter recovers with exponential
rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).  A click is recorded as \(A_n=1\), and the
boundary-reset convention assigns the post-click state at the next sample to
\(D\).  This construction derives the labelled kernel pair displayed in
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}: \(T_0+T_1\) is the hidden transition
matrix, \(T_1\) marks an observed \(1\), and the killed entries
\(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\) encode the rule that every recorded click leaves the
counter dead at the next boundary.  No theorem below allows either
one-labelled entry into \(R\) to be positive; such mass is same-bin reset
leakage and belongs to a different labelled kernel model.  Thus
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) are not formal decoration; they are
the attempt rate, recovery rate, and sampling interval of this sampled-counter
realization of the D-MAP.

The paper-level contribution is the closed-form record law, quotient inverse,
and fixed-setting retained-record asymptotic analysis of this constrained
D-MAP(2) subfamily, together with an \(n\)-state identifiability theorem that
separates pure serial models from general killed-reset realizations by their
Markovian similarity-orbit fibres. The following contract fixes the physical
model used by the two-state record and inference statements.

This positioning is narrower than several nearby literatures.  The closest
probability comparator is Zamparo's stationary renewal-binary framework
\cite{Zamparo2022RenewalBinary}: it supplies a renewal-binary language and
Gaussian limit technology, but it does not derive the renewal law from a
triangular killed-leakage sampled-counter D-MAP, compute the Palm gap law and
monotone hazard in \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\), or invert three
visible inclusion coordinates to \((p+s,ps)\).  Classical MAP and D-MAP theory
\cite{Neuts1979MAP,Lucantoni1991BMAP,AlfaNeuts1995DMAP} supplies the
arrival-process background, while MAP2 and phase-type nonidentifiability
\cite{RamirezCoboLilloWiper2010MAP2Nonidentifiability,
OCinneide1989PhaseTypeNonuniqueness} explain why our inverse is deliberately a
visible quotient inverse rather than a labelled hidden-kernel reconstruction.

Recent physics-math work on weak memory, waiting times, correlations, and
partial observations is close in motivation but not in theorem object.  Weak
memory and Markov-state diagnostics
\cite{Brandner2025WeakMemoryPRE,MeyerBrandner2026WeakMemory,
HartichGodec2021EmergentMemory,VollmarEtAl2024Memory,
ZhaoMakarovGodec2025MarkovStateHolography,LoosGodec2025NonMarkovianEffects}
study memory strength, response, or adequacy of coarse dynamical models; here
the record theorem gives the exact one-mode covariance tail
\(\gamma_n=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid}^{\,n-1}\), the exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold, and a visible quotient inverse.  Transition
waiting-time and partial-observation inference
\cite{VanDerMeerErtelSeifert2022TransitionWTD,
HarunariDuttaPolettiniRoldan2022VisibleTransitions,
ErtelSeifert2024BlurredTransitions,BauerSeifertVanderMeer2025Stroboscopic,
MaierSeifertVanderMeer2025HiddenPaths} targets generators, entropy production,
hidden paths, or transition-waiting-time functionals under partial access.  The
waiting-time object here is instead the Palm zero-run gap law \(g_k\) of the
binary click record, used to build the complete visible renewal law and the
symmetric sampled-counter quotient.

Detector dead-time and photon-counting models are the closest instrument-level
comparators
\cite{Muller1973,CantorTeich1975,YuFessler2000,Hadfield2009,
EisamanEtAl2011,StrakaEtAl2020SPADCounting,KirchhoffEtAl2025Deadtime,
JorgensenJohnson2026DED}.  They address count corrections, device recovery,
afterpulsing, finite-window distributions, or detector-specific estimators.
The theorem chain below instead fixes the boundary-reset killed-leakage kernel
and asks exactly what its stationary binary record identifies.  The structural
fragility of this convention is recorded in
\Cref{prop:model-fragility-boundary-reset-main}: allowing one-labelled
same-bin recovery into \(R\) destroys the deterministic post-click reset on
which the renewal product, quotient inverse, and retained-record covariance
route depend.

\begin{assumption}[Constrained sampled-counter D-MAP model]\label{ass:model-inference-contract-main}
Fix \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\in(0,\infty)^3\) and define
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},
\]
\[
a=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\bigr)\,dr,\qquad
b=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr .
\]
The record model is the two-state D-MAP with hidden states ordered as
\((R,D)\) and labelled kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The convention is
\((T_i)_{XY}=\PP(A_n=i,H_{n+1}=Y\mid H_n=X)\), the visible process
\((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) is the stationary binary process generated by these
kernels, and formulas on \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) are interpreted by
continuous limits.  The killed entries \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\) are a structural
constraint of this D-MAP, not optional boundary cases of the finite-record
theorems.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Scope of finite-record reports}\label{subsec:finite-record-report-scope-main}

Finite-record reporting procedures are fixed-setting retained-record
asymptotics inside the stationary law specified in
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}.  In every theorem that invokes this
scope convention, the model in \Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main},
the population point
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\), the statistic, the deterministic
thresholds, and the reporting event \(\mathcal E_N\) are all fixed before the
record is interpreted.  Finite-record confidence language is therefore
strictly conditional on that model contract and on the named pre-specified
reporting gate.  On \(\mathcal E_N\) the paper evaluates the fixed procedure;
on \(\mathcal E_N^c\) the corresponding finite-record report is withheld.
The event \(\mathcal E_N\) is not a model-selection device, and observing it
does not create coverage for a model or branch chosen after inspecting the
record.  The paper does not assert finite-sample coverage, uniform coverage
over \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\), or model-selection-valid coverage across
diagnostic outcomes or reporting branches.

The only model interpretation assumed by this contract is the classical
sampled-counter protocol of
\Cref{lem:classical-counter-kernel-construction-main}.  If the sampling
interval \(\Delta\tau\) is a known design interval, the visible stationary
record identifies the unordered survival-factor set
\(\{p,s\}=\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}\) and
hence the unordered numerical rate set
\(\{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\}\).  It does not identify which member is the
attempt rate and which is the recovery rate without side information.  If
\(\Delta\tau\) is not treated as a known design interval, the visible law
identifies only the dimensionless unordered product set
\(\{\Gamma\Delta\tau,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\}\); the same record does
not put those products on an absolute numerical rate scale.

The diagonal retained-record theorem is the fixed-candidate asymptotic
statement at
\(z_0=1/2\), using the printed branch certificate in
\Cref{lem:z-half-diagonal-branch-certificate-main}; its precise scope is
restated immediately before Theorem~E.  Other diagonal candidates and
continuum displays remain diagnostics unless a later implementation extension
supplies its own printed finite certificate and critical-value transcript.
For \(z_0=1/2\) itself, this article prints only the population branch
certificate.  It does not print a retained-record critical-value bracket
\([L,U]\) or statistic interval \([Q_N]\), and therefore does not assert a
realized finite-record accept/reject/undecided output.
Ordered labelled rates require the separate branch-map convention of
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.  The paper does not claim
simultaneous inversion over \(0<z<1\), nor identification of UDW, KMS,
thermodynamic, response-profile, or ordered-rate quantities unless the
particular result supplies the extra hypotheses and proof.

\subsection{Visible-record diagnostics for the model contract}\label{subsec:visible-record-model-diagnostics-main}

\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main} and
\Cref{subsec:finite-record-report-scope-main} form a conditional contract, not a
model-selection theorem.  The retained-record reports below are statements
inside the killed-leakage sampled-counter D-MAP with known \(\Delta\tau\);
they are not procedures that first validate that model from the same record and
then inherit coverage.  No size, power, or post-selection coverage theorem is
proved for model diagnostics.  The diagnostic protocol is therefore
supplementary: complete-gap renewal checks, inclusion-product residuals, and
low-lag covariance residuals can falsify the standing renewal D-MAP
interpretation, but they do not certify it.  A material unexplained diagnostic
failure is only a pre-specified reason to withhold the model-conditioned
finite-record reports in
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main,thm:finite-record-quotient-delta-main,thm:e-fixed-candidate-exchange-diagonal-main};
it is not one branch of a valid post-diagnostic confidence procedure.


\begin{lemma}[Classical sampled-counter derivation of the D-MAP kernels]\label{lem:classical-counter-kernel-construction-main}
The kernels in \Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main} are exactly the
one-step kernels of the following discrete sampled-counter protocol.  At the
start of a sampling interval of length \(\Delta\tau\), the hidden state is
either \(R\) or \(D\).  From \(R\), an independent attempt clock with rate
\(\Gamma\) either rings before the end of the interval, in which case the
visible output is \(1\) and the next state is \(D\), or it does not ring, in
which case the visible output is \(0\) and the next state remains \(R\).
From \(D\), an independent recovery clock with rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\)
either does not ring, giving output \(0\) and next state \(D\), or rings at
time \(r\in(0,\Delta\tau)\).  Conditional on recovery at \(r\), the same
attempt clock is run only during the remaining time \(\Delta\tau-r\): no
attempt gives output \(0\) and next state \(R\), while an attempt gives output
\(1\) and next state \(D\).  This protocol gives
\[
T_0(R,R)=p,\quad T_1(R,D)=1-p,\quad T_0(D,D)=s,
\]
\[
T_0(D,R)=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr=b,
\quad
T_1(D,D)=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\bigr)\,dr=a,
\]
and the two killed entries \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The \(R\)-row is the survival and ringing decomposition of an exponential
clock over one interval.  For the \(D\)-row, no recovery before
\(\Delta\tau\) has probability \(s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\) and
contributes to \(T_0(D,D)\).  If recovery occurs at \(r\), its density is
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}\,dr\).  Survival of the attempt
clock for the remaining time \(\Delta\tau-r\) gives the \(T_0(D,R)\) integral,
and its complement gives the \(T_1(D,D)\) integral.  The construction never
allows a marked transition landing in \(R\), which gives the two killed
entries.  These mutually exclusive cases exhaust each row, since
\(s+a+b=1\) and \(p+(1-p)=1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fragility under non-killed one-labelled leakage]\label{prop:model-fragility-boundary-reset-main}
The killed-leakage convention in
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main} is not a removable presentation
choice.  It is a structural hypothesis behind the renewal law, quotient
inverse, and retained-record inference in Theorems~A--E.  Let
\((\widetilde T_0,\widetilde T_1)\) be any two-state labelled kernel with the
same visible convention
\[
(\widetilde T_i)_{XY}
=\PP(A_n=i,H_{n+1}=Y\mid H_n=X),\qquad X,Y\in\{R,D\},
\]
but suppose that either
\[
\widetilde T_1(R,R)>0
\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
\widetilde T_1(D,R)>0 .
\]
Then a click no longer forces the next hidden state to be the deterministic
state \(D\).  Consequently the click epochs are not regeneration epochs in
general.  In particular, the Palm renewal product
\[
\PP(B\subset\Xi)=\rho\prod_j u_{i_{j+1}-i_j}
\]
for \(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\) does not follow from the D-MAP structure alone;
nor do the Palm gap formula
\(g_k=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1\), the single-mode covariance identity
\[
u_2=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid},
\]
or the quotient recovery
\[
\lambda_{\rm hid}=(r_2-r_0^2)/(r_1-r_0^2),\qquad
\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps).
\]
Therefore Theorems~A and B, and the finite-record Theorems~C--E that use the
same renewal inverse and covariance route, fail to be valid generic
sampled-counter or generic D-MAP(2) statements unless additional hypotheses
restore a deterministic post-click reset or supply a replacement regeneration
theory.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proofs of Theorems~A--E use the following deterministic post-click fact:
on every event \(\{A_n=1\}\), the next hidden state is \(H_{n+1}=D\).  In the
kernel notation this is exactly
\(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\).  It makes each observed click a reset to the same
state \(D\), so the post-click future starts from the fixed vector \(e_D\),
independently of the pre-click hidden state and of the earlier visible record.
The inter-click zero runs are then i.i.d. under the Palm law, their tail is
computed by iterating \(T_0\) from \(e_D\), and inclusion probabilities factor
as the displayed renewal product.

If \(\widetilde T_1(R,R)>0\), a click from \(R\) can leave the next hidden
state equal to \(R\).  If \(\widetilde T_1(D,R)>0\), a click from \(D\) can
leave the next hidden state equal to \(R\).  Whenever either transition has
positive probability, conditioning on \(A_n=1\) need not put \(H_{n+1}\) at
the deterministic state \(D\); it produces a post-click distribution that can
depend on the pre-click hidden state and on the stationary mixture at the
click time.  The D-MAP property by itself only records labelled Markov
transitions.  It does not turn this state-dependent post-click mixture into
the fixed Palm restart required for a renewal record.

The continuous same-bin recovery comparator is a concrete instance: the paths
``click--recover'' from \(R\) and ``recover--click--recover'' from \(D\) give
positive entries \(\widetilde T_1(R,R)\) and
\(\widetilde T_1(D,R)\).  The Palm gap law is therefore no longer a consequence
of the labelled kernel without extra assumptions.  The renewal product
formulas and the covariance transfer lemma are downstream of that Palm renewal
law, while the quotient inverse in Theorem~B explicitly divides the covariance
identity \(r_2-r_0^2=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid}\) by
\(r_1-r_0^2=\rho(a-\rho)\).  Once the renewal identity is unavailable, the
displayed inverse and the retained-record covariance estimator are no longer
proved for the modified kernel.  This proves the stated logical dependence.
\end{proof}

The main text below is organized around exactly five paper-level theorem
statements for this constrained D-MAP(2) subfamily.  Supporting lemmas,
propositions, corollaries, certificate interfaces, and algorithms are used only
to prove or apply these five statements.  The killed-leakage dependency is
explicit in \Cref{prop:model-fragility-boundary-reset-main}: if same-bin
recovery after a click is retained in the one-labelled kernel, this theorem
chain is not the applicable theorem chain.

\subsection{Consolidated main theorem block}\label{subsec:main-theorem-boundary}

\begin{paperthm}[Constrained D-MAP record law]\label{thm:main-record-wrapper}
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) have the stationary visible law generated by the
sampled-counter kernels in \Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}.  Put
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},\qquad
\rho=\PP(A_0=1),\qquad
\lambda_{\rm hid}=p-b .
\]
Then \((A_n)\) is a full-support renewal process.  If \(G\) is the Palm number
of zeros between consecutive ones, then
\[
g_k=\PP(G=k)=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1{\bf 1},\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
For \(\Gamma\ne\kappa_{\mathrm r}\),
\[
g_k=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-s)s^k}
          {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad
S_k=\PP(G\ge k)=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
with continuous-limit formulas on \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).  The zero-run
hazard \(h_k=g_k/S_k\) is strictly increasing.  The visible covariance has the
single hidden-mode tail
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1 .
\]
The record is exact \(1\)-dependent if and only if
\(\lambda_{\rm hid}=0\), and in no positive parameter case is it a
finite-order Markov chain.
\end{paperthm}

\begin{paperthm}[Quotient inverse]\label{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}
Work in the same constrained D-MAP record model.  Let
\[
r_0=\rho,\qquad r_1=\rho a,\qquad r_2=\rho u_2,
\]
and assume the quotient inverse domain
\[
r_0>0,\qquad \Delta_{\rm inv}:=r_1-r_0^2\ne0 .
\]
Define
\[
a(r)=\frac{r_1}{r_0},\qquad
\lambda(r)=\frac{r_2-r_0^2}{r_1-r_0^2},
\]
and
\[
\Phi(r)=
\left(1-a(r)+\lambda(r),\,
r_0(1-\lambda(r))-a(r)+\lambda(r)\right).
\]
On the constrained D-MAP image,
\[
\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps).
\]
Thus the visible law determines the unordered survival-factor pair
\(\{p,s\}\), equivalently the unordered roots of
\[
z^2-\Phi_1(r)z+\Phi_2(r)=0,
\]
and determines all visible renewal probabilities through this quotient.  For
known design interval \(\Delta\tau\), the same quotient determines the
unordered numerical rate set
\[
\left\{-\frac{\log p}{\Delta\tau},-\frac{\log s}{\Delta\tau}\right\}
=\{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\}.
\]
If \(\Delta\tau\) is not supplied as a known design interval, the numerical
rate display is replaced by the dimensionless identifiable set
\[
\{-\log p,-\log s\}
=\{\Gamma\Delta\tau,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\}.
\]
In either case the visible law does not determine an ordered labelled vector
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\) unless an external branch map is supplied as in
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.  On the exchange diagonal, the
invariant visible-law estimand is the common dimensionless product, and only
for known \(\Delta\tau\) the common numerical rate, not an ordered
attempt/recovery label.
\end{paperthm}

\begin{proposition}[No-post-selection reporting event]\label{thm:no-post-selection-theorem-main}
Let \(A_N^{\rm aux}\) be a fixed auxiliary acceptance event defined before the
record is inspected, and let
\[
A_N^{\rm rep}=E_N\cap A_N^{\rm aux}.
\]
If \(\PP(E_N^c)\to0\), then
\[
\left|\PP(A_N^{\rm rep})-\PP(A_N^{\rm aux})\right|
\le \PP(E_N^c)\to0 .
\]
This proposition fails to justify any event \(A_N^{\rm aux}\) chosen after
inspecting diagnostics, reporting branches, retained ranks, covariance
estimates, candidate diagonal points, or critical-value transcripts.  Hence
each finite-record assertion in Theorems~C--E is a fixed-model,
fixed-protocol statement under the sampled-counter law, estimand map,
retained-cycle statistic, covariance-estimation rule, retained-rank threshold,
reporting gate, branch certificate, and limiting critical value fixed before
the record is inspected.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(A_N^{\rm rep}\subset A_N^{\rm aux}\),
\[
0\le \PP(A_N^{\rm aux})-\PP(A_N^{\rm rep})
=\PP(A_N^{\rm aux}\cap E_N^c)\le \PP(E_N^c).
\]
The asserted convergence follows.  The proof uses only the fixed event
\(A_N^{\rm aux}\) and the pre-specified gate \(E_N\), so it gives no
probability statement for events selected after the record has been examined.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Specialized separated covariance estimator for adjacent gap rewards]\label{lem:paper-level-separated-covariance-main}
Let \(G_j\) be i.i.d. nonnegative integer-valued variables with
\(\EE e^{\eta G_0}<\infty\) for some \(\eta>0\).  Put \(L_j=G_j+1\),
\(\mu=\EE L_0\), \(K_N=\max\{k:\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}L_j\le N\}\), and
\(Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1})\in\RR^d\), where \(\EE Y_j=0\) and
\(\EE\|Y_j\|^4<\infty\).  Set
\[
\Sigma_r=\frac{1}{\mu}\{\operatorname{Var}Y_0+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0)\}.
\]
With \(b_N=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\), \(d_N=b_N+1\),
\[
I_{\ell,N}=\{(\ell-1)d_N,\ldots,(\ell-1)d_N+b_N-1\},
\qquad
M_N=\max\{M:(M-1)d_N+b_N\le K_N-1\},
\]
let \(B_{\ell,N}=\sum_{j\in I_{\ell,N}}Y_j\).  On \(M_N\ge2\), put
\(\bar B_N=M_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}B_{\ell,N}\) and
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}
=\frac{K_N}{N}\frac{1}{b_N(M_N-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}(B_{\ell,N}-\bar B_N)(B_{\ell,N}-\bar B_N)^\top .
\]
Then \(M_N\ge2\) with probability tending to one, and the auxiliary zero
completion on \(M_N<2\) satisfies
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/3}).
\]
If \(\widehat Y_j=Y_j-\Delta_NL_j\) with
\(\Delta_N=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), the same rate holds for the corresponding
plug-in separated-batch estimator.

\begin{proof}
The variables \(Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1})\) are centered and \(1\)-dependent.  Since
the deterministic blocks \(I_{\ell,N}\) are separated by one deleted cycle,
the block sums \(B_{\ell,N}\) are independent for deterministic prefixes.
Writing
\[
T_1=\operatorname{Var}Y_0+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0),
\]
one has \(b_N^{-1}\operatorname{Var}B_{\ell,N}=T_1+O(b_N^{-1})\).  The
exponential moment of \(G_0\) gives uniform fourth moments for the block sums,
and maximal inequalities for the independent matrices
\(b_N^{-1}\{B_{\ell,N}B_{\ell,N}^{\top}-\EE B_{\ell,N}B_{\ell,N}^{\top}\}\)
over all prefixes \(m\le C N/b_N\) give stochastic error
\(O_{\PP}(\sqrt{b_N/N})\); the empirical block mean contributes only
\(O_{\PP}(b_N/N)\).  The renewal count obeys
\[
K_N=N/\mu+O_{\PP}(\sqrt N),
\]
so the stopped block count is controlled by
\[
M_N=\frac{N}{\mu d_N}+O_{\PP}(\sqrt N/b_N+1),
\]
equivalently
\[
M_N=\frac{N}{\mu b_N}
  +O_{\PP}(N/b_N^2+\sqrt N/b_N+1),
\]
where the deterministic \(N/b_N^2\) term is only the one-cycle separator
correction, and \(b_NM_N/K_N=1+O_{\PP}(b_N^{-1})\).  Combining these estimates with
\(K_N/N\to1/\mu\) gives the displayed \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/3})\) rate.  For the
plug-in rewards,
\[
\max_{\ell\le M_N}\left\|\sum_{j\in I_{\ell,N}}(\widehat Y_j-Y_j)\right\|
\le \|\Delta_N\|\max_{\ell\le M_N}\sum_{j\in I_{\ell,N}}L_j
=O_{\PP}(b_NN^{-1/2}),
\]
and the corresponding cross and quadratic perturbations of the centered batch
covariance are of smaller order than \(N^{-1/3}\).
\end{proof}
\end{lemma}

\begin{paperthm}[Fixed-setting retained-record inclusion CLT]\label{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main}

\Cref{thm:no-post-selection-theorem-main} with the finite-record master
assumption \Cref{ass:finite-record-master-main}, specifically
\ref{fr:law-main}, \ref{fr:estimands-main}, \ref{fr:protocol-main}, and
\ref{fr:covariance-main}.  Form the complete-cycle inclusion estimator

\Rinch_N^{\rm cyc}
=\left(\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_{0,N}}U_j^{(0)},
       \frac1N\sum_{j\in J_{1,N}}U_j^{(1)},
       \frac1N\sum_{j\in J_{2,N}}U_j^{(2)}\right)^\top ,
\qquad
\Rinc=(r_0,r_1,r_2)^\top .
\]
In Palm cycle coordinates put
\[
U_j=
\bigl(1,\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0\}},
\mathbf1_{\{G_j=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0,G_{j+1}=0\}}\bigr)^\top,
\qquad
L_j=G_j+1,\qquad
Y_j=U_j-\Rinc L_j .

Then \(\EE Y_j=0\), \((Y_j)\) is \(1\)-dependent, and

\Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,\Sigma_r),

where

\Sigma_r=\frac1\mu\{\operatorname{Var}(Y_0)
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0)\},

The separated-batch covariance estimator with
\(b_N=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\) and plug-in centering obeys

\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\rm tot}-\Sigma_r\|_{\rm op}=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/3})

as the covariance input for any reported event \(E_N\cap A_N^{\rm aux}\)

\end{paperthm}

\section{D-MAP setting and record-level observables}\label{sec:operational}

We first consider only the stationary D-MAP record law of
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}.  The hidden chain has two states, the
output alphabet is \(\{0,1\}\), and the visible output is the stationary
two-sided binary process
\[
(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z},
\]
where \(A_n=1\) denotes a marked transition of the D-MAP in the \(n\)th
discrete step.  All Palm, covariance, renewal, and independence
statements below refer to this two-sided stationary law.  A finite observed
record of length \(N\) is only the restriction
\[
A^{(N)}=(A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_{N-1}),
\]
of the stationary process.  The only nonstationary finite-start convention
used below is the post-burn-in retained-length convention: generate
\(L_N+N\) intervals from an arbitrary hidden initial law, discard the first
\(L_N\), and reindex the retained word as
\((A_{L_N},\ldots,A_{L_N+N-1})\), so \(N\) is always the retained sample size.
Compressing such a finite record to the empirical click
frequency recovers only the one-point observable
\[
\widehat\rho_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n.
\]
The full record, however, carries more. In particular, one may ask:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item what words belong to the support of the stationary law;
\item how the zero-run lengths between clicks are distributed;
\item whether the click hazard after a long run of zeros remembers the run length;
\item how the low-lag covariance sequence behaves;
\item what counting noise remains after coarse time averaging.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{definition}[Record observables]\label{def:record-benchmarks-main}
For a stationary binary record $(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$, a \emph{zero-run length} is the number $G$ of consecutive zeros following a click and preceding the next click. If the zero-run law has full support, its hazard is
\[
h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k),\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
A stationary record is \emph{exact $1$-dependent} when, for every $m$, the sigma algebras generated by $\{A_j:j\le m\}$ and $\{A_j:j\ge m+2\}$ are independent.
Equivalently, one leaves the intermediate sample at time $m+1$ unobserved: a block ending at time $m$ is tested for independence from a block beginning at time $m+2$, not conditioned on the middle sample.
\end{definition}

These observables are not themselves one-point rate observables, yet in the
explicit D-MAP family above they reduce to sharp thresholds in the coordinate
\(\Gamma\).

It is useful to keep the D-MAP record construction in two logically separate
steps.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item The labelled kernels determine the stationary word law, the zero-run
hazard, and the covariance sequence of the visible binary record.
\item Exact \(1\)-dependence for this D-MAP is first equivalent to the scalar
coordinate condition \(\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\).
That condition is labelled according to \Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}:
the inverse theorem gives
\[
\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}
\quad\text{and hence only}\quad
\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}.
\]
Thus, without a supplied branch label, the statement is made on the quotient by
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\sim(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)\).
\end{enumerate}
The constrained D-MAP produces a full-support renewal law.  Its distinguished
record threshold in the main theorem chain is therefore the exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold where the hidden eigenmode vanishes.

\begin{proposition}[Detailed form of Theorem~A: constrained D-MAP record law]\label{prop:main-record-wrapper-detailed}
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) have law
\(\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau}\) from
\Cref{def:main-sampled-kernel-law}.  Let \(G\) be the
number of zeros between consecutive ones, and put
\[
\rho=\PP(A_0=1),\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b .
\]
Then \((A_n)\) is a full-support renewal process with Palm gap law
\[
g_k=\PP(G=k)=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1,\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
If \(\Gamma\ne\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), then
\[
g_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-s)s^k}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
while on the equal-rate diagonal the formula is interpreted by the continuous
limit, equivalently for
\(y=\Gamma\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\) and \(p=e^{-y}\),
\[
g_k=p^k\bigl((1-p)(1+yk)-yp\bigr).
\]
Writing
\[
S_k=\PP(G\ge k),\qquad h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k)=g_k/S_k ,
\]
the survival tail, hazard, and hazard increment are explicit.  Off the
equal-rate diagonal,
\[
S_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad
h_k=
1-\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^{k+1}-\Gamma s^{k+1}}
        {\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k},
\]
and
\[
h_{k+1}-h_k
=
\frac{
  \Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}\,
  (p-s)^2\,p^k s^k
}{
  \bigl(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k\bigr)
  \bigl(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^{k+1}-\Gamma s^{k+1}\bigr)
}>0 .
\]
On the equal-rate diagonal,
\[
S_k=p^k(1+yk),\qquad
h_k=1-p-\frac{yp}{1+yk},
\qquad
h_{k+1}-h_k=
\frac{y^2p}{(1+yk)(1+y(k+1))}>0 .
\]
Thus the zero-run hazard is strictly increasing.  For every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\gamma_n:=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)
=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}.
\]
The record is exact \(1\)-dependent iff \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), and in no
positive parameter case is it a finite-order Markov chain.
\end{proposition}
