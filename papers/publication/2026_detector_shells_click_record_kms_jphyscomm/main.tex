\begin{abstract}
This paper proves exact record laws for a stipulated classical two-state
sampled renewal HMM.  Time is sampled in intervals of length \(\Delta\tau\),
the hidden state is ready or recovering, the sampled post-event boundary
convention sets the return-to-ready entries of the event kernel to zero, and
the visible record is a stationary binary sequence.  The ideal one-step
blanking model is retained as the standard Parry benchmark
\(\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi\).  The main killed-leakage sampled-boundary
theorem treats the one-recovery-opportunity HMM with attempt rate \(\Gamma\)
and recovery rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\): its zero-run law has full support on
\(\mathbb N_0\), its zero-run hazard is strictly increasing, and its
binary-record covariance has the hidden-eigenvalue tail
\(\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{n-1}\).
The visible record is exact \(1\)-dependent exactly at the unique rate
\(\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) where
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\).

A stationary-renewal lemma first constructs click-set probabilities from
renewal products and finite inclusion--exclusion.  After those probabilities
are known, the same finite algebra gives a finite LU product identity for
that product; the boundary-reset law specializes the inclusion
product to \(\rho\prod_r u_{i_{r+1}-i_r}\).  The same renewal record law has
a quotient inverse: on the inverse domain \(D=r_1-r_0^2\ne0\), three inclusion
probabilities recover the symmetric coordinate vector
\(\sigma_{\rm sym}=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)=(p+s,ps)\), and hence the unordered
survival-factor pair
\(\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}\).  The
discriminant
\(\Delta_\sigma=\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2=(p-s)^2\) controls separated labelled
recovery: when \(\Delta_\sigma>0\), labelled roots and labelled rates are
available only after an external branch label chooses which root is the
attempt survival factor and which is the recovery survival factor; when
\(\Delta_\sigma=0\), the separated-root chart is singular, although the
diagonal common rate \(-\log z/\Delta\tau\) is identified at
\(\sigma=(2z,z^2)\).  Thus
\(\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}\), and one
visible sampled record identifies only the exchange quotient.  The finite-record
part proves a fixed-setting lag-two covariance CLT and a complete-cycle
variance estimator for \(\gamma_2\) in this sampled HMM law.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} classical sampled HMM, renewal process, exact \(1\)-dependence, renewal inclusion products, finite-record diagnostics

\section{Introduction}

This paper is about the record law of a two-state sampled renewal HMM.  The
model has an internal attempt rate \(\Gamma\), one hidden readiness bit, and a
binary output recording whether the sampling interval contains an event.  Its
visible stationary process has grammar, waiting-time, dependence, covariance,
and counting-noise observables that are invisible in a rate-only summary.  The
paper-level object is therefore the sampled record itself: one stipulated
interval law, its stationary binary distribution, its dependence threshold, its
renewal inclusion products, its quotient inverse, and its fixed-setting
long-record lag-two covariance estimator.

The scalar rate \(\Gamma\) is an internal parameter of the sampled counter.
All main-text claims below concern the induced binary record law.  The sampled
boundary-reset kernels are stipulated classical kernels, not conclusions drawn
from a quantum-detector, KMS, thermodynamic, or continuous-time measurement
model.  The continuous intra-bin recovery model discussed in the supplementary
material is a fixed-window comparator after the sampled kernels have been
specified, and the response-side calibration at the end of the article is the
separate composition \(\Gamma=R_\Omega(x)\) with a supplied positive \(C^1\)
function.  The scope convention is collected in
\Cref{rem:scope-nonclaims-main}.

The main text below is organized around three paper-level main theorems:
the record law, the quotient inverse, and fixed-record inference.  Supporting
lemmas, propositions, and corollaries supply the renewal bookkeeping, generic
cycle-reward CLTs, separated-batch covariance estimates, and projected-Gaussian
boundary calculations needed by those three results.  The theorem-by-theorem
comparison in \Cref{sec:theorem-led-comparison} then separates these
contributions from standard HMM, renewal, and simulation-output theorems.  The
words event, click, recovery, reset, and count are names for variables and
transitions in this stipulated binary record model.  They do not add any
device-level assumptions beyond the sampled kernels displayed below.

\begin{remark}[Scope and nonclaims]\label{rem:scope-nonclaims-main}
The mathematical object of the paper is the stationary visible law generated
by the displayed labelled kernels, so the record, inverse, and finite-record
inference theorems are classical sampled-HMM and renewal theorems.  The
killed-leakage entries \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\) are part of the sampled boundary
convention.  A physical response model may later supply a scalar map
\(\Gamma=R_\Omega(x)\), in which case the article composes already proved
record-selected constants with that map.  That optional composition is not a
converse construction of \(T_0,T_1\), a validation of the boundary convention,
or a transfer theorem from the fixed-window continuous comparator to
stationary record spectra, inverse maps, Fano limits, or CLTs.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Main two-state sampled-renewal HMM theorem}\label{subsec:main-theorem-boundary}\label{subsec:scope-nonclaims}

For fixed
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\in(0,\infty)^3\) and the sampled
boundary-reset kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix},
\]
the visible click process is a stationary renewal process with gap law \(g_k\),
increasing hazard \(h_k\), covariance
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1,
\]
and exact \(1\)-dependence if and only if
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), equivalently
\(\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\).  This is
Main Theorem~1 and the sole theorem-level statement whose conclusion is the
stationary record law itself.  Main Theorem~2 is the quotient inverse from
visible inclusion probabilities to the exchange quotient
\((p+s,ps)\).  Main Theorem~3 is split into three fixed-record inference
statements: Theorem~3A gives the complete-cycle inclusion-coordinate CLT and
covariance estimator, Theorem~3B gives the quotient inverse delta method and
separated labelled rates only under \(\Delta_\sigma>0\) plus an external branch
map, and Theorem~3C gives the exchange-diagonal projected limit and pushes the
diagonal set forward to the common numerical rate.  The lag-two studentized
scalar interval is stated separately as a corollary.  The remaining renewal,
covariance,
finite LU product identity, batching, projection, and estimand statements are
supporting lemmas, propositions, or corollaries of \(T_0,T_1\) and the
associated stationary hidden Markov law.

The three main theorems and their supporting result groups are listed in
\Cref{tab:four-main-blocks}.  Lemmas, propositions, corollaries, and proof
devices are subordinated to one of these groups.  In particular, the generic
cycle-reward and separated-batch lemmas support the fixed-record inference
theorem rather than forming additional paper-level theorems.

\subsection{Sampled boundary-reset convention}\label{subsec:model-validity-boundary}

The record analysis begins from the following sampled boundary convention.

\begin{assumption}[Killed-leakage sampled-boundary regime]\label{ass:killed-leakage-boundary-reset-main}
The record results are stated for the sampled boundary-reset kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Equivalently, a bin that contains a click passes the refractory state \(D\) to
the next sampling boundary:
\[
T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0 .
\]
Thus every stationary, spectral, Fano-factor, inverse, and finite-record
inference claim below is about these sampled kernels, in the sense of
\Cref{rem:scope-nonclaims-main}.
\end{assumption}

\section{Classical sampled-counter setting and record-level observables}\label{sec:operational}

We first consider only the sampled readout law. Proper time is divided into
intervals of length $\Delta\tau$, a classical counter carries a hidden readiness
state, and the visible output is the stationary two-sided binary process
\[
(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z},
\]
where \(A_n=1\) denotes a click in the \(n\)th sampling interval of the
stationary extension.  All Palm, covariance, renewal, and independence
statements below refer to this two-sided stationary law.  A finite observed
record of length \(N\) is only the restriction
\[
A^{(N)}=(A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_{N-1}),
\]
of the stationary process.  The only nonstationary finite-start convention
used below is the post-burn-in retained-length convention: generate
\(L_N+N\) intervals from an arbitrary hidden initial law, discard the first
\(L_N\), and reindex the retained word as
\((A_{L_N},\ldots,A_{L_N+N-1})\), so \(N\) is always the retained sample size.
Compressing such a finite record to the empirical click
frequency recovers only the one-point observable
\[
\widehat\rho_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n.
\]
The full record, however, carries more. In particular, one may ask:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item what words belong to the support of the stationary law;
\item how the zero-run lengths between clicks are distributed;
\item whether the click hazard after a long run of zeros remembers the run length;
\item how the low-lag covariance sequence behaves;
\item what counting noise remains after coarse time averaging.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{definition}[Record benchmarks]\label{def:record-benchmarks-main}
For a stationary binary record $(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$, a \emph{zero-run length} is the number $G$ of consecutive zeros following a click and preceding the next click. If the zero-run law has full support, its hazard is
\[
h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k),\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
The ideal record is on the \emph{Parry benchmark} when its stationary law is the Parry measure on the golden-mean shift, i.e. the unique measure of maximal entropy subject to the grammar constraint $11\notin\operatorname{supp}\mu$. A stationary record is \emph{exact $1$-dependent} when, for every $m$, the sigma algebras generated by $\{A_j:j\le m\}$ and $\{A_j:j\ge m+2\}$ are independent.
Equivalently, one leaves the intermediate sample at time $m+1$ unobserved: a block ending at time $m$ is tested for independence from a block beginning at time $m+2$, not conditioned on the middle sample.
\end{definition}

These observables are not themselves rate observables, yet in the explicit
readout models below they reduce to sharp thresholds in the internal counter
rate $\Gamma$.

It is useful to keep the record construction in two logically separate steps.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item A counter model determines a stationary word law and a record-level benchmark: the Parry law in the ideal case, exact $1$-dependence in the sampled boundary-reset case.
\item For the two models studied here, that benchmark is first equivalent to a scalar internal counter-rate condition, respectively $\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$ and $\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$.  The memory condition is labelled only after the recovery rate has been independently identified: the inverse theorem gives
\[
\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}
\quad\text{and hence only}\quad
\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}.
\]
Without an external label the memory statement is made on the quotient by
$(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\sim(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)$.
\end{enumerate}
The two counter readout regimes studied in this work have intrinsically
different record thresholds. The ideal model imposes a hard symbolic grammar, so its
distinguished value is the entropy benchmark on the golden-mean shift. The
sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset model instead produces a
full-support renewal law, so its distinguished value is the exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold where the hidden eigenmode vanishes.

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem 1: record law]\label{thm:main-record-wrapper}
Fix \(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\) and the killed-leakage
sampled-boundary kernels \(T_0,T_1\) of
\Cref{prop:instrument-kernels-main}.  Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be the
stationary visible binary process generated by these kernels, let \(G\) be the
number of zeros between consecutive clicks, and put
\[
\rho=\PP(A_0=1),\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b .
\]
Then \((A_n)\) is a full-support renewal process with Palm gap law
\[
g_k=\PP(G=k)=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1,\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
If \(\Gamma\ne\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), then
\[
g_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-s)s^k}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
while on the equal-rate diagonal the formula is interpreted by the continuous
limit, equivalently for
\(y=\Gamma\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\) and \(p=e^{-y}\),
\[
g_k=p^k\bigl((1-p)(1+yk)-yp\bigr).
\]
The zero-run hazard \(h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k)\) is strictly increasing.  For
every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\gamma_n:=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)
=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}.
\]
The record is exact \(1\)-dependent iff \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), and in no
positive parameter case is it a finite-order Markov chain.  Finite dependence
of separated sigma-fields is not the same as a finite-order Markov transition
rule; the increasing renewal hazard rules out finite Markov order.  Labelled-rate
reporting throughout the paper follows
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}; in particular this record law
does not by itself label the two survival roots as attempt and recovery rates.
\end{theorem}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The killed-leakage sampled-boundary theorem is the main mathematical result:
one classical sampled counter law produces a full-support renewal record with
strictly increasing hazard, suppressed adjacent-click conditional probability,
classical sub-Poisson count noise, a unique exact \(1\)-dependence threshold at
the zero of the hidden eigenmode, renewal inclusion products with optional
finite LU product identity, a closed Fano law, and a three-inclusion inverse with
\(D=r_1-r_0^2\ne0\), \(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) exchange
duality, no separated labelled-rate chart at \(p=s\), and an identifiable
diagonal common rate there. The ideal Parry
point remains the benchmark comparison rather than the main theorem.
All items in this list are sampled-record statements governed by
\Cref{rem:scope-nonclaims-main}.

The theorem-family comparison in \Cref{tab:main-jpa-comparison} records, one
main theorem at a time, how these sampled-counter results differ from recent
partially observed record, finite-dependence, and renewal-inference papers.

\section{Theorem-by-Theorem Comparison}\label{sec:theorem-led-comparison}

\Cref{tab:main-jpa-comparison} is a theorem-by-theorem comparison.  Each row

nearest recent observed-record comparator.  The physical scope boundary is the

The present paper is mathematically closer to functions of finite Markov
chains, MAP representation theory, renewal CLTs, and simulation-output
covariance estimation than to thermodynamic inference.  The
stochastic-thermodynamic papers are useful recent comparators for partially
observed records, but they are not the primary mathematical baseline for the
record-law and inference theorems.  The older baseline therefore consists of the
Blackwell--Koopmans and Gilbert identifiability problem for finite-chain
functions \cite{BlackwellKoopmans1957,Gilbert1959}, Ryd\'en's aggregated-chain
and MAP/phase-type identifiability results \cite{Ryden1996Aggregated}, the
MAP representation survey of Asanjarani and Nazarathy
\cite{AsanjaraniNazarathy2022MAP}, regenerative standardized-time-series
covariance estimation of Glynn and Iglehart \cite{GlynnIglehart1990STS}, and
the simulation and renewal limit theory summarized by Asmussen and Glynn
\cite{AsmussenGlynn2007}.

The visible process is a function of a finite Markov chain.  The appropriate
classical baseline therefore includes Heller's characterization of processes
derived from Markov chains, Fox--Rubin and Erickson on functions of Markovian
states/chains, Gurvits--Ledoux on the Markov property of a function of a Markov
chain, and Spreij on finite hidden Markov chains
\cite{Heller1965,FoxRubin1968,Erickson1970,GurvitsLedoux2005,
FinessoGrassiSpreij2010}.  Burke--Rosenblatt's Markovian-function example, the
Kemeny--Snell finite-chain lumpability criterion, and Dharmadhikari's
finite-chain-function tests supply the surrounding finite-state context
\cite{BurkeRosenblatt1958MarkovianFunction,KemenySnell1960FiniteMarkovChains,
Dharmadhikari1963Functions,Dharmadhikari1963Sufficient}.  The present theorem
differs from those works by exploiting the triangular killed-leakage kernel to
obtain an exact renewal law and an explicit quotient inverse.

Recent physics--math memory papers such as Zhao--Hartich--Godec's
equilibrium-versus-driven emergence comparison
\cite{ZhaoHartichGodec2024Memory} and the J. Phys. A ``Non-Markovian effects''
framing of Loos--Godec \cite{LoosGodec2025NonMarkovianEffects} concern the
emergence, diagnosis, or organization of memory in projected nonequilibrium
records.  They are useful scope comparators, but the submitted theorems below
prove exact record-law and quotient-inverse statements for a fixed two-state
sampled renewal model.

\paragraph{Determinant notation boundary.}
The finite LU product identity in
\Cref{prop:renewal-principal-minor-lu-main,rem:finite-determinant-language-main}
uses \(D_{\rm LU}(B)=\det M(B)\) only as the LU product determinant for the
renewal inclusion product.  Determinantal
point-process existence theory
\cite{Macchi1975Fermion,Lyons2003DPP} uses admissible kernels, with the usual
Hermitian discrete criterion \(0\le K\le I\), to define an entire process from
determinantal inclusion probabilities.  No positive contraction kernel, DPP
sampling rule, repulsion property, negative association theorem, or
determinantal-process construction is asserted here.
