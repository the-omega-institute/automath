\begin{abstract}
This paper proves exact record laws for a stipulated classical sampled counter.
Proper time is sampled in intervals of length \(\Delta\tau\), the hidden state
records readiness or recovery, and the visible record is the binary click
sequence. The theorem chain is entirely record-level until the optional
appendix calibration is invoked. The ideal one-step blanking model is retained
only as the standard Parry benchmark
\(\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi\). The main sampled boundary-reset theorem
treats the one-recovery-opportunity counter with attempt rate \(\Gamma\) and
recovery rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\): its zero-run law has full support on
\(\mathbb N_0\), its zero-run hazard is strictly increasing, and its click
covariance has the hidden-eigenvalue tail
\(\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{n-1}\).
The visible record is exact \(1\)-dependent exactly at the unique rate
\(\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) where
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\).

A stationary-renewal lemma gives a renewal finite-principal-minor encoding for
the click set, and the boundary-reset law specializes it to an explicit
Toeplitz matrix whose finite principal minors equal the renewal inclusion
products. These finite matrices are nonsymmetric finite-dimensional
determinantal correlation representatives supplied by the renewal law, not
Hermitian positive-contraction or Fredholm operator kernels. Thus the
determinant result is a renewal inclusion determinant, not a hyperuniformity,
DPP-sampling, or nonsymmetric-DPP well-definedness theorem. Its role is the
compact nonsymmetric finite-minor representation of this sampled renewal
family. The same finite-principal-minor record law has a quotient inverse:
three inclusion probabilities recover the symmetric coordinates, and hence at
the population level the unordered survival-factor pair
\(\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}\), equivalently
\(\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}\). Thus one
visible sampled record does not label which recovered rate is the recovery
rate. Finite-record inverse inference is stated first for the symmetric
coordinates \((s_1,s_2)\); ordinary root and rate covariance estimates require
a separated-root hypothesis away from the exchange diagonal
\(e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\). The finite-record
part then proves a fixed-setting lag-two covariance CLT and a complete-cycle
variance estimator for this sampled-counter law. The finite-record CLT
estimates only fixed-setting \(\gamma_2\); no scan-root or response-locus
confidence set is proved.

The appendix contains only a conditional calibration example. If,
independently of the record theorem, one supplies a positive \(C^1\) response
profile \(R_\Omega\) and imposes the external scalar closure
\(\Gamma=R_\Omega(x)=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)\), then the record-selected rates become
ordinary response-level equations for that already chosen function. The memory
level \(\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) is labelled only after
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) has been independently supplied by calibration,
hardware recovery measurement, or an external protocol; otherwise the statement
remains a quotient statement under
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\sim(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)\). No response
profile, UDW measurement model, microscopic output law, or response-locus
location inference is derived here.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} sampled counter, hidden Markov model, renewal finite-principal-minor encoding, finite-record diagnostics, conditional response calibration

\section{Introduction}

This paper is about the record law of a classical sampled counter. The counter
has an internal attempt rate \(\Gamma\), one hidden readiness bit, and a binary
output recording whether the sampling interval contains a click. Its visible
process has grammar, waiting-time, dependence, covariance, and counting-noise
observables that are invisible in a rate-only summary. The paper-level object
is therefore the sampled record itself: one stipulated interval instrument, its
stationary binary law, its dependence threshold, its renewal-product
finite-principal-minor encoding, its quotient inverse, and its fixed-record
lag-two covariance estimator.

Transition rates, detailed balance, and Tolman redshift remain the standard
observables used to characterize Unruh and Hawking thermality
\cite{Tolman1930,TolmanEhrenfest1930,Unruh1976,DeWitt1979,BirDav1982,Takagi1986,CrispinoHiguchiMatsas2008,LoukoSatz2006,LoukoSatz2008},
but they are not inputs to the sampled-counter theorem. They enter only in the
appendix-level calibration bridge, after the classical record law has already
selected scalar target rates.

The theorem-by-theorem comparison with nearby recent \emph{J. Phys. A},
mathematical-physics, and standard probability results is placed with the
scope boundaries in Appendix~\ref{app:scope-boundaries}; see
\Cref{tab:main-jpa-comparison}.  The main text below is organized as four
record-side theorem blocks, followed only by the conditional response
calibration appendix.

The answer has two layers. The first layer is record theory, with the new
mathematics concentrated in the sampled boundary-reset law. In the ideal
one-step dead-time mode the record is the stipulated golden-mean Markov chain,
and the standard Parry calculation gives the benchmark clock value
$\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$. In the sampled one-recovery-opportunity
boundary-reset mode the visible process is instead a full-support renewal law.
Its zero-run hazard is strictly increasing, every finite binary word remains
admissible, and the visible record becomes exact $1$-dependent exactly when
the hidden eigenvalue vanishes, at the unique rate
$\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$. A general renewal
finite-principal-minor lemma gives a compact encoding of all finite inclusion
probabilities, and the boundary-reset law supplies the explicit Toeplitz
finite-principal-minor specialization with geometric strict-upper tail. At
$\Gamma_c$ its transfer parameter vanishes, giving exact $1$-dependence
without finite visible Markov order. The finite-principal-minor record law also has an exact
population inverse: three inclusion probabilities recover the symmetric
polynomial coordinates of the unordered attempt/recovery rate pair, revealing
a $\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}$ exchange duality and a
labelled-rate non-identifiability from one visible record. Finite-record
inference is globally regular in the symmetric coordinates; root and rate
delta-method inference is asserted only under a formal separated-root condition
away from the exchange diagonal. The labelled recovery rate used by the later
memory response locus is therefore an independent calibration input, not
something recovered with a label by the inverse theorem. The
memory threshold is therefore not an entropy benchmark but an exact dependence
transition inside a renewal family that still supports the full binary
grammar.

The submitted theorem chain has four main blocks, listed in
\Cref{tab:four-main-blocks}.  Lemmas, corollaries, proof devices, and response
examples are subordinated to one of these blocks.  In particular, the generic
cycle-reward lemmas prove Block~IV, and the response-calibration material after
the appendix marker is a conditional translation of the record-selected rates,
not a fifth main theorem family.

\subsection{Scope and nonclaims}\label{subsec:scope-nonclaims}

The scope restrictions used throughout the theorem statements are collected
here so they do not have to be restated in every block.  The paper proves
record laws for one stipulated classical sampled counter.  It does not derive
that counter from a UDW measurement model, field-state update rule, microscopic
output law, or continuous-monitoring scheme.  Response geometry enters only
after a scalar response profile \(R_\Omega\) has been supplied and the
calibration closure \(\Gamma=R_\Omega(x)=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)\) has been imposed.

The finite-principal-minor matrices are algebraic representatives of renewal
inclusion products on finite index sets.  They are not Hermitian
positive-contraction kernels, Fredholm kernels, CAR/quasi-free correlation
kernels, or sources of operator-DPP consequences such as negative association.
The visible inverse is likewise a fixed-family quotient inverse: it recovers
only the unordered survival-factor set
\(\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}\).  Any later
use of a labelled recovery rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) is therefore an
external calibration input.

Finite-record inference is fixed-setting inference unless an additional
scan-design theorem is explicitly supplied.  The self-contained CLT estimates
\(\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)\) and its variance for one stationary
setting; it is not a scan-root CLT, response-locus confidence set,
response-locus-radius confidence interval, or model-free memory-inference
theorem.  Any regular-level-set terminology is used only after the response
hypotheses have supplied the response profile.

The finite-record theorem on the record side estimates only the fixed-setting
covariance $\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)$ and its variance; for the
sampled boundary-reset law, a coboundary argument proves the cycle variance is
strictly positive for all positive parameters, so the fixed-setting

\Cref{tab:main-jpa-comparison} records the theorem-level comparisons in three
groups: finite-state/HMM probability, determinant-process theory, and
UDW/local-measurement theory.

Dead time and recovery are also physically natural. In nuclear and photon counting, refractory effects have been central since the earliest pulse detectors and remain standard in modern single-photon models \cite{Muller1973,CantorTeich1975,Hadfield2009,EisamanEtAl2011}. The observables used here---grammar, hazard, dependence class, covariance tail, and low-frequency noise---therefore belong to the standard counting-statistics fingerprint of a sampled detector output. Their role is not to replace the local rate, but to show that keeping the full record produces exact thresholds that can later be lifted to response-model level sets.

The paper is organized accordingly. \Cref{sec:operational} and
\Cref{sec:records} define the record-level observables, record the standard
exact-clock Parry benchmark for the ideal counter, and carry the four
record-side theorem blocks in \Cref{tab:four-main-blocks}.  The appendix then
gives a conditional calibration example: after one positive \(C^1\) response
profile and the external A0 closure have been supplied, the record-selected
rates become ordinary response-locus level constants for that profile. Longer
homogeneous, near-horizon, fixed-radius, Schwarzschild, external-ratio, and
residual-graph developments are outside the submitted theorem chain.

\section{Classical sampled-counter setting and record-level observables}\label{sec:operational}

We first consider only the sampled readout law. Proper time is divided into
intervals of length $\Delta\tau$, a classical counter carries a hidden readiness
state, and the visible output is a stationary binary process
\[
A_0,A_1,A_2,\dots \in \{0,1\},
\]
where $A_n=1$ denotes a click in the $n$th sampling interval. The word
``detector'' in Sections~\ref{sec:operational}--\ref{sec:records} refers to
this classical readout mode unless the later calibration closure
$\Gamma=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)$ is explicitly invoked. Compressing the record to the
empirical click frequency recovers only the one-point observable
\[
\widehat\rho_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n.
\]
The full record, however, carries more. In particular, one may ask:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item what words belong to the support of the stationary law;
\item how the zero-run lengths between clicks are distributed;
\item whether the click hazard after a long run of zeros remembers the run length;
\item how the low-lag covariance profile behaves;
\item what counting noise remains after coarse time averaging.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{definition}[Record benchmarks]\label{def:record-benchmarks-main}
For a stationary binary record $(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$, a \emph{zero-run length} is the number $G$ of consecutive zeros following a click and preceding the next click. If the zero-run law has full support, its hazard is
\[
h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k),\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
The ideal record is on the \emph{Parry benchmark} when its stationary law is the Parry measure on the golden-mean shift, i.e. the unique measure of maximal entropy subject to the grammar constraint $11\notin\operatorname{supp}\mu$. A stationary record is \emph{exact $1$-dependent} when, for every $m$, the sigma algebras generated by $\{A_j:j\le m\}$ and $\{A_j:j\ge m+2\}$ are independent.
Equivalently, one leaves the intermediate sample at time $m+1$ unobserved: a block ending at time $m$ is tested for independence from a block beginning at time $m+2$, not conditioned on the middle sample.
\end{definition}

These observables are not themselves rate observables, yet in the explicit
readout models below they reduce to sharp thresholds in the internal counter
rate $\Gamma$. That reduction is important: response geometry enters only after
the stochastic-record benchmark has already fixed the target rate and the
separate calibration closure has been imposed.

It is useful to keep the construction in three logically separate steps.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item A counter model determines a stationary word law and a record-level benchmark: the Parry law in the ideal case, exact $1$-dependence in the sampled boundary-reset case.
\item For the two models studied here, that benchmark is first equivalent to a scalar internal counter-rate condition, respectively $\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$ and $\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$.  The memory condition is labelled only after the recovery rate has been independently identified: the inverse theorem gives
\[
\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}
\quad\text{and hence only}\quad
\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}.
\]
Under the paper's calibration closure $\Gamma=\Gamma_+$, the two conditions
therefore become $\Gamma_+\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$ and, with that external
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) label, $\Gamma_+=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$; without it the memory statement is made on the quotient by
$(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\sim(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)$.
\item Substitution of the specified static KMS response then turns those rate conditions into level-set equations for the detector response.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the response loci are model level sets whose constants are selected by
full-record statistics; detailed balance supplies only the thermal rate ratio.

The two detector regimes studied in this work have intrinsically different
record thresholds. The ideal model imposes a hard symbolic grammar, so its
distinguished value is the entropy benchmark on the golden-mean shift. The
sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset model instead produces a
full-support renewal law, so its distinguished value is the exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold where the hidden eigenmode vanishes.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The sampled boundary-reset theorem is the main mathematical result: one
classical sampled counter law produces a full-support renewal record with
strictly increasing hazard, suppressed adjacent-click conditional probability,
classical sub-Poisson count noise, a unique exact \(1\)-dependence threshold at
the zero of the hidden eigenmode, a Toeplitz renewal
finite-principal-minor encoding, a closed Fano law, and a three-inclusion inverse with
\(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) exchange duality. The ideal Parry
point remains the benchmark comparison rather than the main theorem.

The response corollary is the fixed-profile consequence of those
record-selected target rates under A0. It turns the two scalar rates into level
sets of one supplied \(C^1\) response function, with the terminology governed
by \Cref{subsec:scope-nonclaims}. The theorem-family comparison in
\Cref{tab:main-jpa-comparison} records how this sampled-counter theorem chain
differs from record-HMM, one-dependent/regenerative determinant formulas,
stochastic-trajectory, memory-inference, and UDW/AQFT detector results.

\paragraph{Theorem-level comparison distinctions.}
Our determinant theorem is a renewal-supplied nonsymmetric finite-dimensional
correlation representation.  This finite correlation-representative statement is
separate from Hermitian positive-contraction/operator DPP theory and also
separate from nonsymmetric-DPP admissibility or sampling theorems; compare
genuine DPP hyperuniformity, sampling, and nonsymmetric-kernel well-definedness
\cite{MatsuiKatoriShirai2021HeisenbergDPP,BardenetFanuelFeller2024SamplingDPP,GartrellEtAl2019NonsymmetricDPP,GartrellEtAl2021ScalableNonsymmetricDPP,HanEtAl2022SamplingNonsymmetricDPP,Poinas2026NonsymmetricDPP}.
Our inverse theorem is fixed-family quotient identifiability, not generic HMM
identifiability, model-free trajectory-memory inference, or weak-memory
reduction theory
\cite{AllmanMatiasRhodes2009,GassiatCleynenRobin2016,AlexandrovichHolzmannLeister2016,VollmarEtAl2024Memory,MeyerBrandner2026WeakMemory}.
Our response corollary is not a detector measurement theorem and does not
derive the click record from UDW dynamics; compare recent UDW, detector-based,
localized-detector, and AQFT/local-measurement derivations and critiques
\cite{LoukoSatz2006,LoukoSatz2008,FewsterJuarezLouko2016,TjoaEtAl2023click,PercheLocalized2023,FewsterVerch2020,PoloGomezEtAl2022,PapageorgiouFraser2024LocalMeasurement,PranziniKeskiVakkuri2025AQFT,Oeckl2025CausalMeasurement}.

First, the determinant language in Sections~\ref{sec:operational}--\ref{sec:records}
means only a \emph{renewal finite-principal-minor encoding}.  For every
finite set \(B\), the manuscript constructs a finite matrix whose principal
minor equals \(\PP(B\subset\Xi)\).  For a finite restriction this is a
nonsymmetric determinantal correlation representation of the renewal marginal,
with exact probabilities supplied by finite inclusion--exclusion from the
renewal law.  This is not the same assertion as a Hermitian
positive-contraction DPP, a fermionic determinantal process, or a
negative-association theorem.  No operator on \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\), Fourier
symbol \(0\le f\le1\), CAR/quasi-free state, Fredholm determinant, or canonical
operator correlation kernel is constructed.  Consequently none of the standard
operator-DPP consequences in Macchi, Shirai--Takahashi, Lyons, Soshnikov,
Borodin, or Lyons--Steif is imported except where explicitly cited as a
comparison point
\cite{Macchi1975,ShiraiTakahashi2003,Lyons2003,Soshnikov2000,Borodin2009DPP,LyonsSteif2003}.

Second, the hidden-Markov statements are fixed-family statements.  The sampled
interval instrument is specified before inference begins, and the inverse
theorem recovers only the visible-family quotient coordinates.  It does not
claim generic HMM identifiability, hidden-state order recovery, or labelled
kernel recovery in the sense of the classical finite-Markov-function literature
or modern rank/emission-identifiability, model-free memory-inference, and
weak-memory reduction theorems
\cite{BlackwellKoopmans1957,Gilbert1959,BaumPetrie1966,AllmanMatiasRhodes2009,GassiatCleynenRobin2016,AlexandrovichHolzmannLeister2016,VollmarEtAl2024Memory,MeyerBrandner2026WeakMemory}.
In particular,
\[
\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}
\]
for the visible sampled law, so a record identifies only
\(\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}\).  Any statement
using \(R_\Omega(x)=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) therefore assumes
an independent label for \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), supplied for example by
detector calibration, hardware recovery measurement, or an external
experimental protocol; otherwise it is a quotient statement under
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\sim(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)\).

Third, the response theorem is a conditional fixed-profile corollary.  It
starts after a scalar response profile \(R_\Omega\) has been supplied and checks
range and regularity for the record-selected target values.  It is not a UDW
detector-dynamics derivation, not a microscopic output-statistics or
continuous-monitoring theorem, not a repeated-measurement law for
non-replicable systems, and not an AQFT local-measurement scheme
\cite{LoukoSatz2006,LoukoSatz2008,Glauber1963,SrinivasDavies1981,Carmichael1993Book,WisemanMilburn2009,BarchielliGregoratti2009,FewsterVerch2020,Fewster2020,PoloGomezEtAl2022,PranziniEtAl2024,PranziniKeskiVakkuri2025AQFT,Oeckl2025CausalMeasurement}.
The terminology ``shell'' is used only for codimension-one spatial regular
level-set components of that already specified response profile.  Non-spatial
or one-dimensional examples are described as response loci.


\section{Renewal--Reward Proof Package for Block IV}\label{app:renewal-reward-proof-package}

This appendix contains one reusable finite-lag renewal-reward theorem.  It
combines the renewal CLT, stationary-window boundary control, random complete
cycle counts, plug-in centering, and separated-batch variance consistency used
by the finite-record theorem.  The result is proof machinery for the sampled
boundary-reset theorem, not an additional statistical output of the paper.

\begin{lemma}[Separated-batch consistency for random all-cycle counts]\label{lem:separated-batch-qdependent-main}
Fix \(d\ge1\) and \(q\ge0\).  Let \(L_1,L_2,\ldots\) be i.i.d. positive
integer-valued cycle lengths with
\[
\mu_L=\EE L_1\in(0,\infty),
\qquad
\PP(L_1\ge k)\le C e^{-\eta k}\quad(k\ge1).
\]
Let \((Y_j)_{j\ge1}\) be a mean-zero \(\RR^d\)-valued stationary
\(q\)-dependent cycle-reward sequence of the form
\[
Y_j=\psi(L_j,\ldots,L_{j+q}),\qquad
\|Y_j\|\le C_\psi\Bigl(1+\sum_{\ell=0}^q L_{j+\ell}\Bigr).
\]
Set
\[
\Gamma_Y=
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_1)
+\sum_{h=1}^q\left\{
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_{1+h})
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{1+h},Y_1)
\right\},
\]
where the covariance is interpreted by stationarity.  Let \(b_N\to\infty\) and
\(b_N/N\to0\), and let \(K_N=\max\{k:\sum_{j=1}^k L_j\le N\}\).  Define
\[
M_N=
\begin{cases}
\left\lfloor K_N/b_N\right\rfloor,&q=0,\\[3pt]
\left\lfloor K_N/(b_N+q)\right\rfloor,&q\ge1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
s_{r,N}=(r-1)(b_N+q)+1,
\]
with the convention \(s_{r,N}=(r-1)b_N+1\) when \(q=0\).  For \(1\le r\le M_N\)
put
\[
B_{r,N}^{\circ}=\sum_{j=s_{r,N}}^{s_{r,N}+b_N-1}Y_j,\qquad
\bar B_N^{\circ}=\frac1{M_N}\sum_{r=1}^{M_N}B_{r,N}^{\circ},
\]
and
\[
R_N=M_Nb_N+(M_N-1)q,\qquad
\bar L_N=\frac1{R_N}\sum_{j=1}^{R_N}L_j
\]
on \(\{M_N\ge2\}\).  Then \(M_N\to\infty\) in probability,
\(\bar L_N\to\mu_L\) in probability, and
\[
\frac{1}{\bar L_N\,b_N\,(M_N-1)}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_N}
(B_{r,N}^{\circ}-\bar B_N^{\circ})
(B_{r,N}^{\circ}-\bar B_N^{\circ})^\top
\longrightarrow
\frac{\Gamma_Y}{\mu_L}
\qquad\text{in probability}.
\]
The displayed estimator is interpreted arbitrarily on \(\{M_N<2\}\), an event
whose probability tends to zero.
The same conclusion holds for a stationary renewal window after deleting the
initial incomplete cycle, the terminal incomplete cycle, and the terminal
remainder of fewer than \(b_N+q\) complete cycles.

Moreover, suppose feasible rewards \(\widehat Y_{j,N}\) satisfy, uniformly over
the batched cycle indices,
\[
\|\widehat Y_{j,N}-Y_j\|\le C\,\delta_N L_j,
\qquad
\delta_N=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}).
\]
If \(\widehat B_{r,N}\) and \(\bar{\widehat B}_N\) are formed from
\(\widehat Y_{j,N}\) with the same separated batches, then replacing
\(B_{r,N}^{\circ}\) by \(\widehat B_{r,N}\) in the displayed matrix estimator
does not change its probability limit.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The exponential tail gives moments of all orders for \(L_1\), and the linear
growth assumption gives \(\EE\|Y_1\|^4<\infty\).  For a deterministic batch
length \(b\), the separated batch sums
\[
B_{r,b}^{\circ}=\sum_{j=(r-1)(b+q)+1}^{(r-1)(b+q)+b}Y_j
\]
are independent in \(r\), because the \(q\) omitted rewards separate the
supporting cycle-length variables of neighboring included blocks.  For \(q=0\)
there is no omission and the rewards are independent.  They are identically
distributed and mean zero.  Their covariance satisfies
\[
\frac1b\EE B_{1,b}^{\circ}(B_{1,b}^{\circ})^\top
=
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_1)
+\sum_{h=1}^q\left(1-\frac{h}{b}\right)
\left\{\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_{1+h})
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{1+h},Y_1)\right\}
\longrightarrow \Gamma_Y .
\]
Also,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:batch-fourth-main}
\EE\|B_{1,b}^{\circ}\|^4\le C b^2 .
\end{equation}
Indeed, split the \(q\)-dependent sequence into \(q+1\) residue classes; each
class is a sum of independent variables with finite fourth moment, so the
fourth moment of each class sum is \(O(b^2)\), and Minkowski's inequality gives
\eqref{eq:batch-fourth-main}.

Let \(X_{r,N}=b_N^{-1/2}B_{r,b_N}^{\circ}\).  The preceding bounds imply
\[
\sup_N \EE\|X_{1,N}\|^4<\infty,\qquad
\EE X_{1,N}X_{1,N}^\top\to\Gamma_Y .
\]
For deterministic \(m_N\to\infty\), the ordinary triangular-array weak law for
independent rows gives
\[
\frac1{m_N}\sum_{r=1}^{m_N}X_{r,N}X_{r,N}^\top
\to\Gamma_Y,\qquad
\frac1{m_N}\sum_{r=1}^{m_N}X_{r,N}\to0
\]
in probability.  The same convergence is uniform over deterministic indices in
any relative window \(m\in[m_N(1-\varepsilon),m_N(1+\varepsilon)]\).  For
example, for each matrix entry,
Kolmogorov's maximal inequality applied to the independent centered variables
\((X_{r,N}X_{r,N}^\top-\EE X_{1,N}X_{1,N}^\top)_{ab}\) gives
\[
\PP\!\left(
\max_{m_-\le m\le m_+}
\left|
\frac1m\sum_{r=1}^{m}
(X_{r,N}X_{r,N}^\top-\EE X_{1,N}X_{1,N}^\top)_{ab}
\right|>\varepsilon
\right)
\le
\frac{C\,m_+}{\varepsilon^2 m_-^2},
\]
which tends to zero when \(m_-/m_N\) and \(m_+/m_N\) stay bounded away from
\(0\) and infinity.  The vector average is handled identically using the
second-moment bound.  Hence the centered sample covariance of the first \(m\)
separated batches converges uniformly in that relative window to \(\Gamma_Y\).

The renewal strong law gives \(K_N/N\to1/\mu_L\), and therefore
\[
M_N\sim \frac{N}{\mu_L(b_N+q)}
\]
with the evident interpretation \(b_N+q=b_N\) when \(q=0\).  Since
\(b_N=o(N)\), \(M_N\to\infty\), and with probability tending to one \(M_N\)
lies in one of the deterministic relative windows just considered.  The
uniform deterministic-index convergence therefore applies at the random index
\(M_N\), even though \(M_N\) is a function of the same cycle lengths.  The
ordinary strong law for the cycle lengths, again evaluated at the random index
\(R_N\to\infty\), gives \(\bar L_N\to\mu_L\).  This proves the oracle matrix
estimator.

For a stationary renewal window, the first incomplete cycle is a size-biased
cycle and the residual time to the first observed renewal has an exponential
tail.  After that first observed renewal the subsequent complete cycles have
the Palm law used above.  The initial and terminal incomplete cycles are tight,
and the terminal unbatched remainder contains fewer than \(b_N+q\) complete
cycles.  These deletions change neither \(K_N/N\to1/\mu_L\) nor the relative
position of \(M_N\), because \(b_N=o(N)\).  The same proof therefore applies
to all-complete-cycle batches extracted from a stationary record.

It remains to check plug-in centering.  Put
\[
\Delta_{r,N}=\widehat B_{r,N}-B_{r,N}^{\circ}.
\]
The displayed perturbation hypothesis gives
\[
\|\Delta_{r,N}\|
\le C\delta_N\sum_{j=s_{r,N}}^{s_{r,N}+b_N-1}L_j .
\]
The same fourth-moment argument used above, now for the positive cycle lengths,
combined with the uniform relative-window argument at the random index \(M_N\),
yields
\[
\frac1{M_Nb_N}\sum_{r=1}^{M_N}\|\Delta_{r,N}\|^2
=O_{\PP}(\delta_N^2 b_N)=O_{\PP}(b_N/N)=o_{\PP}(1).
\]
After subtracting the batch means this bound can only increase by a fixed
factor.  The oracle normalized second moment is \(O_{\PP}(1)\), so
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\frac1{M_Nb_N}\sum_{r=1}^{M_N}
\|(B_{r,N}^{\circ}-\bar B_N^{\circ})
(\Delta_{r,N}-\bar\Delta_N)^\top\|=o_{\PP}(1),
\]
and the quadratic term in \(\Delta_{r,N}-\bar\Delta_N\) is \(o_{\PP}(1)\) as
well.  Multiplication by \(\bar L_N^{-1}\to\mu_L^{-1}\) proves the plug-in
version.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-lag renewal-reward CLT and separated-batch variance]\label{thm:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main}\label{def:cycle-block-main}\label{lem:cycle-one-dep-main}\label{lem:renewal-reward-boundary-main}\label{lem:renewal-local-clt-main}\label{lem:batch-var-random-count-main}\label{lem:deterministic-prefix-main}\label{lem:lag2-boundary-main}\label{thm:cycle-block-main}
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be a stationary binary renewal record whose click
times are regeneration times.  In the Palm law started from a click at time
\(0\), write
\[
0=T_0<T_1<T_2<\cdots,\qquad L_j=T_{j+1}-T_j,\qquad G_j=L_j-1,
\]
and assume the i.i.d. gaps have an exponential tail:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp-tail-main}
\PP(G_1\ge k)\le C e^{-\eta k}\qquad(k\ge0).
\end{equation}
Let \(q\ge0\) be fixed, and let \((Z_n)\) be a bounded stationary local
observable with \(\EE_{\mathrm{stat}} Z_0=0\).  Suppose its Palm cycle rewards
\[
Y_j=\sum_{n=T_j}^{T_{j+1}-1}Z_n
\]
have a finite-lag gap representation
\[
Y_j=f(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+q}),\qquad
|f(g_0,\ldots,g_q)|\le C_f\Bigl(1+\sum_{i=0}^q g_i\Bigr).
\]
Then \((Y_j)\) is stationary \(q\)-dependent, has moments of every order, and
with
\[
\mu_L=\EE L_1,\qquad
\tau_q^2=\Var(Y_1)+2\sum_{h=1}^q\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_{1+h}),
\]
the stationary-window renewal-reward CLT is
\[
\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} Z_n
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{\tau_q^2}{\mu_L}\right).
\]
The boundary between the stationary window and the complete Palm cycles is
\(o_{\PP}(\sqrt N)\); equivalently the initial incomplete cycle and the terminal
incomplete cycle do not contribute at root-\(N\) scale.

The same theorem gives the all-complete-cycle separated-batch variance
estimator.  Let \(0\le\tau_1<\cdots<\tau_{J_N}\le N-1\) be the observed click
times, choose \(b_N\to\infty\) with \(b_N/N\to0\), and for \(q\ge1\) put
\[
M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}
=\left\lfloor\frac{J_N-1}{b_N+q}\right\rfloor,\qquad
R_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}
=M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}b_N+(M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}-1)q,
\]
with value \(0\) when \(M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}=0\).  For \(q=0\), use the same
formulas with \(b_N+q=b_N\) and no omitted cycle.  Form batch sums from \(b_N\)
successive rewards and omit \(q\) rewards between neighboring batches.  If the
oracle population-centered rewards are used, then
\[
\frac{1}{\bar L_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}\,b_N\,(M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}-1)}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}}
\bigl(B_{r,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}-\bar B_N^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}\bigr)^2
\to
\frac{\tau_q^2}{\mu_L}
\qquad\text{in probability}.
\]
The same limit holds for plug-in rewards \(\widehat Y_{j,N}\) whenever the
plug-in centering error satisfies \(\delta_N=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) and, uniformly
over batched cycles,
\[
|\widehat Y_{j,N}-Y_{j,N}^{\circ,\mathrm{all}}|
\le C\,\delta_N\,(\tau_{j+1}-\tau_j).
\]

For the lag-two covariance statistic used in Block~IV, take \(q=1\),
\[
Z_n=(A_nA_{n+2}-\mu_2)-2\rho(A_n-\rho),
\qquad
\rho=\EE A_0,\quad \mu_2=\EE(A_0A_2).
\]
Then
\[
\widehat\gamma_2-\gamma_2
=
\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Z_n+O_{\PP}(N^{-1}),
\qquad
\gamma_2=\mu_2-\rho^2,
\]
where
\[
\widehat\gamma_2
=\frac1{N-2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-3}
(A_n-\widehat\rho_N)(A_{n+2}-\widehat\rho_N).
\]
Thus
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat\gamma_2-\gamma_2)
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{\tau_1^2}{\mu_L}\right).
\]
If \(G,H,H'\) are i.i.d. copies of the zero-run gap and
\[
\pi_g=\PP(G=g),\qquad m_1=\EE G,\qquad m_2=\EE G^2,
\]
\[
\alpha=2\rho^2-\mu_2,\qquad \beta=\alpha-2\rho,
\]
then the lag-two cycle reward has the explicit form
\[
Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1}),\qquad
f(g,h)=\alpha g+\beta+\mathbf 1_{\{g=1\}}+\mathbf 1_{\{g=0,h=0\}},
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau2-explicit-main}
\tau_1^2
=
\alpha^2m_2
+2\alpha\beta m_1
+\beta^2
+(2\alpha+2\beta+1)\pi_1
+(4\beta+1)\pi_0^2
+2\pi_0^3 .
\end{equation}
The all-cycle estimator specialized to \(q=1\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycle-block-est-all-main}
\widehat\sigma_{2,N}^{\mathrm{all},2}
=
\frac{1}{\bar L_N^{\mathrm{all}}\,b_N\,(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}-1)}
\sum_{r=1}^{M_N^{\mathrm{all}}}
\bigl(\widehat B_{r,N}^{\mathrm{all}}-\bar B_N^{\mathrm{all}}\bigr)^2 ,
\end{equation}
where
\[
M_N^{\mathrm{all}}
=\left\lfloor\frac{J_N-1}{b_N+1}\right\rfloor,\qquad
R_N^{\mathrm{all}}=M_N^{\mathrm{all}}b_N+(M_N^{\mathrm{all}}-1),
\]
and it converges in probability to \(\tau_1^2/\mu_L\).  If this variance is
positive, the corresponding studentized statistic converges to
\(\mathcal N(0,1)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cycle-reward dependence assertion is immediate from the i.i.d. gap
sequence: \(Y_j\) depends on \(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+q}\), so rewards separated by
more than \(q\) cycle indices are independent.  The exponential tail and the
linear growth bound give all moments.  The ordinary \(q\)-dependent CLT gives
\[
m^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^mY_j\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,\tau_q^2).
\]
The renewal strong law gives \(K_N/N\to1/\mu_L\) for the number \(K_N\) of
complete Palm cycles by time \(N\).  Anscombe's random-index transfer, applied
to the \(q\)-dependent partial sums by splitting them into finitely many
independent subsequences, changes \(m\) to \(K_N\) and contributes the factor
\(\mu_L^{-1/2}\).

It remains only to compare the stationary fixed window with the complete-cycle
sum.  The part before the first observed click and the part after the last
complete cycle are bounded by a constant times the lengths of the two boundary
cycles.  Under the stationary renewal law these lengths are dominated by the
size-biased Palm cycle length, which has an exponential tail by
\eqref{eq:exp-tail-main}.  Hence the boundary contribution is
\(o_{\PP}(\sqrt N)\), proving the CLT.

The batch statement is the scalar \(d=1\) case of
\Cref{lem:separated-batch-qdependent-main}.  Its hypotheses hold because the
cycle rewards are stationary, \(q\)-dependent, mean zero, and have the displayed
linear growth in exponentially-tailed cycle lengths.  The lemma proves the
needed fourth-moment triangular-array bound for the independent separated
batches, transfers the deterministic-index sample-variance law to the random
all-complete-cycle count \(M_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}\), deletes the stationary
initial and terminal incomplete cycles and the terminal unbatched remainder,
and gives the stated plug-in stability under the displayed perturbation
condition.  Its scalar covariance \(\Gamma_Y\) is exactly
\(\tau_q^2=\Var(Y_1)+2\sum_{h=1}^q\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_{1+h})\), and its
normalizing factor \(\bar L_{N,q}^{\mathrm{all}}\) converges to \(\mu_L\).

For the lag-two specialization, expand the empirical covariance and compare
the denominators \(N\) and \(N-2\):
\[
\widehat\gamma_2-\gamma_2
=
\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Z_n+O_{\PP}(N^{-1}).
\]
The possible lag-two success within a click-to-click cycle is either \(101\)
inside a gap of length one or the across-click \(111\) event produced by two
adjacent zero-length gaps.  This gives the displayed formula for \(f(g,h)\).
Writing \(Y_1=f(G,H)\) and \(Y_2=f(H,H')\), direct expansion yields
\[
\EE Y_1^2
=
\alpha^2m_2+2\alpha\beta m_1+\beta^2
+(2\alpha+2\beta+1)\pi_1+(2\beta+1)\pi_0^2,
\]
\[
\EE(Y_1Y_2)=\beta\pi_0^2+\pi_0^3,\qquad \EE Y_1=0.
\]
Thus \(\tau_1^2=\EE Y_1^2+2\EE(Y_1Y_2)\), which is
\eqref{eq:tau2-explicit-main}.  The \(q=1\) batch formula is
\eqref{eq:cycle-block-est-all-main}; positivity plus Slutsky's theorem gives
the studentized limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Positivity of the sampled-law lag-two cycle variance]\label{lem:boundary-reset-tau-positive-main}
For the stationary sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset record with
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\), the explicit cycle variance in
\eqref{eq:tau2-boundary-reset-main} is strictly positive:
\[
\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2>0 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(G_1,G_2,\ldots\) be the i.i.d.\ post-click zero-run lengths from
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}.  The explicit zero-run law has full support:
\(\PP(G=g)>0\) for every \(g\ge0\).  In the proof of
\Cref{thm:cycle-block-main}, the centered lag-two cycle reward has the form
\[
Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1}),\qquad
f(g,h)=c(g)+\mathbf 1_{\{g=0,h=0\}},
\]
where \(c(g)=\alpha g+\beta+\mathbf 1_{\{g=1\}}\), and
\[
\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2=\Var(Y_1)+2\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_2).
\]
For such a stationary \(1\)-dependent sequence, zero long-run variance forces
a coboundary.  To see this explicitly, take the Hoeffding decomposition of
\(f(G,H)\) for two independent gap variables \(G,H\):
\[
f(g,h)=A(g)+B(h)+R(g,h),
\]
where \(\EE A(G)=\EE B(G)=0\), \(\EE[R(G,h)]=0\) for every \(h\), and
\(\EE[R(g,H)]=0\) for every \(g\).  The mean term is absent because
\(\EE Y_j=0\).  For \(S_n=\sum_{j=1}^n f(G_j,G_{j+1})\),
\[
S_n=A(G_1)+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\bigl(A+B\bigr)(G_j)+B(G_{n+1})
     +\sum_{j=1}^{n} R(G_j,G_{j+1}).
\]
The two sums on the right are orthogonal to each other and to the boundary
terms, and the degenerate variables \(R(G_j,G_{j+1})\) are themselves
uncorrelated at distinct lags.  Hence
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\Var(S_n)
=\Var\bigl((A+B)(G)\bigr)+\EE R(G,H)^2 .
\]
Thus \(\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2=0\) implies \(R=0\) and \(A+B=0\) almost surely.
On the full support this is exactly the telescoping form
\[
f(g,h)=u(h)-u(g)
\]
with \(u=B=-A\).

This alternative is impossible here.  The pairs \((0,0)\) and \((0,1)\) both
have positive probability, and the displayed formula for \(f\) gives
\[
f(0,0)=\beta+1,\qquad f(0,1)=\beta .
\]
A coboundary representation would therefore imply
\[
0=f(0,0)=\beta+1,\qquad u(1)-u(0)=f(0,1)=\beta=-1 .
\]
On the other hand, for every \(g\ge2\) and every \(h\ge0\),
\[
f(g,h)=\alpha g+\beta
\]
is independent of \(h\).  The same representation \(f(g,h)=u(h)-u(g)\) would
make \(u(h)-u(g)\) independent of \(h\), so \(u\) is constant on
\(\mathbb N_0\).  This contradicts \(u(1)-u(0)=-1\).  Therefore the coboundary
case is excluded, and \(\tau_{2,\mathrm{br}}^2>0\).
\end{proof}
