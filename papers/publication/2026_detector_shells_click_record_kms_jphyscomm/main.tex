\begin{abstract}
This paper gives a closed-form analysis of one stipulated constrained
D-MAP(2) subfamily.  The input is a pair of labelled stochastic kernels
\((T_0,T_1)\) with a triangular killed-leakage form, parametrized by positive
numbers \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\).  These parameters are
coordinates for the discrete-time probability model fixed in the introduction.
The ideal one-step blanking law is kept only as the standard Parry benchmark
\(\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi\), not as the main object.

For this constrained D-MAP law, the visible record is a full-support
stationary renewal process whose zero-run hazard is strictly increasing, whose
covariance has a single hidden-mode tail, and whose exact \(1\)-dependence
occurs precisely at the unique hidden-eigenvalue zero.
A stationary-renewal construction gives all finite marked-set and word
probabilities from renewal products and finite inclusion--exclusion.

The same record law has an explicit quotient inverse: three inclusion
probabilities recover the symmetric survival-factor coordinates and hence the
unordered survival-factor pair, while an ordered labelled rate pair is
available only after a separated-root branch label is supplied.
At the exchange diagonal the separated-root chart is singular, but the common
numerical rate remains an invariant visible-law estimand.

For fixed-setting retained-record asymptotics, a single complete-cycle
protocol fixes the batch length, thresholds, covariance estimator, projection
rule, and chart-retention rule before proving the inclusion-coordinate CLT, the
quotient-inverse delta method, and the projected Gaussian limit on the exchange
diagonal.  The diagonal confidence construction is pointwise in the true common
survival factor \(z\), with the reporting and studentization scope fixed in the
introduction.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} constrained D-MAP, renewal process, exact \(1\)-dependence, renewal inclusion products, finite-record diagnostics

\section{Introduction}

This paper is about a constrained two-state discrete-time Markovian-arrival
process (D-MAP) and the stationary binary renewal record it generates.  The
primitive object is the labelled kernel pair displayed in
\Cref{ass:constrained-dmap-record-main}: \(T_0+T_1\) is the hidden transition
matrix, \(T_1\) marks an observed \(1\), and the killed entries
\(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\) are part of the stipulated triangular constraint.
The parameters \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) are a positive
parametrization of this D-MAP.

The paper-level contribution is therefore the closed-form record-law,
quotient-inverse, and fixed-setting retained-record asymptotic analysis of this
one constrained D-MAP(2) subfamily.  All main record, inverse, and
retained-record asymptotic claims are read under the following model contract.

\begin{definition}[Model contract: constrained two-state D-MAP record]\label{ass:constrained-dmap-record-main}
Fix \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\in(0,\infty)^3\) and define
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},
\]
\[
a=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\bigr)\,dr,\qquad
b=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr .
\]
The record model is the two-state D-MAP with hidden states ordered as
\((R,D)\) and labelled kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The convention is
\((T_i)_{XY}=\PP(A_n=i,H_{n+1}=Y\mid H_n=X)\), the visible process
\((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) is the stationary binary process generated by these
kernels, and formulas on \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) are interpreted by
continuous limits.  The killed entries \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\) are a structural
constraint of this D-MAP.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Model contract.}\label{par:model-contract-main}
Throughout Sections~\ref{sec:operational}--\ref{sec:discussion}, the
mathematical input is exactly the constrained D-MAP in
\Cref{ass:constrained-dmap-record-main}.  The labels \(R,D\), the coordinates
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), and the scale \(\Delta\tau\) name the two hidden
states and the survival-factor parametrization
\(p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau}\), \(s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\) of that
discrete law.  The sampled-counter construction below is an equivalent
finite-state probability realization of the same kernels, included only to
make the triangular killed-leakage transition rule transparent; it is not an
additional source of assumptions.

\begin{lemma}[Classical sampled-counter construction of the stipulated kernels]\label{lem:classical-counter-kernel-construction-main}
The kernels in \Cref{ass:constrained-dmap-record-main} are exactly the
one-step kernels of the following discrete sampled-counter protocol.  At the
start of a sampling interval of length \(\Delta\tau\), the hidden state is
either \(R\) or \(D\).  From \(R\), an independent attempt clock with rate
\(\Gamma\) either rings before the end of the interval, in which case the
visible output is \(1\) and the next state is \(D\), or it does not ring, in
which case the visible output is \(0\) and the next state remains \(R\).
From \(D\), an independent recovery clock with rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\)
either does not ring, giving output \(0\) and next state \(D\), or rings at
time \(r\in(0,\Delta\tau)\).  Conditional on recovery at \(r\), the same
attempt clock is run only during the remaining time \(\Delta\tau-r\): no
attempt gives output \(0\) and next state \(R\), while an attempt gives output
\(1\) and next state \(D\).  This protocol gives
\[
T_0(R,R)=p,\quad T_1(R,D)=1-p,\quad T_0(D,D)=s,
\]
\[
T_0(D,R)=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr=b,
\quad
T_1(D,D)=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\bigr)\,dr=a,
\]
and the two killed entries \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The \(R\)-row is the survival and ringing decomposition of an exponential
clock over one interval.  For the \(D\)-row, no recovery before
\(\Delta\tau\) has probability \(s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\) and
contributes to \(T_0(D,D)\).  If recovery occurs at \(r\), its density is
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}\,dr\).  Survival of the attempt
clock for the remaining time \(\Delta\tau-r\) gives the \(T_0(D,R)\) integral,
and its complement gives the \(T_1(D,D)\) integral.  The construction never
allows a marked transition landing in \(R\), which gives the two killed
entries.  These mutually exclusive cases exhaust each row, since
\(s+a+b=1\) and \(p+(1-p)=1\).
\end{proof}

The main text below is organized around three theorem families for this
constrained D-MAP(2) subfamily: the record law, the quotient inverse, and
fixed-setting retained-record asymptotics.  Supporting
lemmas, propositions, and corollaries supply the renewal bookkeeping, generic
cycle-reward CLTs, separated-batch covariance estimates, and projected-Gaussian
boundary calculations needed by those three results.  The theorem-by-theorem
comparison in \Cref{sec:theorem-led-comparison} then separates these

\subsection{Scope of claims}\label{subsec:scope-nonclaims}\label{rem:scope-nonclaims-main}

The record, inverse, and retained-record asymptotic theorems are conditional
statements about the discrete D-MAP law in
\Cref{ass:constrained-dmap-record-main}.  They do not derive
\((T_0,T_1)\) from detector dynamics, KMS detailed balance, thermodynamics, or
a continuous-time measurement model.  The optional external calibration in
Appendix~A only composes an externally supplied scalar response profile with
the already fixed D-MAP coordinate \(\Gamma\); it does not identify the record
kernels from response level sets.  The ideal Parry point is a benchmark
comparison, not the main model.

\paragraph{Inference scope.}\label{par:inference-scope-main}
All inferential statements in the main text are fixed-setting retained-record
asymptotic statements for the stationary D-MAP law in
\Cref{ass:constrained-dmap-record-main}.  The visible law identifies the
symmetric survival coordinates \((p+s,ps)\), the unordered root/rate set, and
visible functionals such as \(\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)\).
Separated ordered labelled rates are reported only under the branch-map
convention of \Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}; without that branch
input, the protocol reports only quotient or unordered objects.  Covariance and
confidence procedures are reported only on their retained-record events, with
``insufficient information'' reported otherwise.  The only fully studentized
scalar interval proved here is the lag-two covariance interval; other
diagnostics are population formulas unless a separate finite-record theorem is
stated.

\subsection{Main theorem map}\label{subsec:main-theorem-boundary}\label{subsec:model-validity-boundary}

Under the constrained D-MAP record model of
\Cref{ass:constrained-dmap-record-main}, the visible binary process is a
stationary renewal process with gap law \(g_k\), increasing hazard \(h_k\),
covariance
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1,
\]
and exact \(1\)-dependence if and only if
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), equivalently
\(\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\).  This is
Main Theorem~1 and the sole theorem-level statement whose conclusion is the
stationary record law itself.  Main Theorem~2 is the quotient inverse from
visible inclusion probabilities to the exchange quotient
\((p+s,ps)\).  Main Theorem~3 is split into three fixed-setting
retained-record asymptotic statements: Main Theorem~3A gives the complete-cycle inclusion-coordinate CLT
and covariance estimator, Main Theorem~3B gives the quotient inverse delta
method for \((p+s,ps)\) and the unordered root/rate set, and Main Theorem~3C
gives the exchange-diagonal projected limit and pushes the diagonal set forward
to the common numerical rate.  The remaining renewal, covariance, batching,
projection, and estimand statements are supporting lemmas, propositions, or
corollaries of \(T_0,T_1\) and the associated stationary hidden Markov law.

The three main theorems and their supporting result groups are listed in
\Cref{tab:four-main-blocks}.  Lemmas, propositions, corollaries, and proof
devices are subordinated to one of these groups.  In particular, the generic
cycle-reward and separated-batch lemmas support the fixed-setting
retained-record asymptotic theorem rather than forming additional paper-level
theorems.

\section{D-MAP setting and record-level observables}\label{sec:operational}

We first consider only the stationary D-MAP record law of
\Cref{ass:constrained-dmap-record-main}.  The hidden chain has two states, the
output alphabet is \(\{0,1\}\), and the visible output is the stationary
two-sided binary process
\[
(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z},
\]
where \(A_n=1\) denotes a marked transition of the D-MAP in the \(n\)th
discrete step.  All Palm, covariance, renewal, and independence
statements below refer to this two-sided stationary law.  A finite observed
record of length \(N\) is only the restriction
\[
A^{(N)}=(A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_{N-1}),
\]
of the stationary process.  The only nonstationary finite-start convention
used below is the post-burn-in retained-length convention: generate
\(L_N+N\) intervals from an arbitrary hidden initial law, discard the first
\(L_N\), and reindex the retained word as
\((A_{L_N},\ldots,A_{L_N+N-1})\), so \(N\) is always the retained sample size.
Compressing such a finite record to the empirical click
frequency recovers only the one-point observable
\[
\widehat\rho_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n.
\]
The full record, however, carries more. In particular, one may ask:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item what words belong to the support of the stationary law;
\item how the zero-run lengths between clicks are distributed;
\item whether the click hazard after a long run of zeros remembers the run length;
\item how the low-lag covariance sequence behaves;
\item what counting noise remains after coarse time averaging.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{definition}[Record benchmarks]\label{def:record-benchmarks-main}
For a stationary binary record $(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$, a \emph{zero-run length} is the number $G$ of consecutive zeros following a click and preceding the next click. If the zero-run law has full support, its hazard is
\[
h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k),\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
The ideal record is on the \emph{Parry benchmark} when its stationary law is the Parry measure on the golden-mean shift, i.e. the unique measure of maximal entropy subject to the grammar constraint $11\notin\operatorname{supp}\mu$. A stationary record is \emph{exact $1$-dependent} when, for every $m$, the sigma algebras generated by $\{A_j:j\le m\}$ and $\{A_j:j\ge m+2\}$ are independent.
Equivalently, one leaves the intermediate sample at time $m+1$ unobserved: a block ending at time $m$ is tested for independence from a block beginning at time $m+2$, not conditioned on the middle sample.
\end{definition}

These observables are not themselves one-point rate observables, yet in the
explicit D-MAP family above they reduce to sharp thresholds in the coordinate
\(\Gamma\).

It is useful to keep the record construction in two logically separate steps.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item A binary law determines a stationary word law and a record-level benchmark: the Parry law in the ideal blanking comparison, exact \(1\)-dependence in the constrained D-MAP.
\item For these two discrete record laws, that benchmark is first equivalent to a scalar coordinate condition, respectively \(\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi\) and \(\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\).  The latter condition is labelled according to the inference-scope convention in \hyperref[par:inference-scope-main]{the inference-scope paragraph}: the inverse theorem gives
\[
\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}
\quad\text{and hence only}\quad
\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}.
\]
so without a supplied branch label the statement is made on the quotient by
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\sim(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)\).
\end{enumerate}
The two discrete binary laws have intrinsically different record thresholds.
The ideal model imposes a hard symbolic grammar, so its distinguished value is
the entropy benchmark on the golden-mean shift.  The constrained
D-MAP instead produces a full-support renewal law, so its distinguished value
is the exact \(1\)-dependence threshold where the hidden eigenmode vanishes.

\begin{definition}[Constrained D-MAP law used by the main theorems]\label{def:main-sampled-kernel-law}
Let \(\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau}\) denote the stationary
visible binary law generated by the kernel pair in
\Cref{ass:constrained-dmap-record-main}.  Equivalently, that pair is
\[
\mathcal K(p,s)=
\left(
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\quad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&1-s-b\end{pmatrix}
\right),
\]
where
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},\qquad
b=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}
\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr .
\]
Equivalently \(a=1-s-b\) is the lower-right one-labelled entry.  This is the
kernel pair fixed by the model contract.
\end{definition}
\par\medskip

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem 1: record law]\label{thm:main-record-wrapper}
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) have law
\(\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau}\) from
\Cref{def:main-sampled-kernel-law}.  Let \(G\) be the
number of zeros between consecutive ones, and put
\[
\rho=\PP(A_0=1),\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b .
\]
Then \((A_n)\) is a full-support renewal process with Palm gap law
\[
g_k=\PP(G=k)=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1,\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
If \(\Gamma\ne\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), then
\[
g_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-s)s^k}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
while on the equal-rate diagonal the formula is interpreted by the continuous
limit, equivalently for
\(y=\Gamma\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\) and \(p=e^{-y}\),
\[
g_k=p^k\bigl((1-p)(1+yk)-yp\bigr).
\]
The zero-run hazard \(h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k)\) is strictly increasing.  For
every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\gamma_n:=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)
=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}.
\]
The record is exact \(1\)-dependent iff \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), and in no
positive parameter case is it a finite-order Markov chain.
\end{theorem}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The contribution is the closed-form analysis of one constrained D-MAP(2)
subfamily.  For the stipulated triangular killed-leakage labelled kernels,
the paper proves that the visible record is a full-support renewal process
with strictly increasing hazard, suppressed adjacent-one conditional
probability, classical sub-Poisson count noise, a unique exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold at the zero of the hidden eigenmode, renewal
inclusion products and finite inclusion--exclusion word laws, a closed Fano
law, and a three-inclusion inverse with
\(D=r_1-r_0^2\ne0\), \(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) exchange
duality, no separated labelled-rate chart at \(p=s\), and an identifiable
diagonal common rate there.  These conclusions use the scope conventions in
\Cref{subsec:scope-nonclaims}.  The ideal Parry point remains the benchmark
comparison rather than the main theorem.

The comparison in \Cref{tab:theorem-comparison} separates direct theorem
comparators from strict 2021-07-04--2026-07-04 neighbours and from broader
partially observed record, coarse-graining, and memory-inference motivation.
