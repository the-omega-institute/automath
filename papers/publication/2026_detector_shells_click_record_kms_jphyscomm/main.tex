\begin{abstract}
We study a classical sampled counter whose visible binary law is stipulated at
the readout level. Its internal attempt rate is a parameter \(\Gamma\). Only
after the record law has been proved may one impose a separate calibration
closure identifying this parameter with a supplied stationary excitation-rate
profile, \(\Gamma=R_\Omega(x)=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)\), under the formal Model~A
closure and named hypothesis package in the response theorem. No UDW measurement model is
derived: the theorem-level record law is the law of a stipulated classical
sampled hidden Markov model and its visible output, not a consequence of KMS
thermality or of general Unruh--DeWitt detector theory. The main contribution
is the exact analysis of the sampled boundary-reset record selector, with the
ideal dead-time Parry point used only as a standard benchmark comparison. With
ideal one-step dead time, the record is the stipulated golden-mean Markov chain
and the standard Parry calculation gives
\(\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi\). With a sampled
effective recovery model, namely the sampled one-recovery-opportunity
boundary-reset rule, the zero-run law has full support on \(\mathbb N_0\), the
zero-run hazard is strictly increasing, and the visible record becomes exact
\(1\)-dependent exactly at the unique rate where the hidden eigenvalue
vanishes. The full sampled boundary-reset click set has an explicit
Toeplitz principal-minor finite-inclusion formula for all parameters. Its
kernel is non-Hermitian, and the formula is only an algebraic family of
finite principal-minor identities: it does not give a Hermitian
positive-contraction kernel, negative association, or a fermionic
interpretation. At \(\Gamma_c\) the Toeplitz transfer parameter vanishes,
giving exact \(1\)-dependence without finite visible Markov order. The
Toeplitz record law
also has an exact inverse: three inclusion probabilities recover the unordered
attempt/recovery rate pair, revealing a
\(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) exchange duality and a
labelled-rate non-identifiability from one visible record. Relative to the
continuous intra-bin recovery comparator, the click kernel differs by a
post-click return-to-ready leakage of order
\(\frac12\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}(\Delta\tau)^2\) from an initially ready bin
and order \(\frac16\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}^2(\Delta\tau)^3\) from an initially
refractory bin. The same sampled HMM therefore gives a full-support renewal
process with a dependence-transition selector rather than an entropy
benchmark.

Only after those classical thresholds have been proved do we impose conditional
UDW-rate calibration. The main article keeps one formal bridge: the response
theorem names the Model~A closure
\(\Gamma=R_\Omega(x)=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)\), the fixed stationary response
profile, the range check, and the generic regularity check as explicit
hypotheses before the two record-selected rates become response loci of that
fixed profile. The threshold-specific homogeneous-shell, near-horizon,
fixed-radius, ratio-inverse, and
Schwarzschild extensions are not used in the present theorem chain; they
require separate response-model or external-data hypotheses. For such a fixed calibrated response function
\[
R_\Omega(x)=\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\widehat W_x(\Omega)
=
\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\frac{\mathcal S_x(\Omega)}{e^{\beta_\infty N(x)\Omega}-1},
\]
and write the two classical targets as the response loci
\[
R_\Omega^{-1}(\gamma_\varphi),
\qquad
R_\Omega^{-1}(\gamma_{\mathrm{mem}}).
\]
They are called shells only after the target lies in the range of $R_\Omega$
and $dR_\Omega\neq 0$ on the relevant level set. KMS detailed balance fixes
only the thermal ratio $\Gamma_-/\Gamma_+$; it does not by itself produce the
loci, shells, or inverse map.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} sampled counter, hidden Markov model, Toeplitz principal-minor formula, conditional UDW-rate calibration, KMS response

\section{Introduction}

This paper is about the record law of a classical sampled counter. The counter
has an internal attempt rate \(\Gamma\), one hidden readiness bit, and a binary
output recording whether the sampling interval contains a click. Its visible
process has grammar, waiting-time, dependence, covariance, and counting-noise
observables that are invisible in a rate-only summary. Transition rates,
detailed balance, and Tolman redshift remain the standard observables used to
characterize Unruh and Hawking thermality
\cite{Tolman1930,TolmanEhrenfest1930,Unruh1976,DeWitt1979,BirDav1982,Takagi1986,CrispinoHiguchiMatsas2008,LoukoSatz2006,LoukoSatz2008},
but here they enter only after the record theorem has been proved. The
paper-level object is a \emph{classical sampled counter with conditional
UDW-rate calibration}: one specific classical sampled interval instrument whose
attempt rate may later be identified with a supplied stationary UDW excitation
profile only through the explicit Model~A closure
\(\Gamma=R_\Omega(x)=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)\). This naming fixes the logical
order. The sampled counter is specified first; the response theorem then names
the closure, calibrated rate profile, target-range checks, and regularity
checks as explicit hypotheses; and no claim is made that general UDW
measurement theory derives the sampled instrument or its binary HMM law.

The answer has two layers. The first layer is record theory, with the new
mathematics concentrated in the sampled boundary-reset law. In the ideal
one-step dead-time mode the record is the stipulated golden-mean Markov chain,
and the standard Parry calculation gives the benchmark clock value
$\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$. In the sampled one-recovery-opportunity
boundary-reset mode the visible process is instead a full-support renewal law.
Its zero-run hazard is strictly increasing, every finite binary word remains
admissible, and the visible record becomes exact $1$-dependent exactly when
the hidden eigenvalue vanishes, at the unique rate
$\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$. The full sampled boundary-reset
click set has an explicit non-Hermitian Toeplitz principal-minor
finite-inclusion formula for all parameters; this is only a finite-dimensional
determinant identity, not a Hermitian positive-contraction DPP theorem. At
$\Gamma_c$ its transfer parameter vanishes, giving exact $1$-dependence
without finite visible Markov order. The Toeplitz record law also has an exact
inverse: three inclusion probabilities recover the unordered attempt/recovery
rate pair, revealing a $\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}$ exchange
duality and a labelled-rate non-identifiability from one visible record. The
memory threshold is therefore not an entropy benchmark but an exact dependence
transition inside a renewal family that still supports the full binary
grammar.

After these two record thresholds are fixed, Section~4 keeps only the short
fixed-response theorem for the calibrated stationary response profile
\[
R_\Omega(x)=\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\frac{\mathcal S_x(\Omega)}{e^{\beta_\infty N(x)\Omega}-1}.
\]
For one calibrated response function, the ideal and memory target equations
define two response loci, and the theorem explains that they are called shells
only after the range check and the regularity condition \(dR_\Omega\neq 0\) are
verified. The longer homogeneous quotient, near-horizon,
fixed-radius inverse/sensitivity, and admissibility statements are not part of
the main article; they are split-candidate conditional response-model algebra
requiring their own stable-system, external-data, and comparison assumptions,
rather than new detector-dynamics theorems in this paper.
The finite-record theorem on the record side estimates only the fixed-setting
covariance $\gamma_2=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)$ and its variance; for the
sampled boundary-reset law, a coboundary argument proves the cycle variance is
strictly positive for all positive parameters, so the fixed-setting
studentized Wald interval is not conditional on a numerical positivity check.
Any shell-location or ratio-coordinate asymptotic requires separate scan or
external ratio-data input beyond that one-record CLT.

This problem sits near hidden-Markov process theory, finite-dependence asymptotics, trajectory memory inference, dead-time counting, stationary UDW response theory, detector regularization, and detector-based QFT measurement theory, but it does not make the same claims as any of those literatures. Recent \emph{J. Phys. A} trajectory-memory work estimates non-Markovianity directly from observed conformational dynamics \cite{VollmarEtAl2024Memory}; the present record-side increment is instead the exact critical-rate criterion, renewal-cycle lag-two covariance CLT, and variance formula for one sampled boundary-reset HMM. Recent stochastic-trajectory work studies waiting-time and Zeno behavior for continuously monitored quantum systems \cite{WallsEtAl2024StochasticTrajectoriesZeno}; here the spectrum and Fano formulas are closed consequences of the one-mode covariance tail of a classical sampled renewal counter. Recent \emph{J. Phys. A} DPP work treats spatial DPP ensembles and quantum sampling algorithms \cite{MatsuiKatoriShirai2021HeisenbergDPP,BardenetFanuelFeller2024SamplingDPP}; foundational DPP references include the discrete and Fredholm frameworks of Macchi, Shirai--Takahashi, Soshnikov, Lyons, Borodin, and Hough--Krishnapur--Peres--Virag \cite{Macchi1975,ShiraiTakahashi2003,Lyons2003,Borodin2009DPP,Soshnikov2000,HoughKrishnapurPeresVirag2009}. The determinant formula here is narrower: it is a non-Hermitian Toeplitz principal-minor finite-inclusion formula for the click set of this one sampled record, with no Hermitian positive-contraction, negative-association, or fermionic consequence claimed. Likewise, recent \emph{J. Phys. A} UDW and AQFT/UDW papers clarify the joint interacting Hilbert-space status of the UDW model and field-mediated communication channels \cite{TjoaGray2024UDWJointHilbert,KasprzakTjoa2025Transmission}; the response statement here is only conditional level-set algebra after a calibrated response profile has been fixed. The stochastic comparison classes matter for the sampled-record theorems; the detector and AQFT comparison classes matter only as boundary references for the conditional response-locus interpretation. \Cref{tab:main-jpa-comparison} gives the resulting theorem-by-theorem comparison in the main text.

Two additional related-work boundaries are important enough to state in the main text. On the output-statistics side, Glauber's coherence theory, Mandel--Wolf's photon-counting framework, Srinivas--Davies continuous photodetection, and the later trajectory/filtering treatments of Carmichael, Wiseman--Milburn, and Barchielli--Gregoratti already explain how waiting-time laws, coincidence rates, and output spectra arise from microscopic quantum measurement models \cite{Glauber1963,MandelWolf1995,SrinivasDavies1981,Carmichael1993Book,WisemanMilburn2009,BarchielliGregoratti2009}. Trajectory-memory inference gives another adjacent comparison class \cite{VollmarEtAl2024Memory}: it motivates estimating memory from records, but it does not supply the renewal-cycle lag-two covariance CLT used here for $\widehat\gamma_2$. These references delimit the record-side claims made here: this paper does not derive the sampled binary law from a microscopic quantum-optical or quantum-filtering model, and it does not prove a general theorem for continuously monitored quantum outputs. It fixes one classical sampled interval instrument and then proves exact theorems for its visible record. On the QFT-measurement side, the response-locus statement remains narrower than the general measurement frameworks developed in AQFT and localized-detector work \cite{Sorkin1993,FewsterVerch2020,Fewster2020,PoloGomezEtAl2022,PercheLocalized2023,TjoaGray2024UDWJointHilbert,PranziniKeskiVakkuri2025AQFT,KasprzakTjoa2025Transmission,Oeckl2025CausalMeasurement}: it is conditional level-set algebra inside one fixed calibrated response model, not a new theorem about general detector back-action, interacting Hilbert spaces, field-mediated communication, or locality.

Dead time and recovery are also physically natural. In nuclear and photon counting, refractory effects have been central since the earliest pulse detectors and remain standard in modern single-photon models \cite{Muller1973,CantorTeich1975,Hadfield2009,EisamanEtAl2011}. The observables used here---grammar, hazard, dependence class, covariance tail, and low-frequency noise---therefore belong to the standard counting-statistics fingerprint of a sampled detector output. Their role is not to replace the local rate, but to show that keeping the full record produces exact thresholds that can later be lifted to response-model level sets.

The paper is organized accordingly. \Cref{sec:operational} and
\Cref{sec:records} define the record-level observables, record the standard
exact-clock Parry benchmark for the ideal counter, and carry the main
sampled-boundary-reset theorem chain for the memory-transition threshold and
its operational signatures.
\Cref{sec:kms-shells} then gives the short fixed-response theorem for one
stationary response function, with the Model~A closure, target-range check, and
regularity check named in the theorem statement. Longer abstract calibrated
toy-model homogeneous, near-horizon, fixed-radius, Schwarzschild,
external-ratio, and residual-graph developments are deliberately kept outside
the submitted theorem chain.

calculations, Tables~\ref{tab:notation-record}--\ref{tab:notation-response}
split the recurring notation into record and response layers for
Sections~\ref{sec:records} and~\ref{sec:kms-shells}.

\section{Classical sampled-counter setting and record-level observables}\label{sec:operational}

We first consider only the sampled readout law. Proper time is divided into
intervals of length $\Delta\tau$, a classical counter carries a hidden readiness
state, and the visible output is a stationary binary process
\[
A_0,A_1,A_2,\dots \in \{0,1\},
\]
where $A_n=1$ denotes a click in the $n$th sampling interval. The word
``detector'' in Sections~\ref{sec:operational}--\ref{sec:records} refers to
this classical readout mode unless the later calibration closure
$\Gamma=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)$ is explicitly invoked. Compressing the record to the
empirical click frequency recovers only the one-point observable
\[
\widehat\rho_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n.
\]
The full record, however, carries more. In particular, one may ask:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item what words belong to the support of the stationary law;
\item how the zero-run lengths between clicks are distributed;
\item whether the click hazard after a long run of zeros remembers the run length;
\item how the low-lag covariance profile behaves;
\item what counting noise remains after coarse time averaging.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{definition}[Record benchmarks]\label{def:record-benchmarks-main}
For a stationary binary record $(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$, a \emph{zero-run length} is the number $G$ of consecutive zeros following a click and preceding the next click. If the zero-run law has full support, its hazard is
\[
h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k),\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
The ideal record is on the \emph{Parry benchmark} when its stationary law is the Parry measure on the golden-mean shift, i.e. the unique measure of maximal entropy subject to the grammar constraint $11\notin\operatorname{supp}\mu$. A stationary record is \emph{exact $1$-dependent} when, for every $m$, the sigma algebras generated by $\{A_j:j\le m\}$ and $\{A_j:j\ge m+2\}$ are independent.
Equivalently, one leaves the intermediate sample at time $m+1$ unobserved: a block ending at time $m$ is tested for independence from a block beginning at time $m+2$, not conditioned on the middle sample.
\end{definition}

The homogeneous spectral-weight and common-core shell conventions are not part
of the main record theorem chain. They belong only to supplementary calibrated
toy-model algebra. The main text uses only the two record benchmarks in
\Cref{def:record-benchmarks-main} and the calibrated-response theorem of
Section~\ref{sec:kms-shells}.

These observables are not themselves rate observables, yet in the explicit
readout models below they reduce to sharp thresholds in the internal counter
rate $\Gamma$. That reduction is important: response geometry enters only after
the stochastic-record benchmark has already fixed the target rate and the
separate calibration closure has been imposed.

We also separate the field-facing detector core from the classical counter mode. The core consists of the gap $\Omega$, coupling scale $\lambda_c$, detector profile, field state, and stationary excitation rate supplied by the UDW response. The counter mode is the readout layer: ideal one-step blanking for the Parry selector, or sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary reset at rate $\kappa_{\mathrm r}$ for the memory selector. The two selectors are therefore obtained from two operating modes of the same field-facing core, not from a single record that is simultaneously ideal and sampled-boundary-reset. The shell-pair cancellation results require this shared core and a common response amplitude at the two shell points; they do not identify the ideal and sampled boundary-reset records as the same stochastic law.

It is useful to keep the construction in three logically separate steps.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item A counter model determines a stationary word law and a record-level benchmark: the Parry law in the ideal case, exact $1$-dependence in the sampled boundary-reset case.
\item For the two models studied here, that benchmark is first equivalent to a scalar internal counter-rate condition, respectively $\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$ and $\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$. Under the paper's calibration closure $\Gamma=\Gamma_+$, these become $\Gamma_+\Delta\tau=\log\varphi$ and $\Gamma_+=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)$.
\item Substitution of the specified static KMS response then turns those rate conditions into level-set equations for the detector response.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the shells are not claimed to be new detector-independent geometric invariants, nor are they derived from static KMS detailed balance alone. They are response-model level sets whose constants are selected by full-record statistics. This ordering of the argument is important: detailed balance supplies the thermal rate ratio, the readout law supplies the record benchmark, the positive spectral weight supplies the remaining one-gap response datum, and only their combination supplies the shell equation.

The two detector regimes studied in this work are illustrated schematically in \cref{fig:scheme}. The ideal model imposes a hard symbolic grammar, while the sampled one-recovery-opportunity boundary-reset model produces a renewal law whose zero-run distribution has full support and hence allows every finite binary word. The thresholds associated with the two regimes are therefore intrinsically different: one is entropic and grammar-based, the other is correlation-based.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The main mathematical contribution is the sampled boundary-reset recovery theorem: one explicit sampled counter law produces a full-support renewal record with strictly increasing hazard, universal antibunching, and a unique exact-$1$-dependence threshold at the zero of the hidden eigenmode, while the full sampled boundary-reset click set has an explicit non-Hermitian Toeplitz principal-minor finite-inclusion formula for all parameters. The determinant formula is not a Hermitian DPP construction and does not imply positive-contraction, negative-association, or fermionic structure. At $\Gamma_c$ its transfer parameter vanishes, giving exact $1$-dependence without finite visible Markov order. The ideal Parry point remains the benchmark comparison rather than the main theorem. Two operational signatures sharpen that distinction: the sampled law approaches the ideal grammar only singularly, with consecutive-click violations $O((\Delta\tau)^2)$ on fixed windows, and it remains strictly sub-Poissonian across the parameter range; for every finite window, the count law is a finite principal-minor determinant of the Toeplitz kernel. At the paper level, this record law is carried into the response section only through conditional UDW-rate calibration: the record instrument is chosen first, and the only UDW input is the Model~A closure $\Gamma=R_\Omega(x)=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)$ after a fixed response profile has been supplied.

The response-side theorem chain is intentionally narrower. Once the sampled
instrument and the fixed stationary response profile have been chosen,
\Cref{thm:response-locus-main} gives the main fixed-response theorem: the
Model~A closure, fixed \(R_\Omega\), target-range check, and regularity check
are formal hypotheses, the two record-selected target rates define response
loci, and shell language is justified only after those checks. Longer quotient,
horizon, fixed-radius, external-ratio, and residual-graph statements are kept
out of the main article as split-candidate toy-model algebra with their own
homogeneous, small-shell, admissibility, Jacobian, and external ratio-data
assumptions. KMS detailed balance still fixes only the thermal ratio
$\Gamma_-/\Gamma_+$; the positive one-gap spectral weight and any later
inverse-data inputs remain calibration hypotheses inside the chosen response
model.

The novelty claim is therefore split on purpose. The genuinely new theorem-level content is the sampled-record process theory, while the response-locus statement describes what that record theory implies after one has fixed the closure $\Gamma=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)$ and a calibrated response profile. The main-text recent-\emph{J. Phys. A} comparison in \Cref{tab:main-jpa-comparison} records how the record-HMM, DPP, stochastic-trajectory, memory-inference, and UDW/AQFT detector theorem families differ from the paper's sampled-counter theorem chain.
In particular, the paper does not claim a new UDW response theorem, a new
general QFT measurement framework, or a new stationary-trajectory
classification. The high-novelty claim is the exact all-parameter sampled boundary-reset
Toeplitz principal-minor finite-inclusion formula, its critical kernel-collapse phase, its
three-inclusion inverse with \(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) exchange
duality, and the conditional response-model consequences built from it.
