\begin{abstract}
This paper gives a closed-form analysis of one constrained classical
sampled-counter D-MAP(2) subfamily.  The input is a classical sampled counter:
an attempt clock with rate \(\Gamma\), a recovery clock with rate
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), and a sampling interval \(\Delta\tau\).  Sampling the
counter with the boundary-reset convention derives a pair of labelled
stochastic kernels \((T_0,T_1)\) with triangular killed-leakage form.  The
ideal one-step blanking law is mentioned only as a short Parry comparator, not
as the main object.

For this constrained D-MAP law, the visible record is a full-support
stationary renewal process whose zero-run hazard is strictly increasing, whose
covariance has a single hidden-mode tail, and whose exact \(1\)-dependence
occurs precisely at the unique hidden-eigenvalue zero.
A stationary-renewal construction gives all finite marked-set and word
probabilities from renewal products and finite inclusion--exclusion.

The same record law has an explicit quotient inverse: three inclusion
probabilities recover the symmetric survival-factor coordinates and hence the
unordered survival-factor pair, while an ordered labelled rate pair is
available only after a separated-root branch label is supplied.
At the exchange diagonal the separated-root chart is singular, but the common
numerical rate remains an invariant visible-law estimand.

For fixed-setting retained-record asymptotics, a single complete-cycle
protocol fixes the batch length, thresholds, covariance estimator, projection
rule, and chart-retention rule before proving the inclusion-coordinate CLT, the
quotient-inverse delta method, and the projected Gaussian limit on the exchange
diagonal.  The submitted diagonal acceptance theorem is the fixed-candidate
test at \(z_0=1/2\), the only diagonal candidate with a printed branch
certificate in this manuscript.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} constrained D-MAP, renewal process, exact \(1\)-dependence, renewal inclusion products, finite-record diagnostics

\section{Introduction}

This paper is about a constrained two-state discrete-time Markovian-arrival
process (D-MAP) and the stationary binary renewal record it generates.  The
physical input is deliberately modest: a classical sampled counter with two
internal states, \(R\) for ready and \(D\) for dead/recovering.  During one
sampling interval of length \(\Delta\tau\), a ready counter attempts to click
with exponential rate \(\Gamma\), and a dead counter recovers with exponential
rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).  A click is recorded as \(A_n=1\), and the
boundary-reset convention assigns the post-click state at the next sample to
\(D\).  This construction derives the labelled kernel pair displayed in
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}: \(T_0+T_1\) is the hidden transition
matrix, \(T_1\) marks an observed \(1\), and the killed entries
\(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\) encode the rule that every recorded click leaves the
counter dead at the next boundary.  Thus
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) are not formal decoration; they are
the attempt rate, recovery rate, and sampling interval of this sampled-counter
realization of the D-MAP.

The paper-level contribution is therefore the closed-form record-law,
quotient-inverse, and fixed-setting retained-record asymptotic analysis of this
one constrained D-MAP(2) subfamily.  The following contract fixes the model used
by all paper-level theorem statements.

\begin{assumption}[Constrained sampled-counter D-MAP model]\label{ass:model-inference-contract-main}
Fix \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\in(0,\infty)^3\) and define
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},
\]
\[
a=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\bigr)\,dr,\qquad
b=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr .
\]
The record model is the two-state D-MAP with hidden states ordered as
\((R,D)\) and labelled kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The convention is
\((T_i)_{XY}=\PP(A_n=i,H_{n+1}=Y\mid H_n=X)\), the visible process
\((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) is the stationary binary process generated by these
kernels, and formulas on \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) are interpreted by
continuous limits.  The killed entries \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\) are a structural
constraint of this D-MAP.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Scope of finite-record reports}\label{subsec:finite-record-report-scope-main}

Finite-record confidence procedures are fixed-setting retained-record
asymptotics for the stationary law in
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}.  In every theorem that invokes this
scope convention, \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) is fixed at the
population point under discussion, \(\mathcal E_N\) denotes the named reporting
event for that procedure, and confidence sets are formed only on
\(\mathcal E_N\).  If \(\mathcal E_N\) fails, the corresponding finite-record
report is withheld.
The paper does not assert finite-sample coverage, uniform coverage over
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\), or model-selection-valid coverage across
reporting branches.

The only model interpretation assumed by this contract is the classical
sampled-counter protocol of
\Cref{lem:classical-counter-kernel-construction-main}.  If the sampling
interval \(\Delta\tau\) is known, the visible stationary
record identifies the unordered survival-factor set
\(\{p,s\}=\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}\) and
hence the unordered numerical rate set
\(\{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\}\).  It does not identify which member is the
attempt rate and which is the recovery rate without side information.  If
\(\Delta\tau\) is not treated as a known design constant, the visible law
identifies only the unordered products
\(\{\Gamma\Delta\tau,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\}\).

The submitted diagonal projected-Gaussian confidence statement is the
fixed-candidate statement at \(z_0=1/2\), using the printed branch certificate in
\Cref{lem:z-half-diagonal-branch-certificate-main}.  Other diagonal candidates
and continuum displays are diagnostics only unless a later implementation
extension supplies its own printed finite certificate and critical-value
transcript.  Ordered labelled rates require the separate branch-map convention of
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.  The paper does not claim
simultaneous inversion over \(0<z<1\), nor identification of UDW, KMS,
thermodynamic, response-profile, or ordered-rate quantities unless the
particular result supplies the extra hypotheses and proof.

\subsection{Visible-record diagnostics for the model contract}\label{subsec:visible-record-model-diagnostics-main}

\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main} and
\Cref{subsec:finite-record-report-scope-main} form a conditional contract, not a
model-selection theorem.  The confidence statements below are meaningful only
after the retained visible record is treated as coming from the killed-leakage
sampled-counter D-MAP with known \(\Delta\tau\).  The following diagnostics are
therefore part of the finite-record reporting protocol.  They use only the
observed binary record and are meant to falsify, not certify, the renewal
sample-counter assumption.

Let \(A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}\) be the retained record and let
\[
\widehat\tau_0<\widehat\tau_1<\cdots<\widehat\tau_{\widehat K}
\]
be its observed click times.  The complete observed inter-click gaps are
\[
\widehat G_j=\widehat\tau_{j+1}-\widehat\tau_j-1,\qquad
0\le j\le \widehat K-1 .
\]
Endpoint censored runs before \(\widehat\tau_0\) and after
\(\widehat\tau_{\widehat K}\) are not used as complete gap observations.  Any
burn-in deletion or all-complete-cycle deletion used later is applied before
forming these gaps.

\paragraph{Renewal-i.i.d. gap checks.}
Under the model, the complete gaps are consecutive observations from one
i.i.d. Palm gap law \(g\), up to the fixed endpoint deletions described in the
retained-record protocol.  Basic visible-record checks therefore include:
\[
\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}\{\varphi(\widehat G_j),\psi(\widehat G_{j+\ell})\}\approx0,
\qquad \ell\ge1,
\]
for low lags and simple bounded functions such as indicators
\(\mathbf1_{\{G=k\}}\), tail indicators \(\mathbf1_{\{G\ge k\}}\), and the
identity truncated to a stable finite range.  They also include comparisons of
early, middle, and late empirical gap distributions, empirical survival curves,
and empirical hazards
\[
\widehat h_k
=\frac{\#\{j:\widehat G_j=k\}}{\#\{j:\widehat G_j\ge k\}},
\]
on the range where the denominator is not small.  The fitted constrained
D-MAP gap law used for comparison is obtained only from the retained quotient
coordinates of \Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality} or, equivalently, the
projected renewal law in \Cref{def:projected-plugin-word-law-report-main};
one does not fit an unrelated renewal family and then reuse the confidence
theorems below.

\paragraph{Inclusion-product residuals.}
For a finite lag pattern \(B=\{0=i_1<i_2<\cdots<i_m\}\), the renewal law
implies
\[
\PP(B+t\subset\varXi)
=\rho\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}u_{i_{r+1}-i_r},
\]
where \(u_n=\PP(n\in\varXi\mid0\in\varXi)\).  From the observed record define
the sliding-window inclusion frequency
\[
\widehat p_N(B)
=\frac{1}{N-i_m}\sum_{t=0}^{N-i_m-1}
  \prod_{i\in B}A_{t+i},
\]
and let \(\widehat\rho_N\) and \(\widehat u_{n,N}\) be the corresponding
retained empirical or projected renewal estimates used in the finite-record
report.  The inclusion-product residual is
\[
\widehat R_N(B)
=\widehat p_N(B)
 -\widehat\rho_N\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}\widehat u_{i_{r+1}-i_r,N}.
\]
Systematic nonzero residuals over several small patterns \(B\) are direct
visible evidence against the renewal product identity of
\Cref{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}.  The three-inclusion inverse
uses only \(B=\{0\}\), \(\{0,1\}\), and \(\{0,2\}\), but the diagnostic should
also include longer patterns, since a non-renewal binary process can match the
first few inclusion probabilities while failing the product law for larger
sets.

\paragraph{Lag-covariance residuals.}
The killed-leakage sampled-counter law has the single-tail covariance formula
\[
\gamma_n=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)
=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1},\qquad n\ge1 .
\]
After the quotient fit has supplied
\((\widehat\rho_N,\widehat a_N,\widehat\lambda_N)\), compare the empirical
lag covariances
\[
\widehat\gamma_{n,N}
=\frac{1}{N-n}\sum_{t=0}^{N-n-1}
  (A_t-\widehat\rho_N)(A_{t+n}-\widehat\rho_N)
\]
with
\[
\widehat\gamma^{\,\mathrm{mod}}_{n,N}
=\widehat\rho_N(\widehat a_N-\widehat\rho_N)
  \,\widehat\lambda_N^{\,n-1}.
\]
Equivalently, inspect the residuals
\[
\widehat C_N(n)
=\widehat\gamma_{n,N}-\widehat\gamma^{\,\mathrm{mod}}_{n,N}
\]
for low and moderate lags \(n\).  A change of sign pattern, a second decay
rate, or residuals large compared with the separated-batch uncertainty scale
used later are visible-record failures of the single hidden-mode D-MAP
structure.  At the exact \(1\)-dependence threshold the same check specializes
to \(\widehat\gamma_{n,N}\approx0\) for \(n\ge2\), with the lag-two
studentized interval of \Cref{cor:finite-record-lag-two-studentized-main}
serving as the formal scalar version for \(n=2\).

These diagnostics do not prove that the sampled-counter model is true.  They
are falsification checks for the assumptions under which the later confidence
reports are interpreted.  If the complete-gap renewal-i.i.d. checks,
inclusion-product residuals, or lag-covariance residuals show a material and
unexplained failure, the model-conditioned confidence reports in
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main,thm:finite-record-quotient-delta-main,thm:e1-ideal-fixed-candidate-projection-main,thm:e2-reported-fixed-candidate-diagonal-main,thm:e3-canonical-dkw-monte-carlo-main}
are invalid for that record and must be withheld rather than reported with
their nominal conditional coverage.


\begin{lemma}[Classical sampled-counter derivation of the D-MAP kernels]\label{lem:classical-counter-kernel-construction-main}
The kernels in \Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main} are exactly the
one-step kernels of the following discrete sampled-counter protocol.  At the
start of a sampling interval of length \(\Delta\tau\), the hidden state is
either \(R\) or \(D\).  From \(R\), an independent attempt clock with rate
\(\Gamma\) either rings before the end of the interval, in which case the
visible output is \(1\) and the next state is \(D\), or it does not ring, in
which case the visible output is \(0\) and the next state remains \(R\).
From \(D\), an independent recovery clock with rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\)
either does not ring, giving output \(0\) and next state \(D\), or rings at
time \(r\in(0,\Delta\tau)\).  Conditional on recovery at \(r\), the same
attempt clock is run only during the remaining time \(\Delta\tau-r\): no
attempt gives output \(0\) and next state \(R\), while an attempt gives output
\(1\) and next state \(D\).  This protocol gives
\[
T_0(R,R)=p,\quad T_1(R,D)=1-p,\quad T_0(D,D)=s,
\]
\[
T_0(D,R)=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr=b,
\quad
T_1(D,D)=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\bigr)\,dr=a,
\]
and the two killed entries \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The \(R\)-row is the survival and ringing decomposition of an exponential
clock over one interval.  For the \(D\)-row, no recovery before
\(\Delta\tau\) has probability \(s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\) and
contributes to \(T_0(D,D)\).  If recovery occurs at \(r\), its density is
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}\,dr\).  Survival of the attempt
clock for the remaining time \(\Delta\tau-r\) gives the \(T_0(D,R)\) integral,
and its complement gives the \(T_1(D,D)\) integral.  The construction never
allows a marked transition landing in \(R\), which gives the two killed
entries.  These mutually exclusive cases exhaust each row, since
\(s+a+b=1\) and \(p+(1-p)=1\).
\end{proof}

\section{D-MAP setting and record-level observables}\label{sec:operational}

We first consider only the stationary D-MAP record law of
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}.  The hidden chain has two states, the
output alphabet is \(\{0,1\}\), and the visible output is the stationary
two-sided binary process
\[
(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z},
\]
where \(A_n=1\) denotes a marked transition of the D-MAP in the \(n\)th
discrete step.  All Palm, covariance, renewal, and independence
statements below refer to this two-sided stationary law.  A finite observed
record of length \(N\) is only the restriction
\[
A^{(N)}=(A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_{N-1}),
\]
of the stationary process.  The only nonstationary finite-start convention
used below is the post-burn-in retained-length convention: generate
\(L_N+N\) intervals from an arbitrary hidden initial law, discard the first
\(L_N\), and reindex the retained word as
\((A_{L_N},\ldots,A_{L_N+N-1})\), so \(N\) is always the retained sample size.
Compressing such a finite record to the empirical click
frequency recovers only the one-point observable
\[
\widehat\rho_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n.
\]
The full record, however, carries more. In particular, one may ask:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item what words belong to the support of the stationary law;
\item how the zero-run lengths between clicks are distributed;
\item whether the click hazard after a long run of zeros remembers the run length;
\item how the low-lag covariance sequence behaves;
\item what counting noise remains after coarse time averaging.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{definition}[Record observables]\label{def:record-benchmarks-main}
For a stationary binary record $(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$, a \emph{zero-run length} is the number $G$ of consecutive zeros following a click and preceding the next click. If the zero-run law has full support, its hazard is
\[
h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k),\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
A stationary record is \emph{exact $1$-dependent} when, for every $m$, the sigma algebras generated by $\{A_j:j\le m\}$ and $\{A_j:j\ge m+2\}$ are independent.
Equivalently, one leaves the intermediate sample at time $m+1$ unobserved: a block ending at time $m$ is tested for independence from a block beginning at time $m+2$, not conditioned on the middle sample.
\end{definition}

These observables are not themselves one-point rate observables, yet in the
explicit D-MAP family above they reduce to sharp thresholds in the coordinate
\(\Gamma\).

It is useful to keep the D-MAP record construction in two logically separate
steps.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item The labelled kernels determine the stationary word law, the zero-run
hazard, and the covariance sequence of the visible binary record.
\item Exact \(1\)-dependence for this D-MAP is first equivalent to the scalar
coordinate condition \(\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\).
That condition is labelled according to \Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}:
the inverse theorem gives
\[
\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}
\quad\text{and hence only}\quad
\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}.
\]
Thus, without a supplied branch label, the statement is made on the quotient by
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\sim(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)\).
\end{enumerate}
The constrained D-MAP produces a full-support renewal law.  Its distinguished
record threshold in the main theorem chain is therefore the exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold where the hidden eigenmode vanishes.

\begin{definition}[Constrained D-MAP law used by Theorems A--E]\label{def:main-sampled-kernel-law}
Let \(\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau}\) denote the stationary
visible binary law generated by the kernel pair in
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}.  Equivalently, that pair is
\[
\mathcal K(p,s)=
\left(
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\quad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&1-s-b\end{pmatrix}
\right),
\]
where
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},\qquad
b=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}
\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr .
\]
Equivalently \(a=1-s-b\) is the lower-right one-labelled entry.  This is the
kernel pair derived from the sampled-counter model and fixed by
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem A: constrained D-MAP record law]\label{thm:main-record-wrapper}
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) have law
\(\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau}\) from
\Cref{def:main-sampled-kernel-law}.  Let \(G\) be the
number of zeros between consecutive ones, and put
\[
\rho=\PP(A_0=1),\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b .
\]
Then \((A_n)\) is a full-support renewal process with Palm gap law
\[
g_k=\PP(G=k)=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1,\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
If \(\Gamma\ne\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), then
\[
g_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-s)s^k}
     {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
while on the equal-rate diagonal the formula is interpreted by the continuous
limit, equivalently for
\(y=\Gamma\Delta\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\) and \(p=e^{-y}\),
\[
g_k=p^k\bigl((1-p)(1+yk)-yp\bigr).
\]
Writing
\[
S_k=\PP(G\ge k),\qquad h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k)=g_k/S_k ,
\]
the survival tail, hazard, and hazard increment are explicit.  Off the
equal-rate diagonal,
\[
S_k=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad
h_k=
1-\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^{k+1}-\Gamma s^{k+1}}
        {\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k},
\]
and
\[
h_{k+1}-h_k
=
\frac{
  \Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}\,
  (p-s)^2\,p^k s^k
}{
  \bigl(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k\bigr)
  \bigl(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^{k+1}-\Gamma s^{k+1}\bigr)
}>0 .
\]
On the equal-rate diagonal,
\[
S_k=p^k(1+yk),\qquad
h_k=1-p-\frac{yp}{1+yk},
\qquad
h_{k+1}-h_k=
\frac{y^2p}{(1+yk)(1+y(k+1))}>0 .
\]
Thus the zero-run hazard is strictly increasing.  For every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\gamma_n:=\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)
=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}^{\,n-1}.
\]
The record is exact \(1\)-dependent iff \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=0\), and in no
positive parameter case is it a finite-order Markov chain.
\end{theorem}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The contribution is the closed-form analysis of one constrained classical
sampled-counter D-MAP(2) subfamily.  For the triangular killed-leakage labelled
kernels derived from the sampled-counter construction, the paper proves that
the visible record is a full-support renewal process
with strictly increasing hazard, suppressed adjacent-one conditional
probability, classical sub-Poisson count noise, a unique exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold at the zero of the hidden eigenmode, and a
three-inclusion inverse with
\(\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}=r_1-r_0^2\ne0\), \(\Gamma\leftrightarrow\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) exchange
duality, no separated labelled-rate chart at \(p=s\), and an identifiable
diagonal common rate there.  The associated Section~\ref{sec:records}
supporting formulas give the renewal inclusion products, finite
inclusion--exclusion word laws, finite-window count diagnostics, and closed
population Fano law for that same record, but these formulas are kept outside
the formal statement of Theorem~A.  These conclusions use the contract in
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}.  The physical parameters
identifiable from the visible record are only the unordered survival/rate
coordinates described there; response and thermodynamic parameters require external
calibration.  The short Parry-comparator appendix records the ideal Parry
comparison only as a comparator rather than a paper-level theorem.

The theorem-by-theorem comparison in \Cref{tab:theorem-comparison} positions
the five main results against recent 2021--2026 comparators where they are
available, while separating older papers as foundational antecedents.  For
each result it records the overlapping object and the precise increment
specific to the constrained sampled-counter D-MAP record.

or singular point and, in the physics-facing rows, inference from incomplete
visible transition information.  In this paper the set is the exchange-diagonal

\section{Literature Search Protocol for \texorpdfstring{\Cref{sec:theorem-led-comparison}}{Section 5}}\label{app:literature-search-protocol-main}

This appendix makes the comparison protocol behind
\Cref{tab:theorem-comparison} reproducible.  It is not a priority claim over
the broad renewal, MAP, HMM, projection, or boundary-inference literatures.
It records the narrower rule used in the table: a recent paper enters the
second column only when it contains a theorem, proposition, lemma, or
corollary for the same mathematical object as the corresponding row, or for
the exact limiting step imported by that row.

\paragraph{Databases and fields.}
The frozen search used for \Cref{tab:theorem-comparison} was run on
2026-07-04 in arXiv, Google Scholar, MathSciNet, zbMATH Open, Scopus, and Web
of Science.  For arXiv the search field was all metadata plus abstract.  For
Google Scholar the search was all indexed text, with title-phrase searches
repeated when a candidate title was known.  For MathSciNet and zbMATH Open the
search was title, author, review/abstract, MSC/keyword, and reference metadata
where available.  For Scopus and Web of Science the search was
title/abstract/keyword, with cited-reference expansion for the retained
foundational antecedents.  Hyphenation variants were searched together:
\(\mathrm{D}\)-MAP, DMAP, discrete MAP, discrete-time MAP, Markovian arrival
process, and Markov-modulated arrival process.  Formula searches were repeated
with and without subscripts, for example \(r_2-r_0^2\), ``r2-r0\verb|^|2'',
``visible renewal quotient'', and ``binary inclusion probabilities''.

\paragraph{Search strings.}
The following Boolean families were used.  Database-specific syntax was the
literal analogue of the displayed strings: quotation marks were used for
phrases, \texttt{OR} grouped synonyms, and \texttt{AND} intersected concepts.
\begin{enumerate}[label=S\arabic*.,leftmargin=2.8em]
\item \texttt{("stationary renewal" OR "renewal binary" OR "binary renewal process" OR "stationary binary renewal") AND ("finite-dimensional" OR "cylinder probabilities" OR CLT OR "central limit theorem")}
\item \texttt{("Markovian arrival process" OR "discrete Markovian arrival process" OR "D-MAP" OR DMAP OR MAP2) AND (identifiability OR nonidentifiability OR "weak equivalence" OR "canonical form")}
\item \texttt{("Markovian arrival process" OR "D-MAP" OR DMAP OR MAP2) AND (moment OR moments OR covariance OR "second order") AND (structured OR two-state OR rational)}
\item \texttt{("visible renewal" OR "binary inclusion" OR "inclusion probabilities" OR "renewal quotient" OR "sampled counter") AND (inverse OR identifiability OR quotient OR "delta method")}
\item \texttt{("projection" OR "metric projection" OR "cone projection") AND ("shape restriction" OR "shape restrictions" OR boundary OR constrained) AND (test OR inference OR asymptotic OR bootstrap)}
\item \texttt{("nonconvex boundary" OR "boundary constraints" OR "proximal bootstrap" OR "directionally differentiable") AND (likelihood OR estimator OR inference)}
\item \texttt{("visible transition" OR "partial observation" OR "coarse graining" OR "hidden Markov" OR "Markov network") AND (inference OR reconstruction OR thermodynamic OR "broken detailed balance")}
\item \texttt{("r2-r0\textasciicircum 2" OR "r\_2-r\_0\textasciicircum 2" OR "retained complete-cycle" OR "finite-certificate projected Gaussian" OR "sampled-counter D-MAP")}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Inclusion and exclusion criteria.}
A paper was eligible for the recent-comparator column only if it was dated
2021--2026 and contained a stated theorem-level result whose hypotheses and
conclusion matched the row object after only notational translation.  For
Theorem~A this means a stationary renewal-binary finite-dimensional law or
renewal-binary CLT.  For Theorem~B it means an inverse theorem from visible
binary inclusion probabilities, or an algebraically equivalent renewal
quotient, to \((p+s,ps)\) or the unordered survival roots.  For Theorem~C it
means either the renewal-binary Gaussian input or the retained complete-cycle
covariance estimator and reporting gate.  For Theorem~D it means a
delta-method theorem for the explicit quotient
\(\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps)\) or an equivalent visible-renewal inverse.  For
Theorem~E it means the finite-certificate projected-Gaussian acceptance rule
for a D-MAP quotient, or the projection-limit theorem used as a component of
that rule.  Foundational antecedents before 2021 were retained only in the
third column.

Items were excluded from the recent-comparator column, but could still be
listed as contextual papers in the overlap column, when they addressed only
one of the following broader topics: generic HMM inference, generic MAP
moment calculation without the visible-renewal quotient, generic
coarse-graining or thermodynamic inference from partial observations, generic
boundary or bootstrap theory without the D-MAP feasible set, or representation
nonuniqueness without the three-inclusion inverse.  This is why several recent
papers remain cited in \Cref{tab:theorem-comparison} but are not counted as
exact analogues.
