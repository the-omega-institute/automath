\begin{abstract}
We study the visible binary output of a boundary-reset sampled counter whose
click and no-click kernels form a two-state killed-leakage D-MAP.  The physical
input is an attempt clock with rate \(\Gamma\), a recovery clock with rate
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\), and a sampling interval \(\Delta\tau\); the
killed-leakage convention is a structural part of the model, not a generic
property of two-state D-MAPs.

For this fixed subfamily the click record is a full-support stationary renewal
process.  We derive its Palm gap law, show that the zero-run hazard is strictly
increasing, compute the one-mode covariance tail, locate the exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold, and construct all finite word probabilities by
renewal products and finite inclusion--exclusion.  The visible law also admits
a quotient inversion: three inclusion probabilities determine the symmetric
survival-factor coordinates and hence the unordered survival-factor pair.  An
ordered labelled rate pair is not identified by one visible record unless an
external separated-root branch label is supplied.
For the physical two-state kernel we classify the complete killed-reset
visible fibre: off the exchange diagonal it is a closed similarity arc joining
the two rate-swapped physical kernels through strictly positive nontriangular
kernels, whereas on the diagonal the entire fibre collapses to a singleton.
For minimal \(n\)-state killed-reset D-MAPs, standard realization uniqueness
reduces equality of visible laws to reset-preserving similarity; we use this
only as recalled background.  Our higher-state result is quantitative: at
every minimal interior kernel, the unrestricted visible fibre contains a
locally immersed similarity manifold of dimension \((n-1)^2\), and strict
Markov positivity persists along this manifold.  Separately, the pure serial
subclass identifies the unordered positive-rate multiset, including repeated
sampled poles.

We then give fixed-setting retained-record asymptotics for these visible
coordinates.  Under the stated sampled-counter model and pre-specified
reporting gates, complete-cycle estimators satisfy an inclusion-coordinate CLT,
the quotient inverse has the corresponding delta method, including the
canonically sorted roots and log-rates.  A root-free analytic continuation of
the image equation and the discriminant inequality yield a studentized joint
physical-image test with compact-uniform size control across the exchange
diagonal and an explicit dominated-renewal local-power function.  This tests
the three-inclusion physical image, not arbitrary time-series misspecification.
To separate that limited diagnostic from model checking, we also construct a
total-space complete-visible-law test.  A distributional-distance guard is
consistent against every fixed stationary ergodic law separated from the
sampled-counter family, while a bounded finite-range gap score has
compact-uniform size and detects an explicit nonrenewal local alternative that
preserves all three inclusion coordinates, at the optimal $N^{-1/2}$ rate.
At an interior exchange point we further identify the complete
$L^2(g\otimes g)$ Markov--Palm interaction tangent space subject to those
three coordinates, compute its nuisance-orthogonal information operator, and
give a weighted pointwise-omnibus score with exchange-boundary local size
control.  The associated infinite-dimensional limit is isonormal; no uniform
optimality over its full unit sphere is claimed.  For the canonical weighted
Helmert truncation we obtain the sharp second-order region for uniform
Euclidean Gaussian coupling over bounded local nulls.  The proof uses an
imported dimension-free comparison between second-order Zolotarev and
quadratic Wasserstein distances rather than a dimension-dependent Yurinskii
probability bound.
At the exchange diagonal we identify the singular local experiment.  Uniformly
for \(z\) in compact subsets of \((0,1)\), the split
\(p_N=z+hN^{-1/4}\), \(s_N=z-hN^{-1/4}\) has a scalar Gaussian likelihood
limit indexed by \(h^2\), with an explicit gap score and information series.
Thus the sign of the hidden split remains invisible in the limit, while its
magnitude is detectable on the \(N^{-1/4}\) scale.
At the single preselected exchange
point \(z_0=1/2\), we give a pointwise projected-Gaussian limit as an example of
the nonregular geometry.  It is not a confidence procedure, test, or coverage
theorem.  All asymptotic claims are conditional on the model contract;
diagnostic outcomes are not used to select a model or inferential branch.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} constrained D-MAP, phase-type identifiability,
similarity orbit, confluent Prony method, renewal process, exact
\(1\)-dependence, boundary inference, specification testing

\section{Introduction}

This paper is about a constrained two-state discrete-time Markovian-arrival
process (D-MAP) and the stationary binary renewal record it generates.  The
physical input is deliberately modest: a latched killed-leakage sampled
counter with two
internal states, \(R\) for ready and \(D\) for dead/recovering.  During one
sampling interval of length \(\Delta\tau\), a ready counter attempts to click
with exponential rate \(\Gamma\), and a dead counter recovers with exponential
rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).  A click is recorded as \(A_n=1\), and the
boundary-reset convention assigns the post-click state at the next sample to
\(D\).  This construction derives the labelled kernel pair displayed in
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}: \(T_0+T_1\) is the hidden transition
matrix, \(T_1\) marks an observed \(1\), and the killed entries
\(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\) encode the rule that every recorded click leaves the
counter dead at the next boundary.  No theorem below allows either
one-labelled entry into \(R\) to be positive; such mass is same-bin reset
leakage and belongs to a different labelled kernel model.  Thus
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) are not formal decoration; they are
the attempt rate, recovery rate, and sampling interval of this sampled-counter
realization of the D-MAP.

The paper-level contribution is the closed-form record law, quotient inverse,
complete two-state Markovian fibre classification,
joint three-inclusion physical-image test, complete-visible-law specification
test, complete exchange-point Markov--Palm tangent analysis, and fixed-setting
retained-record asymptotic analysis of this constrained D-MAP(2) subfamily,
together with an
\(n\)-state identifiability theorem that
separates pure serial models from general killed-reset realizations by their
Markovian similarity-orbit fibres. The following contract fixes the physical
model used by the two-state record and inference statements.

This positioning is narrower than several nearby literatures.  The closest
probability comparator is Zamparo's stationary renewal-binary framework
\cite{Zamparo2022RenewalBinary}: it supplies a renewal-binary language and
Gaussian limit technology, but it does not derive the renewal law from a
triangular killed-leakage sampled-counter D-MAP, compute the Palm gap law and
monotone hazard in \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\), or invert three
visible inclusion coordinates to \((p+s,ps)\).  Classical MAP and D-MAP theory
\cite{Neuts1979MAP,Lucantoni1991BMAP,AlfaNeuts1995DMAP} supplies the
arrival-process background, while MAP2 and phase-type nonidentifiability
\cite{RamirezCoboLilloWiper2010MAP2Nonidentifiability,
OCinneide1989PhaseTypeNonuniqueness} explain why our inverse is deliberately a
visible quotient inverse rather than a labelled hidden-kernel reconstruction.
The \(N^{-1/4}\) rate caused by a quadratic local coordinate is classical in
loss-of-identifiability and locally conic models
\cite{DacunhaCastelleGassiat1997LocallyConic,
LiuShao2003LossIdentifiability}.  Our singular-limit theorem does not claim
that general mechanism as new.  It computes the complete stationary-record
likelihood experiment for this sampled-counter renewal law, including its
explicit score and information, and proves the expansion uniformly along
compact subsets of the exchange diagonal.

Recent physics-math work on weak memory, waiting times, correlations, and
partial observations is close in motivation but not in theorem object.  Weak
memory and Markov-state diagnostics
\cite{Brandner2025WeakMemoryPRE,MeyerBrandner2026WeakMemory,
HartichGodec2021EmergentMemory,VollmarEtAl2024Memory,
ZhaoMakarovGodec2025MarkovStateHolography,LoosGodec2025NonMarkovianEffects}
study memory strength, response, or adequacy of coarse dynamical models; here
the record theorem gives the exact one-mode covariance tail
\(\gamma_n=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid}^{\,n-1}\), the exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold, and a visible quotient inverse.  Transition
waiting-time and partial-observation inference
\cite{VanDerMeerErtelSeifert2022TransitionWTD,
HarunariDuttaPolettiniRoldan2022VisibleTransitions,
ErtelSeifert2024BlurredTransitions,BauerSeifertVanderMeer2025Stroboscopic,
MaierSeifertVanderMeer2025HiddenPaths} targets generators, entropy production,
hidden paths, or transition-waiting-time functionals under partial access.  The
waiting-time object here is instead the Palm zero-run gap law \(g_k\) of the
binary click record, used to build the complete visible renewal law and the
symmetric sampled-counter quotient.

The growing-word distributional distance used below is imported from the
stationary-ergodic testing framework of Ryabko and Ryabko
\cite{RyabkoRyabko2010ErgodicInference,Ryabko2012StationaryTesting}; it is not
claimed as a new general time-series method.  Recurrent-event and Markov/HMM
goodness-of-fit procedures are likewise established
\cite{AgustinPena2005RecurrentGOF,TitmanSharples2008MarkovGOF}.  The new object
here is narrower: a sampled-counter specialization that combines the complete
word-law guard with a finite-range score which remains regular at the exchange
diagonal and has an explicit three-inclusion-invisible nonrenewal LAN path.
At a fixed exchange point, the same Palm representation also isolates the
complete double-centred interaction space after removal of the single
zero-gap atom direction.  Generic serial-independence basis tests remain
imported methodology; the model-specific content is the additional atom
constraint, its efficient-information projection, and boundary calibration.

The general Gaussian approximation machinery is imported, not a contribution
of this paper.  Meckes gives the coordinate-free regularity estimate for the
multivariate Gaussian Stein equation
\cite{Meckes2009MultivariateStein}, while Bo{\l}botowski--Bouchitt\'e prove the
sharp, dimension-free comparison between the second-order Zolotarev distance
and quadratic Wasserstein distance
\cite{BolbotowskiBouchitte2026ZolotarevWasserstein}.  CMU's Yurinskii bounds
provide an instructive comparator but retain an ambient-dimension factor and
count both actual and Gaussian comparison-block moments
\cite{CattaneoMasiniUnderwood2025Yurinskii}.  Our contribution is restricted
to verifying the imported Zolotarev--Wasserstein route for the overlapping
Helmert renewal score and deriving its sharp model-specific layer boundary.

Detector dead-time and photon-counting models are the closest instrument-level
comparators
\cite{Muller1973,CantorTeich1975,YuFessler2000,Hadfield2009,
EisamanEtAl2011,StrakaEtAl2020SPADCounting,KirchhoffEtAl2025Deadtime,
JorgensenJohnson2026DED}.  They address count corrections, device recovery,
afterpulsing, finite-window distributions, or detector-specific estimators.
The theorem chain below instead fixes the boundary-reset killed-leakage kernel
and asks exactly what its stationary binary record identifies.  The structural
fragility of this convention is recorded in
\Cref{prop:model-fragility-boundary-reset-main}: allowing one-labelled
same-bin recovery into \(R\) destroys the deterministic post-click reset on
which the renewal product, quotient inverse, and retained-record covariance
route depend.

\begin{assumption}[Constrained sampled-counter D-MAP model]\label{ass:model-inference-contract-main}
Fix \((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\in(0,\infty)^3\) and define
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},
\]
\[
a=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\bigr)\,dr,\qquad
b=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr .
\]
The record model is the two-state D-MAP with hidden states ordered as
\((R,D)\) and labelled kernels
\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\ b&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1-p\\0&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The convention is
\((T_i)_{XY}=\PP(A_n=i,H_{n+1}=Y\mid H_n=X)\), the visible process
\((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) is the stationary binary process generated by these
kernels, and formulas on \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) are interpreted by
continuous limits.  The killed entries \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\) are a structural
constraint of this D-MAP, not optional boundary cases of the finite-record
theorems.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Scope of finite-record reports}\label{subsec:finite-record-report-scope-main}

The retained-record limits are fixed-setting asymptotic statements inside the
stationary law specified in \Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}.  The
model, population point, complete-cycle convention, and deterministic batch
sequence are fixed before the record is interpreted.  Theorems~C and D are CLT
and delta-method statements, respectively; Theorem~E is only a pointwise
projection-limit example.  This article does not construct confidence sets,
tests, or coverage guarantees from these limits.

The only model interpretation assumed by this contract is the latched
killed-leakage sampled-counter protocol of
\Cref{lem:classical-counter-kernel-construction-main}.  If the sampling
interval \(\Delta\tau\) is a known design interval, the visible stationary
record identifies the unordered survival-factor set
\(\{p,s\}=\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}\) and
hence the unordered numerical rate set
\(\{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\}\).  It does not identify which member is the
attempt rate and which is the recovery rate without side information.  If
\(\Delta\tau\) is not treated as a known design interval, the visible law
identifies only the dimensionless unordered product set
\(\{\Gamma\Delta\tau,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\}\); the same record does
not put those products on an absolute numerical rate scale.

The singular likelihood experiment is uniform for \(z\) in compact subsets of
\((0,1)\) and bounded local split \(h\).  It identifies only \(h^2\), not the
sign of \(h\) or an ordering of the two hidden labels.  The separate diagonal
projection result is only the pointwise limit at the preselected population
point \(z_0=1/2\), using the printed covariance certificate in
\Cref{lem:z-half-diagonal-branch-certificate-main}.  It is included to exhibit
the nonregular geometry of the quotient boundary.  No confidence set, test,
critical value, or coverage conclusion is attached to it, and no uniform
projection statement is made over the exchange diagonal.
Ordered labelled rates require external side information fixed independently
of this record.  The paper does not claim
simultaneous inversion over \(0<z<1\), nor identification of UDW, KMS,
thermodynamic, response-profile, or ordered-rate quantities unless the
particular result supplies the extra hypotheses and proof.

\subsection{Visible-record diagnostics for the model contract}\label{subsec:visible-record-model-diagnostics-main}

\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main} and
\Cref{subsec:finite-record-report-scope-main} form a conditional contract, not a
model-selection theorem.  The retained-record limits below are statements
inside the killed-leakage sampled-counter D-MAP with known \(\Delta\tau\);
they are not procedures that first validate that model from the same record and
then authorize inference under it.  No size, power, or post-selection theorem
is proved for model diagnostics.  The diagnostic protocol is therefore
supplementary: complete-gap renewal checks, inclusion-product residuals, and
low-lag covariance residuals can falsify the standing renewal D-MAP
interpretation, but they do not certify it and cannot validate the assumptions
used by
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main,thm:finite-record-quotient-delta-main,thm:e-fixed-candidate-exchange-diagonal-main}.


\begin{lemma}[Latched sampled-counter derivation of the D-MAP kernels]\label{lem:classical-counter-kernel-construction-main}
The kernels in \Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main} are exactly the
one-step kernels of the following discrete sampled-counter protocol.  At the
start of a sampling interval of length \(\Delta\tau\), the hidden state is
either \(R\) or \(D\).  From \(R\), an independent attempt clock with rate
\(\Gamma\) either rings before the end of the interval, in which case the
visible output is \(1\) and the counter is latched in \(D\) until the boundary,
or it does not ring, in which case the visible output is \(0\) and the next
state remains \(R\).
From \(D\), an independent recovery clock with rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\)
either does not ring, giving output \(0\) and next state \(D\), or rings at
time \(r\in(0,\Delta\tau)\).  Conditional on recovery at \(r\), the same
attempt clock is run only during the remaining time \(\Delta\tau-r\): no
attempt gives output \(0\) and next state \(R\), while an attempt gives output
\(1\) and latches the counter in \(D\) until the boundary.  No post-click
recovery or further within-bin evolution is permitted.  This protocol gives
\[
T_0(R,R)=p,\quad T_1(R,D)=1-p,\quad T_0(D,D)=s,
\]
\[
T_0(D,R)=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\,dr=b,
\quad
T_1(D,D)=\int_0^{\Delta\tau}\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}
\bigl(1-e^{-\Gamma(\Delta\tau-r)}\bigr)\,dr=a,
\]
and the two killed entries \(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The \(R\)-row is the survival and ringing decomposition of an exponential
clock over one interval.  For the \(D\)-row, no recovery before
\(\Delta\tau\) has probability \(s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\) and
contributes to \(T_0(D,D)\).  If recovery occurs at \(r\), its density is
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}r}\,dr\).  Survival of the attempt
clock for the remaining time \(\Delta\tau-r\) gives the \(T_0(D,R)\) integral,
and its complement gives the \(T_1(D,D)\) integral.  The latching rule never
allows a marked transition landing in \(R\), which gives the two killed
entries.  These mutually exclusive cases exhaust each row, since
\(s+a+b=1\) and \(p+(1-p)=1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fragility under non-killed one-labelled leakage]\label{prop:model-fragility-boundary-reset-main}
The killed-leakage convention in
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main} is not a removable presentation
choice.  It is a structural hypothesis behind the renewal law, quotient
inverse, and retained-record inference in Theorems~A--E.  Let
\((\widetilde T_0,\widetilde T_1)\) be any two-state labelled kernel with the
same visible convention
\[
(\widetilde T_i)_{XY}
=\PP(A_n=i,H_{n+1}=Y\mid H_n=X),\qquad X,Y\in\{R,D\},
\]
but suppose that either
\[
\widetilde T_1(R,R)>0
\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
\widetilde T_1(D,R)>0 .
\]
Then a click no longer forces the next hidden state to be the deterministic
state \(D\).  Consequently the click epochs are not regeneration epochs in
general.  In particular, the Palm renewal product
\[
\PP(B\subset\Xi)=\rho\prod_j u_{i_{j+1}-i_j}
\]
for \(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\) does not follow from the D-MAP structure alone;
nor do the Palm gap formula
\(g_k=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1\), the single-mode covariance identity
\[
u_2=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid},
\]
or the quotient recovery
\[
\lambda_{\rm hid}=(r_2-r_0^2)/(r_1-r_0^2),\qquad
\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps).
\]
Therefore Theorems~A and B, and the finite-record Theorems~C--E that use the
same renewal inverse and covariance route, fail to be valid generic
sampled-counter or generic D-MAP(2) statements unless additional hypotheses
restore a deterministic post-click reset or supply a replacement regeneration
theory.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proofs of Theorems~A--E use the following deterministic post-click fact:
on every event \(\{A_n=1\}\), the next hidden state is \(H_{n+1}=D\).  In the
kernel notation this is exactly
\(T_1(R,R)=T_1(D,R)=0\).  It makes each observed click a reset to the same
state \(D\), so the post-click future starts from the fixed vector \(e_D\),
independently of the pre-click hidden state and of the earlier visible record.
The inter-click zero runs are then i.i.d. under the Palm law, their tail is
computed by iterating \(T_0\) from \(e_D\), and inclusion probabilities factor
as the displayed renewal product.

If \(\widetilde T_1(R,R)>0\), a click from \(R\) can leave the next hidden
state equal to \(R\).  If \(\widetilde T_1(D,R)>0\), a click from \(D\) can
leave the next hidden state equal to \(R\).  Whenever either transition has
positive probability, conditioning on \(A_n=1\) need not put \(H_{n+1}\) at
the deterministic state \(D\); it produces a post-click distribution that can
depend on the pre-click hidden state and on the stationary mixture at the
click time.  The D-MAP property by itself only records labelled Markov
transitions.  It does not turn this state-dependent post-click mixture into
the fixed Palm restart required for a renewal record.

The continuous same-bin recovery comparator is a concrete instance: the paths
``click--recover'' from \(R\) and ``recover--click--recover'' from \(D\) give
positive entries \(\widetilde T_1(R,R)\) and
\(\widetilde T_1(D,R)\).  The Palm gap law is therefore no longer a consequence
of the labelled kernel without extra assumptions.  The renewal product
formulas and the covariance transfer lemma are downstream of that Palm renewal
law, while the quotient inverse in Theorem~B explicitly divides the covariance
identity \(r_2-r_0^2=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid}\) by
\(r_1-r_0^2=\rho(a-\rho)\).  Once the renewal identity is unavailable, the
displayed inverse and the retained-record covariance estimator are no longer
proved for the modified kernel.  This proves the stated logical dependence.
\end{proof}

The main text below is organized around exactly five paper-level theorem
statements for this constrained D-MAP(2) subfamily.  Supporting lemmas,
propositions, corollaries, certificate interfaces, and algorithms are used only
to prove or apply these five statements.  The killed-leakage dependency is
explicit in \Cref{prop:model-fragility-boundary-reset-main}: if same-bin
recovery after a click is retained in the one-labelled kernel, this theorem
chain is not the applicable theorem chain.

\subsection{Consolidated main theorem block}\label{subsec:main-theorem-boundary}

\begin{paperthm}[Constrained D-MAP record law]\label{thm:main-record-wrapper}
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) have the stationary visible law generated by the
sampled-counter kernels in \Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}.  Put
\[
p=e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},\qquad
s=e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau},\qquad
\rho=\PP(A_0=1),\qquad
\lambda_{\rm hid}=p-b .
\]
Then \((A_n)\) is a full-support renewal process.  If \(G\) is the Palm number
of zeros between consecutive ones, then
\[
g_k=\PP(G=k)=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1{\bf 1},\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
For \(\Gamma\ne\kappa_{\mathrm r}\),
\[
g_k=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-s)s^k}
          {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\qquad
S_k=\PP(G\ge k)=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
with continuous-limit formulas on \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\).  The zero-run
hazard \(h_k=g_k/S_k\) is strictly increasing.  The visible covariance has the
single hidden-mode tail
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid}^{\,n-1},
\qquad n\ge1 .
\]
  The record is exact \(1\)-dependent if and only if
  \(\lambda_{\rm hid}=0\).  Moreover
  \[
  \gamma_1=\rho(a-\rho)<0,
  \]
  so adjacent outputs are not independent and the record is not
  \(0\)-dependent.  In no positive parameter case is it a finite-order Markov
  chain.
\end{paperthm}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp hidden-mode bound and correlation phase]
\label{cor:sharp-hidden-mode-phase-main}
Put \(x=\Gamma\Delta\tau\), \(y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\), and
\(f(t)=te^{-t}\).  The nontrivial hidden eigenvalue satisfies
\[
\lambda_{\rm hid}=\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\quad(x\ne y),\qquad
\lambda_{\rm hid}=f'(x)=e^{-x}(1-x)\quad(x=y),
\]
and its sharp global range is
\[
-e^{-2}\le\lambda_{\rm hid}<1.
\]
Equality in the lower bound holds only at \(x=y=2\), whereas the upper
endpoint is approached only as \(x,y\downarrow0\).  Since
\(\rho(a-\rho)<0\), all positive-lag covariances are negative when
\(\lambda_{\rm hid}>0\); at \(\lambda_{\rm hid}=0\), only the lag-one
covariance is nonzero; and when \(\lambda_{\rm hid}<0\), the covariances
strictly alternate in sign.  In the last case their successive absolute
ratio is universally bounded by
\[
\left|\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_{n+1})}
{\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)}\right|\le e^{-2},\qquad n\ge1.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The symmetric hidden-mode formula in the proof of Theorem~A gives the stated
secant slope.  For \(x\ne y\), the mean-value theorem writes it as
\(f'(\xi)\) for a point \(\xi\) strictly between \(x\) and \(y\).  Now
\(f'(t)=e^{-t}(1-t)\) has its unique global minimum \(-e^{-2}\) at \(t=2\)
and has supremum \(1\) as \(t\downarrow0\).  A nondegenerate secant cannot
attain the lower endpoint, because that would require \(f'\) to equal its
strict global minimum throughout a nontrivial interval.  Hence equality is
possible only in the diagonal limit \(x=y=2\).  The covariance assertions
then follow directly from Theorem~A.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Small-sampling-interval cycle and intensity bias]
\label{prop:small-sampling-interval-bias-main}
Fix \(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}>0\), write the variable sampling interval as
\(\Delta>0\), and let \(\mu_\Delta=\EE(G+1)=1/\rho_\Delta\).  With
\(x=\Gamma\Delta\) and \(y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\),
\[
\mu_\Delta=\frac{y/(1-e^{-x})-x/(1-e^{-y})}{y-x},
\]
where the equal-rate case is the continuous limit.  As \(\Delta\downarrow0\),
\[
\Delta\mu_\Delta=\frac1\Gamma+\frac1{\kappa_{\mathrm r}}
+\frac{\Delta}{2}
+\frac{\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}(\Gamma+\kappa_{\mathrm r})}{720}\Delta^4
+O(\Delta^6),
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac{\rho_\Delta}{\Delta}
=\frac{\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}}{\Gamma+\kappa_{\mathrm r}}
-\frac{\Delta}{2}
\left(\frac{\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}}{\Gamma+\kappa_{\mathrm r}}\right)^2
+O(\Delta^2).
\]
Thus boundary sampling adds a universal half-interval term to the mean
physical inter-click time and produces the displayed first-order downward
bias in the click intensity per unit time.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Summing \((k+1)g_k\) in Theorem~A gives
\[
\mu_\Delta=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}/(1-e^{-\Gamma\Delta})
-\Gamma/(1-e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta})}
{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
which is the displayed dimensionless formula.  Substitution of
\[
\frac{u}{1-e^{-u}}=1+\frac u2+\frac{u^2}{12}
-\frac{u^4}{720}+\frac{u^6}{30240}+O(u^8)
\]
and cancellation of the quadratic terms yield the expansion of
\(\Delta\mu_\Delta\), including its continuous equal-rate limit.  Expanding
its reciprocal gives the intensity formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{paperthm}[Quotient inverse]\label{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}
Work in the same constrained D-MAP record model.  Let
\[
r_0=\rho,\qquad r_1=\rho a,\qquad r_2=\rho u_2,
\]
and assume the quotient inverse domain
\[
r_0>0,\qquad \Delta_{\rm inv}:=r_1-r_0^2\ne0 .
\]
Define
\[
a(r)=\frac{r_1}{r_0},\qquad
\lambda(r)=\frac{r_2-r_0^2}{r_1-r_0^2},
\]
and
\[
\Phi(r)=
\left(1-a(r)+\lambda(r),\,
r_0(1-\lambda(r))-a(r)+\lambda(r)\right).
\]
On the constrained D-MAP image,
\[
\Phi(r)=(p+s,ps).
\]
Thus the visible law determines the unordered survival-factor pair
\(\{p,s\}\), equivalently the unordered roots of
\[
z^2-\Phi_1(r)z+\Phi_2(r)=0,
\]
and determines all visible renewal probabilities through this quotient.  For
known design interval \(\Delta\tau\), the same quotient determines the
unordered numerical rate set
\[
\left\{-\frac{\log p}{\Delta\tau},-\frac{\log s}{\Delta\tau}\right\}
=\{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\}.
\]
If \(\Delta\tau\) is not supplied as a known design interval, the numerical
rate display is replaced by the dimensionless identifiable set
\[
\{-\log p,-\log s\}
=\{\Gamma\Delta\tau,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\}.
\]
In either case the visible law does not determine an ordered labelled vector
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\) unless external side information fixes the
physical assignment.  On the exchange diagonal, the
invariant visible-law estimand is the common dimensionless product, and only
for known \(\Delta\tau\) the common numerical rate, not an ordered
attempt/recovery label.
\end{paperthm}

\begin{proposition}[Exact three-inclusion image]
\label{prop:exact-three-inclusion-image-main}
Let \(r=(r_0,r_1,r_2)\) be a real triple in the stable inverse chart
\(r_0>0\), \(r_1-r_0^2\ne0\), form
\(\sigma=\Phi(r)=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)\), and suppose the roots \(p,s\) of
\(z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2\) belong to \((0,1)\), with equality allowed.  Define
\[
\mathfrak C(p,s)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{s\log p-p\log s}{\log p-\log s},&p\ne s,\\[6pt]
p(1-\log p),&p=s,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\mathcal E(r)=\frac{r_1}{r_0}-1+\mathfrak C(p,s).
\]
Then \(r\) is generated by positive sampled-counter parameters
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) if and only if
\(\mathcal E(r)=0\).  On the distinct-root stratum this is a regular
codimension-one real-analytic hypersurface.  The symmetric function
\(\mathfrak C\) has a real-analytic continuation in \((\sigma_1,\sigma_2)\)
through \(p=s\); the physical image at the exchange diagonal is the feasible
side of the continued hypersurface and hence has a boundary there.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For necessity, put \(x=\Gamma\Delta\tau\), \(y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\),
so \(p=e^{-x}\), \(s=e^{-y}\), and
\[
b=\frac{y(p-s)}{y-x}.
\]
The continuous diagonal interpretation is understood.  Direct cancellation
gives
\[
s+b=\frac{yp-xs}{y-x}
=\frac{s\log p-p\log s}{\log p-\log s}=\mathfrak C(p,s).
\]
Since \(a=1-s-b\), this proves \(\mathcal E(r)=0\).

Conversely, choose any \(\Delta\tau>0\), set
\(\Gamma=-\log p/\Delta\tau\),
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}=-\log s/\Delta\tau\), and define
\(b=\mathfrak C(p,s)-s\), \(a=1-\mathfrak C(p,s)\).  These are precisely the
strictly positive integral weights in the sampled-counter kernels.  The first
symmetric-coordinate identity gives
\(\lambda=p+s+a-1=p-b\), while the second gives
\[
r_0=\frac{ps+a-\lambda}{1-\lambda}
=\frac{(1-p)(1-s)}{1-p+b}=\rho.
\]
The definitions of \(a(r)\) and \(\lambda(r)\) then recover \(r_1=\rho a\)
and \(r_2=\rho^2+\lambda(r_1-\rho^2)\), proving sufficiency.

For regularity, write
\[
D(\sigma)=1-\sigma_1+\sigma_2=(1-p)(1-s),\qquad
Q(\sigma)=1-\sigma_1+\mathfrak C(p,s).
\]
On the inverse chart, the change of variables
\(r\mapsto(r_0,\sigma_1,\sigma_2)\) is locally invertible: in the intermediate
coordinates \((r_0,a,\lambda)\), the determinant of
\(\partial(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)/\partial(a,\lambda)\) is \(r_0>0\).  Moreover,
\[
\mathcal E(r)=Q(\sigma)-\frac{D(\sigma)}{r_0}
=\frac{Q(\sigma)}{r_0}
\left\{r_0-\frac{D(\sigma)}{Q(\sigma)}\right\}.
\]
Thus its zero set is the graph \(r_0=D/Q\), so it is regular.  Away from the
diagonal analyticity is immediate.  At \(p=m+d\), \(s=m-d\), both the
numerator and denominator defining \(\mathfrak C\) are analytic odd functions
of \(d\) for \(|d|<m\), and the denominator has a simple zero at \(d=0\).
Their quotient therefore has a removable analytic extension; invariance under
\(d\mapsto-d\) makes that extension even.  Its Taylor expansion begins
\[
\mathfrak C(m+d,m-d)=m(1-\log m)
+\frac{d^2}{m}\left(\frac12+\frac13\log m\right)+O(d^4).
\]
Since \(m=\sigma_1/2\) and \(d^2=\sigma_1^2/4-\sigma_2\), this supplies the
claimed symmetric analytic continuation.  The real-root constraint is the
one-sided inequality \(\sigma_2\le\sigma_1^2/4\), which proves the final
boundary qualification.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Analytic joint image chart at the exchange boundary]
\label{prop:analytic-joint-image-chart-main}
Fix \(0<\varepsilon<M<\infty\) and let

\[
\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}
=\{(x,y):\varepsilon\le x,y\le M\}.
\]
For \(\sigma_1>0\), \(\sigma_2>0\), put
\[
w(\sigma)=1-\frac{4\sigma_2}{\sigma_1^2}
\]
and, for \(w<1\), define
\[
H(w)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\sqrt w}{\operatorname{artanh}\sqrt w},&0<w<1,\\[5pt]
1,&w=0,\\[2pt]
\dfrac{\sqrt{-w}}{\arctan\sqrt{-w}},&w<0.
\end{cases}
\]
Then \(H\) is real analytic on \((-\infty,1)\), with
\[
H(w)=1-\frac w3-\frac{4w^2}{45}+O(w^3)
\quad(w\longrightarrow0).
\]
The symmetric continuation in
\Cref{prop:exact-three-inclusion-image-main} has the root-free expression
\[
\mathfrak C_{\rm an}(\sigma)
=\frac{\sigma_1}{2}
\left\{1-\frac12\log(\sigma_2)
H\!\left(1-\frac{4\sigma_2}{\sigma_1^2}\right)\right\}.
\]
Consequently there is an open neighbourhood \(\mathcal U\) of the compact
three-inclusion image of \(\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}\) on which
\[
e(r)=\frac{r_1}{r_0}-1+\mathfrak C_{\rm an}\{\Phi(r)\},
\qquad
\mathcal D(r)=\Phi_1(r)^2-4\Phi_2(r)
\]
are real analytic and
\[
r\text{ is a positive sampled-counter image point}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
e(r)=0,\qquad \mathcal D(r)\ge0.
\]
Moreover, with \(\psi=(e,\mathcal D)^\top\),
\[
\operatorname{rank}D\psi(r)=2
\]
at every physical point in this compact image, including the exchange
diagonal.  The equivalence is local to \(\mathcal U\); no global extension
of the logarithmic chart is asserted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(m=\sigma_1/2\).  On the real-root side set
\[
p=m(1+t),\qquad s=m(1-t),\qquad t^2=w.
\]
Direct expansion of the numerator gives
\[
s\log p-p\log s
=m\{2\operatorname{artanh}t-t\log(\sigma_2)\}.
\]
Division by
\(\log p-\log s=2\operatorname{artanh}t\) proves the displayed formula.
The power series of \(t/\operatorname{artanh}t\) contains only even powers
and begins with the stated coefficients.  Replacing \(t\) by
\(i\sqrt{-w}\) gives the real expression on \(w<0\), so the two formulas
are restrictions of one real-analytic germ through \(w=0\).

On the compact physical image,
\(\sigma_1,\sigma_2\) are bounded away from zero and \(w\) is bounded away
from one.  The quotient denominator is also uniformly separated from zero
by \Cref{prop:stable-quotient-chart-main}.  A sufficiently small uniform
neighbourhood therefore lies in the displayed analytic chart and has both
quadratic roots in \((0,1)\) whenever \(\mathcal D\ge0\).  The image
equivalence now follows from
\Cref{prop:exact-three-inclusion-image-main}.

For the rank assertion use the analytic coordinates
\((r_0,\sigma_1,\sigma_2)\).  With
\[
D(\sigma)=1-\sigma_1+\sigma_2,
\qquad
Q(\sigma)=1-\sigma_1+\mathfrak C_{\rm an}(\sigma),
\]
the calculation in the proof of
\Cref{prop:exact-three-inclusion-image-main} gives
\[
e(r)=Q(\sigma)-\frac{D(\sigma)}{r_0},
\qquad
\frac{\partial e}{\partial r_0}
=\frac{D(\sigma)}{r_0^2}>0.
\]
By contrast,
\[
\frac{\partial\mathcal D}{\partial r_0}=0,
\qquad
\nabla_\sigma\mathcal D=(2\sigma_1,-4)\ne0.
\]
Thus the two rows of \(D\psi\) are linearly independent.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Separated-root scalar image diagnostic]
\label{cor:separated-root-image-diagnostic-main}
Assume Theorem~C, a physical population point with \(p\ne s\), and a
consistent covariance estimator \(\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\rm tot}\).  If
\[
\nu_{\mathcal E}^2=
\nabla\mathcal E(r)^\top\Sigma_r
\nabla\mathcal E(r)>0,
\]
then, with
\(\widehat\nu_{\mathcal E,N}^{\,2}=
\nabla\mathcal E(\Rinch_N^{\rm cyc})^\top
\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\rm tot}
\nabla\mathcal E(\Rinch_N^{\rm cyc})\),
\[
\frac{N\,\mathcal E(\Rinch_N^{\rm cyc})^2}
{\widehat\nu_{\mathcal E,N}^{\,2}}\ \Rightarrow\ \chi_1^2.
\]
This is only a diagnostic for the scalar three-inclusion image equation on a

\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
When \(p\ne s\), the discriminant remains positive on a neighbourhood of the

\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniformly stable quotient chart]\label{prop:stable-quotient-chart-main}
Put \(x=\Gamma\Delta\tau\), \(y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\), and, for
\(0<\varepsilon\le M<\infty\), restrict the dimensionless parameters to
\[
\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}=\{(x,y):\varepsilon\le x,y\le M\}.
\]
Writing \(q=1-e^{-x}\) and \(q_{\min}=1-e^{-\varepsilon}\), every point in
this chart satisfies
\[
r_0\ge \frac{q_{\min}^2}{2},\qquad
|\Delta_{\rm inv}|
\ge \frac{\varepsilon e^{-2M}q_{\min}^3}{4}.
\]
Consequently \(D\Phi\) is uniformly bounded on
\(\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}\).  If in addition \(|p-s|\ge\delta>0\), the sorted
root Jacobian is bounded by a constant of order \(\delta^{-1}\), while
\(p,s\ge e^{-M}\) uniformly bounds the log-rate derivative.  Thus
\(\Theta_{\varepsilon,M,\delta}:=\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}\cap
\{|p-s|\ge\delta\}\) is a uniformly stable separated-root/rate chart; the
exchange diagonal is excluded and remains nonregular.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The integral representation of \(b\) gives
\[
b=y\int_0^1e^{-yt-x(1-t)}\,dt\ge \varepsilon e^{-M}.
\]
Moreover \(1-s\ge q_{\min}\), \(a\le q(1-s)\), and hence
\(q-a\ge qs\ge q_{\min}e^{-M}\).  From
\[
\rho=\frac{q(1-s)}{q+b},\qquad
\rho-a=\frac{b(q-a)}{q+b},
\]
and \(q+b\le2\),
\[
r_0=\rho\ge\frac{q_{\min}^2}{2},\qquad
|\Delta_{\rm inv}|=\rho(\rho-a)
=\frac{q(1-s)b(q-a)}{(q+b)^2}
\ge\frac{\varepsilon e^{-2M}q_{\min}^3}{4}.
\]
All entries of the rational matrix \(D\Phi\) are therefore uniformly bounded
on the compact chart.  The root derivative contains only the additional
denominator \(z_+-z_-=|p-s|\), and the derivative of
\(-\log z/\Delta\tau\) is \(-(\Delta\tau z)^{-1}\); the last two assertions
follow from \(|p-s|\ge\delta\) and \(p,s\ge e^{-M}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Specialized separated covariance estimator for adjacent gap rewards]\label{lem:paper-level-separated-covariance-main}
Let \(G_j\) be i.i.d. nonnegative integer-valued variables with
\(\EE e^{\eta G_0}<\infty\) for some \(\eta>0\).  Put \(L_j=G_j+1\),
\(\mu=\EE L_0\), \(K_N=\max\{k:\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}L_j\le N\}\), and
\(Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1})\in\RR^d\), where \(\EE Y_j=0\) and
\(\EE\|Y_j\|^4<\infty\).  Set
\[
\Sigma_r=\frac{1}{\mu}\{\operatorname{Var}Y_0+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0)\}.
\]
With \(b_N=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\), \(d_N=b_N+1\),
\[
I_{\ell,N}=\{(\ell-1)d_N,\ldots,(\ell-1)d_N+b_N-1\},
\qquad
M_N=\max\{M:(M-1)d_N+b_N\le K_N-1\},
\]
let \(B_{\ell,N}=\sum_{j\in I_{\ell,N}}Y_j\).  On \(M_N\ge2\), put
\(\bar B_N=M_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}B_{\ell,N}\) and
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}
=\frac{K_N}{N}\frac{1}{b_N(M_N-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}(B_{\ell,N}-\bar B_N)(B_{\ell,N}-\bar B_N)^\top .
\]
Then \(M_N\ge2\) with probability tending to one, and the auxiliary zero
completion on \(M_N<2\) satisfies
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/3}).
\]
If \(\widehat Y_j=Y_j-\Delta_NL_j\) with
\(\Delta_N=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), the same rate holds for the corresponding
plug-in separated-batch estimator.
For any deterministic \(m_N\to\infty\) with
\(m_N=o(N/b_N)\), the operational reporting gate is strengthened to
\(M_N\ge m_N\); this event also has probability tending to one.  The condition
\(M_N\ge2\) is used only to define the auxiliary estimator on all samples.

\begin{proof}
The variables \(Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1})\) are centered and \(1\)-dependent.  Since
the deterministic blocks \(I_{\ell,N}\) are separated by one deleted cycle,
the block sums \(B_{\ell,N}\) are independent for deterministic prefixes.
Writing
\[
T_1=\operatorname{Var}Y_0+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0),
\]
one has \(b_N^{-1}\operatorname{Var}B_{\ell,N}=T_1+O(b_N^{-1})\).  The
exponential moment of \(G_0\) gives uniform fourth moments for the block sums.
Define the independent centered matrices
\[
X_{\ell,N}=b_N^{-1}\{B_{\ell,N}B_{\ell,N}^{\top}
-\EE(B_{\ell,N}B_{\ell,N}^{\top})\},
\qquad \sup_{\ell,N}\EE\|X_{\ell,N}\|_F^2\le C_X.
\]
For fixed \(0<c<C<\infty\), on
\(cN/b_N\le M_N\le CN/b_N\), Kolmogorov's maximal inequality applied
entrywise, followed by a union bound over the \(d^2\) entries, gives
\[
\PP\!\left\{
\max_{m\le CN/b_N}\left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^mX_{\ell,N}\right\|_F
>\eta N/b_N\right\}
\le \frac{C_d b_N}{\eta^2N}.
\]
At the natural \(\sqrt{N/b_N}\) scale the same inequality yields
\[
\left\|\frac1{M_N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}X_{\ell,N}\right\|_{\rm op}
=O_{\PP}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{b_N}{N}}\right).
\]
The empirical block mean contributes \(O_{\PP}(b_N/N)\).  The renewal count
obeys
\[
K_N=N/\mu+O_{\PP}(\sqrt N),
\]
so the stopped block count is controlled by
\[
M_N=\frac{N}{\mu d_N}+O_{\PP}(\sqrt N/b_N+1),
\]
equivalently
\[
M_N=\frac{N}{\mu b_N}
  +O_{\PP}(N/b_N^2+\sqrt N/b_N+1),
\]
where the deterministic \(N/b_N^2\) term is only the one-cycle separator
correction, and \(b_NM_N/K_N=1+O_{\PP}(b_N^{-1})\).  Combining these estimates
with \(K_N/N\to1/\mu\) proves the stopped-prefix replacement.  For the plug-in
rewards, the exponential moment of \(L_j\) supplies constants \(c_L,C_0>0\),
with \(C_0\) sufficiently large, such that the Chernoff and union bounds give
\[
\PP\!\left\{
\max_{\ell\le CN/b_N}\sum_{j\in I_{\ell,N}}L_j>C_0b_N
\right\}
\le \frac{CN}{b_N}e^{-c_Lb_N}=o(1).
\]
Consequently
\[
\max_{\ell\le M_N}\left\|\sum_{j\in I_{\ell,N}}(\widehat Y_j-Y_j)\right\|
\le \|\Delta_N\|\max_{\ell\le M_N}\sum_{j\in I_{\ell,N}}L_j
=O_{\PP}(b_NN^{-1/2}),
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz, the covariance cross term is
\(O_{\PP}(\sqrt{b_N/N})\); the quadratic perturbation is
\(O_{\PP}(b_N/N)\).  Combining these terms with the block bias gives
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}-\Sigma_r\|_{\rm op}
=O_{\PP}\!\left(b_N^{-1}+\sqrt{\frac{b_N}{N}}\right).
\]
The choice \(b_N=\lfloor N^{1/3}\rfloor\) proves the stated
\(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/3})\) rate.
\end{proof}
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform joint three-inclusion physical-image test]
\label{thm:uniform-joint-physical-image-test-main}
Fix \(0<\varepsilon<M<\infty\), and let \(\mathcal U\), \(e\),
\(\mathcal D\), and \(\psi=(e,\mathcal D)^\top\) be as in
\Cref{prop:analytic-joint-image-chart-main}.  Choose, before observing the

\[
\widehat r_N=\Rinch_N^{\rm cyc},
\qquad
\mathcal G_N=\{\widehat r_N\in\mathcal U_0\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal G_N\), define
\[
J(r)=D\psi(r),qquad
\widehat\Omega_N
=J(\widehat r_N)\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\rm tot}
J(\widehat r_N)^\top .
\]
Let \(\widehat\Omega_N^{\rm tr}\) be obtained by replacing every eigenvalue
of \(\widehat\Omega_N\) below \(N^{-1/4}\) by \(N^{-1/4}\), and write
\[
\widehat\Omega_N^{\rm tr}
=\begin{pmatrix}
\widehat\omega_{11,N}&\widehat\omega_{12,N}\\
\widehat\omega_{12,N}&\widehat\omega_{22,N}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Set
\[
\widehat\beta_N
=\frac{\widehat\omega_{12,N}}{\widehat\omega_{11,N}},
\qquad
\widehat v_N
=\widehat\omega_{22,N}
-\frac{\widehat\omega_{12,N}^2}{\widehat\omega_{11,N}},
\]
\[
Z_{e,N}
=\frac{\sqrt N\,e(\widehat r_N)}
{\sqrt{\widehat\omega_{11,N}}},
\qquad
Z_{d,N}
=\frac{\sqrt N\{\mathcal D(\widehat r_N)
-\widehat\beta_Ne(\widehat r_N)\}}
{\sqrt{\widehat v_N}},
\]
and
\[
T_N=Z_{e,N}^2+(Z_{d,N}^{-})^2,
\qquad z^-:=\max\{-z,0\}.
\]
For \(0<\alpha<1\), let
\[
c_{1,\alpha}=F_{\chi_1^2}^{-1}(1-\alpha)
\]
and let \(c_{\partial,\alpha}\) be the unique solution of
\[
\frac12F_{\chi_1^2}(c_{\partial,\alpha})
+\frac12F_{\chi_2^2}(c_{\partial,\alpha})=1-\alpha.
\]
With \(\kappa_N=\log N\), use
\[
c_{N,\alpha}=
\begin{cases}
c_{1,\alpha},&Z_{d,N}>\kappa_N,\\
c_{\partial,\alpha},&Z_{d,N}\le\kappa_N.
\end{cases}
\]
The operational procedure rejects when
\(\mathcal G_N\cap\{T_N>c_{N,\alpha}\}\) occurs and otherwise withholds
the report off \(\mathcal G_N\).  For a total-space mathematical test, the
off-gate decision is defined to be no rejection.

Then the following assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Uniformly on \(\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}\),
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\rm tot}-\Sigma_r(\theta)
\longrightarrow_{\PP_\theta}0,
\qquad
\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}}
\lambda_{\min}\{J(r(\theta))\Sigma_r(\theta)J(r(\theta))^\top\}>0.
\]
The convergence is uniform in probability, and the chart gate and spectral
truncation are inactive with probability tending to one uniformly in
\(\theta\).

\item At every fixed physical point with \(p\ne s\),
\[
T_N\Rightarrow\chi_1^2,
\qquad
\PP_\theta\{Z_{d,N}>\kappa_N\}\longrightarrow1.
\]
At every fixed exchange-diagonal point,
\[
T_N\Rightarrow Z_1^2+(Z_2^-)^2
\sim\frac12\chi_1^2+\frac12\chi_2^2,
\]
where \(Z_1,Z_2\) are independent standard normal variables.  In particular,
\[
c_{1,0.05}=3.8414588\ldots,\qquad
c_{\partial,0.05}=5.1383808\ldots .
\]

\item The test has compact-uniform asymptotic size at most \(\alpha\):
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}}
\PP_\theta\{\mathcal G_N,\ T_N>c_{N,\alpha}\}
\le\alpha.
\]
More precisely, along a null sequence define
\[
\eta_N=
\frac{\sqrt N\,(p_N-s_N)^2}
{\sqrt{v(\theta_N)}},
\qquad
v=\omega_{22}-\frac{\omega_{12}^2}{\omega_{11}}.
\]
If \(\eta_N\to\eta<\infty\), then
\[
T_N\Rightarrow Z_1^2+(Z_2+\eta)^-{}^2
\]
and the boundary critical value is selected with probability tending to one.
If \(\eta_N\to\infty\), then
\(T_N-Z_{e,N}^2\to_{\PP}0\); if also
\(\eta_N/\log N\to\infty\), the interior critical value is selected with
probability tending to one.

\item Let a stationary full-support renewal alternative have an exponential
gap moment, a limiting inclusion triple \(r\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal U_0\),
and the complete-cycle CLT and covariance consistency in Theorem~C with a
positive-definite constraint covariance.  If \(e(r)\ne0\) or
\(\mathcal D(r)<0\), then \(T_N\to_{\PP}\infty\).  Thus the stated
consistency conclusion is conditional on an explicit renewal alternative;
no test of arbitrary nonrenewal time-series misspecification is claimed.
\end{enumerate}
The chi-bar-square and boundary-selection principles are standard
\cite{Chernoff1954LikelihoodRatio,Shapiro1987MetricProjection,
Andrews2001BoundaryTesting}; the theorem is their studentized specialization
to the sampled-counter three-inclusion image, including the uniform
nondegeneracy verification below.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On \(\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}\), both eigenvalues of the zero-labelled kernel
are at most \(e^{-\varepsilon}\).  At a repeated eigenvalue its powers carry
only a first-degree Jordan factor.  Hence, for constants independent of
\(\theta\),
\[
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}}
\PP_\theta(G\ge k)\le C(1+k)e^{-\varepsilon k},
\qquad k\ge0.
\]
Every exponential moment of \(G\) of order below \(\varepsilon\) is
therefore uniformly bounded.  The complete-cycle reward in Theorem~C is a
function of two consecutive independent gaps, so the corresponding
triangular arrays are \(1\)-dependent and have uniformly bounded fourth
moments.  The usual even--odd blocking proof of the \(1\)-dependent CLT is
then uniform along every convergent parameter sequence.  The renewal-count
replacement and the two endpoint deletions are uniform by the same
exponential bound.  Compactness and the subsequence criterion give the
uniform bounded-Lipschitz CLT.  The maximal and Chernoff bounds in the proof
of \Cref{lem:paper-level-separated-covariance-main} use only these uniform
moment constants, so they also give the stated uniform covariance
consistency.

It remains to prove nondegeneracy rather than assume it.  If
\(c^\top\Sigma_rc=0\), put
\[
f(i,j)=c^\top
\left[
\begin{pmatrix}
1\\ \mathbf1_{\{i=0\}}\\
\mathbf1_{\{i=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{i=0,j=0\}}
\end{pmatrix}
-r(i+1)
\right].
\]
For a full-support i.i.d. gap law, the Hoeffding row--column decomposition
of a one-dependent sequence says that its long-run variance is zero exactly
when \(f(i,j)=u_i-u_j\).  The degenerate interaction residual of \(f\) is
\[
c_2(\mathbf1_{\{i=0\}}-g_0)
(\mathbf1_{\{j=0\}}-g_0).
\]
Since \(0<g_0<1\), vanishing of the interaction forces \(c_2=0\).  Then
\(f\) depends only on \(i\), and an identity \(f(i)=u_i-u_j\) for all
\(i,j\) forces \(f\equiv0\).  For all \(i\ge1\), this reads
\[
c_0-(c^\top r)(i+1)=0,
\]
so \(c^\top r=c_0=0\); the value \(i=0\) then gives \(c_1=0\).  Thus
\(c=0\), and \(\Sigma_r\) is positive definite.  Its entries are continuous
in \(\theta\) by dominated convergence.  Compactness and the rank-two
conclusion of \Cref{prop:analytic-joint-image-chart-main} now give the
uniform positive lower bound for \(\Omega=J\Sigma_rJ^\top\).

For any positive-definite
\(\Omega=(\omega_{ij})_{i,j=1}^2\), direct completion of squares gives
\[
\inf_{u\ge0}
\left\|\binom e{d-u}\right\|_{\Omega^{-1}}^2
=\frac{e^2}{\omega_{11}}
+\frac{\{d-(\omega_{12}/\omega_{11})e\}_{-}^2}
{\omega_{22}-\omega_{12}^2/\omega_{11}}.
\]
Hence \(T_N\) is exactly the estimated covariance-metric distance to
\(\{0\}\times[0,\infty)\).  Residualization makes its two Gaussian limits
independent.  At a fixed interior point the standardized slack diverges to
\(+\infty\), leaving one squared normal.  At the boundary it is centered,
and conditioning on its sign gives the equal mixture of one and two degrees
of freedom.

For uniform size, take an arbitrary null sequence and a convergent parameter
subsequence.  If \(\eta_N\) is bounded, take a further subsequence on which
it converges.  The sequential CLT gives the asserted limit, and
\((z+\eta)^-\le z^-\) for \(\eta\ge0\) shows stochastic domination by the
boundary law.  If \(\eta_N\to\infty\), its negative part vanishes in
probability and \(c_{N,\alpha}\ge c_{1,\alpha}\), so the rejection
probability has upper limit at most \(\alpha\).  The usual
near-maximizing-sequence contradiction proves the displayed supremum bound.
Finally, under the explicit alternative assumptions, the continuous mapping
theorem makes \(N^{-1}T_N\) converge to the strictly positive squared
distance from \(\psi(r)\) to the constraint cone, proving consistency.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Dominated renewal local power]
\label{prop:joint-physical-image-local-power-main}
Fix an exchange-diagonal physical point \(r_\circ\) in
\(\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}\), and let \(g^\circ=(g_k^\circ)_{k\ge0}\) be its
Palm gap law, with mean cycle length \(\mu^\circ\).  For
\(\eta=(\eta_\mu,\eta_0,\eta_1)\), define
\[
g_\eta(k)=g^\circ(k)+v_\eta(k),
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_\eta(0)&=\eta_0,\\
v_\eta(1)&=\eta_1,\\
v_\eta(2)&=-\eta_\mu-3\eta_0-2\eta_1,\\
v_\eta(3)&=\eta_\mu+2\eta_0+\eta_1,\\
v_\eta(k)&=0,\qquad k\ge4.
\end{aligned}
\]
On a sufficiently small fixed neighbourhood of the origin this is a
full-support probability law.  The resulting stationary renewal experiment
is differentiable in quadratic mean and locally asymptotically normal per
unit calendar time, with information
\[
\mathcal I_{ij}
=\frac1{\mu^\circ}\sum_{k=0}^3
\frac{v_i(k)v_j(k)}{g_k^\circ},
\]
where \(v_i\) denotes the coefficient vector of the \(i\)-th component of
\(\eta\).  The matrix \(\mathcal I\) is positive definite.

All first-order directions in three-inclusion space are represented by this
experiment.  Indeed, for any renewal gap law,
\[
r=\left(\frac1\mu,\frac{g_0}{\mu},
\frac{g_1+g_0^2}{\mu}\right),
\]
and the Jacobian with respect to \((\mu,g_0,g_1)\) is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
-\mu^{-2}&0&0\\
-g_0\mu^{-2}&\mu^{-1}&0\\
-(g_1+g_0^2)\mu^{-2}&2g_0\mu^{-1}&\mu^{-1}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det=-\mu^{-4}\ne0.
\]

Let a local sequence in this experiment satisfy
\[
\sqrt N\,e(r_N)\longrightarrow\delta,\qquad
\sqrt N\,\mathcal D(r_N)\longrightarrow\tau.
\]
At \(r_\circ\), write
\[
\Omega=
\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{11}&\omega_{12}\\
\omega_{12}&\omega_{22}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{\omega_{12}}{\omega_{11}},
\qquad
v=\omega_{22}-\frac{\omega_{12}^2}{\omega_{11}},
\]
and put
\[
a_*=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\omega_{11}}},
\qquad
b_*=\frac{\tau-\beta\delta}{\sqrt v}.
\]
For the test in
\Cref{thm:uniform-joint-physical-image-test-main},
\[
T_N\Rightarrow X^2+(Y^-)^2,
\qquad
X\sim N(a_*,1),\quad Y\sim N(b_*,1),
\]
with \(X\) and \(Y\) independent, and the boundary critical value is
selected with probability tending to one.  Hence its complete local-power
function is
\[
\pi_\alpha(\delta,\tau)
=\PP\{X^2+(Y^-)^2>c_{\partial,\alpha}\}.
\]
For deterministic evaluation, define
\[
A(q,a)=\Phi_{\rm N}(\sqrt q-a)-\Phi_{\rm N}(-\sqrt q-a),
\]
where \(\Phi_{\rm N}\) and \(\phi_{\rm N}\) denote the standard normal
distribution and density.  Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_\alpha(\delta,\tau)
=1&-\Phi_{\rm N}(b_*)A(c_{\partial,\alpha},a_*)\\
&-\int_{-\sqrt{c_{\partial,\alpha}}}^{0}
A(c_{\partial,\alpha}-y^2,a_*)\phi_{\rm N}(y-b_*)\,dy .
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, a physical sequence satisfying
\[
N^{1/4}|p_N-s_N|\longrightarrow\xi
\]
has \((\delta,\tau)=(0,\xi^2)\), so
\(\pi_\alpha(0,\xi^2)\le\alpha\).  Conversely, for every \(\xi>0\), the
dominated renewal experiment contains a nonreal-root-side sequence with
\((\delta,\tau)=(0,-\xi^2)\), and
\[
\pi_\alpha(0,-\xi^2)>\alpha.
\]
The restriction \(\xi>0\) is essential; at \(\xi=0\) the power equals
\(\alpha\).

Finally, the null tangent cone and its first-order indistinguishable
subspace are
\[
T_{H_0}(r_\circ)
=\{h:De(r_\circ)h=0,\ D\mathcal D(r_\circ)h\ge0\},
\]
\[
\ker D\psi(r_\circ)
=\{h:De(r_\circ)h=0,\ D\mathcal D(r_\circ)h=0\}.
\]
Thus a direction with \(De\,h=0\) and \(D\mathcal D\,h>0\) is a physical
interior direction whose root separation occurs at the
\(N^{-1/4}\) scale; it is not model misspecification.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed coefficients satisfy
\[
\sum_kv_\eta(k)=0,\qquad
\sum_k(k+1)v_\eta(k)=\eta_\mu,
\]
and change \(g_0,g_1\) by \(\eta_0,\eta_1\), respectively.  On the compact
physical set, \(g_k^\circ\) is uniformly positive for \(0\le k\le3\);
hence sufficiently small perturbations remain probability laws dominated by
counting measure.  The one-cycle score is \(v_i(k)/g_k^\circ\).  The three
coefficient vectors are linearly independent, which proves positive
definiteness of \(\mathcal I\).  A Taylor expansion of the finite changed
part of the one-cycle log likelihood gives quadratic-mean differentiability.
The initial and terminal incomplete cycles contribute \(o_{\PP}(1)\) to the
local log-likelihood ratio, while the number of complete cycles divided by
\(N\) converges to \(1/\mu^\circ\).  The independent-cycle score CLT
therefore proves the stated calendar-time LAN expansion.  This is the
finite-support regenerative specialization of standard LAN theory
\cite{vanDerVaart1998AsymptoticStatistics}.

The three moment identities and the nonzero Jacobian determinant prove local
surjectivity onto inclusion space.  The rank-two constraint conclusion in
\Cref{prop:analytic-joint-image-chart-main} then makes every pair
\((\delta,\tau)\) locally attainable.

Under a \(N^{-1/2}\) local perturbation, the uniform studentization in
\Cref{thm:uniform-joint-physical-image-test-main} and the third lemma give
two residualized independent normals with the displayed means.  Since
\(Z_{d,N}=O_{\PP}(1)\), the boundary critical value is selected.  To compute
the acceptance probability, condition first on \(Y\ge0\), and then integrate
over \(-\sqrt{c_{\partial,\alpha}}\le Y<0\); this gives the one-dimensional
integral.

For a physical root pair,
\(\mathcal D(r_N)=(p_N-s_N)^2\) and \(e(r_N)=0\), proving the physical-side
claim.  On the nonreal side, \(b_*<0\) for \(\xi>0\).  Coupling
\(Y=Z+b_*\) to the boundary variable \(Z\) shows that \(Y^-\) strictly
stochastically exceeds \(Z^-\) on a set of positive probability, proving
strictly greater power.  At \(\xi=0\) the laws coincide.  The tangent-cone
formulas follow from the rank-two constraint qualification and the local
description \(e=0,\mathcal D\ge0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{paperthm}[Single-record complete visible-law specification test]
\label{thm:complete-visible-law-specification-main}
Fix $0<\varepsilon<M<\infty$, put

\[
\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}=[\varepsilon,M]^2,
\qquad \mathcal P_0=\{P_\theta:\theta\in\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}\},
\]

where $P_\theta$ is the stationary visible law of the sampled-counter kernels
at the dimensionless parameter $\theta=(x,y)$.  For a binary word
$w=(w_1,\ldots,w_m)$, write

\[
P_\theta[w]=\pi_\theta T_{w_1}(\theta)\cdots
T_{w_m}(\theta)\mathbf1,
\]

where $\pi_\theta$ is stationary for $T_0(\theta)+T_1(\theta)$.  On
stationary binary laws define

\[
d(P,Q)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}4^{-m}
\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^m}|P[w]-Q[w]|,
\qquad
d(P,\mathcal P_0)=\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}}d(P,P_\theta).
\]

For a record $A_0,\ldots,A_{N-1}$ with $N\ge3$, let

\[
\widehat P_N[w]=\frac1{N-m+1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-m}
\mathbf1_{\{A_j\cdots A_{j+m-1}=w\}},
\qquad
L_N=\max\left\{3,\left\lfloor\frac18\log_2N\right\rfloor\right\},
\]

and set

\[
d_N=\min_{\theta\in\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}}
\sum_{m=1}^{L_N}4^{-m}\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^m}
|\widehat P_N[w]-P_\theta[w]|.
\]

Choose a minimizer $\widehat\theta_N$ by a fixed lexicographic rule.  Delete
the initial and terminal incomplete cycles and denote the remaining complete
zero gaps by $G_1,\ldots,G_{K_N}$.  If $g_\theta$ is the model Palm gap
mass function, define

\[
h_\theta(k)=\mathbf1_{\{k=1\}}
-\frac{g_\theta(1)}{g_\theta(2)}\mathbf1_{\{k=2\}}.
\]

When $K_N\ge3$, put

\[
\widehat\nu_N=\frac1{K_N}\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}
h_{\widehat\theta_N}(G_j)^2,
\qquad
\widehat I_N=\frac{K_N}{N}\widehat\nu_N^2,
\]

\[
Z_N=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\widehat I_N}}
\sum_{j=1}^{K_N-1}
h_{\widehat\theta_N}(G_j)h_{\widehat\theta_N}(G_{j+1}).
\]

If $K_N<3$ or $\widehat I_N=0$, set $Z_N=0$.  Thus, for every binary
record of length $N\ge3$, the test

\[
\varphi_N=\mathbf1_{\{d_N>N^{-1/4}\ \text{or}\ Z_N>z_{1-\alpha}\}}
\tag{F}
\]

is defined without empirical roots, logarithms, or evaluations of the analytic
root chart outside its domain.  It has the following properties.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Its null size is compact-uniform:
\[
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}}
\left|\PP_\theta(\varphi_N=1)-\alpha\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
This includes the exchange diagonal and every null triangular array, in
particular arrays satisfying $N^{1/4}|p_N-s_N|=O(1)$.

\item If $P$ is a fixed stationary ergodic binary law and
$d(P,\mathcal P_0)>0$, then
\[
\PP_P(\varphi_N=1)\longrightarrow1.
\]
The convergence is uniform on any class with a common geometric
$\alpha$-mixing envelope $\alpha_P(k)\le Cq^k$, $q<1$, and a common
separation $d(P,\mathcal P_0)\ge\delta>0$.  No consistency assertion is made
for arbitrary stationary nonergodic mixtures.

\item Fix an interior exchange point $\theta_0=(x_0,x_0)$, and abbreviate
$g_k=g_{\theta_0}(k)$, $\mu=\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)g_k$, and
\[
h(k)=\mathbf1_{\{k=1\}}-\frac{g_1}{g_2}\mathbf1_{\{k=2\}}.
\]
For $0\le\eta<g_2/g_1$, the transition matrix
\[
Q_\eta(\ell\mid k)=g_\ell\{1+\eta h(k)h(\ell)\}
\tag{G}
\]
is stochastic, has stationary marginal $g$, and defines by Palm inversion a
stationary nonrenewal visible law when $\eta>0$.  This law preserves exactly
the sampled-counter coordinates $r_0,r_1,r_2$, because
\[
r_0=\mu^{-1},\qquad r_1=\mu^{-1}g_0,
\qquad r_2=\mu^{-1}\{g_1+g_0Q_\eta(0\mid0)\},
\]
and $Q_\eta(0\mid0)=g_0$.  Nevertheless,
\[
\PP_\eta(10101)=\mu^{-1}g_1^2(1+\eta),
\]
so a length-five word separates every $\eta>0$ from the renewal null.

\item Put
\[
\nu=\EE_g h(G)^2=g_1+\frac{g_1^2}{g_2},
\qquad \mathcal I=\frac{\nu^2}{\mu}.
\]
Along $\eta_N=t/\sqrt N$, $t\ge0$, the stationary record experiments in
part~\textup{(iii)} satisfy
\[
\log\frac{dP_{\eta_N}^{(N)}}{dP_0^{(N)}}
=t\Delta_N-\frac12t^2\mathcal I+o_{P_0}(1),
\qquad \Delta_N\Rightarrow N(0,\mathcal I).
\]
Moreover, $\widehat I_N\to_P\mathcal I$ and
\[
Z_N\Rightarrow N(t\sqrt{\mathcal I},1),
\qquad
\pi_\alpha(t)=1-\Phi_{\rm N}
\{z_{1-\alpha}-t\sqrt{\mathcal I}\}.
\]
Thus $\pi_\alpha(t)>\alpha$ for every $t>0$.  If
$\eta_N=o(N^{-1/2})$, the Kullback--Leibler divergence tends to zero, while
the score component of \textup{(F)} attains the Neyman--Pearson power envelope
of this one-dimensional Gaussian-shift subexperiment at the $N^{-1/2}$
rate.
\end{enumerate}

The distributional-distance guard in parts~\textup{(i)}--\textup{(ii)} is a
specialization of established stationary-ergodic testing methods
\cite{RyabkoRyabko2010ErgodicInference,Ryabko2012StationaryTesting}.  Generic
LAN and Gaussian-shift optimality are also standard
\cite{vanDerVaart1998AsymptoticStatistics}.  The model-specific assertions are
the exchange-uniform finite-range score, the exactly
$(r_0,r_1,r_2)$-preserving alternative \textup{(G)}, and their joint use with
the complete visible-law guard.
\end{paperthm}

\begin{proof}
On $\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}$, the hidden transition matrix is
\[
T_0+T_1=\begin{pmatrix}p&b\\1-p&1-b\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Each entry has a positive parameter-independent lower bound.  The hidden chain
therefore satisfies a uniform Doeblin condition, and every bounded function of
the visible record has a common geometric mixing envelope.  For a word $w$
of length $m\le N$, overlap for lags below $m$ and the mixing covariance
bound above that lag give
\[
\sup_\theta\operatorname{Var}_\theta\{\widehat P_N[w]\}
\le \frac{Cm}{N}.
\]
Consequently, by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\sup_\theta\EE_\theta
\sum_{m=1}^{L_N}4^{-m}\sum_w
|\widehat P_N[w]-P_\theta[w]|
\le \frac{C'}{\sqrt N}
\sum_{m\ge1}2^{-m}\sqrt m=O(N^{-1/2}).
\]
Since the minimum defining $d_N$ is no larger than its value at the true
parameter, Markov's inequality yields
\[
\sup_\theta\PP_\theta(d_N>N^{-1/4})=O(N^{-1/4}).
\tag{1}
\]

Let $\widehat\sigma_N=(\widehat p_N+\widehat s_N,
\widehat p_N\widehat s_N)$ and $\sigma_\theta=(p+s,ps)$.  The terms of
length at most three in the distance contain the three inclusion coordinates
$r_0,r_1,r_2$: $r_0=P[1]$, $r_1=P[11]$, and
$r_2=P[101]+P[111]$.  Optimality of $\widehat\theta_N$, the preceding
bound for these finitely many word frequencies, and the uniformly bounded
derivative of the quotient map $\Phi$ on
$\Theta_{\varepsilon,M}$ therefore give
\[
\widehat\sigma_N-\sigma_\theta=O_{P_\theta}(N^{-1/2})
\tag{2}
\]
uniformly.  No ordered-root map is used in this step.  For every fixed gap
index, $g_\theta(k)$ is a symmetric analytic function of the two roots;
compactness and $\inf_\theta g_\theta(2)>0$ make $h_\theta$ and its first
two $\sigma$-derivatives uniformly bounded.

Under the null, the complete Palm gaps are consecutive members of an i.i.d.
gap sequence.  With
\[
q_j(\theta)=h_\theta(G_j)h_\theta(G_{j+1}),
\]
one has
\[
\EE_\theta q_j=0,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\theta(q_j)=\nu_\theta^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}_\theta(q_j,q_{j+1})=0,
\]
where $\nu_\theta=\EE_\theta h_\theta(G)^2$.  These rewards are bounded and
one-dependent, $K_N/N\to1/\mu_\theta$, and the initial and terminal deletions
affect only $o_P(\sqrt N)$ reward.  The uniform one-dependent CLT and the
renewal-index replacement thus give
\[
\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{K_N-1}q_j(\theta)}
{\sqrt{K_N}\nu_\theta}\Rightarrow N(0,1)
\]
uniformly in $\theta$.  The plug-in has no first-order effect: independence
and $\EE_\theta h_\theta(G)=0$ imply
\[
\EE_\theta\left[
\partial_\sigma\{h_\sigma(G_0)h_\sigma(G_1)\}
\right]_{\sigma=\sigma_\theta}=0.
\]
Hence the sum of the first derivatives is $O_P(\sqrt N)$; multiplying by
$(2)$, and bounding the summed Taylor remainder by
$O_P(N\|\widehat\sigma_N-\sigma_\theta\|^2)$, shows that both terms are
$o_P(\sqrt N)$.  The same bounds give
$\widehat I_N\to_P\nu_\theta^2/\mu_\theta$, uniformly.  Together with
$(1)$, this proves part~\textup{(i)}.

For part~\textup{(ii)}, Birkhoff's theorem gives almost-sure convergence of
every fixed empirical word probability under a stationary ergodic law.  For
every $L$, the finitely many terms with $m\le L$ converge uniformly in
$\theta$ by continuity and compactness, while, for all probability laws,
\[
\sum_{m>L}4^{-m}\sum_w|P[w]-Q[w]|\le2^{1-L}.
\]
It follows that $d_N\to d(P,\mathcal P_0)$ almost surely.  Under a common
geometric mixing envelope the preceding variance calculation is uniform over
the alternative class, which proves the stated uniform version.

For part~\textup{(iii)}, $\EE_g h=0$ gives both
$\sum_\ell Q_\eta(\ell\mid k)=1$ and $gQ_\eta=g$.  The only negative
product among nonzero score values is
$h(1)h(2)=-g_1/g_2$; hence $0\le\eta<g_2/g_1$ gives a nonnegative kernel.
Its rows depend on $k$ when $\eta>0$, so the stationary Palm gap sequence
is not i.i.d.  The three inclusion identities follow by enumerating a click at
lags zero, one, and two, and $h(0)=0$ gives
$Q_\eta(0\mid0)=g_0$.  Two consecutive gaps equal to one produce the word
$10101$, and
\[
\mu^{-1}g_1Q_\eta(1\mid1)
=\mu^{-1}g_1^2(1+\eta),
\]
which proves the separation assertion.

Finally, apart from $O_P(1)$ endpoint terms, the log likelihood ratio along
$\eta_N=t/\sqrt N$ is
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{K_N-1}\log\{1+\eta_Nh(G_j)h(G_{j+1})\}.
\]
The score is bounded.  Taylor expansion, the one-dependent CLT, and
$K_N/N\to1/\mu$ give the displayed LAN expansion with
$\mathcal I=\nu^2/\mu$.  The score is orthogonal to every renewal marginal
score $s(G_j)$, since
\[
\EE\{h(G_j)h(G_{j+1})s(G_j)\}
=\EE\{h(G_j)s(G_j)\}\EE h(G_{j+1})=0,
\]
and similarly at the other endpoint.  Thus estimating the symmetric renewal
nuisance coordinates causes no information loss, including at the exchange
boundary.  Under the local alternative, $d_N=O_P(N^{-1/2})$, so the guard in
\textup{(F)} is inactive with probability tending to one.  Le Cam's third
lemma gives the stated normal shift and power.  The local divergence is
$N\eta_N^2\mathcal I/2+o(N\eta_N^2)$; contiguity below $N^{-1/2}$ and the
Gaussian-shift Neyman--Pearson bound complete part~\textup{(iv)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete Markov--Palm tangent space and pointwise omnibus score]
\label{thm:complete-markov-palm-tangent-omnibus-main}
Fix $0<\alpha<1$, an interior exchange point $\theta_0=(x_0,x_0)$, and let
$g=(g_k)_{k\geq0}$ be its Palm zero-gap law.  Thus $g_k>0$,
$\mu=\sum_{k\geq0}(k+1)g_k<\infty$, and, for this sampled-counter law,
$g$ has an exponential tail.  Put
\[
 \mathsf H=L^2(g\otimes g),\qquad
 \mathsf H_{\rm int}=\{q\in\mathsf H:
 \EE(q\mid G_0)=\EE(q\mid G_1)=0\},
\]
where $G_0,G_1$ are independent with law $g$, and define
\[
 \mathsf T=\{q\in\mathsf H_{\rm int}:q(0,0)=0\}.
 \tag{H}
\]
Consider all quadratic-mean differentiable Markov-gap paths
\[
 Q_t(\ell\mid k)=g_\ell w_t(k,\ell),\qquad w_0=1,
 \qquad \left\|\frac{2(\sqrt{w_t}-1)}t-q\right\|_{\mathsf H}
 \longrightarrow0,
\tag{I}
\]
whose rows sum to one, for which $gQ_t=g$, and which preserve
$(r_0,r_1,r_2)$ to first order.  Then the following statements hold.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item The closed tangent space of these paths is exactly $\mathsf T$; in
particular it is infinite dimensional.  If
\[
 u_0(k)=\mathbf1_{\{k=0\}}-g_0,\qquad
 v_0(k,\ell)=u_0(k)u_0(\ell),
\]
then
\[
 \mathsf T=\mathsf H_{\rm int}\ominus\operatorname{span}\{v_0\}.
 \tag{J}
\]
Every $q\in\mathsf T$ is realized by a two-sided path in \textup{(I)} whose
kernels satisfy a Doeblin minorization by $(1-o(1))g$ and preserve $g$ and
all three inclusion coordinates exactly.  A nonzero such path is nonrenewal
for all sufficiently small nonzero $t$.

\item Every adjacent-pair score generated by a quadratic-mean differentiable
renewal marginal path belongs to the closed span of
$s(G_0)+s(G_1)$, with $s\in L^2_0(g)$.  Hence the sampled-counter renewal
nuisance space is orthogonal to $\mathsf T$.  On the ambient space
$\mathsf H$, the efficient information operator for the Markov interaction
is
\[
 \mathcal I_{\rm eff}=\mu^{-1}\Pi_{\mathsf T},
 \tag{K}
\]
where
\[
 \Pi_{\mathsf T}f=f-\EE(f\mid G_0)-\EE(f\mid G_1)+\EE f
 -\frac{\langle f,v_0\rangle_{\mathsf H}}
 {\|v_0\|_{\mathsf H}^2}v_0.
 \tag{L}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \ker\mathcal I_{\rm eff}
 =\mathsf H_{\rm int}^{\perp}\oplus\operatorname{span}\{v_0\},
 \qquad
 \left.\mathcal I_{\rm eff}\right|_{\mathsf T}
 =\mu^{-1}I_{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{M}
\]

\item Delete the two incomplete endpoint cycles from a stationary record and
write its complete gaps as $G_1,\ldots,G_{K_N}$, where
$K_N/N\to_P\mu^{-1}$.  For $q\in\mathsf T$, let
\[
 \Delta_N(q)=N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{K_N-1}q(G_i,G_{i+1}).
\]
For each fixed finite collection $q_1,\ldots,q_d\in\mathsf T$, the null
joint limit is centred Gaussian with covariance
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{Cov}
 \{\Delta_N(q_a),\Delta_N(q_b)\}
 =\mu^{-1}\langle q_a,q_b\rangle_{\mathsf H}.
 \tag{N}
\]
Along the realizing paths constructed in part~\textup{(i)}, or any other
such path whose retained-record endpoint log likelihood is $o_{P_0}(1)$,
the score $q$, evaluated at $t_N=N^{-1/2}$, satisfies
\[
 \log\frac{dP_{N,q}}{dP_{N,0}}
 =\Delta_N(q)-\frac1{2\mu}\|q\|_{\mathsf H}^2+o_{P_0}(1).
 \tag{O}
\]
Thus the limit is the isonormal process on $\mathsf T$ with covariance
\textup{(N)}, rather than a $\mathsf T$-valued normal random element with
identity covariance.  No uniform-LAN assertion over arbitrary norm-compact
subsets of $\mathsf T$ is made.

\item Put
\[
 e_0(k)=\frac{\mathbf1_{\{k=0\}}-g_0}{\sqrt{g_0(1-g_0)}}
\]
and choose a bounded orthonormal basis $e_1,e_2,\ldots$ of
$L^2_0(g)\cap\{e_0\}^{\perp}$.  Enumerate
\[
 \{e_a\otimes e_b:a,b\geq0,\ (a,b)\neq(0,0)\}
 =\{\phi_1,\phi_2,\ldots\};
\]
this is an orthonormal basis of $\mathsf T$.  Fix strictly positive weights
$\lambda_j$ satisfying
\[
 \sum_{j\geq1}\lambda_j<\infty,
 \qquad
 \sum_{j\geq1}\lambda_j\|\phi_j\|_\infty^2<\infty,
 \tag{P}
\]
and any deterministic $J_N\uparrow\infty$.  Put
\[
 \widehat\mu_N=
 \begin{cases}
 K_N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{K_N}(G_i+1),&K_N\geq1,\\
 1,&K_N=0,
 \end{cases}
\]
and define, when $K_N\geq2$,
\[
 Z_{j,N}=\sqrt{\frac{\widehat\mu_N}{N}}
 \sum_{i=1}^{K_N-1}\phi_j(G_i,G_{i+1}),\qquad
 Q_N=\sum_{j=1}^{J_N}\lambda_jZ_{j,N}^2,
 \tag{Q}
\]
and put $Q_N=0$ otherwise.  If $\zeta_j$ are independent standard normals,
then
\[
 Q_N\Rightarrow Q_0:=\sum_{j\geq1}\lambda_j\zeta_j^2.
 \tag{R}
\]
Let $c_{\alpha,\lambda}$ be the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of $Q_0$.  Under the
local direction $q=\sum_jq_j\phi_j\in\mathsf T$ along one of the paths
admitted in \textup{(O)},
\[
 Q_N\Rightarrow
 \sum_{j\geq1}\lambda_j
 \left(\zeta_j+\frac{q_j}{\sqrt\mu}\right)^2.
 \tag{S}
\]
Consequently the test
$\psi_N=\mathbf1_{\{Q_N>c_{\alpha,\lambda}\}}$ has limiting power strictly
larger than $\alpha$ for every fixed $q\in\mathsf T\setminus\{0\}$.

\item The null calibration in \textup{(R)} is stable along the exchange
boundary in the following explicit local sense.  Let
$\sigma=(p+s,ps)$ be the symmetric sampled-counter coordinate and, for fixed
$C<\infty$, let $\sigma_N$ range over null sequences satisfying
\[
 \|\sqrt N(\sigma_N-\sigma_0)\|\leq C.
 \tag{T}
\]
Use in \textup{(Q)} the basis fixed at $\sigma_0$ and the displayed
$\widehat\mu_N$.  Then
\[
 \sup_{\sigma_N:\,\textup{(T)}}
 \left|\PP_{\sigma_N}(Q_N>c_{\alpha,\lambda})-\alpha\right|
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{U}
\]
This class includes exchange-root splittings
$N^{1/4}|p_N-s_N|=O(1)$ provided their symmetric coordinates obey
\textup{(T)}.
\end{enumerate}

The algebraic assertions in parts~\textup{(i)}--\textup{(ii)} use only full
support of $g$, finite $\mu$, and square-integrability of each local score.
For the explicit realizing paths, the uniform density-ratio bound controls
the endpoint terms in parts~\textup{(iii)}--\textup{(iv)}.  The
sampled-counter exponential tail and analytic dependence on $\sigma$ are used
in part~\textup{(v)} for its uniform triangular-array statement.  No claim
that finite mean alone controls the endpoints of every quadratic-mean
differentiable stationary-record path is made.

Generic basis tests of serial independence, Hilbert-space central limit
theorems, and Gaussian-shift arguments are established methods
\cite{SkaugTjostheim1993SerialIndependence,
DedeckerMerlevede2003HilbertCLT,vanDerVaart1998AsymptoticStatistics}.
The assertions here are restricted to the sampled-counter Markov--Palm
interaction: the additional atom constraint in \textup{(H)}, its information
projection, and the exchange-boundary calibration of \textup{(Q)}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The row and stationary-marginal identities for \textup{(I)} are
\[
 \sum_\ell g_\ell w_t(k,\ell)=1,
 \qquad
 \sum_k g_kw_t(k,\ell)=1.
\]
Differentiating in quadratic mean gives the two conditional-mean constraints
in \textup{(H)}.  Fixed $g$ fixes $r_0=\mu^{-1}$ and
$r_1=\mu^{-1}g_0$, whereas a stationary Markov gap law satisfies
\[
 r_2=\mu^{-1}\{g_1+g_0Q_t(0\mid0)\}.
\]
Since point evaluation at $(0,0)$ is continuous in $\mathsf H$,
$Q_t(0\mid0)=g_0\{1+tq(0,0)+o(t)\}$; hence preservation of $r_2$ to first
order is equivalent to $q(0,0)=0$.

For $q\in\mathsf H_{\rm int}$, direct expansion and double centring give
\[
 \langle q,v_0\rangle_{\mathsf H}=g_0^2q(0,0),
\]
which proves \textup{(J)} and \textup{(L)}.  To prove realizability, truncate
$q$ at levels $m$ and write
$q_m=\Pi_{\mathsf T}\{(-m)\vee(q\wedge m)\}$.  Then $q_m$ is bounded and
$q_m\to q$ in $\mathsf H$.  Choose $m(t)\uparrow\infty$ so slowly that
$|t|\|q_{m(t)}\|_\infty\to0$ and set
$w_t=1+tq_{m(t)}$.  Its weighted row and column sums are exactly one and
$w_t(0,0)=1$; hence all three inclusion coordinates are exact.  Moreover,
$Q_t(\ell\mid k)\geq\{1-o(1)\}g_\ell$, proving the Doeblin assertion.  If
a nonzero score generated a renewal kernel, it would have the form
$a(\ell)$; the two centring identities would then force $a=0$.
The inequality
\[
 \left|2\{\sqrt{1+x}-1\}-x\right|\leq Cx^2,
 \qquad |x|\leq\tfrac12,
\]
together with $|t|\|q_{m(t)}\|_\infty\to0$ proves the quadratic-mean
differentiability in \textup{(I)}.

The Hoeffding row--column decomposition of $L^2(g\otimes g)$ is
\[
 \mathbb R\oplus L^2_0(G_0)\oplus L^2_0(G_1)
 \oplus\mathsf H_{\rm int}.
\]
A renewal marginal perturbation contributes only its one-gap score at the two
ends of an adjacent pair.  It is therefore orthogonal to $\mathsf T$.
Under the null, successive interaction rewards have zero lag-one covariance:
\[
 \EE\{q_a(G_0,G_1)q_b(G_1,G_2)\}=0.
\]
The number of complete cycles per calendar bin tends to $\mu^{-1}$, so the
information quadratic form is $\mu^{-1}\|q\|_{\mathsf H}^2$.  Projection
onto the admissible interaction scores proves \textup{(K)}--\textup{(M)}.

For each fixed finite collection of scores, the one-dependent central limit
theorem and random-index replacement prove \textup{(N)}.  For the realizing
paths in part~\textup{(i)}, the bound
$|t|\|q_{m(t)}\|_\infty\to0$ also makes the endpoint log likelihood
negligible.  Quadratic-mean differentiability then gives \textup{(O)}; the
same conclusion holds under the alternative endpoint hypothesis stated in
part~\textup{(iii)}.  This proves the finite-dimensional local experiment.
Identity covariance on the
infinite-dimensional space is not trace class, so the limit can only be
interpreted cylindrically as the displayed isonormal process.

The normalized $e_0$ is proportional to $u_0$.  Tensoring an orthonormal
basis of $L^2_0(g)$ gives an orthonormal basis of
$\mathsf H_{\rm int}$, and deleting $e_0\otimes e_0$ imposes \textup{(J)}.
Condition \textup{(P)} makes
\[
 \bigl(\sqrt{\lambda_j}\phi_j(G_i,G_{i+1})\bigr)_{j\geq1}
\]
a square-integrable, one-dependent $\ell^2$-valued random variable.  Its
lag-one covariance vanishes by double centring.  The Hilbert-space central
limit theorem, random-index replacement, and truncation in $J_N$ therefore
give \textup{(R)}.  Joint convergence with the likelihood score and the third
lemma give \textup{(S)}.  If $q\neq0$, some $q_j\neq0$; for every positive
threshold the square of a normal with nonzero mean strictly stochastically
dominates a central normal square.  Conditioning on the remaining independent
coordinates proves strict power because every $\lambda_j$ is positive.

It remains to prove the uniform calibration in \textup{(U)}.  We give the
truncation argument because covariance convergence alone is not a
triangular-array Hilbert-space CLT.  Write
\[
 g_N=g_{\sigma_N},\qquad \delta_N=g_N-g .
\]
Analyticity in the symmetric coordinate and the locally uniform exponential
tail give, uniformly under \textup{(T)},
\[
 \|\delta_N\|_1=O(N^{-1/2}),\qquad
 \mu_N-\mu=O(N^{-1/2}),
\tag{U1}
\]
together with a common exponential moment for the gap laws \(g_N\).
Introduce the weighted feature
\[
 \Psi(k,\ell)=
 \bigl(\sqrt{\lambda_j}\phi_j(k,\ell)\bigr)_{j\ge1}\in\ell^2,
\qquad
 B_\lambda^2=\sum_{j\ge1}\lambda_j\|\phi_j\|_\infty^2<\infty .
\tag{U2}
\]
Thus \(\|\Psi(k,\ell)\|_{\ell^2}\le B_\lambda\) for every pair.  Since every
\(\phi_j\) is double centred under \(g\),
\[
\begin{split}
 m_N:=\EE_{g_N\otimes g_N}\Psi
 &=\sum_{j\ge1}\sqrt{\lambda_j}
 \langle\phi_j,\delta_N\otimes\delta_N\rangle\,\mathbf e_j,\\
 \|m_N\|_{\ell^2}
 &\le B_\lambda\|\delta_N\|_1^2=O(N^{-1}).
\end{split}
\tag{U3}
\]
This displays, rather than assumes, the cancellation of the two first-order
marginal terms.

Fix \(J\).  The first \(J\) coordinates of
\(\Psi(G_i,G_{i+1})-m_N\) form a uniformly bounded one-dependent triangular
array.  Its lag-zero covariance tends to
\(\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_J)\), while its two lag-one
covariances tend to zero: at \(g\) they vanish exactly by double centring, and
\textup{(U1)} controls the perturbation.  The fixed-dimensional
one-dependent triangular-array CLT therefore gives
\[
 (Z_{1,N},\ldots,Z_{J,N})
 \Longrightarrow(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_J)
\tag{U4}
\]
uniformly under \textup{(T)}.  For completeness, uniformity follows by the
subsequence criterion: from every sequence satisfying \textup{(T)}, extract
a convergent subsequence of
\(\sqrt N(\sigma_N-\sigma_0)\); boundedness gives Lindeberg, \textup{(U1)}
gives the covariance limit, and the usual even--odd blocking proves the
one-dependent CLT.  The uniform renewal law of large numbers replaces the
deterministic number of pairs by \(K_N-1\), and
\(\widehat\mu_N-\mu_N=o_P(1)\).  These replacements are uniform because the
common exponential moment controls both endpoint cycles and the renewal-count
deviation.  The mean contribution in \textup{(U3)} is
\(O(\sqrt N\,\|m_N\|_{\ell^2})=o(1)\).

The passage from fixed \(J\) to the full statistic is also uniform.  Put
\[
 L_J=\sum_{j>J}\lambda_j\|\phi_j\|_\infty^2\longrightarrow0 .
\]
The even--odd maximal inequality for the centred one-dependent tail feature,
followed by the renewal-count bound \(K_N\le N\), gives
\[
 \sup_{\sigma_N:\,\textup{(T)}}
 \EE_{\sigma_N}\!\left[
 \sum_{j>J}\lambda_jZ_{j,N}^2;\,
 K_N\ge2\right]
 \le C L_J+o(1).
\tag{U5}
\]
Here the \(o(1)\) contains the squared mean from \textup{(U3)} and the event
on which \(\widehat\mu_N\) is outside a fixed compact interval.  The latter
event is exponentially small by the common exponential renewal bound; on its
complement \(\widehat\mu_N\) is uniformly bounded.  This proves
\textup{(U5)} without treating the random number of overlapping pairs as a
conditionally binomial count.

For the limit variable,
\[
 \EE\sum_{j>J}\lambda_j\zeta_j^2
 =\sum_{j>J}\lambda_j\longrightarrow0.
\tag{U6}
\]
Equations \textup{(U4)}--\textup{(U6)} and Markov's inequality give uniform
bounded-Lipschitz convergence of \(Q_N\) to
\(Q_0=\sum_j\lambda_j\zeta_j^2\).  The law of \(Q_0\) is continuous because
\(\lambda_1>0\), so evaluation at \(c_{\alpha,\lambda}\) proves
\textup{(U)}.  Finally,
\[
 (p_N+s_N,p_Ns_N)
 =(2z,z^2-h^2N^{-1/2}),
\]
so the stated \(N^{-1/4}\) root-splitting sequences satisfy \textup{(T)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sharp local-null Helmert coupling boundary]
\label{prop:helmert-growing-layer-sharp-main}
Retain the exchange-point notation of
\Cref{thm:complete-markov-palm-tangent-omnibus-main}, put
$p_0=e^{-x_0}$, and write
\[
 S_j=\PP_g(G\geq j)=p_0^j(1+x_0j),\qquad j\geq0.
\]
For $j\geq0$ define the weighted Helmert contrast
\[
 e_j(k)=
 \sqrt{\frac{S_{j+1}}{g_jS_j}}\,\mathbf1_{\{k=j\}}
 -\sqrt{\frac{g_j}{S_jS_{j+1}}}\,\mathbf1_{\{k\geq j+1\}}.
 \tag{V}
\]
Thus $e_0$ is the contrast used in
\Cref{thm:complete-markov-palm-tangent-omnibus-main}, and
$e_0,e_1,\ldots$ is an orthonormal basis of $L^2_0(g)$.  For $J\geq1$ let
\[
 \boldsymbol\Phi_J=
 \bigl(e_a\otimes e_b:0\leq a,b\leq J,\ (a,b)\neq(0,0)\bigr),
 \qquad d_J=(J+1)^2-1,
 \tag{W}
\]
in any fixed order, and let $\mathbf Z_{J,N}$ be the corresponding vector of
scores in \textup{(Q)}, with the basis in \textup{(V)} fixed at $\theta_0$.
Set $n_N=N/\mu$, fix $C<\infty$, and let $\mathcal H_N(C)$ be the physical
sampled-counter null parameters $\sigma$ satisfying
$\|\sqrt N(\sigma-\sigma_0)\|\leq C$, as in \textup{(T)}.  If
$J_N\to\infty$, the following condition is necessary and sufficient:
\[
 n_NS_{J_N}^2\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{X}
\]
More precisely, \textup{(X)} is equivalent to the existence, on extensions of
the probability spaces, of
$\boldsymbol\xi_{N,\sigma}\sim\mathcal N(0,I_{d_{J_N}})$ such that, for every
$\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 \sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal H_N(C)}
 \PP_\sigma\!\left(
  \|\mathbf Z_{J_N,N}-\boldsymbol\xi_{N,\sigma}\|_2>\varepsilon
 \right)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Y}
\]
In particular, the same equivalence holds at the fixed exchange point
$\sigma_0$.

Replacing the deterministic cycle count by the
calendar stopping index and replacing $\mu$ by $\widehat\mu_N$ contribute,
respectively,
\[
 O_P(J_NN^{-1/4})\quad\hbox{and}\quad O_P(J_NN^{-1/2})
 \tag{Z}
\]
in Euclidean norm.  The local null covariance perturbation contributes
$O_P(J_N^3N^{-1/2})$; all three terms are uniformly $o_P(1)$ under
\textup{(X)}.

The necessity in \textup{(X)} already follows from the fact that a standard
Gaussian limit for the moving corner coordinate
$e_{J_N}\otimes e_{J_N}$ requires
\[
 n_NS_{J_N}^2\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{AA}
\]
More strongly, if some $r_N<J_N$ satisfies
$n_NS_{r_N}^2\to0$ and $J_N-r_N\asymp J_N$, then, with probability tending
to one, every score in the block
\[
 \{e_a\otimes e_b:r_N\leq a,b\leq J_N\}
 \tag{AB}
\]
is zero.  The corresponding standard Gaussian block has
$(J_N-r_N+1)^2$ degrees of freedom, so neither an unweighted chi-square
approximation for that block nor an $o_P(1)$ Euclidean coupling can hold.

In the second-order band
$2x_0J_N=\log n_N+O(\log\log n_N)$, put
\[
 \omega_N=\log n_N+2\log\log n_N-2x_0J_N.
\]
Then \textup{(Y)} holds if and only if $\omega_N\to\infty$.  Equivalently,
the sharp second-order region is
\[
 J_N\leq
 \frac{\log n_N+2\log\log n_N-\omega_N}{2x_0}
 \tag{AC}
\]
for some $\omega_N\to\infty$.
If
$2x_0J_N=\log n_N+2\log\log n_N-c+o(1)$ for a fixed $c\in\mathbb R$, then
$n_NS_{J_N}^2\to e^c/4$, so \textup{(Y)} fails.  The previously verified
necessary boundary is therefore also sufficient; no assertion of Le Cam
equivalence is made.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The equal-rate dictionary gives the displayed formula for $S_j$.
The two values in \textup{(V)} have weighted mean zero and weighted squared
norm one.  Nested supports then give
$\langle e_j,e_k\rangle_{L^2(g)}=0$ for $j\neq k$; completeness follows by
successively separating the atoms $0,1,\ldots$ from the remaining tail.
Since $S_{j+1}/S_j\to p_0$ and $g_j/S_j\to1-p_0$,
\[
 \|e_j\|_\infty\asymp S_j^{-1/2},\qquad
 \max_{a,b\leq J}\|e_a\otimes e_b\|_\infty\asymp S_J^{-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]

For the deterministic-cycle argument, let
$K_J(k)=\sum_{a=0}^Je_a(k)^2$.  Adding the constant function to the Helmert
contrasts gives the projection onto the partition
$\{0\},\ldots,\{J\},\{J+1,J+2,\ldots\}$; hence
\[
 1+K_J(k)=
 \begin{cases}
  g_k^{-1},&0\leq k\leq J,\\
  S_{J+1}^{-1},&k\geq J+1.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{2}
\]
The equal-rate exponential tail and \textup{(2)} imply
\[
 \EE K_J(G)^{3/2}\leq C S_J^{-1/2},\qquad
 \EE K_J(G)^2\leq C S_J^{-1}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus, for the vector $\boldsymbol\Phi_J(G_i,G_{i+1})$, independence of the
two gaps yields
\[
 \EE\|\boldsymbol\Phi_J(G_i,G_{i+1})\|_2^3\leq C S_J^{-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]

We first record the imported dimension-free bridge used below.  For
probability laws $\nu,\gamma\in\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)$ with the same
mean, let
\[
 Z_2(\nu,\gamma)
 =\sup_{\operatorname{Lip}(\nabla h)\leq1}
   \left|\int h\,d\nu-\int h\,d\gamma\right|.
\]
If $X_1,\ldots,X_M$ are independent centred vectors,
$W=\sum_bX_b$, and
$\Gamma=\sum_b\EE(X_bX_b^\top)\succ0$, then
\[
 Z_2\{\mathcal L(W),\mathcal N(0,\Gamma)\}
 \leq C\|\Gamma^{-1/2}\|_{\rm op}
       \sum_{b=1}^M\EE\|X_b\|_2^3.
 \tag{B0}
\]
Indeed, Meckes' coordinate-free estimate for the Gaussian Stein solution
$f_h$ gives
$M_3(f_h)\leq C\|\Gamma^{-1/2}\|_{\rm op}
\operatorname{Lip}(\nabla h)$
\cite{Meckes2009MultivariateStein}.  With $W_b=W-X_b$, Taylor expansion at
$W_b$, independence, and centring cancel the constant and quadratic terms.
The two remainders are bounded by a constant times
\[
 M_3(f_h)\sum_b
 \left\{\EE\|X_b\|_2^3
 +\EE\|X_b\|_2\,\EE\|X_b\|_2^2\right\}.
\]
H\"older's inequality bounds the product by $\EE\|X_b\|_2^3$, proving
\textup{(B0)}; smooth approximation removes the provisional smoothness of
$h$.  The sharp, dimension-free comparison of
Bo{\l}botowski--Bouchitt\'e is
\cite{BolbotowskiBouchitte2026ZolotarevWasserstein}
\[
 \frac14W_2^2(\nu,\gamma)\leq Z_2(\nu,\gamma).
 \tag{B0$'$}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 W_2\{\mathcal L(W),\mathcal N(0,\Gamma)\}
 \leq C\left(
  \|\Gamma^{-1/2}\|_{\rm op}\sum_b\EE\|X_b\|_2^3
 \right)^{1/2}.
 \tag{B0$''$}
\]
Neither \textup{(B0)} nor \textup{(B0$'$)} is claimed as a new general
probability inequality.

We now prove the fixed exchange-point sufficiency.  Write $m=J+1$, let
$P_J:\mathbb R^{m^2}\to\mathbb R^{d_J}$ delete the $e_0\otimes e_0$
coordinate, and set
\[
 h_J(G)=(e_0(G),\ldots,e_J(G))^\top,
 \qquad
 Y_{i,J}=P_J\{h_J(G_i)\otimes h_J(G_{i+1})\}.
 \tag{B1}
\]
Then $\EE Y_{i,J}=0$ and
$\EE(Y_{i,J}Y_{i,J}^\top)=I_{d_J}$.  Moreover, double centring gives, for
all retained coordinates,
\[
 \EE\{e_a(G_i)e_b(G_{i+1})e_c(G_{i+1})e_d(G_{i+2})\}=0.
 \tag{B2}
\]
Thus distinct $Y_{i,J}$ are uncorrelated, and they are independent when their
indices differ by more than one.

Put $n=\lfloor n_N\rfloor$ and
$L=\lceil d_J^2+\log n\rceil$.  Partition the edge indices into consecutive
blocks of length at most $L$, deleting one connecting edge between successive
blocks.  The retained block sums are independent because their underlying
gap sets are disjoint.  If $I$ and $D$ denote the retained and deleted edge
sets, respectively, then
\[
 r:=|D|\leq \frac{n}{L+1}+L+1,
 \qquad
 \EE\left\|\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i\in D}Y_{i,J}\right\|_2^2
 =\frac{rd_J}{n}
 \leq C\left(\frac{d_J}{L}+\frac{Ld_J}{n}\right)=o(1).
 \tag{B3}
\]
For a retained block $B$, split its edges by parity.  Each parity class is
independent, so the Hilbert-space Rosenthal--Burkholder inequality
\cite{Pinelis1994OptimalMartingaleBounds}, together with \textup{(4)}, yields
\[
 \EE\left\|\sum_{i\in B}Y_{i,J}\right\|_2^3
 \leq C\{(Ld_J)^{3/2}+L/S_J\}.
 \tag{B4}
\]
Let $\mathcal B$ be the retained blocks, put
$X_B=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in B}Y_{i,J}$, and let
$V_B=(|B|/n)I_{d_J}$ be its covariance.  The actual-block third-moment
quantity is
\[
 \beta_N^{(Z)}
 :=\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}\EE\|X_B\|_2^3
 \leq C\left\{
  \frac{\sqrt L\,d_J^{3/2}}{\sqrt n}
  +\frac1{\sqrt n\,S_J}
 \right\}.
 \tag{B5}
\]
The factor $d_J^{3/2}$ has not been discarded.  It is contained explicitly
in the first term of \textup{(B5)}.  Since the covariance of the retained sum
$A=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in I}Y_{i,J}$ is $cI_{d_J}$, where $c=|I|/n\to1$,
\textup{(B0$''$)} yields a coupling with
$T\sim\mathcal N(0,cI_{d_J})$ satisfying
\[
 \EE\|A-T\|_2^2\leq C\beta_N^{(Z)}.
 \tag{B6}
\]
Under \textup{(X)}, $J=O(\log n)$, $d_J=O((\log n)^2)$, and
\[
 \frac{\sqrt L\,d_J^{3/2}}{\sqrt n}
 =O\!\left(\frac{(\log n)^5}{\sqrt n}\right)=o(1),
 \qquad
 \frac1{\sqrt n\,S_J}=o(1).
\]
Thus \textup{(B6)} is $o(1)$.  This is precisely where the new argument
differs from the retracted Yurinskii argument.  In CMU's Corollary~2.3 the
moment quantity also contains the Gaussian comparison blocks, and its
Euclidean probability bound has $\phi_2(d_J)^2=2d_J$; applied to
\textup{(B5)}, that theorem still requires
$d_J/(\sqrt nS_J)\to0$.  We neither omit the Gaussian blocks from CMU's
quantity nor use that corollary here; the dimension-free
\textup{(B0$'$)} route has a different, actual-block Stein quantity.

If $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,I_{d_J})$ is independent of $T$, then
\[
 \xi=T+\sqrt{1-c}\,Z\sim\mathcal N(0,I_{d_J}),
 \qquad
 \EE\|\xi-T\|_2^2=(1-c)d_J=\frac{rd_J}{n}=o(1).
 \tag{B7}
\]
Combining \textup{(B3)}--\textup{(B7)} proves the deterministic-cycle
exchange-point coupling under \textup{(X)}.

The renewal central limit theorem gives
$K_N-n_N=O_P(\sqrt N)$.  A Hilbert-space martingale maximal inequality over
that random terminal window gives squared Euclidean error
$O_P(d_J/\sqrt N)$ after normalization, which is the square of the first
order in
\textup{(Z)}.  The usual root-$N$ consistency of $\widehat\mu_N$ gives the
second.  It remains to make the triangular local-null comparison explicit.
Let $\mathcal P_J$ be the partition in \textup{(2)} and let
$g_N=g_{\sigma_N}$.  Analytic differentiation of the exact sampled-counter
atom and tail formulae at the exchange point gives, uniformly under
\textup{(T)} and for $J=O(\log N)$,
\[
 \max_{C\in\mathcal P_J}
 \left|\frac{g_N(C)}{g(C)}-1\right|
 \leq C N^{-1/2}(1+J^2).
 \tag{6}
\]
For the singleton cells this follows from the density-ratio derivatives,
which are $O(1+k^2)$ for $k\leq J$; for the final tail cell it follows by
differentiating the closed tail formula before taking the ratio.  Every
coordinate in \textup{(W)} is constant on each product cell of
$\mathcal P_J\otimes\mathcal P_J$.  Consequently \textup{(6)} implies
\[
 \|\Sigma_{J,N}-I_{d_J}\|_{\rm op}=O(J^2N^{-1/2}),
 \tag{7}
\]
where $\Sigma_{J,N}$ is the score covariance under $g_N$.  Moreover, if
$u_{a,N}=\EE_{g_N}e_a$, double centring under $g$ gives
\[
 \left\|(u_{a,N}u_{b,N})_{(a,b)\neq(0,0)}\right\|_2
 \leq\sum_{a=0}^J u_{a,N}^2=O(N^{-1}),
 \tag{8}
\]
so the normalized score mean is $O(N^{-1/2})$.  Coupling
$\Sigma_{J,N}^{1/2}\boldsymbol\xi_N$ and $\boldsymbol\xi_N$ with the same
standard Gaussian vector, \textup{(7)} and the square-root perturbation
bound give
\[
 \|(\Sigma_{J,N}^{1/2}-I)\boldsymbol\xi_N\|_2
 =O_P(\sqrt{d_J}J^2N^{-1/2})=O_P(J^3N^{-1/2}).
 \tag{9}
\]
We now apply the same independent-block argument uniformly, rather than
returning to a dimension-dependent martingale coupling.  Put
$\overline Y_{i,J,N}=Y_{i,J}-\EE_{g_N}Y_{i,J}$.  The ratio in
\textup{(6)} is $o(1)$ because $J=O(\log N)$.  Hence the product-cell
density ratio is bounded above and below by fixed constants, uniformly over
$\sigma_N\in\mathcal H_N(C)$; these constants may depend on the fixed
$x_0,C$, but not on $N,J$, or $d_J$.  It follows that
\textup{(B3)}--\textup{(B5)} hold for
$\overline Y_{i,J,N}$ with one common constant.  Disjoint retained blocks
remain independent under every $\sigma_N\in\mathcal H_N(C)$.

Let $\Gamma_{I,N}$ be the covariance of the normalized retained centred
sum.  Besides the lag-zero perturbation in \textup{(7)}, direct factorization
over three independent gaps gives
\[
 \|\operatorname{Cov}_{g_N}(Y_{i,J},Y_{i+1,J})\|_{\rm op}
 \leq C\sum_{a=0}^Ju_{a,N}^2=O(N^{-1}).
\]
Consequently, uniformly on $\mathcal H_N(C)$,
\[
 \|\Gamma_{I,N}-cI_{d_J}\|_{\rm op}
 =O(J^2N^{-1/2}),
 \qquad \lambda_{\min}(\Gamma_{I,N})\geq\tfrac12
 \tag{9$'$}
\]
for all sufficiently large $N$.  Applying \textup{(B0$''$)} with
$\Gamma_{I,N}$ gives the same right-hand side as \textup{(B6)}.  Coupling
the two centred Gaussian vectors synchronously and using the square-root
perturbation bound yields
\[
 W_2\{\mathcal N(0,\Gamma_{I,N}),\mathcal N(0,cI_{d_J})\}
 \leq C\sqrt{d_J}\,\|\Gamma_{I,N}-cI_{d_J}\|_{\rm op}
 =O(J^3N^{-1/2}).
 \tag{9$''$}
\]
Equation \textup{(8)} makes the normalized uncentred mean $O(N^{-1/2})$.
Thus the deterministic-cycle coupling is uniform on $\mathcal H_N(C)$.
Condition \textup{(X)} implies $J_N=O(\log N)$, so
\textup{(9$''$)} and both calendar-stopping terms in \textup{(Z)} are
uniformly $o_P(1)$.  Markov's inequality applied to the squared
Wasserstein coupling cost proves \textup{(Y)}.  An optimal transport plan in
finite-dimensional Euclidean space admits a regular conditional kernel, so
this coupling can be adjoined to the original stopped score on an extension
of its probability space.

The corner contrast is nonzero exactly when both gaps are at least $J$.
On that event its magnitude is bounded above and below by constant multiples
of $S_J^{-1}$, by \textup{(1)}.  Its triangular-array Lindeberg condition is
therefore exactly $\sqrt{n_N}S_{J_N}\to\infty$, which is \textup{(AA)}.
If this fails along a subsequence, the number of nonzero corner summands has
a nonvanishing atom at zero (and is asymptotically Poisson whenever
$n_NS_{J_N}^2$ has a finite positive limit), excluding a standard normal
limit.  For \textup{(AB)}, the expected number of adjacent pairs with both
gaps at least $r_N$ is $O(n_NS_{r_N}^2)=o(1)$.  Markov's inequality proves
the zero-block assertion, and the Gaussian comparison follows from its
chi-square block energy.

Finally,
\[
 \log(n_NS_{J_N}^2)
 =\log n_N-2x_0J_N+2\log(1+x_0J_N).
 \tag{10}
\]
In the displayed second-order band,
$J_N\asymp\log n_N$ and
$2\log(1+x_0J_N)=2\log\log n_N-2\log2+o(1)$.
Hence
\[
 \log(n_NS_{J_N}^2)=\omega_N-2\log2+o(1).
\]
Together with the proved equivalence, this gives \textup{(AC)}.  Under the
fixed critical correction displayed in the proposition, \textup{(10)}
converges to $c-2\log2$, and therefore
$n_NS_{J_N}^2\to e^c/4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{paperthm}[Fixed-setting retained-record quotient delta method]\label{thm:finite-record-quotient-delta-main}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~C and suppose the population inverse lies in
the stable quotient chart
\[
r_0>0,\qquad \Delta_{\rm inv}=r_1-r_0^2\ne0 .
\]
For the quotient map \(\Phi\) in Theorem~B,
\[
\sqrt N\{\Phi(\Rinch_N^{\rm cyc})-\Phi(\Rinc)\}
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_\sigma),
\qquad
\Sigma_\sigma=D\Phi(\Rinc)\Sigma_rD\Phi(\Rinc)^\top .
\]
For the constrained D-MAP population, \(\Phi(\Rinc)=(p+s,ps)\).  If
\[
\Delta_\sigma=\Phi_1(\Rinc)^2-4\Phi_2(\Rinc)=(p-s)^2>0,
\]
let \(z_-<z_+\) be the sorted roots and let
\(\widehat z_{-,N}<\widehat z_{+,N}\) be the correspondingly sorted roots
formed from \(\Phi(\Rinch_N^{\rm cyc})\).  This ordering is visible and
requires no external physical label.  With
\[
J_{\rm root}=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{z_-}{z_--z_+}&-\dfrac1{z_--z_+}\\[6pt]
\dfrac{z_+}{z_+-z_-}&-\dfrac1{z_+-z_-}
\end{pmatrix},
\]
the label-free root CLT is
\[
\sqrt N
\begin{pmatrix}\widehat z_{-,N}-z_-\\ \widehat z_{+,N}-z_+\end{pmatrix}
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(0,J_{\rm root}\Sigma_\sigma J_{\rm root}^{\top}\right).
\]
For known design interval \(\Delta\tau\), define the root-ordered rates
\[
\vartheta=
\begin{pmatrix}-\log z_-/\Delta\tau\\-\log z_+/\Delta\tau\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\widehat\vartheta_N=
\begin{pmatrix}-\log\widehat z_{-,N}/\Delta\tau\\
-\log\widehat z_{+,N}/\Delta\tau\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat\vartheta_N-\vartheta)
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,J_{\rm rate}\Sigma_\sigma J_{\rm rate}^{\top}),
\qquad
J_{\rm rate}=
\begin{pmatrix}-(\Delta\tau z_-)^{-1}&0\\
0&-(\Delta\tau z_+)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}J_{\rm root}.
\]
Thus the roots identify the unordered survival-factor set \(\{p,s\}\) and
the displayed root ordering gives a canonical, label-free version of the
unordered numerical rate set
\[
\left\{-\frac{\log p}{\Delta\tau},-\frac{\log s}{\Delta\tau}\right\}.
\]
If \(\Delta\tau\) is not supplied as a known design interval, the theorem
identifies only the dimensionless unordered product set
\[
\{-\log p,-\log s\}
=\{\Gamma\Delta\tau,\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau\},
\]
not numerical rates per unit time.
Only the assignment of the two numerical entries to the physical names
\(\Gamma\) and \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) requires the side-data branch
convention.
\end{paperthm}

\begin{maintheorem}[Compact-uniform singular exchange experiment]
\label{thm:singular-exchange-limit-experiment-main}
Let \(\mathcal K\Subset(0,1)\) and \(H<\infty\).  Denote by
\(P_{p,s}^{(N)}\) the law of the stationary visible record
\((A_1,\ldots,A_N)\) under the sampled-counter model with survival factors
\((p,s)\).  For \(z\in\mathcal K\), \(|h|\le H\), and all sufficiently large
\(N\), put
\[
 p_{N,z,h}=z+hN^{-1/4},\qquad
 s_{N,z,h}=z-hN^{-1/4},\qquad
 P_{N,z,h}=P_{p_{N,z,h},s_{N,z,h}}^{(N)}.
\tag{SE1}
\]
At the diagonal let
\[
 D_z(k)=1-z+\log z\{kz-k+z\},\qquad
 g_z(k)=z^kD_z(k),\qquad k\ge0,
\tag{SE2}
\]
and define
\[
\begin{split}
B_z(k)=\frac{z^{k-2}}6\bigl[&
k^3(z-1)\log z+3k^2\{\log z+1-z\}\\
&-3kz\log z-6kz-2z\log z-3z\bigr],
\qquad b_z(k)=\frac{B_z(k)}{g_z(k)} .
\end{split}
\tag{SE3}
\]
Then \(g_z\) is the diagonal Palm gap mass and
\[
 g_{z+d,z-d}(k)=g_z(k)+d^2B_z(k)+O(d^4)
\tag{SE4}
\]
for every \(k\).  Moreover,
\[
 \sum_{k\ge0}g_z(k)b_z(k)=0,\qquad
 \mu_z=\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)g_z(k)
 =\frac{1-z-z\log z}{(1-z)^2},
\tag{SE5}
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal I(z)=\frac1{\mu_z}
 \sum_{k\ge0}g_z(k)b_z(k)^2
 =\frac1{\mu_z}\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{B_z(k)^2}{g_z(k)}
\tag{SE6}
\]
is finite, strictly positive, and continuous on \((0,1)\).

Delete the two incomplete endpoint cycles and write the complete gaps as
\(G_{1,N},\ldots,G_{K_N,N}\).  Set
\[
 \Delta_{N,z}=\frac1{\sqrt N}
 \sum_{j=1}^{K_N}b_z(G_{j,N}).
\tag{SE7}
\]
Uniformly for \(z\in\mathcal K\),
\[
 \Delta_{N,z}\ \Longrightarrow\
 \mathcal N\{0,\mathcal I(z)\}
\tag{SE8}
\]
in bounded-Lipschitz distance under \(P_{N,z,0}\).  More precisely, for every
\(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\sup_{\substack{z\in\mathcal K\\ |h|\le H}}
P_{N,z,0}\!\left(
\left|
\log\frac{dP_{N,z,h}}{dP_{N,z,0}}
-h^2\Delta_{N,z}
+\frac12h^4\mathcal I(z)
\right|>\varepsilon
\right)\longrightarrow0 .
\tag{SE9}
\]
Consequently the likelihood-ratio processes, indexed by \(|h|\le H\),
converge uniformly over \(z\in\mathcal K\) to the scalar Gaussian experiments
\[
 X_z\sim\mathcal N\!\left(h^2\sqrt{\mathcal I(z)},1\right),
\qquad |h|\le H.
\tag{SE10}
\]
Equivalently, under \(P_{N,z,h}\),
\[
 \Delta_{N,z}\Longrightarrow
 \mathcal N\{h^2\mathcal I(z),\mathcal I(z)\}
\tag{SE11}
\]
uniformly over \(z\in\mathcal K\) and bounded \(h\).
The limit identifies \(h^2\), not the sign of \(h\).  This is the complete
singular experiment for the specified path; it is not an ordered hidden-label
experiment.
\end{maintheorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(x=-\log p\), \(y=-\log s\).  The Palm gap formula in Theorem~A is
\[
 g_{p,s}(k)
 =\frac{y(1-p)p^k-x(1-s)s^k}{y-x},
\tag{SE12}
\]
with its continuous value when \(p=s\).  Substitute \(p=z+d\) and \(s=z-d\)
and expand the even analytic function of \(d\).  The constant and quadratic
coefficients are exactly \textup{(SE2)} and \textup{(SE3)}, proving
\textup{(SE4)}.  The diagonal survival tail is
\[
 S_z(k)=z^k(1-k\log z).
\]
Thus \(g_z(k)=S_z(k)-S_z(k+1)\), which proves the first identity in
\textup{(SE2)} and, by summing the tails, the formula for \(\mu_z\).
Differentiating \(\sum_kg_{z+d,z-d}(k)=1\) at order \(d^2\) gives
\(\sum_kB_z(k)=0\), hence the centring in \textup{(SE5)}.

On every \(\mathcal K\Subset(0,1)\), the masses in \textup{(SE12)} and all
their derivatives through any fixed order have a common
\(C(1+k)^m r^k\) envelope for some \(r<1\).  Termwise differentiation and
summation are therefore valid uniformly on \(\mathcal K\).  Formula
\textup{(SE3)} gives \(|b_z(k)|\le C_{\mathcal K}(1+k)^2\), so
\textup{(SE6)} is finite and continuous.  It is positive: if it vanished,
then \(B_z(k)=0\) for every \(k\).  But
\[
 B_z(0)=-\frac{2\log z+3}{6z};
\]
its only zero is \(z=e^{-3/2}\), and at that point the numerator in
\textup{(SE3)} for \(k=1\) equals \(-6z\), so \(B_z(1)\ne0\).

Put \(q=d^2\).  Uniform analytic differentiation in the symmetric coordinate
\((p+s,ps)=(2z,z^2-q)\) gives
\[
 g_{z+\sqrt q,z-\sqrt q}(k)
 =g_z(k)\{1+qb_z(k)+q^2c_z(k)+q^3R_{z,q}(k)\},
\tag{SE13}
\]
where \(\sum_kg_z(k)c_z(k)=0\) and \(c_z,R_{z,q}\) have common polynomial
envelopes on \(\mathcal K\) for \(q\) in a fixed right neighbourhood of zero.
The same statement follows directly from \textup{(SE12)}.  Uniform
exponential gap tails imply
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le K_N}G_{j,N}=O_{P_{N,z,0}}(\log N)
\tag{SE14}
\]
uniformly in \(z\in\mathcal K\).  Taylor expansion of the logarithm in
\textup{(SE13)}, on the event in \textup{(SE14)}, therefore gives uniformly
for \(|h|\le H\), with \(q=h^2N^{-1/2}\),
\[
\begin{split}
\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}\log
\frac{g_{z+\sqrt q,z-\sqrt q}(G_{j,N})}{g_z(G_{j,N})}
={}&h^2\Delta_{N,z}\\
&+\frac{h^4}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}
\{c_z(G_{j,N})-\tfrac12b_z(G_{j,N})^2\}+o_P(1).
\end{split}
\tag{SE15}
\]

Under the diagonal law the complete Palm gaps are consecutive i.i.d.
variables with mass \(g_z\), and the renewal count satisfies
\(K_N/N\to1/\mu_z\).  The uniform regenerative law of large numbers applied
to the two polynomially bounded rewards in \textup{(SE15)} makes its second
line converge to \(-h^4\mathcal I(z)/2\), uniformly on
\(\mathcal K\times[-H,H]\).  The uniform regenerative central limit theorem
applied to \(b_z\) proves \textup{(SE8)}.  Both results can also be obtained by
the subsequence criterion: every sequence \(z_N\in\mathcal K\) has a
convergent subsequence, and the common exponential envelope supplies
Lindeberg, renewal-count replacement, and uniform integrability along that
subsequence.

It remains to compare the product of complete-gap likelihoods with the
stationary \(N\)-bin likelihood.  Their ratio contains only the left
equilibrium-delay factor, the right censored-tail factor, and the normalizing
cycle mean.  Their logarithmic \(q\)-derivatives have polynomial envelopes in
the two endpoint lengths.  Those lengths are \(O_P(\log N)\) uniformly by the
same exponential-tail bound, so their total log-likelihood contribution is
\(O_P(N^{-1/2}\operatorname{polylog}N)=o_P(1)\).  This proves
\textup{(SE9)}.

The right side of \textup{(SE9)} is a continuous function of \(h\) and the
remainder is uniform on the compact index set.  Hence the likelihood-ratio
processes converge in \(C([-H,H])\) to
\[
 h\longmapsto
 \exp\{h^2\sqrt{\mathcal I(z)}\,Z-\tfrac12h^4\mathcal I(z)\},
 \qquad Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1),
\]
which is \textup{(SE10)}.  Le Cam's third lemma gives \textup{(SE11)}.
Finally,
\[
\sqrt N\{(p_{N,z,h}+s_{N,z,h},p_{N,z,h}s_{N,z,h})
-(2z,z^2)\}=(0,-h^2),
\]
which explains both the \(N^{-1/4}\) scale and the loss of the sign.  General
locally conic and loss-of-identifiability asymptotics are established in
\cite{DacunhaCastelleGassiat1997LocallyConic,
LiuShao2003LossIdentifiability}; the calculation above is the
compact-uniform stationary-renewal specialization.
\end{proof}

\begin{paperthm}[Pointwise exchange-diagonal projection example]\label{thm:e-fixed-candidate-exchange-diagonal-main}
Fix
\[
z_0=\frac12,\qquad \sigma_0=(1,1/4),\qquad
H_{z_0}=\{h\in\mathbb R^2:h_2\le z_0h_1\},
\]
and let \(F=\{(\sigma_1,\sigma_2):\sigma_2\le\sigma_1^2/4\}\).  Suppose the
true population satisfies \(\sigma=\sigma_0\), the printed interval
certificate proves
\(\lambda_{\min}\{\Sigma_\sigma(z_0)\}>0.037\), and
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat\sigma_N^{\rm raw}-\sigma_0)
\Rightarrow Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_\sigma(z_0)).
\]
Let \(\Pi_F\) be the locally unique Euclidean projection, with any fixed
measurable tie rule outside the local neighbourhood, and set
\[
\widehat\sigma_N=\Pi_F(\widehat\sigma_N^{\rm raw}),\qquad
X_N=\sqrt N(\widehat\sigma_N-\sigma_0).
\]
Then
\[
X_N\Rightarrow \Pi_{H_{z_0}}Z .
\]
This theorem is only a pointwise projection example at the preselected point
\(z_0=1/2\).  It defines no statistic for testing, no simulated or limiting
critical value, no confidence region, and no feasible or oracle coverage
claim.  Selection of \(z_0\) from the data and uniformity over \(0<z<1\) are
outside its scope.
\end{paperthm}

\begin{proof}
Near \(\sigma_0\), the boundary of \(F\) is the smooth graph
\(\sigma_2=\sigma_1^2/4\), whose tangent cone at \(\sigma_0\) is
\(H_{z_0}\).  The standard first-order metric-projection expansion at a smooth
inequality boundary is
\[
\sqrt N\{\Pi_F(\sigma_0+N^{-1/2}h_N)-\sigma_0\}
=\Pi_{H_{z_0}}h_N+o(1)
\]
for every bounded convergent sequence \(h_N\)
\cite{Shapiro1987MetricProjection}.  Tightness of the raw quotient CLT permits
the expansion on events with probability tending to one.  Slutsky's theorem
and continuity of projection onto the closed half-space give the asserted
limit.  The certificate establishes only that the covariance in this limit is
nonsingular; it is not used to calibrate a procedure.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proofs of Theorems A--D}\label{subsec:proofs-a-d-main}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~A]
Write \(\beta=e_D^\top\) and
\(c=((1-p),a)^\top\).  The marked kernel has rank-one reset form
\(T_1=c\beta\).  Conditional on every click, the next hidden state is therefore
\(D\), independently of the pre-click state.  Successive click epochs are
regeneration epochs, and the numbers of intervening zeros are independent with
common Palm mass
\[
g_k=\beta T_0^kT_1\mathbf1,\qquad k\ge0.
\]
The entries of \(T_0\) are strictly positive along every admissible serial path,
so \(g_k>0\) for all \(k\); the spectral radius of \(T_0\) is less than one,
and \(\sum_{k\ge0}g_k=1\).  This proves the full-support renewal assertion.

Since \(T_0\) is lower triangular, direct evaluation of \(T_0^k\), followed by
substitution of the integral defining \(b\), gives, off the equal-rate diagonal,
\[
g_k=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-
\Gamma(1-s)s^k}{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},\qquad
S_k=\sum_{j\ge k}g_j=
\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k}
{\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma}.
\]
The continuous limit at \(\Gamma=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) follows either by
l'H\^opital's rule or by exponentiating the repeated-eigenvalue triangular
matrix.  Because \(h_k=1-S_{k+1}/S_k\), elementary cancellation yields
\[
h_{k+1}-h_k=
\frac{\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}(p-s)^2p^ks^k}
{(\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^k-\Gamma s^k)
 (\kappa_{\mathrm r}p^{k+1}-\Gamma s^{k+1})}>0.
\]
On the diagonal, with \(y=\Gamma\Delta\tau\) and \(p=e^{-y}\), the same
calculation gives
\[
S_k=p^k(1+yk),\qquad
h_{k+1}-h_k=\frac{y^2p}{(1+yk)(1+y(k+1))}>0.
\]

Let \(P=T_0+T_1\) be the hidden transition matrix.  Its eigenvalues are
\(1\) and \(\lambda_{\rm hid}=p-b\).  Conditional on a click at time zero,
the state at time one is \(D\), and hence
\[
u_n:=\PP(A_n=1\mid A_0=1)
=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid}^{n-1},\qquad n\ge1.
\]
Multiplication by \(\rho\) gives the covariance formula.  With
\(q=1-p\), stationarity gives
\[
\rho=\frac{q(1-s)}{q+b},\qquad
\rho-a=\frac{b(q-a)}{q+b}>0,
\]
so \(\gamma_1=\rho(a-\rho)<0\) and \(0\)-dependence is excluded.  If
\(\lambda_{\rm hid}=0\), the two rows of \(P\) coincide; the hidden state after
one intervening step has forgotten the past, which makes visible blocks
separated by one omitted observation independent.  Conversely, exact
\(1\)-dependence implies \(\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_2)=0\), and the strict
prefactor in the covariance formula forces \(\lambda_{\rm hid}=0\).
Finally, in a binary renewal process an order-\(m\) Markov property would make
the click hazard constant after every zero run of length at least \(m\).  The
strictly increasing hazards above rule this out for every finite \(m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~B]
The regenerative reset gives
\[
r_0=\rho,\qquad
r_1=\rho\PP(A_1=1\mid A_0=1)=\rho a,
\qquad r_2=\rho u_2.
\]
The spectral calculation in the proof of Theorem~A gives
\(u_2=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\rm hid}\).  Therefore, on
\(r_1-r_0^2\ne0\),
\[
a=\frac{r_1}{r_0},qquad
\lambda_{\rm hid}=
\frac{r_2-r_0^2}{r_1-r_0^2}.
\]
Since \(a=1-s-b\) and \(\lambda_{\rm hid}=p-b\),
\[
1-a+\lambda_{\rm hid}=p+s.
\]
Substitution of \(\rho=q(1-s)/(q+b)\), \(q=1-p\), into the second coordinate
gives
\[
\rho(1-\lambda_{\rm hid})-a+\lambda_{\rm hid}=ps.
\]
These are precisely the two coordinates of \(\Phi\).  Their polynomial has
root multiset \(\{p,s\}\).  That multiset determines the displayed Palm gap
law, including its continuous repeated-root limit, and renewal products then
determine every finite inclusion probability; finite inclusion--exclusion
determines every word probability.  Thus the three coordinates are
law-determining.  This argument does not select a physical label for either
root and makes no minimal-cardinality claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~C]
Under Palm sampling, the gaps \((G_j)\) are i.i.d. with an exponential moment.
The displayed cycle reward satisfies \(\EE U_j=\Rinc\EE L_j\), hence
\(\EE Y_j=0\), and it depends only on \((G_j,G_{j+1})\), hence is
\(1\)-dependent.  Complete-cycle deletion removes at most a fixed number of
endpoint cycles; their total length is \(O_{\PP}(\log N)\) by the exponential
moment.  The renewal count satisfies
\(K_N=N/\mu+O_{\PP}(\sqrt N)\).  The random-index version of the
Hoeffding--Robbins CLT for finite-range dependent variables therefore yields
\[
N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{K_N-1}Y_j
\Rightarrow\mathcal N\!\left(0,
\mu^{-1}\{\Var Y_0+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)
+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_1,Y_0)\}\right).
\]
The endpoint remainder is \(o_{\PP}(1)\) on this scale, proving the stated
linearization and CLT.  Consistency and the rate of the stopped separated-batch
covariance estimator are exactly the content of
\Cref{lem:paper-level-separated-covariance-main}, including plug-in centering
and the strengthened gate \(M_N\ge m_N\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~D]
On the stable quotient chart, \(\Phi\) is continuously differentiable with the
uniform bounds in \Cref{prop:stable-quotient-chart-main}.  Applying the
multivariate delta method to Theorem~C gives the symmetric-coordinate CLT and
its sandwich covariance.  When \(z_-<z_+\), implicit differentiation of
\(z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2=0\) gives the displayed matrix \(J_{\rm root}\).
The sorted roots remain in the same order with probability tending to one, so
a second delta-method application gives the root CLT.  Composition with
\(z\mapsto-\log z/\Delta\tau\) gives \(J_{\rm rate}\) and the root-ordered
rate CLT.  No step assigns the two entries to physical labels.
\end{proof}

\section{D-MAP setting and record-level observables}\label{sec:operational}

We first consider only the stationary D-MAP record law of
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}.  The hidden chain has two states, the
output alphabet is \(\{0,1\}\), and the visible output is the stationary
two-sided binary process
\[
(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z},
\]
where \(A_n=1\) denotes a marked transition of the D-MAP in the \(n\)th
discrete step.  All Palm, covariance, renewal, and independence
statements below refer to this two-sided stationary law.  A finite observed
record of length \(N\) is only the restriction
\[
A^{(N)}=(A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_{N-1}),
\]
of the stationary process.  The only nonstationary finite-start convention
used below is the post-burn-in retained-length convention: generate
\(L_N+N\) intervals from an arbitrary hidden initial law, discard the first
\(L_N\), and reindex the retained word as
\((A_{L_N},\ldots,A_{L_N+N-1})\), so \(N\) is always the retained sample size.
Compressing such a finite record to the empirical click
frequency recovers only the one-point observable
\[
\widehat\rho_N=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n.
\]
The full record, however, carries more. In particular, one may ask:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item what words belong to the support of the stationary law;
\item how the zero-run lengths between clicks are distributed;
\item whether the click hazard after a long run of zeros remembers the run length;
\item how the low-lag covariance sequence behaves;
\item what counting noise remains after coarse time averaging.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{definition}[Record observables]\label{def:record-benchmarks-main}
For a stationary binary record $(A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}$, a \emph{zero-run length} is the number $G$ of consecutive zeros following a click and preceding the next click. If the zero-run law has full support, its hazard is
\[
h_k=\PP(G=k\mid G\ge k),\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
A stationary record is \emph{exact $1$-dependent} when, for every $m$, the sigma algebras generated by $\{A_j:j\le m\}$ and $\{A_j:j\ge m+2\}$ are independent.
Equivalently, one leaves the intermediate sample at time $m+1$ unobserved: a block ending at time $m$ is tested for independence from a block beginning at time $m+2$, not conditioned on the middle sample.
\end{definition}

These observables are not themselves one-point rate observables, yet in the
explicit D-MAP family above they reduce to sharp thresholds in the coordinate
\(\Gamma\).

It is useful to keep the D-MAP record construction in two logically separate
steps.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item The labelled kernels determine the stationary word law, the zero-run
hazard, and the covariance sequence of the visible binary record.
\item Exact \(1\)-dependence for this D-MAP is first equivalent to the scalar
coordinate condition \(\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{crit}}(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\).
That condition is labelled according to \Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}:
the inverse theorem gives
\[
\mu_{\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}}=\mu_{\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma}
\quad\text{and hence only}\quad
\{e^{-\Gamma\Delta\tau},e^{-\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau}\}.
\]
Thus, without a supplied branch label, the statement is made on the quotient by
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\sim(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Gamma)\).
\end{enumerate}
The constrained D-MAP produces a full-support renewal law.  Its distinguished
record threshold in the main theorem chain is therefore the exact
\(1\)-dependence threshold where the hidden eigenmode vanishes.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The paper gives a closed-form record-law and fixed-setting asymptotic analysis
for one constrained latched killed-leakage sampled-counter D-MAP(2) subfamily.
The visible
record identifies the unordered survival/rate coordinates, not hidden labels
or response-side thermodynamic parameters without external calibration.  The
two-state fibre theorem makes this ambiguity exact in the larger killed-reset
class: separated rates generate a closed continuum arc whose only triangular
members are the two physical rate orderings, while equal rates generate a
singleton fibre.  The diagonal statement is deliberately narrow: it is only
the pointwise projected
limit at the fixed \(z_0=1/2\) candidate with its printed population covariance
certificate, and it carries no confidence-procedure interpretation.

The theorem-by-theorem comparison in
\Cref{sec:theorem-led-comparison} separates recalled proof inputs from the
model-specific calculations proved here.

\section{Related Work and Mathematical Comparison}\label{sec:theorem-led-comparison}

The introduction gives the mathematical narrative comparison with the closest
renewal-binary, weak-memory, waiting-time, partial-observation, and detector
papers.  This final section gives a compact theorem-by-theorem comparison with

\subsection{Detector dead-time comparator}\label{subsec:detector-dead-time-comparator-main}

In the nonparalyzable continuous dead-time model, an accepted event is followed
by a fixed or random inactive period and then by a live waiting time; this
structure produces renewal count statistics and motivates classical
dead-time corrections and detector checks
\cite{YuFessler2000,SarbaziSafariHaas2018LongDeadTime,
GronbergDanielssonSjolin2018Nonparalyzable,MeeksSiegel2008DeadTime}.
Krause--Walenta's continuous exponential-recovery model for free-running SPADs
allows detection efficiency to recover continuously after dead time and fits
parameters from inter-detection intervals \cite{KrauseWalenta2025SPADRecovery}.
Such a detector need not have \(T_1(\cdot,R)=0\): after a click, physical
recovery and further evolution can occur before the next externally chosen bin
boundary.  In contrast, the present model is a sampled discrete D-MAP with
stochastic recovery before a click and an explicit post-click latch until the
boundary.
Its Palm zero-run law is
\[
g_k=e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1
=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm r}(1-p)p^k-\Gamma(1-s)s^k}
       {\kappa_{\mathrm r}-\Gamma},
\]
with the continuous equal-rate limit understood.  The contribution relative to
the detector dead-time literature is therefore not a generic dead-time
correction.  It is an exact quotient inverse for the sampled killed-leakage
D-MAP visible law, together with the renewal product and retained-record
covariance route that are valid only under the boundary-reset convention in
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main,prop:model-fragility-boundary-reset-main}.

\subsection{Theorem-by-theorem comparison with DMAP, DPH, and MAP theory}

\paragraph{Theorem A (record law).}
Alfa--Neuts supply the general D-MAP construction
\cite{AlfaNeuts1995DMAP}, while M\'esz\'aros--Telek study canonical
representations for general discrete order-two MAPs and RAPs
\cite{MeszarosTelek2013DiscreteMAP2Canonical}.  Those representation results
do not assert that the present triangular, deterministically reset subfamily is
renewal.  The additional calculation here is restricted to that subfamily: it
derives the Palm gap law, increasing hazard, one-mode covariance, and exact
\(1\)-dependence criterion.  These are elementary model-specific consequences,
not a new general DMAP theorem.

\paragraph{Theorem B (three-inclusion quotient inverse).}
The closest prior result is the canonical-coordinate analysis of discrete
order-two MAP/RAP models by M\'esz\'aros--Telek
\cite{MeszarosTelek2013DiscreteMAP2Canonical}, together with canonical MAP2
and matrix-exponential representation work
\cite{BodrogHeindlHorvathTelek2008MAP2Canonical,
TelekHorvath2007MinimalMAP}.  That literature constructs sparse or canonical
representatives and moment-matching maps for broader representation classes.
Theorem~B neither replaces nor strengthens those canonical-form theorems.  Its
narrow additional statement is the direct rational identity
\[
\frac{r_2-r_0^2}{r_1-r_0^2}=p-b
\]
for the latched killed-reset image, which converts three visible binary
inclusion probabilities into \((p+s,ps)\) and hence the complete visible
renewal law.  The estimand is a law-determining quotient, not a labelled
minimal DMAP2 representative.  We do not claim that this identity is absent as
an implicit specialization of canonical DMAP2 coordinates; the contribution
is the explicit three-inclusion specialization and its stability bounds.

\paragraph{Theorems C and D (asymptotics).}
The regenerative CLT, separated-batch method, and multivariate delta method are
standard inputs \cite{Zamparo2022RenewalBinary,Janson2024RenewalCLT,
AsmussenGlynn2007,FlegalJones2010BatchMeansSpectral}.  The paper's additional
work is bookkeeping for complete retained cycles, the explicit stopped-prefix
bounds, and transport to the three-inclusion quotient.  The sorted-root and
log-rate CLTs are direct delta-method consequences and are not claimed as new
asymptotic theory.

\paragraph{Theorem E (projection example).}
Metric-projection limits at smooth inequality boundaries are standard
\cite{Shapiro1987MetricProjection,FangSeo2021ProjectionTests}.  Theorem~E is
therefore presented only as a pointwise worked projection at \(z_0=1/2\), with
a reproducible covariance nondegeneracy certificate.  It makes no inferential
or novelty claim beyond this specialization.

\paragraph{Complete-law checking and the Markov--Palm score.}
Distributional-distance consistency, serial-independence basis tests,
finite-range and Hilbert-space central limit theorems, and local Gaussian
shift calculus are standard inputs
\cite{RyabkoRyabko2010ErgodicInference,Ryabko2012StationaryTesting,
SkaugTjostheim1993SerialIndependence,DedeckerMerlevede2003HilbertCLT,
vanDerVaart1998AsymptoticStatistics}.
\Cref{thm:complete-visible-law-specification-main} combines those inputs with
the sampled-counter word law and an explicit rank-one Markov-gap path.  The
model-specific extension in
\Cref{thm:complete-markov-palm-tangent-omnibus-main} is the exact closed
interaction space obtained by adding $q(0,0)=0$ to double centring, its
$\mu^{-1}$-scaled efficient projection, and the exchange-boundary calibration
of a trace-weighted score.  It claims pointwise strict local power in every
nonzero interaction direction, but neither a new general independence test
nor a uniform infinite-dimensional minimax envelope.

The dimension-free comparison
$W_2^2/4\leq Z_2$ and the multivariate Stein regularity estimate are external
\cite{BolbotowskiBouchitte2026ZolotarevWasserstein,
Meckes2009MultivariateStein}.  The model-specific calculation in
\Cref{prop:helmert-growing-layer-sharp-main} applies them to independent sums
of retained overlapping-Helmert blocks.  It keeps the block factor
$d_J^{3/2}$ explicit, while avoiding the additional ambient factor and
Gaussian comparison-block moment required by CMU's Yurinskii probability
bound.  This yields the sharp local-null boundary; no new general coupling
inequality is claimed.

\paragraph{Fibre results.}
Minimal-realization uniqueness is classical, beginning with Kalman and used
directly for MAP reduction and moment matching by Telek--Horv\'ath
\cite{Kalman1963LinearSystems,TelekHorvath2007MinimalMAP}.  Accordingly,
\Cref{thm:nstate-killed-reset-identifiability-dichotomy-main} is explicitly a
recalled orbit-fibre reformulation.  The new two-state assertion is
\Cref{thm:two-state-complete-fibre-arc-main}: for the physical triangular
kernel, the complete Markovian orbit intersection is an explicit closed arc
between the rate-swapped endpoints and collapses exactly on the exchange
diagonal.  General MAP2 nonidentifiability and weak-equivalence results are the
nearest representation-theoretic comparators
\cite{RamirezCoboLilloWiper2010MAP2Nonidentifiability,
RamirezCoboLillo2012WeakEquivalentMAP}; the claim here is only this exact
killed-reset sampled-counter specialization.

The further new assertion is
\Cref{thm:nstate-interior-continuum-nonidentifiability-main}: the unrestricted
interior fibre contains a positivity-preserving similarity manifold of exact
dimension \((n-1)^2\).  For serial kernels, Cumani's canonical failure-time
forms, acyclic DPH estimation, and order-two/three discrete PH canonical forms
are direct predecessors
\cite{Cumani1982CanonicalFailure,BobbioEtAl2003AcyclicDPH,
PappTelek2013DiscretePHCanonical,MeszarosPappTelek2014Traffic}.
\Cref{cor:nstate-serial-all-collision-identifiability-main} is consequently
stated as a killed-reset orbit-intersection calculation: the pure serial fibre
is exactly the rate-multiset permutation class, with collisions handled by the
confluent recurrence.  It is not claimed as a new general acyclic-DPH
canonical-form theorem.  General phase-type nonuniqueness remains fully
consistent with O'Cinneide's invariant-polytope analysis
\cite{OCinneide1989PhaseTypeNonuniqueness,OCinneide1991InvariantPolytopes}.
