\begin{proposition}[Sharp local-null Helmert coupling boundary]
\label{prop:helmert-growing-layer-sharp-main}
Retain the exchange-point notation of
\Cref{thm:complete-markov-palm-tangent-omnibus-main}, put
$p_0=e^{-x_0}$, and write
\[
 S_j=\PP_g(G\geq j)=p_0^j(1+x_0j),\qquad j\geq0.
\]
For $j\geq0$ define the weighted Helmert contrast
\[
 e_j(k)=
 \sqrt{\frac{S_{j+1}}{g_jS_j}}\,\mathbf1_{\{k=j\}}
 -\sqrt{\frac{g_j}{S_jS_{j+1}}}\,\mathbf1_{\{k\geq j+1\}}.
 \tag{V}
\]
Thus $e_0$ is the contrast used in
\Cref{thm:complete-markov-palm-tangent-omnibus-main}, and
$e_0,e_1,\ldots$ is an orthonormal basis of $L^2_0(g)$.  For $J\geq1$ let
\[
 \boldsymbol\Phi_J=
 \bigl(e_a\otimes e_b:0\leq a,b\leq J,\ (a,b)\neq(0,0)\bigr),
 \qquad d_J=(J+1)^2-1,
 \tag{W}
\]
in any fixed order, and let $\mathbf Z_{J,N}$ be the corresponding vector of
scores in \textup{(Q)}, with the basis in \textup{(V)} fixed at $\theta_0$.
Set $n_N=N/\mu$, fix $C<\infty$, and let $\mathcal H_N(C)$ be the physical
sampled-counter null parameters $\sigma$ satisfying
$\|\sqrt N(\sigma-\sigma_0)\|\leq C$, as in \textup{(T)}.  If
$J_N\to\infty$, the following condition is necessary and sufficient:
\[
 n_NS_{J_N}^2\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{X}
\]
More precisely, \textup{(X)} is equivalent to the existence, on extensions of
the probability spaces, of
$\boldsymbol\xi_{N,\sigma}\sim\mathcal N(0,I_{d_{J_N}})$ such that, for every
$\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 \sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal H_N(C)}
 \PP_\sigma\!\left(
  \|\mathbf Z_{J_N,N}-\boldsymbol\xi_{N,\sigma}\|_2>\varepsilon
\right)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{Y}
\]
The couplings in \textup{(Y)} may be selected jointly Borel measurably in
$\sigma$ and the stopped score: there is a Borel Markov kernel
$\mathcal K_N(\sigma,z,d\xi)$ from
$\mathcal H_N(C)\times\mathbb R^{d_{J_N}}$ to
$\mathbb R^{d_{J_N}}$ whose second marginal is standard Gaussian and whose
uniform quadratic transport cost tends to zero.
In particular, the same equivalence holds at the fixed exchange point
$\sigma_0$.

Replacing the deterministic cycle count by the
calendar stopping index and replacing $\mu$ by $\widehat\mu_N$ contribute,
respectively,
\[
 O_P(J_NN^{-1/4})\quad\hbox{and}\quad O_P(J_NN^{-1/2})
 \tag{Z}
\]
in Euclidean norm.  The local null covariance perturbation contributes
$O_P(J_N^3N^{-1/2})$; all three terms are uniformly $o_P(1)$ under
\textup{(X)}.

The necessity in \textup{(X)} already follows from the fact that a standard
Gaussian limit for the moving corner coordinate
$e_{J_N}\otimes e_{J_N}$ requires
\[
 n_NS_{J_N}^2\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{AA}
\]
More strongly, if some $r_N<J_N$ satisfies
$n_NS_{r_N}^2\to0$ and $J_N-r_N\asymp J_N$, then, with probability tending
to one, every score in the block
\[
 \{e_a\otimes e_b:r_N\leq a,b\leq J_N\}
 \tag{AB}
\]
is zero.  The corresponding standard Gaussian block has
$(J_N-r_N+1)^2$ degrees of freedom, so neither an unweighted chi-square
approximation for that block nor an $o_P(1)$ Euclidean coupling can hold.

In the second-order band
$2x_0J_N=\log n_N+O(\log\log n_N)$, put
\[
 \omega_N=\log n_N+2\log\log n_N-2x_0J_N.
\]
Then \textup{(Y)} holds if and only if $\omega_N\to\infty$.  Equivalently,
the sharp second-order region is
\[
 J_N\leq
 \frac{\log n_N+2\log\log n_N-\omega_N}{2x_0}
 \tag{AC}
\]
for some $\omega_N\to\infty$.
If
$2x_0J_N=\log n_N+2\log\log n_N-c+o(1)$ for a fixed $c\in\mathbb R$, then
$n_NS_{J_N}^2\to e^c/4$, so \textup{(Y)} fails.  The previously verified
necessary boundary is therefore also sufficient; no assertion of Le Cam
equivalence is made.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The equal-rate dictionary gives the displayed formula for $S_j$.
The two values in \textup{(V)} have weighted mean zero and weighted squared
norm one.  Nested supports then give
$\langle e_j,e_k\rangle_{L^2(g)}=0$ for $j\neq k$; completeness follows by
successively separating the atoms $0,1,\ldots$ from the remaining tail.
Since $S_{j+1}/S_j\to p_0$ and $g_j/S_j\to1-p_0$,
\[
 \|e_j\|_\infty\asymp S_j^{-1/2},\qquad
 \max_{a,b\leq J}\|e_a\otimes e_b\|_\infty\asymp S_J^{-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]

\paragraph{Helmert cell-ratio and moment bounds.}
For the deterministic-cycle argument, let
$K_J(k)=\sum_{a=0}^Je_a(k)^2$.  Adding the constant function to the Helmert
contrasts gives the projection onto the partition
$\{0\},\ldots,\{J\},\{J+1,J+2,\ldots\}$; hence
\[
 1+K_J(k)=
 \begin{cases}
  g_k^{-1},&0\leq k\leq J,\\
  S_{J+1}^{-1},&k\geq J+1.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{2}
\]
The equal-rate exponential tail and \textup{(2)} imply
\[
 \EE K_J(G)^{3/2}\leq C S_J^{-1/2},\qquad
 \EE K_J(G)^2\leq C S_J^{-1}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus, for the vector $\boldsymbol\Phi_J(G_i,G_{i+1})$, independence of the
two gaps yields
\[
 \EE\|\boldsymbol\Phi_J(G_i,G_{i+1})\|_2^3\leq C S_J^{-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]

We first record the imported dimension-free bridge used below.  For
probability laws $\nu,\gamma\in\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)$ with the same
mean, let
\[
 Z_2(\nu,\gamma)
 =\sup_{\operatorname{Lip}(\nabla h)\leq1}
   \left|\int h\,d\nu-\int h\,d\gamma\right|.
\]
If $X_1,\ldots,X_M$ are independent centred vectors,
$W=\sum_bX_b$, and
$\Gamma=\sum_b\EE(X_bX_b^\top)\succ0$, then
\[
 Z_2\{\mathcal L(W),\mathcal N(0,\Gamma)\}
 \leq C\|\Gamma^{-1/2}\|_{\rm op}
       \sum_{b=1}^M\EE\|X_b\|_2^3.
 \tag{B0}
\]
Indeed, Meckes' coordinate-free estimate for the Gaussian Stein solution
$f_h$ gives
$M_3(f_h)\leq C\|\Gamma^{-1/2}\|_{\rm op}
\operatorname{Lip}(\nabla h)$
\cite{Meckes2009MultivariateStein}.  With $W_b=W-X_b$, Taylor expansion at
$W_b$, independence, and centring cancel the constant and quadratic terms.
The two remainders are bounded by a constant times
\[
 M_3(f_h)\sum_b
 \left\{\EE\|X_b\|_2^3
 +\EE\|X_b\|_2\,\EE\|X_b\|_2^2\right\}.
\]
H\"older's inequality bounds the product by $\EE\|X_b\|_2^3$, proving
\textup{(B0)}; smooth approximation removes the provisional smoothness of
$h$.  The sharp, dimension-free comparison of
Bo{\l}botowski--Bouchitt\'e is
\cite{BolbotowskiBouchitte2026ZolotarevWasserstein}
\[
 \frac14W_2^2(\nu,\gamma)\leq Z_2(\nu,\gamma).
 \tag{B0$'$}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 W_2\{\mathcal L(W),\mathcal N(0,\Gamma)\}
 \leq C\left(
  \|\Gamma^{-1/2}\|_{\rm op}\sum_b\EE\|X_b\|_2^3
 \right)^{1/2}.
 \tag{B0$''$}
\]
Neither \textup{(B0)} nor \textup{(B0$'$)} is claimed as a new general
probability inequality.

\paragraph{Independent-block sufficiency at the exchange point.}
Write $m=J+1$, let
$P_J:\mathbb R^{m^2}\to\mathbb R^{d_J}$ delete the $e_0\otimes e_0$
coordinate, and set
\[
 h_J(G)=(e_0(G),\ldots,e_J(G))^\top,
 \qquad
 Y_{i,J}=P_J\{h_J(G_i)\otimes h_J(G_{i+1})\}.
 \tag{B1}
\]
Then $\EE Y_{i,J}=0$ and
$\EE(Y_{i,J}Y_{i,J}^\top)=I_{d_J}$.  Moreover, double centring gives, for
all retained coordinates,
\[
 \EE\{e_a(G_i)e_b(G_{i+1})e_c(G_{i+1})e_d(G_{i+2})\}=0.
 \tag{B2}
\]
Thus distinct $Y_{i,J}$ are uncorrelated, and they are independent when their
indices differ by more than one.

Put $n=\lfloor n_N\rfloor$ and
$L=\lceil d_J^2+\log n\rceil$.  Partition the edge indices into consecutive
blocks of length at most $L$, deleting one connecting edge between successive
blocks.  The retained block sums are independent because their underlying
gap sets are disjoint.  If $I$ and $D$ denote the retained and deleted edge
sets, respectively, then
\[
 r:=|D|\leq \frac{n}{L+1}+L+1,
 \qquad
 \EE\left\|\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i\in D}Y_{i,J}\right\|_2^2
 =\frac{rd_J}{n}
 \leq C\left(\frac{d_J}{L}+\frac{Ld_J}{n}\right)=o(1).
 \tag{B3}
\]
For a retained block $B$, split its edges by parity.  Each parity class is
independent, so the Hilbert-space Rosenthal--Burkholder inequality
\cite{Pinelis1994OptimalMartingaleBounds}, together with \textup{(4)}, yields
\[
 \EE\left\|\sum_{i\in B}Y_{i,J}\right\|_2^3
 \leq C\{(Ld_J)^{3/2}+L/S_J\}.
 \tag{B4}
\]
Let $\mathcal B$ be the retained blocks, put
$X_B=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in B}Y_{i,J}$, and let
$V_B=(|B|/n)I_{d_J}$ be its covariance.  The actual-block third-moment
quantity is
\[
 \beta_N^{(Z)}
 :=\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}\EE\|X_B\|_2^3
 \leq C\left\{
  \frac{\sqrt L\,d_J^{3/2}}{\sqrt n}
  +\frac1{\sqrt n\,S_J}
 \right\}.
 \tag{B5}
\]
The factor $d_J^{3/2}$ has not been discarded.  It is contained explicitly
in the first term of \textup{(B5)}.  Since the covariance of the retained sum
$A=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in I}Y_{i,J}$ is $cI_{d_J}$, where $c=|I|/n\to1$,
\textup{(B0$''$)} yields a coupling with
$T\sim\mathcal N(0,cI_{d_J})$ satisfying
\[
 \EE\|A-T\|_2^2\leq C\beta_N^{(Z)}.
 \tag{B6}
\]
Under \textup{(X)}, $J=O(\log n)$, $d_J=O((\log n)^2)$, and
\[
 \frac{\sqrt L\,d_J^{3/2}}{\sqrt n}
 =O\!\left(\frac{(\log n)^5}{\sqrt n}\right)=o(1),
 \qquad
 \frac1{\sqrt n\,S_J}=o(1).
\]
Thus \textup{(B6)} is $o(1)$.  The independent-block estimate is used here
because CMU's Corollary~2.3 includes the Gaussian comparison blocks in its
moment quantity, and its
Euclidean probability bound has $\phi_2(d_J)^2=2d_J$; applied to
\textup{(B5)}, that theorem still requires
$d_J/(\sqrt nS_J)\to0$.  We neither omit the Gaussian blocks from CMU's
quantity nor use that corollary here; the dimension-free
\textup{(B0$'$)} route has a different, actual-block Stein quantity.

If $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,I_{d_J})$ is independent of $T$, then
\[
 \xi=T+\sqrt{1-c}\,Z\sim\mathcal N(0,I_{d_J}),
 \qquad
 \EE\|\xi-T\|_2^2=(1-c)d_J=\frac{rd_J}{n}=o(1).
 \tag{B7}
\]
Combining \textup{(B3)}--\textup{(B7)} proves the deterministic-cycle
exchange-point coupling under \textup{(X)}.

\paragraph{Calendar-stopping bound.}
The common exponential moment on the fixed local-null neighbourhood gives,
with one constant for every $\sigma\in\mathcal H_N(C)$,
\[
 \EE_\sigma|K_N-N/\mu_\sigma|^2\leq CN,
 \qquad |\mu_\sigma-\mu|\leq CN^{-1/2}.
\]
Hence $K_N-n_N=O_{P,\mathcal H_N(C)}(\sqrt N)$.  Conditional on a terminal
window of length $r$, double centring makes the vector edge scores
uncorrelated and their total second moment at most $Crd_J$.  The maximal
inequality applied on deterministic dyadic windows, followed by the preceding
renewal-count bound, gives
\[
 \sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal H_N(C)}
 \PP_\sigma\!\left(
  \left\|n_N^{-1/2}\!\sum_{i=n_N\wedge K_N}^{n_N\vee K_N}Y_{i,J}\right\|_2
  >M JN^{-1/4}\right)\longrightarrow0
\]
as $M\to\infty$.  This proves the first order in \textup{(Z)}.  On the event
$|\widehat\mu_N-\mu_\sigma|\leq M N^{-1/2}$, the square-root normalization
is Lipschitz on a common compact interval around $\mu$, and the normalized
score has Euclidean norm $O_{P,\mathcal H_N(C)}(J)$.  This gives the second
order $O_{P,\mathcal H_N(C)}(JN^{-1/2})$ in \textup{(Z)}.

\paragraph{Local-null cell ratio and moving covariance.}
Let $\mathcal P_J$ be the partition in \textup{(2)} and let
$g_N=g_{\sigma_N}$.  Analytic differentiation of the exact sampled-counter
atom and tail formulae at the exchange point gives, uniformly under
\textup{(T)} and for $J=O(\log N)$,
\[
 \max_{C\in\mathcal P_J}
 \left|\frac{g_N(C)}{g(C)}-1\right|
 \leq C N^{-1/2}(1+J^2).
 \tag{6}
\]
For the singleton cells this follows from the density-ratio derivatives,
which are $O(1+k^2)$ for $k\leq J$; for the final tail cell it follows by
differentiating the closed tail formula before taking the ratio.  Every
coordinate in \textup{(W)} is constant on each product cell of
$\mathcal P_J\otimes\mathcal P_J$.  Consequently \textup{(6)} implies
\[
 \|\Sigma_{J,N}-I_{d_J}\|_{\rm op}=O(J^2N^{-1/2}),
 \tag{7}
\]
where $\Sigma_{J,N}$ is the score covariance under $g_N$.  Moreover, if
$u_{a,N}=\EE_{g_N}e_a$, double centring under $g$ gives
\[
 \left\|(u_{a,N}u_{b,N})_{(a,b)\neq(0,0)}\right\|_2
 \leq\sum_{a=0}^J u_{a,N}^2=O(N^{-1}),
\tag{8}
\]
This bound is uniform in $J$.  Parseval on the partition in \textup{(2)}
bounds $\sum_{a=0}^Ju_{a,N}^2$ by the chi-square divergence of the partition
law of $g_N$ from that of $g$.  The sampled-counter density-ratio score has a
common square-integrable polynomial envelope on the compact parameter
neighbourhood, so this divergence is $O(N^{-1})$.
so the normalized score mean is $O(N^{-1/2})$.  Coupling
$\Sigma_{J,N}^{1/2}\boldsymbol\xi_N$ and $\boldsymbol\xi_N$ with the same
standard Gaussian vector, \textup{(7)} and the square-root perturbation
bound give
\[
 \|(\Sigma_{J,N}^{1/2}-I)\boldsymbol\xi_N\|_2
 =O_P(\sqrt{d_J}J^2N^{-1/2})=O_P(J^3N^{-1/2}).
 \tag{9}
\]
We now apply the same independent-block argument uniformly, rather than
returning to a dimension-dependent martingale coupling.  Put
$\overline Y_{i,J,N}=Y_{i,J}-\EE_{g_N}Y_{i,J}$.  The ratio in
\textup{(6)} is $o(1)$ because $J=O(\log N)$.  Hence the product-cell
density ratio is bounded above and below by fixed constants, uniformly over
$\sigma_N\in\mathcal H_N(C)$; these constants may depend on the fixed
$x_0,C$, but not on $N,J$, or $d_J$.  It follows that
\textup{(B3)}--\textup{(B5)} hold for
$\overline Y_{i,J,N}$ with one common constant.  Disjoint retained blocks
remain independent under every $\sigma_N\in\mathcal H_N(C)$.

Let $\Gamma_{I,N}$ be the covariance of the normalized retained centred
sum.  Besides the lag-zero perturbation in \textup{(7)}, direct factorization
over three independent gaps gives
\[
 \|\operatorname{Cov}_{g_N}(Y_{i,J},Y_{i+1,J})\|_{\rm op}
 \leq C\sum_{a=0}^Ju_{a,N}^2=O(N^{-1}).
\]
Consequently, uniformly on $\mathcal H_N(C)$,
\[
 \|\Gamma_{I,N}-cI_{d_J}\|_{\rm op}
 =O(J^2N^{-1/2}),
 \qquad \lambda_{\min}(\Gamma_{I,N})\geq\tfrac12
 \tag{9$'$}
\]
for all sufficiently large $N$.  Applying \textup{(B0$''$)} with
$\Gamma_{I,N}$ gives the same right-hand side as \textup{(B6)}.  Coupling
the two centred Gaussian vectors synchronously and using the square-root
perturbation bound yields
\[
 W_2\{\mathcal N(0,\Gamma_{I,N}),\mathcal N(0,cI_{d_J})\}
 \leq C\sqrt{d_J}\,\|\Gamma_{I,N}-cI_{d_J}\|_{\rm op}
 =O(J^3N^{-1/2}).
 \tag{9$''$}
\]
Equation \textup{(8)} makes the normalized uncentred mean $O(N^{-1/2})$.
Thus the deterministic-cycle coupling is uniform on $\mathcal H_N(C)$.
Condition \textup{(X)} implies $J_N=O(\log N)$, so
\textup{(9$''$)} and both calendar-stopping terms in \textup{(Z)} are
uniformly $o_P(1)$.  Markov's inequality applied to the squared
Wasserstein coupling cost proves \textup{(Y)}.

\paragraph{Measurability of the uniform coupling.}
For fixed $N$, the map
$\sigma\mapsto\mathcal L_\sigma(\mathbf Z_{J_N,N})$ is Borel in quadratic
Wasserstein space: the stopped score is a Borel function of the countable
gap record and every finite-cylinder probability is analytic in the physical
parameter.  The set of quadratic-cost optimal plans between this law and the
standard Gaussian is nonempty and compact, and its graph is Borel because
the cost is lower semicontinuous.  Measurable selection supplies a Borel map
$\sigma\mapsto\pi_{N,\sigma}$ of optimal plans.  Joint disintegration on the
standard Borel space gives a kernel
$\mathcal K_N(\sigma,z,d\xi)$, and one auxiliary uniform random variable
realizes that kernel on each original probability space.  Its integrated
cost is the selected $W_2^2$ cost.  All preceding constants are independent
of $\sigma\in\mathcal H_N(C)$, so the supremum of these costs tends to zero;
Markov's inequality gives the asserted jointly measurable form of
\textup{(Y)}.

\paragraph{Necessity in every bounded corner regime.}
The corner contrast is nonzero exactly when both gaps are at least $J$.
On that event its magnitude is bounded above and below by constant multiples
of $S_J^{-1}$, by \textup{(1)}.  Its triangular-array Lindeberg condition is
therefore exactly $\sqrt{n_N}S_{J_N}\to\infty$, which is \textup{(AA)}.
If $n_NS_{J_N}^2\to0$, the corner coordinate is zero with probability tending
to one.  If $n_NS_{J_N}^2\to c\in(0,\infty)$, the active-pair count converges
to $\operatorname{Poisson}(c)$: its factorial moments have the independent
rare-pair limits, while overlapping pairs contribute at most
$O(n_NS_{J_N}^3)=o(1)$.  The normalized corner coordinate consequently has
a compound-Poisson limit with a positive atom and cannot be standard normal.
If the corner intensity is bounded but has no limit, every subsequence has a
further subsequence in one of these two regimes.  Thus failure of
\textup{(X)} always supplies a subsequence on which even the fixed-point
corner marginal is non-Gaussian, which rules out the uniform Euclidean
coupling.  For \textup{(AB)}, the expected number of adjacent pairs with both
gaps at least $r_N$ is $O(n_NS_{r_N}^2)=o(1)$.  Markov's inequality proves
the zero-block assertion, and the Gaussian comparison follows from its
chi-square block energy.

Finally,
\[
 \log(n_NS_{J_N}^2)
 =\log n_N-2x_0J_N+2\log(1+x_0J_N).
 \tag{10}
\]
In the displayed second-order band,
$J_N\asymp\log n_N$ and
$2\log(1+x_0J_N)=2\log\log n_N-2\log2+o(1)$.
Hence
\[
 \log(n_NS_{J_N}^2)=\omega_N-2\log2+o(1).
\]
Together with the proved equivalence, this gives \textup{(AC)}.  Under the
fixed critical correction displayed in the proposition, \textup{(10)}
converges to $c-2\log2$, and therefore
$n_NS_{J_N}^2\to e^c/4$.
\end{proof}
