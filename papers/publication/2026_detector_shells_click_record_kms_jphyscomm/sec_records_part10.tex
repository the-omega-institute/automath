convention.  This exchange
non-identifiability does not remove the diagonal common-rate estimand in
\Cref{lem:diagonal-common-rate-main}, where the two entries are equal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two parameter points induce the same finite-dimensional visible law by
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}; hence every visible-record
statistic has the same distribution under them.  This is the exchange
specialization inside the present constrained D-MAP(2) family, consistent with the
broader MAP2 lesson that a hidden arrival representation need not be uniquely
identified from the stationary observable arrival law
\cite{RamirezCoboLilloWiper2010MAP2Nonidentifiability}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fully studentized lag-two covariance interval}\label{subsec:studentized-lag-two-main}

This subsection proves the lag-two covariance interval.  The first two lemmas
are generic finite-lag CLT and batch-covariance tools, reused here for the
scalar lag-two observable.  The remaining results verify the lag-two reward
identity, prove the positivity of the scalar variance, and state the observable
\(\widehat\sigma^2_{\gamma_2,N}\)-based interval, with no report when fewer
than two complete separated lag-two batches are available,
\eqref{eq:lag-two-studentized-interval-main}.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-lag regenerative CLT]\label{lem:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main}
Let \((A_n)\) be a stationary renewal indicator process with i.i.d. gaps
satisfying \(\PP(G\ge k)\le Ce^{-\eta k}\).  Let
\(Z_n=\psi(A_n,\ldots,A_{n+h_{\rm cal}})\in\RR^{d_{\rm rew}}\) be a bounded
centered local observable, with fixed reward dimension \(d_{\rm rew}\) and
fixed calendar look-ahead \(h_{\rm cal}\).  Let
\[
m_{\rm dep}:=q_*(0,h_{\rm cal})=\max\{h_{\rm cal}-1,0\},
\]
where \(q_*\) is the explicit local-window range from
\Cref{prop:standard-complete-cycle-separated-batch-facts-main}.  Define the Palm
cycle reward by the measurable map
\[
Y_j:=F_{0,h_{\rm cal}}(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+m_{\rm dep}}).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
Y_j=f(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+m_{\rm dep}})\in\RR^{d_{\rm rew}},\qquad
\|f\|\le C_f\left(1+\sum_{i=0}^{m_{\rm dep}}G_{j+i}\right).
\]
Put \(\mu=\EE(G+1)\) and
\[
\mathcal T_{m_{\rm dep}}=
\Var(Y_0)+
\sum_{h=1}^{m_{\rm dep}}\{\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_h)+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_h,Y_0)\}.
\]
Then the exact calendar-time empirical vector average satisfies
\[
\sqrt N\left(\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Z_n\right)
=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Z_n
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\left(0,\frac{\mathcal T_{m_{\rm dep}}}{\mu}\right).
\]
In the scalar case this covariance reduces to \(\tau_{m_{\rm dep}}^2/\mu\),
where
\[
\tau_{m_{\rm dep}}^2=\Var(Y_0)+2\sum_{h=1}^{m_{\rm dep}}\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_h).
\]
The same conclusion holds after the finite-start burn-in in
\Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}.  In the constrained D-MAP
applications below, \((A_n)\) is the retained visible renewal record from that
standing assumption.  No continuous-comparator or growing-window perturbation
statement is used in this lemma.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{prop:standard-complete-cycle-separated-batch-facts-main} supplies the displayed
Palm reward \(Y_j=F_{0,h_{\rm cal}}(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+m_{\rm dep}})\), its
centering, fixed \(m_{\rm dep}\)-dependence, and the
\(O_{\PP}(\log N)\) endpoint replacement for the local calendar sum.
\Cref{prop:standard-complete-cycle-separated-batch-facts-main} then gives the
deterministic-prefix CLT and the renewal random-index replacement, with
calendar-time covariance \(\mathcal T_{m_{\rm dep}}/\mu\).  The finite-start
burn-in case follows by applying
\Cref{lem:finite-hidden-start-path-contraction-main} to the retained
post-burn-in block.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite-lag separated-batch covariance]\label{lem:finite-lag-separated-batch-main}
Under the hypotheses and notation of
\Cref{lem:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main}, form all-complete-cycle batches of
cycle rewards \(Y_j\) with deterministic length
\[
b_N=\lfloor N^\beta\rfloor,\qquad 0<\beta<1,
\]
and leave \(m_{\rm dep}\) complete cycle rewards between consecutive batches.
Let \(K_N=\max\{k:\tau_k<N\}\), \(d_N=b_N+m_{\rm dep}\), and use only batch
indices for which every local calendar window entering the batched rewards is
observed.  With
\[
I_{\ell,N}=\{(\ell-1)d_N,\ldots,(\ell-1)d_N+b_N-1\},
\qquad
B_{\ell,N}=\sum_{j\in I_{\ell,N}}Y_j,
\]
and \(M_N\) the number of all-complete-cycle batches selected before calendar
time \(N\), with \(M_N=0\) when no such batch exists.  Define
\(\bar B_N=M_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}B_{\ell,N}\) on
\(\{M_N\ge1\}\), and set the auxiliary completed statistic
\[
\widehat\Sigma_N^{\rm batch,aux}
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{K_N}{N}\,
\frac1{b_N(M_N-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_N}(B_{\ell,N}-\bar B_N)(B_{\ell,N}-\bar B_N)^\top,
&M_N\ge2,\\[1.1ex]
0_{d_{\rm rew}\times d_{\rm rew}},
&M_N<2 .
\end{cases}
\]
Then \(M_N\ge2\) with probability tending to one and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-lag-separated-batch-rate-main}
\left\|\widehat\Sigma_N^{\rm batch,aux}
-\frac{\mathcal T_{m_{\rm dep}}}{\mu}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=O_{\PP}\left(b_N^{-1}+\sqrt{\frac{b_N}{N}}\right).
\end{equation}
Thus the auxiliary completed statistic estimates the calendar-time covariance
\(\mathcal T_{m_{\rm dep}}/\mu\) at the displayed rate.  The operational
finite-record report uses the \(M_N\ge2\) branch only; on \(\{M_N<2\}\) it
reports insufficient complete cycles.

In the constrained D-MAP applications below, \((A_n)\) is the retained visible
renewal record from \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}.  No
continuous-comparator or growing-window perturbation statement is used in this
lemma.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{prop:standard-complete-cycle-separated-batch-facts-main} identifies the
rewards as an \(m_{\rm dep}\)-dependent fixed-window function of the i.i.d.
gaps, and the exponential gap tail gives the moment hypothesis of
\Cref{prop:calendar-stopped-separated-batch-plugin-main}.  That appendix
fixed-range wrapper gives the displayed separated-batch rate, including the
calendar-stopped batch count,
endpoint/look-ahead deletion, and empirical-mean subtraction.  Since
\(b_N=\lfloor N^\beta\rfloor\) with \(0<\beta<1\), the retained batch count
satisfies \(M_N\ge2\) with probability tending to one; the low-cycle
exception is covered by \Cref{prop:standard-complete-cycle-separated-batch-facts-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Observed lag-two windows in the all-complete-cycle batches]\label{lem:all-cycle-lag-two-windows-main}
For lag-two rewards, using all-complete-cycle batches and leaving one complete
cycle after the last batched cycle ensures every term \(A_nA_{n+2}\) used by
the statistic is observed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(n\in[\tau_j,\tau_{j+1}-1]\), then \(A_{n+2}\) is determined by the
click pattern up to time \(\tau_{j+1}+1\).  This time lies no later than the
end of cycle \(j+1\): if \(G_j\ge1\), then \(\tau_{j+1}+1\le\tau_{j+2}\);
if \(G_j=0\), then the cycle \(j\) interval contains only \(n=\tau_j\), and
\(A_{\tau_j+2}\) is determined by whether \(G_{j+1}=0\).  Thus every lag-two
term assigned to cycle \(j\) is determined by \(G_j\) and \(G_{j+1}\).  The
all-complete-cycle convention uses only batched indices for which cycle
\(j+1\) is complete and, at the terminal edge, leaves one complete cycle after
the last batched cycle.  Hence no term \(A_nA_{n+2}\) in a used batch reaches
outside the observed record.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lag-two reward identity, centering, and one-gap dependence]\label{lem:lag-two-reward-main}
For \(Z_n=(A_n-\rho)(A_{n+2}-\rho)-\gamma_2\), the Palm cycle reward has the
explicit form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-cycle-reward-main}
Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1}),
\end{equation}
where
\[
f(g,h)=
\mathbf1_{\{g=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{g=0,\ h=0\}}
-\rho\bigl(1+\mathbf1_{\{g\ge1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{h=0\}}\bigr)
+(\rho^2-\gamma_2)(g+1).
\]
In particular \(\EE Y_j=0\), and \((Y_j)\) is \(1\)-dependent in the cycle
index.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work in Palm coordinates with \(\tau_j=0\).  The cycle \(j\) contains the
times \(0,1,\ldots,G_j\), the next click is at \(G_j+1\), and the following
click is at \(G_j+G_{j+1}+2\).  Hence
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{G_j}A_n=1,
\]
while the shifted count in the same cycle is
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{G_j}A_{n+2}
=\mathbf1_{\{G_j\ge1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_{j+1}=0\}}.
\]
The lag-two product count is nonzero only when the click at the cycle start
is followed by another click exactly two sampled steps later:
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{G_j}A_nA_{n+2}
=\mathbf1_{\{G_j=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{G_j=0,\ G_{j+1}=0\}}.
\]
Summing
\((A_n-\rho)(A_{n+2}-\rho)-\gamma_2\) over the cycle therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y_j
&=\sum_{n=0}^{G_j}A_nA_{n+2}
-\rho\sum_{n=0}^{G_j}A_n
-\rho\sum_{n=0}^{G_j}A_{n+2}
+(\rho^2-\gamma_2)(G_j+1)  \\
&=f(G_j,G_{j+1}),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is \eqref{eq:lag-two-cycle-reward-main}.

The centering is not an additional assumption.  The stationary renewal record
has \(\EE[(A_0-\rho)(A_2-\rho)-\gamma_2]=0\), and Palm inversion gives
\[
\EE Y_j
=\EE L\,
\EE\bigl[(A_0-\rho)(A_2-\rho)-\gamma_2\bigr]
=0.
\]
Finally, \(Y_j\) is a function only of the adjacent pair
\((G_j,G_{j+1})\).  If \(|j-k|>1\), the pairs
\((G_j,G_{j+1})\) and \((G_k,G_{k+1})\) are disjoint functions of i.i.d. gaps,
so \(Y_j\) and \(Y_k\) are independent.  Thus the reward sequence is
\(1\)-dependent.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Hoeffding row--column decomposition and coboundary zero criterion]\label{lem:row-column-coboundary-criterion-main}
Let \(G_0,G_1,G_2\) be i.i.d. with a full-support law \(g\) on
\(\mathbb N_0\).  Let \(f:\mathbb N_0^2\to\RR\) have
\(\EE f(G_0,G_1)^2<\infty\), and put
\[
M=\EE f(G_0,G_1),\qquad
R_i=\EE\{f(i,G_1)\},\qquad
C_i=\EE\{f(G_0,i)\}.
\]
Then, with \(X=f(G_0,G_1)\), \(X'=f(G_1,G_2)\), and
\[
E_{ij}=f(i,j)-R_i-C_j+M,
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-column-variance-decomposition-main}
\EE(X-M)^2+2\EE\{(X-M)(X'-M)\}
=
\EE E_{G_0,G_1}^2+\EE(R_{G_0}+C_{G_0}-2M)^2 .
\end{equation}
Consequently the zero-variance coboundary criterion is the following: the
left-hand side is zero if and only if there is a sequence
\((u_i)_{i\ge0}\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row-column-zero-coboundary-main}
f(i,j)-M=u_i-u_j,\qquad i,j\ge0 .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The residual has zero conditional mean in each coordinate:
\[
\EE\{E_{G_0,G_1}\mid G_0\}=0,\qquad
\EE\{E_{G_0,G_1}\mid G_1\}=0 .
\]
The Hoeffding row--column decomposition
\[
X-M=(R_{G_0}-M)+(C_{G_1}-M)+E_{G_0,G_1}
\]
is therefore orthogonal in \(L^2\).  Hence
\[
\EE(X-M)^2
=\EE(R_{G_0}-M)^2+\EE(C_{G_1}-M)^2+\EE E_{G_0,G_1}^2 .
\]
For the adjacent copy \(X'\), independence gives the only nonzero cross-term
after conditioning on the shared gap:
\[
\EE\{(X-M)(X'-M)\}
=\EE\{\EE(X-M\mid G_1)\EE(X'-M\mid G_1)\}
=\EE\{(C_{G_1}-M)(R_{G_1}-M)\}.
\]
Adding the preceding two displays proves
\eqref{eq:row-column-variance-decomposition-main}.

Both terms on the right of
\eqref{eq:row-column-variance-decomposition-main} are nonnegative.  Since
\(g_i>0\) for every \(i\), equality to zero is equivalent to
\[
E_{ij}=0,\qquad R_i+C_i=2M,\qquad i,j\ge0 .
\]
The first relation gives \(f(i,j)-M=(R_i-M)+(C_j-M)\).  The second gives
\(C_j-M=-(R_j-M)\).  Thus
\eqref{eq:row-column-zero-coboundary-main} holds with \(u_i=R_i-M\).
Conversely, if \eqref{eq:row-column-zero-coboundary-main} holds, then
\(R_i-M=u_i-\EE u_{G_0}\), \(C_i-M=\EE u_{G_0}-u_i\), and
\(E_{ij}=0\), so both square terms vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Explicit lag-two variance and finite-pair nondegeneracy certificate]\label{lem:lag-two-variance-nondegenerate-main}
Let \(g_k=\PP(G=k)\) be the full-support gap law from
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}, let \(\mu=\EE(G+1)=1/\rho\), and let
\[
f(i,j)=
\mathbf1_{\{i=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{i=0,\ j=0\}}
-\rho\bigl(1+\mathbf1_{\{i\ge1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{j=0\}}\bigr)
+(\rho^2-\gamma_2)(i+1),
\qquad i,j\ge0 .
\]
Equivalently, for independent gaps \(G_0,G_1,G_2\) with law \(g\),
the centered Palm reward for
\((A_n-\rho)(A_{n+2}-\rho)-\gamma_2\) is \(f(G_j,G_{j+1})\).  The
lag-two calendar-time variance in
\Cref{cor:finite-record-lag-two-studentized-main} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-probabilistic-variance-main}
\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2
=\frac{
\EE f(G_0,G_1)^2
+2\EE\{f(G_0,G_1)f(G_1,G_2)\}}
{\EE(G+1)} .
\end{equation}
It is also the absolutely convergent double/triple sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-explicit-variance-main}
\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2
=\frac1{\mu}\left[
\sum_{i,j\ge0}f(i,j)^2g_ig_j
+2\sum_{i,j,k\ge0}f(i,j)f(j,k)g_ig_jg_k
\right].
\end{equation}
Thus numerical verification of the studentizing variance is finite and
monotone: truncate the two sums to \(0\le i,j,k\le K\), bound the omitted tail
with any slack envelope \(g_k\le C_{\bar r}\bar r^k\),
\(\bar r\in(\max\{p,s\},1)\), from
\Cref{lem:killed-leakage-gap-exponential-moment-main} and the linear growth of
\(f\), and increase \(K\) until the tail bound is below the required tolerance.  The
closed nondegeneracy certificate below shows that this numerical recipe is a
check on arithmetic rather than an additional assumption.
Equivalently, with
\[
R_i=\sum_{j\ge0}f(i,j)g_j,\qquad
C_i=\sum_{j\ge0}f(j,i)g_j,\qquad
M=\sum_{i,j\ge0}f(i,j)g_ig_j,
\]
one has the sum-of-squares form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-variance-squares-main}
\mu\,\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2
=\sum_{i\ge0}g_i(R_i+C_i-2M)^2
+\sum_{i,j\ge0}g_ig_j\bigl(f(i,j)-R_i-C_j+M\bigr)^2 .
\end{equation}
The zero-variance criterion from \eqref{eq:row-column-zero-coboundary-main}
reduces nondegeneracy to checking that such a coboundary cannot hold.  The
finite certificate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-finite-nondegeneracy-certificate-main}
f(0,0)-f(0,1)=1-\rho,\qquad
f(1,0)-f(1,1)=-\rho
\end{equation}
will be compared after the zero-variance criterion is identified in the proof.
It excludes the only possible zero-variance coboundary.  Hence
the expression in \eqref{eq:lag-two-probabilistic-variance-main} is strictly
positive.
In particular \(\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2>0\) for every
\(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau>0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{lem:lag-two-reward-main}, the centered Palm reward is
\(Y_j=f(G_j,G_{j+1})\), where the gaps are i.i.d. with law \(g\).  The
exponential tail of \(g\) and the linear growth of \(f(i,j)\) in \(i\) give
absolute convergence of all displayed second-moment sums.  Since
\(\EE Y_j=0\) and the sequence is \(1\)-dependent,
\[
\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2
=\frac{\Var(Y_0)+2\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)}{\mu}
=\frac{\EE(Y_0^2)+2\EE(Y_0Y_1)}{\mu},
\]
which is exactly \eqref{eq:lag-two-probabilistic-variance-main} and
\eqref{eq:lag-two-explicit-variance-main} after expanding
\[
\EE(Y_0^2)=\sum_{i,j\ge0}f(i,j)^2g_ig_j,\qquad
\EE(Y_0Y_1)=\sum_{i,j,k\ge0}f(i,j)f(j,k)g_ig_jg_k .
\]

It remains to make the nondegeneracy explicit rather than implicit.  The
unconditional mean \(M=\EE f(G_0,G_1)\) is zero, because \(\EE Y_j=0\), but
we keep it in the displayed row--column formula.  Applying
\Cref{lem:row-column-coboundary-criterion-main} gives
\eqref{eq:lag-two-variance-squares-main}.  The same lemma also states the
only way the variance can vanish: because the gap law has full support, there
would have to be a sequence \((u_i)\) such that
\[
f(i,j)=u_i-u_j,\qquad i,j\ge0 .
\]
This coboundary form has a row-independent difference between any two fixed
columns:
\[
f(i,0)-f(i,1)=u_1-u_0,\qquad i\ge0 .
\]
For the lag-two reward this fails on the finite \(2\times2\) corner.  Directly
from the displayed formula for \(f\),
\[
f(0,0)-f(0,1)=1-\rho,\qquad
f(1,0)-f(1,1)=-\rho .
\]
These two values are unequal for \(0<\rho<1\).  Hence the zero-variance
coboundary case is excluded, and \(\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2>0\) throughout the
positive parameter domain.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Trimmed shifted-click centering]\label{lem:trimmed-shifted-click-centering-main}
Let \((A_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be a stationary renewal indicator process with
i.i.d. gaps \(G_j\) satisfying \(\PP(G\ge k)\le Ce^{-\eta k}\), click epochs
\((\tau_j)\), and click density \(\rho=\EE A_0\).  Fix
\(\ell\ge0\).  Let \(J_N^{(\ell)}\) be any all-complete-cycle index set
obtained from the complete observed cycles in \([0,N)\) by deleting only a
fixed number of endpoint cycles and by requiring that every term
\(A_{n+\ell}\), \(n\in[\tau_j,\tau_{j+1})\), is observed.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trimmed-shifted-click-centering-main}
\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_N^{(\ell)}}
\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}(A_{n+\ell}-\rho)
=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}) .
\end{equation}
Consequently, for any fixed finite set of shifts
\(\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), fixed coefficients \(c_\ell\), and any
all-complete-cycle index set on which all the shifted terms are observed and
which differs from the complete-cycle set by only fixed endpoint trimming,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trimmed-shifted-linear-combination-main}
\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_N}
\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
\sum_{\ell\in\Lambda}c_\ell(A_{n+\ell}-\rho)
=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}) .
\end{equation}
In particular the conclusion holds for the lag-two all-complete-cycle set
\(J_{2,N}\) with \(\ell=0\) and \(\ell=2\), and for their sum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{prop:standard-complete-cycle-separated-batch-facts-main} to the bounded
local observable \(Z_n=A_{n+\ell}-\rho\), with \(r=0\) and the fixed
look-ahead \(\ell\).  Since the process is stationary,
\(\EE Z_0=0\).  Palm inversion therefore centers the assigned cycle reward
\[
W_j^{(\ell)}
=\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}(A_{n+\ell}-\rho)
\]
by the explicit local-window map with \(r=0\):
\(\EE W_j^{(\ell)}=0\).  The reward is a function of the fixed future gap
block \(F_{0,\ell}(G_j,\ldots,G_{j+q_\ell})\), where
\(q_\ell=\max\{\ell-1,0\}\).  Moreover
\(|W_j^{(\ell)}|\le L_j+\ell+1\), so the exponential tail of \(G_j\) gives
\(\EE |W_j^{(\ell)}|^2<\infty\).  Hence the centered sequence
\((W_j^{(\ell)})\) is \(q_\ell\)-dependent with finite variance, and its
partial sums over any deterministic \(m=O(N)\) consecutive cycles are
\(O_{\PP}(\sqrt N)\).

The renewal law gives \(K_N=O_{\PP}(N)\) for the number of complete cycles
intersecting \([0,N)\).  Replacing the deterministic \(O(N)\) prefix by the
calendar-stopped prefix \(K_N\), or by a subset that differs from it by only a
fixed number of endpoint and look-ahead cycles, changes the preceding partial
sum by \(O_{\PP}(\log N)\) by the endpoint bound in
\Cref{prop:standard-complete-cycle-separated-batch-facts-main}.  Thus the numerator
in \eqref{eq:trimmed-shifted-click-centering-main} is \(O_{\PP}(\sqrt N)\),
and division by \(N\) proves the claim.
The linear-combination form follows by applying the preceding bound to each
fixed shift after replacing \(J_N\) by the corresponding shift-specific
observed all-complete-cycle set; the finitely many discrepancies between these
sets are endpoint cycles, hence contribute only \(O_{\PP}(\log N)\) before
division by \(N\).  A fixed finite linear combination of
\(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\) terms is again \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).
\end{proof}



\begin{corollary}[Lag-two studentized scalar interval]\label{cor:finite-record-lag-two-studentized-main}
Work under \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}.  Define the observed
complete-cycle lag-two covariance estimator by
\[
\widehat\gamma_{2,N}
=\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_{2,N}}\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
(A_n-\widehat\rho_N)(A_{n+2}-\widehat\rho_N),
\qquad
\widehat\rho_N=\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}A_n,
\]
where \(J_{2,N}\) contains exactly those all-complete-cycle indices for which
the lag-two windows lie in the observed record.
This is the paper's fully studentized scalar visible-record interval.

The scalar studentizer used for this statistic is the following observable
separated-batch estimator.  Let
\[
b_N=\lfloor N^\beta\rfloor,\qquad 0<\beta<1,\qquad d_N=b_N+1,
\]
and let
\[
M_{2,N}=\max\{m\ge0:(m-1)d_N+b_N-1\in J_{2,N}\},
\qquad
I^{\gamma}_{\ell,N}
=\{(\ell-1)d_N,\ldots,(\ell-1)d_N+b_N-1\},
\]
with \(M_{2,N}=0\) if the displayed set is empty.  For
\(j\in J_{2,N}\), define the plug-in lag-two cycle reward
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-plugin-cycle-reward-main}
\widehat Y^{\gamma}_{j,N}
=
\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
\left\{(A_n-\widehat\rho_N)(A_{n+2}-\widehat\rho_N)
-\widehat\gamma_{2,N}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Put
\[
\widehat C^{\gamma}_{\ell,N}
=\sum_{j\in I^{\gamma}_{\ell,N}}\widehat Y^{\gamma}_{j,N},
\qquad
\bar{\widehat C}^{\gamma}_N
=M_{2,N}^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{2,N}}\widehat C^{\gamma}_{\ell,N}
\quad\text{on }\{M_{2,N}\ge1\}.
\]
The scalar lag-two variance estimator is reported only when at least two
complete separated lag-two batches are available.  For the proof of consistency
on a total probability space, use the auxiliary completed statistic
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-scalar-studentizer-main}
\widehat\sigma^{2,\rm aux}_{\gamma_2,N}
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{K_N}{N}\,
\frac1{b_N(M_{2,N}-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{2,N}}
\left(\widehat C^{\gamma}_{\ell,N}
-\bar{\widehat C}^{\gamma}_N\right)^2,
&M_{2,N}\ge2,\\[1.1ex]
0,&M_{2,N}<2 .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
On \(\{M_{2,N}<2\}\) the lag-two interval is not reported; the zero branch in
\(\widehat\sigma^{2,\rm aux}_{\gamma_2,N}\) is used only in the convergence
proof.  On \(\{M_{2,N}\ge2\}\), put
\(\widehat\sigma^2_{\gamma_2,N}:=
\widehat\sigma^{2,\rm aux}_{\gamma_2,N}\).
Then
\[
\sqrt N(\widehat\gamma_{2,N}-\gamma_2)
\Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2),
\]
where \(\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2\) is the explicit positive variance
\eqref{eq:lag-two-explicit-variance-main}, and
\[
\widehat\sigma^{2,\rm aux}_{\gamma_2,N}
\xrightarrow{\PP}\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2 .
\]
Consequently, for \(0<\alpha<1\), with
\(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) denoting the standard-normal \(1-\alpha/2\) quantile and
\(\widehat\sigma_{\gamma_2,N}=(\widehat\sigma^2_{\gamma_2,N})^{1/2}\),
the fixed-setting two-sided interval reported on \(\{M_{2,N}\ge2\}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-studentized-interval-main}
\left[
\widehat\gamma_{2,N}
-z_{1-\alpha/2}\frac{\widehat\sigma_{\gamma_2,N}}{\sqrt N},
\quad
\widehat\gamma_{2,N}
+z_{1-\alpha/2}\frac{\widehat\sigma_{\gamma_2,N}}{\sqrt N}
\right]
\end{equation}
has limiting coverage \(1-\alpha\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:lag-two-reward-main} identifies the complete-cycle reward for the
true-centered statistic as a centered \(1\)-dependent reward, and
\Cref{lem:all-cycle-lag-two-windows-main} verifies that every lag-two term
used by \(J_{2,N}\) is observed.  Thus the \(m_{\rm dep}=1\) case of
\Cref{lem:finite-lag-renewal-reward-main} applies with
\[
\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2
=\frac{\Var(Y_0)+2\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_1)}{\EE(G+1)}.
\]
\Cref{lem:lag-two-variance-nondegenerate-main} identifies this quantity with
the double/triple sum \eqref{eq:lag-two-explicit-variance-main} and proves
that it is strictly positive on the full positive parameter domain.
Replacing \(\rho\) by \(\widehat\rho_N\) in the displayed estimator changes
the true-centered average by
\[
-(\widehat\rho_N-\rho)\frac1N
\sum_{j\in J_{2,N}}\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
\bigl((A_n-\rho)+(A_{n+2}-\rho)\bigr)
+(\widehat\rho_N-\rho)^2\frac1N\sum_{j\in J_{2,N}}L_j .
\]
The stationarity and exponential renewal tail give the ordinary record mean
\(\widehat\rho_N-\rho=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).  The trimmed bracket is exactly the
linear-combination estimate
\eqref{eq:trimmed-shifted-linear-combination-main} with
\(\Lambda=\{0,2\}\) and \(c_0=c_2=1\):
\[
\frac1N
\sum_{j\in J_{2,N}}\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
\bigl((A_n-\rho)+(A_{n+2}-\rho)\bigr)
=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2}).
\]
Also \(N^{-1}\sum_{j\in J_{2,N}}L_j=O_{\PP}(1)\).  Therefore the two plug-in
terms are respectively \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1})\) and \(O_{\PP}(N^{-1})\), hence
\(o_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).  The CLT therefore has the same variance as the
true-centered reward.

It remains only to identify the displayed scalar studentizer with the
batch-estimated variance of that same limiting reward.  Let
\[
Y^\gamma_j
=\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
\{(A_n-\rho)(A_{n+2}-\rho)-\gamma_2\},
\qquad
C^\gamma_{\ell,N}=\sum_{j\in I^\gamma_{\ell,N}}Y^\gamma_j .
\]
Write
\(\bar C^\gamma_N=M_{2,N}^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{2,N}}C^\gamma_{\ell,N}\)
on \(\{M_{2,N}\ge1\}\).
The \(m_{\rm dep}=1\) case of
\Cref{lem:finite-lag-separated-batch-main} applied to
\((Y^\gamma_j)\) gives consistency of
\[
\frac{K_N}{N}\,
\frac1{b_N(M_{2,N}-1)}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{2,N}}
\left(C^\gamma_{\ell,N}-\bar C^\gamma_N\right)^2
\]
for \(\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2\), with the same auxiliary zero completion on
\(\{M_{2,N}<2\}\), an event whose probability tends to zero and on which the
lag-two interval is not reported.

For the plug-in batch rewards, write
\[
\Delta_{\rho,N}=\widehat\rho_N-\rho,\qquad
\Delta_{\gamma,N}=\widehat\gamma_{2,N}-\gamma_2,
\]
and
\[
W_j=\sum_{n=\tau_j}^{\tau_{j+1}-1}
\bigl\{(A_n-\rho)+(A_{n+2}-\rho)\bigr\}.
\]
Then, for every used cycle,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-two-plugin-cycle-difference-main}
\widehat Y^\gamma_{j,N}-Y^\gamma_j
=-\Delta_{\rho,N}W_j
+(\Delta_{\rho,N}^2-\Delta_{\gamma,N})L_j .
\end{equation}
The CLT already proved above gives
\(\Delta_{\gamma,N}=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\), and the ordinary renewal mean gives
\(\Delta_{\rho,N}=O_{\PP}(N^{-1/2})\).  Since \(|W_j|\le2L_j+4\),
\Cref{prop:calendar-stopped-separated-batch-plugin-main} gives the same uniform
\(O_{\PP}(b_NN^{-1/2})\) block perturbation as
\eqref{eq:separated-batch-plugin-block-main}.  The plug-in covariance
perturbation bound \eqref{eq:separated-batch-plugin-covariance-main}, applied
in this scalar \(m=1\) setting, is \(o_{\PP}(1)\) because \(b_N/N\to0\).
Thus
\(\widehat\sigma^{2,\rm aux}_{\gamma_2,N}\to_{\PP}\sigma_{\gamma_2}^2\), and
the reported \(\widehat\sigma^2_{\gamma_2,N}\) has the same limit on the
retained event.
Since the limiting variance is positive by
\Cref{lem:lag-two-variance-nondegenerate-main}, Slutsky's theorem gives the
studentized statistic and hence the interval
\eqref{eq:lag-two-studentized-interval-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Side-data-only labelled-rate transport after the quotient inverse]\label{cor:side-data-labelled-rate-wald-main}
This corollary is not a visible-record parameter-estimation result.  It is the
ordinary delta-method transport obtained after the quotient-only estimator
\(\Phi(\Rinch_N)\) has been formed and after external side data have
chosen an ordering of the two separated roots.  It also conditions on the
sampling interval \(\Delta\tau\) being a known design interval; otherwise the
same construction transports only the ordered dimensionless products.  Work
under the fixed finite-record experiment and reporting gate \(\mathcal E_N\).
Suppose the quotient population point is separated:
\[
\Delta_\sigma=(p-s)^2>0,\qquad p,s\in(0,1),
\]
and suppose the visible-record estimator satisfies the inclusion CLT and
covariance rate used in \Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main}:
\[
\sqrt N(\Rinch_N-\Rinc)\Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,\Sigma_r),\qquad
\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}^{\rm tot}-\Sigma_r\|_{\operatorname{op}}
=o_{\PP}(t_N)
\]
for the retained-rank threshold \(t_N\).  Add one of the following side-data
hypotheses, neither of which is a consequence of the visible record:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0.35em]
\item A deterministic oracle branch map \(\mathcal B\) is fixed on a
neighbourhood of the unordered root set and orders the two roots as the
attempt and recovery branches.
\item A calibration or side-data estimator \(\widehat{\mathcal B}_N\) is used
only on an event \(\mathcal C_N\) such that
\[
\PP(\mathcal C_N)\to1,\qquad
\mathcal C_N\subseteq
\{\widehat{\mathcal B}_N=\mathcal B
\text{ on the retained separated-root neighbourhood}\}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

Then the visible-record part of the construction supplies only
\(\Phi(\Rinch_N)\) and the unordered root set.  On \(\mathcal E_N\) and on
the corresponding side-data retained chart, after applying the external branch
map, the ordered labelled-rate transport
\[
(\widehat\Gamma_N,\widehat\kappa_{\mathrm r,N})
=\left(-\frac{\log\widehat p_N}{\Delta\tau},
       -\frac{\log\widehat s_N}{\Delta\tau}\right)
\]
satisfies
\[
\sqrt N\{(\widehat\Gamma_N,\widehat\kappa_{\mathrm r,N})
-(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\}
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa}),
\qquad
\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa}
=J_{\Gamma,\kappa}(\Rinc)\Sigma_rJ_{\Gamma,\kappa}(\Rinc)^\top .
\]
With
\[
\widehat\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa,N}
=\widehat J_{\Gamma,\kappa,N}\widehat\Sigma_{r,N}
\widehat J_{\Gamma,\kappa,N}^{\top},
\]
where \(\widehat J_{\Gamma,\kappa,N}\) is
\eqref{eq:inclusion-to-rate-jacobian-main} evaluated on the retained labelled
chart, the covariance is consistent at the same operator-norm scale.  If
\[
\|\widehat\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa,N}
-\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa}\|_{\operatorname{op}}=o_{\PP}(t_N),
\]
then the thresholded retained-rank Wald statistic converges to
\(\chi_k^2\), \(k=\operatorname{rank}\Sigma_{\Gamma,\kappa}\).  No labelled-rate
conclusion is asserted on \(\Delta_\sigma=0\), without an oracle map, or for an
estimated branch map outside the high-probability agreement event.  In
particular, this corollary never converts Theorem~B into identification of an
ordered hidden labelled D-MAP representation from the visible law alone.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On a separated-root neighbourhood the oracle branch map fixes one \(C^1\)
ordering of the two roots, and
\Cref{lem:separated-root-rate-delta-main} gives the root and rate Jacobians.
The complete-cycle reporting gate has probability tending to one at the stable
population point, and the separated discriminant/end-point thresholds are
eventually retained.  The multivariate delta method therefore gives the
displayed Gaussian limit after the external branch map has supplied the
ordering.  If an estimated map is used, the event
\(\mathcal C_N\) makes the estimated-map estimator identical to the oracle-map
estimator with probability tending to one, so the same limit follows by the
standard high-probability replacement argument.  The covariance consistency is
the same continuous-mapping argument applied to the plug-in Jacobian on the
retained separated chart.  Finally, Weyl's eigenvalue inequality retains
exactly the positive limiting covariance eigenvalues above the threshold
\(t_N\), and Slutsky's theorem gives the retained-rank \(\chi_k^2\) limit.
Every step after \(\Phi(\Rinch_N)\) is conditional on side-data ordering;
the quotient estimator and its covariance remain the visible-record output.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Quotient-only estimands from one constrained D-MAP fixed-setting long record]\label{thm:one-record-estimates-main}
Under \Cref{ass:fixed-sampled-boundary-record-main}, a single fixed-setting
record estimates only quantities of the stationary visible law: the inclusion
coordinates, the projected
symmetric coordinates \((p+s,ps)\), the unordered survival-factor pair and
unordered numerical rate set when \(\Delta\tau\) is a known design interval,
the corresponding dimensionless product set otherwise, the fixed-setting
lag-two covariance, and their regenerative covariances under the assumptions
above.  It does not label two separated rates and does not identify any object
outside the visible stationary record law.  Response loci, KMS quantities,
spectral weights, shell locations, ordered hidden labels, and ordered
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\) are therefore not outputs of the one-record
CLT or of the quotient inverse.  The branch-map convention is
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}; at \(\sigma=(2z,z^2)\) the common
dimensionless product, and only for known \(\Delta\tau\) the common numerical
rate, is the invariant visible-law exception described in
\Cref{lem:diagonal-common-rate-main}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The positive estimands are exactly the CLT targets in
\Cref{thm:finite-record-inference-audit-main,cor:finite-record-lag-two-studentized-main}
together with the quotient transform in
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality,thm:finite-record-quotient-delta-main}.
The non-identifiability statement is
\Cref{prop:hidden_label_exchange_nonidentifiability}: exchanging the two
hidden labels leaves every finite-dimensional visible distribution unchanged.
Hence a statistic of one visible record can be a function only of the quotient
law, not of a chosen hidden ordering.  Side-data branch maps enter only through
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main,cor:side-data-labelled-rate-wald-main}.
Response-locus statements are appendix/supplementary conditional corollaries
that require an independently calibrated response closure beyond the
visible-record law.  The record analysis starts from \(T_0,T_1\) and therefore
supplies no estimator for
any externally defined scalar response map, shell location, KMS parameter, or
inverse image of such a map.
\end{proof}
