\section{Short calibrated-response theorem}\label{sec:kms-shells}

The record theorems above are stated for the classical sampled counter before
any detector-response calibration is imposed. This section contains only the
paper-level bridge needed to read those record thresholds as response targets.
Homogeneous-shell, horizon, fixed-radius, Schwarzschild, external-ratio, and
residual-graph algebra is kept outside the present theorem chain. The
response-side assumptions are therefore
attached directly to the fixed-response theorem instead of being left as a
separate prose contract.

\begin{theorem}[Model A fixed-response loci with named hypothesis package]\label{ass:udw-rate-calibration-main}\label{cor:conditional-calibration-bridge-main}\label{thm:response-locus-main}
Fix a static region \(U\), a detector gap \(\Omega>0\), a sampling interval
\(\Delta\tau>0\), and, for the sampled boundary-reset target, a labelled
recovery rate \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}>0\). Assume the following named
response-side hypotheses.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em,style=nextline,itemsep=0.25em]
\item[\(\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{rec}}\) (verifiable record laws).]
The record-side hypotheses of \Cref{prop:ideal-main} and
\Cref{thm:recovery-main,lem:one-dep-criterion-main} hold. Hence the classical
record-selected target rates are
\[
\gamma_\varphi=\frac{\log\varphi}{\Delta\tau},
\qquad
\gamma_{\mathrm{mem}}=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau).
\]
\item[\(\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{cl}}\) (open calibration closure).]
One fixed stationary excitation-rate profile \(R_\Omega:U\to(0,\infty)\) has
been supplied, and the sampled counter is coupled to it only through the
Model~A closure
\[
\Gamma=R_\Omega(x)=\Gamma_+(x,\Omega).
\]
For the memory target this is a labelled-rate statement: \(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\)
is externally fixed or externally labelled. Without that label, the visible
sampled record supplies only the unordered attempt/recovery rate pair of
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}.
\item[\(\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{KMS}}\) (standard input plus open spectral weight).]
When the fixed profile is represented by the static KMS response convention
used in this paper, it has the form
\[
R_\Omega(x)
=\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2
\frac{\mathcal S_x(\Omega)}
{e^{\beta_\infty\Omega N(x)}-1}.
\]
Here \(x\mapsto\mathcal S_x(\Omega)\) is a positive one-gap spectral-weight
profile supplied by the calibrated response model. KMS detailed balance fixes
the ratio \(\Gamma_-/\Gamma_+\), not this absolute positive-frequency profile.
\item[\(\mathbf{H}_{C^1}\) (generic regularity of the chosen profile).]
The fixed function \(R_\Omega\) is \(C^1\) on the region under discussion. In
the displayed KMS representation this means, in particular, that the supplied
spectral-weight profile and lapse are \(C^1\) where the denominator is
nonzero.
\item[\(\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{rng}}(\gamma)\) (verifiable range check).]
For the target rate \(\gamma>0\) under consideration, membership
\(\gamma\in R_\Omega(U)\) is checked in the chosen calibrated profile.
\item[\(\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x_0)\) (generic level-set check).]
At a point \(x_0\in U\) with \(R_\Omega(x_0)=\gamma\), the nondegeneracy
condition
\[
dR_\Omega(x_0)\ne0
\]
is checked for the chosen calibrated profile.
\end{description}
Then the response loci selected by the two record laws are exactly
\[
\Sigma_\varphi=R_\Omega^{-1}(\gamma_\varphi),
\qquad
\Sigma_{\mathrm{mem}}=R_\Omega^{-1}(\gamma_{\mathrm{mem}}).
\]
For any \(\gamma>0\), \(R_\Omega^{-1}(\gamma)\) is empty if and only if
\(\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{rng}}(\gamma)\) fails, equivalently
\(\gamma\notin R_\Omega(U)\). If \(x_0\in R_\Omega^{-1}(\gamma)\) and
\(\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x_0)\) holds, then \(R_\Omega^{-1}(\gamma)\) is,
near \(x_0\), a \(C^1\) hypersurface with normal covector \(dR_\Omega(x_0)\).
In the KMS representation above,
\[
dR_\Omega
=\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\left[
\frac{d\mathcal S_x(\Omega)}{e^{\beta_\infty\Omega N}-1}
-\frac{\mathcal S_x(\Omega)\beta_\infty\Omega e^{\beta_\infty\Omega N}}
{(e^{\beta_\infty\Omega N}-1)^2}\,dN
\right].
\]
Thus shell language is justified only after the range and regularity checks
have been made for the fixed calibrated response profile. The theorem is
target-rate level-set bookkeeping after one response profile has been supplied;
it is not a new UDW response theorem and it does not derive the sampled counter
law from detector dynamics. No result in this theorem depends on an
unpublished companion theorem; a companion statement cited elsewhere only as
reference [1] is unavailable to the present proof unless its proof is included
in these paper sources.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{prop:ideal-main} gives the ideal-counter condition
\(\Gamma\Delta\tau=\log\varphi\), equivalently
\(\Gamma=\gamma_\varphi\). \Cref{thm:recovery-main,lem:one-dep-criterion-main}
give the sampled boundary-reset memory-transition condition
\(\Gamma=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)=\gamma_{\mathrm{mem}}\).
Substituting the Model~A closure \(\Gamma=R_\Omega(x)\) gives the two displayed
inverse images.

The nonemptiness statement is exactly the definition of the range of the fixed
function \(R_\Omega\). For the regularity statement, write
\(F(x)=R_\Omega(x)-\gamma\). Since \(\gamma\) is constant, \(dF=dR_\Omega\).
The displayed differential is the ordinary product and chain rule applied to
the fixed calibrated KMS response formula. If
\(dF(x_0)=dR_\Omega(x_0)\) is nonzero, the regular level-set theorem gives a
local \(C^1\) hypersurface at \(x_0\in F^{-1}(0)\), with normal covector
\(dR_\Omega(x_0)\). This is the only regular-level-set implication used here.
No converse is asserted: a level set may be a smooth hypersurface at a point
where this particular defining function has vanishing differential. Only after
the displayed nondegeneracy check is verified do we call the response locus a
regular shell in this theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Stationary one-gap UDW input used for calibration]\label{ass:kms-response-main}\label{ass:long-time-switch-main}
For each static worldline $x\in U$ under discussion, assume the stationary
UDW detector response theorem applies with a fixed smooth switching convention,
detector profile, coupling scale $\lambda_c$, and gap $\Omega>0$. The pulled-back
two-point function has a positive spectral measure whose restriction to a
neighborhood of $\pm\Omega$ is absolutely continuous with finite continuous
density $\widehat W_x(\nu)$ and has no atom or singular component at either gap
frequency. If the state is KMS with respect to static Killing time at inverse
temperature $\beta_\infty$, the proper-time KMS parameter on the static orbit is
\(\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)=\beta_\infty N(x)\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Stationary UDW response input at one gap]\label{prop:stationary-udw-response-main}
Under \Cref{ass:kms-response-main}, the long-time weak-coupling excitation and
de-excitation rates at the fixed detector gap are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationary-udw-rate-input-main}
\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)=\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\widehat W_x(\Omega),
\qquad
\Gamma_-(x,\Omega)=\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\widehat W_x(-\Omega),
\end{equation}
where \(\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2=\lambda_c^2\|\chi\|_2^2\) for the fixed
switching normalization. If the same stationary input is KMS, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kms-rate-main}
\frac{\Gamma_-(x,\Omega)}{\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)}
=e^{\beta_\infty N(x)\Omega},
\qquad
\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)=
\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2
\frac{\mathcal S_x(\Omega)}{e^{\beta_\infty N(x)\Omega}-1}
\end{equation}
for the positive one-gap spectral weight
\(\mathcal S_x(\Omega)=(e^{\beta_\infty N(x)\Omega}-1)\widehat W_x(\Omega)\).
This is the only UDW/KMS input used by the response bridge.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The standard stationary Unruh--DeWitt response theorem with smooth switching
and finite-size regularization identifies the transition rate with the
absolutely continuous spectral density of the pulled-back two-point function at
the detector gap; in the present normalization this gives
\eqref{eq:stationary-udw-rate-input-main} \cite{Letaw1981,Schlicht2004,Satz2007,LoukoSatz2006,LoukoSatz2008,FewsterJuarezLouko2016}. The gap-regularity
assumption excludes atomic or singular contributions at \(\pm\Omega\), so no
additional distributional evaluation is being used. The KMS condition for
static Killing time, together with Tolman scaling of proper time on the static
orbit, gives the local detailed-balance relation
\(\widehat W_x(-\Omega)=e^{\beta_\infty N(x)\Omega}\widehat W_x(\Omega)\).
Multiplication by the common positive factor \(\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\) gives
the rate ratio in \eqref{eq:kms-rate-main}. The displayed form of
\(\Gamma_+\) is then just the definition of \(\mathcal S_x(\Omega)\), and no
claim is made that KMS detailed balance determines that positive function.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One-gap KMS underdetermination]\label{lem:one-gap-kms-underdetermination-main}
Fix the static lapse \(N\), inverse temperature \(\beta_\infty\), switching
scale \(\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}>0\), and gap \(\Omega>0\). KMS detailed balance at
this one gap does not determine the positive excitation profile
\(x\mapsto\Gamma_+(x,\Omega)\). More precisely, for every positive \(C^1\)
function \(G:U\to(0,\infty)\), the one-gap assignments
\[
\Gamma_+^G(x,\Omega)=G(x),
\qquad
\Gamma_-^G(x,\Omega)=e^{\beta_\infty N(x)\Omega}G(x),
\qquad
\mathcal S_x^G(\Omega)=
\frac{e^{\beta_\infty N(x)\Omega}-1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2}G(x)
\]
satisfy the same KMS detailed-balance ratio and the same calibrated response
form \eqref{eq:kms-rate-main}. Consequently detailed balance plus Tolman
scaling alone cannot determine the response loci
\(R_\Omega^{-1}(\gamma)\); the positive profile \(R_\Omega=\Gamma_+\) is an
external calibration input.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The displayed assignments give
\(\Gamma_-^G/\Gamma_+^G=e^{\beta_\infty N(x)\Omega}\), so they obey the same
one-gap KMS detailed-balance relation for every choice of \(G\). Substituting
\(\mathcal S_x^G(\Omega)\) into \eqref{eq:kms-rate-main} gives
\(\Gamma_+^G(x,\Omega)=G(x)\). Because \(G\) was arbitrary among positive
\(C^1\) functions, the level set \(G^{-1}(\gamma)\) may have whatever range and
regularity behavior that chosen profile supplies. Hence KMS detailed balance
and Tolman scaling fix only the ratio of de-excitation to excitation rates, not
the positive excitation profile or its response loci.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Default fixed-response spectral transport identity]\label{cor:spectral-transport-main}
Work inside one fixed calibrated response profile of \Cref{ass:udw-rate-calibration-main}. Suppose \(x_\varphi,x_{\mathrm{mem}}\in U\) satisfy the two
record-selected response equations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-transport-source-main}
\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\mathcal S_{x_\varphi}(\Omega)\Delta\tau}
     {D_\varphi}
=\log\varphi,
\qquad
\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\mathcal S_{x_{\mathrm{mem}}}(\Omega)}
     {D_{\mathrm{mem}}}
=\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau),
\end{equation}
where
\[
D_\varphi=e^{\beta_\infty\Omega N(x_\varphi)}-1,
\qquad
D_{\mathrm{mem}}=e^{\beta_\infty\Omega N(x_{\mathrm{mem}})}-1.
\]
Then the default common-core fixed-response transport identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-transport-main}
\frac{D_\varphi}{D_{\mathrm{mem}}}
=
\frac{\Delta\tau\,\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)}{\log\varphi}
\frac{\mathcal S_{x_\varphi}(\Omega)}
     {\mathcal S_{x_{\mathrm{mem}}}(\Omega)}.
\end{equation}
If the two points also pass the range and regularity checks of
\Cref{thm:response-locus-main}, this identity is the corresponding local
transport identity for the two regular response loci. It is conditional algebra
inside one chosen calibrated response profile, not a new shell-geometry or UDW
measurement theorem.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
All factors in \eqref{eq:general-transport-source-main} are positive under the
standing fixed-response hypotheses. Solving the two source equations for the
Planck denominators gives
\[
D_\varphi=
\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\mathcal S_{x_\varphi}(\Omega)\Delta\tau}
     {\log\varphi},
\qquad
D_{\mathrm{mem}}=
\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\mathcal S_{x_{\mathrm{mem}}}(\Omega)}
     {\Gamma_c(\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)}.
\]
Dividing the first expression by the second cancels the common switched
detector factor \(\lambda_{\mathrm{sw}}^2\) and gives
\eqref{eq:spectral-transport-main}. The final sentence only applies the
regular-level-set conclusion of \Cref{thm:response-locus-main} to the two
already selected levels.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the response bridge]\label{rem:response-bridge-scope-main}
KMS detailed balance fixes the ratio $\Gamma_-/\Gamma_+$ at a fixed gap once
the stationary response calculation is supplied; it does not by itself specify
the positive excitation profile $R_\Omega$, the sampled readout instrument, or
the record thresholds. The main theorem-level contribution therefore remains
the sampled-record law. The response loci above are conditional consequences
of applying one calibrated profile to those record-selected target rates, with
\(\kappa_{\mathrm r}\) supplied as an external label whenever a labelled
\(\Gamma=\Gamma_+\) locus rather than an unordered rate pair is used.
\end{remark}
