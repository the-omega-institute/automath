\subsubsection{Sharp boundary for higher-state identifiability}

The appropriate boundary is not the coincidence pattern of the sampled
survival poles. It is the intersection of a minimal scalar-realization
similarity orbit with the Markovian cone and with the declared hidden subclass.
We first record the minimal-realization theorem in the precise form used
below, and then give the orbit-fibre characterization. The substantive
conclusions are the all-collision serial corollary and the interior continuum
nonidentifiability theorem. Nonunique MAP and Coxian representatives are
documented in
\cite{RamirezCoboLilloWiper2010MAP2Nonidentifiability,
RamirezCoboLillo2012WeakEquivalentMAP,RizkBurkeWalsh2019CoxianNonuniqueness}.
Graph-specific time-to-event inverses are treated algebraically by
Radulescu et al.\ \cite{RadulescuEtAl2025TimeToEventIdentifiability}; the
criterion below instead isolates the exact orbit fibre for any declared
minimal killed-reset subclass.

\paragraph{Minimal-realization theorem invoked.}
Let \((a,A,b)\) and \((\widetilde a,\widetilde A,\widetilde b)\) be two real
scalar realizations of the same dimension \(n\) such that
\[
aA^k b=\widetilde a\widetilde A^k\widetilde b
\qquad(k\ge0).
\]
Assume that both reachability matrices
\[
[\,b,Ab,\ldots,A^{n-1}b\,],\qquad
[\,\widetilde b,\widetilde A\widetilde b,\ldots,
\widetilde A^{n-1}\widetilde b\,]
\]
and both observability matrices
\[
\begin{bmatrix}a\\aA\\\vdots\\aA^{n-1}\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde a\\\widetilde a\widetilde A\\\vdots\\
\widetilde a\widetilde A^{n-1}\end{bmatrix}
\]
have rank \(n\). Then there is a unique \(M\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\RR)\)
such that
\[
\widetilde a=aM,
\qquad \widetilde A=M^{-1}AM,
\qquad \widetilde b=M^{-1}b.
\]
These rank hypotheses are precisely properties \textup{(R)} and \textup{(L)}
of Bladt and Nielsen, whose Theorem~4.2.16 characterizes minimality by their
simultaneous validity; the corresponding intertwining relations are given in
their Lemmas~4.2.5--4.2.6
\cite[Theorem~4.2.16, p.~216, and Lemmas~4.2.5--4.2.6,
p.~212]{BladtNielsen2017MatrixExponential}. This standard result is invoked
here and is not claimed as a new theorem; see also
\cite{Ryden1996Aggregated,OCinneide1989PhaseTypeNonuniqueness}.

\begin{proposition}[Characterization by the Markovian orbit fibre]
\label{thm:nstate-killed-reset-identifiability-dichotomy-main}
Fix \(n\ge2\), put \(\beta=e_n^\top\), and let
\[
\mathcal K_n=\{K\in\RR^{n\times n}:K\ge0,\ K\mathbf1\le\mathbf1,\
\operatorname{spr}(K)<1\}.
\]
For \(K\in\mathcal K_n\), define the killed-reset D-MAP by
\[
T_0=K,\qquad T_1=(I-K)\mathbf1\beta
\]
and its Palm survival coordinates by \(S_k(K)=\beta K^k\mathbf1\).
Call \(K\) minimal when both
\[
\mathcal C(K)=[\,\mathbf1,K\mathbf1,\ldots,K^{n-1}\mathbf1\,],
\qquad
\mathcal O(K)=
\begin{bmatrix}\beta\\ \beta K\\ \vdots\\ \beta K^{n-1}\end{bmatrix}
\]
have rank \(n\). Let
\[
\mathcal G_n=\{M\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\RR):
M\mathbf1=\mathbf1,\ \beta M=\beta\},
\]
and let \(\mathfrak P_n\subset\mathcal G_n\) be the reset-preserving
permutation matrices.

For any declared subclass \(\mathcal C\subset\mathcal K_n\) of minimal
kernels, fix the structural equivalence \(\sim_{\mathcal C}\) under which
identification is requested, and assume that \(K\sim_{\mathcal C}L\) implies
equality of visible laws. Examples are reset-preserving hidden-state
relabeling and, in a serial rate chart, permutation of the rate coordinates.
Define the Markovian orbit fibre
\[
\mathfrak F_{\mathcal C}(K)=
\left\{M^{-1}KM\in\mathcal C:M\in\mathcal G_n\right\}
\big/\!\sim_{\mathcal C}.
\]
Then the following characterization is exact.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Two minimal kernels \(K,L\in\mathcal K_n\) generate the same stationary
visible click law if and only if
\[
\begin{aligned}
L&=M^{-1}KM &&\text{for the unique }M\in\mathcal G_n\\
&&&\text{implementing their normalized minimal realizations}.
\end{aligned}
\]
\item Equivalently, the declared hidden object is identifiable from the visible law within
\(\mathcal C\), modulo \(\sim_{\mathcal C}\), if and only if
\[
\#\mathfrak F_{\mathcal C}(K)=1\qquad\text{for every }K\in\mathcal C.
\]
Pointwise identifiability at \(K\) is equivalent to the same condition at
that \(K\); if the cardinality exceeds one, its distinct classes are exactly
the hidden nonidentifiability fibre. Thus this assertion is the orbit-fibre
reformulation of part \textup{(i)}.
\end{enumerate}
Thus \(\mathfrak F_{\mathcal C}\), rather than eigenvalue multiplicity alone,
is the sharp structural invariant separating identifiable from
nonidentifiable subclasses.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Deterministic reset makes the visible process renewal. Its Palm gap tail is
exactly \(S_k(K)\), so equality of visible stationary laws is equivalent to
equality of all the scalar coordinates \(\beta K^k\mathbf1\), \(k\ge0\).
Apply the minimal-realization theorem stated above to the triples
\((\beta,K,\mathbf1)\) and \((\beta,L,\mathbf1)\). Its hypotheses are exactly
the two displayed rank conditions in the proposition, and it gives a unique
similarity \(L=M^{-1}KM\). Preservation of the fixed input and output vectors
gives \(M\mathbf1=\mathbf1\) and \(\beta M=\beta\), respectively.
Conversely, these two identities give
\[
\beta(M^{-1}KM)^k\mathbf1=\beta K^k\mathbf1
\]
for every \(k\), proving (i). Taking the intersection with
\(\mathcal C\) and then quotienting precisely the declared structural
equivalence proves
the necessary-and-sufficient assertion (ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[All-collision serial identifiability]
\label{cor:nstate-serial-all-collision-identifiability-main}
Fix \(n\ge2\), a known \(\Delta\tau>0\), and arbitrary positive rates
\(\theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n)\), with repetitions allowed. Let
\[
Q(\theta)_{ii}=-\theta_i,\qquad
Q(\theta)_{i,i-1}=\theta_i\quad(2\le i\le n),
\]
with all other entries zero, and put
\[
T_0(\theta)=e^{\Delta\tau Q(\theta)},\qquad
T_1(\theta)=(I-T_0(\theta))\mathbf1e_n^\top.
\]
Then the visible record identifies exactly the unordered rate multiset
\(\{\!\{\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n\}\!\}\). Equivalently, the fibre of the
labelled serial parameter is its \(\mathfrak S_n\)-permutation orbit.

More explicitly, if the distinct rates are
\(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\) with multiplicities
\(m_1,\ldots,m_r\), and \(z_j=e^{-\lambda_j\Delta\tau}\), then the Palm tail
has the confluent Prony form
\[
S_k=\sum_{j=1}^r\sum_{\ell=0}^{m_j-1}
c_{j\ell}k^\ell z_j^k,\qquad c_{j,m_j-1}\ne0,
\]
and its minimal recurrence polynomial is
\[
\chi_\theta(x)=\prod_{j=1}^r(x-z_j)^{m_j}.
\]
The leading \(n\times n\) Hankel matrix
\((S_{i+j})_{0\le i,j<n}\) is invertible; hence
\(S_0,\ldots,S_{2n-1}\) determine \(\chi_\theta\), including all collision
multiplicities. Pole collision therefore changes conditioning and the local
root chart, but does not change population identifiability.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Starting in phase \(n\), the absorption time is
\(W=X_n+\cdots+X_1\), where the \(X_i\) are independent exponentials with
rates \(\theta_i\). Its Laplace transform is
\[
\EE e^{-sW}=\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{\theta_i}{s+\theta_i}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-st}\PP(W>t)\,dt
=\frac{1-\prod_{i=1}^n\theta_i/(s+\theta_i)}{s}
\]
has at
\(-\lambda_j\) a pole of exact order \(m_j\). Partial-fraction inversion and
sampling at \(t=k\Delta\tau\) give the displayed exponential-polynomial form
with nonzero top confluent coefficient. Standard confluent Prony algebra
then gives the exact minimal annihilator \(\chi_\theta\)
\cite{KunisRoemerVonDerOhe2020Prony}.

For completeness of the finite-coordinate assertion, the matrices
\(\mathcal O(Q)\) and \(\mathcal C(Q)\) are nonsingular: successive rows
\(e_n^\top Q^j\) and successive columns \(Q^j\mathbf1\) expose the nonzero
bidiagonal products triangularly. Because the exponential is injective on
the real spectrum of \(Q\), Hermite interpolation supplies a polynomial
\(p\) with \(Q=p(e^{\Delta\tau Q})\); hence the corresponding observation and
reachability spaces for \(T_0=e^{\Delta\tau Q}\) are also full. Therefore
\[
(S_{i+j})_{0\le i,j<n}=\mathcal O(T_0)\mathcal C(T_0)
\]
is invertible, and the first \(2n\) tails solve uniquely for the order-\(n\)
recurrence. The roots and their algebraic multiplicities recover
\(\lambda_j=-\Delta\tau^{-1}\log z_j\) and \(m_j\). Conversely, permuting
the independent summands does not change \(W\), the Palm gap law, or the
stationary renewal law. This proves the exact fibre claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Interior continuum nonidentifiability]
\label{thm:nstate-interior-continuum-nonidentifiability-main}
For the unrestricted class with \(\sim_{\mathcal K_n}\) equal to
reset-preserving state relabelling, the hidden kernel is locally
nonidentifiable at every minimal interior point \(K\), where interior means
\(K>0\) and \((I-K)\mathbf1>0\) entrywise. More precisely, the
reset-preserving similarity group has Lie algebra
\[
\mathfrak g_n=\{B:B\mathbf1=0,\ \beta B=0\},
\qquad \dim\mathfrak g_n=(n-1)^2,
\]
and the visible fibre through \(K\) contains a continuum of pairwise
non-relabelled Markovian kernels.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Decompose
\(\RR^n=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}\oplus\ker\beta\). On this
decomposition the elements of \(\mathfrak g_n\) are
\(\operatorname{diag}(0,A)\) with arbitrary
\(A\in\operatorname{End}(\ker\beta)\), which gives the displayed dimension.
There is \(B\in\mathfrak g_n\) with \([K,B]\ne0\). Indeed, a matrix commuting
with all such elements has the form \(\operatorname{diag}(a,dI_{n-1})\); it
maps \(\mathbf1\) to \(a\mathbf1\), so \(\mathcal C(K)\) would have rank one,
contrary to minimality. Put \(M_\varepsilon=\exp(\varepsilon B)\). Then
\(M_\varepsilon\in\mathcal G_n\), the kernels
\(K_\varepsilon=M_\varepsilon^{-1}KM_\varepsilon\) are nonconstant in
\(\varepsilon\), and strict positivity of the entries and deficits is
preserved for all sufficiently small \(|\varepsilon|\). A finite permutation
orbit cannot exhaust this continuum. Part~\textup{(i)} of
\Cref{thm:nstate-killed-reset-identifiability-dichotomy-main} gives the same
visible law throughout, completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Nonminimal and graph-specific open interfaces]
\label{rem:nstate-identifiability-open-interfaces-main}
The characterization and the two headline results deliberately assume
Hankel/minimal rank \(n\). If a nominal
\(n\)-state representation has lower visible rank, equal laws need not be
related by an invertible \(n\times n\) similarity; a sharp classification then
requires a stratified treatment of nonminimal augmentations. For a fixed
nonserial zero pattern on the boundary of the Markovian cone, evaluating the
orbit intersection \(\mathfrak F_{\mathcal C}(K)\) is an exact semialgebraic
problem, but no graph-uniform closed form is claimed here. Finally, collision
multiplicity is population-identifiable in the serial model but statistically
singular under noisy finite records. These are explicit interfaces, not
consequences of the present results.
\end{remark}

For orientation, the unrestricted fibre is already nontrivial in two states:
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0\\1/2&1/4\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M=\begin{pmatrix}9/10&1/10\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M^{-1}KM=\begin{pmatrix}9/20&1/45\\9/20&3/10\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both kernels are nonnegative and substochastic,
\(M\mathbf1=\mathbf1\), \(e_2^\top M=e_2^\top\), and their entire visible
renewal laws coincide, although the hidden kernels do not.
