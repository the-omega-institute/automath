for known \(\Delta\tau\) the common numerical rate \(-\log z/\Delta\tau\),
remains identifiable even though no separated ordered branch is.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
First identify the only cancellation in the inverse.  From
\[
r_0=\rho,\qquad r_1=\PP(A_0=A_1=1)=\rho a,\qquad
r_2=\PP(A_0=A_2=1)=\rho u_2
\]
and \(r_0>0\), one obtains \(a=r_1/r_0\) and \(u_2=r_2/r_0\).  The Palm
covariance identity \Cref{lem:covariance-transfer-main} gives
\[
u_2=\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}},
\]
so, since \(r_0=\rho\),
\[
r_2-r_0^2
=\rho\{\rho+(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}\}-\rho^2
=\rho(a-\rho)\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}},
\]
while
\[
r_1-r_0^2=\rho a-\rho^2=\rho(a-\rho).
\]
For the constrained D-MAP, \(\rho>0\) and \(a-\rho<0\) by
\Cref{lem:stationary-main}.  Hence
\[
r_1-r_0^2=\rho(a-\rho)\neq0,
\]
and the quotient division is by the nonzero scalar
\(\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}\), not by a quantity that may vanish.  Therefore
\[
\lambda
=\frac{u_2-r_0}{a-r_0}
=\frac{r_2/r_0-r_0}{r_1/r_0-r_0}
=\frac{r_2-r_0^2}{r_1-r_0^2}.
\]
The final equality uses only scalar division by \(r_0>0\) and
\(\Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}\ne0\), precisely the stated inverse domain.

The two coordinates of the boxed map are now computed directly.  Since
\(a=1-s-b\) and \(\lambda=\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}=p-b\),
\[
\Phi_1=1-(1-s-b)+(p-b)=p+s.
\]
For the second coordinate, write \(q=1-p\).  Also
\[
1-\lambda=1-p+b=q+b,
\]
and the stationary click probability from \Cref{lem:stationary-main} is
\[
\rho=\frac{q(1-s)}{q+b}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\rho(1-\lambda)
=\frac{q(1-s)}{q+b}(q+b)
=q(1-s).
\]
Since \(r_0=\rho\), substituting this value, \(a=1-s-b\), and
\(\lambda=p-b\) into the boxed formula gives the full scalar identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_2
&=\rho(1-\lambda)-a+\lambda\\
&=q(1-s)-(1-s-b)+(p-b)\\
&=q(1-s)+p+s-1\\
&=q-qs+p+s-1\\
&=s-qs\\
&=ps .
\end{aligned}
\]
These are exactly the two identities summarized in
\Cref{lem:quotient-inverse-symmetric-algebra-main}.  Hence
\[
\{p,s\}=\text{roots of }z^2-\Phi_1z+\Phi_2=0.
\]
The unordered roots are therefore identified, but their labels are not.  If
\(\Delta\tau\) is fixed by design, applying
\(z\mapsto-\log z/\Delta\tau\) to these roots gives the unordered numerical
rate set; without that known interval the root set gives only the
dimensionless products \(\{-\log p,-\log s\}\).  Finally,
\Cref{lem:gap-law-exchange-symmetry-main} proves that swapping
the two roots also leaves the stationary renewal gap law, and hence all
finite-dimensional visible distributions, unchanged.  The inverse therefore
lands on the quotient by the exchange \((p,\Gamma)\leftrightarrow
(s,\kappa_{\mathrm r})\), not on separated labelled rates.  At a double root
the quotient itself contains the common survival factor and dimensionless
product, with the common numerical rate obtained only for known
\(\Delta\tau\), as made explicit in
\Cref{lem:diagonal-common-rate-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact sampled-counter image and regular residual]
\label{thm:exact-sampled-counter-image-main}
Let \(r=(r_0,r_1,r_2)\) lie in the real quotient-inverse domain
\(r_0>0\), \(r_1-r_0^2\ne0\), and let the two roots \(p,s\) of
\(z^2-\Phi_1(r)z+\Phi_2(r)\) lie in \((0,1)\).  Define
\[
\mathfrak C(p,s)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{s\log p-p\log s}{\log p-\log s},&p\ne s,\\[6pt]
p(1-\log p),&p=s,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\mathcal E(r)=\frac{r_1}{r_0}-1+\mathfrak C(p,s).
\]
Then \(r\) is generated by the sampled-counter model in
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main} for some positive
\((\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r},\Delta\tau)\) if and only if
\(\mathcal E(r)=0\).  The function \(\mathfrak C\) is symmetric and real
analytic in the elementary symmetric coordinates across \(p=s\).  More
precisely, for \(p=m+d\), \(s=m-d\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-divided-difference-diagonal-main}
\mathfrak C(m+d,m-d)
=m(1-\log m)+\frac{d^2}{m}
 \left(\frac12+\frac13\log m\right)+O(d^4).
\end{equation}
Consequently the image is a regular analytic hypersurface on this inverse
chart, with the boundary inherited from the nonnegative-discriminant
constraint; it is not asserted to be an open surface across that boundary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(x=-\log p\) and \(y=-\log s\).  The sampled-counter integral gives,
with its continuous value at \(x=y\),
\[
b=\frac{y(p-s)}{y-x},
\qquad
s+b=\frac{yp-xs}{y-x}=\mathfrak C(p,s).
\]
Since \(a=1-s-b\), every sampled-counter point satisfies
\(a-1+\mathfrak C(p,s)=0\), and \(a=r_1/r_0\) proves necessity.

Conversely, assume \(\mathcal E(r)=0\), choose any
\(\Delta\tau>0\), and set
\[
\Gamma=x/\Delta\tau,
\qquad \kappa_{\mathrm r}=y/\Delta\tau,
\qquad b=\mathfrak C(p,s)-s,
\qquad a=1-\mathfrak C(p,s).
\]
These are exactly the positive-rate sampled-counter integral values.  From
\(\Phi_1=p+s=1-a+\lambda\),
\[
\lambda=p+s+a-1=p-b.
\]
The identity \(\Phi_2=ps\) then gives
\[
r_0=\frac{ps+a-\lambda}{1-\lambda}
=\frac{(1-p)(1-s)}{1-p+b}=\rho.
\]
Thus \(r_1=r_0a\), while the defining equation for \(\lambda\) gives
\(r_2=r_0^2+\lambda(r_1-r_0^2)\).  All three coordinates therefore equal
those of the constructed positive sampled-counter model.

Taylor expansion of the logarithms in \(d/m\) gives
\eqref{eq:log-divided-difference-diagonal-main}; only even powers occur, so
the symmetric function is analytic in
\(m=(p+s)/2\) and \(d^2=(p+s)^2/4-ps\).  Regularity can be checked without
differentiating the individual roots.  In the analytic coordinates
\((r_0,\sigma_1,\sigma_2)=(r_0,p+s,ps)\), the quotient identities give
\[
a=2-\sigma_1+\frac{\sigma_1-1-\sigma_2}{r_0},
\]
and hence
\[
\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal E}{\partial r_0}
\right|_{\sigma_1,\sigma_2}
=-\frac{\sigma_1-1-\sigma_2}{r_0^2}
=\frac{(1-p)(1-s)}{r_0^2}>0.
\]
Conversely these coordinates recover
\[
\lambda=\sigma_1-1+a,
\qquad r_1=r_0a,
\qquad r_2=r_0^2+\lambda(r_1-r_0^2),
\]
so the displayed change of variables is an analytic chart on the stated
inverse domain.  The analytic implicit-function theorem proves the
hypersurface assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sharp hidden-mode range and oscillatory damping]
\label{prop:sharp-hidden-mode-range-main}
For all \(x=\Gamma\Delta\tau>0\) and
\(y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta\tau>0\),
\[
-e^{-2}\le\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}<1.
\]
The lower endpoint occurs only at \(x=y=2\), while the upper endpoint is a
nonattained supremum approached as \(x,y\downarrow0\).  In the alternating
correlation phase \(\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}<0\),
\[
\left|\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_{n+1})}
{\operatorname{Cov}(A_0,A_n)}\right|
=|\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}|\le e^{-2},\qquad n\ge1.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The secant identity already established in
\eqref{eq:symmetric-lambda-hid-main} can be written
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{hid}}
=\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x},\qquad f(t)=te^{-t},
\]
with value \(f'(x)\) on the diagonal.  Since
\(f'(t)=e^{-t}(1-t)\) has the unique global minimum \(-e^{-2}\) at
\(t=2\), is strictly less than \(1\) for \(t>0\), and tends to \(1\) as
\(t\downarrow0\), averaging \(f'\) over the interval between \(x\) and \(y\)
gives the stated sharp range and equality cases.  The covariance ratio follows
from \eqref{eq:cov-transfer-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fast-sampling bias for the sampled-counter cycle]
\label{prop:fast-sampling-cycle-bias-main}
Fix \(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r}>0\) and let
\(\Delta=\Delta\tau\downarrow0\).  The Palm cycle has exact mean, with
continuous interpretation at equal rates,
\[
\mu_\Delta=\EE(G+1)
=\frac{y/(1-e^{-x})-x/(1-e^{-y})}{y-x},
\qquad x=\Gamma\Delta,\quad y=\kappa_{\mathrm r}\Delta.
\]
Its fixed-rate expansion is
\[
\Delta\mu_\Delta
=\frac1\Gamma+\frac1{\kappa_{\mathrm r}}+\frac\Delta2
+\frac{\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}(\Gamma+\kappa_{\mathrm r})}{720}
 \Delta^4+O(\Delta^6),
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac\rho\Delta
=\frac{\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}}{\Gamma+\kappa_{\mathrm r}}
-\frac\Delta2
 \left(\frac{\Gamma\kappa_{\mathrm r}}
 {\Gamma+\kappa_{\mathrm r}}\right)^2+O(\Delta^2).
\]
Thus the leading lattice correction to the mean physical cycle duration is
the rate-independent half interval \(\Delta/2\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Summing the survival probabilities \(S_k\) in Theorem~A gives the displayed
exact mean.  Finally substitute
\[
\frac1{1-e^{-u}}=\frac1u+\frac12+\frac{u}{12}
-\frac{u^3}{720}+\frac{u^5}{30240}+O(u^7)
\]
into that mean.  The \(\Delta^2\) terms cancel, giving the stated expansion;
expanding \(1/(\Delta\mu_\Delta)=\rho/\Delta\) gives the intensity formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Projected plug-in word-law reconstruction]\label{def:projected-plugin-word-law-reconstruction-main}
A finite-record projected plug-in word-law reconstruction is a consistency
construction formed from a noisy inclusion triple only on the retained event
\(\mathcal E_N^{\rm law}\) fixed in advance.  That event is a deterministic
domain-of-definition and withholding rule inside
\Cref{ass:model-inference-contract-main}, not a model-validation test and not
an uncertainty statement.  On that retained branch, the raw quotient coordinate
\(\Phi(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}})\) is first projected to
\[
F=\{(x+y,xy):0\le x,y\le1\}
\]
by the tie-broken nearest-point projection
\(\widetilde\sigma_N=\Pi_F^{\rm tb}\{\Phi(\widehat r_N^{\mathrm{cyc}})\}\).
The reconstruction is the lifted renewal-consistent coordinate
\[
(\rho_*(\widetilde\sigma_N),\widetilde\sigma_N)\in
\mathcal M_{\rm ren}
:=\{(\rho_*(\sigma),\sigma):\sigma\in F\},
\]
where \(\rho_*(\widetilde\sigma_N)\) is computed from the projected root gap
law, not from the raw empirical intensity
\(\widehat r_{0,N}^{\mathrm{cyc}}\).  The associated finite-window word
probabilities are then the renewal inclusion products and finite
inclusion--exclusion probabilities of this lifted projected law.
If any denominator, projection-root, interior-root, or declared numerical
certificate required by the operational protocol fails, no projected plug-in
word probabilities are formed.  The output is a projected plug-in word-law
reconstruction only: it gives projected word probabilities as a
renewal-consistent plug-in law, not confidence intervals, bands, \(p\)-values,
or coverage statements for those word probabilities.
Algorithm~4 in \Cref{app:finite-record-implementation-protocols-main} gives the finite-window
coefficient recursion, truncation convention, scalar-mean tail certificate,
and withholding rule used for numerical reconstruction.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Population complete-law reconstruction from three inclusions]\label{lem:algorithmic-complete-law-reconstruction-main}
Work in the stable quotient domain of
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}.  The following deterministic
map reconstructs the complete stationary visible renewal law from the
population three-inclusion coordinates
\[
r=(r_0,r_1,r_2)
=\bigl(\PP(A_0=1),\PP(A_0=A_1=1),\PP(A_0=A_2=1)\bigr).
\]
It is a population reconstruction theorem only; the finite-record projection
theorem is \Cref{prop:finite-record-complete-law-renewal-consistency-main},
and numerical truncation guidance is isolated in
\Cref{rem:complete-law-numerical-implementation-main}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Start from the population three inclusion coordinates
\[
r_0=\PP(A_0=1),\qquad
r_1=\PP(A_0=A_1=1),\qquad
r_2=\PP(A_0=A_2=1),
\]
and retain the triple only when
\[
r_0>0,\qquad \Delta_{\mathrm{inv}}:=r_1-r_0^2\ne0 .
\]

\item Compute the adjacent Palm mass and hidden-tail ratio
\[
a=\frac{r_1}{r_0},\qquad
\lambda=\frac{r_2-r_0^2}{r_1-r_0^2}.
\]
Then form the symmetric root coordinates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-sigma-main}
\sigma_1=1-a+\lambda,\qquad
\sigma_2=r_0(1-\lambda)-a+\lambda .
\end{equation}
For the constrained D-MAP population,
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality} gives
\[
(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)=(p+s,ps).
\]

\item Recover the unordered survival-factor pair
\[
\{z_1,z_2\}
=\{ \text{roots of } z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2=0\}.
\]
Only this unordered pair is a visible-law object.  At the population
constrained D-MAP point, \(\sigma\) lies in the feasible set \(F\) equivalently
characterized in \eqref{eq:feasible-symmetric-coordinate-set-main}.

\item Recover the Palm zero-run gap law.  Put
\(\theta_i=-\log z_i\).  If \(z_1\ne z_2\), set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-gap-main}
g_k=
\frac{\theta_2(1-z_1)z_1^k-\theta_1(1-z_2)z_2^k}
     {\theta_2-\theta_1},
\qquad k\ge0 .
\end{equation}
If \(z_1=z_2=z\), put \(\theta=-\log z\) and use the continuous-limit law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-gap-diagonal-main}
g_k=z^k\bigl((1-z)(1+\theta k)-\theta z\bigr),
\qquad k\ge0 .
\end{equation}
For population roots this is the D-MAP Palm gap law.  For any later
finite-record projected root pair retained in \((0,1)^2\), the same formulas
are certified as a nonnegative normalized gap law by
\Cref{lem:projected-root-gap-law-valid-main}, independently of the noisy
empirical intensity used to construct the projected coordinate.

\item Compute the Palm renewal masses
\[
u_0=1,\qquad
u_n=\PP(n\in\varXi\mid0\in\varXi)
    =\sum_{j=1}^{n}\!
      \sum_{\substack{k_1,\ldots,k_j\ge0\\
      (k_1+1)+\cdots+(k_j+1)=n}}
      g_{k_1}\cdots g_{k_j},
\qquad n\ge1 .
\]
Equivalently, if \(G\) has law \(g\), then
\[
u_n=\sum_{j\ge1}\PP\bigl((G_1+1)+\cdots+(G_j+1)=n\bigr),
\]
where the sum is finite because each cycle length \(G_i+1\) is at least
\(1\).
Equivalently, with the cycle generating function
\[
H(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}g_k t^{k+1},
\]
the Palm renewal generating function is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renewal-generating-function-complete-law-main}
U(t):=\sum_{n\ge0}u_n t^n
=\frac{1}{1-H(t)}
=\frac{1}{1-\sum_{k\ge0}g_k t^{k+1}} .
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the coefficients satisfy the finite recursion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-window-renewal-recursion-main}
u_0=1,\qquad
u_n=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}g_{\ell-1}u_{n-\ell},
\qquad n\ge1 .
\end{equation}

\item Compute the stationary renewal intensity from the same gap law,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algorithmic-rho-star-main}
\rho_*(\sigma)
=\frac{1}{\EE(G+1)}
=\left(1+\sum_{k\ge0}k\,g_k\right)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
At the population input produced by the constrained D-MAP, this value equals
the original inclusion coordinate \(r_0\).  This equality is a conclusion of
the population model, not a license to combine a noisy finite-record
\(\widehat r_0\) with a separately projected root pair.

\item Apply the renewal inclusion product and then finite
inclusion--exclusion.  For every nonempty
\(B=\{i_1<\cdots<i_m\}\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-inclusion-main}
\PP(B\subset\varXi)
=\rho_*(\sigma)\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}u_{i_{\ell+1}-i_\ell},
\end{equation}
with the empty-set inclusion probability equal to \(1\).  For every finite
window \(I\) and word \(S\subset I\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-word-main}
\PP(\varXi\cap I=S)
=\sum_{C\subset I\setminus S}(-1)^{|C|}
\PP(S\cup C\subset\varXi).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

At the population constrained D-MAP point the procedure is exact and
determines the complete finite-dimensional visible law on the exchange
quotient.  No step assigns the two roots to labelled attempt/recovery rates;
that additional ordering is precisely the branch-map convention of
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The domain test permits the scalar divisions in Step~2.  The formulas for
\(a\) and \(\lambda\) are those in
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}, and
\Cref{lem:quotient-inverse-symmetric-algebra-main} identifies
\eqref{eq:algorithmic-reconstruction-sigma-main} with \((p+s,ps)\) at the
constrained D-MAP population point.  Thus the roots in Step~3 are the
unordered pair \(\{p,s\}\), with no labelled branch information.

Step~4 is exactly \Cref{lem:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-main}: the
unequal-root formula is the symmetric dimensionless gap law
\eqref{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main}, and the equal-root formula is its continuous
limit.  Given this gap law, the visible process is the stationary renewal
process supplied by \Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}.  Step~5 is the renewal
equation for Palm return probabilities, written first as a finite convolution
sum at fixed time \(n\) and equivalently as the generating-function identity
\eqref{eq:renewal-generating-function-complete-law-main}.  Comparing
coefficients in \(U(t)(1-H(t))=1\) gives
\eqref{eq:finite-window-renewal-recursion-main}.
The stationary renewal intensity in Step~6 is \(1/\EE(G+1)\); for the
constrained D-MAP population this equals
\(\PP(A_0=1)=r_0\) by stationarity of the renewal process already identified
in \Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main}.  Finally, Step~7 is
\Cref{cor:renewal-principal-minor-word-law-main}: first multiply Palm renewal
masses to obtain marked-set inclusions, then use finite inclusion--exclusion
over the unprescribed sites.  These operations determine every finite word
probability and therefore the complete stationary visible law.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Numerical note for complete-law finite-window evaluation]\label{rem:complete-law-numerical-implementation-main}
This note concerns only the numerical evaluation of the population law in
\Cref{lem:algorithmic-complete-law-reconstruction-main} or of a retained
projected finite-record law in
\Cref{prop:finite-record-complete-law-renewal-consistency-main}; it is not an
additional statistical assertion.  For a finite window \(I\), put \(d_I=0\) if
\(|I|\le1\), and otherwise put \(d_I=\max I-\min I\).  The inclusion products
need only \(u_0,\ldots,u_{d_I}\).  If an implementation forms the truncated
cycle polynomial
\[
H_K(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{K}g_k t^{k+1},
\]
then every coefficient \(u_n\) with \(0\le n\le d_I\) is exactly unchanged
whenever \(K\ge d_I-1\).  Thus renewal-mass truncation contributes no error to
word probabilities on \(I\) once the gap law is kept through \(g_{d_I-1}\).

Only the scalar mean in \(\rho_*(\sigma)\) may require an infinite-tail
evaluation.  It may be computed from the closed root sums
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}k g_k
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{\theta_2 z_1/(1-z_1)-\theta_1 z_2/(1-z_2)}
     {\theta_2-\theta_1},
& z_1\ne z_2,\\[1.2em]
\displaystyle
\frac{z}{1-z}+\frac{\theta z}{(1-z)^2},
& z_1=z_2=z,\ \theta=-\log z,
\end{cases}
\]
or from a truncated sum with a reported tail certificate.  For any displayed
\(\bar r\) with \(\max(z_1,z_2)\le \bar r<1\), set
\[
C_{\bar r}=
\frac{\theta_2(1-z_1)+\theta_1(1-z_2)}
     {|\theta_2-\theta_1|}
\]
in the unequal-root case, and set
\[
C_{\bar r}=(1-z)+\theta,\qquad \theta=-\log z
\]
in the diagonal case.  Then
\[
0\le g_k\le C_{\bar r}(1+k)\bar r^k,\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
For
\[
m_K=1+\sum_{k=0}^{K}k g_k,\qquad
\varepsilon_K=C_{\bar r}\sum_{k>K}k(1+k)\bar r^k ,
\]
one has \(0\le\EE(G+1)-m_K\le\varepsilon_K\).  If
\(\varepsilon_K<m_K\), the intensity \(\rho_K=1/m_K\) computed from the
truncated mean satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho-truncation-error-bound-main}
|\rho_K-\rho_*(\sigma)|
\le \frac{\varepsilon_K}{m_K(m_K-\varepsilon_K)} .
\end{equation}
Since every renewal mass used in the inclusion product is a probability, this
contributes at most
\[
2^{|I|}\frac{\varepsilon_K}{m_K(m_K-\varepsilon_K)}
\]
to any word probability on \(I\) after finite inclusion--exclusion.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Separated-root labelled chart]\label{prop:population-separated-root-labelled-chart-main}
In the setting of \Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}, suppose that the
quotient inverse gives
\(\sigma=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)=(p+s,ps)\) with
\[
\Delta_\sigma=\sigma_1^2-4\sigma_2>0 .
\]
Then the two roots of \(z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2=0\) are distinct elements of
\((0,1)\).  For a known design interval \(\Delta\tau\), and under the
branch-map convention of
\Cref{conv:branch-map-labelled-rates-main}, a supplied branch map
\(\mathcal B\) orders those roots as \((p_{\mathcal B},s_{\mathcal B})\), and
on that chart
\[
(\Gamma,\kappa_{\mathrm r})
=\left(-\frac{\log p_{\mathcal B}}{\Delta\tau},
       -\frac{\log s_{\mathcal B}}{\Delta\tau}\right).
\]
If \(\Delta_\sigma=0\), the separated labelled chart is not defined; the
quotient still identifies the common root and hence the common dimensionless
product, with the common numerical rate available only when \(\Delta\tau\) is
known, as in \Cref{lem:diagonal-common-rate-main}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The roots of \(z^2-\sigma_1z+\sigma_2=0\) are the unordered pair
\(\{p,s\}\) by \Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}.  The strict
discriminant condition is exactly \(p\ne s\), so the two roots can be
separated as numerical values.  The exchange symmetry proved in
\Cref{lem:gap-law-exchange-symmetry-main} shows that the visible stationary
law is unchanged by swapping the two roots.  Therefore the assignment of one
root to the attempt branch and the other to the recovery branch cannot be
inferred from the visible law and must be supplied externally.  Applying
\(z\mapsto-\log z/\Delta\tau\) to the two externally ordered roots gives the
displayed labelled rates for the known design interval.  At
\(\Delta_\sigma=0\) the two roots coalesce, and the denominator of the
separated-root differential vanishes; only the quotient common-root statement,
or the common-rate statement when \(\Delta\tau\) is known, remains.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Gap law from the quotient root pair]\label{lem:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-main}
Let \((\sigma_1,\sigma_2)\) be the symmetric coordinates recovered in
\Cref{thm:toeplitz_image_inverse_duality}, and let
\(\{z_1,z_2\}\subset(0,1)\) be the unordered roots of
\[
z^2-\sigma_1 z+\sigma_2=0 .
\]
Put \(\theta_i=-\log z_i\).  If \(z_1\ne z_2\), then the Palm zero-run law is
the symmetric expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-main}
g_k=
\frac{\theta_2(1-z_1)z_1^k-\theta_1(1-z_2)z_2^k}
     {\theta_2-\theta_1},
\qquad k\ge0 .
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is unchanged by exchanging \(z_1\) and \(z_2\).  On the
diagonal \(\sigma=(2z,z^2)\), \(0<z<1\), where
\(\theta=-\log z\), the same quotient coordinate gives the continuous-limit
law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-diagonal-main}
g_k=z^k\bigl((1-z)(1+\theta k)-\theta z\bigr),
\qquad k\ge0 .
\end{equation}
Consequently the recovered unordered root pair determines the complete renewal
gap law, including at a double root.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For distinct roots choose an arbitrary ordering and write
\[
p=z_1,\qquad s=z_2,\qquad
x=-\log p=\theta_1,\qquad y=-\log s=\theta_2 .
\]
Substitution in the dimensionless form
\eqref{eq:symmetric-gap-law-main} gives
\eqref{eq:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-main}.  If the two roots are exchanged,
both the numerator and the denominator in
\eqref{eq:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-main} change sign, so the value of
\(g_k\) is an unordered-root function.  At a double root \(z_1=z_2=z\),
\Cref{lem:renewal-gap-main} reads the unequal-root expression by the continuous
equal-rate dictionary \eqref{eq:equal-rate-dictionary-main}; with
\(\theta=-\log z\), this is exactly
\eqref{eq:gap-law-from-quotient-roots-diagonal-main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Projected quotient roots define a stochastic gap law]\label{lem:projected-root-gap-law-valid-main}
Let \(z_1,z_2\in(0,1)\) and put \(\theta_i=-\log z_i\).  If
\(z_1\ne z_2\), define
\[
g_k=
\frac{\theta_2(1-z_1)z_1^k-\theta_1(1-z_2)z_2^k}
     {\theta_2-\theta_1},
\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
If \(z_1=z_2=z\), put \(\theta=-\log z\) and define
\[
g_k=z^k\{(1-z)(1+\theta k)-\theta z\},
\qquad k\ge0 .
\]
Then \(g_k\ge0\) for every \(k\ge0\) and
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}g_k=1 .
\]
More explicitly, the displayed \(g_k\) is the \(D\)-row path probability
\[
g_k=e_D^\top T_0(z_1,z_2)^kT_1(z_1,z_2)\mathbf1
\]
of a two-state stochastic triangular labelled kernel.  Thus every finite
projected root pair retained in the interior of \(F\) produces an honest
nonnegative normalized Palm zero-run law before any finite-window
probabilities are formed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First suppose \(z_1\ne z_2\).  Define
\[
b=\frac{\theta_2(z_1-z_2)}{\theta_2-\theta_1},
\qquad
a=\frac{\theta_2(1-z_1)-\theta_1(1-z_2)}
        {\theta_2-\theta_1}.
\]
These coefficients are nonnegative.  Indeed, after choosing the order
\(z_1=e^{-\theta_1}\), \(z_2=e^{-\theta_2}\), they have the integral
representations
\[
b=\int_0^1\theta_2 e^{-\theta_2 r}e^{-\theta_1(1-r)}\,dr,
\qquad
a=\int_0^1\theta_2 e^{-\theta_2 r}
       \{1-e^{-\theta_1(1-r)}\}\,dr ,
\]
with the displayed quotient values obtained by evaluating the elementary
integrals.  Moreover \(a+b=1-z_2\).  Hence
\[
T_0(z_1,z_2)=
\begin{pmatrix}
z_1&0\\ b&z_2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
T_1(z_1,z_2)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1-z_1\\0&a
\end{pmatrix}
\]
are nonnegative labelled kernels and \(T_0+T_1\) is row-stochastic.

For \(k\ge0\),
\[
T_0(z_1,z_2)^k=
\begin{pmatrix}
z_1^k&0\\
b\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}z_2^jz_1^{k-1-j}&z_2^k
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with the sum empty when \(k=0\).  Therefore the \(D\)-row probability of
seeing \(k\) zeros and then a one is
\[
e_D^\top T_0^kT_1\mathbf1
=b(1-z_1)\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}z_2^jz_1^{k-1-j}+az_2^k .
\]
Using
\(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}z_2^jz_1^{k-1-j}=(z_1^k-z_2^k)/(z_1-z_2)\), this becomes
\[
\frac{\theta_2(1-z_1)z_1^k-\theta_1(1-z_2)z_2^k}
     {\theta_2-\theta_1},
\]
which is the stated off-diagonal formula.  Thus \(g_k\ge0\) because it is a
path probability.

Since the spectral radius of \(T_0\) is \(\max(z_1,z_2)<1\) and
\(T_1\mathbf1=(I-T_0)\mathbf1\),
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}g_k
=e_D^\top\left(\sum_{k\ge0}T_0^k\right)T_1\mathbf1
=e_D^\top(I-T_0)^{-1}(I-T_0)\mathbf1
=1 .
\]
