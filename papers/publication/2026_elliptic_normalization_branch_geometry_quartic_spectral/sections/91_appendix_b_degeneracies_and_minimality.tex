\section{Degenerate parameters and resultant check}\label{app:degeneracies}

This appendix records the explicit cancellations at the exceptional parameter values \(y\in\{-1,0,1\}\) and the resultant identity used in Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-order}.

\subsection{Resultant}

Using the numerator and denominator from Section~\ref{subsec:ogf},
\[
N(t,y)=1+yt+(y^2-y-1)t^2+(y^3-2y)t^3,
\]
\[
D(t,y)=1-t-(2y+1)t^2+t^3+y(y+1)t^4,
\]
the Sylvester resultant in the variable $t$ is the determinant of the $7\times7$ matrix with the four shifted coefficient rows of $N$ followed by the three shifted coefficient rows of $D$:
\[
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$
\operatorname{Res}_t(N,D)=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
 y^3-2y&y^2-y-1&y&1&0&0&0\\
0&y^3-2y&y^2-y-1&y&1&0&0\\
0&0&y^3-2y&y^2-y-1&y&1&0\\
0&0&0&y^3-2y&y^2-y-1&y&1\\
y(y+1)&1&-(2y+1)&-1&1&0&0\\
0&y(y+1)&1&-(2y+1)&-1&1&0\\
0&0&y(y+1)&1&-(2y+1)&-1&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$}
\]
Fraction-free elimination over $\QQ[y]$ gives the following Bareiss pivots for the displayed matrix:
\begin{gather*}
y(y^2-2),\quad y^2(y^2-2)^2,\quad
y^3(y^2-2)^3,\quad y^4(y^2-2)^4,\\
y(y-1)(y+1)^2
 (y^8-3y^6-2y^5+6y^4-4y^3+4y+1),\\
y^2(y-1)^2(y+1)^4(y^4+y^3-2y^2+2y+1),
y^3(y-1)^3(y+1)^6.
\end{gather*}
The last pivot is the determinant, hence
\[
\operatorname{Res}_t(N,D)=y^3(y-1)^3(y+1)^6.
\]
This determinant identity is an algebraic criterion: a common factor of $N(t,y)$ and $D(t,y)$ over $\CC[t]$ forces this resultant to vanish, and hence can occur only when $y=-1,0,1$.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(y=1\)}{y=1}}

At \(y=1\),
\[
N(t,1)=-(t-1)(t+1)^2,
\qquad
D(t,1)=(t-1)(t+1)^2(2t-1),
\]
so
\[
Z(t,1)=\frac{N(t,1)}{D(t,1)}=\frac{1}{1-2t},
\qquad
Z_m(1)=2^m.
\]

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(y=0\)}{y=0}}

At \(y=0\),
\[
N(t,0)=-(t-1)(t+1),
\qquad
D(t,0)=(t-1)^2(t+1),
\]
so
\[
Z(t,0)=\frac{N(t,0)}{D(t,0)}=\frac{1}{1-t}.
\]
Thus the reduced denominator degree drops from \(4\) to \(1\), and the effective recurrence collapses to order \(1\) with \(Z_m(0)=1\) for all \(m\ge 0\).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(y=-1\)}{y=-1}}

At \(y=-1\), one has \(N(t,-1)=D(t,-1)\), hence \(Z(t,-1)\equiv 1\).

\subsection{Minimality for generic \texorpdfstring{\(y\)}{y}}

For \(y\notin\{-1,0,1\}\), the resultant is nonzero and none of the above common factors occur; equivalently, \(N(t,y)\) and \(D(t,y)\) are coprime.
Therefore the reduced denominator has degree \(4\) and the order-\(4\) recurrence \eqref{eq:recurrence} is minimal, as used in Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-order}.

\subsection{Positive-axis Sturm criterion}\label{app:positive-axis-sturm}

This appendix supplies an explicit Sturm and pseudo-remainder reproducibility check for the positive-axis cells of Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-axis-dominance-criterion}.
It is separated from the main positive-axis argument so that the body of the paper retains the conceptual dominance comparison while the exact finite criterion remains reproducible.

\begin{proposition}[Primitive Sturm chain for the positive-axis criterion]\label{prop:positive-axis-sturm-chain}
Let
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1)
\]
and let \(c(y)=256y^3+411y^2+165y+32\).
On the positive-axis cells of Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-axis-dominance-criterion}, a Sturm chain for the finite real-root counts is obtained, up to multiplication by the positive denominator factors displayed below on each cell, from
\begin{align*}
S_0&=\Pi(\lambda,y),\\
S_1&=\partial_\lambda\Pi(\lambda,y),\\
16S_2&=(16y+11)\lambda^2+(4y-10)\lambda-16y^2-16y-1,\\
-\frac{(16y+11)^2}{16}S_3
&=4\lambda y^2-68\lambda y-8\lambda-64y^3-41y^2+25y+8,\\
S_4&=-\frac{y(y-1)(16y+11)^2c(y)}{256(y^2-17y-2)^2}.
\end{align*}
Here the signs in the displayed definitions of \(S_2,S_3,S_4\) are part of the Sturm normalization; the only suppressed factors are powers of \(16\), \((16y+11)^2\), and \((y^2-17y-2)^2\), which are positive away from the explicitly discussed exceptional parameter.
Thus multiplying by these factors does not change any Sturm sign variation on a subcell where the denominators are nonzero; the proof below checks that their possible positive zero is not a new root-count boundary.
The ordinary Euclidean divisions satisfy
\[
\operatorname{rem}(S_0,S_1)=-S_2,
\qquad
\operatorname{rem}(S_1,S_2)=-S_3,
\qquad
\operatorname{rem}(S_2,S_3)=-S_4,
\]
where the last two identities are read in \(\QQ(y)[\lambda]\).
After clearing denominators, the primitive pseudo-remainder criteria are
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{prem}(S_0,S_1)
&= -16S_2,\\
\operatorname{prem}(S_1,16S_2)
&=16(4\lambda y^2-68\lambda y-8\lambda-64y^3-41y^2+25y+8),\\
\operatorname{prem}(16S_2,
&\ 4\lambda y^2-68\lambda y-8\lambda-64y^3-41y^2+25y+8)\\
&=y(y-1)(16y+11)^2c(y).
\end{align*}
Thus no zero of a Euclidean denominator, and no hidden sign normalization, introduces an additional positive-axis root-count cell.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed remainder identities are direct polynomial divisions in \(\QQ(y)[\lambda]\).
On any subinterval of \((0,1)\) or \((1,\infty)\) where the rational denominators are nonzero, the sequence \((S_0,S_1,S_2,S_3,S_4)\) is therefore an ordinary Sturm chain for \(\Pi\), with the harmless normalization factors shown explicitly.

It remains to check that clearing denominators has not hidden an additional cell boundary.
The only displayed denominators that can vanish for positive \(y\) come from powers of \(16y+11\) and \(y^2-17y-2\); the first is never zero on \((0,\infty)\), and the second has a single positive zero, lying in \((17,18)\).
At a zero of \(y^2-17y-2\), the discriminant identity
\[
\Disc_\lambda\Pi=-y(y-1)c(y)
\]
from Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-axis-dominance-criterion} is nonzero: the zero is not \(0\) or \(1\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic-branch-real-root} puts the unique real zero of \(c\) in \((-2,-1)\).
Hence the fibre has no multiple root there, and the number of real roots is locally constant across that parameter.
Because the possible denominator zero lies in the interior of the already-open cell \((1,\infty)\), this check shows that it is not an endpoint of a new Sturm cell.
The primitive pseudo-remainder identities show that the cleared chain remains a gcd criterion at the same parameter.
Thus the finite positive-axis decomposition has no extra cells beyond \((0,1)\), \(\{1\}\), and \((1,\infty)\).
\end{proof}
