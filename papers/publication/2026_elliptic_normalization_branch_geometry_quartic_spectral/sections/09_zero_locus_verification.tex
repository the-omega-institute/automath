\section{Numerical checks and reproducibility}\label{sec:verification}

The preceding sections establish the branch structure and endpoint asymptotics of the quartic family by algebraic and analytic methods.
The computations in this section serve only as consistency checks for the real-axis modulus ordering and the endpoint zero formula.

\subsection{Validation targets from the exact theory}

Set
\[
\Omega:=\CC\setminus\bigl(\Branch\cup\{-1,0,1\}\bigr).
\]
For each fixed $y \in \Omega$, the spectral polynomial $\Pi(\cdot, y)$ has four simple roots $\lambda_1(y), \dots, \lambda_4(y)$, and Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching} gives the spectral decomposition
\[
Z_m(y)=\sum_{j=1}^4 C_j(y)\lambda_j(y)^m,
\qquad
C_j(y)=\frac{\lambda_j(y)^3\,N(1/\lambda_j(y),y)}{\partial_\lambda\Pi(\lambda_j(y),y)}.
\]
The Beraha--Kahane--Weiss theorem, in the form used here \cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1975,BerahaKahaneWeiss1978,Sokal2004}, gives two alternatives for a limit point of zeros of \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge0}\): either two or more eigenvalues of maximal modulus tie, or a unique dominant eigenvalue exists but its coefficient vanishes.
On \(\Omega\), the second alternative does not occur.
Indeed, for a simple root \(\lambda_j(y)\) one has \(\partial_\lambda\Pi(\lambda_j(y),y)\neq 0\), and Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-order} shows that \(N(t,y)\) and \(D(t,y)\) are coprime for \(y\notin\{-1,0,1\}\).
Since \(D(1/\lambda_j(y),y)=0\), it follows that \(N(1/\lambda_j(y),y)\neq 0\), hence \(C_j(y)\neq 0\) on \(\Omega\).
Therefore, on the generic set \(\Omega\), the limiting accumulation set is governed solely by dominant equimodularity:
\[
\bigl\{y \in \Omega : \exists\, i \neq j \text{ with }
\abs{\lambda_i(y)} = \abs{\lambda_j(y)} = \max_k \abs{\lambda_k(y)}\bigr\}.
\]
Branch points in $\Branch$ arise as natural endpoints or singularities of such equimodular curves.

\subsection{Numerical consistency checks}

The numerical material in this section is supplementary: it checks the exact formulas against independently evaluated roots of the recurrence polynomials, but no theorem above depends on it.
The checks used for the submission source are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Along the real axis, the ordered moduli of the four roots of \(\Pi(\lambda,y)=0\) show that the dominant-modulus collision occurs at \(y=0\), while the collisions at \(y=1\) and \(y=\ySub\) are subdominant, in agreement with Theorem~\ref{thm:ep-classification} and Proposition~\ref{prop:global-dominance}.
\item Direct recurrence evaluation of \(Z_m\) for \(m=80,120,200\) gives negative zeros whose scaled values \(-m^2y_{m,k}\), for fixed \(k\), agree with the leading constants \(\pi^2(2k-1)^2/2\) and the two-term correction in Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}.
\item The algebraically forced factor \((y+1)^2\) from Proposition~\ref{prop:double-zero} is present for every tested \(m\ge2\), and no analogous forced zero appears at the subdominant branch value \(y=1\).
\end{enumerate}
These computations are included as reproducibility evidence rather than as graphical data in the article source.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Reproducibility.}
Appendix~\ref{app:pseudocode} records optional commands for reproducing the numerical checks outside the proof.
The script constructs \(Z_m(y)\) from the recurrence \eqref{eq:recurrence}, evaluates the roots of \eqref{eq:spectral-poly}, factors out \((y+1)^2\) symbolically when Proposition~\ref{prop:double-zero} applies, and writes diagnostic tables and optional plots.
A separate high-precision check verifies the modulus ordering near \(y\in\{0,1,y_{\mathrm{sub}}\}\).
