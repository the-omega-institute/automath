\begin{abstract}
We study the polynomial family $\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}$ defined by the rational generating function $Z(t,y)=N(t,y)/D(t,y)$ and the spectral polynomial
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1).
\]
This paper gives a complete exact theorem chain for this quartic spectral curve and its recurrence polynomials.
First, an auxiliary balanced quartic family
\[
P_{a,c,d,g}(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4+a\lambda^3+(c-2y)\lambda^2+d\lambda+y(y-c)+g
\]
is used only as a normalization device: the substitution $\xi=y-\lambda^2$ converts the affine curve into a Weierstrass model, and the imposed double roots at $(\lambda,y)=(1,0)$ and $(\lambda,y)=(-1,1)$ force the specialization $(a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)$.
For this specialization, the projective quartic curve is normalized by the elliptic curve
\[
Y^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1,
\]
Hence the four algebraic branches form a genus-one degree-$4$ cover of the $y$-line.
Second, the normalization determines all finite branch values and yields the discriminant factorization
\[
\Disc_\lambda \Pi=-y(y-1)(256y^3+411y^2+165y+32).
\]
Among the three real branch values, $y=0$ is the only branch point at which the colliding branches are maximal in modulus on the positive side, whereas $y=1$ and the negative partner $y_{\mathrm{sub}}$ are non-dominant; the real locus projects to $[y_{\mathrm{sub}},1]\cup[0,\infty)$ with an overlap on $[0,1]$, so the total real image is $[y_{\mathrm{sub}},\infty)$, determining the real-root count of $\Pi(\cdot,y)$ for every real $y$.
Third, local branch expansions, amplitude symmetry, and complex-uniform remainder bounds give the endpoint oscillation law at $y=0$.
Consequently the negative zeros nearest $y=0$ satisfy
\[
y_{m,k}
=
-\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}
-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4}),
\]
and the positive endpoint exhibits a quarter-power splitting governed by a double tangency at projective infinity.
The article also proves the degree law $\deg_y Z_m=\lfloor(m+3)/2\rfloor$ with explicit top-degree coefficients and an algebraically enforced double zero at $y=-1$.
Thus the central contribution is a worked-out quartic case study: an explicit elliptic normalization, an exact real-branch phase diagram, a maximal-modulus branch theorem on \(y>0\), and the resulting endpoint zero asymptotics.
\end{abstract}
