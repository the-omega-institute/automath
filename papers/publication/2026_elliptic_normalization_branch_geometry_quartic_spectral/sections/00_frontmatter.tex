\begin{abstract}
We analyze the recurrence polynomials $Z_m(y)$ whose denominator has spectral polynomial
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1).
\]
A balanced quartic normalization sends the affine spectral curve to the elliptic model $Y^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1$ and shows that the prescribed double roots force the specialization under study.
This normalization gives the finite branch values, the real projection of the two ovals, and the exact real-root count of $\Pi(\cdot,y)$ for every real $y$.
On the positive axis, finite algebraic interval certificates identify a unique maximal-modulus branch and distinguish the dominant endpoint collision at $y=0$ from the non-dominant real collisions at $y=1$ and $y=y_{\mathrm{sub}}$.
Combining this branch classification with local Puiseux expansions and uniform two-branch estimates gives a fixed-index endpoint law: for each fixed $k$,
\[
y_{m,k}
=
-\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}
-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4})
\]
for the negative zeros nearest $0$.
Equivalently, the result is uniform on fixed compact endpoint windows containing only finitely many indexed zeros; it is not a macroscopic density theorem.
We also record supporting recurrence consequences, including the degree law for $Z_m$ and a forced double zero at $y=-1$.
\end{abstract}
