\section{Discriminant computation}\label{app:disc}

This appendix is part of the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:disc-factor}: it supplies the expanded Sylvester resultant \eqref{eq:disc-expanded-polynomial} used there to fix the leading coefficient and exclude hidden branch factors.
All eliminations in this appendix are performed in \(\QQ[y]\); hence the displayed Sylvester determinant is an exact polynomial identity, not a floating-point or sampled computation.
Recall
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1).
\]
Its derivative with respect to \(\lambda\) is
\[
\partial_\lambda \Pi(\lambda,y)=4\lambda^3-3\lambda^2-2(2y+1)\lambda+1.
\]

For a monic polynomial \(P(\lambda)\) of degree \(n\), the discriminant satisfies
\[
\Disc_\lambda P = (-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\Res_\lambda(P,P').
\]
Here \(n=4\) so \((-1)^{n(n-1)/2}=(-1)^6=1\), and thus
\[
\Disc_\lambda \Pi(\lambda,y)=\Res_\lambda\bigl(\Pi(\lambda,y),\partial_\lambda \Pi(\lambda,y)\bigr).
\]

The resultant is the determinant of the following \(7\times 7\) Sylvester matrix, formed from the coefficient rows of \(\Pi\) and \(\partial_\lambda\Pi\):
\[
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$
\Res_\lambda\bigl(\Pi,\partial_\lambda\Pi\bigr)
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1&-(2y+1)&1&y(y+1)&0&0\\
0&1&-1&-(2y+1)&1&y(y+1)&0\\
0&0&1&-1&-(2y+1)&1&y(y+1)\\
4&-3&-2(2y+1)&1&0&0&0\\
0&4&-3&-2(2y+1)&1&0&0\\
0&0&4&-3&-2(2y+1)&1&0\\
0&0&0&4&-3&-2(2y+1)&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$}
\]
Expanding this determinant gives the polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disc-expanded-polynomial}
\Res_\lambda\bigl(\Pi,\partial_\lambda\Pi\bigr)
=
-256y^5-155y^4+246y^3+133y^2+32y.
\end{equation}
This expansion is obtained by fraction-free Gaussian elimination over $\QQ[y]$; the Bareiss divisions below are exact divisions in that ring, so their intermediate pivots are not additional hypotheses on the parameter \(y\).
With the displayed row order, the Bareiss pivots are
\begin{gather*}
1,\quad 1,\quad 1,\quad 6y+1,\\
-4(4y^3+6y^2+9y+2),
\end{gather*}
\[
2y(32y^3+35y^2-7y+12),
\quad -y(y-1)(256y^3+411y^2+165y+32).
\]
Since the matrix is square, the last Bareiss pivot is the determinant.
Multiplying out the last factor gives exactly the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:disc-expanded-polynomial}, so this is an exact elimination identity rather than a numerical expansion.
Factoring \eqref{eq:disc-expanded-polynomial} yields the complete factorization
\[
\Disc_{\lambda}\Pi(\lambda,y)=-y(y-1)\bigl(256y^3+411y^2+165y+32\bigr),
\]
which is the claim in Corollary~\ref{cor:disc-factor}.
