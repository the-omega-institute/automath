\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion-new}

The paper gives a closed algebraic analysis of a quartic recurrence spectrum whose projective curve is normalized by the elliptic model
\[
Y^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1.
\]
For the fixed canonical balanced member selected in the paper, the normalization makes the branch values explicit, the real oval images computable by finite comparisons, and the real-root count of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) completely determined.
The same finite algebraic control separates the positive maximal-modulus branch from the other sheets on the canonical side \(y>0\), so the local square-root collision at \(y=0\) becomes a genuine endpoint for the fixed-window zero problem rather than merely a ramification point of the cover.

The endpoint analysis shows how the algebraic geometry of the spectral curve enters the recurrence zeros.
At \((\lambda,y)=(1,0)\), the two colliding branches have Puiseux expansions with explicitly computed amplitudes; after the scaling \(y=-u^2/m^2\), their contribution reduces uniformly on fixed compact windows to a cosine with a computable first correction.
Rouch\'e isolation then gives the two-term law
\[
y_{m,k}
=
-\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}
-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4})
\]
for each fixed endpoint index.
All constants and thresholds in this discussion remain attached to fixed endpoint indices and fixed compact endpoint windows; the paper does not prove a mesoscopic edge process, an expanding-window law, or a global zero-distribution theorem.
The degree law and the forced double zero at \(y=-1\) record only the recurrence-side algebra needed for this fixed family, while the projective-infinity computation is kept as an appendix consistency check rather than a separate asymptotic theorem.

Several directions naturally follow from this exact case study, but none is part of the theorem chain proved here.
Examples include chamber analysis for broader balanced quartic families, mesoscopic or global zero statistics, higher-order endpoint expansions, recurrence-side support rigidity in broader rational families, and a separate large-parameter problem at projective infinity.
Each would require hypotheses and proofs beyond the fixed quartic, fixed normalization, and fixed-window endpoint regime established in this article, so these topics are left outside the present article rather than folded into the main line.
