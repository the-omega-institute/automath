\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion-new}

The paper gives a closed algebraic analysis of a quartic recurrence spectrum whose projective curve is normalized by the elliptic model
\[
Y^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1.
\]
The normalization makes the branch values explicit, the real oval images computable by finite comparisons, and the real-root count of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) completely determined.
The same finite algebraic control separates the positive maximal-modulus branch from the other sheets on \(y>0\), so the local square-root collision at \(y=0\) becomes a genuine endpoint for the zero problem rather than merely a ramification point of the cover.

The endpoint analysis shows how the algebraic geometry of the spectral curve enters the recurrence zeros.
At \((\lambda,y)=(1,0)\), the two colliding branches have Puiseux expansions with explicitly computed amplitudes; after the scaling \(y=-u^2/m^2\), their contribution reduces uniformly on fixed compact windows to a cosine with a computable first correction.
Rouch\'e isolation then gives the two-term law
\[
y_{m,k}
=
-\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}
-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4})
\]
for each fixed endpoint index.
The degree law and the forced double zero at \(y=-1\) record the recurrence-side algebra needed for this fixed family, while the projective-infinity computation is kept as an appendix consistency check rather than a separate asymptotic theorem.

Several directions naturally follow from this exact case study.
The finite real-projection criterion could be developed into a chamber analysis for broader balanced quartic families; the fixed-window endpoint method suggests a separate analysis of growing-index or expanding-window negative zeros; the support and boundary identities invite comparison with structural recurrence-rigidity questions; and the projective-infinity expansion points to a distinct large-parameter problem.
