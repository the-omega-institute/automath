\section{Positive-real maximal-modulus branch and endpoint asymptotics}\label{sec:positive-maximal-modulus-branch}

Having classified all real branch points, we now parametrize the real branch over positive \(y\), prove by direct modulus inequalities that it is the unique maximal-modulus branch, and derive the Puiseux expansion at the endpoint \(y=0\).
The section has two headline outputs.
Theorem~D, the positive-real dominance classification, is proved in the first half: Subsections~\ref{subsec:positive-branch-parametrization}--\ref{subsec:positive-axis-dominance} construct the positive sheet, exclude competing sheets on each real branch cell, and assemble the canonical dominance classification.
Theorem~E, the fixed-index endpoint zero law, is proved in the second half: Subsections~\ref{subsec:endpoint-puiseux}--\ref{subsec:endpoint-quantization} start from the dominant square-root endpoint, compute its two amplitudes, and isolate zeros only after the scaled window and the finite index set have been fixed.
All other labelled statements in this section are local tools, finite algebraic criteria, or fixed-scale corollaries serving one of these two theorem blocks.

The proof order separates dominance from endpoint zero counting.
Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-maximal-modulus-branch} constructs the real sheet over \((0,\infty)\) directly from the explicit branch formula \eqref{eq:y-phys} and its derivative, before any endpoint-zero asymptotic is invoked.
Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-axis-dominance-criterion} then proves, by exact resultants and interval inequalities, that this sheet is the unique maximal-modulus sheet on every positive-axis cell.
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance} combines these positive-axis criteria with the genus-one branch data from Corollary~\ref{cor:canonical-theorem-a-specialization} and the real branch-point classification from Theorem~\ref{thm:ep-classification}; Proposition~\ref{prop:positive-side-real-scaling-stability} records the resulting compact positive-side gap after real normalized scaling.
This result supplies the dominance input for the zero analysis of the canonical quartic.

The endpoint part begins only after that dominance input is available, and its route is deliberately fixed-scale.
First, Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-puiseux} identifies the one-sided positive Puiseux expansion of the dominant branch, while Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-ep2-branching} records the abstract simple-double-point normal form.
The source of truth for the two-branch endpoint coefficients, residues, and compact-uniform remainder is Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching}, especially Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-expansion}; Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-cosine-hypotheses} imports exactly those constants into the main proof.
Second, Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching} computes the local branches, residue amplitudes, amplitude symmetries, and compact-uniform remainders.  Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-cosine-hypotheses} records the exact constants \(a=1/\sqrt2\), \(b=3/8\), \(A_*=1/2\), \(B_*=3\sqrt2/16\), together with the estimate \(R_m(-u^2/m^2)=O_K(m^{-2})\), so the main proof can invoke Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} without any hidden computation.
Third, Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} converts those listed hypotheses into a two-branch estimate on each preassigned compact \(u\)-window and proves Rouch\'e isolation there.
Fourth, Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-neighborhood-isolation} applies the same fixed-window estimate in compact rectangles of the scaled coordinate \(u=m\sqrt{-y}\), and Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization} transfers the isolated \(u\)-zeros through \(y=-u^2/m^2\) for fixed finite index sets.
Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed-scale-endpoint-consequences} records the citation-ready statement: every compact window, endpoint window, and finite index set is chosen independently of \(m\) before constants are asserted.
Consequently the proof of Theorem~E uses Theorem~D through the verified maximal-modulus endpoint, the appendix coefficients imported by Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-cosine-hypotheses}, and the two-branch oscillation lemma, with all zero isolation performed in fixed compact endpoint windows.

\subsection{Positive branch parametrization and monotonicity}\label{subsec:positive-branch-parametrization}

The following lemma records the only local facts used in this section:
simple roots vary analytically in the parameter, and a strict modulus gap persists locally by continuity.

\begin{lemma}[Local simplicity and persistence on $(0,\infty)$]\label{lem:local-simplicity-persistence}
Fix \(y_0>0\) with \(y_0\neq 1\).
Then the four roots of \(\Pi(\cdot,y_0)\) are simple.
Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:local-analyticity} Each root extends to an analytic function on a neighborhood of \(y_0\).
\item\label{item:local-persistence} If one of these local branches, say \(\lambda_1(y)\), satisfies
\[
\abs{\lambda_1(y_0)}>\abs{\lambda_j(y_0)}
\qquad (j\neq 1),
\]
then the same strict inequality holds for all \(y\) in a neighborhood of \(y_0\).
\end{enumerate}
At \(y_0=1\), the root \(\lambda=2\) is simple and therefore also admits a local analytic continuation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The discriminant \(\Disc_\lambda\Pi(\lambda,y)\) was computed in Corollary~\ref{cor:disc-factor}:
\[
\Disc_\lambda\Pi(\lambda,y) = -y(y-1)(256y^3+411y^2+165y+32).
\]
This vanishes on \((0,\infty)\) only at \(y=1\), because the cubic factor has its unique real root at \(y_{\mathrm{sub}}<0\) by Theorem~\ref{thm:ep-classification}(3).
Therefore the four roots are simple at every \(y_0>0\) with \(y_0\neq1\).
The analytic continuation in \ref{item:local-analyticity} is the standard implicit-function theorem for isolated simple roots of a polynomial with analytic coefficients \cite{Kato1976}.
The persistence statement \ref{item:local-persistence} is immediate from continuity of the local branches.
At \(y_0=1\), the factorization
\[
\Pi(\lambda,1)=(\lambda-2)(\lambda-1)(\lambda+1)^2
\]
shows directly that \(\lambda=2\) is simple, so the same isolated-root perturbation statement applies to that branch.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Reducible boundary at $y=1$]\label{rem:y1-reducible}
At $y=1$ the polynomial factors as $\Pi(\lambda,1)=(\lambda-2)(\lambda-1)(\lambda+1)^2$.
The double root $\lambda=-1$ is non-dominant (modulus $1$) while the simple root $\lambda=2$ is the maximal-modulus eigenvalue.
Since $\lambda=2$ is simple at $y=1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:local-simplicity-persistence}\ref{item:local-analyticity}---the first lemma in this section---applies to this root: the branch $\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}$ extends analytically through $y=1$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Positive-real parametrization on $y>0$]\label{thm:positive-maximal-modulus-branch}
The real branch of the spectral curve connecting $(\lambda, y) = (1, 0)$ to $(\lambda, y) = (2, 1)$ is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:y-phys}
y_{\mathrm{dom}}(\lambda)
= \lambda^2 - \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4\lambda^3 - 4\lambda + 1},
\qquad \lambda \ge 1.
\end{equation}
The function $y_{\mathrm{dom}}$ is strictly increasing on $(1, \infty)$ and maps $(1, \infty)$ bijectively onto $(0, \infty)$.
Consequently it has an inverse
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}:(0,\infty)\to(1,\infty).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:Pi-as-quadratic-in-y}, the spectral curve is quadratic in $y$ with solutions
\[
y = \lambda^2 - \frac{1}{2} \pm \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4\lambda^3 - 4\lambda + 1}.
\]
At $(\lambda, y) = (1, 0)$ we have $4 \cdot 1 - 4 + 1 = 1$, and $1 - 1/2 - 1/2 = 0$, confirming that the minus-sign branch passes through $(1, 0)$.
Similarly, at $(\lambda, y) = (2, 1)$, one has $4 \cdot 8 - 8 + 1 = 25$ and $4 - 1/2 - 5/2 = 1$, confirming continuity to $(2, 1)$.

Differentiating \eqref{eq:y-phys} with respect to $\lambda$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dy-dlambda}
\frac{\dd y_{\mathrm{dom}}}{\dd\lambda}
= \frac{2\lambda\sqrt{4\lambda^3 - 4\lambda + 1} - (3\lambda^2 - 1)}
{\sqrt{4\lambda^3 - 4\lambda + 1}}.
\end{equation}
The critical points of $y_{\mathrm{dom}}$ on $(1, \infty)$ satisfy
\[
2\lambda\sqrt{4\lambda^3 - 4\lambda + 1} = 3\lambda^2 - 1.
\]
Squaring both sides and simplifying yields
\[
(\lambda - 1)(\lambda+1)(16\lambda^3 - 9\lambda^2 + 1) = 0.
\]
For $\lambda > 1$, both sides of
\[
2\lambda\sqrt{4\lambda^3 - 4\lambda + 1} = 3\lambda^2 - 1
\]
are strictly positive, so this squaring step introduces no spurious solution on $(1,\infty)$.
The factors $\lambda-1$ and $\lambda+1$ have no zero on $(1,\infty)$.
Moreover the polynomial $16\lambda^3 - 9\lambda^2 + 1$ has no zero on $[0, \infty)$: its derivative is $6\lambda(8\lambda - 3)$, so the unique interior minimum on $[0, \infty)$ is at $\lambda = 3/8$, where
\[
16\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)^3 - 9\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)^2 + 1 = \frac{37}{64} > 0.
\]
Hence the numerator of \eqref{eq:dy-dlambda} has no zero on $(1, \infty)$.
At $\lambda = 2$ it equals $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 5 - 11 = 9 > 0$, so continuity implies that it is strictly positive throughout $(1,\infty)$; the denominator in \eqref{eq:dy-dlambda} is also strictly positive there.
Therefore $y_{\mathrm{dom}}$ is strictly increasing on $(1, \infty)$.

Since $y_{\mathrm{dom}}(1) = 0$ and $y_{\mathrm{dom}}(\lambda) \to +\infty$ as $\lambda \to +\infty$ (the leading term is $\lambda^2$), the map is a bijection $(1, \infty) \to (0, \infty)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Comparison of the two positive branches for \(y>1\)]\label{lem:ygt1-sheet-comparison}
For \(\lambda>1\), define
\[
y_-(\lambda)=\lambda^2-\frac12-\frac12\sqrt{4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1},
\qquad
y_+(\lambda)=\lambda^2-\frac12+\frac12\sqrt{4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1}.
\]
Then \(y_+(\lambda)>y_-(\lambda)\) for every \(\lambda>1\), and \(y_+\) is strictly increasing on \((1,\infty)\).
If \(y>1\) and
\[
y=y_-(q)=y_+(p)
\]
for positive roots \(0<p<q\) of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\), then \(q>p\).
Consequently, for \(y>1\) the larger positive root of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) lies on the minus-sign branch \(y_-=y_{\mathrm{dom}}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The strict inequality \(y_+(\lambda)>y_-(\lambda)\) is immediate from the definition.
Moreover,
\[
y_+'(\lambda)=2\lambda+\frac{3\lambda^2-1}{\sqrt{4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1}}>0
\qquad (\lambda>1),
\]
because both summands are positive.
Hence \(y_+\) is strictly increasing on \((1,\infty)\).

Now suppose \(y=y_-(q)=y_+(p)\) with \(0<p<q\).
Then
\[
y_+(q)>y_-(q)=y=y_+(p).
\]
By the strict monotonicity of \(y_+\), this implies \(q>p\).
Therefore the root on the minus-sign sheet is the larger of the two positive roots for \(y>1\).
\end{proof}


\subsection{Algebraic dominance criterion on the positive axis}\label{subsec:positive-axis-dominance}


\begin{lemma}[Positive-axis dominance criterion]\label{lem:positive-axis-dominance-criterion}
Let
\begin{align*}
\Pi(\lambda,y)
&=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1),\\
c(y)&=256y^3+411y^2+165y+32.
\end{align*}
Then the positive real axis has exactly the three cells
\[
(0,1),\qquad \{1\},\qquad (1,\infty),
\]
and, on every one of them, the root on the branch \(\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)>1\) is strictly larger in modulus than every other root of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\).
Equivalently,
\[
r(y)=\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)
\qquad (y>0),
\]
so the branch parameterized by \eqref{eq:y-phys} is the maximal-modulus branch on the whole positive axis.
The proof certificate is the following displayed algebra; the later appeal to Appendix~\ref{app:disc} only records the fraction-free expansion of the Sylvester determinant appearing here.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The discriminant and endpoint resultants are
\begin{align}
\Disc_\lambda \Pi
&=
\Res_\lambda(\Pi,\partial_\lambda\Pi)
=-y(y-1)c(y),\label{eq:positive-axis-discriminant}\\
\Pi(-1,y)&=y(y-1),\qquad
\Pi(0,y)=y(y+1),\qquad
\Pi(1,y)=y(y-1).\label{eq:positive-axis-resultants}
\end{align}
and \(c(y)>0\) for \(y\ge0\).
Thus the only positive branch parameters are \(0\) and \(1\), with
\[
\Pi(\lambda,1)=(\lambda-2)(\lambda-1)(\lambda+1)^2.
\]
\item If \(0<y<1\), the primitive Sturm chain of Proposition~\ref{prop:positive-axis-sturm-chain} has variation drop \(4\), so the four roots are real and ordered
\[
r_1<r_2<r_3<r_4,
\qquad
r_1<-1<r_2<0<r_3<1<r_4,
\]
with \(r_4=\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)\), and
\[
\abs{r_1},\abs{r_2},\abs{r_3}<r_4.
\]
\item If \(y>1\), the same Sturm chain has variation drop \(2\), so the two real roots are positive, say \(0<p<q\), the remaining two roots are a non-real conjugate pair \(z,\overline z\), one has \(q=\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)\), and
\[
p<q,
\qquad
\abs z<q.
\]
\item Opposite real roots can have equal modulus only at the positive endpoints.  The determinant criterion is
\begin{align}
\Pi(-x,y)-\Pi(x,y)&=2x(x^2-1),\notag\\
\Res_x\bigl(\Pi(x,y),\Pi(-x,y)\bigr)
&=\Res_x\bigl(\Pi(x,y),2x(x^2-1)\bigr)\notag\\
&=2^4\Pi(0,y)\Pi(1,y)\Pi(-1,y)\notag\\
&=16y^3(y-1)^2(y+1).\label{eq:opposite-modulus-resultant}
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The criterion is read cell-by-cell in the open intervals where the displayed denominators do not vanish, and the only possible cell boundaries allowed into the argument are zeros of the discriminant, the endpoint resultants, or the explicitly displayed opposite-modulus resultant.
The discriminant identity \eqref{eq:positive-axis-discriminant} is the Sylvester determinant \(\Res_\lambda(\Pi,\partial_\lambda\Pi)\); Appendix~\ref{app:disc} prints the matrix, the expanded polynomial, and the exact Bareiss pivots certifying the factorization and excluding hidden branch factors.
The endpoint resultants in \eqref{eq:positive-axis-resultants} are direct substitutions in the displayed quartic.
The opposite-modulus identity \eqref{eq:opposite-modulus-resultant} is the Sylvester determinant of the two quartics \(\Pi(x,y)\) and \(\Pi(-x,y)\), with the second input reduced by the determinant-preserving operation \(g\mapsto g-\Pi\) and then evaluated by multiplicativity of the resultant over \(2x(x^2-1)\); explicitly its primitive output is \(16y^3(y-1)^2(y+1)\), so every possible equality \(\lambda=-\mu\) between real roots in a simple positive fibre is recorded by a displayed resultant rather than by numerical sampling.
Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic-branch-real-root} gives the unique real zero of \(c\) in \((-2,-1)\), hence \(c(y)>0\) on \([0,\infty)\); Proposition~\ref{prop:positive-axis-sturm-chain} supplies the primitive Sturm divisions, the denominator sign checks, and the variation counts \(4\) on \((0,1)\) and \(2\) on \((1,\infty)\).
In particular the possible Euclidean denominator zero \((17+\sqrt{297})/2\) is not a root-count boundary.
Therefore \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) has simple roots on \((0,1)\cup(1,\infty)\), the only positive multiple fibres occur at \(y=0,1\), and the real-root counts used below are certified by a printed algebraic Sturm chain.
The factorization at \(y=1\) is immediate by substitution.

Let \(0<y<1\).
The signs
\[
\Pi(-1,y)<0,
\qquad
\Pi(0,y)>0,
\qquad
\Pi(1,y)<0
\]
and \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\to+\infty\) as \(\lambda\to\pm\infty\) put at least one root in each interval
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-axis-root-boxes}
(-\infty,-1),
\qquad
(-1,0),
\qquad
(0,1),
\qquad
(1,\infty).
\end{equation}
Since \(\Pi\) is quartic, these are all the roots, and Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-maximal-modulus-branch} identifies the root \(r_4>1\) as \(\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)\).
It remains in this cell only to rule out dominance by the negative root.
For \(x\ge1\),
\begin{align*}
\partial_\lambda\Pi(-x,y)
&=-4x^3-3x^2+2(2y+1)x+1\\
&\le -4x^3-3x^2+6x+1
=-(x-1)(4x^2+7x+1).
\end{align*}
This upper bound is strictly negative for \(x>1\), while at \(x=1\) the exact derivative is \(4(y-1)<0\); hence \(\partial_\lambda\Pi(-x,y)<0\) for every \(x\ge1\).
Hence \(\lambda\mapsto\Pi(\lambda,y)\) is strictly decreasing on \((-\infty,-1]\).  Since \(r_4>1\) is a root,
\[
\Pi(-r_4,y)-\Pi(r_4,y)=2r_4(r_4^2-1)>0,
\]
so \(\Pi(-r_4,y)>0\).  The decreasing function \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\) vanishes at \(r_1\in(-\infty,-1)\); therefore \(r_1>-r_4\), and \(\abs{r_1}<r_4\).  The inequalities \(\abs{r_2}<1<r_4\) and \(0<r_3<r_4\) are immediate from the ordering.

At \(y=1\), the displayed factorization shows that \(2\) is visibly the unique root of largest modulus.  The equality \(2=\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(1)\) follows from \(y_{\mathrm{dom}}(2)=1\).

Let \(y>1\).
The Sturm criterion in Proposition~\ref{prop:positive-axis-sturm-chain} gives exactly two real roots on the whole cell \((1,\infty)\); equivalently, the discriminant is negative there and so a simple real quartic has exactly one non-real conjugate pair.
Both real roots are positive: for \(x\ge0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ygt1-no-negative-roots}
\Pi(-x,y)=x^4+x^3-(2y+1)x^2-x+y(y+1)>0
\qquad (y>1).
\end{equation}
Indeed, viewed as a quadratic in \(y\), the right-hand side is
\[
y^2+(1-2x^2)y+x^4+x^3-x^2-x.
\]
If \(0\le x^2\le3/2\), it is increasing for \(y\ge1\) and its value at \(y=1\) is \((x-1)^2(x+1)(x+2)\); strict positivity for \(y>1\) follows.
If \(x^2>3/2\), its minimum over all real \(y\) occurs at \(y=x^2-1/2>1\) and equals \((4x^3-4x-1)/4>0\).
Write the two real roots as \(0<p<q\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:ygt1-sheet-comparison} identifies the larger positive root as the one on the minus-sign sheet, hence \(q=\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)\).  Factor
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=(\lambda-p)(\lambda-q)(\lambda^2+a\lambda+b),
\]
where \(b=\abs z^2>0\).  Comparing coefficients of \(\lambda^3\) and \(\lambda\) gives
\[
a=p+q-1,
\qquad
b=\frac{(p+q-1)pq-1}{p+q}
=pq-\frac{pq+1}{p+q}<pq<q^2.
\]
Thus \(\abs z=\sqrt b<q\), and the remaining real root also satisfies \(p<q\).

Finally, the displayed determinant criterion \eqref{eq:opposite-modulus-resultant} gives the opposite-modulus resultant.
Its positive zeros are only \(0\) and \(1\), so no interior opposite-real-root modulus crossing is missed.
The three cases exhaust \((0,\infty)\), and in each case \(\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)\) is strictly larger in modulus than every other root.
\end{proof}

and, if \(\alpha\) is the unique real root of \(16\lambda^3-9\lambda^2+1\), then

Consequently this table is a single citable criterion list: after these five rows and displayed factorizations are verified, the specialized quartic has exactly the real branch values, real-root transitions, and positive-axis dominance behavior stated in Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance}.  Each row points to a theorem, lemma, displayed identity, or appendix computation.

\begin{proof}
The first row is Corollary~\ref{cor:disc-factor} together with Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic-branch-real-root}: the discriminant identifies all finite branch values, and the cubic lemma isolates exactly one real zero of \(c\), denoted \(\ySub\), on the negative axis.
The endpoint factorizations in the second row are direct substitutions in \(\Pi\); their modulus consequences are the endpoint parts of Theorem~\ref{thm:ep-classification}.
The displayed factorization at \(\ySub\) is Lemma~\ref{lem:ysub-exact-subdominance}, and the strict inequality for \(|z|^2\) is exactly the modulus gap that makes the negative real collision subdominant.
The real-image row is Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-b-real-projection-criterion}, whose proof comes from the elliptic normalization and the two real oval images; a regular value in the interior of one oval image has exactly two real preimages on that oval, giving the four displayed counts when the two interval images are superposed.
The fourth row is Lemma~\ref{lem:ysub-exact-subdominance}: the factorization in \(\QQ(\alpha)[\lambda]\) shows that the residual roots at \(\ySub\) form a non-real conjugate pair, and \eqref{eq:ysub-modulus-gap} gives their modulus strictly larger than that of the colliding double root.
For the final row, Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-axis-dominance-criterion} proves that the discriminant and endpoint resultants leave only the cells \((0,1)\) and \((1,\infty)\), that opposite real roots can have equal modulus only at the endpoint values recorded by \eqref{eq:opposite-modulus-resultant}, and that the positive sheet dominates the remaining real or conjugate competitors on each cell.
The possible failure mechanisms for the three conclusions are therefore exhausted: a real-root count can change only at a real branch value, and a positive-axis maximal branch can change only at a positive branch value or an equal-modulus crossing.
All such values and crossings are included in the five displayed rows, proving completeness of the criterion list.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Positive-real dominance classification (Theorem D)]\label{thm:canonical-branch-dominance}
For the specialized quartic
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1),
\]
the following statements give the definitive branch and dominance classification used in the sequel.
This theorem is the canonical citation for the assembled branch-point classification, real-fibre count, and dominance assertions; the branch-value computations and local subdominance criteria in Section~\ref{sec:ep-classification} are inputs to this theorem, not competing theorem results.
The compact dependency criterion is Proposition~\ref{prop:consolidated-real-branch-classification}; the numbered assertions below spell out the resulting branch-value, real-root, and dominance conclusions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The finite branch values of the degree-$4$ map $E\to\mathbf P^1_y$ are exactly
\[
0,
\qquad
1,
\qquad
\ySub,
\qquad
\eta,
\qquad
\overline\eta,
\]
where \(\ySub\) is the unique real root of \(c(y)=256y^3+411y^2+165y+32\), and \(\eta,\overline\eta\) are the non-real roots of \(c\).
The discriminant factorization is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consolidated-disc-factor}
\Disc_\lambda\Pi(\lambda,y)
=
-y(y-1)\bigl(256y^3+411y^2+165y+32\bigr).
\end{equation}
\item The real branch values are exactly \(\ySub<0<1\), and the spectral roles are:
$y=0$ has factorization
\[
\Pi(\lambda,0)=\lambda(\lambda+1)(\lambda-1)^2
\]
and the double root $\lambda=1$ is the square-root endpoint of the maximal-modulus branch over $y>0$;
$y=1$ has factorization
\[
\Pi(\lambda,1)=(\lambda-2)(\lambda-1)(\lambda+1)^2
\]
so the colliding double root $\lambda=-1$ is subdominant to the simple root $2$;
and $y=\ySub$ is strictly subdominant because, with \(\alpha\) as in Proposition~\ref{prop:consolidated-real-branch-classification}, the double root is \(\lambda=\alpha\) while the residual complex-conjugate pair has modulus squared
\[
\frac{9\alpha^2-144\alpha-1}{256\alpha^2}>\alpha^2.
\]
\item For real $y$ away from \(\ySub,0,1\), the number of real roots is
\[
\#\{\lambda\in\RR:\Pi(\lambda,y)=0\}
=
\begin{cases}
0, & y<\ySub,\\
2, & \ySub<y<0,\\
4, & 0<y<1,\\
2, & y>1.
\end{cases}
\]
Equivalently, the compact real oval maps to \([\ySub,1]\), the noncompact real oval maps to \([0,\infty)\), and the total real image is \([\ySub,\infty)\).
\item For every $y>0$, the root \(\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)\) on the positive real sheet is simple, positive, and uniquely maximal in modulus among the four roots of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\).
Thus \(r(y)=\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)\) on the whole positive axis.
\item The non-dominant branch roles are exactly these: at \(y=1\) the colliding double root \(\lambda=-1\) has modulus smaller than the simple root \(2\), and at \(y=\ySub\) the colliding pair is dominated by the residual complex-conjugate pair through \eqref{eq:ysub-modulus-gap}.
Thus \(y=1\) and \(y=\ySub\) are branch points of the discriminant curve but are not maximal-modulus endpoints for the positive real envelope.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The consolidated dependency list is Proposition~\ref{prop:consolidated-real-branch-classification}.  We unpack it against the five assertions.
The discriminant first partitions the real $y$-axis into the four regular cells
$(-\infty,\ySub)$, $(\ySub,0)$, $(0,1)$, and $(1,\infty)$, with only the three real branch values as possible places where the fibre type can change.
The real-oval description then fixes, on each regular cell, exactly how many real sheets are present and which oval supplies them.
Thus the real-root-count table in part~(3) is carried by the component-image identities \([\ySub,1]\) and \([0,\infty)\) in Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-b-real-projection-criterion} together with the two-to-one covering over their interiors, not by an additional Sturm variation-count argument.
Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-axis-dominance-criterion} is the positive-axis criterion: the discriminant and endpoint resultants define the positive cells; \eqref{eq:opposite-modulus-resultant} excludes interior opposite-root equal-modulus crossings; the derivative comparison on \((0,1)\) separates the positive sheet from the negative competitor; and \eqref{eq:ygt1-no-negative-roots} plus the coefficient comparison handles the residual conjugate pair on \((1,\infty)\).
On $(0,1)$ all four sheets are real; the positive sheet is separated from the possible negative competitor by the reciprocal-type endpoint comparison in Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-axis-dominance-criterion}.
At $y=1$ the explicit factorization isolates the double root at $-1$ and leaves the root $2$ as the only largest-modulus root, so no dominance exchange occurs there.
On $(1,\infty)$ the real-root framework leaves two positive real roots and one complex-conjugate pair; coefficient comparison in the same criterion shows that the conjugate modulus is smaller than the larger positive root.
Thus the positive sheet cannot lose dominance anywhere on $(0,\infty)$: the only possible loss points would be a branch value or an equal-modulus crossing, and the interval criteria and endpoint factorizations exclude both.
The finite branch values and the naming of \(\ySub,\eta,\overline\eta\) are Theorem~\ref{thm:elliptic-reduction}; the discriminant factorization is Corollary~\ref{cor:disc-factor}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:cubic-branch-real-root} shows that the cubic factor has exactly one real zero, and Theorem~\ref{thm:ep-classification} gives the exact factorization and modulus comparison at the three real branch values.
The real oval images and the resulting real-root counts are Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-b-real-projection-criterion}; Corollary~\ref{cor:real-phase-diagram} records only the component description used to prove that theorem.
Finally, Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-maximal-modulus-branch} constructs the positive real sheet over \((0,\infty)\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-axis-dominance-criterion} identifies that sheet with the spectral radius for every $y>0$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:ep-classification} gives the exact non-dominant roles at \(y=1\) and \(y=\ySub\), including the strict modulus gap \eqref{eq:ysub-modulus-gap} at the negative real branch value.
Equivalently, Proposition~\ref{prop:consolidated-real-branch-classification} supplies the single criterion list for the branch values, interval images, real-root counts, and strict modulus comparisons, and the cited inputs prove all five assertions with the same constants and branch labels.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Normalization and dominance comparison for Theorems C and D]\label{cor:normalized-branch-classification}
Let $u\in\RR$ and $\rho\in\RR^\times$, and let $P_{a,c,d,g}$ be a balanced polynomial with normalized collision data $(\rho,u)$ in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:normalized-collision-data}.  Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-specialization-up-to-normalization} supplies the algebraic normalization data, while Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance} supplies the canonical dominance data.
Then the weighted affine normalization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:carried-normalized-branch-change}
\lambda=\rho\widetilde\lambda,
\qquad
y=u+\rho^2\widetilde y
\end{equation}
identifies $P_{a,c,d,g}$ with the canonical quartic by
\[
P_{a,c,d,g}(\rho\widetilde\lambda,u+\rho^2\widetilde y)
=
\rho^4\Pi(\widetilde\lambda,
\widetilde y).
\]
Consequently the full real-fibre and branch-dominance classification for this normalized member, under this real nonzero weighted scaling, is the pullback of Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance} via Lemma~\ref{lem:real-weighted-pullback-dominance}, as summarized in Corollary~\ref{cor:canonical-dominance-after-normalization}.
In particular, the finite real branch values are exactly
\[
u+\rho^2\ySub,
\qquad
u,
\qquad
u+\rho^2,
\]
the finite real branch values over $\CC$ are $u$, $u+\rho^2$, $u+\rho^2\ySub$, $u+\rho^2\eta$, and $u+\rho^2\overline\eta$, and the regular real-fibre root counts are obtained from the canonical intervals by the affine coordinate $\widetilde y=(y-u)/\rho^2$.
The chosen collision $(\rho,u)$ is therefore a dominant positive collision in the carried sense: for every $y>u$, equivalently every $\widetilde y>0$, the sheet $\lambda=\rho\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(\widetilde y)$ is simple and strictly larger in modulus than every other root of $P_{a,c,d,g}(\cdot,y)$, and $(\rho,u)$ is its square-root endpoint as $y\downarrow u$ on that side.
This carried normalized corollary applies precisely to the collision data of Definition~\ref{def:normalized-collision-data}.
It is not a dominance principle for arbitrary balanced members, complex scalings, or the side $\widetilde y<0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-specialization-up-to-normalization} proves that normalized collision data $(\rho,u)$ are equivalent to the two algebraic double-root constraints at $(\rho,u)$ and $(-\rho,u+\rho^2)$ and that these constraints force
\[
(a,c,d,g)=(-\rho,\,2u-\rho^2,\,\rho^3,\,u(u-\rho^2)).
\]
The same theorem gives the displayed identity with $\Pi=P_{-1,-1,1,0}$.
Thus \eqref{eq:carried-normalized-branch-change} is an invertible change of variables between the original fibre over $y$ and the canonical fibre over $\widetilde y=(y-u)/\rho^2$.
For each fixed $\widetilde y$, roots of $P_{a,c,d,g}(\cdot,u+\rho^2\widetilde y)$ are exactly $\rho$ times the roots of $\Pi(\cdot,\widetilde y)$, so the number of real roots, equality of moduli, strict modulus gaps, simplicity away from branch values, and maximal-modulus status are preserved; every modulus is multiplied by the same factor $\abs\rho$.
Branch values of the projection are carried by $\widetilde y\mapsto u+\rho^2\widetilde y$, giving the listed real branch values and the two non-real conjugate branch values.
The canonical root counts and dominance roles are precisely Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance}; pulling them back through the affine map gives the asserted classification and the strict positive-side dominance inequality.
Finally, the canonical maximal endpoint $(\widetilde\lambda,
\widetilde y)=(1,0)$ pulls back to $(\lambda,y)=(\rho,u)$, and the side $\widetilde y>0$ is exactly the side on which the canonical positive sheet is dominant.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Positive-side dominance gap under real scaling]\label{prop:positive-side-real-scaling-stability}
Let $u\in\RR$, $\rho\in\RR^\times$, and let $P_{a,c,d,g}$ be a balanced polynomial with normalized collision data $(\rho,u)$.
Let $I\subset(0,\infty)$ be a compact interval in the normalized coordinate $\widetilde y$.
For $\widetilde y\in I$, write $\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(\widetilde y)$ for the canonical positive real dominant root of $\Pi(\cdot,\widetilde y)$ and set
\[
\delta(\widetilde y)
=
\abs{\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(\widetilde y)}
-
\max\{\abs\lambda:\Pi(\lambda,\widetilde y)=0,
\lambda\ne\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(\widetilde y)\}.
\]
Then
\[
\gamma_I:=\min_{\widetilde y\in I}\delta(\widetilde y)>0.
\]
For every $\widetilde y\in I$, and $y=u+\rho^2\widetilde y$, the root
\[
\lambda=\rho\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(\widetilde y)
\]
is the unique maximal-modulus root of $P_{a,c,d,g}(\cdot,y)$.
Moreover, if the carried fibre gap is denoted
\[
\delta_{P}(u+\rho^2\widetilde y)
=
\abs{\rho\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(\widetilde y)}
-
\max\{\abs\lambda:P_{a,c,d,g}(\lambda,u+\rho^2\widetilde y)=0,
\lambda\ne\rho\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(\widetilde y)\},
\]
then the exact transported identity
\[
\delta_{P}(u+\rho^2\widetilde y)=\abs\rho\,\delta(\widetilde y)
\]
holds on $I$, and hence the uniform carried gap is precisely
\[
\min_{\widetilde y\in I}\delta_{P}(u+\rho^2\widetilde y)
=
\abs\rho\,\gamma_I.
\]
Conversely, uniqueness of the maximal-modulus root is invariant under the real scaling: for any fixed $\widetilde y>0$, a root $\lambda_*$ of $P_{a,c,d,g}(\cdot,u+\rho^2\widetilde y)$ is unique maximal if and only if $\lambda_*/\rho$ is the unique maximal root of $\Pi(\cdot,\widetilde y)$.
Thus the only dominance information transported after the algebraic normalization of Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-specialization-up-to-normalization} is the canonical dominance of Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance}, carried through Lemma~\ref{lem:real-weighted-pullback-dominance}, namely this exact gap for the normalized member on compact subsets of the positive side; the proposition is not a deformation or stability theorem for arbitrary balanced quartic families.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The carried identity in Corollary~\ref{cor:normalized-branch-classification} gives, for every fixed $\widetilde y$,
\[
P_{a,c,d,g}(\rho\widetilde\lambda,u+\rho^2\widetilde y)
=
\rho^4\Pi(\widetilde\lambda,\widetilde y).
\]
Because $\rho\ne0$, the roots of the original fibre over $y=u+\rho^2\widetilde y$ are exactly $\rho$ times the roots of the canonical fibre over $\widetilde y$.
Multiplication by the real nonzero scalar $\rho$ multiplies every modulus by the common positive factor $\abs\rho$ and therefore preserves and reflects strict maximal-modulus inequalities.
This proves the asserted if and only if for each fixed fibre, identifies the carried dominant root as $\rho\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(\widetilde y)$, and gives the exact fibrewise identity \(\delta_P(u+\rho^2\widetilde y)=\abs\rho\,\delta(\widetilde y)\).

It remains only to prove the uniform positive lower bound on a compact normalized interval.
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance} gives
\[
\delta(\widetilde y)>0
\qquad(\widetilde y>0).
\]
The root $\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(\widetilde y)$ is simple on $(0,\infty)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-maximal-modulus-branch}, including across the non-dominant branch collision at $\widetilde y=1$, where the explicit factorization leaves the dominant root simple.
Hence this root is locally analytic in $\widetilde y$, while the remaining three roots form a continuous multiset as functions of the coefficients of the polynomial.
The maximum of the moduli of that remaining multiset is therefore continuous, and so $\delta$ is continuous on $I$.
Since $I$ is compact and $\delta$ is everywhere positive on $I$, it has a positive minimum $\gamma_I$.
The exact fibrewise identity then gives the displayed minimum formula for \(\delta_P\) on the carried interval.

Finally, the side condition is not cosmetic: because $\rho^2>0$, the normalized positive side $\widetilde y>0$ is exactly $y>u$.
The proof has used only the canonical positive-axis dominance theorem and the real nonzero scaling above, so it supplies no claim for complex scalings, unrelated balanced members, perturbations of the coefficients, or regions outside the chosen fixed positive-side compact interval.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Endpoint at \texorpdfstring{\(y=0\)}{y=0} and Puiseux expansion}\label{subsec:endpoint-puiseux}

The endpoint $y = 0$ is not an ordinary analytic point of the inverse function $\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)$, but a square-root ramification point.
The local expansion is a classical Puiseux series \cite{BrieskornKnorrer1986,Walker1950}.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint Puiseux expansion at \(y=0\)]\label{prop:endpoint-puiseux}
Setting $\lambda = 1 + \varepsilon$, the maximal-modulus branch satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:y-puiseux}
y_{\mathrm{dom}}(1 + \varepsilon)
= 2\varepsilon^2 - 5\varepsilon^3 + 21\varepsilon^4 - 94\varepsilon^5 + O(\varepsilon^6).
\end{equation}
Inverting, the maximal-modulus eigenvalue on the maximal-modulus branch over \(y>0\) expands as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda-puiseux}
\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)
= 1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,y^{1/2}
+ \frac{5}{8}\,y
- \frac{43}{64\sqrt{2}}\,y^{3/2}
- \frac{1}{16}\,y^2
+ O(y^{5/2}).
\end{equation}
The logarithmic spectral radius
$f_{\mathrm{dom}}(y) = \log\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)$
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f-puiseux}
f_{\mathrm{dom}}(y)
= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,y^{1/2}
+ \frac{3}{8}\,y
- \frac{217\sqrt{2}}{384}\,y^{3/2}
+ O(y^2),
\end{equation}
and the scaled logarithmic slope
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho-endpoint}
\sigma(y):=y\,f_{\mathrm{dom}}'(y)
= \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\,y^{1/2}
+ \frac{3}{8}\,y
- \frac{217\sqrt{2}}{256}\,y^{3/2}
+ O(y^2).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At $\lambda = 1 + \varepsilon$,
\[
4\lambda^3 - 4\lambda + 1
= 1 + 8\varepsilon + 12\varepsilon^2 + 4\varepsilon^3.
\]
Taking the square root by the binomial series:
\[
\sqrt{4\lambda^3 - 4\lambda + 1}
= 1 + 4\varepsilon - 2\varepsilon^2 + 10\varepsilon^3 - 42\varepsilon^4 + 188\varepsilon^5 + O(\varepsilon^6).
\]
Substituting into \eqref{eq:y-phys}:
\begin{align*}
y_{\mathrm{dom}}(1 + \varepsilon)
&= (1 + \varepsilon)^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}
  - \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1 + 4\varepsilon - 2\varepsilon^2 + 10\varepsilon^3 - 42\varepsilon^4 + 188\varepsilon^5 + \cdots\bigr) \\
&= 1 + 2\varepsilon + \varepsilon^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}
  - \tfrac{1}{2} - 2\varepsilon + \varepsilon^2 - 5\varepsilon^3 + 21\varepsilon^4 - 94\varepsilon^5 - \cdots \\
&= 2\varepsilon^2 - 5\varepsilon^3 + 21\varepsilon^4 - 94\varepsilon^5 + O(\varepsilon^6).
\end{align*}
To invert the series, write
\[
\varepsilon = A y^{1/2} + B y + C y^{3/2} + D y^2 + O(y^{5/2}).
\]
Substituting this ansatz into \eqref{eq:y-puiseux} and matching the coefficients of \(y\), \(y^{3/2}\), \(y^2\), and \(y^{5/2}\) gives
\[
2A^2=1,\qquad 4AB-5A^3=0,
\]
\[
4AC+2B^2-15A^2B+21A^4=0,
\]
\[
4AD+4BC-15A^2C-15AB^2+84A^3B-94A^5=0.
\]
Choosing the positive branch \(A=1/\sqrt2\) and solving successively yields
\[
A=\frac{1}{\sqrt2},\qquad
B=\frac58,\qquad
C=-\frac{43}{64\sqrt2},\qquad
D=-\frac{1}{16},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:lambda-puiseux}.
Then
\[
\log(1+\varepsilon)=\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^3}{3}+O(y^2)
\]
gives \eqref{eq:f-puiseux}, and term-by-term differentiation yields \eqref{eq:rho-endpoint}.
\end{proof}

Equation \eqref{eq:rho-endpoint} shows that the scaled logarithmic slope does not open linearly from the endpoint \(y=0\), but with a square-root singularity:
\[
\sigma(y) \sim \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\,y^{1/2}
\qquad (y \to 0^+).
\]

\subsection{Standard square-root branching and local cosine law}\label{subsec:endpoint-quantization}

The same square-root branch point controls the first zeros of \(Z_m\) on the negative real axis.
There are two distinct ingredients.
First, the spectral polynomial itself forces a square-root splitting whenever \(\partial_\lambda P\) vanishes simply in the \(y\)-direction.
Second, once the maximal-modulus pair for this quartic endpoint has symmetric amplitudes and the remaining branches are shown to be uniformly smaller on the complex microscopic scale, the fixed recurrence reduces to a shifted cosine law.
We state the needed local tools in an abstract notation only to avoid repeating the endpoint calculation; their hypotheses are verified here only for \(\Pi\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-ep2-branching} is the usual Puiseux square-root branching at a simple double point, equivalently the local analytic perturbation of a double eigenvalue in this scalar spectral setting \cite{Walker1950,BrieskornKnorrer1986,Kato1976}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:quartic-ep2-verification} is the separate quartic verification at \((\lambda,y)=(1,0)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:generic-ep2-to-cosine-interface} then assembles the EP2 branching, fixed-window scaling, and Rouch\'e isolation into the reusable analytic interface used by the endpoint theorem.  Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} is the zero-isolation component of this interface, a localized two-branch version of the standard Beraha--Kahane--Weiss analysis of zeros of spectral sums, with the final zero isolation supplied by Rouch\'e's theorem \cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1975,BerahaKahaneWeiss1978,Sokal2004}.
In the BKW--Sokal mechanism, limiting zeros occur where two or more dominant spectral branches have equal modulus, provided the corresponding amplitudes do not remove the leading competition; the lemma below is the microscopic square-root endpoint specialization of that mechanism after the fixed positive-axis dominance theorem and local remainder bounds have reduced the endpoint-scale spectral sum to one conjugate pair.
In the argument below the external perturbation and BKW references are used only for this local template: after the square-root base change the two colliding roots and residues are holomorphic, the remaining spectral sum is bounded by an explicit compact-uniform remainder, and Rouch\'e's theorem is applied on fixed contours; all constants, amplitudes, and remainder bounds for the quartic are verified separately in Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-cosine-hypotheses} and Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching}.

\begin{lemma}[Local square-root branching at a simple double point]\label{lem:generic-ep2-branching}
Let \(P(\lambda,y)\) be holomorphic near \((\lambda_c,y_c)\in\CC^2\), and assume
\[
P(\lambda_c,y_c)=P_\lambda(\lambda_c,y_c)=0,
\qquad
P_{\lambda\lambda}(\lambda_c,y_c)P_y(\lambda_c,y_c)\neq 0.
\]
Then, after setting \(y=y_c+s^2\), there exist \(s_0>0\) and holomorphic branches \(\lambda_\pm(s)\) for \(|s|<s_0\) such that
\[
P(\lambda_\pm(s),y_c+s^2)=0,
\qquad
\lambda_\pm(0)=\lambda_c,
\]
and
\[
\lambda_\pm(s)=\lambda_c\pm \alpha s+\beta s^2+O(s^3),
\qquad
\alpha^2=-\frac{2P_y}{P_{\lambda\lambda}}(\lambda_c,y_c).
\]
After relabeling, one may choose the branches so that \(\lambda_-(s)=\lambda_+(-s)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the standard Puiseux proof for a plane-curve double point, written out in the local coordinates needed below; the cited Weierstrass--Puiseux/implicit-function theorem is used only to assert that, after replacing \(y-y_c\) by \(s^2\), the two isolated roots through \(\lambda_c\) become holomorphic functions of \(s\) \cite{Walker1950,BrieskornKnorrer1986,Kato1976}.
Write \(h=\lambda-\lambda_c\).
Taylor expansion at \((\lambda_c,y_c)\) gives
\[
P(\lambda_c+h,y_c+s^2)
=
\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda\lambda}(\lambda_c,y_c)h^2
+P_y(\lambda_c,y_c)s^2
+R(h,s),
\]
where
\[
R(h,s)=O\!\bigl(|h|^3+|h||s|^2+|s|^4\bigr).
\]
Set \(h=su\) and define
\[
G(u,s)=
\begin{cases}
s^{-2}P(\lambda_c+su,y_c+s^2), & s\neq 0,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}P_{\lambda\lambda}(\lambda_c,y_c)u^2+P_y(\lambda_c,y_c), & s=0.
\end{cases}
\]
The remainder estimate shows that \(G\) is holomorphic near \((u,s)=(\pm\alpha,0)\), where \(\alpha\) is any square root of \(-2P_y/P_{\lambda\lambda}\); this is the local analytic perturbation step for the two roots after the square-root base change \(y-y_c=s^2\) \cite{Kato1976}.
Moreover,
\[
G(\pm\alpha,0)=0,
\qquad
\partial_u G(\pm\alpha,0)=P_{\lambda\lambda}(\lambda_c,y_c)(\pm\alpha)\neq 0.
\]
By the implicit-function theorem, there exist holomorphic functions \(u_\pm(s)=\pm\alpha+O(s)\) such that
\[
G(u_\pm(s),s)=0.
\]
Setting \(\lambda_\pm(s)=\lambda_c+s\,u_\pm(s)\) yields
\[
\lambda_\pm(s)=\lambda_c\pm\alpha s+\beta_\pm s^2+O(s^3).
\]
Replacing \(s\) by \(-s\) leaves \(y_c+s^2\) unchanged and interchanges the two roots issuing from \(\lambda_c\).
After relabeling, this gives \(\lambda_-(s)=\lambda_+(-s)\), and hence \(\beta_-=\beta_+=:\beta\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Quartic verification of the simple endpoint double point]\label{prop:quartic-ep2-verification}
For the spectral quartic \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\), the point \((\lambda_c,y_c)=(1,0)\) satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-ep2-branching}.
Consequently, after setting \(y=s^2\), the two branches through \(\lambda=1\) have the form
\[
\lambda_\pm(s)=1\pm\frac{s}{\sqrt2}+\beta s^2+O(s^3),
\qquad
\lambda_-(s)=\lambda_+(-s),
\]
for a common coefficient \(\beta\).
These are exactly the two local branches used in the endpoint zero theorem; the sharper coefficient \(\beta=5/8\), the logarithmic coefficient \(3/8\), and the amplitude constants are the quartic-specific data verified separately in Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-cosine-hypotheses}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct differentiation gives
\[
\Pi(1,0)=0,
\qquad
\Pi_\lambda(1,0)=4-3-2+1=0,
\]
and
\[
\Pi_{\lambda\lambda}(1,0)=12-6-2=4,
\qquad
\Pi_y(1,0)=-2+1=-1.
\]
Thus \(\Pi_{\lambda\lambda}(1,0)\Pi_y(1,0)=-4\ne0\), so Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-ep2-branching} applies.
Its leading constant satisfies
\[
\alpha^2=-\frac{2\Pi_y(1,0)}{\Pi_{\lambda\lambda}(1,0)}=\frac12,
\]
and choosing the positive square root on the branch opening into \(y>0\) gives \(\alpha=1/\sqrt2\).
The branch symmetry \(\lambda_-(s)=\lambda_+(-s)\) is part of the generic conclusion.
The identification with the endpoint branches used below follows because \(
\Pi(\lambda,0)=\lambda(\lambda-1)^2(\lambda+1)
\), so the two branches through \(1\) are precisely the colliding pair at the maximal-modulus endpoint of Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance}.
This is exactly the elementary EP2 situation covered by the Puiseux normal form of Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-ep2-branching}; no higher-order singularity or matrix Jordan-chain input is being used beyond the scalar analytic-perturbation theorem cited there.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-window two-branch scaling asymptotic]\label{lem:two-branch-scaling-asymptotic}
Let \(y_c\in\RR\), and let \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}\) be holomorphic in a neighborhood of \(y_c\).
Assume also that \(Z_m(y)\) is real for real \(y\) near \(y_c\).
Assume that there exists \(s_0>0\) such that, for \(|s|<s_0\),
\[
Z_m(y_c+s^2)=C_+(s)\lambda_+(s)^m + C_-(s)\lambda_-(s)^m + R_m(y_c+s^2),
\]
where \(\lambda_\pm\) and \(C_\pm\) are holomorphic on \(|s|<s_0\), the branches are labelled so that
\[
\lambda_-(s)=\lambda_+(-s),
\qquad
C_-(s)=C_+(-s),
\]
and they satisfy
\[
\lambda_\pm(s)=\lambda_c\exp\!\left(\pm as + bs^2 + O(s^3)\right),
\qquad
C_\pm(s)=A_*\mp B_*s+O(s^2),
\]
with real constants \(\lambda_c>0\), \(a>0\), \(b\in\RR\), \(A_*\neq 0\), and \(B_*\in\RR\).
After possibly decreasing \(s_0\), assume also that \(\lambda_\pm(s)\ne0\) and \(C_\pm(s)\ne0\) on \(|s|<s_0\).
Assume moreover that for every compact \(K\subset\CC\) there exists \(M_K>0\) such that
\[
\sup_{u\in K}
\left|
R_m\!\left(y_c-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)
\right|
\le M_K \lambda_c^m m^{-2}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\).
Define
\[
F_m(u):=
\frac{e^{b u^2/m}}{2A_*\lambda_c^m}
Z_m\!\left(y_c-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)
\]
and
\[
g_m(u):=\cos\!\left(\left(a-\frac{B_*}{A_*m}\right)u\right).
\]
Then, for every fixed compact \(K\subset\CC\), there are constants
\(C_K>0\) and \(m_K^{(0)}\), depending only on \(K\), the branch expansions, and the remainder constant for \(K\), such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-window-ep2-scaling-explicit-bound}
\sup_{u\in K}\abs{F_m(u)-g_m(u)}\le \frac{C_K}{m^2}
\qquad (m\ge m_K^{(0)}).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for every fixed compact \(K\subset\CC\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-window-ep2-scaling}
F_m(u)
=
\cos\!\left(
a u-\frac{B_*}{A_*}\frac{u}{m}
\right)+O_K(m^{-2})
\end{equation}
uniformly for \(u\in K\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a compact set \(K\subset\CC\) and set \(s=iu/m\), so that \(y=y_c-u^2/m^2\).
For all sufficiently large \(m\), the set \(iK/m\) lies in \(|s|<s_0\), so the chosen branch and amplitude labels are used on a single domain throughout the compact window.
Because the expansions are holomorphic in \(s\), the \(O(s^3)\) and \(O(s^2)\) remainders are uniform on compact subsets of \(\CC\) near \(0\).
The nonvanishing of \(\lambda_\pm\) permits the logarithmic form on this disc, and the nonvanishing of \(C_\pm\) ensures that the leading amplitude \(A_*\) cannot be cancelled by a zero of either maximal branch.
Therefore,
\[
\lambda_\pm\!\left(\frac{iu}{m}\right)^m
=
\lambda_c^m
\exp\!\left(
\pm iau-\frac{bu^2}{m}+O_K(m^{-2})
\right),
\]
and similarly
\[
C_\pm\!\left(\frac{iu}{m}\right)
=
A_*\mp iB_*\frac{u}{m}+O_K(m^{-2}).
\]
Multiplying and adding the two maximal-modulus contributions gives
\begin{align*}
\frac{e^{b u^2/m}}{2A_*\lambda_c^m}
\bigl(C_+\lambda_+^m + C_-\lambda_-^m\bigr)
&=
\cos(au)+\frac{B_*}{A_*}\frac{u}{m}\sin(au)+O_K(m^{-2}).
\end{align*}
Using
\[
\cos(\theta-\delta)=\cos\theta+\delta\sin\theta+O(\delta^2),
\qquad
\delta=\frac{B_*}{A_*}\frac{u}{m},
\]
we obtain
\[
\frac{e^{b u^2/m}}{2A_*\lambda_c^m}
\bigl(C_+\lambda_+^m + C_-\lambda_-^m\bigr)
=
g_m(u)+O_K(m^{-2}).
\]
The constants in the last display are obtained by bounding the holomorphic remainders in the branch and amplitude expansions on the fixed compact set \(K\), multiplying by the bounded factors \(\exp(\pm iau-bu^2/m)\), and adding the normalized remainder term
\[
\frac{e^{b u^2/m}}{2A_*\lambda_c^m}
R_m\!\left(y_c-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right).
\]
Thus, after increasing the threshold if necessary, there is a constant \(C_K\) such that
\[
\sup_{u\in K}\abs{F_m(u)-g_m(u)}\le \frac{C_K}{m^2}.
\]
The compact set \(K\) has been fixed throughout this estimate, so the constants just obtained are the constants for that compact endpoint window.
This is \eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-scaling-explicit-bound}, and \eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-scaling} is its asymptotic form.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-window zero isolation for the two-branch cosine limit]\label{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law}
Assume the hypotheses and notation of Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-scaling-asymptotic}.
This lemma is a fixed-window assertion: every compact set, every index set, and every half-window below is chosen independently of \(m\) before the Rouch\'e estimates are applied.
Fix \(k\ge 1\) and set
\[
q_k:=\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{2},
\qquad
u_k^{(0)}:=\frac{q_k}{a}.
\]
Then there exist \(\eta_k>0\) and \(m_k\) such that, for every \(m\ge m_k\), the function \(F_m\) has exactly one zero \(u_{m,k}\) in the disc
\[
\{u\in\CC:\abs{u-u_k^{(0)}}<\eta_k\},
\]
this zero is real and positive, the zeros \(u_{m,1},\ldots,u_{m,k}\) are ordered increasingly for \(m\) large, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-window-ep2-u}
u_{m,k}
=
\frac{q_k}{a}
\left(
1+\frac{B_*}{aA_*m}
\right)
+O_k(m^{-2}),
\end{equation}
whence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-window-ep2-y}
y_{m,k}
=
y_c-\frac{q_k^2}{a^2m^2}
-\frac{2B_*q_k^2}{a^3A_*m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4}).
\end{equation}
For every fixed integer \(K_0\ge1\), the constants and thresholds in \eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-u} and \eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-y} may be chosen uniformly for all \(1\le k\le K_0\) by taking the finitely many Rouch\'e discs disjoint and then taking the maximum of their constants and thresholds.
Moreover, if \(U>0\) is fixed and is not a cosine-zero threshold, in the sense that \(aU\neq q_j\) for every \(j\ge1\), and if
\[
J(U):=\{j\ge1:q_j/a<U\},
\]
then the fixed closed half-window
\[
Q_U:=\{u\in\CC:0\le \Re u\le U,\ \abs{\Im u}\le U\}
\]
admits the following explicit Rouch\'e result.
Choose pairwise disjoint discs
\[
D_j=\{u\in\CC:\abs{u-u_j^{(0)}}<\eta_j\}
\qquad (j\in J(U))
\]
whose closures lie in the interior of \(Q_U\).
If \(J(U)=\emptyset\), no discs are chosen.
Put
\[
K_U:=Q_U\setminus\bigcup_{j\in J(U)}D_j
\]
and
\[
\delta_U:=
\min\left(
\inf_{u\in K_U}\abs{\cos(au)},
\inf_{j\in J(U)}\inf_{u\in\partial D_j}\abs{\cos(au)}
\right)>0,
\]
where the second infimum is omitted when \(J(U)=\emptyset\).
There is a threshold \(m_U\), depending only on \(Q_U\), the chosen discs,
\(\delta_U\), and the compact constant \(C_{Q_U}\) in
\eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-scaling-explicit-bound}, such that for every
\(m\ge m_U\) the contours \(\partial D_j\) satisfy
\[
\abs{g_m(u)}\ge \frac{\delta_U}{2},
\qquad
\abs{F_m(u)-g_m(u)}\le \frac{\delta_U}{4}
\qquad (u\in\partial D_j,\ j\in J(U)),
\]
the complement satisfies
\[
\abs{F_m(u)}\ge \frac{\delta_U}{4}
\qquad (u\in K_U),
\]
and the only zeros of \(F_m\) in \(Q_U\) are the zeros \(u_{m,j}\) with
\(j\in J(U)\), one in each disc \(D_j\).
The constants and the threshold are attached to the fixed window \(Q_U\), the finite set \(J(U)\), the nonzero leading amplitude \(A_*\), and the displayed compact-uniform remainder bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-scaling-asymptotic} supplies the compact-uniform estimate \eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-scaling-explicit-bound} on every fixed compact set used below.
The BKW--Sokal principle is therefore used only as motivation for retaining the two dominant branches; the quantitative input for the present proof is the explicit two-branch expansion and the compact remainder hypothesis, which are verified for the quartic application in Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching}.

Now fix \(k\ge1\).
Choose \(\eta_k>0\) so small that \(\cos(au)\) has no zero on the circle \(\abs{u-u_k^{(0)}}=\eta_k\).
Then
\[
\delta_k:=\inf_{\abs{u-u_k^{(0)}}=\eta_k}\abs{\cos(au)}>0.
\]
Since \(g_m\to \cos(au)\) uniformly on that circle, there exists \(m_k\) such that for all \(m\ge m_k\),
\[
\abs{g_m(u)}\ge \frac{\delta_k}{2}
\qquad
\text{whenever }\abs{u-u_k^{(0)}}=\eta_k.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-scaling} gives a matching bound
\[
\abs{F_m(u)-g_m(u)}\le \frac{\delta_k}{4}
\qquad
\text{whenever }\abs{u-u_k^{(0)}}=\eta_k
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\).
Therefore
\[
\abs{F_m(u)}\ge \abs{g_m(u)}-\abs{F_m(u)-g_m(u)}\ge \frac{\delta_k}{4}
\qquad
\text{whenever }\abs{u-u_k^{(0)}}=\eta_k.
\]
Rouch\'e's theorem applied to the positively oriented circle \(\abs{u-u_k^{(0)}}=\eta_k\) now implies that \(F_m\) and \(g_m\) have the same number of zeros in \(\abs{u-u_k^{(0)}}<\eta_k\).
This is the zero-isolation step after the compact-window two-branch approximation has been made explicit.
Since \(g_m(u)=0\) exactly when
\[
au-\frac{B_*}{A_*}\frac{u}{m}=q_k
\]
and the affine phase has a unique solution
\[
u_{m,k}^{(0)}:=\frac{q_k}{a-\frac{B_*}{A_*m}}
\]
in that disc for \(m\) large, \(F_m\) has exactly one zero \(u_{m,k}\) there.

Because \(Z_m\) is real on the real axis and the constants in the normalization of \(F_m\) are real, Schwarz reflection gives
\[
F_m(\overline u)=\overline{F_m(u)}
\]
on every sufficiently small conjugation-invariant compact set in the \(u\)-plane.
The disc \(\abs{u-u_k^{(0)}}<\eta_k\) is centered on the real axis.
If its unique zero were non-real, its complex conjugate would be a distinct zero in the same disc, contradicting uniqueness.
Hence \(u_{m,k}\in\RR\).
Since \(u_{m,k}=u_k^{(0)}+o(1)\) and \(u_k^{(0)}>0\), this zero is positive for \(m\) large.
For any fixed \(k\), the centers satisfy
\[
0<u_1^{(0)}<u_2^{(0)}<\cdots<u_k^{(0)}.
\]
Choosing the finitely many radii \(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_k\) disjoint and then taking \(m\) large gives
\[
0<u_{m,1}<u_{m,2}<\cdots<u_{m,k}.
\]

Moreover,
\[
g_m'(u_{m,k}^{(0)})
=
-\left(a-\frac{B_*}{A_*m}\right)\sin q_k
\]
stays uniformly away from \(0\).
Since \(F_m(u_{m,k})=0\) and \(F_m-g_m=O_k(m^{-2})\) uniformly on the disc, a first-order Taylor expansion about \(u_{m,k}^{(0)}\) gives
\[
u_{m,k}=u_{m,k}^{(0)}+O_k(m^{-2}).
\]
Expanding \(u_{m,k}^{(0)}\) yields \eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-u}, and squaring \(u_{m,k}\) gives \eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-y}.

It remains to prove the compact-window exclusion with the stated dependence of
constants.
Fix \(U>0\) with \(aU\neq q_j\) for every \(j\ge1\), and set
\[
Q_U:=\{u\in\CC:0\le \Re u\le U,\ \abs{\Im u}\le U\}.
\]
Only finitely many zeros of \(\cos(au)\) lie in \(Q_U\), namely the points \(u_j^{(0)}=q_j/a\) with \(j\in J(U)\).
Choose pairwise disjoint discs
\[
D_j:=\{u\in\CC:\abs{u-u_j^{(0)}}<\eta_j\}
\qquad (j\in J(U))
\]
with closures contained in the interior of \(Q_U\).
If \(J(U)=\emptyset\), this family of discs is empty.
The complement
\[
K_U:=Q_U\setminus\bigcup_{j\in J(U)}D_j
\]
is compact and contains no zero of \(\cos(au)\).
By the non-threshold condition at the right boundary and by the disc removal
around every interior cosine zero, the positive number
\[
\delta_U:=
\min\left(
\inf_{u\in K_U}\abs{\cos(au)},
\inf_{j\in J(U)}\inf_{u\in\partial D_j}\abs{\cos(au)}
\right)>0
\]
is well-defined, with the second infimum omitted when no discs have been
chosen.
Since \(g_m(u)=\cos((a-B_*/(A_*m))u)\) converges uniformly to \(\cos(au)\) on \(Q_U\), there is a threshold depending only on \(Q_U\) and \(\delta_U\) such that
\[
\abs{g_m(u)}\ge \frac{\delta_U}{2}
\]
on \(K_U\) and on every boundary circle \(\partial D_j\).
Increasing the threshold once more so that
\(C_{Q_U}m^{-2}\le\delta_U/4\), the explicit compact estimate
\eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-scaling-explicit-bound} gives
\[
\abs{F_m(u)-g_m(u)}\le \frac{\delta_U}{4}
\]
throughout \(Q_U\).
Therefore, on each positively oriented contour \(\partial D_j\),
\[
\abs{F_m(u)-g_m(u)}<\abs{g_m(u)},
\]
and Rouch\'e's theorem gives the same number of zeros of \(F_m\) and
\(g_m\) in \(D_j\).
For all large \(m\), the affine zero
\[
\frac{q_j}{a-\frac{B_*}{A_*m}}
\]
is the unique zero of \(g_m\) in \(D_j\), while no other zero of \(g_m\) enters
the compact half-window outside the chosen discs.
On the complement we have the explicit lower bound
\[
\abs{F_m(u)}\ge \frac{\delta_U}{4}
\qquad (u\in K_U)
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\), so \(F_m\) has no zeros in \(K_U\).
The resulting threshold is the maximum of the finitely many requirements just
listed: branch-domain inclusion for \(Q_U\), uniform convergence of \(g_m\) to
\(\cos(au)\) on \(Q_U\), the compact error inequality
\(C_{Q_U}m^{-2}\le\delta_U/4\), and containment of the shifted affine zeros in
their discs.
Hence it depends only on the fixed half-window, the chosen Rouch\'e contours,
the lower bound \(\delta_U\), and the compact two-branch constants.
This proves both the contour result and the fixed-window exclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Fixed-window real EP2 package]\label{def:fixed-window-real-ep2-package}
Let \((\lambda_c,y_c)\) be a real exceptional point with \(\lambda_c>0\).
We say that a sequence of local spectral sums \(Z_m\) has a fixed-window real EP2 package at \((\lambda_c,y_c)\) if the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-ep2-branching} hold there and, in the square-root coordinate \(y=y_c+s^2\), the two branches through \(\lambda_c\) are the retained endpoint-scale branches in a decomposition
\[
Z_m(y_c+s^2)=C_+(s)\lambda_+(s)^m+C_-(s)\lambda_-(s)^m+R_m(y_c+s^2),
\]
with symmetric labels
\[
\lambda_-(s)=\lambda_+(-s),
\qquad
C_-(s)=C_+(-s),
\]
and constants \(a>0\), \(b\in\RR\), \(A_*\ne0\), \(B_*\in\RR\) such that
\[
\lambda_\pm(s)=\lambda_c\exp(\pm as+bs^2+O(s^3)),
\qquad
C_\pm(s)=A_*\mp B_*s+O(s^2).
\]
The package is required to be fixed-window: for every compact \(K\subset\CC\) there is \(M_K\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(m\),
\[
\sup_{u\in K}\left|R_m\!\left(y_c-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)\right|
\le M_K\lambda_c^m m^{-2},
\]
and the functions \(Z_m\) are real for real \(y\) near \(y_c\).
No uniformity in moving windows or growing index sets is included in the definition.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Generic EP2-to-cosine interface]\label{thm:generic-ep2-to-cosine-interface}\label{cor:generic-ep2-to-cosine-interface}
Assume that \(Z_m\) has a fixed-window real EP2 package at \((\lambda_c,y_c)\), with constants \(a,b,A_*,B_*\) as in Definition~\ref{def:fixed-window-real-ep2-package}.
Then the normalized endpoint functions
\[
F_m(u)=
\frac{e^{b u^2/m}}{2A_*\lambda_c^m}
Z_m\!\left(y_c-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)
\]
satisfy the fixed-window cosine law
\[
F_m(u)=
\cos\!\left(au-\frac{B_*}{A_*}\frac{u}{m}\right)+O_K(m^{-2})
\]
uniformly on every compact \(K\subset\CC\).
Consequently, for each fixed \(k\ge1\), with
\[
q_k=\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{2},
\]
there is a unique real positive zero \(u_{m,k}\) of \(F_m\) near \(q_k/a\) for all
large \(m\), and
\[
u_{m,k}=
\frac{q_k}{a}
\left(1+\frac{B_*}{aA_*m}\right)+O_k(m^{-2}),
\]
with the corresponding endpoint-scale zero
\[
y_{m,k}=
y_c-\frac{q_k^2}{a^2m^2}
-\frac{2B_*q_k^2}{a^3A_*m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4}).
\]
The same conclusion holds uniformly for every fixed finite set of indices and
with the compact half-window exclusion stated in Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law}.
More explicitly, if \(U>0\) is fixed and \(aU\ne q_j\) for every \(j\ge1\), then for all sufficiently large \(m\) the fixed half-rectangle
\[
Q_U=\{u\in\CC:0\le \Re u\le U,\ \abs{\Im u}\le U\}
\]
contains exactly
\[
N_a(U)=\#\{j\ge1:q_j/a<U\}=\left\lfloor \frac{aU}{\pi}+\frac12\right\rfloor
\]
zeros of \(F_m\), all simple, one near each point \(q_j/a<U\), and no further zeros.
The threshold for this assertion may depend on the fixed half-window and on the finitely many Rouch\'e contours chosen inside it, but not on any moving window or growing index set.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fixed-window real EP2 package includes the square-root coordinate and the symmetric labeling supplied by Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-ep2-branching}.
Its remaining clauses are exactly the spectral-decomposition, amplitude, nonvanishing, real-structure, and compact-uniform remainder assumptions of Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-scaling-asymptotic}, after shrinking the square-root disc if necessary to avoid zeros of \(\lambda_\pm\) and \(C_\pm\) near the origin.
That lemma gives the displayed compact-uniform cosine approximation.
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} to this approximation then
gives the unique zeros, their reality and ordering on fixed finite index sets,
the two displayed expansions, and the compact half-window zero exclusion.
For a fixed non-threshold half-window \(Q_U\), the same lemma applies Rouch\'e's theorem on pairwise disjoint contours around the finitely many cosine zeros \(q_j/a<U\) and on the compact complement; this gives exactly one simple zero of \(F_m\) in each such contour and no zero in the complement.
Counting those contours gives \(N_a(U)=\lfloor aU/\pi+1/2\rfloor\).
Thus the three local ingredients---EP2 branching, endpoint-scale two-branch
scaling, and Rouch\'e isolation---assemble into one reusable analytic interface.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint cosine-law hypotheses]\label{prop:endpoint-cosine-hypotheses}
For the quartic transfer family at the endpoint \((\lambda_c,y_c)=(1,0)\), the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} are supplied by the local calculations in Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching}, specifically by the fixed branch-coordinate disc, amplitude symmetry, nonvanishing, and compact-uniform noncolliding remainder in Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-expansion}.  The following list gives the precise branch expansions, amplitude identities, and uniform estimates used in the endpoint theorem.
\begin{enumerate}
\item There is a common radius \(r>0\) and the branch coordinate \(y=s^2\), \(|s|<\sqrt r\), on which the two colliding roots are single-valued holomorphic functions \(\lambda_\pm(s)\) with \(\lambda_-(s)=\lambda_+(-s)\).  This is the branch domain fixed at the start of Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching} and in \eqref{eq:lambda-plus-minus}.  Shrinking \(r\) if necessary, \(\lambda_\pm(s)\ne0\) and the amplitudes below satisfy \(C_\pm(s)\ne0\) on the same disc, since \(\lambda_\pm(0)=1\) and \(C_\pm(0)=1/2\).
\item On the punctured disc \(0<\abs y<r\), the local spectral decomposition
\[
Z_m(y)=\sum_{j=1}^4 C_j(y)\lambda_j(y)^m
\]
holds for \(0<\abs y<r\), with the amplitudes given by the residue formula \eqref{eq:partial-fractions}; this is \eqref{eq:spectral-decomposition} in Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching} and it is the decomposition used below, not a numerical check.
\item After writing \(y=s^2\), the colliding pair is holomorphic in \(s\) and satisfies
\[
\lambda_\pm(s)=1\pm \frac{s}{\sqrt2}+\frac58s^2+O(s^3),
\qquad
\log\lambda_\pm(s)=\pm\frac{s}{\sqrt2}+\frac38s^2+O(s^3),
\]
so the exponential hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} holds with \(\lambda_c=1\), \(a=1/\sqrt2\), and \(b=3/8\); these constants are recorded in \eqref{eq:appendix-cosine-constants} and derived in \eqref{eq:lambda-plus-minus}--\eqref{eq:lambda-pm-exp}, with the endpoint-scale form in \eqref{eq:lambda-power}.
\item The two amplitudes extend holomorphically in the same coordinate, satisfy the same conjugate branch symmetry \(C_-(s)=C_+(-s)\), and obey
\[
C_\pm(s)=\frac12\mp\frac{3\sqrt2}{16}s+O(s^2),
\]
so \(A_*=1/2\ne0\) and \(B_*=3\sqrt2/16\); these constants are recorded in \eqref{eq:appendix-cosine-constants} and derived from the exact coefficient comparisons for \(Z_0,Z_1,Z_2,Z_3\) in \eqref{eq:Bstar-m2}--\eqref{eq:C-plus-minus}, with the endpoint-scale amplitude estimate in \eqref{eq:amplitude-scale}.
\item The two noncolliding branches satisfy, uniformly for \(u\) in any compact set \(K\subset\CC\), the preliminary bounds
\begin{gather*}
\abs{\lambda_{-1}(-u^2/m^2)+1},\quad
\abs{C_{-1}(-u^2/m^2)}=O_K(m^{-2}),\\
\abs{\lambda_0(-u^2/m^2)},\quad
\abs{C_{\mathrm{small}}(-u^2/m^2)}=O_K(m^{-2}),
\end{gather*}
which are exactly \eqref{eq:y-bound}.  The logarithmic estimate \eqref{eq:log-lambda-minus-one} then gives the contribution bound \eqref{eq:minus-one-contribution-bound}, and the small-root estimate gives \eqref{eq:small-root-contribution-bound}.
\item Combining \eqref{eq:minus-one-contribution-bound} and \eqref{eq:small-root-contribution-bound}, the total remainder satisfies, uniformly for \(u\) in any compact set \(K\subset\CC\),
\[
R_m\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)=O_K(m^{-2}),
\]
which is the remainder bound required in Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} because \(\lambda_c=1\).  In the notation of the lemma, for each compact \(K\) there is a constant \(M_K\) such that the displayed bound is at most \(M_K\lambda_c^m m^{-2}\); this is \eqref{eq:appendix-remainder-bound}.
\item The polynomial coefficients of \(Z_m(y)\) are real, indeed integral by Section~\ref{sec:model}.
Hence the Schwarz-reflection and real-zero assertions in Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} apply to the rescaled functions.
\item The compact-window exclusion in Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} uses only the same constants \(M_K\) on the rectangles \(Q_U\) and on the finitely many Rouch\'e circles around the points \(u_k^{(0)}\).  Since these sets are compact, the appendix estimates apply uniformly on every such set.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the localized two-branch oscillation law applies to this endpoint with no additional uniformity or amplitude assumptions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first two items provide the local analytic setting required before Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} can be applied: a common branch coordinate on one disc and the partial-fraction spectral decomposition on the punctured disc.
Item~(3) is the branch-expansion hypothesis of the lemma.
Equations \eqref{eq:lambda-plus-minus} and \eqref{eq:lambda-pm-exp} give the logarithmic normal form with
\[
\lambda_c=1,
\qquad
 a=\frac{1}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
 b=\frac38,
\]
and \eqref{eq:lambda-power} is the same estimate after substituting \(s=iu/m\), uniformly for \(u\) in compact sets.
Item~(4) is the amplitude-symmetry hypothesis.
The residue formula \eqref{eq:partial-fractions}, the coefficient comparisons \eqref{eq:Bstar-m2}--\eqref{eq:Bstar-m3}, and the expansion \eqref{eq:C-plus-minus} give
\[
A_*=\frac12,
\qquad
B_*=\frac{3\sqrt2}{16},
\]
with the rescaled compact-uniform form \eqref{eq:amplitude-scale}.
Items~(5) and (6) verify the non-maximal-modulus remainder hypothesis: the preliminary estimates \eqref{eq:y-bound}, the contribution bounds \eqref{eq:minus-one-contribution-bound} and \eqref{eq:small-root-contribution-bound}, and their sum \eqref{eq:appendix-remainder-bound} give, for every compact \(K\subset\CC\), a constant \(M_K\) such that
\[
\abs{R_m(-u^2/m^2)}\le M_K\lambda_c^m m^{-2}
\qquad (u\in K),
\]
because \(\lambda_c=1\).
These are precisely the constants and the \(O_K\)-uniformity recorded in \eqref{eq:appendix-cosine-constants}, equivalently \eqref{eq:appendix-cosine-constants-repeated}.
Item~(7) supplies the real-coefficient hypothesis used for the Schwarz-reflection and real-zero part of the lemma, and item~(8) records that the rectangles \(Q_U\) and the finitely many Rouch\'e circles used there are compact sets to which the same \(O_K\) estimates apply.
Thus every hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:generic-ep2-to-cosine-interface}, equivalently of its zero-isolation component Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law}, is verified with the constants displayed above, and substitution of those constants gives the parameter tuple used in Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}.
\end{proof}

\qquad

\begin{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Punctured endpoint isolation]\label{thm:endpoint-neighborhood-isolation}
Let \(U>0\) be non-threshold in the endpoint scale, meaning
\[
U\ne \frac{(2j-1)\pi}{\sqrt2}
\qquad (j\ge1).
\]
Then there exists \(m_U\), depending on this fixed \(U\), such that, for every \(m\ge m_U\), the negative real zeros of \(Z_m\) in the punctured endpoint window
\[
-\frac{U^2}{m^2}\le y<0
\]
are precisely
\[
y_{m,j}^{(U)}=-\frac{u_{m,j}^2}{m^2}
\qquad
\left(1\le j\le N(U)\right),
\]
where \(N(U)\) is fixed before \(m\) tends to infinity and each \(u_{m,j}>0\) is the unique zero supplied by Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} near
\[
u_j^{(0)}=\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{\sqrt2},
\]
and
\[
N(U)=\#\left\{j\ge1:\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{\sqrt2}<U\right\}
=\left\lfloor \frac{U}{\pi\sqrt2}+\frac12\right\rfloor .
\]
Each zero in the window is simple, and the ordering is
\[
0>y_{m,1}^{(U)}>y_{m,2}^{(U)}>\cdots>y_{m,N(U)}^{(U)}.
\]
In particular, for all sufficiently large \(m\), the window \([-U^2/m^2,0)\) contains no negative real zeros of \(Z_m\) other than this listed finite set.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-cosine-hypotheses} to the assembled EP2-to-cosine interface of Theorem~\ref{thm:generic-ep2-to-cosine-interface}, with the endpoint constants
\[
y_c=0,
\qquad
a=\frac1{\sqrt2},
\qquad
q_j=\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{2}.
\]
The non-threshold condition on \(U\) is exactly the lemma's condition \(aU\ne q_j\) for every \(j\ge1\), and the lemma gives a threshold \(m_U\) such that the only zeros of
\[
u\longmapsto F_m(u)
\]
in the compact half-rectangle
\[
Q_U=\{u\in\CC:0\le \Re u\le U,\ \abs{\Im u}\le U\}
\]
are the zeros \(u_{m,j}\) with \(q_j/a<U\).  These zeros are obtained in pairwise disjoint Rouch\'e discs centered at the real points \(u_j^{(0)}=q_j/a\).  Each such disc is chosen invariant under complex conjugation, and it contains exactly one zero of \(F_m\), counted with multiplicity.  Since the coefficients of \(Z_m\) are real and the normalizing constants in \(F_m\) are real, Schwarz reflection gives \(F_m(\overline u)=\overline{F_m(u)}\) on the disc.  Therefore the conjugate of the unique zero in the disc is again a zero in the same disc; by uniqueness it is the same zero.  Thus every \(u_{m,j}\) is real.  As \(u_{m,j}=u_j^{(0)}+o(1)\) and \(u_j^{(0)}>0\), these zeros are positive and ordered increasingly for \(m\) large.
Hence each \(u_{m,j}\) is a simple zero of \(F_m\).

The normalizing factor in the definition of \(F_m\),
\[
\frac{e^{b u^2/m}}{2A_*\lambda_c^m},
\]
is nonzero.  Thus the zeros of \(F_m\) in \(Q_U\) are exactly the zeros of
\[
u\longmapsto Z_m\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)
\]
in the same set, with the same multiplicities.  If \(y\in[-U^2/m^2,0)\) is a real zero of \(Z_m\), then the unique positive number \(u=m\sqrt{-y}\) lies in \([0,U]\subset Q_U\) and satisfies
\[
Z_m\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)=0.
\]
The compact-window exclusion therefore forces \(u=u_{m,j}\) for some \(j\) with \(q_j/a<U\).  Conversely each such \(u_{m,j}\) gives the displayed negative real zero.

Since \(u\mapsto -u^2/m^2\) has nonzero derivative at every positive \(u_{m,j}\), simplicity is preserved after passing from the \(u\)-variable to the \(y\)-variable.  The increasing order
\[
0<u_{m,1}<u_{m,2}<\cdots<u_{m,N(U)}
\]
from Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} is reversed by \(u\mapsto -u^2/m^2\), giving the stated order of the negative real zeros.  Finally, the count formula follows from
\[
\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{\sqrt2}<U
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
j<\frac{U}{\pi\sqrt2}+\frac12,
\]
together with the non-threshold assumption, which excludes equality at the right-hand endpoint.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Endpoint zero ordering]\label{def:endpoint-zero-ordering}
Fix \(K\ge1\), and choose any non-threshold \(U\) satisfying
\[
u_K^{(0)}=\frac{(2K-1)\pi}{\sqrt2}<U<\frac{(2K+1)\pi}{\sqrt2}=u_{K+1}^{(0)}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(m\), Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-neighborhood-isolation} gives exactly the first \(K\) simple negative real zeros of \(Z_m\), counted from \(0\) toward the left within the endpoint window \([-U^2/m^2,0)\).  We denote them by
\[
0>y_{m,1}>y_{m,2}>\cdots>y_{m,K},
\]
so \(y_{m,k}\) is the \(k\)th negative real zero nearest \(0\).  This notation is independent of the chosen non-threshold \(U\), because the isolation theorem gives the same ordered zeros on overlapping endpoint windows once both thresholds are large enough.  Equivalently, if \(y_{m,k}=-u_{m,k}^2/m^2\) with \(u_{m,k}>0\), then
\[
0<u_{m,1}<u_{m,2}<\cdots<u_{m,K}.
\]
The phrase ``for each fixed \(k\)'' below means that, after fixing \(k\), the assertion holds for all \(m\ge m_k\); for a finite set \(1\le k\le K\), one may take a common threshold \(m_K\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint coordinate transfer]\label{lem:endpoint-coordinate-transfer}
Fix the endpoint square-root coordinate by writing \(y=s^2\) and, on the negative endpoint scale, by choosing
\[
s=\frac{iu}{m},
\qquad
y=-\frac{u^2}{m^2}.
\]
Let \(K\subset\CC\) be compact.  After the branch-coordinate disc of Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-expansion} has been fixed, the points \(s=iu/m\) lie in that disc for all \(u\in K\) and all sufficiently large \(m\), and the substitution preserves the compact-uniform meaning of every estimate in Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-expansion}.
Moreover, if \(\alpha>0\), \(\gamma\in\RR\), and
\[
u_m=\alpha\left(1+\frac{\gamma}{m}\right)+O(m^{-2})
\]
for one fixed index, then
\[
-\frac{u_m^2}{m^2}
=
-\frac{\alpha^2}{m^2}-\frac{2\gamma\alpha^2}{m^3}+O(m^{-4}).
\]
The same implication is uniform over any fixed finite set of indices for which the corresponding \(O(m^{-2})\) constants in the \(u_m\)-expansions are chosen uniformly.  Finally, on \(u>0\) the map \(u\mapsto -u^2/m^2\) is injective with nonzero derivative and reverses the endpoint order: \(0<u_1<u_2\) implies \(0>-u_1^2/m^2>-u_2^2/m^2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If the branch-coordinate disc has radius \(r>0\), then \(\abs{iu/m}\le \sup_{K}\abs u/m<r\) for all sufficiently large \(m\), so the substitution \(s=iu/m\) remains inside the single labelled square-root chart used by Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-expansion}.  Hence all branch, amplitude, and noncolliding-remainder estimates remain uniform on the fixed compact set \(K\), with constants depending only on \(K\) and the chosen chart.
Write \(u_m=\alpha(1+\gamma/m)+\varepsilon_m\) with \(\varepsilon_m=O(m^{-2})\).  Since \(\alpha\) and \(\gamma\) are fixed,
\[
u_m^2
=
\alpha^2\left(1+\frac{2\gamma}{m}\right)+O(m^{-2}),
\]
and division by \(-m^2\) gives the displayed \(y\)-expansion with an \(O(m^{-4})\) remainder.  If only finitely many indices are involved, taking the maximum of the finitely many constants in \(\varepsilon_m=O(m^{-2})\) gives the same conclusion uniformly over that finite set.  The derivative of \(-u^2/m^2\) is \(-2u/m^2\), which is nonzero for \(u>0\), and the stated ordering is immediate from strict monotonicity on the positive real axis.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-index, fixed-window endpoint zero law (Theorem E)]\label{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}
Use the endpoint square-root coordinate $y=s^2$ at the dominant branch point $y=0$, on the branch-coordinate disc of Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-expansion} after shrinking inside \(|s|<1/2\) if necessary, with labels
\[
\lambda_+(s)=1+\frac{s}{\sqrt2}+\frac58s^2+O(s^3),
\qquad
\lambda_-(s)=\lambda_+(-s),
\]
and continue to the negative endpoint scale by the branch choice
\[
y=-\frac{u^2}{m^2},
\qquad
s=\frac{iu}{m}.
\]
On that same disc the proposition proves \(\lambda_\pm(s)\neq0\), \(C_\pm(s)\neq0\), \(C_-(s)=C_+(-s)\), and, for every fixed compact \(K\subset\CC\), the noncolliding contribution bound \(R_m(-u^2/m^2)=O_K(m^{-2})\) uniformly for \(u\in K\).
The exact Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching} input used here is Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-expansion}: after the same possible shrinking of the fixed branch-coordinate disc inside \(|s|<1/2\),
\[
\lambda_\pm(s)
=
1\pm \frac{s}{\sqrt2}+\frac58s^2\mp\frac{43}{64\sqrt2}s^3+O(s^4),
\qquad
C_\pm(s)=\frac12\mp\frac{3\sqrt2}{16}s+O(s^2),
\]
with the amplitude symmetry \(C_-(s)=C_+(-s)\), nonvanishing of the two branches and amplitudes on that disc, and the fixed-compact remainder estimate
\[
R_m\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)=O_K(m^{-2})
\]
for each compact \(K\subset\CC\) chosen independently of \(m\).
The constants in every subsequent \(O_K\)-term may depend on this fixed compact set, and the constants in every \(O_k\)-term may depend on the fixed index, or on a fixed finite index set after one threshold is chosen.
The constants in the two-branch normal form are fixed throughout this theorem as
\[
a=\frac1{\sqrt2},
\qquad
b=\frac38,
\qquad
A_*=\frac12,
\qquad
B_*=\frac{3\sqrt2}{16},
\]
and the unperturbed positive $u$-zeros are
\[
q_k=\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{2},
\qquad
\nu_k^{(0)}=\frac{q_k}{a}=\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{\sqrt2}
\qquad(k\ge1).
\]
For every fixed compact set $K\subset\CC$ chosen independently of $m$, uniformly for $u\in K$ as $m\to\infty$, Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-cosine-hypotheses} and Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-scaling}
Z_m\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)
=
\exp\!\left(-\frac{3u^2}{8m}\right)
\left[
\cos\!\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{3u}{4\sqrt{2}\,m}\right)
+ O_K(m^{-2})
\right].
\end{equation}
Here the symbol \(O_K\) refers exactly to the fixed-compact constants supplied by Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-expansion} through Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-cosine-hypotheses}; no moving compact set or growing endpoint window is included in this notation.
If $U>0$ is non-threshold, meaning
\[
U\ne \nu_j^{(0)}=\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{\sqrt2}
\qquad (j\ge1),
\]
and
\[
N(U)=\#\left\{j\ge1:\nu_j^{(0)}=\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{\sqrt2}<U\right\}
=\left\lfloor \frac{U}{\pi\sqrt2}+\frac12\right\rfloor,
\]
then there exists $m_U$ such that, for every $m\ge m_U$, the whole punctured endpoint window
\[
-\frac{U^2}{m^2}\le y<0
\]
contains exactly $N(U)$ negative real zeros of $Z_m$, all simple, ordered as
\[
0>y_{m,1}^{(U)}>\cdots>y_{m,N(U)}^{(U)}
\qquad\text{when }N(U)>0,
\]
and contains no other negative real zero.
The order convention in this theorem is intrinsic: the zeros are counted from \(0\) leftwards, so $y_{m,1}^{(U)}$ is nearest to \(0\), and if $y_{m,k}^{(U)}=-u_{m,k}^2/m^2$ with $u_{m,k}>0$, then
\[
0<u_{m,1}<u_{m,2}<\cdots<u_{m,N(U)}.
\]
For every fixed $1\le k\le N(U)$ these window zeros agree with the fixed-index endpoint zeros of Definition~\ref{def:endpoint-zero-ordering} and satisfy the expansion below, after increasing the same threshold for the finite set of indices in the window if necessary.
In this order convention, for each fixed $k\ge 1$ the $k$th negative real zero counted from $y=0$ leftwards inside any fixed endpoint window containing that index satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-zero-quantization}
y_{m,k}
=
-\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}
-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}
+ O_k(m^{-4}).
\end{equation}
The \(O_k\)-term is obtained by applying the same compact-uniform estimate from Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-expansion} on one fixed Rouch\'e disc around \(\nu_k^{(0)}\), then transporting it through the fixed analytic change of variables \(y=-u^2/m^2\); for a finite index set, the finite union of such discs is fixed before taking one common threshold and constant.
All quantifiers in this theorem are fixed-scale: $K$, $U$, and $k$ or any finite index set are chosen before $m\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-cosine-hypotheses} supplies the explicit hypotheses for Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law}, and it points to the model-specific algebra in Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching}.
In particular, Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-expansion} works on the uniform disc $D_r$ introduced there and proves that on the local scale \(y=s^2\) the maximal-modulus pair satisfies the square-root branching predicted by Lemma~\ref{lem:generic-ep2-branching}:
\[
\lambda_\pm(s)=1\pm \frac{1}{\sqrt2}s+\frac58 s^2+O(s^3),
\qquad
C_\pm(s)=\frac12\mp \frac{3\sqrt2}{16}s+O(s^2),
\]
and that the remaining two branches contribute only \(O_K(m^{-2})\) on compact complex sets \(K\) under the scaling \(y=-u^2/m^2\).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint-coordinate-transfer}, this substitution uses the single branch choice \(s=iu/m\) on every fixed compact \(u\)-set for all sufficiently large \(m\), so no additional square-root choice is made in the Rouch\'e step.
Thus the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} hold with the stated constants and \(\lambda_c=1\).
Applying \eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-scaling} gives \eqref{eq:endpoint-scaling}, and applying \eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-y} on discs around the points \(u_k^{(0)}=\pi(2k-1)/\sqrt2\) gives \eqref{eq:endpoint-zero-quantization}.
Equivalently, \eqref{eq:fixed-window-ep2-u} gives
\[
u_{m,k}=\frac{q_k}{a}\left(1+\frac{B_*}{aA_*m}\right)+O_k(m^{-2}),
\]
and Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint-coordinate-transfer}, with \(a=1/\sqrt2\), \(A_*=1/2\), \(B_*=3\sqrt2/16\), and \(q_k=(2k-1)\pi/2\), converts this exactly into
\[
y_{m,k}
=
-\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}
-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4}).
\]
The dependence of the constants is the one in Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law}: the compact-scaling constant is the constant for the chosen compact $K$, while the zero constant for a fixed $k$ is obtained on one conjugation-invariant Rouch\'e disc around $u_k^{(0)}$.
For a fixed finite set $1\le k\le K_0$, chosen independently of $m$, choose these discs pairwise disjoint and take the maximum of their error constants and thresholds; this gives uniformity over that finite set and nothing beyond it.
The same lemma applies with conjugation-invariant real-centered discs, because \(Z_m\in\ZZ[y]\) by Section~\ref{sec:model}.  In each such Rouch\'e disc the zero is unique; Schwarz reflection sends it to its conjugate, which is again a zero in the same disc, so uniqueness forces equality with its conjugate.  Hence the extracted \(u\)-zeros are real, positive, and ordered for each fixed finite set of indices once \(m\) is large.
Their images \(y_{m,k}=-u_{m,k}^2/m^2\) are therefore the ordered negative real zeros counted from the endpoint within the fixed finite endpoint window under consideration.
It remains only to identify this fixed finite list with all zeros in an arbitrary non-threshold endpoint window.
For such a fixed $U$, Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-neighborhood-isolation} applies the same oscillation lemma on the compact rectangle $Q_U$ and gives a threshold $m_U$ for which the only zeros of $u\mapsto Z_m(-u^2/m^2)$ in that rectangle are the Rouch\'e zeros near the centres $u_j^{(0)}<U$.
The positive real change of variables $u=m\sqrt{-y}$ is a bijection from the real interval $[-U^2/m^2,0)$ to $0<u\le U$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint-coordinate-transfer} shows that the map $u\mapsto -u^2/m^2$ has nonzero derivative at every listed centre and at every nearby zero for all sufficiently large $m$ and reverses the endpoint ordering in exactly the stated way.
Thus simplicity, ordering, and exclusion of all other real zeros in the whole fixed window are exactly the conclusions of Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-neighborhood-isolation} transferred back to the $y$-line.
Since $N(U)$ is finite, the fixed-index expansion already proved above applies simultaneously to every index $1\le k\le N(U)$ after taking the maximum of the finitely many thresholds and constants.
The non-threshold assumption is used only to keep the right endpoint of the compact window away from a limiting cosine zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-scale consequences of Theorem E]\label{cor:fixed-scale-endpoint-consequences}
The endpoint-zero consequences of Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization} used elsewhere in the paper consist exactly of the following fixed-scale assertions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For each fixed compact set \(K\subset\CC\) in the scaling variable \(u\), the two-branch approximation \eqref{eq:endpoint-scaling} holds with constants depending on \(K\).
\item For each fixed non-threshold \(U>0\), the punctured window \([-U^2/m^2,0)\) contains precisely the finite ordered set of simple negative zeros listed in Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}, for all sufficiently large \(m\) depending on \(U\).
\item For each fixed finite index set \(1\le k\le K_0\), the expansion \eqref{eq:endpoint-zero-quantization} holds simultaneously for those indices after increasing one threshold and one set of constants depending on \(K_0\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently any later use first fixes the compact \(u\)-window, the non-threshold endpoint window, or the finite index set independently of \(m\).
The statement is therefore tied to endpoint windows and finite index sets fixed before the large-$m$ limit.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the compact-uniform statement \eqref{eq:endpoint-scaling} in Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}; its proof invokes Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law} only after a compact set \(K\) has been chosen, so the implied constants depend on that fixed set.
For the second assertion, fix a non-threshold \(U\) and use the compact rectangle
\[
Q_U=\{u\in\CC:0\le \Re u\le U,\ \abs{\Im u}\le U\}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-neighborhood-isolation}, as used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}, gives a threshold \(m_U\) such that the zeros of \(u\mapsto Z_m(-u^2/m^2)\) in this rectangle are exactly the Rouch\'e zeros near the finitely many centres \(\nu_j^{(0)}<U\).
The positive real change of variables \(u=m\sqrt{-y}\), equivalently \(y=-u^2/m^2\), identifies the real interval \([-U^2/m^2,0)\) with \(0<u\le U\) and has nonzero derivative in that range; hence those and only those positive real \(u\)-zeros give the listed simple negative real zeros in the stated window.
For the third assertion, after \(K_0\) is fixed, choose pairwise disjoint Rouch\'e discs around the finitely many centres \(\nu_k^{(0)}\) with \(1\le k\le K_0\), and take the maximum of the finitely many thresholds and constants obtained in Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}.
Each step fixes \(K\), \(U\), or \(K_0\) before passing to large \(m\), which is the fixed-scale convention used throughout the endpoint argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Scope-complete fixed-member theorem chain]\label{cor:fixed-member-theorem-chain}
The main conclusions used by the endpoint zero theorem form the following fixed-member chain, and no stronger parameter-space or moving-window assertion is used.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For a smooth real balanced quartic fixed in advance, Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced-real-projection} and Remark~\ref{rem:balanced-fixed-criterion-complete} give a necessary and sufficient finite algebraic criterion for the real \(y\)-image to be a single interval, two adjacent intervals, or two disjoint intervals.
For the canonical member \((a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)\), Corollary~\ref{cor:quartic-real-projection-framework} and Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-b-real-projection-criterion} evaluate this criterion as
\[
y(E_{\mathrm c}(\RR))=[\ySub,1],
\qquad
y(E_{\mathrm n}(\RR))=[0,\infty).
\]
\item If a balanced member has the normalized double-root data of Definition~\ref{def:normalized-collision-data}, then Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-specialization-up-to-normalization} first gives only the algebraic forcing
\[
P_{a,c,d,g}(\rho\widetilde\lambda,u+\rho^2\widetilde y)
=
\rho^4P_{-1,-1,1,0}(\widetilde\lambda,\widetilde y).
\]
The dominance statement for the positive-side collision is then supplied separately by Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance} and transported by Lemma~\ref{lem:real-weighted-pullback-dominance} and Corollary~\ref{cor:canonical-dominance-after-normalization}.
Thus a phrase such as ``dominant collision forces the specialization'' means precisely the conjunction of this algebraic forcing with those separate dominance verifications.
\item The endpoint-zero assertions following from that dominant canonical endpoint are exactly the fixed-scale assertions of Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}: first a compact set \(K\) in the variable \(u\), a non-threshold window \([-U^2/m^2,0)\), or a finite index set \(1\le k\le K_0\) is fixed, and only then is \(m\) taken large.
Consequently the theorem chain proves the listed simple negative zeros and the expansion
\[
y_{m,k}
=
-\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}
-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4})
\]
for fixed \(k\), uniformly over any fixed finite set of such indices, but it does not assert a moving-window or global zero distribution.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is only a re-indexing of the fixed-member real-projection results already proved in Section~\ref{sec:genus-one}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced-real-projection} lists every endpoint and finite critical value of one fixed real map \(y:E_{a,c,d,g}(\RR)\to\RR\); Remark~\ref{rem:balanced-fixed-criterion-complete} proves that this finite tagged list is complete for interval connectedness and real-fibre transitions.
Substituting the single canonical quadruple and carrying out the resulting algebraic comparisons is exactly Corollary~\ref{cor:quartic-real-projection-framework}, and Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-b-real-projection-criterion} records the two displayed oval images.

For the second assertion, Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-specialization-up-to-normalization} proves the displayed identity after the real weighted affine change and explicitly excludes dominance from its hypotheses.
Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance} proves the canonical strict maximal-modulus statement on \(\widetilde y>0\), Lemma~\ref{lem:real-weighted-pullback-dominance} proves that multiplication of all roots by the common real factor \(\rho\) preserves and reflects strict modulus inequalities, and Corollary~\ref{cor:canonical-dominance-after-normalization} combines these two inputs.
Hence algebraic forcing and dominance verification are logically separate and together give the stated interpretation.

For the third assertion, Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed-scale-endpoint-consequences} is precisely the fixed-scale unpacking of Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}.
Its proof fixes the compact set, window, or finite index set before applying the compact-uniform two-branch estimate and Rouch\'e isolation, and the constants and thresholds are allowed to depend on that prior fixed choice.
The displayed expansion is the fixed-index expansion of Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}; the finite-index uniformity is obtained only by taking a maximum over finitely many fixed Rouch\'e discs.
Therefore the theorem chain gives exactly the advertised fixed-member and fixed-window conclusions and no global chamber or moving-window conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed compact endpoint-window counting law]\label{cor:edge-zero-counting}
Define
\[
N_m(\xi)
:=
\#\left\{
y\in\RR:
Z_m(y)=0,
\quad
-\frac{\xi}{m^2}\le y<0
\right\},
\qquad
\text{for a fixed }\xi>0.
\]
If $\xi$ does not belong to the threshold set
\[
\left\{
\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2}:k\ge1
\right\},
\]
then for all sufficiently large $m$ one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge-counting-law}
N_m(\xi)
=
\left\lfloor
\frac{\sqrt{\xi}}{\pi\sqrt2}+\frac12
\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
The threshold in this count depends only on the fixed value of \(\xi\), equivalently on the fixed compact endpoint window \([-\xi/m^2,0)\), and on the corresponding finite set of endpoint indices.
Consequently the corollary is a fixed-window counting statement: the parameter \(\xi\) and the corresponding finite set of endpoint indices are chosen before the large-$m$ limit.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Put \(U=\sqrt{\xi}\).
The non-threshold assumption on \(\xi\) is equivalent to
\[
U\neq \frac{q_k}{a}
\qquad(k\ge1)
\]
for the endpoint constants \(a=1/\sqrt2\) and \(q_k=(2k-1)\pi/2\), because
\[
\left(\frac{q_k}{a}\right)^2=\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2}.
\]
By the compact-window exclusion in Lemma~\ref{lem:two-branch-oscillation-law}, all zeros of
\[
u\longmapsto Z_m\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)
\]
with \(0\le \Re u\le U\) and \(\abs{\Im u}\le U\) are, for all sufficiently large \(m\), exactly the real positive zeros \(u_{m,k}\) with \(q_k/a<U\).
The real change of variables \(y=-u^2/m^2\) maps these and only these positive \(u\)-zeros to the interval \([-\xi/m^2,0)\).
Thus no additional real zeros of \(Z_m\) occur in this endpoint window, and the set counted by \(N_m(\xi)\) is exactly
\[
\left\{
k\ge1:
\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2}<\xi
\right\},
\]
which yields \eqref{eq:edge-counting-law}.
All compactness constants and the threshold for Rouch\'e isolation were chosen after fixing \(U=\sqrt\xi\).  The proof therefore gives the stated count for that fixed window and for the finite set of indices whose limiting centres lie inside it.
\end{proof}

The fixed-window count above is the only zero-counting consequence used in the paper.
It should not be read as a moving-window or global density statement.
