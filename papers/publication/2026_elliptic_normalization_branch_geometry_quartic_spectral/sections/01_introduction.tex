\section{Introduction}

We study the polynomial family $\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}$ defined by the rational generating function
\[
Z(t,y)=\sum_{m\ge 0} Z_m(y)t^m
=
\frac{1+yt+(y^2-y-1)t^2+(y^3-2y)t^3}
{1-t-(2y+1)t^2+t^3+y(y+1)t^4}.
\]
Equivalently, $\{Z_m(y)\}$ is generated by an order-$4$ companion transfer matrix with characteristic polynomial
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1).
\]
The paper is organized as a complete exact analysis of this quartic spectral family.
We regard \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\) as a fixed algebraic-spectral object and ask for an exact description of its elliptic normalization, its real branch geometry, its positive-real maximal-modulus branch, and the fixed-index zeros of \(Z_m\) in fixed compact windows at the maximal-modulus square-root endpoint \(y=0\).

Section~\ref{sec:genus-one} places \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\) inside the balanced family
\[
P_{a,c,d,g}(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4+a\lambda^3+(c-2y)\lambda^2+d\lambda+y(y-c)+g,
\]
for which the substitution \(\xi=y-\lambda^2\) removes the quartic obstruction and produces a Weierstrass model.
Within that family, the normalized double-root statement shows that fixing the neighbouring colliding points at $(\lambda,y)=(1,0)$ and $(\lambda,y)=(-1,1)$ forces the balanced parameters to be \((a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)\).
For this specialization, the projective closure of \(\Pi(\lambda,y)=0\) is normalized by the elliptic curve
\[
Y^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1.
\]
This turns the fixed canonical problem into a degree-$4$ genus-one covering of the \(y\)-line; the surrounding balanced-family skeleton is used only as a finite fixed-member projection test, not as a parameter-space chamber classification.
The endpoint theorem is then proved by combining the real branch classification, local Puiseux expansions, and compact-uniform two-branch estimates; the endpoint expansion appendix supplies part of this proof.

\subsection{Relation to existing literature}

The ingredients used in the paper are classical.
The Yang--Lee papers and Fisher's edge-singularity paper provide the historical background for zero distributions in parameter-dependent polynomial families \cite{YangLee1952I,LeeYang1952II,Fisher1978}, while modern Beraha--Kahane--Weiss formulations organize limiting zero sets through dominant equimodularity \cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1975,BerahaKahaneWeiss1978,Sokal2004,BrownOtto2025}.
Companion matrices and cyclicity are standard \cite{HornJohnson2012}; rational generating functions, linear recurrences, and finite Hankel rank belong to the general theory of rational sequences \cite{FlajoletSedgewick2009,Stanley2012,Partington1989}; and the reduction to Weierstrass form, the Riemann--Hurwitz count, and local Puiseux expansions are standard algebraic-curve tools \cite{Walker1950,Silverman2009,Miranda1995,BrieskornKnorrer1986}.

Within the recurrence-generated zero literature, the closest references are Tran's discriminant approach \cite{Tran2014,Tran2015}, the four-term work of de Bruin, Forg\'acs--Tran, Tran--Zumba, Adams, and Botta--Fornazieri \cite{deBruin1984,ForgacsTran2016,ForgacsTran2017,ForgacsTran2020,TranZumba2018,TranZumba2021,Adams2020,BottaFornazieri2023}, and the exact-support and Tran-conjecture papers of Ndikubwayo and of B{\o}gvad--Ndikubwayo--Shapiro \cite{Ndikubwayo2020,BogvadNdikubwayoShapiro2020}.
Related geometric viewpoints also appear in Goh--He--Ricci and in Wang--Zhang \cite{GohHeRicci2009,WangZhang2021}, while Ndikubwayo's later Toeplitz-based work treats a different recurrence regime \cite{Ndikubwayo2022}.

\subsubsection*{Contribution and scope}

The contribution here is the exact integration of several classical tools for one concrete quartic.
The paper carries the quartic from its rational generating function through elliptic normalization, branch locus, real oval projection, maximal-modulus separation, endpoint two-branch oscillation, and degree growth without numerical fitting or unproved genericity assumptions.
This manuscript is deliberately confined to this quartic spectral curve and its elliptic normalization.
It does not duplicate the sibling finite-fold, Sturmian, DFA-prime, or BEDC theoremization manuscripts: those projects concern different ambient objects and different invariants, whereas the present paper uses only the rational recurrence, the quartic spectral polynomial, the balanced genus-one reduction, and the resulting branch geometry stated below.
For real $y>0$ we write
\[
r(y):=\max_{1\le j\le 4}\abs{\lambda_j(y)},
\qquad
f_{\mathrm{dom}}(y):=\log r(y).
\]
The maximal-modulus branch over $y>0$ is the connected component of the smooth spectral curve on which $\abs{\lambda}=r(y)$.
The problem is thus to determine this branch and the corresponding zero geometry explicitly for \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\).

\begin{proposition}[Scope separation for the quartic spectral chain]\label{prop:quartic-scope-separation}
Every theorem in this paper is a consequence of the following quartic data and their algebraic transforms:
the rational generating function for \(Z_m(y)\), the order-four recurrence with spectral polynomial \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\), the balanced family \(P_{a,c,d,g}\), the specialization \((a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)\), and the elliptic normalization
\[
Y^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1.
\]
In particular, no result below uses, implies, or re-proves a finite-fold invariant, a Sturmian coding theorem, a DFA-prime automaton statement, or a BEDC theoremization statement except through ordinary background facts explicitly cited in the text.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:hankel} derive the recurrence, the transfer matrix, and the minimal order directly from the displayed rational generating function and from the characteristic equation \(\Pi(\lambda,y)=0\).
Section~\ref{sec:genus-one} introduces the balanced family \(P_{a,c,d,g}\), performs the change of variables \(\xi=y-\lambda^2\), \(Y=2\xi-c\), and specializes the resulting Weierstrass model to \((a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)\).
Sections~\ref{sec:ep-classification} and~\ref{sec:positive-maximal-modulus-branch} use only that normalization, the finite branch values, real-root counts, and interval certificates for \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) to determine the real projection and the positive maximal-modulus branch.
Sections~\ref{sec:support-law} and~\ref{sec:boundary-zeros} return to the same recurrence and generating function to prove the degree-growth and boundary-divisibility identities.
Thus each theorem in the chain is obtained from the listed quartic inputs by algebraic elimination, real-root separation, branch comparison, or recurrence extraction.

The four excluded sibling themes require additional primitive objects not introduced in this manuscript: finite-fold inverse or folding data, Sturmian symbolic codings, deterministic finite automata and primality recognizers, or BEDC-specific structures.
Since no definition, hypothesis, or proof below invokes such an object, none of the present arguments can be read as proving one of those sibling results.
Conversely, the statements proved here include the explicit Weierstrass model, branch polynomial, real oval image, positive-axis maximal-modulus separation, endpoint zero law, and recurrence-side degree and boundary identities for this particular \(\Pi\); these are not assertions about those sibling structures.
This proves the claimed separation of scope.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Theorem overview.}
The paper proves five named results which form a single theorem chain.
Theorem~A is the balanced elliptic-normalization theorem: the family \(P_{a,c,d,g}\) is reduced by \(\xi=y-\lambda^2\), \(Y=2\xi-c\), to
\[
Y^2=c^2-4a\lambda^3-4d\lambda-4g.
\]
For the selected spectral quartic this gives the model \(Y^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1\), the degree-four map to the \(y\)-line, and the finite branch polynomial.
Theorem~B applies the finite real-projection skeleton for a fixed smooth balanced member to the canonical specialization and obtains the two real oval images
\[
[\ySub,1]
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
[0,\infty),
\]
together with the exact real-root count of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) on every real interval.
Theorem~C proves the normalized double-root selection: the collisions at $(\lambda,y)=(1,0)$ and $(-1,1)$ force \((a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)\), and the affine normalization formula transports the statement to the corresponding two-point collision data only under the stated real nonzero weighted scaling.
Theorem~D combines the discriminant factorization, finite branch values, real-root counts, and positive-axis interval certificates to identify the unique maximal-modulus branch on \(y>0\); in particular the endpoint at \(y=0\) is the controlling square-root collision for that branch, while the other real branch collisions are subdominant.
Theorem~E converts this branch information into endpoint zeros in the fixed-index, fixed-window regime: for each fixed \(k\ge1\), if the negative zeros in a fixed compact endpoint window are ordered from \(0\) leftwards, then
\[
y_{m,k}
=
-\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}
-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4}).
\]
Equivalently, on every fixed compact endpoint window \(y=-u^2/m^2\) with the window fixed independently of \(m\), the theorem isolates exactly the corresponding finite set of simple zeros for all sufficiently large \(m\); it is not a statement about growing indices or expanding endpoint windows.

The recurrence-side results support this theorem chain.
Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-order} establishes the generic order-four minimal spectral closure used throughout.
Theorem~\ref{thm:support-law} proves
\[
\deg_y Z_m=\left\lfloor\frac{m+3}{2}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
[y^{\deg Z_{2n+1}}]Z_{2n+1}=n,
\qquad
[y^{\deg Z_{2n}}]Z_{2n}=\frac{n(n^2+5)}6,
\]
and Proposition~\ref{prop:double-zero} proves the uniform divisibility \((y+1)^2\mid Z_m(y)\) for \(m\ge2\).
These algebraic consequences are proved from the recurrence and generating function after the spectral family has been fixed.

\paragraph{Roadmap.}
Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:hankel} define the family and prove the minimal recurrence closure.
Section~\ref{sec:genus-one} contains the balanced reduction, the fixed-member real-projection skeleton, the canonical double-root normalization, and the elliptic branch computation.
Section~\ref{sec:ep-classification} classifies the real branch values, and Section~\ref{sec:positive-maximal-modulus-branch} proves the positive-axis dominance theorem and endpoint zero law.
Sections~\ref{sec:support-law} and~\ref{sec:boundary-zeros} record the supporting degree and boundary-zero identities.
The appendices give the displayed elimination, degeneracy, endpoint-expansion, computational-verification, and projective-infinity computations used by or checking the proofs.
