\section{Introduction}

We study the polynomial family $\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}$ defined by the rational generating function
\[
Z(t,y)=\sum_{m\ge 0} Z_m(y)t^m
=
\frac{1+yt+(y^2-y-1)t^2+(y^3-2y)t^3}
{1-t-(2y+1)t^2+t^3+y(y+1)t^4}.
\]
Equivalently, $\{Z_m(y)\}$ is generated by an order-$4$ companion transfer matrix with characteristic polynomial
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1).
\]
The paper is organized as a complete exact analysis of one quartic spectral family.
We regard \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\) as a fixed algebraic-spectral object and ask for an exact description of its elliptic normalization, its real branch geometry, its positive-real maximal-modulus branch, and the zeros of \(Z_m\) nearest the maximal-modulus square-root endpoint at \(y=0\).

Section~\ref{sec:genus-one} places \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\) inside the balanced family
\[
P_{a,c,d,g}(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4+a\lambda^3+(c-2y)\lambda^2+d\lambda+y(y-c)+g,
\]
for which the substitution \(\xi=y-\lambda^2\) removes the quartic obstruction and produces a Weierstrass model.
Within that family we use only a normalized double-root statement: Lemma~\ref{lem:balanced-classification} shows that, once the two neighbouring colliding points are fixed at $(\lambda,y)=(1,0)$ and $(\lambda,y)=(-1,1)$, the balanced parameters are forced to be \((a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)\).

For the specialization \((a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)\), the projective closure of \(\Pi(\lambda,y)=0\) is normalized by the elliptic curve
\[
Y^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1.
\]
This turns the problem into a degree-$4$ genus-one covering of the \(y\)-line.
As a result one can compute the finite branch values exactly, determine the real-spectral phase diagram, identify the maximal-modulus branch on \(y>0\), and derive explicit endpoint zero asymptotics near \(y=0\).
The endpoint theorem is proved by combining the localized two-branch oscillation lemma in Section~\ref{sec:positive-maximal-modulus-branch} with the algebraic expansion package in Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching}; the appendix is part of the proof, not a numerical supplement.

\subsection{Relation to existing literature}

The ingredients used in the paper are classical.
The Yang--Lee papers and Fisher's edge-singularity paper provide the historical background for zero distributions in parameter-dependent polynomial families \cite{YangLee1952I,LeeYang1952II,Fisher1978}, while modern Beraha--Kahane--Weiss formulations organize limiting zero sets through dominant equimodularity \cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1975,BerahaKahaneWeiss1978,Sokal2004,BrownOtto2025}.
Companion matrices and cyclicity are standard \cite{HornJohnson2012}; rational generating functions, linear recurrences, and finite Hankel rank belong to the general theory of rational sequences \cite{FlajoletSedgewick2009,Stanley2012,Partington1989}; and the reduction to Weierstrass form, the Riemann--Hurwitz count, and local Puiseux expansions are standard algebraic-curve tools \cite{Walker1950,Silverman2009,Miranda1995,BrieskornKnorrer1986}.

Within the recurrence-generated zero literature, the closest references are Tran's discriminant approach \cite{Tran2014,Tran2015}, the four-term work of de Bruin, Forg\'acs--Tran, Tran--Zumba, Adams, and Botta--Fornazieri \cite{deBruin1984,ForgacsTran2016,ForgacsTran2017,ForgacsTran2020,TranZumba2018,TranZumba2021,Adams2020,BottaFornazieri2023}, and the exact-support and Tran-conjecture papers of Ndikubwayo and of B{\o}gvad--Ndikubwayo--Shapiro \cite{Ndikubwayo2020,BogvadNdikubwayoShapiro2020}.
Related geometric viewpoints also appear in Goh--He--Ricci and in Wang--Zhang \cite{GohHeRicci2009,WangZhang2021}, while Ndikubwayo's later Toeplitz-based work treats a different recurrence regime \cite{Ndikubwayo2022}.

\subsubsection*{Contribution and scope}

The contribution here is the exact integration of several classical tools for one concrete quartic.
The paper carries the quartic from its rational generating function through its elliptic normalization, branch locus, real oval projection, maximal-modulus separation, endpoint two-branch oscillation law, and degree-growth law without numerical fitting or unproved genericity assumptions.
Section~\ref{sec:genus-one} records the balanced-family algebra needed for this specialization: a uniform Weierstrass reduction, a finite real-projection criterion for a fixed smooth member, and the normalized double-root selection that identifies the quartic \(\Pi\).
These auxiliary results keep the subsequent phase diagram algebraic: the real images of the two ovals are obtained by isolating finitely many endpoint and ramification values, and the canonical specialization is then read off from two prescribed neighbouring collisions.
The article therefore treats one exact degree-$4$ spectral cover of the \(y\)-line, rather than a general moduli problem for balanced quartics.

For real $y>0$ we write
\[
r(y):=\max_{1\le j\le 4}\abs{\lambda_j(y)},
\qquad
f_{\mathrm{dom}}(y):=\log r(y).
\]
The maximal-modulus branch over $y>0$ is the connected component of the smooth spectral curve on which $\abs{\lambda}=r(y)$.
The problem is thus to determine this branch and the corresponding zero geometry explicitly for the fixed quartic \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\).

\paragraph{Main results.}
The paper proves five results which form a single exact analysis of the quartic spectral family.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Theorem A: balanced elliptic normalization.}
The balanced family
\[
P_{a,c,d,g}(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4+a\lambda^3+(c-2y)\lambda^2+d\lambda+y(y-c)+g
\]
is reduced by \(\xi=y-\lambda^2\), \(Y=2\xi-c\), to the Weierstrass model
\[
Y^2=c^2-4a\lambda^3-4d\lambda-4g.
\]
The corresponding body result is Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-a-balanced-elliptic-normalization}, specialized to \(\Pi\) in Theorem~\ref{thm:elliptic-reduction} and Corollary~\ref{cor:canonical-theorem-a-package}.

\item \textbf{Theorem B: balanced real-projection skeleton and canonical image.}
For a fixed smooth real balanced member, Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced-real-projection} gives a finite componentwise criterion for the real \(y\)-image using the real cubic endpoints, the ramification polynomial \(R_{a,c,d,g}\), and the exceptional \(\lambda=0\) fibre in \eqref{eq:balanced-real-extrema-set}; it is not a chamber decomposition of the full parameter space.
Proposition~\ref{prop:balanced-finite-comparison-algorithm} reduces the disjoint, adjacent, and overlapping alternatives for one fixed member to finitely many comparisons of explicitly listed real algebraic numbers.
For the canonical specialization, Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-b-real-projection-package} packages Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced-real-projection}, Proposition~\ref{prop:quartic-real-projection-skeleton}, and Corollary~\ref{cor:real-phase-diagram} into the two component images \([\ySub,1]\) and \([0,\infty)\), together with the exact component-by-component real-root count.

\item \textbf{Theorem C: algebraic normalized double-root specialization.}
The normalized collision data of Definition~\ref{def:normalized-collision-data} are equivalent to the displayed algebraic double-root constraints, and these constraints fix both neighbouring normalized collisions and force \((a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)\).
More generally, after the shift \(y=u+\rho^2\widetilde y\) and dilation \(\lambda=\rho\widetilde\lambda\), the parameters are exactly
\[
(a,c,d,g)=(-\rho,\,2u-\rho^2,\,\rho^3,\,u(u-\rho^2)),
\]
as proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-specialization-up-to-normalization} from Lemma~\ref{lem:balanced-affine} and Lemma~\ref{lem:balanced-classification}.

\item \textbf{Theorem D: branch and dominance classification.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:consolidated-branch-dominance-package} gives the discriminant factorization, the exact finite branch values \(0\), \(1\), \(\ySub\), \(\eta\), and \(\overline\eta\), the real-root counts on the four real intervals, and the positive-real spectral-radius branch for every \(y>0\).
It also records that \(y=1\) and \(\ySub\) are subdominant for the maximal-modulus envelope.
Corollary~\ref{cor:transported-normalized-branch-package} then transports this classification to any balanced member already satisfying the normalized collision data of Definition~\ref{def:normalized-collision-data}; the transport is a normalized consequence of Theorem~C and is not a separate classification of arbitrary dominant square-root collisions.

\item \textbf{Theorem E: endpoint zero law.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization} proves that at the maximal-modulus square-root endpoint \((\lambda,y)=(1,0)\), for each fixed \(k\ge1\) the corresponding endpoint zero satisfies
\[
y_{m,k}
=
-\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}
-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4}).
\]
Equivalently, every fixed compact edge-scaling window \(y=-u^2/m^2\) contains exactly the corresponding finite set of endpoint zeros for all sufficiently large \(m\).
\end{enumerate}

The recurrence-side minimality theorem justifies the order-four spectral closure used throughout, while the degree law and the boundary zero at \(y=-1\) remain supporting algebraic consequences of the same recurrence.
In outline, the balanced reduction gives the elliptic model; the finite projection criterion identifies its real oval images; the normalized collision theorem selects the canonical quartic; the branch and dominance classification isolates the positive maximal-modulus endpoint; and the local two-branch expansion gives the fixed-index zero law.

\begin{corollary}[Supporting algebraic consequences of the recurrence]\label{cor:supporting-algebraic-consequences}
For the same rational family, the recurrence polynomials have the following algebraic boundary data.
For every \(m\ge2\),
\[
\deg_y Z_m=\left\lfloor\frac{m+3}{2}\right\rfloor,
\]
with leading coefficients
\[
[y^{\deg Z_{2n+1}}]Z_{2n+1}=n\quad(n\ge1),
\qquad
[y^{\deg Z_{2n}}]Z_{2n}=\frac{n(n^2+5)}6\quad(n\ge1).
\]
Moreover \((y+1)^2\mid Z_m(y)\) for every \(m\ge2\).
These consequences are not additional branch-classification hypotheses: they follow from the recurrence once the spectral family has been fixed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The degree formula and the two displayed leading-coefficient identities are exactly Theorem~\ref{thm:support-law}.
The divisibility statement is Proposition~\ref{prop:double-zero}.
Both results are proved by identities in the recurrence and generating function, and neither is used to add a branch value, a normalization constraint, or a new endpoint to Theorems A--E.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Summary of results}

For this quartic family we obtain the following.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Balanced-family reduction, real-projection description, and double-root normalization} (Section~\ref{sec:genus-one}).
We identify a four-parameter balanced family \(P_{a,c,d,g}\) only as the algebraic scaffold needed to convert the specialized quartic curve to a Weierstrass model by the substitution \(\xi=y-\lambda^2\).
For a fixed smooth real balanced member, Theorem~\ref{thm:balanced-real-projection} gives the finite componentwise projection criterion used to compute the real \(y\)-images of this specialization; no complete moduli classification of balanced quartics is asserted.
We then prove the normalized double-root statement of Lemma~\ref{lem:balanced-classification}: if the balanced polynomial has double roots at $(\lambda,y)=(1,0)$ and $(\lambda,y)=(-1,1)$, then \((a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)\).
For the present quartic this yields the elliptic model with invariants \(g_2=4\), \(g_3=-1\), and \(\Delta=37\), together with the finite branch values \(y=0\), \(y=1\), and the three roots of \(256y^3+411y^2+165y+32\).

\item \textbf{Real branch-point classification and real-root counts} (Section~\ref{sec:ep-classification}).
Among the three real branch values, $y=0$ is the only one whose outgoing pair contains the maximal-modulus root for $y>0$ small; $y=1$ and the negative branch value \(y_{\mathrm{sub}}\) are non-dominant.
The real locus of the elliptic curve has component images
\[
[y_{\mathrm{sub}},1]
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
[0,\infty),
\]
so the total real image is \( [y_{\mathrm{sub}},\infty) \) with overlap on \([0,1]\).
This yields the exact count of real roots of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) for every real \(y\).

\item \textbf{Square-root branching and endpoint quantization} (Section~\ref{sec:positive-maximal-modulus-branch}).
We verify the local square-root branching, amplitude symmetry, and complex-uniform remainder bounds needed for the localized two-branch oscillation law at \(y=0\).
Consequently, for each fixed \(k\ge 1\), after fixing \(k\) and taking \(m\) sufficiently large, the \(k\)th negative zero nearest \(y=0\) satisfies
\[
y_{m,k}
=
-\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}
-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4}).
\]

\item \textbf{Supporting algebraic consequences and a supplementary large-$y$ check} (Section~\ref{sec:support-law} and Appendix~\ref{app:projective-infinity}).
On the polynomial side, \(\deg_y Z_m=\lfloor(m+3)/2\rfloor\), and the top-degree coefficients are explicit and subexponential in \(m\).
At the opposite end of the positive real axis, the large-$y$ maximal-modulus root has a quarter-power splitting governed by a double tangency at projective infinity; this calculation is relegated to a supplementary appendix because it is not used in the local-to-global endpoint zero theorem.

\item \textbf{A rigid boundary value at \(y=-1\)} (Section~\ref{sec:boundary-zeros}).
For every \(m\ge 2\), the polynomial \(Z_m(y)\) has a zero of multiplicity at least two at \(y=-1\).
This gives a uniform algebraic boundary point for the whole sequence.
\end{enumerate}

Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:hankel} define the family and establish the generic order-$4$ minimality of the recurrence.
Sections~\ref{sec:genus-one}--\ref{sec:boundary-zeros} contain the explicit spectral and algebraic results for the quartic specialization.
Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility-note} records supplementary numerical comparisons for the real-axis modulus ordering and the first endpoint zeros, Appendix~\ref{app:projective-infinity} records the large-\(y\) consistency check, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion-new} closes with the scope and limitations of the quartic analysis.
