\section{Introduction}

We study the polynomial family $\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge0}$ defined by
\[
Z(t,y)=\sum_{m\ge0}Z_m(y)t^m=
\frac{1+yt+(y^2-y-1)t^2+(y^3-2y)t^3}
{1-t-(2y+1)t^2+t^3+y(y+1)t^4}.
\]
Equivalently, the spectral polynomial of the order-$4$ transfer recurrence is
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1).
\]
The paper is deliberately fixed-object: all structural and asymptotic statements concern this quartic, and endpoint windows are chosen before taking $m\to\infty$.

The construction embeds $\Pi$ in the balanced family
\[
P_{a,c,d,g}(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4+a\lambda^3+(c-2y)\lambda^2+d\lambda+y(y-c)+g.
\]
With $\xi=y-\lambda^2$ and $\WeierY=2\xi-c$, this family becomes the Weierstrass model
$\WeierY^2=c^2-4a\lambda^3-4d\lambda-4g$.
The normalized double roots at $(1,0)$ and $(-1,1)$ select the single member
$(a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)$, hence the elliptic normalization
\[
E:\quad \WeierY^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1,
\qquad
 y=\lambda^2-\frac12+\frac12\WeierY.
\]
The rest of the paper follows this degree-$4$ map $E\to\mathbf P^1_y$ through its real projection, its positive-axis dominant spectral branch, and its square-root endpoint at $y=0$.

\subsection{Relation to existing literature}

The background is the Beraha--Kahane--Weiss description of limiting zero sets by dominant equimodularity \cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1975,BerahaKahaneWeiss1978,Sokal2004,BrownOtto2025}, together with standard facts on rational generating functions, companion matrices, Weierstrass curves, and Newton--Puiseux expansions \cite{FlajoletSedgewick2009,Stanley2012,Partington1989,HornJohnson2012,Miranda1995,Silverman2009,Walker1950,BrieskornKnorrer1986}.
Closest on the recurrence side are Tran's discriminant method \cite{Tran2014,Tran2015}, four-term recurrence zero-location papers \cite{deBruin1984,ForgacsTran2016,ForgacsTran2017,ForgacsTran2020,TranZumba2018,TranZumba2021,Adams2020,BottaFornazieri2023}, and the Tran-conjecture/root-separation work of Ndikubwayo and B{\o}gvad--Ndikubwayo--Shapiro \cite{Ndikubwayo2020,BogvadNdikubwayoShapiro2020}.
Those works give broad location, separation, or hyperbolicity criteria for recurrence families, usually without resolving one distinguished algebraic sheet globally.  Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance} instead proves, for one rational quartic, the exact positive-axis maximal-modulus sheet: the finite branch values are explicit, the real-root counts are controlled across the Sturm denominator wall, and the final dominance comparison is an algebraic modulus comparison on the selected sheet.

Recent \emph{Indagationes} work is adjacent but not overlapping.
Dur\'an's fixed-index asymptotics for rightmost negative zeros of Bell, Eulerian, $r$-Bell, and related families \cite{Duran2025} also produce fixed-index zero locations, but for different families and by asymptotic methods that do not identify an elliptic spectral curve, a quartic transfer spectrum, or the residue amplitudes of colliding spectral branches.  Koelink--Rom\'an--Zudilin study zero location for partial-sum deformations of orthogonal polynomial families \cite{KoelinkRomanZudilin2025}, while Peth\H{o}'s recurrence paper concerns common values rather than zero attractors \cite{Petho2022}.
The new ingredients here are the fixed quartic rational recurrence, the elliptic normalization of its spectral curve, the exact real projection and positive-axis dominance geometry, and the explicit endpoint residue amplitudes $A_*=1/2$ and $B_*=3\sqrt2/16$ used in Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}.

\subsection*{Main results and scope}

The main theorem chain is the following five-result list; the double-root selection is a preparatory lemma, not a separate main theorem.  In the consolidated numbering there is no separate Theorem~1.1: this list is the introductory replacement for the former omnibus main-theorem statement.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-a-balanced-elliptic-normalization}: the balanced change of variables gives the elliptic normalization and ramification equation; for $\Pi$ this is $\WeierY^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1$.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-b-real-projection-criterion}: for the canonical curve, the compact real oval maps to $[\ySub,1]$ and the noncompact real oval maps to $[0,\infty)$; it also gives the exact distinct and multiplicity-counted real-root count for every real $y$.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance}: for every $y>0$, the positive real spectral branch is the unique root of maximal modulus.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}: in each fixed non-threshold endpoint window,
\[
Z_m\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)=
\exp\!\left(-\frac{3u^2}{8m}\right)
\left[\cos\!\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt2}-\frac{3u}{4\sqrt2\,m}\right)+O_K(m^{-2})\right]
\]
uniformly for $u$ in each fixed compact $K\subset\CC$, and the $k$th endpoint zero satisfies
\[
y_{m,k}= -\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}+O_k(m^{-4}).
\]
\item Propositions~\ref{prop:degree-law} and~\ref{prop:double-zero}: the recurrence-side consequences are the exact degree law and the forced boundary double zero at $y=-1$.
\end{enumerate}
The fixed-window limitation in the last item is part of the statement: neither the window nor the index set grows with $m$.
Recurrence-side degree and cancellation facts are collected in Section~\ref{sec:minimal-spectral-closure}; they are auxiliary to the elliptic and endpoint analysis rather than a separate general theory.
