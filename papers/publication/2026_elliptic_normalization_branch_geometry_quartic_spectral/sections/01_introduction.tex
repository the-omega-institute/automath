\section{Introduction}

We study the polynomial family $\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge0}$ defined by
\[
Z(t,y)=\sum_{m\ge0}Z_m(y)t^m=
\frac{1+yt+(y^2-y-1)t^2+(y^3-2y)t^3}
{1-t-(2y+1)t^2+t^3+y(y+1)t^4}.
\]
Equivalently, the spectral polynomial of the order-$4$ transfer recurrence is
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1).
\]
All structural and asymptotic statements concern this quartic, with endpoint windows fixed before taking $m\to\infty$.

The construction embeds $\Pi$ in the balanced family
\[
P_{a,c,d,g}(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4+a\lambda^3+(c-2y)\lambda^2+d\lambda+y(y-c)+g.
\]
With $\xi=y-\lambda^2$ and $\WeierY=2\xi-c$, this family becomes the Weierstrass model
$\WeierY^2=c^2-4a\lambda^3-4d\lambda-4g$.
The normalized double roots at $(1,0)$ and $(-1,1)$ select the single member
$(a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)$, hence the elliptic normalization
\[
E:\quad \WeierY^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1,
\qquad
 y=\lambda^2-\frac12+\frac12\WeierY.
\]
The rest of the paper follows this degree-$4$ map $E\to\mathbf P^1_y$ through its real projection, its positive-axis dominant spectral branch, and its square-root endpoint at $y=0$.

\subsection{Relation to existing literature}

The proof inputs are standard but used in a fixed-object way: Beraha--Kahane--Weiss dominance theory for recurrence zeros \cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1975,BerahaKahaneWeiss1978,Sokal2004,BrownOtto2025}, rational generating functions and companion matrices \cite{FlajoletSedgewick2009,Stanley2012,Partington1989,HornJohnson2012}, and elementary Weierstrass-curve and Newton--Puiseux tools \cite{Miranda1995,Silverman2009,Walker1950,BrieskornKnorrer1986}.  These sources provide the general language; the branch values, real projections, maximal-modulus comparison, and endpoint amplitudes are computed directly for the quartic \(\Pi\).

Several other literatures are contextual rather than proof input.  Tran's discriminant method \cite{Tran2014,Tran2015}, four-term recurrence zero-location papers \cite{deBruin1984,ForgacsTran2016,ForgacsTran2017,ForgacsTran2020,TranZumba2018,TranZumba2021,Adams2020,BottaFornazieri2023}, and the Tran-conjecture/root-separation work of Ndikubwayo and B{\o}gvad--Ndikubwayo--Shapiro \cite{Ndikubwayo2020,BogvadNdikubwayoShapiro2020} give broad location, separation, or hyperbolicity criteria for recurrence families.  The Toeplitz, ACSV, stability-preserver, and Lee--Yang references \cite{SchmidtSpitzer1960,Hirschman1967,BottcherGrudsky2005,DuitsKuijlaars2012,PemantleWilson2013,BorceaBranden2009,YangLee1952I,LeeYang1952II,Fisher1978,KortmanGriffiths1971} explain why dominant spectral sheets and endpoint zero laws are natural objects, but their theorems are not invoked to prove the fixed quartic statements below.

The closest comparisons split by method rather than by venue.  Recent \emph{Indagationes Mathematicae} papers involving recurrence arithmetic or zero location provide adjacent problems, while exponential-section and Toeplitz spectral-curve papers provide the nearest asymptotic viewpoints.  The present contribution is narrower: it gives exact constants for one rational recurrence, not a general equilibrium measure, zero-location criterion, or entire-function section theorem.

A fixed-quartic computation is broad enough to be useful because it completes a full chain that general criteria normally leave open: exact normalization, exact real projection, exact positive-axis dominant sheet, and a quantified fixed-window endpoint zero law for the same recurrence.  Thus the article provides one fully worked spectral curve for which the algebraic geometry and recurrence zeros can be checked end-to-end.
\subsection*{Main results and scope}

The proof is organized around four mathematical blocks.  The double-root selection and recurrence-side cancellation facts are used as preparatory inputs, while the main assertions are the elliptic normalization, real projection and root counts, positive-axis dominance, and fixed-window endpoint-zero asymptotics listed below.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-a-balanced-elliptic-normalization}: the balanced change of variables gives the elliptic normalization and ramification equation; for $\Pi$ this is $\WeierY^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1$.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-b-real-projection-criterion}: for the canonical curve, the compact real oval maps to $[\ySub,1]$ and the noncompact real oval maps to $[0,\infty)$; it also gives the exact distinct and multiplicity-counted real-root count for every real $y$.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance}: for every $y>0$, the positive real spectral branch is the unique root of maximal modulus.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}: in each fixed non-threshold endpoint window,
\[
Z_m\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)=
\exp\!\left(-\frac{3u^2}{8m}\right)
\left[\cos\!\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt2}-\frac{3u}{4\sqrt2\,m}\right)+O_K(m^{-2})\right]
\]
uniformly for $u$ in each fixed compact $K\subset\CC$, and the $k$th endpoint zero satisfies
\[
y_{m,k}= -\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}+O_k(m^{-4}).
\]
\end{enumerate}
The fixed-window limitation in the last item is part of the statement: neither the window nor the index set grows with $m$.
Recurrence-side degree, cancellation, and boundary-zero facts, including Propositions~\ref{prop:degree-law} and~\ref{prop:double-zero}, are collected in Sections~\ref{sec:minimal-spectral-closure} and~\ref{sec:boundary-zeros}; they are auxiliary to the elliptic and endpoint analysis rather than a separate general theory.
