\section{Introduction}

We study the polynomial family $\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge0}$ defined by
\[
Z(t,y)=\sum_{m\ge0}Z_m(y)t^m=
\frac{1+yt+(y^2-y-1)t^2+(y^3-2y)t^3}
{1-t-(2y+1)t^2+t^3+y(y+1)t^4}.
\]
Equivalently, the spectral polynomial of the order-$4$ transfer recurrence is
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1).
\]
The paper is deliberately fixed-object: all structural and asymptotic statements concern this quartic, and endpoint windows are chosen before taking $m\to\infty$.

The construction embeds $\Pi$ in the balanced family
\[
P_{a,c,d,g}(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4+a\lambda^3+(c-2y)\lambda^2+d\lambda+y(y-c)+g.
\]
With $\xi=y-\lambda^2$ and $\WeierY=2\xi-c$, this family becomes the Weierstrass model
$\WeierY^2=c^2-4a\lambda^3-4d\lambda-4g$.
The normalized double roots at $(1,0)$ and $(-1,1)$ select the single member
$(a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)$, hence the elliptic normalization
\[
E:\quad \WeierY^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1,
\qquad
 y=\lambda^2-\frac12+\frac12\WeierY.
\]
The rest of the paper follows this degree-$4$ map $E\to\mathbf P^1_y$ through its real projection, its positive-axis dominant spectral branch, and its square-root endpoint at $y=0$.

\subsection{Relation to existing literature}

The proof inputs are standard but used in a fixed-object way: Beraha--Kahane--Weiss dominance theory for recurrence zeros \cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1975,BerahaKahaneWeiss1978,Sokal2004,BrownOtto2025}, rational generating functions and companion matrices \cite{FlajoletSedgewick2009,Stanley2012,Partington1989,HornJohnson2012}, and elementary Weierstrass-curve and Newton--Puiseux tools \cite{Miranda1995,Silverman2009,Walker1950,BrieskornKnorrer1986}.  These sources provide the general language; the branch values, real projections, maximal-modulus comparison, and endpoint amplitudes are computed directly for the quartic \(\Pi\).

Several other literatures are contextual rather than proof input.  Tran's discriminant method \cite{Tran2014,Tran2015}, four-term recurrence zero-location papers \cite{deBruin1984,ForgacsTran2016,ForgacsTran2017,ForgacsTran2020,TranZumba2018,TranZumba2021,Adams2020,BottaFornazieri2023}, and the Tran-conjecture/root-separation work of Ndikubwayo and B{\o}gvad--Ndikubwayo--Shapiro \cite{Ndikubwayo2020,BogvadNdikubwayoShapiro2020} give broad location, separation, or hyperbolicity criteria for recurrence families.  The Toeplitz, ACSV, stability-preserver, and Lee--Yang references \cite{SchmidtSpitzer1960,Hirschman1967,BottcherGrudsky2005,DuitsKuijlaars2012,PemantleWilson2013,BorceaBranden2009,YangLee1952I,LeeYang1952II,Fisher1978,KortmanGriffiths1971} explain why dominant spectral sheets and endpoint zero laws are natural objects, but their theorems are not invoked to prove the fixed quartic statements below.

For journal fit and overlap, we compared the paper against recent \emph{Indagationes Mathematicae} papers on linear recurrences, recurrence-zero arithmetic, zero asymptotics, and orthogonal-polynomial deformations, and against the closest non-\emph{Indagationes} rational-recurrence zero-location literature cited above.  The search scope was deliberately broader than papers whose titles mention polynomial zeros: it included the last five years of \emph{Indagationes} articles in which linear recurrences occur as coefficients or as arithmetic objects.  Within this scope, Fuchs--Heintze's theorem on integral zeros of polynomials with linear recurrences as coefficients \cite{FuchsHeintze2021} is adjacent but arithmetic-Diophantine; it does not study the zero set of a single polynomial sequence in a complex parameter, normalize a spectral quartic, or prove a dominant-sheet endpoint law.  The comparison is theorem-by-theorem.  Peth\H{o}'s common-value theorem for linear recurrences \cite{Petho2022} is likewise arithmetic and does not normalize a recurrence spectral quartic; Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-a-balanced-elliptic-normalization} gives the explicit fixed model \(\WeierY^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1\), the degree-four projection, and the ramification equation.  Koelink--Rom\'an--Zudilin study zero location for partial-sum deformations of orthogonal polynomials \cite{KoelinkRomanZudilin2025}; Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-b-real-projection-criterion} instead computes the exact images of the two real ovals of this elliptic normalization, namely \([\ySub,1]\) and \([0,\infty)\), and the real-root count of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) for every real \(y\).  The non-\emph{Indagationes} four-term recurrence papers cited above control zeros for parametrized recurrence classes, but do not identify one algebraic sheet as the unique maximal-modulus root for all positive parameters; this is Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance}.  Dur\'an's fixed-index asymptotics for rightmost negative zeros of Bell, Eulerian, \(r\)-Bell, and related families \cite{Duran2025} are the closest analogue to our endpoint result, but use different families and not elliptic colliding-branch amplitudes; Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization} derives the two-term law from the constants \(A_*=1/2\) and \(B_*=3\sqrt2/16\).

The endpoint proof is also best compared with zero asymptotics for sections of exponential sums, especially Bleher--Mallison's extension of Szeg\H{o}-type section asymptotics \cite{BleherMallison2006}.  Our local two-exponential calculation is much narrower: it applies only after the fixed quartic has been reduced to the two colliding branches at \(y=0\), but it gives the exact constants in the negative endpoint window.  The spectral-curve and dominant-sheet viewpoint should likewise be read next to non-self-adjoint banded Toeplitz spectral-curve work, from Schmidt--Spitzer and Hirschman to the monograph treatment of B\"ottcher--Grudsky and the rational Toeplitz equilibrium formulation of Duits--Kuijlaars \cite{SchmidtSpitzer1960,Hirschman1967,BottcherGrudsky2005,DuitsKuijlaars2012}.  The present paper does not prove a general limiting-measure theorem for Toeplitz spectra or a general entire-function section theorem; its new contribution is the complete exact calculation for one rational recurrence, including branch values, real-oval covering degrees, the positive-axis dominant sheet, and the first correction term for endpoint zeros.

A fixed-quartic computation is broad enough to be useful because it completes a full chain that general criteria normally leave open: exact normalization, exact real projection, exact positive-axis dominant sheet, and a quantified fixed-window endpoint zero law for the same recurrence.  The point is not to classify balanced quartics, but to provide one fully worked spectral curve where the algebraic geometry and recurrence zeros can be checked end-to-end.
\subsection*{Main results and scope}

The proof is organized around four mathematical blocks; the double-root selection and recurrence-side cancellation facts are preparatory inputs, not separate main-theorem claims.  In the consolidated numbering there is no separate Theorem~1.1: this list is the introductory replacement for the former omnibus main-theorem statement.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-a-balanced-elliptic-normalization}: the balanced change of variables gives the elliptic normalization and ramification equation; for $\Pi$ this is $\WeierY^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1$.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-b-real-projection-criterion}: for the canonical curve, the compact real oval maps to $[\ySub,1]$ and the noncompact real oval maps to $[0,\infty)$; it also gives the exact distinct and multiplicity-counted real-root count for every real $y$.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance}: for every $y>0$, the positive real spectral branch is the unique root of maximal modulus.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}: in each fixed non-threshold endpoint window,
\[
Z_m\!\left(-\frac{u^2}{m^2}\right)=
\exp\!\left(-\frac{3u^2}{8m}\right)
\left[\cos\!\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt2}-\frac{3u}{4\sqrt2\,m}\right)+O_K(m^{-2})\right]
\]
uniformly for $u$ in each fixed compact $K\subset\CC$, and the $k$th endpoint zero satisfies
\[
y_{m,k}= -\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}+O_k(m^{-4}).
\]
\end{enumerate}
The fixed-window limitation in the last item is part of the statement: neither the window nor the index set grows with $m$.
Recurrence-side degree, cancellation, and boundary-zero facts, including Propositions~\ref{prop:degree-law} and~\ref{prop:double-zero}, are collected in Sections~\ref{sec:minimal-spectral-closure} and~\ref{sec:boundary-zeros}; they are auxiliary to the elliptic and endpoint analysis rather than a separate general theory.
