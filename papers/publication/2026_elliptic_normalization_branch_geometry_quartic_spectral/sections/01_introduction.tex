\section{Introduction}

We study the polynomial family $\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}$ defined by the rational generating function
\[
Z(t,y)=\sum_{m\ge 0} Z_m(y)t^m
=
\frac{1+yt+(y^2-y-1)t^2+(y^3-2y)t^3}
{1-t-(2y+1)t^2+t^3+y(y+1)t^4}.
\]
Equivalently, $\{Z_m(y)\}$ is generated by an order-$4$ companion transfer matrix with characteristic polynomial
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1).
\]
The paper is organized as a complete exact analysis of this quartic spectral family.
We regard \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\) as a fixed algebraic-spectral object and ask for an exact description of its elliptic normalization, its real branch geometry, its positive-real maximal-modulus branch, and the fixed-index zeros of \(Z_m\) in fixed compact windows at the maximal-modulus square-root endpoint \(y=0\).

Section~\ref{sec:genus-one} places \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\) inside the balanced family
\[
P_{a,c,d,g}(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4+a\lambda^3+(c-2y)\lambda^2+d\lambda+y(y-c)+g,
\]
for which the substitution \(\xi=y-\lambda^2\) removes the quartic obstruction and produces a Weierstrass model.
Within that family, the normalized double-root statement shows that fixing the neighbouring colliding points at $(\lambda,y)=(1,0)$ and $(\lambda,y)=(-1,1)$ forces the balanced parameters to be \((a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)\).
For this specialization, the projective closure of \(\Pi(\lambda,y)=0\) is normalized by the elliptic curve
\[
Y^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1.
\]
This turns the fixed canonical problem into a degree-$4$ genus-one covering of the \(y\)-line; the surrounding balanced family is used to formulate the finite real-projection criterion for fixed members.
The endpoint theorem is then proved by combining the real branch classification, local Puiseux expansions, and compact-uniform two-branch estimates; the endpoint expansion appendix supplies part of this proof.

\subsection{Relation to existing literature}

The ingredients used in the paper are classical, and the novelty claimed here is not any one of those tools in isolation.
The Yang--Lee papers and Fisher's edge-singularity paper are part of the historical background for zero distributions in parameter-dependent polynomial families \cite{YangLee1952I,LeeYang1952II,Fisher1978}; here they serve only as orientation, not as a physical-model interpretation.  The operative framework is algebraic and spectral: modern Beraha--Kahane--Weiss formulations organize limiting zero sets through dominant equimodularity \cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1975,BerahaKahaneWeiss1978,Sokal2004,BrownOtto2025}.
The outside inputs are used in pinpoint form: the cyclic-vector theorem for companion matrices \cite[Sec.~3.3]{HornJohnson2012}; the equivalence between reduced rational generating functions, constant-coefficient recurrences, and finite Hankel rank \cite[Sec.~4.1]{FlajoletSedgewick2009}, \cite[Chap.~4]{Stanley2012}, \cite[Chap.~1]{Partington1989}; the nonsingular projective model, Riemann--Hurwitz formula, and real-locus description of a Weierstrass double cover \cite[Chap.~I]{Miranda1995}, \cite[Chap.~III]{Silverman2009}; and the local Newton--Puiseux theorem for a plane branch \cite[Chap.~I]{Walker1950}, \cite[Chap.~8]{BrieskornKnorrer1986}.
For this reason the proofs below keep the classical steps visible: the companion-matrix and rational-sequence facts are used only to identify the fixed spectral recurrence, the Weierstrass and real-oval/proper-covering facts are used only for the degree-four cover of the fixed quartic, and the Puiseux--Rouch\'e endpoint argument is used only after the explicit constants for this quartic have been computed.
The contribution is the calibrated assembly of these standard inputs into a complete fixed-member theorem chain for the displayed quartic.

Within the recurrence-generated zero literature, the closest references are Tran's discriminant approach \cite{Tran2014,Tran2015}, the four-term work of de Bruin, Forg\'acs--Tran, Tran--Zumba, Adams, and Botta--Fornazieri \cite{deBruin1984,ForgacsTran2016,ForgacsTran2017,ForgacsTran2020,TranZumba2018,TranZumba2021,Adams2020,BottaFornazieri2023}, and the exact zero-location and Tran-conjecture papers of Ndikubwayo and of B{\o}gvad--Ndikubwayo--Shapiro \cite{Ndikubwayo2020,BogvadNdikubwayoShapiro2020}.
Related geometric viewpoints also appear in Goh--He--Ricci and in Wang--Zhang \cite{GohHeRicci2009,WangZhang2021}, while Ndikubwayo's later Toeplitz-based work treats a different recurrence regime \cite{Ndikubwayo2022}.

\subsubsection*{Contribution and scope}

The contribution here is the exact integration of several classical tools for one concrete quartic.
The paper carries the quartic from its rational generating function through elliptic normalization, branch locus, real oval projection, maximal-modulus separation, and endpoint two-branch oscillation without numerical fitting or unproved genericity assumptions; the degree and boundary-zero facts are recorded only as recurrence-side appendices for the same family.
The scope is the fixed quartic spectral curve and its elliptic normalization.
All statements below use only the rational recurrence, the quartic spectral polynomial, the balanced genus-one reduction, and the resulting branch geometry.
The neighbouring regimes that would require parameter variation, external models, or windows growing with the recurrence index are not part of this article; here the fixed quartic, its fixed balanced normalization, and fixed endpoint windows are the objects of study.
For real $y>0$ we write
\[
r(y):=\max_{1\le j\le 4}\abs{\lambda_j(y)},
\qquad
f_{\mathrm{dom}}(y):=\log r(y).
\]
The maximal-modulus branch over $y>0$ is the connected component of the smooth spectral curve on which $\abs{\lambda}=r(y)$.
The problem is thus to determine this branch and the corresponding zero geometry explicitly for \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\).

\paragraph{Theorem overview and source order.}
The paper proves five named results; their logical order is Theorems~A, B, C, D, E, while the source proves the parameter-normalization input for Theorem~C immediately after the fixed-member projection machinery from which Theorem~B is evaluated.
Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:hankel} first derive the recurrence, companion matrix, and minimal order directly from the displayed rational generating function and \(\Pi(\lambda,y)=0\).
Theorem~A, \emph{balanced elliptic normalization}, then uses the balanced family only to obtain the substitution \(\xi=y-\lambda^2\), \(Y=2\xi-c\), and the Weierstrass model
\[
Y^2=c^2-4a\lambda^3-4d\lambda-4g.
\]
For the selected spectral quartic this gives the model \(Y^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1\), the degree-four map to the \(y\)-line, and the finite branch polynomial.
Theorem~B, \emph{canonical real projection}, is the canonical specialization of the fixed-member finite criterion of Section~\ref{sec:genus-one}: after the single member \(P_{-1,-1,1,0}\) has been selected, its real \(y\)-image is decided by finitely many endpoint, ramification, and exceptional critical values.  It gives the two real oval images
\[
[\ySub,1]
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
[0,\infty),
\]
together with the exact real-root count of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) on every real interval.
Theorem~C, \emph{canonical double-root specialization}, proves the normalized double-root selection: the collisions at $(\lambda,y)=(1,0)$ and $(-1,1)$ force \((a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)\), and the affine normalization formula carries the statement to the corresponding two-point collision data under the stated real nonzero weighted scaling.
In the source order, the proof of Theorem~C appears before the final statement of Theorem~B because it identifies the canonical member to which the preceding fixed-member criterion is then applied.  Dominance is treated separately in Theorem~D.
Theorem~D, \emph{positive-real dominance classification}, combines the discriminant factorization, finite branch values, real-root counts, and positive-axis interval comparisons to identify the unique maximal-modulus branch on \(y>0\); in particular the endpoint at \(y=0\) is the controlling square-root collision for that branch, while the other real branch collisions are subdominant.
Theorem~E, \emph{fixed-index endpoint zero law}, converts this branch information into endpoint zeros in the fixed-index, fixed-window regime: for each fixed \(k\ge1\), if the negative zeros in a fixed compact endpoint window are ordered from \(0\) leftwards, then
\[
y_{m,k}
=
-\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}
-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4}).
\]
Equivalently, after a compact endpoint window \(y=-u^2/m^2\) has been fixed independently of \(m\), the theorem isolates exactly the corresponding finite set of simple zeros for all sufficiently large \(m\).

The auxiliary appendices have a separate role.
Appendices~\ref{sec:degree-law} and~\ref{sec:boundary-zeros} record recurrence-side consequences used only to keep the spectral and polynomial formulations aligned; Appendix~\ref{app:disc}, Appendix~\ref{app:degeneracies}, and Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility-note} collect the displayed determinant, resultant, Sturm, and coefficient-comparison inputs; Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching} supplies the endpoint local expansions used in Theorem~E; and Appendix~\ref{app:projective-infinity} is a fixed-quartic consistency check at the opposite end of the same positive branch.
Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-order} establishes the generic order-four minimal spectral closure used throughout.
Proposition~\ref{prop:degree-law} proves
\begin{align*}
\deg_y Z_m&=\left\lfloor\frac{m+3}{2}\right\rfloor,\\
[y^{\deg Z_{2n+1}}]Z_{2n+1}&=n,
\qquad
[y^{\deg Z_{2n}}]Z_{2n}=\frac{n(n^2+5)}6,
\end{align*}
and Proposition~\ref{prop:double-zero} proves the uniform divisibility \((y+1)^2\mid Z_m(y)\) for \(m\ge2\).
These algebraic consequences are proved from the recurrence and generating function after the spectral family has been fixed, and remain appendicial support for the same fixed-quartic analysis.

\paragraph{Roadmap.}
Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:hankel} define the family and prove the minimal recurrence closure.
Section~\ref{sec:genus-one} contains the balanced reduction, the fixed-member real-projection criterion, the canonical double-root normalization, and the elliptic branch computation.
Section~\ref{sec:ep-classification} classifies the real branch values, and Section~\ref{sec:positive-maximal-modulus-branch} proves the positive-axis dominance theorem and endpoint zero law.
The appendices record the auxiliary degree and boundary-zero identities for the same recurrence, the displayed discriminant elimination, Sturm and pseudo-remainder computations, endpoint local expansions, exact algebraic certificates, and a fixed positive-branch computation at projective infinity.  The projective-infinity calculation is included only as a consistency computation for this fixed quartic, not as an input to the endpoint theorem or as a large-parameter asymptotic theorem.
