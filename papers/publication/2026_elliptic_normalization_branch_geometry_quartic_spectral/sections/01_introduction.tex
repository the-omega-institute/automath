\section{Introduction}

We study the polynomial family $\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}$ defined by the rational generating function
\[
Z(t,y)=\sum_{m\ge 0} Z_m(y)t^m
=
\frac{1+yt+(y^2-y-1)t^2+(y^3-2y)t^3}
{1-t-(2y+1)t^2+t^3+y(y+1)t^4}.
\]
Equivalently, $\{Z_m(y)\}$ is generated by an order-$4$ companion transfer matrix with characteristic polynomial
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4-\lambda^3-(2y+1)\lambda^2+\lambda+y(y+1).
\]
The paper is organized around one fixed algebraic-spectral object, namely the displayed quartic \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\).
For this quartic, and for endpoint windows fixed before the large-\(m\) limit, we give exact descriptions of the elliptic normalization, real branch geometry, positive-real maximal-modulus branch, and fixed-index zeros of \(Z_m\) near the maximal-modulus square-root endpoint \(y=0\).

Section~\ref{sec:genus-one} places \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\) inside the balanced family
\[
P_{a,c,d,g}(\lambda,y)=\lambda^4+a\lambda^3+(c-2y)\lambda^2+d\lambda+y(y-c)+g,
\]
where the substitution \(\xi=y-\lambda^2\), \(Y=2\xi-c\), gives a Weierstrass model.
The canonical specialization forced by the two neighbouring double roots is \((a,c,d,g)=(-1,-1,1,0)\), whose normalized projective model is
\[
Y^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1.
\]
The rest of the paper follows this fixed degree-$4$ genus-one covering of the \(y\)-line through its real projection, positive-axis dominance, and endpoint zero asymptotics.

\subsection{Relation to existing literature}

The historical background for zero distributions in parameter-dependent polynomial families includes the Yang--Lee papers and Fisher's edge-singularity paper \cite{YangLee1952I,LeeYang1952II,Fisher1978}.
The operative framework here is algebraic and spectral: Beraha--Kahane--Weiss type results organize limiting zero sets through dominant equimodularity \cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1975,BerahaKahaneWeiss1978,Sokal2004,BrownOtto2025}.
The proof also uses standard companion-matrix cyclic-vector facts \cite[Sec.~3.3]{HornJohnson2012}, the rational-sequence/finite-Hankel-rank equivalence \cite[Sec.~4.1]{FlajoletSedgewick2009}, \cite[Chap.~4]{Stanley2012}, \cite[Chap.~1]{Partington1989}, the basic geometry of nonsingular Weierstrass models and real double covers \cite[Chap.~I]{Miranda1995}, \cite[Chap.~III]{Silverman2009}, and local Newton--Puiseux expansions \cite[Chap.~I]{Walker1950}, \cite[Chap.~8]{BrieskornKnorrer1986}.
The contribution is to make these inputs exact for the displayed quartic, including the finite branch data and endpoint constants needed for the zero law.

Within the recurrence-generated zero literature, the closest references are Tran's discriminant approach \cite{Tran2014,Tran2015}, the four-term work of de Bruin, Forg\'acs--Tran, Tran--Zumba, Adams, and Botta--Fornazieri \cite{deBruin1984,ForgacsTran2016,ForgacsTran2017,ForgacsTran2020,TranZumba2018,TranZumba2021,Adams2020,BottaFornazieri2023}, and the exact zero-location and Tran-conjecture papers of Ndikubwayo and of B{\o}gvad--Ndikubwayo--Shapiro \cite{Ndikubwayo2020,BogvadNdikubwayoShapiro2020}.
Related geometric viewpoints also appear in Goh--He--Ricci and in Wang--Zhang \cite{GohHeRicci2009,WangZhang2021}, while Ndikubwayo's later Toeplitz-based work treats a different recurrence regime \cite{Ndikubwayo2022}.

Recent \emph{Indagationes} work closest to the endpoint-zero result is Dur\'an's fixed-index asymptotics for the rightmost negative zeros of Bell, Eulerian, $r$-Bell, and related polynomial families \cite{Duran2025}.
That result is not a special case of Theorem~E, and Theorem~E is not obtained from it: here the polynomials are generated by a rational recurrence, the local scale is produced by a collision of two dominant branches of the quartic characteristic equation \(\Pi(\lambda,y)=0\), and the proof uses explicit Puiseux and residue amplitudes on the resulting elliptic spectral curve.
Koelink--Rom\'an--Zudilin study zero location for partial-sum deformations of orthogonal polynomial families \cite{KoelinkRomanZudilin2025}, an adjacent setting in which root separation and dominance are governed by the orthogonal-polynomial and partial-sum structure.
Our Theorem~D is different: it is a finite algebraic dominance classification for this single quartic rational recurrence, combining the exact branch values, real-root counts, endpoint factorizations, and interval comparisons to identify the unique positive-real maximal-modulus sheet.
Thus the new ingredients are the quartic rational recurrence, its elliptic spectral curve, and the explicit endpoint amplitudes that feed the fixed-window zero asymptotics.

\subsubsection*{Main results and scope}

For real $y>0$ let
\[
r(y):=\max_{1\le j\le4}\abs{\lambda_j(y)}.
\]
The main results, all for the fixed quartic \(\Pi\), are the following.
\begin{theorem}[Overview of Theorems A--E]
Under the balanced substitution \(\xi=y-\lambda^2\), \(Y=2\xi-c\), the canonical member has
\[
\Pi(\lambda,y)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Y^2=4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1,
\qquad
y=\lambda^2-\frac12+\frac12Y.
\]
If \(E_{\mathrm c}(\RR)\) and \(E_{\mathrm n}(\RR)\) are the compact and noncompact real ovals of this curve, then
\[
y(E_{\mathrm c}(\RR))=[y_{\mathrm{sub}},1],
\qquad
y(E_{\mathrm n}(\RR))=[0,\infty).
\]
The two prescribed double-root constraints, after the allowed weighted affine normalization, force the canonical parameter quadruple \((-1,-1,1,0)\).
For every \(y>0\), the positive real root on the dominant sheet satisfies
\[
r(y)=\lambda_{\mathrm{dom}}(y).
\]
Finally, the negative endpoint zeros are fixed-index and fixed-window asymptotic:
\[
y_{m,k}
=
-\frac{\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{2m^2}
-\frac{3\pi^2(2k-1)^2}{4m^3}
+O_k(m^{-4})
\]
for each fixed \(k\ge1\), with no assertion that the endpoint window or the index set may grow with \(m\).
\end{theorem}

The scope of the article is the single quartic \(\Pi\), its balanced elliptic normalization, and endpoint windows fixed independently of \(m\); the balanced family is used to derive and certify this canonical member, while recurrence-side degree growth, boundary divisibility, determinant/resultant/Sturm certificates, endpoint coefficient comparisons, and the projective-infinity check are recorded in the appendices as supporting exact data for the same fixed spectral problem.
