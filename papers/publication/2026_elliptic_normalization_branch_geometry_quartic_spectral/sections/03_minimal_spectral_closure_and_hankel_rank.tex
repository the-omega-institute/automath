\section{Minimal spectral closure and Hankel rank}\label{sec:hankel}

\subsection{Reduced rationality and minimal recurrence order}

Since $Z(t,y)=N(t,y)/D(t,y)$ with $\deg_t D=4$, the sequence $\{Z_m(y)\}$ satisfies a linear recurrence of order at most $4$.
Away from the standard degeneracy set in $y$, there is no numerator--denominator cancellation, so the order is exactly $4$; this is a standard property of reduced rational generating functions \cite{Stanley2012,FlajoletSedgewick2009,Partington1989}.

\begin{lemma}[Reduced rational sequences and Hankel rank]\label{lem:reduced-rational-hankel}
Let
\[
A(t)=\sum_{m\ge0}a_m t^m=\frac{P(t)}{Q(t)}\in\CC[[t]],
\qquad
Q(0)=1,
\]
with \(P,Q\in\CC[t]\), \(\gcd(P,Q)=1\), and \(r=\deg Q\ge1\).
Then the least order of a constant-coefficient recurrence satisfied by \(\{a_m\}_{m\ge0}\) for all sufficiently large \(m\) is \(r\).
If in addition \(\deg P<r\), then the infinite Hankel matrix \((a_{i+j})_{i,j\ge0}\) has rank \(r\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the standard reduced-rational-function argument for linear recurrences and finite Hankel rank; we include the proof to fix the exact denominator-degree convention used later \cite{Stanley2012,FlajoletSedgewick2009,Partington1989}.
Write \(Q(t)=1+q_1t+\cdots+q_rt^r\) with \(q_r\ne0\).
The coefficient identity \(Q(t)A(t)=P(t)\) gives
\[
a_m+q_1a_{m-1}+\cdots+q_ra_{m-r}=0
\]
for all \(m>\deg P\), so an order-\(r\) tail recurrence holds.
Conversely, suppose a tail recurrence of order \(s<r\) holds, and put \(L(t)=1+\ell_1t+\cdots+\ell_st^s\) for its monic recurrence polynomial.
Then \(L(t)A(t)\) has only finitely many nonzero coefficients outside its initial polynomial part, so \(L(t)A(t)=B(t)\) is a polynomial.
Thus \(L(t)P(t)=B(t)Q(t)\).
Since \(P\) and \(Q\) are coprime, \(Q\mid L\), impossible because \(\deg L=s<r=\deg Q\).
Hence the tail order is exactly \(r\).

Assume now \(\deg P<r\), so no polynomial part is present and the recurrence begins at \(m=r\).
The recurrence expresses every Hankel column with index \(j\ge r\) as a linear combination of the preceding \(r\) columns, hence the Hankel rank is at most \(r\).
If the rank were at most \(r-1\), then the first \(r\) columns would be linearly dependent: for some nonzero vector \((c_0,\ldots,c_{r-1})\),
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}c_j a_{i+j}=0
\qquad (i\ge0).
\]
After dividing by the highest nonzero coefficient and shifting indices, this is a recurrence of order at most \(r-1\) beginning from some finite index, contradicting the minimal tail order just proved.
Therefore the Hankel rank is exactly \(r\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Generic reduced recurrence and Hankel rank]\label{thm:minimal-order}
For \(y\notin\{-1,0,1\}\), the polynomials \(N(t,y)\) and \(D(t,y)\) are coprime in \(\CC[t]\).
Consequently, the minimal linear recurrence order of \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}\) is \(4\), and its Hankel rank is \(4\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Appendix~\ref{app:degeneracies} records the resultant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ND-resultant}
\operatorname{Res}_t(N,D)=y^3(y-1)^3(y+1)^6,
\end{equation}
showing that cancellations occur only at \(y\in\{-1,0,1\}\).
For all other $y$, the rational function $Z(t,y)$ is reduced with a denominator of degree $4$ and a numerator of degree less than $4$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduced-rational-hankel} therefore shows both that the recurrence order cannot drop below $4$ and that the Hankel rank is $4$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:recurrence} already displays the order-$4$ relation explicitly.

Let \(H=(Z_{i+j}(y))_{i,j\ge 0}\) be the infinite Hankel matrix.
The preceding paragraph applies the finite-rank part of the lemma to this $H$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Degenerate parameters}

The three excluded fibers are not left implicit.  They are exactly the fibers where the order-$4$ rational presentation ceases to be minimal.

\begin{proposition}[Exact degenerate fibers]\label{prop:degenerate-fibers}
At the exceptional parameters \(y=-1,0,1\), the reduced generating functions, reduced denominators, recurrence orders, and Hankel ranks are as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
y & Z(t,y) & \text{red. denom.} & \text{tail order} & \operatorname{rank} \\
\hline
-1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & \dfrac{1}{1-t} & 1-t & 1 & 1 \\
1 & \dfrac{1}{1-2t} & 1-2t & 1 & 1
\end{array}
\]
Here ``minimal tail order'' means the degree of the reduced denominator, equivalently the order of the minimal constant-coefficient recurrence after the finite polynomial part has been separated.  Thus at \(y=-1\) the sequence is \(Z_0(-1)=1\) and \(Z_m(-1)=0\) for all \(m\ge1\); if one requires a positive-order recurrence beginning at \(m=1\), it is the order-one relation \(Z_m(-1)=0\cdot Z_{m-1}(-1)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct specialization of the polynomials from Section~\ref{subsec:ogf} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
N(t,1)&=-(t-1)(t+1)^2,&
D(t,1)&=(t-1)(t+1)^2(2t-1),\\
N(t,0)&=-(t-1)(t+1),&
D(t,0)&=(t-1)^2(t+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
After cancellation, these give
\[
Z(t,1)=\frac{1}{1-2t},
\qquad
Z(t,0)=\frac{1}{1-t}.
\]
At \(y=-1\), one has
\[
N(t,-1)=D(t,-1)=1-t+t^2-t^3,
\]
and hence \(Z(t,-1)=1\).  These identities prove the entries in the first three columns of the table.

The recurrence order column follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:reduced-rational-hankel}: a reduced denominator of degree \(r\) is the minimal denominator of the sequence after removing any finite polynomial part, and its coefficients give the minimal tail recurrence of order \(r\).  Thus the two geometric sequences at \(y=0\) and \(y=1\) have order one, while the polynomial generating function at \(y=-1\) has tail order zero.

It remains only to compute the Hankel ranks.  For \(y=0\), \(Z_m(0)=1\), so
\[
Z_{i+j}(0)=1=1\cdot1
\]
and the infinite Hankel matrix is nonzero of rank one.  For \(y=1\), \(Z_m(1)=2^m\), so
\[
Z_{i+j}(1)=2^{i+j}=2^i2^j,
\]
again a nonzero rank-one matrix.  For \(y=-1\), the only nonzero coefficient is \(Z_0(-1)=1\), so the Hankel matrix has a single nonzero entry at \((0,0)\), and therefore also has rank one.  This proves the classification.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Complete minimal spectral-closure classification]\label{cor:complete-minimal-closure-classification}
For every parameter \(y\in\CC\), the reduced recurrence order and Hankel rank of \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge0}\) are completely classified as follows.
If \(y\notin\{-1,0,1\}\), then the reduced denominator has degree \(4\), the recurrence has minimal order \(4\), and the Hankel rank is \(4\).
At \(y=0\) and \(y=1\), the reduced denominator has degree \(1\), the sequence is geometric, and the Hankel rank is \(1\).
At \(y=-1\), the generating function is the polynomial \(1\), the tail recurrence order is \(0\), and the Hankel rank is \(1\).
Thus the quartic spectral closure is genuinely four-dimensional precisely off the three exceptional fibres \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The generic statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-order}: outside \(\{-1,0,1\}\), the numerator and denominator are coprime and the degree-four denominator is minimal, so the recurrence order and Hankel rank are both four.
The three exceptional cases are exactly Proposition~\ref{prop:degenerate-fibers}, whose table gives the reduced denominator, tail order, and Hankel rank at \(y=-1,0,1\).
Combining the two disjoint cases covers all \(y\in\CC\), and the final assertion is only the resulting dichotomy between the generic order-four closure and the explicit rank-one degeneracies.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Spectral-closure support for the branch analysis]\label{cor:spectral-closure-support}
On the positive real axis the open cells used in the dominance proof have the following recurrence-side closure.
For every \(y\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)\), the four roots of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) are all present in the reduced generating function of \(\{Z_m(y)\}\): no proper product of fewer than four factors of \(D(t,y)\) can be a recurrence denominator, and the Hankel rank is \(4\).
At the only positive interior exceptional point, \(y=1\), the sequence reduces to \(Z_m(1)=2^m\), the reduced denominator is \(1-2t\), and the support collapses to the simple spectral factor \(\lambda=2\).
Consequently, the positive-axis branch and dominance statements of Theorem~\ref{thm:consolidated-branch-dominance-package} are not artifacts of hidden numerator--denominator cancellation on either open positive cell; the only positive collapse is the explicitly isolated geometric fibre at \(y=1\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If \(y\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)\), then \(y\notin\{-1,0,1\}\), so Corollary~\ref{cor:complete-minimal-closure-classification} gives reduced denominator degree four and Hankel rank four.
Since \(D(t,y)=t^4\Pi(1/t,y)\), replacing \(D(t,y)\) by a product of fewer spectral factors would give a reduced recurrence denominator of degree at most three, contradicting the minimal degree-four classification.

At \(y=1\), Proposition~\ref{prop:degenerate-fibers} gives
\[
Z(t,1)=\frac{1}{1-2t},
\qquad
Z_m(1)=2^m.
\]
Equivalently, the only surviving reduced spectral factor is \(1-2t\), or \(\lambda=2\) under the relation \(D(t,y)=t^4\Pi(1/t,y)\).
The remaining assertion is the combination of these two alternatives with the positive-axis cell decomposition \((0,1)\), \(\{1\}\), and \((1,\infty)\) used in Theorem~\ref{thm:consolidated-branch-dominance-package}.
\end{proof}
