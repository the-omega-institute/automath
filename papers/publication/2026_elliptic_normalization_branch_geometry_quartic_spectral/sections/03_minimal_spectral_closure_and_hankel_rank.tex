\section{Recurrence-side spectral closure}\label{sec:hankel}

\subsection{Reduced rationality and minimal recurrence order}

This section records the recurrence facts needed later to pass from the
quartic spectral curve to the coefficient sequence \(Z_m(y)\).
The general reduced-rational statement is included as the reduction needed for denominator-degree and Hankel-rank claims, with the surrounding citations serving only to identify the standard background;
the main theorem chain begins with the balanced elliptic normalization
and the real branch analysis in Sections~\ref{sec:genus-one}--\ref{sec:positive-maximal-modulus-branch}.

Since $Z(t,y)=N(t,y)/D(t,y)$ with $\deg_t D=4$, the sequence $\{Z_m(y)\}$ satisfies a linear recurrence of order at most $4$.
Away from the standard degeneracy set in $y$, there is no numerator--denominator cancellation, so the order is exactly $4$; this is a standard property of reduced rational generating functions \cite{Stanley2012,FlajoletSedgewick2009,Partington1989}.

\begin{lemma}[Reduced rational sequences and tail Hankel rank]\label{lem:reduced-rational-hankel}
Let
\[
A(t)=\sum_{m\ge0}a_m t^m=\frac{P(t)}{Q(t)}\in\CC[[t]],
\qquad
Q(0)=1,
\]
with \(P,Q\in\CC[t]\), \(\gcd(P,Q)=1\), and \(r=\deg Q\ge1\).
Then the least order of a constant-coefficient recurrence satisfied by \(\{a_m\}_{m\ge0}\) for all sufficiently large \(m\) is \(r\).
Moreover, for every integer \(n_0\ge0\) with \(n_0+r>\deg P\), the shifted infinite Hankel matrix
\[
H^{(n_0)}=(a_{n_0+i+j})_{i,j\ge0}
\]
has rank \(r\).
If in addition \(\deg P<r\), then the infinite Hankel matrix \((a_{i+j})_{i,j\ge0}\) has rank \(r\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the reduced-rational-function theorem for linear recurrences, together with Kronecker's finite-rank Hankel criterion in this rational case; we include the proof to fix the exact denominator-degree convention used later \cite[Sec.~4.1]{FlajoletSedgewick2009}, \cite[Chap.~4]{Stanley2012}, \cite[Chap.~1]{Partington1989}.
Write \(Q(t)=1+q_1t+\cdots+q_rt^r\) with \(q_r\ne0\).
The coefficient identity \(Q(t)A(t)=P(t)\) gives
\[
a_m+q_1a_{m-1}+\cdots+q_ra_{m-r}=0
\]
for all \(m>\deg P\), so an order-\(r\) tail recurrence holds.
Conversely, suppose a tail recurrence of order \(s<r\) holds, and put \(L(t)=1+\ell_1t+\cdots+\ell_st^s\) for its monic recurrence polynomial.
Then \(L(t)A(t)\) has only finitely many nonzero coefficients outside its initial polynomial part, so \(L(t)A(t)=B(t)\) is a polynomial.
Thus \(L(t)P(t)=B(t)Q(t)\).
Since \(P\) and \(Q\) are coprime, \(Q\mid L\), impossible because \(\deg L=s<r=\deg Q\).
Hence the tail order is exactly \(r\).

Fix \(n_0\ge0\) with \(n_0+r>\deg P\).
For \(j\ge r\) and \(i\ge0\), the recurrence applies to the coefficient with index \(m=n_0+i+j\).
Thus the \(j\)-th column of \(H^{(n_0)}\) is a linear combination of the preceding \(r\) columns, and \(\operatorname{rank}H^{(n_0)}\le r\).
If the rank were at most \(r-1\), then the first \(r\) columns would be linearly dependent: for some nonzero vector \((c_0,\ldots,c_{r-1})\),
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}c_j a_{n_0+i+j}=0
\qquad (i\ge0).
\]
After dividing by the highest nonzero coefficient and shifting indices, this is a recurrence of order at most \(r-1\) beginning from a finite index, contradicting the minimal tail order just proved.
Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}H^{(n_0)}=r\).

If \(\deg P<r\), then \(n_0=0\) satisfies \(n_0+r>\deg P\), so the ordinary infinite Hankel matrix has rank \(r\).
This last proper-rational case is the only Hankel-rank case used for the generic quartic fibres below; the shifted statement only records how finite polynomial parts are discarded before applying the same denominator-degree argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generic reduced recurrence and Hankel rank]\label{prop:minimal-order}
For \(y\notin\{-1,0,1\}\), the polynomials \(N(t,y)\) and \(D(t,y)\) are coprime in \(\CC[t]\).
Consequently, on this generic locus the minimal linear recurrence order of \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}\) is \(4\), and its Hankel rank is \(4\).
The full recurrence-side closure, including the three exceptional fibres where this generic conclusion fails, is Corollary~\ref{cor:complete-minimal-closure-classification} below.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Appendix~\ref{app:degeneracies} records the resultant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ND-resultant}
\operatorname{Res}_t(N,D)=y^3(y-1)^3(y+1)^6,
\end{equation}
showing that cancellations occur only at \(y\in\{-1,0,1\}\).
For all other $y$, the rational function $Z(t,y)$ is reduced with a denominator of degree $4$ and a numerator of degree less than $4$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduced-rational-hankel} therefore shows both that the recurrence order cannot drop below $4$ and that the Hankel rank is $4$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:recurrence} already displays the order-$4$ relation explicitly.

Let \(H=(Z_{i+j}(y))_{i,j\ge 0}\) be the infinite Hankel matrix.
The preceding paragraph applies the finite-rank part of the lemma to this $H$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Degenerate parameters}

The three excluded fibers are not left implicit.  They are exactly the fibers where the order-$4$ rational presentation ceases to be minimal.

\begin{proposition}[Exact degenerate fibers]\label{prop:degenerate-fibers}
At the exceptional parameters \(y=-1,0,1\), the reduced generating functions, reduced denominators, recurrence orders, and Hankel ranks are as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
y & Z(t,y) & \text{red. denom.} & \text{tail order} & \operatorname{rank} \\
\hline
-1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & \dfrac{1}{1-t} & 1-t & 1 & 1 \\
1 & \dfrac{1}{1-2t} & 1-2t & 1 & 1
\end{array}
\]
Here ``minimal tail order'' means the degree of the reduced denominator, equivalently the order of the minimal constant-coefficient recurrence after the finite polynomial part has been separated.  Thus at \(y=-1\) the sequence is \(Z_0(-1)=1\) and \(Z_m(-1)=0\) for all \(m\ge1\); if one requires a positive-order recurrence beginning at \(m=1\), it is the order-one relation \(Z_m(-1)=0\cdot Z_{m-1}(-1)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct specialization of the polynomials from Section~\ref{subsec:ogf} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
N(t,1)&=-(t-1)(t+1)^2,&
D(t,1)&=(t-1)(t+1)^2(2t-1),\\
N(t,0)&=-(t-1)(t+1),&
D(t,0)&=(t-1)^2(t+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
After cancellation, these give
\[
Z(t,1)=\frac{1}{1-2t},
\qquad
Z(t,0)=\frac{1}{1-t}.
\]
At \(y=-1\), one has
\[
N(t,-1)=D(t,-1)=1-t+t^2-t^3,
\]
and hence \(Z(t,-1)=1\).  These identities prove the entries in the first three columns of the table.

The recurrence order column follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:reduced-rational-hankel}: a reduced denominator of degree \(r\) is the minimal denominator of the sequence after removing any finite polynomial part, and its coefficients give the minimal tail recurrence of order \(r\).  Thus the two geometric sequences at \(y=0\) and \(y=1\) have order one, while the polynomial generating function at \(y=-1\) has tail order zero.

It remains only to compute the Hankel ranks.  For \(y=0\), \(Z_m(0)=1\), so
\[
Z_{i+j}(0)=1=1\cdot1
\]
and the infinite Hankel matrix is nonzero of rank one.  For \(y=1\), \(Z_m(1)=2^m\), so
\[
Z_{i+j}(1)=2^{i+j}=2^i2^j,
\]
again a nonzero rank-one matrix.  For \(y=-1\), the only nonzero coefficient is \(Z_0(-1)=1\), so the Hankel matrix has a single nonzero entry at \((0,0)\), and therefore also has rank one.  This proves the classification.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Complete minimal spectral-closure classification]\label{cor:complete-minimal-closure-classification}
For every parameter \(y\in\CC\), the reduced recurrence order and Hankel rank of \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge0}\) are completely classified as follows.
If \(y\notin\{-1,0,1\}\), then the reduced denominator has degree \(4\), the recurrence has minimal order \(4\), and the Hankel rank is \(4\).
At \(y=0\) and \(y=1\), the reduced denominator has degree \(1\), the sequence is geometric, and the Hankel rank is \(1\).
At \(y=-1\), the generating function is the polynomial \(1\), the tail recurrence order is \(0\), and the Hankel rank is \(1\).
Thus the quartic spectral closure is genuinely four-dimensional precisely off the three exceptional fibres \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The generic statement is Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-order}: outside \(\{-1,0,1\}\), the numerator and denominator are coprime and the degree-four denominator is minimal, so the recurrence order and Hankel rank are both four.
The three exceptional cases are exactly Proposition~\ref{prop:degenerate-fibers}, whose table gives the reduced denominator, tail order, and Hankel rank at \(y=-1,0,1\).
Combining the two disjoint cases covers all \(y\in\CC\), and the final assertion is only the resulting dichotomy between the generic order-four closure and the explicit rank-one degeneracies.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Supporting maximality of the order-four recurrence locus]\label{prop:maximal-order-four-closure-locus}
The open set
\[
\CC\setminus\{-1,0,1\}
\]
is the largest subset of the parameter line on which the following two assertions hold simultaneously for the sequence \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge0}\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item the denominator of the reduced ordinary generating function has degree \(4\), equivalently the minimal tail recurrence order is \(4\);
\item the infinite Hankel matrix \((Z_{i+j}(y))_{i,j\ge0}\) has rank \(4\).
\end{enumerate}
At each point of the complementary skeleton \(\{-1,0,1\}\), the four-dimensional closure collapses to a rank-one Hankel matrix.
More precisely, the collapsed sequences are
\[
Z_m(-1)=\begin{cases}1,&m=0,\\0,&m\ge1,\end{cases}
\qquad
Z_m(0)=1,
\qquad
Z_m(1)=2^m.
\]
Consequently any recurrence-side input to the branch and endpoint arguments must use the order-four statement only on the generic locus and must treat \(y=-1,0,1\) through the explicit rank-one fibres above.
This is a supporting recurrence-side calibration for the elliptic-normalization and endpoint theorem chain, not a headline theorem independent of that chain.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-order} proves both assertions on \(\CC\setminus\{-1,0,1\}\): the resultant identity \eqref{eq:ND-resultant} excludes numerator--denominator cancellation there, and Lemma~\ref{lem:reduced-rational-hankel} converts the unreduced denominator degree \(4\) into minimal tail order and Hankel rank \(4\).

It remains to show that no point can be added to this locus.
Proposition~\ref{prop:degenerate-fibers} computes the three exceptional fibres directly after cancellation:
\[
Z(t,-1)=1,
\qquad
Z(t,0)=\frac1{1-t},
\qquad
Z(t,1)=\frac1{1-2t}.
\]
The coefficient identities displayed in the statement follow immediately from these three reduced generating functions.
For \(y=0\) and \(y=1\), the Hankel matrices factor respectively as
\[
Z_{i+j}(0)=1\cdot1,
\qquad
Z_{i+j}(1)=2^i2^j,
\]
and are nonzero rank-one matrices.
For \(y=-1\), the Hankel matrix has the single nonzero entry \(Z_0(-1)=1\) at \((0,0)\), hence again rank one.
Thus none of the three exceptional parameters satisfies either rank-four assertion, and adding any of them would make the order-four closure statement false.
The final sentence is just this maximality applied to the later branch and endpoint arguments, whose active generic fibres are recorded in Proposition~\ref{prop:spectral-closure}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral closure for branch and endpoint analysis]\label{prop:spectral-closure}
On the positive real axis the open cells used in the dominance proof have the following recurrence-side closure.
For every \(y\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)\), the four roots of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) are all present in the reduced generating function of \(\{Z_m(y)\}\): no proper product of fewer than four factors of \(D(t,y)\) can be a recurrence denominator, and the Hankel rank is \(4\).
At the only positive interior exceptional point, \(y=1\), the sequence reduces to \(Z_m(1)=2^m\), the reduced denominator is \(1-2t\), and the spectral closure collapses to the simple factor \(\lambda=2\).
Moreover, there is a radius \(r>0\) such that, for every \(0<\abs y<r\), all four roots of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) are simple and occur with nonzero partial-fraction coefficients in the spectral decomposition of \(Z_m(y)\).
Consequently, the positive-axis branch and dominance statements of Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance}, and the two colliding endpoint branches used in Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}, are not artifacts of hidden numerator--denominator cancellation.
The only positive collapse is the explicitly isolated geometric fibre at \(y=1\), and the endpoint collapse at \(y=0\) is the controlled double-root limit treated by the endpoint expansion result.
Equivalently, this proposition is the no-missing-spectral-factor result invoked in Proposition~\ref{prop:consolidated-real-branch-classification} and Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-cosine-hypotheses}: the later dominance and endpoint decompositions use every active spectral factor supplied here, except at the two explicitly classified collapse fibres.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \(y\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)\), then \(y\notin\{-1,0,1\}\), so Corollary~\ref{cor:complete-minimal-closure-classification} gives reduced denominator degree four and Hankel rank four.
Since \(D(t,y)=t^4\Pi(1/t,y)\), replacing \(D(t,y)\) by a product of fewer spectral factors would give a reduced recurrence denominator of degree at most three, contradicting the minimal degree-four classification.

At \(y=1\), Proposition~\ref{prop:degenerate-fibers} gives
\[
Z(t,1)=\frac{1}{1-2t},
\qquad
Z_m(1)=2^m.
\]
Equivalently, the only surviving reduced spectral factor is \(1-2t\), or \(\lambda=2\) under the relation \(D(t,y)=t^4\Pi(1/t,y)\).
This proves the positive-axis alternatives and identifies the single positive cancellation fibre.

It remains to record the endpoint specialization.
By Corollary~\ref{cor:disc-factor},
\[
\Disc_\lambda \Pi=-y(y-1)(256y^3+411y^2+165y+32).
\]
Choose \(r>0\) so small that \(r<1\) and
\[
256y^3+411y^2+165y+32\ne0
\qquad (\abs y<r).
\]
Then \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) has four simple roots for \(0<\abs y<r\).
The same punctured disc avoids \(\{-1,0,1\}\), so Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal-order} shows that \(N(t,y)\) and \(D(t,y)\) are coprime there.
For a simple root \(\lambda_j(y)\) of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\), the corresponding pole of \(Z(t,y)\) is at \(t=1/\lambda_j(y)\), because
\[
D(t,y)=t^4\Pi(1/t,y).
\]
If its partial-fraction coefficient were zero, then the numerator would also vanish at that pole, giving a common zero of \(N(\cdot,y)\) and \(D(\cdot,y)\), contrary to coprimality.
Equivalently, in the notation of Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching}, every coefficient in
\[
Z_m(y)=\sum_{j=1}^4 C_j(y)\lambda_j(y)^m
\]
is nonzero on the punctured disc.
The two branches coalescing at \((\lambda,y)=(1,0)\) are therefore genuine active spectral contributions before the limiting coalescence, and their holomorphic limiting amplitudes are the nonzero values computed in Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-expansion}.
This is precisely the recurrence-side spectral closure used by the dominance result and by the fixed-window endpoint zero law: Proposition~\ref{prop:consolidated-real-branch-classification} uses the positive-axis part when passing from real branch data to strict dominance, while Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-cosine-hypotheses} uses the punctured endpoint part before applying the two-branch oscillation lemma.
\end{proof}
