\section{Recurrence-side spectral closure}\label{sec:hankel}\label{sec:minimal-spectral-closure}

\subsection{Reduced rationality and minimal recurrence order}

This section records only the recurrence facts needed later to pass from the
quartic spectral curve to the coefficient sequence \(Z_m(y)\).
The general reduced-rational statement is included as the reduction needed for denominator-degree claims, with the surrounding citations serving only to identify the standard background;
the main theorem chain begins with the balanced elliptic normalization
and the real branch analysis in Sections~\ref{sec:genus-one}--\ref{sec:positive-maximal-modulus-branch}.

Since $Z(t,y)=N(t,y)/D(t,y)$ with $\deg_t D=4$, the sequence $\{Z_m(y)\}$ satisfies a linear recurrence of order at most $4$.
Away from the standard degeneracy set in $y$, there is no numerator--denominator cancellation, so the order is exactly $4$; this is a standard property of reduced rational generating functions \cite{Stanley2012,FlajoletSedgewick2009,Partington1989}.

\begin{lemma}[Reduced rational sequences and tail recurrence order]\label{lem:reduced-rational-hankel}
Let
\[
A(t)=\sum_{m\ge0}a_m t^m=\frac{P(t)}{Q(t)}\in\CC[[t]],
\qquad
Q(0)=1,
\]
with \(P,Q\in\CC[t]\), \(\gcd(P,Q)=1\), and \(r=\deg Q\ge1\).
Then the least order of a constant-coefficient recurrence satisfied by \(\{a_m\}_{m\ge0}\) for all sufficiently large \(m\) is \(r\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the reduced-rational-function theorem for linear recurrences; we include the proof to fix the exact denominator-degree convention used later \cite[Sec.~4.1]{FlajoletSedgewick2009}, \cite[Chap.~4]{Stanley2012}.
Write \(Q(t)=1+q_1t+\cdots+q_rt^r\) with \(q_r\ne0\).
The coefficient identity \(Q(t)A(t)=P(t)\) gives
\[
a_m+q_1a_{m-1}+\cdots+q_ra_{m-r}=0
\]
for all \(m>\deg P\), so an order-\(r\) tail recurrence holds.
Conversely, suppose a tail recurrence of order \(s<r\) holds, and put \(L(t)=1+\ell_1t+\cdots+\ell_st^s\) for its monic recurrence polynomial.
Then \(L(t)A(t)\) has only finitely many nonzero coefficients outside its initial polynomial part, so \(L(t)A(t)=B(t)\) is a polynomial.
Thus \(L(t)P(t)=B(t)Q(t)\).
Since \(P\) and \(Q\) are coprime, \(Q\mid L\), impossible because \(\deg L=s<r=\deg Q\).
Hence the tail order is exactly \(r\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Active factors with multiplicity]\label{lem:active-factors-with-multiplicity}
Let
\[
A(t)=\frac{P(t)}{D(t)},
\qquad
D(t)=\prod_{j=1}^r(1-\lambda_j t)^{e_j},
\]
where the nonzero numbers \(\lambda_j\) are distinct, \(e_j\ge1\), \(D(0)=1\), and \(\gcd(P,D)=1\).  Then for every $j$ the coefficient sequence of $A$ contains a nonzero contribution of the form
\[
Q_j(m)\lambda_j^m,
\qquad
\deg Q_j=e_j-1.
\]
In particular, a repeated non-cancellation fibre contributes the full polynomial-in-$m$ Jordan term rather than merely a simple pole.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The partial-fraction expansion at the pole $t=1/\lambda_j$ has the form
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{e_j}\frac{A_{j,k}}{(1-\lambda_j t)^k}.
\]
If $A_{j,e_j}=0$, then the pole order of $A$ at $1/\lambda_j$ is smaller than $e_j$, so the corresponding factor cancels from the denominator of the reduced rational function.  This contradicts $\gcd(P,D)=1$.  Hence $A_{j,e_j}\ne0$.  Extracting coefficients gives
\[
[t^m]\frac{1}{(1-\lambda_j t)^k}=\binom{m+k-1}{k-1}\lambda_j^m,
\]
so the $j$th pole contributes a polynomial in $m$ of degree $e_j-1$ with nonzero leading coefficient $A_{j,e_j}/(e_j-1)!$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal spectral closure used below]\label{prop:minimal-order}\label{prop:degenerate-fibers}\label{cor:complete-minimal-closure-classification}\label{prop:maximal-order-four-closure-locus}\label{prop:spectral-closure}
The open set \(\CC\setminus\{-1,0,1\}\) is exactly the locus on which the rational function \(Z(t,y)=N(t,y)/D(t,y)\) is reduced with denominator degree \(4\).
On this locus the sequence \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge0}\) has minimal tail recurrence order \(4\); more precisely, each factor of \(D(t,y)=t^4\Pi(1/t,y)\) contributes the full partial-fraction term prescribed by Lemma~\ref{lem:active-factors-with-multiplicity}.
The three cancellation fibres are precisely the following:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
y & N(t,y) & D(t,y) & \text{reduced }Z(t,y) & \text{tail order} \\
\hline
-1 & t^3+t^2-t+1 & t^3+t^2-t+1 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & -(t-1)(t+1) & (t-1)^2(t+1) & \dfrac{1}{1-t} & 1 \\
1 & -(t-1)(t+1)^2 & (t-1)(t+1)^2(2t-1) & \dfrac{1}{1-2t} & 1
\end{array}
\]
Thus the recurrence-side exceptional fibres are recorded once, in the table, and on the positive axis the only interior cancellation fibre is \(y=1\), where the surviving spectral factor is \(1-2t\), equivalently \(\lambda=2\).  This proposition is auxiliary: it identifies the active recurrence spectrum and is not used as endpoint asymptotic input.
Moreover, for some \(r>0\), every \(0<\abs y<r\) has four simple roots of \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\), all occurring with nonzero partial-fraction coefficients in the spectral expansion of \(Z_m(y)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Appendix~\ref{app:degeneracies} records
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ND-resultant}
\operatorname{Res}_t(N,D)=y^3(y-1)^3(y+1)^6.
\end{equation}
Hence cancellation can occur only at \(y=-1,0,1\).
For every other \(y\), \(Z(t,y)\) is reduced with denominator degree \(4\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:reduced-rational-hankel} gives minimal tail order \(4\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:active-factors-with-multiplicity}, applied to the factorization of \(D(t,y)=t^4\Pi(1/t,y)\), gives the stated active contribution for each simple or repeated spectral factor.

The table follows by direct specialization:
\[
\begin{gathered}
N(t,1)=-(t-1)(t+1)^2,
\qquad
D(t,1)=(t-1)(t+1)^2(2t-1),\\
N(t,0)=-(t-1)(t+1),
\qquad
D(t,0)=(t-1)^2(t+1),\\
N(t,-1)=D(t,-1)=1-t+t^2-t^3.
\end{gathered}
\]
After cancellation these give the three displayed reduced generating functions.
Their reduced denominator degrees give the listed tail orders, and the resultant computation shows that no exceptional parameter can be added to the order-four locus.

Finally, by Corollary~\ref{cor:disc-factor},
\[
\Disc_\lambda \Pi=-y(y-1)(256y^3+411y^2+165y+32).
\]
Choose \(r>0\) so small that \(r<1\) and \(256y^3+411y^2+165y+32\ne0\) for \(\abs y<r\).
Then \(\Pi(\cdot,y)\) has four simple roots for \(0<\abs y<r\), and this punctured disc avoids \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
If the partial-fraction coefficient at the pole \(t=1/\lambda_j(y)\) of a simple root vanished, then \(N(\cdot,y)\) and \(D(\cdot,y)\) would have a common zero, contrary to the coprimality already proved.
Thus the endpoint branches used later are genuine active spectral contributions before the controlled coalescence at \(y=0\).
\end{proof}
