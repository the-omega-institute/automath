\section{Proof details, hypotheses, and recent comparison}\label{sec:audit-scope-comparison}

This appendix collects supplementary details for the fixed-object theorem chain: the nonformal hypotheses that enter the main results, the exact identities on which the two deepest proofs depend, and the closest recent \emph{Indagationes Mathematicae} comparisons.

\subsection{Two detailed proof checks}\label{subsec:proof-audit}

The two deepest arguments are Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance}, because it turns the elliptic branch geometry into a global maximal-modulus statement, and Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}, because it turns a local algebraic collision into a fixed-window zero count.

\paragraph{Audit of Theorem~\ref{thm:canonical-branch-dominance}.}
The proof invokes the positive branch parametrization, the Sturm count, and a Vieta comparison for the nonreal pair.  The key algebra is the following.
For \(\lambda>1\), the selected sheet is
\[
y_-(\lambda)=\lambda^2-\frac12-\frac12\sqrt{4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1}.
\]
Differentiation gives
\[
y_-'(\lambda)=2\lambda-\frac{3\lambda^2-1}{\sqrt{4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1}}.
\]
Since both sides of
\[
2\lambda\sqrt{4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1}>3\lambda^2-1
\]
are positive for \(\lambda>1\), the squaring step is reversible, and
\[
4\lambda^2(4\lambda^3-4\lambda+1)-(3\lambda^2-1)^2
=(\lambda-1)(\lambda+1)(16\lambda^3-9\lambda^2+1)>0.
\]
Here the last factor is positive because it equals \(8\) at \(1\) and has derivative \(3\lambda(16\lambda-6)>0\) for \(\lambda>1\).  This verifies the monotone bijection stated in Proposition~\ref{prop:positive-maximal-modulus-branch}.

For \(y>1\), the proof produces two positive roots \(1<p<q\), where \(q\) is on the minus sheet.  The Sturm table gives exactly two real roots, so no negative real roots remain.  If the other two roots are \(z,\overline z\), then direct Vieta comparison gives, with \(r=|z|^2\),
\[
z+\overline z=1-p-q
\]
from the \(\lambda^3\)-coefficient, and
\[
-\bigl(pq(z+\overline z)+r(p+q)\bigr)=1
\]
from the \(\lambda\)-coefficient.  Therefore
\[
r=\frac{pq(p+q-1)-1}{p+q}
\]
and
\[
q^2-r
=\frac{q^2(p+q)-pq(p+q-1)+1}{p+q}
=\frac{q(q-p)(p+q)+pq+1}{p+q}>0.
\]
Thus \(|z|<q\).  This is the only product-of-roots identity in the comparison; no identity of the form \(\operatorname{Tr}(AB)=\operatorname{Tr}(A)\operatorname{Tr}(B)\), \(\det(A+B)=\det A+\det B\), or cancellation of a possibly singular operator is used.  The supporting steps are Lemma~\ref{lem:ygt1-no-negative-roots}, Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-axis-dominance-criterion}, and the Sturm count displayed in \eqref{eq:positive-axis-sturm-sign-table-main}.  The denominator wall \(y_0=(17+\sqrt{297})/2\), the positive root of \(y^2-17y-2\), is crossed only by continuity at a non-discriminant value.

For \(0<y<1\), the proof uses the resultant
\[
\Res_x(\Pi(x,y),\Pi(-x,y)-\Pi(x,y))=16y^3(y-1)^2(y+1)
\]
only to exclude opposite real roots of equal modulus.  The strict orientation is then set by evaluating near \(y=1\): \(\Pi(-3/2,1)>0\), \(\Pi(3/2,1)<0\), and \(\Pi(\pm1,y)<0\) for \(y<1\) close to \(1\).  Hence \(r_1\in(-3/2,-1)\) and \(r_4>3/2\) near \(1\), and the excluded equality propagates \(r_4>|r_1|\) through the whole interval, as used in Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-axis-dominance-criterion}.

\paragraph{Audit of Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-zero-quantization}.}
The proof depends on the two colliding branches at \((\lambda,y)=(1,0)\).  Write \(y=s^2\) and
\[
\lambda_\pm(s)=1\pm\frac{s}{\sqrt2}+\frac58s^2\mp\frac{43}{64\sqrt2}s^3+O(s^4).
\]
Expanding \(\log(1+x)=x-x^2/2+x^3/3+O(x^4)\) with
\(x=\pm s/\sqrt2+5s^2/8\mp43s^3/(64\sqrt2)\) gives
\[
\log\lambda_\pm(s)
=\pm\frac{s}{\sqrt2}+\frac38s^2
\mp\frac{217}{192\sqrt2}s^3+O(s^4).
\]
For the plus branch, the cubic coefficient is
\[
-\frac{43}{64\sqrt2}-\frac{5}{8\sqrt2}+\frac{1}{6\sqrt2}
=-\frac{129+120-32}{192\sqrt2}
=-\frac{217}{192\sqrt2},
\]
and the minus branch is obtained by changing the odd signs.  Thus the cubic logarithmic coefficient in Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-puiseux} and Appendix~\ref{app:endpoint-branching} is \(\mp217/(192\sqrt2)\), not \(\mp3/(4\sqrt2)\).

After the endpoint substitution \(s=iu/m\), the cubic logarithmic term contributes
\[
m\,O_K(s^3)=O_K(m^{-2})
\]
uniformly on every fixed compact \(K\subset\CC\).  Hence the first correction in the cosine law uses only
\[
a=\frac1{\sqrt2},\qquad b=\frac38,
\qquad A_*=\frac12,
\qquad B_*=\frac{3\sqrt2}{16}.
\]
The two-branch contribution is
\[
e^{-bu^2/m}\left[2A_*\cos(au)+\frac{2B_*u}{m}\sin(au)+O_K(m^{-2})\right],
\]
which equals
\[
e^{-3u^2/(8m)}
\left[
\cos\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt2}\right)
+\frac{3u}{4\sqrt2\,m}\sin\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt2}\right)+O_K(m^{-2})
\right].
\]
The rewrite as
\[
e^{-3u^2/(8m)}
\left[
\cos\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt2}-\frac{3u}{4\sqrt2\,m}\right)+O_K(m^{-2})\right]
\]
uses only the Taylor expansion of \(\cos(\theta-\delta)\) for \(u\) in a fixed compact set.  The uniform remainder in Proposition~\ref{prop:fixed-window-endpoint-isolation} is a finite local Puiseux/partial-fraction remainder on fixed compact endpoint scales, so the Rouch\'e application is an ordinary compact-uniform perturbation argument; no unproved interchange of a limit with an infinite sum is used.

\subsection{Related work not used as proof input}\label{subsec:missing-related-work}

Several adjacent sources are important context even though their results are not proof inputs for the fixed-object arguments.  The limiting spectra of banded Toeplitz matrices go back to Schmidt--Spitzer \cite{SchmidtSpitzer1960} and are developed further in the modern Toeplitz/equilibrium literature \cite{DuitsKuijlaars2012}.  Analytic combinatorics in several variables gives a general framework for asymptotics of rational functions \cite{PemantleWilson2013}, while the present paper instead uses a one-parameter spectral decomposition and a local two-branch endpoint calculation.  Stability-preserver and Lee--Yang technology, represented for example by Borcea--Br\"and\'en \cite{BorceaBranden2009}, is also adjacent to zero-location questions but is not invoked in the proofs here.

\subsection{Auxiliary consistency checks}\label{subsec:internal-consistency-safeguards}

The theorem chain uses the following checks against standard algebraic pitfalls in recurrence-spectrum arguments.
First, the companion-family cyclic vector is \(e_1\), not \(e_4\): the Krylov determinant in Lemma~\ref{lem:cyclic-companion} is identically \(1\), so the minimal polynomial equals the characteristic polynomial for every \(y\).  Second, the canonical real projection theorem is supported by the finite oval computation before Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-b-real-projection-criterion}: the zeros \(\beta_i\), the ramification abscissae \(-1,1,\alpha\), and the values \(1,0,\ySub\) are computed before the theorem is used.  Third, for \(y>1\), negative real roots are excluded by producing the two positive roots and then applying the Sturm table, rather than by a false monotonicity claim in \(y\).  Fourth, for \(0<y<1\), the comparison between the roots in \((-\infty,-1)\) and \((1,\infty)\) is oriented near \(y=1\) and then propagated by the opposite-modulus resultant.  Fifth, the logarithmic cubic coefficient at the endpoint is \(\mp217/(192\sqrt2)\), and its contribution after \(s=iu/m\) is only \(O_K(m^{-2})\).  Sixth, dominance transfer under normalization is restricted to the weighted dilation \(\lambda=\alpha\Lambda\), \(y=\alpha^2Y_0+\delta\); arbitrary horizontal spectral translations are not used.
