29: \begin{abstract}
30: Formal mathematics projects increasingly use AI agents for drafting,
31: review, theorem search, and manuscript preparation.  Ungated agent output,
32: however, can create theorem-looking but content-poor edits, stale source
33: references, duplicated submission routes, and premature paper promotion.  We
34: report an auditable theory-to-paper pipeline used around a Lean-backed
35: BEDC source workspace and an automath publication workspace.  The
36: pipeline separates source development, intake packets, active paper
37: directories, AI review loops, deterministic gates, and human promotion
38: decisions.  Its central rule is that AI output is advisory: load-bearing
39: mathematical and submission claims must be tied, at the appropriate evidence
40: level, to source paths, command logs, deterministic checks, and human-approved
41: promotion.  Under record-gate soundness, the core interface theorem constructs
42: the finite publication certificates checked by the package verifier from recorded
43: gate-passage entries and explicitly submitted review surfaces; its safety claim is
44: deliberately certificate-level and finite-record only.  It proves neither Lean builds,
45: daemon behaviour, artifact semantics, nor venue acceptance.  The current package
46: then proves schema-relative finite-record certificate exactness for its six-row, path-verified
47: instantiation, while rejecting stronger readings such as external gate
48: implementation soundness, proof-assistant correctness, artifact semantic validity,
49: current execution of every gate, or venue acceptance.  Four case
50: studies illustrate the gates at the path-verified historical-snapshot level: intake
51: isolation, overlap/submitted blocking, fake-extension rejection, and
52: artifact-limitation disclosure.
53: The scoped boundary normal form isolates the five presentation-package weak
54: points---record-gate soundness, imported source-interface evidence, finite
55: witness rows, primary-claim inventories, and theorem inventories---and proves that
56: each supports only its bounded finite-record reading unless a fresh finite upgrade
57: coordinate is supplied.
58: \end{abstract}

60: \section{Problem and contribution}
61: 
62: AI assistance is useful in a formal-mathematics project only if the project can
63: distinguish proposal from evidence.  A generated theorem statement, a legal
64: piece of code, a mechanical expansion, or a lower score in a review rubric does
65: not by itself constitute mathematical discovery.  The practical publication
66: failure modes are equally structural: a route may overlap a submitted sibling,
67: an artifact status file may drift from generated counts, or an intake note may
68: accidentally become a daemon-visible manuscript.
69: 
70: This source is the technical supplement for the presentation-only contribution
71: \emph{An Auditable Theory Compiler for AI-Assisted Formal Mathematics}, whose
72: single primary artifact is the two-page source \path{submission_abstract.tex}.
73: This file is subordinate technical support, not a competing submission title.
74: The final source-level packaging convention used throughout both sources is the
75: following: the primary CICM presentation-only article is
76: \path{submission_abstract.tex}; this file, \path{main.tex}, is the selected
77: technical supplement; \path{theorem_inventory.json} is the selected bounded
78: theorem-obligation inventory; and \path{review_bundle/} is the selected exact
79: supplemental support directory.  No archival replacement is claimed in this
80: source package.  The short artifact states the workshop-facing thesis and
81: boundary; this supplement gives the finite-record definitions, theorem proofs,
82: and case-evidence tables; and the supplemental evidence directory supplies only
83: the finite records named in the bundle-dependent claims when that exact support
84: surface is uploaded with matching final digests.  This
85: file records a compact workflow pattern for avoiding these failures: an auditable
86: theory-to-paper pipeline in which AI agents propose, review, and triage, while
87: deterministic gates decide what may be treated as load-bearing evidence.  The
88: pattern is instantiated in local BEDC and automath workspaces.
89: The central mathematical/software conclusion of the supplement is a typed
90: finite-record certificate calculus for publication claims under record-gate
91: soundness.  Its core chain is Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface}
92: (the finite certificate tuple and gate dispatch),
93: Definition~\ref{def:record-gate-soundness-external-interface} and
94: Proposition~\ref{prop:record-gate-soundness-external-interface-exactness} (the
95: standalone record-gate input/output and non-claim boundary),
96: Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table} (the six finite
97: acceptance predicates), Theorems~\ref{thm:verifier-input-output-contract},
98: \ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness}, and
99: \ref{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary} (schema-projection exactness
100: and non-escalation), Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface} (finite
101: publication-certificate invariants from explicitly supplied record-gate
102: soundness),
103: Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-record-certificate-rigidity} (the sharp bad-subrecord
104: normal form for every stronger reading),
105: Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-axis-non-escalation-exactness} (simultaneous exactness
106: of the source-interface, implementation-soundness, artifact-semantic, and
107: theorem-ledger boundaries), and
108: Theorem~\ref{thm:presentation-scoped-boundary-normal-form} (the five-component
109: presentation-route boundary normal form),
110: Theorem~\ref{thm:current-package-certificate-exactness} (the current package's
111: schema-relative finite-record instance), and
112: Theorem~\ref{thm:current-package-extension-coordinate-rigidity} (the exact
113: absence of current implementation-soundness, fresh-rerun, artifact-semantic, and

115: Theorem~\ref{thm:manual-ledger-dependence-boundary} (the current package's

117: digests, upload conditions, command boundaries, inventories, venue records, and
118: case snapshots are support-boundary audit claims: they are useful finite-record
119: consequences, but they are not mathematical invariants of independent interest

121: intentionally not a proof of external implementation-level soundness:
122: Lean builds, publication-daemon behaviour, artifact semantics, and fresh command
123: runs enter the argument only when a finite certificate records the corresponding
124: evidence surface and boundary.  Root-level diagnostic files outside the submitted
125: primary article, this supplement, and the uploaded-or-equivalent review bundle
126: are treated below only as local historical or path-verified context unless a
127: displayed certificate explicitly places the corresponding record on the submitted
128: support surface.  The CICM presentation-only submission uses the short artifact
129: plus bibliography; this supplement and \path{review_bundle/} are
130: evidence-support materials rather than competing primary submissions.

132: \paragraph{Scope and theoremization boundary.}
133: The supplement is deliberately limited to auditable theory-to-paper workflow
134: claims for formal mathematics.  It does not propose a theorem prover, a Lean
135: hammer, a complete BEDC theory, an automated journal-acceptance method, or a
136: claim that AI output is proof evidence.  Some logistical facts are stated in
137: proposition or theorem environments because they are finite-record consequences
138: used by the certificate calculus; those statements should be read as conditional
139: audit-record claims over printed paths, tables, manifests, and logs.  The
140: mathematical obligations are discharged by the displayed TeX definitions,
141: theorems, and proofs in the core chain, not by the theorem inventory, upload
142: bookkeeping, digest records, or venue-boundary records.

144: \begin{proposition}[Final package-shape convention]
145: \label{prop:final-package-shape-convention}
146: For the current presentation package there is a single source-level package
147: shape.  The primary article is \path{submission_abstract.tex}; the selected
148: technical supplement is \path{main.tex}; the selected bounded theorem-obligation
149: ledger is \path{theorem_inventory.json}; and the selected supplemental support
150: surface is exact inclusion of \path{review_bundle/} with the final digest record
151: \path{review_bundle/FINAL_DIGESTS_SHA256.md}.  No archive locator, DOI-bearing
152: replacement, or alternate supplemental directory is part of the current package
153: convention.  Consequently every bundle-backed support claim in this source is
154: accepted only under exact inclusion of that directory with matching final digests;
155: without that external inclusion record it remains a local package-support claim.
156: \end{proposition}
157: 
158: \begin{proof}
159: The assertion is a finite record comparison.  The primary source names
160: \path{submission_abstract.tex} as the primary presentation article and states
161: that \path{main.tex}, \path{theorem_inventory.json}, and \path{review_bundle/}
162: are supplemental support surfaces.  The opening package paragraph of this
163: supplement names the same four coordinates and explicitly selects exact
164: supplemental inclusion rather than an archive replacement.  The digest manifest
165: \path{review_bundle/FINAL_DIGESTS_SHA256.md} records the same branch: its support
166: mode is conditional exact supplemental inclusion of \path{review_bundle/}, and
167: its archive-replacement fields state that no archive replacement or locator is
168: claimed.  These three finite source records therefore agree on one package
169: shape.
170: 
171: It remains only to identify the boundary of the convention.  A source-level
172: package-shape record fixes which files the manuscript treats as primary,
173: supplemental, and support material; it does not by itself prove that a venue
174: upload has occurred.  Hence bundle-backed claims are admissible externally only
175: after the selected exact support directory is actually supplied with the digests
176: listed in the final manifest.  If that external inclusion record is absent, the
177: finite coordinates still exist locally, but the submitted-support conclusion is
178: not instantiated.  Since the same finite records contain no archive locator, no
179: archive replacement can be inferred from the source.  This proves both the
180: single package convention and its conditional external boundary.
181: \end{proof}

183: \begin{theorem}[Nested six-row and seven-row package states]
184: \label{thm:nested-package-states}
185: Let $P^{+}_{6}$ denote the current row-frozen certificate state whose accepted
186: rows are exactly $a_1,\ldots,a_6$ in
187: Table~\ref{tab:current-acceptance-ledger}.  Let $P^{+}_{7}$ denote the enlarged
188: submitted support state obtained by adjoining the single theorem-ledger
189: row $a_7$ whose submitted coordinate is \path{theorem_inventory.json}.  Then
190: $P^{+}_{6}\subset P^{+}_{7}$ as finite record surfaces, and the inclusion is
191: conservative for the first six certificates: every acceptance decision, evidence
192: level, assigned-gate family, discovery-interface field, and command-boundary
193: field of rows $a_1,\ldots,a_6$ is the same in $P^{+}_{6}$ and $P^{+}_{7}$.  The
194: only new accepted claim of $P^{+}_{7}$ is the bounded theorem-obligation
195: ledger claim with syntactic label coverage for $a_7$.  In particular the two states are nested package states,
196: not competing final ledgers: $P^{+}_{6}$ is the exact six-row current-package
197: certificate state used by Theorem~\ref{thm:current-package-certificate-exactness},
198: while $P^{+}_{7}$ is the submitted theorem-ledger extension used by
199: Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem-obligation-ledger-boundary}.
200: \end{theorem}
201: 
202: \begin{proof}
203: The tables and support records are finite, so the assertion is checked by
204: coordinates.  By definition, $P^{+}_{6}$ contains the primary article, this
205: supplement, the six accepted rows $a_1,\ldots,a_6$, their certificate fields, and
206: the conditional review-bundle support surface named in
207: Proposition~\ref{prop:current-package-acceptance-certificate-ledger}.  The state
208: $P^{+}_{7}$ is formed by adding one additional row, $a_7$, together with the
209: manifest exposure of \path{theorem_inventory.json}.  No row $a_i$ with
210: $1\leq i\leq 6$ is edited in this construction, and no field of its certificate
211: is replaced: the same finite tuple $(K_i,\ell_i,E_i,H_i,R_i,D_i,B_i)$ is read by
212: Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table} in both states.  Hence
213: Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness} gives the same acceptance decision
214: for each of the six rows in both states.
215: 
216: The added coordinate has a different declared role.  Its accepted claim is not a
217: new mathematical proof, a proof-assistant rebuild, or an extractor-completeness
218: claim; it is the obligation-ledger traceability claim with syntactic label coverage
219: later classified in Proposition~\ref{prop:manual-theorem-inventory-boundary}.  Since the finite
220: record added to pass from $P^{+}_{6}$ to $P^{+}_{7}$ is exactly this new row and
221: its manifest exposure, every accepted claim outside $a_7$ is inherited unchanged
222: from $P^{+}_{6}$, while $a_7$ supplies only the bounded theorem-ledger coordinate.
223: This proves both the nesting and the non-competition statement.
224: \end{proof}
