1558: \begin{proposition}[Deterministic claim-kind dispatch]
1559: \label{prop:claim-kind-dispatch}
1560: For every load-bearing manuscript claim whose kind lies in the seven-kind domain
1561: above, $\Gamma$ assigns a unique finite set of deterministic gates.  In a
1562: pipeline that is record-gate-sound for acceptance, a mixed load-bearing claim may be
1563: used as accepted work only if its acceptance entry records passage through every
1564: gate in the union assigned to its component kinds.

1567: \begin{proof}
1568: The seven atomic clauses in the definition of $\Gamma$ assign one explicitly named
1569: finite gate set to each atomic claim kind.  Hence an atomic claim has a unique
1570: assigned gate set.  For a mixed claim, the component-kind set is finite because
1571: it is a subset of the seven-kind domain; taking the union of the corresponding
1572: finite gate sets is deterministic and finite.  Record-gate-soundness for acceptance
1573: requires the acceptance entry of a load-bearing claim to record passage through
1574: every gate assigned by $\Gamma$.  If a component gate of a mixed claim were
1575: omitted, then the projection of the claim to that component kind would lack its
1576: assigned pass record.  Such an entry would not be record-gate-sound.  Therefore accepted
1577: mixed claims in a record-gate-sound pipeline must pass every gate in the union.\end{proof}

1579: \begin{theorem}[Mixed-claim projection obstruction]
1580: \label{thm:mixed-claim-projection-obstruction}
1581: Let $c$ be a load-bearing mixed claim with nonempty component-kind set
1582: $K\subseteq\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)$ and submitted candidate certificate
1583: $\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$.  For a nonempty subset $J\subseteq K$, write
1584: \[
1585: G_J=\bigcup_{k\in J}\Gamma(k)
1586: \]
1587: and let $c_J$ denote the projected reading of $c$ that keeps exactly the
1588: claim-kind components in $J$ and keeps the same declared evidence level,
1589: evidence surface, promotion boundary, and command boundary, except that the
1590: discovery field is required precisely when discovery lies in $J$.  Then the
1591: following are equivalent for the projected reading $c_J$:
1592: \begin{enumerate}
1593: \item the finite verifier accepts the projected certificate
1594: $\mathcal C_J=(J,\ell,E,H,R|_{G_J},D_J,B)$, where $R|_{G_J}$ is the restriction of
1595: $R$ to rows whose gate name lies in $G_J$ and $D_J$ is $D$ exactly when discovery
1596: lies in $J$ and is absent otherwise;
1597: \item every gate $g\in G_J$ has in $R$ a pass record with the five required
1598: fields and with an allowed evidence pointer, the retained boundary fields $E$,
1599: $H$, $D_J$, and $B$ satisfy the corresponding schema clauses at level $\ell$, and
1600: $J\subseteq\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)$.
1601: \end{enumerate}
1602: Consequently, if an acceptance entry for $c$ records passage only through a
1603: proper gate subset $Q\subset\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)$, then every component
1604: subset $J$ with $\neg(G_J\subseteq Q)$ has an inclusion-minimal finite bad subrecord,
1605: namely a missing pass record for some gate in $G_J\setminus Q$.  In particular,
1606: no passage through route, submission, scheduler, artifact, traceability,
1607: formal-source, or discovery gates can certify a mixed claim's remaining component
1608: unless the pass rows assigned to that remaining component are also present.
1609: \end{theorem}
1610: 
1611: \begin{proof}
1612: The projected certificate $\mathcal C_J$ is a certificate of the same schema as
1613: Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table}, with claim-kind set
1614: $J$ in place of $K$ and with gate field restricted to the finite set $G_J$.
1615: Because $J\subseteq K\subseteq\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)$, Proposition~\ref{prop:claim-kind-dispatch}
1616: assigns exactly the finite gate set $G_J$ to the projected reading.  Applying the
1617: schema-relative exactness theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness},
1618: to $\mathcal C_J$ gives the equivalence: acceptance is exactly satisfaction of
1619: the six finite schema clauses for the retained fields.  Clause A1 is the domain
1620: condition $J\subseteq\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)$ and $\ell\in\mathsf{Lev}$; clause A2
1621: checks the evidence surface $E$; clause A3 checks that each gate in $G_J$ has a
1622: pass record in $R|_{G_J}$ with an allowed pointer; clause A4 checks the retained
1623: promotion boundary $H$; clause A5 checks precisely the retained discovery field
1624: $D_J$; and clause A6 checks the retained command boundary $B$.  These are exactly
1625: the conditions in item~2, so items~1 and~2 are equivalent.
1626: 
1627: Now suppose the row for the mixed claim records only pass rows with gate names in
1628: a proper subset $Q$ of the full assigned gate set.  Choose a nonempty
1629: $J\subseteq K$ with $\neg(G_J\subseteq Q)$ and choose $g\in G_J\setminus Q$.  The
1630: projected verifier for $c_J$ must check $g$ by clause A3, because $g$ is assigned
1631: to the retained component-kind set $J$.  No row for $g$ is present among the
1632: recorded gate names in $Q$, so clause A3 fails.  The verifier reads only the
1633: finite fields consisting of $J$, $\Gamma$, the absent gate name $g$, and the gate
1634: row field $R$; deleting fields not read by this failed check leaves an
1635: inclusion-minimal bad subrecord of the second obstruction type in
1636: Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton}.  Since the argument
1637: uses only membership in the assigned gate set, it applies uniformly to each of
1638: the seven atomic gate interfaces.  Thus one component's gate passage cannot be
1639: substituted for another component's missing assigned pass rows in a mixed
1640: load-bearing claim.\end{proof}

These are exactly the fields demanded by the implementation-soundness audit tuple in item~3 of Theorem~\ref{thm:assigned-gate-soundness-audit-boundary}, restricted to the four named scripts.  Hence the enlarged coordinate is available for those finite predicates.
1723: 
1724: Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface} with this enlarged coordinate changes only the command-run support available for the listed finite checks.  The finite predicates do not interpret theorem proofs, run Lean or BEDC, evaluate artifacts, contact an upload system, resolve an archive, or read a venue decision.  Such claims would require additional claim kinds, evidence surfaces, pass records, and boundary fields under Theorem~\ref{thm:implementation-soundness-extension-criterion}.  Therefore the instantiated record-gate support has exactly the boundary stated in the corollary.
1725: \end{proof}
1726: \begin{theorem}[Assigned-gate soundness audit boundary]
1727: \label{thm:assigned-gate-soundness-audit-boundary}
1728: Let $P$ be a finite submitted package with pass-record ledger $L$, dispatch
1729: table $\Gamma$, and submitted support surface $S$.  For a load-bearing claim
1730: $c$, write $G(c)=\bigcup_{k\in K(c)}\Gamma(k)$ for the finite set of gates
1731: assigned to its claim-kind set.  The package may use a ledger row for $c$ as a
1732: sound record-gate witness only through the finite predicate
1733: $\mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)$ of Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface}.
1734: Equivalently, the available finite-record strength is exactly the following:
1735: \begin{enumerate}
1736: \item for every $g\in G(c)$ the submitted ledger contains a pass row for $g$,
1737: including the gate name, input surface, decision, evidence pointer, and evidence
1738: level;
1739: \item every evidence pointer named by those pass rows denotes a submitted path,
1740: manifest row, theorem/proposition/table label, uploaded bundle file, or explicitly
1741: declared historical boundary at the evidence level stated in the row; and
1742: \item if the manuscript asserts any stronger proposition that the underlying gate
1743: implementation was independently sound, freshly executed, or semantically correct
1744: beyond the recorded finite schema check, the submitted package also contains a
1745: gate-soundness audit tuple for that stronger proposition: source path, command,
1746: source state, environment, exit code, and log path for the assigned gate; for the
1747: four named finite-record scripts this tuple has exactly the scope proved in
1748: Theorem~\ref{thm:concrete-finite-script-implementation-soundness}.
1749: \end{enumerate}
1750: Consequently, in the absence of the audit tuple in (3), record-gate soundness is
1751: an explicit hypothesis of the publication-safety theorem, not a theorem proved by
1752: the current package.  The finite pass rows prove only the package-level statement
1753: that no accepted ledger claim omits an assigned gate row or its declared
1754: submitted evidence surface.
1755: \end{theorem}
1756: 
1757: \begin{proof}
1758: The dispatch table has finite values by Proposition~\ref{prop:claim-kind-dispatch};
1759: hence $G(c)$ is finite.  A submitted pass-record ledger and support surface are
1760: also finite.  The predicate $\mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)$ therefore expands to a
1761: finite conjunction of row and pointer tests: for each accepted load-bearing claim
1762: it supplies a certificate satisfying $V(c,\mathcal C)=1$, and for each gate name
1763: in $G(c)$ it searches the rows attached to $c$ and compares the finite fields
1764: gate name, input surface, decision, evidence pointer, and evidence level.  If
1765: one of these rows is missing, the certificate contains the second bad subrecord
1766: of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton}, namely a missing
1767: pass record for an assigned gate.  If a row is present, the verifier has only the
1768: finite string and path fields carried by that row.
1769: 
1770: The second condition is exactly the local-checkability condition for the row's
1771: evidence pointer.  A pointer to a path, manifest row, theorem/proposition/table
1772: label, uploaded bundle file, or explicit historical boundary is a coordinate of
1773: the submitted finite record surface.  If the pointer does not denote one of
1774: those finite surfaces at the stated evidence level, the certificate has the first
1775: bad subrecord of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton}, a
1776: missing evidence surface.  Thus conditions (1) and (2) are precisely the
1777: submitted-row and submitted-pointer clauses of $\mathsf{RGS}_{S}$, and they are
1778: precisely what the package can verify from $L$ and the submitted surfaces.
1779: 
1780: The third condition concerns a different claim kind.  External implementation
1781: soundness, fresh execution, and semantic correctness assert facts about the
1782: operation of a gate outside the row's finite fields.  By the evidence-level
1783: definitions and by Theorem~\ref{thm:executable-record-non-escalation}, a
1784: command-result claim is not supplied by a path-verified pass row; it requires a
1785: command tuple with source state, environment, exit code, and log path.  By
1786: Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary}, two worlds that have
1787: identical submitted pass rows but different unsubmitted gate implementations are
1788: indistinguishable to the finite verifier.  Hence no proof from (1) and (2) can
1789: establish the stronger external-gate proposition.  The stronger proposition is
1790: accepted only after the package is enlarged by the audit tuple in (3).  Without
1791: that enlargement, record-gate soundness remains the explicit finite predicate
1792: $\mathsf{RGS}_{S}$, while the proved package fact is exactly the absence of
1793: missing assigned-gate rows and missing declared evidence surfaces.\end{proof}

1795: \begin{theorem}[Finite certificate obstruction skeleton]
1796: \label{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton}
1797: Let $c$ be a load-bearing manuscript claim whose component kinds lie in the
1798: seven-kind domain.  The verifier of
1799: Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier} gives the following
1800: complete audit classification at the declared evidence level.  A certificate for
1801: $c$ passes the publication-safety interface if and only if it has no bad
1802: subrecord of the following five types:
1803: \begin{enumerate}
1804: \item missing evidence surface for the declared evidence level;
1805: \item missing pass record for a gate in $\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)$;
1806: \item missing human-promotion record when the claim crosses from intake or
1807: source material into active publication work;
1808: \item discovery escalation without the pinned discovery-gate source-interface
1809: rows for certificate, ledger, lineage, and boundary witnesses; or
1810: \item command-run escalation without command, source commit, environment, exit
1811: code, and log path.
1812: \end{enumerate}
1813: Equivalently, every unsafe accepted claim contains an inclusion-minimal finite
1814: bad subrecord of one of these five types, and deleting all other fields preserves
1815: the same rejection by the verifier.  This is an audit-skeleton theorem for the
1816: software-workshop route: it classifies finite certificate failures and makes no
1817: claim about theorem-proving complexity or about fresh execution of the
1818: underlying workflow.
1819: \end{theorem}
1820: 
1821: \begin{proof}
1822: Let $K$ be the finite set of component kinds of $c$.  Since $K$ is a subset of
1823: the seven-kind domain, Proposition~\ref{prop:claim-kind-dispatch} gives a unique
1824: finite required gate set
1825: \[
1826: G(c)=\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k).
1827: \]
1828: The certificate field $R$ is indexed by exactly this finite set, so checking
1829: gate passage is a finite lookup: each $g\in G(c)$ must have a pass record and the
1830: record must cite an item of the finite evidence surface $E$.  The evidence-level
1831: field $\ell$ and boundary field $B$ make the same lookup finite for evidence
1832: strength: at path-verified level the certificate must name the source, artifact,
1833: pinned commit, local publication path, or gate-decision surface used by the
1834: claim; at command-run level it must additionally name command, source commit,
1835: environment, exit code, and log path.  Neither lookup interprets artifact
1836: semantics, reruns Lean, or predicts editorial outcomes; those are stronger claim
1837: kinds or stronger evidence levels that require their own records.  The human-promotion field $H$ separates
1838: the route boundary from all other gates: it is either a concrete promotion
1839: record or a finite marker that no such boundary is crossed.  Finally, when
1840: discovery lies in $K$, the discovery field $D$ names the pinned source-interface
1841: rows certified by Lemma~\ref{lem:source-interface-extraction-correctness} and
1842: Propositions~\ref{prop:reviewable-source-snapshot-interface} and
1843: \ref{prop:source-interface-record-file-consistency}; these rows are finite and
1844: therefore locally checkable in the same way as the other certificate fields.
1845: Thus the certificate is sufficient for a reviewer to check safety by examining a
1846: finite list of fields and cited artifacts, not by trusting generated prose.
1847: 
1848: It remains to prove that the displayed obstruction list is complete.  Suppose an
1849: accepted claim fails the publication-safety interface.  Choose an inclusion-minimal
1850: subrecord of its acceptance entry that still witnesses failure.  The certificate
1851: schema has exactly seven coordinates, and the first two of them are not extra
1852: obstruction families.  The claim-kind coordinate $K$ only determines the finite
1853: gate set $G(c)$ through $\Gamma$; once $K$ lies in the seven-kind domain, any
1854: failure caused by that determination is a missing assigned gate and hence type~2.
1855: The evidence-level coordinate $\ell$ only chooses between the path-verified and
1856: command-run interpretations of the boundary field; its failures therefore appear
1857: either as absent evidence at the declared level, type~1, or as absent command-run
1858: coordinates, type~5.  Thus every possible failure must be witnessed in one of
1859: the five substantive schema fields $E,R,H,D,B$.
1860: 
1861: If the failing field is the evidence surface $E$, the claim is not tied to a
1862: submitted path, bundle path, manifest row, log path, pinned commit, or explicit
1863: historical boundary at the declared level.  The minimal subrecord is therefore
1864: type~1.  If the failing field is the gate-record field $R$, some gate in the
1865: deterministic finite set $G(c)$ has no pass record, no decision, or no evidence
1866: pointer lying in the submitted surface; this is type~2.  If the failing field is
1867: the human-promotion field $H$, then all non-route evidence may be present but the
1868: claim has crossed from intake or source material into active publication work
1869: without the required human promotion marker; this is type~3.  If the failing
1870: field is the discovery field $D$, then a discovery or classifier-shift reading is
1871: being inferred from statement-only, code-only, mechanical, compression-only, or
1872: classifier-scored material without the pinned certificate, ledger, lineage, and
1873: boundary rows; this is type~4.  Finally, if the failing field is the boundary
1874: field $B$, the record either raises path-verified evidence to command-run
1875: strength without command, source commit, environment, exit code, and log path, or
1876: omits the explicit no-command-run boundary; this is type~5.
1877: 
1878: The preceding paragraph exhausts the schema fields: $K$ and $\ell$ reduce to the
1879: same five substantive checks, and $E,R,H,D,B$ are precisely the five displayed
1880: obstruction types.  Minimality removes all unread fields and leaves exactly one
1881: of the five obstruction skeletons.  Conversely, if no bad subrecord of type~1--5
1882: is present, then each schema check in
1883: Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier} succeeds: the evidence
1884: surface is present at the declared level, every required gate has its recorded
1885: pass, the route-boundary field is correct, discovery rows are present exactly
1886: when required, and command-run strength is not asserted without command-run
1887: fields.  Hence the verifier accepts the certificate and the publication-safety
1888: interface holds for $c$ at the declared evidence level.
1889: 
1890: The final sentence follows from the construction.  The verifier reads only the
1891: finite certificate fields and the finite dispatch table, so the theorem
1892: classifies certificate failures for review.  It does not assert a lower bound,
1893: complexity separation, automated theorem-proving method, or fresh run of any
1894: external command.\end{proof}

1896: \begin{theorem}[Finite extension-budget normal form]
1897: \label{thm:finite-extension-budget-normal-form}
1898: Fix the dispatch table $\Gamma$, the finite certificate schema, and an accepted
1899: finite certificate
1900: \(\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\) for a load-bearing package claim $c$.
1901: Let $c^{+}$ be a proposed stronger reading of the same row which is not
1902: record-equivalent to $c$ in the sense of
1903: Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary}.  Then $c^{+}$ is
1904: accepted by the publication-safety interface if and only if the submitted finite
1905: record surface contains an extension record in the following normal form:
1906: \[
1907: \Delta(c,c^{+})=(\Delta K,\Delta E,\Delta H,\Delta R,\Delta D,\Delta B),
1908: \]
1909: where the nonempty coordinates are exactly the coordinates needed by the stronger
1910: reading:
1911: \begin{enumerate}
1912: \item $\Delta K$ names every additional claim-kind component asserted by
1913: $c^{+}$;
1914: \item $\Delta E$ supplies the submitted evidence surfaces for those added
1915: components at the declared evidence level;
1916: \item $\Delta R$ supplies pass records for every gate in
1917: \(\bigcup_{k\in K\cup\Delta K}\Gamma(k)\) not already supplied by $R$;
1918: \item $\Delta H$ supplies any newly asserted promotion, route, venue, or external
1919: instantiation boundary;
1920: \item $\Delta D$ supplies the pinned discovery-source rows whenever the stronger
1921: reading adds discovery, classifier-shift, or theorem-content-delta content; and
1922: \item $\Delta B$ supplies the command, source-state, environment, exit-code, and
1923: log-path tuple whenever the stronger reading raises a path-verified row to
1924: command-run strength.
1925: \end{enumerate}
1926: Equivalently, every genuine strengthening has positive finite extension budget:
1927: at least one coordinate of \(\Delta(c,c^{+})\) must be nonempty, and every
1928: nonempty obstruction class from
1929: Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton} must be paid by the
1930: corresponding coordinate of \(\Delta(c,c^{+})\).  Without this extension record,
1931: the stronger reading has an inclusion-minimal bad subrecord and remains outside
1932: the certificate constructed in
1933: Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}.
1934: \end{theorem}
1935: 
1936: \begin{proof}
1937: First suppose that the extension record is present.  Enlarge the certificate by
1938: replacing its fields with
1939: \[
1940: (K\cup\Delta K,\ell,E\cup\Delta E,H\cup\Delta H,
1941: R\cup\Delta R,D\cup\Delta D,B\cup\Delta B),
1942: \]
1943: where the unions mean finite row union with the original row identifiers retained.
1944: The six clauses of Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table} are
1945: then exactly the six requirements displayed in the statement.  Clause~A1 reads
1946: the enlarged kind set and evidence level.  Clause~A2 reads the enlarged evidence
1947: surface.  Clause~A3 reads the enlarged gate-pass field and checks every gate
1948: assigned by $\Gamma$ to the enlarged kind set.  Clause~A4 reads the enlarged
1949: promotion and boundary field.  Clause~A5 reads the enlarged discovery-interface
1950: field exactly when discovery is present in the enlarged kind set.  Clause~A6
1951: reads the enlarged evidence-boundary field and accepts command-run strength only
1952: when the command tuple is present.  Therefore
1953: Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness} gives
1954: \(\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}(c^{+},\mathcal C\cup\Delta)\), and the finite interface
1955: accepts the stronger reading at precisely the declared evidence level.
1956: 
1957: Conversely, suppose $c^{+}$ is accepted.  Since $c^{+}$ is not
1958: record-equivalent to $c$, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary}
1959: implies that some finite coordinate read by the verifier differs from the
1960: coordinate of \(\mathcal C(c)\).  Collect the differences in the six displayed
1961: components: additional kinds in $\Delta K$, additional submitted surfaces in
1962: $\Delta E$, additional gate rows in $\Delta R$, additional promotion or external
1963: boundary fields in $\Delta H$, additional discovery rows in $\Delta D$, and
1964: additional evidence-boundary or command-run data in $\Delta B$.  These are finite
1965: because the verifier reads only finite records and the dispatch table has finite
1966: domain.  Acceptance of $c^{+}$ forces each changed coordinate to satisfy the
1967: corresponding clause A1--A6, so the collected difference record has the normal
1968: form stated above.
1969: 
1970: It remains only to prove positivity and obstruction completeness.  If every
1971: coordinate of \(\Delta(c,c^{+})\) were empty, then $c$ and $c^{+}$ would induce the
1972: same values on all verifier coordinates and would be record-equivalent, contrary
1973: to hypothesis.  Thus any genuine strengthening has positive finite extension
1974: budget.  If some nonempty obstruction class is not supplied by its corresponding
1975: coordinate, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton} identifies
1976: the first failed finite field as one of the five bad-subrecord types: missing
1977: evidence surface, missing gate record, missing promotion or external boundary,
1978: missing discovery rows, or unsupported command-run escalation.  Removing all
1979: unread fields leaves an inclusion-minimal bad subrecord.  Hence a stronger
1980: reading is accepted exactly when its finite extension budget is paid in submitted
1981: coordinates, and otherwise remains outside the original certificate.\end{proof}

1983: \begin{proposition}[Gate-interface separation]
1984: \label{prop:gate-interface-separation}
1985: The promotion, overlap, submission-state, and theorem-content delta gates are
1986: separate deterministic interfaces.  None can be replaced by passage of another
1987: one when a load-bearing claim has the corresponding component.
1988: \end{proposition}
1989: 
1990: \begin{proof}
1991: Each named gate has a distinct invariant and evidence surface in the operational
1992: definition.  Promotion asks whether a candidate has crossed the human
1993: publication boundary; its evidence is the active-paper directory and pipeline
1994: metadata.  Overlap asks whether the route duplicates or conflicts with a sibling
1995: route; its evidence is route identity, overlap ledgers, submitted-sibling paths,
1996: and board state.  Submission-state asks whether the proposed publication action
1997: is compatible with submission markers, venue state, and submission-pack metadata.
1998: The theorem-content delta gate asks whether an asserted extension contains
1999: load-bearing theorem growth; its evidence is an inventory, hard-lint surface, or
2000: delta record.  These predicates are logically independent at the interface
2001: level: an active paper can still overlap a submitted sibling, a route with no
2002: overlap can still have an impermissible submission state, and a compiling edit in
2003: a permissible route can still lack theorem-content delta.  Therefore a
2004: load-bearing claim with any one of these components must pass that component's
2005: own gate, and passage through the others cannot supply the missing evidence.\end{proof}
