\begin{abstract}
Generated formal-mathematics artifacts need a publication interface that records
which finite evidence rows support which public claims.  We present a
publication-coordinate design pattern and finite checker specification for a
concrete \emph{newmath}/\emph{automath} theory-to-paper package.  The submission
is not an evaluation of tool performance, a formalization-success report, or a
broad mathematical result; its public surface is the finite checker and
replay interface.  An
optional mathematical route is
parked in the supplement as a conditional typed appendix: Richardson
consequences require separately proved semantic rows \((P,S,A)\), concrete
printed catalogue names require the additional \((HW)\) hypothesis, and exact
height formulae require the additional \((\mathrm{EKMP})\) premise.

\textbf{Contributions.}  First, the paper specifies six publication roles:
source object, formal interface, finite evidence row, advisory agent action,
deterministic gate, and human promotion boundary.  Second, it packages the
submitted bytes through a replay-kernel checker consisting of a byte-to-atom
compiler, fixed Horn schema, replayable certificate, replay report, lexical
environment-token ledger, and digest bundle.  Third, this finite package
accepts exactly two positive coordinates.  The first is the public coordinate
\(\qlex\), whose legacy artifact spelling is \(\texttt{qinv}\); it is read only
as lexical environment-token coverage for the artifact appendix's declared five
raw \LaTeX{} environment-token names.  It is not theorem-inventory coverage,
theorem-dependency analysis, proof checking, scope adequacy, audit-record
completeness, or semantic theorem completeness.
The second coordinate is bounded fixed-replay record-gate
soundness.  Fourth, case rows for intake
isolation, overlap blocking, fake-extension rejection, and artifact-limitation
disclosure illustrate the method at a historical, path-complete snapshot level;
the submitted command \path{python certificates/replay_case_rows.py}
reconstructs exactly those four printed rows from
\path{review_bundle/REVIEW_BUNDLE_MANIFEST.json},
\path{review_bundle/case_snapshots/}, and
\path{certificates/case_rows_expected.json}, with expected exit code 0.
The replay-support surface is conditional on a non-PDF artifact submission.
When the archive
\path{cicm_supplement_2026_auditable_theory_to_paper_pipeline.zip} is supplied
as ordinary submitted files and is accompanied by a stable locator row such as a
DOI-bearing supplement, Software Heritage identifier, venue-controlled
supplement identifier, or equivalent immutable byte locator, its root must
contain \path{README.md}, \path{FINAL_DIGESTS_SHA256.md}, the verifier scripts
\path{verify_certificate_records.py}, \path{verify_source_interface_record.py},
and \path{verify_primary_claim_inventory.py}, their JSON inputs, and their
recorded verification logs.  A PDF-only submission instantiates no
reviewer-executable artifact claim; it instantiates only the finite checker
specification printed in the paper.

\textbf{Not claimed.}  The finite package is not a fresh formal-source rebuild,
a dynamic semantic validation of artifacts, an external archive-equivalence
proof, a venue-status proof, a packaged \path{make verify} interface, an
independent clean-machine replay, a theorem-dependency checker, a proof checker,
or a general proof of gate-implementation soundness.  Those stronger readings
require their own upgrade rows.  A single scope register below records these
nonconsequence boundaries.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}
Proof assistants are excellent at checking obligations they are given.  They do
not decide whether a generated theorem statement is mathematically new, whether
it duplicates a submitted sibling, whether an artifact row is being overread, or
whether an intake note has become an active manuscript.  These are publication
routing failures: a local object may be coherent while the global claim status
is wrong.

\emph{Newmath} is our structured-source instance.  It separates mathematical
source, Lean-facing interfaces, and finite substrate witnesses.  Lean markers
record one verification axis, but Lean acceptance is not treated as semantic
closure for every BEDC claim.  \emph{Automath} is the research-publication
instance.  It coordinates theoremization, review, overlap checks, novelty
checks, manuscript assembly, and submission packets.  Both workspaces use the
same rule: a generated increment becomes evidence only after its permitted claim
type has been recorded and gated.

\section{Six-role audit interface}
Each accepted increment is compiled into six coordinates:
\emph{source object}, \emph{formal interface}, \emph{finite evidence row},
\emph{advisory agent action}, \emph{deterministic gate}, and \emph{human
promotion boundary}.  If a coordinate is missing, the increment remains a
proposal.  If all coordinates are present only for a finite record, the public
claim is finite-record only.

The technical supplement's foreground checker package is the proof-carrying
Stage--A replay-kernel closure, with the canonical finite-audit basis used as
its supporting normal form.  That basis proves that each monotone acceptance
predicate is determined by a unique finite antichain of minimal accepting
records, and that unsupported Stage--A upgrades have a canonical bad-subrecord
basis.  The submitted supplementary files
\path{certificates/stage_a_manifest.json},
\path{certificates/stage_a_horn_schema.json},
\path{certificates/stage_a_horn_audit_certificate.json},
\path{certificates/stage_a_replay_report.json},
\path{certificates/stage_a_digest_table.json}, and
\path{certificates/replay_stage_a_audit.py} provide the canonical byte
manifest, the exact byte-to-atom compiler, the fixed Horn schema, the replayable
certificate, the digest table, and the deterministic replay check.  Running
\path{python certificates/replay_stage_a_audit.py} recomputes the finite vector
\[
\qlex=\mathsf{true}\quad(\text{legacy artifact atom }\texttt{qinv}),\quad
q_{\mathrm{rgs}}=\mathsf{true},\quad
q_{\mathrm{src}}=q_{\mathrm{art}}=q_{\mathrm{ext}}=q_{\mathrm{ven}}=
\mathsf{false}.
\]
Here \(q_{\mathrm{rgs}}\) is bounded fixed-replay record-gate soundness for the
fixed Stage--A schema, manifest, replayable certificate, replay report, and
digest bundle.  Older six-role, conditional RGS, theorem-inventory, and
local-support closure results in the supplement are support normal forms for this
vector.  Case-study rows are historical, path-complete method witnesses unless a
fresh command, environment, \path{source_commit}, exit-code, and log row is
supplied for a live validation claim.  The separate case-row replay command reconstructs the
four printed finite rows only; it is not a rerun of the original workflow
pipelines or an artifact-semantic validation.  Its sampling frame is the fixed
compiler case set: Newmath intake isolation, upper-fibers overlap blocking,
fake-extension blocking, and Rule110 limitation disclosure.  A row is eligible
only when the manifest case name is in that set, local paths and submitted
snapshots are paired, every submitted snapshot path is relative and present, and
the boundary declares historical or path-verified scope.  The expected JSON also
contains a negative control: a false fifth row that tries to infer Rule110
dynamic semantic validation from the limitation snapshot is rejected by the same
verifier because it is outside the fixed compiler case set and lacks the
command-run/artifact-semantic coordinates.

This distinction is the main engineering lesson.  The same package may contain
proposal artifacts, repair logs, literature-search records, venue notes, and
generated theorem wrappers, while certification is reserved for deterministic
gates and human promotion decisions.  In practice this prevents four common
failures: treating intake material as active work, routing a submitted sibling
as a fresh paper, accepting file growth as theorem growth, and reading a bounded
artifact manifest as semantic validation.

\subsection{Scope and nonconsequence register}
\label{sec:compact-scope-register}
This subsection is the compact article's single register for excluded stronger
readings.  Later tables and theorem pointers repeat an exclusion only when a
local hypothesis would otherwise be easy to misquote.

\begin{itemize}
\item The positive public vector is exactly \(\qlex\) (legacy artifact atom
\(\texttt{qinv}\), lexical only) and bounded
fixed-replay \(q_{\mathrm{rgs}}\).  Fresh formal-source rebuilds, dynamic
artifact semantics, external archive equality, venue/upload status, and general
implementation soundness require their own accepted finite upgrade rows.
\item A script, digest, inventory, or review-bundle row is evidence only for the
coordinate named by its gate.  It is not promoted by paraphrase into proof
assistant soundness, whole-program soundness, semantic artifact validation, or
external publication evidence.
\item Optional split-route mathematical material is not part of the main theorem
surface and remains interface-scoped in the supplement:
Richardson obstruction uses a verified triple on the preserved source-indexed
real domain.  Concrete printed \(U47,U111\) claims are confined to the separate
\((HW)\)-conditional corollary and are not part of the finite-checker theorem
surface;
\(U126\) is not a Richardson input.  The one-orbit obstruction is the
supplement's \(\mathbf{Orb}_{1}\) interface: a free monogenic orbit, faithful
numeral readout, and same-level ambient product, with the exponent-rank
invariant proving the obstruction.  Numerical height formulae using the ordinary
maximum are conditional on \((\mathrm{EKMP})\).
There is no unconditional Richardson instance in the submitted evidence.  The
appendix contains only a conditional schema: a finite pure-EML triple
\((P,S,A)\) may be used only after its rows \(P=\pi\), \(S(x)=\sin x\), and
\(A(x)=|x|\) for \(x\ne0\) have already been proved on the stated real domains.
The printed pair \((U47,U111)\) is not part of that schema unless \((HW)\) is
assumed explicitly.  No endpoint/central interval-root certificate for printed
roots is part of the public or supplemental proof surface; the optional
\(U126\) syntax row supplies neither endpoint-root inequalities nor a
closed-boundary bridge to a central interval.
\item Support normal forms in the supplement may justify dependency rows,
boundary rows, and case-evidence rows.  They do not create additional public
theorem coordinates beyond the finite replay-kernel vector.
\end{itemize}

\section{Positioning}
Proof-assistant infrastructure such as Lean~\cite{deMouraKADR2015Lean} supplies
trusted checking for obligations that have already been routed to an appropriate
formal interface.  Proof-carrying code and foundational proof certificates
\cite{Necula1997PCC,Appel2001FoundationalPCC,AppelMcAllester2001FPCCRecursiveTypes,ChihaniMillerRenaud2013FPC}
motivate finite, independently checkable certificate objects; here the
certificate is only the Stage--A Horn object for
\(\{\qlex,q_{\mathrm{rgs}}\}\), with \(\qlex\) stored in the artifact as
\(\texttt{qinv}\).  LLM theorem-proving systems,
including LeanDojo~\cite{YangEtAl2023LeanDojo}, Lean
Copilot~\cite{SongYangAnandkumar2024LeanCopilot}, draft--sketch--prove
workflows~\cite{JiangEtAl2023DraftSketchProve}, Lean-oriented LLM
provers~\cite{XinEtAl2024DeepSeekProver}, and mathematical language
models~\cite{AzerbayevEtAl2023Llemma}, improve proposal, search, tactic
assistance, and repair.  They are advisory sources in this paper, not proof or
artifact evidence.  Formal-library and proof-engineering work, including AFP
mining~\cite{BlanchetteHMN2015AFP} and Isabelle/AFP infrastructure
accounts~\cite{Wenzel2019IsabelleAFP}, studies how formal corpora are curated
and maintained.  Artifact and reproducibility norms, such as ACM artifact
badging~\cite{ACM2020ArtifactBadging}, ReproZip-style packaging
\cite{ChirigatiRampinShashaFreire2016ReproZip}, and systems-repeatability
studies~\cite{CollbergProebsting2016Repeatability}, state what computational
objects and reruns can support; here replay is only the digest-bound finite-row
predicate printed in the certificate.

\section{Conclusion}
The useful abstraction is not a claim about tool performance.  It is the finite
route that assigns each generated or machine-maintained object to the strongest
publication coordinate it is entitled to support.  Making that route explicit
permits proposal and repair artifacts to remain visible without letting polished
local output masquerade as accepted mathematical or publication evidence.
