\begin{abstract}
AI-assisted mathematics projects need software infrastructure that keeps fluent
drafts from becoming evidence by accident.  We present an auditable
theory-to-paper compiler around two workspaces: \emph{newmath}, which holds
structured BEDC source theory and verification interfaces, and \emph{automath},
which assembles publication packages from source maps, review records, finite
manifests, route decisions, and human-promotion records.  The contribution is an
architecture and a finite certificate boundary: a publication claim is reportable
only when the submitted package contains the source, gate, promotion, evidence
level, and limitation records assigned to that claim.  The presentation does not
claim fresh Lean rebuilds, dynamic artifact validation, end-to-end gate
correctness, or venue acceptance.
\end{abstract}

This two-page source is the primary presentation article.  The phrase
``auditable theory-to-paper pipeline'' names the compiler's record interface;
\path{main.tex} and, if uploaded or archive-equivalently replaced,
\path{review_bundle/} are supplemental support surfaces rather than a second
submission identity.

\paragraph{System thesis.}
The system problem is not how to generate more mathematical prose; it is how to
keep a publication package auditable after agents, proof assistants, manifests,
and route automation have all contributed records.  The compiler treats source
theory, Lean implementation, finite witnesses, and publication state as distinct
evidence axes.  BEDC contributes a finite-kernel theory together with proof obligations
and separate implementation contracts; Lean markers are verification records for
those contracts, not a source of object-language closure; finite manifests and
evaluator witnesses bound what is checked; agents remain advisory; and
\emph{automath} compiles only the resulting publication package.  A generated
paragraph is therefore not load-bearing evidence by itself; it must cite the
source path, gate record, manifest row, log, or human-promotion record appropriate
to the claim.

\paragraph{Architecture summary.}
The architecture has three visible layers.  First, \path{submission_abstract.tex}
is the primary two-page presentation artifact.  Second, \path{main.tex} is a technical supplement: it defines the finite-record
interfaces and proves the certificate boundary used to audit the package.  Third,
\path{review_bundle/} is supplemental evidence only when uploaded with the
submission or replaced by an archival equivalent with matching digests.
Bundle-backed claims are conditional on one of those support surfaces being
available to reviewers.

The trust boundary is deliberately typed.  Structured theory contributes pinned
source-interface rows; Lean contributes only the build and axiom-purity claims
actually recorded; finite witnesses contribute manifest-bounded rows; automation
contributes proposals, reviews, and scheduling suggestions; and the publication
compiler contributes overlap, route-state, and submission-pack gates.  Moving a
claim between these axes requires an explicit record rather than a rewrite of the
prose.

\paragraph{Agent boundary.}
AI agents are useful because the compiler makes their authority small.  Agents
may propose definitions, search for formalization targets, draft review reports,
rank critical paths, and suggest venue-facing narratives.  They do not promote
source into accepted theory, declare novelty, certify artifacts, or override
submitted-route constraints.  Those transitions are recorded only through
deterministic gates.  This avoids a common failure mode in AI-assisted
mathematics: long sequences of plausible edits that increase manuscript volume
or compile locally while adding no load-bearing theorem content.

\paragraph{Finite-record safety claim.}
The technical supplement proves a certificate-level invariant for finite records
under an explicit record-gate-soundness hypothesis: assigned gate passage is used
only when the submitted ledger gives the gate name, input surface, decision,
evidence pointer, and evidence level.  If the supplement is supplied and the
bundle is uploaded with matching final digests or archive-equivalently replaced,
a load-bearing claim is accepted only with its claim kind,
evidence surface, gate-pass rows, promotion boundary, discovery rows when needed,
and command-run boundary.  The result is path-complete for those records:
stronger readings such as independent gate implementation soundness,
proof-assistant correctness, artifact semantics, fresh daemon execution, or venue
acceptance require source paths, commands, source states, environments, exit
codes, log paths, or venue records and are not inferred.

\paragraph{Review artifact.}
The public external source surface is the pinned newmath repository
\cite{bedc-newmath-source}.  The supplemental review bundle record
\cite{auditable-pipeline-review-bundle} is submission-local support material, not
a public archival publication, venue-upload receipt, or validation service.  The
bundle-backed claims remain conditional unless the bundle is uploaded with the
submission or replaced by an archive-equivalent locator.  When supplied with the
submission, it records the pinned discovery-gate source snapshot,
source-interface record, review manifests, and case-evidence manifest.  The four
case rows---intake isolation, submitted-route overlap blocking, fake-extension
rejection, and Rule110 limitation disclosure---are bounded path-verified lessons,
not claims of fresh whole-tree or dynamic artifact reruns.  The presentation
claim is architectural: theory-to-paper systems should preserve typed evidence
boundaries so that publication claims are traceable to records rather than to
agent fluency.
