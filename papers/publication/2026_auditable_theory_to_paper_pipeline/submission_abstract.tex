\begin{abstract}
AI-assisted mathematics projects need software infrastructure that keeps fluent
drafts from becoming evidence by accident.  We present an auditable
theory-to-paper compiler around two workspaces: \emph{newmath}, which holds
structured BEDC source theory and verification interfaces, and \emph{automath},
which assembles publication packages from source maps, finite manifests, route
decisions, review records, and human-promotion records.  The contribution is a
software architecture with a small trust boundary: source theory, Lean checks,
finite witnesses, agent proposals, and publication-route state are separate
record types.  The accompanying supplement states the precise certificate-level theorem: under
record-gate soundness, publication safety is a finite-record invariant of the
visible support surface, ledger, dispatch table, and schema fields.  A
publication claim is reportable only when that finite surface contains the
source, gate, promotion, evidence-level, and limitation records assigned to that
claim.  The presentation does not claim fresh Lean
rebuilds, dynamic artifact validation, end-to-end gate correctness, venue
compliance, venue acceptance, or completed supplemental upload.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{System picture.}
The system problem is not how to generate more mathematical prose; it is how to
keep a publication package auditable after agents, proof assistants, manifests,
and route automation have all touched it.  The compiler is deliberately ordinary:
it reads source work, checks finite records, rejects unsupported route changes,
and emits a reviewer-facing package.  BEDC contributes structured theory and
proof obligations; Lean contributes recorded verification markers for selected
implementation contracts; finite manifests and evaluator witnesses bound what was
checked; agents suggest edits and reviews; and \emph{automath} compiles only the
accepted publication package.  A generated paragraph is therefore not evidence by
itself.  To become load-bearing, it must point to the source path, gate record,
manifest row, log, or human-promotion record appropriate to the claim.

\paragraph{Architecture summary.}
The architecture has three visible layers.  First, \path{submission_abstract.tex}
is the primary two-page presentation artifact.  Second, \path{main.tex} is the
selected technical supplement: it defines the finite-record interfaces and proves
the certificate boundary used to audit the package.  Third, \path{review_bundle/}
is the selected exact supplemental support directory; its manifest also exposes
the bounded theorem-obligation ledger \path{theorem_inventory.json}, with \path{theorem_inventory.md} as the human-readable companion.  The current
source package records no upload receipt and claims no archival replacement, so
bundle-backed claims are conditional on that exact support surface being made
available to reviewers with matching final digests; until then, those claims are
local/path-verified only.

The trust boundary is deliberately typed.  The package uses five axes: (i) BEDC
source theory, whose admissible evidence is pinned source paths, commits, labels,
and bounded source-interface rows, but not publication acceptance or a fresh
rebuild; (ii) Lean markers, whose admissible evidence is only recorded build,
axiom-purity, or marker-existence rows for named obligations, but not
object-language closure; (iii) finite manifest/evaluator witnesses, whose
evidence is manifest rows, bundled snapshots, verifier logs, digests, and
limitation ledgers, but not unbounded semantic validation unless the exact
evaluator contract and assigned pass or command-run record are also present; (iv) agents, whose
outputs are drafts, reviews, scheduler scores, and repair proposals, but not proof
evidence, novelty certification, or human promotion; and (v) automath, whose
evidence is promotion records, route state, overlap ledgers, submission-interface
maps, digest manifests, command logs, and upload-or-archive records when present,
but not venue acceptance or authority to merge the other axes.  Human promotion
and daemon-visible active-paper markers are separate route-boundary fields, not
interchangeable status labels.  Moving a claim between these axes requires an
explicit record rather than a rewrite of the prose.

\paragraph{Agent boundary.}
AI agents are useful because the compiler makes their authority small.  Agents
may propose definitions, search for formalization targets, draft review reports,
rank critical paths, and suggest venue-facing narratives.  They do not promote
source into accepted theory, declare novelty, certify artifacts, or override
submitted-route constraints.  Those transitions are recorded only through
deterministic gates.  This avoids a common failure mode in AI-assisted
mathematics: long sequences of plausible edits that increase manuscript volume
or compile locally while adding no load-bearing theorem content.

\paragraph{Four lessons.}
The case-study rows are used only as software-engineering lessons.  Intake
isolation prevents a seed note from becoming a public paper without human
promotion.  Submitted-route overlap blocking prevents a new active directory from
silently competing with a prior submission.  Fake-extension rejection prevents
statement reshuffling or code churn from being counted as new theorem content.
Artifact-limitation disclosure prevents finite witness files from being read as a
fresh dynamic validation of the whole artifact suite.  In each case the safe
lesson is the same: the compiler accepts a claim only at the evidence level of
the finite record that names it.

\paragraph{Finite-record safety claim.}
The technical supplement proves a certificate-level invariant for finite records
under an explicit record-gate-soundness hypothesis: assigned gate passage is used
only when the submitted ledger gives the gate name, input surface, decision,
evidence pointer, and evidence level.  If the supplement is supplied and the
bundle is uploaded exactly with matching final digests, or an archive-equivalent
replacement satisfying the supplement's finite equivalence test is supplied,
a load-bearing claim is accepted only with its claim kind,
evidence surface, gate-pass rows, promotion boundary, discovery rows when needed,
and command-run boundary.  The result is path-complete for those records:
stronger readings such as independent gate implementation soundness,
proof-assistant correctness, artifact semantics, fresh daemon execution, or venue
acceptance require source paths, commands, source states, environments, exit
codes, log paths, or venue records and are not inferred.

\paragraph{Support notes.}
The setting is adjacent to proof-assistant infrastructure such as Lean
\cite{deMouraKADR2015Lean}, retrieval-augmented and informal-to-formal systems
such as LeanDojo and Draft--Sketch--Prove
\cite{YangEtAl2023LeanDojo,JiangEtAl2023DraftSketchProve}, and proof-archive
reproducibility work such as the Archive of Formal Proofs
\cite{BlanchetteHMN2015AFP}.  The contribution here is narrower: a publication
compiler trust-boundary layer for typed finite records, not a new theorem prover,
Lean hammer, archive mining method, or general AI-safety result.  The public
external source surface is the pinned newmath repository
\cite{bedc-newmath-source}.  The supplement and review bundle are not cited here
as public archival publications or as already-uploaded venue supplements.  They
are conditional support surfaces: if \path{main.tex} is supplied and
\path{review_bundle/} is uploaded exactly with matching final digests, or an
archive-equivalent replacement is supplied, they record the
pinned discovery-gate source snapshot, source-interface record, review manifests,
case-evidence manifest, and bounded theorem-obligation ledger.  Otherwise the
primary article makes only the architectural claim stated above.  The case rows
and theorem ledger are bounded path-verified lessons, not claims of fresh
whole-tree rebuilds, dynamic artifact reruns, upload-time venue compliance, or
venue acceptance.
