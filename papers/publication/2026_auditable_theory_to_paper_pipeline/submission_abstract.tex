\begin{abstract}
Large AI-assisted mathematics projects need more than a theorem prover and more
than a manuscript workflow.  They need an auditable way to decide which generated
objects are source theory, which are formally checked implementations, which are
finite artifact witnesses, and which may be stated as publication-facing claims.
We present an auditable theory compiler built from two workspaces: \emph{newmath},
a structured mathematics repository for the Binary Emission Discovery Calculus
(BEDC), and \emph{automath}, an automation layer that derives, checks, schedules,
reviews, and packages mathematical work.  The system began with Lean 4
formalization, but its central design choice is to keep Lean verification,
BEDC theoretical closure, finite-witness substrate evidence, and publication
state as separate audit axes.  AI agents operate on proposal, search, review, and
triage layers; accepted theory increments require deterministic gates over
source maps, proof markers, axiom-purity, finite manifests, theorem-content
growth, and route state.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{System thesis.}
The project is a theory compiler, not an AI writing assistant.  Its input is a
maintained mathematical source theory with explicit closure obligations; its
outputs include checked Lean declarations, finite witness manifests, review
packets, and submission-ready papers.  The compiler's main safety property is
typed evidence separation: a fact may be theoretically closed without yet being
machine checked; a Lean theorem may verify an implementation-facing statement
without creating object-language closure; a finite substrate witness may certify
only the manifest family it enumerates; and a polished manuscript paragraph is
not evidence until it is linked back to the appropriate source and gate record.

\paragraph{Structured source theory.}
BEDC is organized as finite-kernel theory plus proof obligations plus separate
implementation contracts.  Its finite-kernel proof path runs through derived
interfaces such as sameness, histories, extensional and containment relations,
signatures, package and gap policies, completion gaps, and naming certificates.
Large interfaces are therefore thin named surfaces whose reconstruction must be
licensed by explicit certificates.  This source discipline is what the compiler
tracks: not just whether text exists, but which closure status, classifier,
lineage, boundary, and witness surface it carries.  The imported discovery-gate
calculus is used only through the pinned source-interface snapshot for newmath
commit \path{3fb3d6a0641767388a401883062aa522ea0b397b}; its statement-only,
code-only, mechanical-expansion, compression, certificate, ledger, demotion,
classifier-change, scheduler, and safety rows are cited as bounded paraphrases,
not as replacement proofs.  These BEDC source-architecture terms are contextual
interfaces imported from that pinned source; this short paper does not reprove
the underlying BEDC finite-kernel theory.

\paragraph{Lean and beyond Lean.}
Lean remains a crucial verification axis.  The newmath formalization is
mathlib-free by design, and local audits check that cited markers resolve to
real declarations and that forbidden axiom surfaces are absent.  However, Lean is
not treated as the only trust substrate.  BEDC also uses finite-witness
interfaces in which manifest rows, evaluator reports, and substrate contracts
are the binding objects.  The Rule 110 and GroundCompiler route illustrates the
principle: the claim at that boundary is a finite assertion family under a
manifest, not an unbounded semantic universe supplied by the Lean kernel.

\paragraph{Agent boundary.}
AI agents are useful because the compiler makes their authority small.  Agents
may propose definitions, search for formalization targets, draft review reports,
rank critical paths, and suggest venue-facing narratives.  They do not promote
source into accepted theory, declare novelty, certify artifacts, or override
submitted-route constraints.  Those transitions are recorded only through
deterministic gates.  This avoids a common failure mode in AI-assisted
mathematics: long sequences of plausible edits that increase manuscript volume
or compile locally while adding no load-bearing theorem content.

\paragraph{Publication-safety interface.}
The long supplement states the conditional interface invariant as
Theorem~\texttt{thm:publication-safety-interface}: if a publication claim is
derived from typed evidence surfaces, deterministic gates, the pinned
discovery-gate source interface, and explicit human promotion state, then the
compiled paper may carry only the matching claim kind and may not upgrade agent
output into proof evidence.  The four compressed case rows are: newmath intake
isolation, upper-fibers overlap block, fake-extension block, and Rule110
limitation gate.  They certify path completeness and reviewability of those
case records; they do not certify fresh daemon execution, full-source rebuilds,
or dynamic Rule110 validation.

\paragraph{Review artifact.}
For this presentation note, the public source surface is the newmath repository
\cite{bedc-newmath-source}; the supplemental review interface is
\path{review_bundle/} with the longer technical account
\cite{auditable-pipeline-review-bundle}.  The bundle records the pinned
discovery-gate source snapshot, source-interface record, review manifests, and
case-evidence manifest.  The note does not claim a fresh full rebuild of every
BEDC declaration or a fresh dynamic rerun of every substrate artifact.  Its
claim is architectural: an AI-assisted mathematics project should compile
theory through separately typed evidence axes, so that publication claims remain
traceable to source theory, formal verification, finite witnesses, and route
governance rather than to agent fluency.
