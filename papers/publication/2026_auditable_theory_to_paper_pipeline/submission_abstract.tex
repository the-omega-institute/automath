\begin{abstract}
AI-assisted mathematics projects need software infrastructure that keeps fluent
drafts from becoming evidence by accident.  We present an auditable
\emph{theory compiler} for formal-mathematics publication work around two
concrete workspaces: \emph{newmath}, which carries structured BEDC source theory
and verification interfaces, and \emph{automath}, which assembles publication
packages from source maps, finite manifests, route decisions, review records,
and human-promotion records.  The contribution is a small typed architecture:
source theory, Lean markers, finite substrate witnesses, agent proposals, and
publication-route state are different record types, checked by deterministic
source, theorem-content, artifact, overlap, and route gates.  The four case
lessons are correspondingly software-facing: isolate intake seeds until human
promotion; block routes that overlap submitted siblings; reject statement churn
or fake extensions as theorem content; and disclose finite artifact witnesses at
the evidence level actually recorded.

The trust model is architectural rather than semantic.  A generated paragraph is
not evidence; a publication claim becomes load-bearing only when the finite
record surface contains its source path, assigned gate, promotion boundary,
evidence level, and limitation record.  The accompanying supplement proves the
supporting interface theorem under record-gate soundness: the checker determines
finite publication certificates and finite bad subrecords from the visible
support surface, ledger, dispatch table, and schema fields.  The current-package
exactness claims are therefore schema-relative and row-frozen to declared
verifier rows.  They are not claims of gate-implementation correctness,
Lean/BEDC rebuild correctness, dynamic artifact semantics, venue compliance,
venue acceptance, or completed supplemental upload.
\end{abstract}

This source is the primary compact presentation article.  Its contribution is a
finite-record trust boundary for AI-assisted formal-mathematics publication work:
the compiler separates source theory, proof-assistant markers, finite artifact
witnesses, agent proposals, human promotion, and route state before any claim is
made reviewer-facing.  The phrase ``auditable theory-to-paper pipeline'' names
this record interface, not an assertion that every underlying tool has been
freshly rebuilt or semantically validated.

\paragraph{System picture.}
The system problem is not how to generate more mathematical prose; it is how to
keep a publication package auditable after agents, proof assistants, manifests,
and route automation have all touched it.  The compiler is deliberately ordinary:
it reads source work, checks finite records, rejects unsupported route changes,
and emits a reviewer-facing package.  BEDC contributes structured theory and
proof obligations; Lean contributes recorded verification markers for selected
implementation contracts; finite manifests and evaluator witnesses bound what was
checked; agents suggest edits and reviews; and \emph{automath} compiles only the
accepted publication package.  A generated paragraph is therefore not evidence by
itself.  To become load-bearing, it must point to the source path, gate record,
manifest row, log, or human-promotion record appropriate to the claim.

\paragraph{Architecture summary.}
The trust boundary is deliberately typed.  The package uses five axes: (i) BEDC
source theory, whose admissible evidence is pinned source paths, commits, labels,
and bounded source-interface rows, but not publication acceptance or a fresh
rebuild; (ii) Lean markers, whose admissible evidence is only recorded build,
axiom-purity, or marker-existence rows for named obligations, but not
object-language closure; (iii) finite manifest/evaluator witnesses, whose
evidence is manifest rows, bundled snapshots, verifier logs, digests, and
limitation ledgers, but not unbounded semantic validation unless the exact
evaluator contract and assigned pass or command-run record are also present; (iv) agents, whose
outputs are drafts, reviews, scheduler scores, and repair proposals, but not proof
evidence, novelty certification, or human promotion; and (v) automath, whose
evidence is promotion records, route state, overlap ledgers, submission-interface
maps, digest manifests, command logs, and upload-or-archive records when present,
but not venue acceptance or authority to merge the other axes.  Human promotion
and daemon-visible active-paper markers are separate route-boundary fields, not
interchangeable status labels.  Moving a claim between these axes requires an
explicit record rather than a rewrite of the prose.

The architecture has three visible layers.  First, \path{submission_abstract.tex}
is the primary compact presentation artifact.  Second, \path{main.tex} is the
technical supplement that defines the finite-record interfaces and proves the
certificate boundary used to audit the package.  Third, \path{review_bundle/}
is a support directory whose manifest exposes the bounded theorem-obligation
ledger \path{theorem_inventory.json}.  The mathematical/software claim sits in
the typed interface and certificate theorem; package-local support surfaces are
used only to instantiate that interface for the present source state.

\paragraph{Agent boundary.}
AI agents are useful because the compiler makes their authority small.  Agents
may propose definitions, search for formalization targets, draft review reports,
rank critical paths, and suggest venue-facing narratives.  They do not promote
source into accepted theory, declare novelty, certify artifacts, or override
submitted-route constraints.  Those transitions are recorded only through
deterministic gates.  This avoids a common failure mode in AI-assisted
mathematics: long sequences of plausible edits that increase manuscript volume
or compile locally while adding no load-bearing theorem content.

finite record that names it.

\paragraph{Finite-record safety claim.}
The presentation reading of ``publication safety'' is deliberately architectural,
conditional, and finite-record only.  The technical supplement supports that
architecture by proving a certificate-level
invariant for finite records under an explicit record-gate-soundness hypothesis:
assigned gate passage is used only when the submitted ledger gives the gate name,
input surface, decision, evidence pointer, and evidence level.  In the local
support calculus, a load-bearing claim is accepted only with its claim kind,
evidence surface, gate-pass rows, promotion boundary, discovery rows when needed,
and command-run boundary.  The result is path-complete for those records:
the current-package exactness claim is agreement with the declared verifier schema
on the submitted rows, not semantic truth of every package assertion.  Stronger
readings such as independent gate implementation soundness, proof-assistant
correctness, artifact semantics, fresh daemon execution, post-edit freshness, or
venue acceptance require source paths, commands, source states, environments, exit
codes, log paths, rerun metadata, or venue records and are not inferred.

\paragraph{Support notes.}
The setting is adjacent to proof-assistant infrastructure such as Lean
\cite{deMouraKADR2015Lean}, retrieval-augmented and informal-to-formal systems
such as LeanDojo and Draft--Sketch--Prove
\cite{YangEtAl2023LeanDojo,JiangEtAl2023DraftSketchProve}, and proof-archive
reproducibility work such as the Archive of Formal Proofs
\cite{BlanchetteHMN2015AFP}.  The contribution here is narrower: a publication
compiler trust-boundary layer for typed finite records, not a new theorem prover,
Lean hammer, archive mining method, or general AI-safety result.  The public
external source surface is the pinned newmath repository
\cite{bedc-newmath-source}.  The supplement and review bundle are not cited here
as public archival publications or as already-uploaded venue supplements.  In the
present route they are local/path-verified support surfaces: they record the
pinned discovery-gate source snapshot, source-interface record, review manifests,
case-evidence manifest, and bounded theorem-obligation ledger.  The
primary article makes only the architectural claim stated above.  The case rows
and theorem ledger are bounded path-verified lessons, not claims of fresh
whole-tree rebuilds, dynamic artifact reruns, upload-time venue compliance, or
venue acceptance.
The present source records no venue-upload receipt, DOI-bearing supplement,
completed supplemental upload, or final archive-equivalence byte-equality
locator; absent such a coordinate, \path{main.tex},
\path{theorem_inventory.json}, and \path{review_bundle/} remain local support
records rather than external submitted support.
