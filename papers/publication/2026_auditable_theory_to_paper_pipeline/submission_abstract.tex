\begin{abstract}
AI-assisted mathematics workflows need a publication boundary that separates
agent fluency from evidence.  We present an auditable theory-to-paper compiler
around two workspaces: \emph{newmath}, a structured BEDC source repository, and
\emph{automath}, a publication workspace that packages source maps, review
records, finite manifests, and route decisions.  The primary claim is a
finite-record one: accepted publication claims are reportable only when their
source, gate, promotion, evidence-level, and limitation records are present in
the submitted package.  This presentation does not claim fresh Lean rebuilds,
dynamic artifact validation, end-to-end gate correctness, or venue acceptance.
\end{abstract}

This two-page source is the primary presentation article.  The phrase
``auditable theory-to-paper pipeline'' names the compiler's record interface and
the optional technical supplement, not a second submission identity.

\paragraph{System thesis.}
The compiler is not an AI writing assistant.  It treats source theory, Lean
implementation, finite witnesses, and publication state as distinct evidence
axes.  BEDC contributes a finite-kernel theory together with proof obligations
and separate implementation contracts; Lean markers are verification records for
those contracts, not a source of object-language closure; finite manifests and
evaluator witnesses bound what is checked; agents remain advisory; and
\emph{automath} compiles only the resulting publication package.  A generated
paragraph is therefore not load-bearing evidence by itself; it must cite the
source path, gate record, manifest row, log, or human-promotion record appropriate
to the claim.

\paragraph{Architecture summary.}
The package has three roles.  First, \path{submission_abstract.tex} is the
primary two-page presentation artifact.  Second, \path{main.tex} is a technical
supplement: its theorem environments define and prove the finite-record
interfaces used to audit the package, but they are not part of the short
presentation paper.  Third, \path{review_bundle/} is supplemental evidence when
uploaded with the submission or replaced by an archival equivalent.  Bundle-backed
claims are conditional on that evidence surface being available to reviewers.

The architecture is deliberately typed.  Structured theory contributes pinned
source-interface rows; Lean contributes only the build and axiom-purity claims
actually recorded; finite witnesses contribute manifest-bounded rows; automation
contributes proposals, reviews, and scheduling suggestions; and the publication
compiler contributes overlap, route-state, and submission-pack gates.  Moving a
claim between these axes requires an explicit record rather than a rewrite of the
prose.

\paragraph{Agent boundary.}
AI agents are useful because the compiler makes their authority small.  Agents
may propose definitions, search for formalization targets, draft review reports,
rank critical paths, and suggest venue-facing narratives.  They do not promote
source into accepted theory, declare novelty, certify artifacts, or override
submitted-route constraints.  Those transitions are recorded only through
deterministic gates.  This avoids a common failure mode in AI-assisted
mathematics: long sequences of plausible edits that increase manuscript volume
or compile locally while adding no load-bearing theorem content.

\paragraph{Finite-record safety claim.}
The technical supplement proves a certificate-level invariant for the submitted
finite records.  If the supplement and bundle are present, a load-bearing claim
is accepted only with its claim kind, evidence surface, gate-pass rows,
promotion boundary, discovery rows when needed, and command-run boundary.  The
result is path-complete for those records: stronger readings such as external
gate soundness, proof-assistant correctness, artifact semantics, fresh daemon
execution, or venue acceptance require additional records and are not inferred.

\paragraph{Review artifact.}
The public external source surface is the pinned newmath repository
\cite{bedc-newmath-source}.  The supplemental review bundle record
\cite{auditable-pipeline-review-bundle} is submission-local support material, not
a public archival publication or validation service.  When supplied with the
submission, it records the pinned discovery-gate source snapshot,
source-interface record, review manifests, and case-evidence manifest.  The four
case rows---intake isolation, submitted-route overlap blocking, fake-extension
rejection, and Rule110 limitation disclosure---are bounded path-verified lessons,
not claims of fresh whole-tree or dynamic artifact reruns.  The presentation
claim is architectural: theory-to-paper systems should preserve typed evidence
boundaries so that publication claims are traceable to records rather than to
agent fluency.
