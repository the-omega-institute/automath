\begin{abstract}
AI-assisted formal mathematics is often presented as a loop in which a model
proposes theorem statements or proof scripts and a proof assistant accepts or
rejects them.  That view is too narrow for sustained research.  We describe an
auditable auto-formalization method developed across two related projects,
\emph{automath} and \emph{newmath}.  Both projects treat formalization as a
research pipeline with structured mathematical source, explicit claim types,
machine-checkable interfaces, finite evidence records, agent proposals, and
human promotion decisions.  The central principle is that generated material is
never evidence by itself: every accepted increment must name the mathematical
object it changes, the verification axis on which it is checked, the finite
record supporting it, and the boundary of what is not claimed.

In \emph{newmath}, this discipline is instantiated by BEDC, a mathlib-free
structured theory whose paper source separates theoretical closure from Lean
verification and from finite substrate witnesses.  In \emph{automath}, the same
discipline appears as an automated theory-development and publication layer:
source maps, theorem inventories, axiom and marker audits, anti-hollow theorem
checks, route-state gates, and review packets prevent agent output from becoming
unsupported mathematical or submission-facing claims.  The contribution is not a
new theorem prover and not an assertion that AI replaces mathematicians.  It is a
reproducible research methodology for scaling AI-assisted formalization while
preserving auditability: formalization targets are decomposed into typed
records, agents operate only on proposal and repair layers, and deterministic
gates decide whether an increment is source theory, checked formal code, finite
artifact evidence, or publication-ready exposition.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Methodological claim.}
The method is designed for projects where the research object is still being
formed while its formalization is being attempted.  In such a setting, a Lean
file that compiles, a generated theorem statement, and a polished paragraph are
not equivalent forms of progress.  The workflow therefore separates five
questions: what is the mathematical source object, what formal interface is
being checked, what finite evidence was produced, what did an agent merely
propose, and what may be stated to a reader.  This separation is the part we aim
to make portable.

\paragraph{Two instances of the same discipline.}
\emph{Newmath} is the structured-theory instance.  Its BEDC source is organized
around finite-kernel theory, proof obligations, and implementation contracts.
Lean markers record an independent verification status, but Lean does not create
BEDC object-language closure.  Finite substrate witnesses, such as manifests,
evaluator reports, and compiler contracts, provide a separate evidence axis.

\emph{Automath} is the automated-research instance.  It coordinates derivation,
formalization, review, and paper assembly through source maps, theorem lists,
stage audits, and route gates.  Its role is not simply to write papers; it
records which generated objects have become accepted source claims, which are
checked implementation claims, which remain proposals, and which are blocked by
overlap, artifact, novelty, or submission-state constraints.

\paragraph{Why this differs from ordinary LLM proving.}
The method does not ask a model to directly certify mathematics.  Instead, it
uses models where they are strongest: locating promising formalization targets,
suggesting repairs, comparing exposition against source records, and finding
missing assumptions or hollow theorem growth.  Certification is deliberately
moved out of the model and into records that can be checked by scripts, proof
assistants, finite witnesses, and human promotion decisions.  A failed or
blocked route is therefore not only a negative outcome; it becomes structured
data about overlap, missing theorem content, inadequate novelty, or insufficient
evidence.

\paragraph{Presentation artifact.}
This presentation reports the method, not a completed universal automation
system.  The public source surface for the BEDC instance is the newmath
repository \cite{bedc-newmath-source}; the local supplement records the source
interface, review bundle, and publication-pipeline evidence used for this
presentation route.  We do not claim a fresh rebuild of every declaration, a
fresh dynamic rerun of every artifact, or automatic venue acceptance.  The
claim is methodological: AI-assisted auto-formalization can be made rigorous by
forcing every generated increment through typed source, verification, evidence,
agent, and publication records before it is treated as accepted research output.
