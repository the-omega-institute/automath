\begin{abstract}
AI-assisted formalization is often described as a loop in which a model
proposes Lean code and a proof assistant accepts or rejects it.  That loop is
necessary, but it is too small for sustained research.  A live project must
also decide whether a generated object is a proposal, a source-theory claim, a
checked formal interface, a finite artifact witness, a publication claim, or a
human decision boundary.  We describe a theory-to-paper pipeline, developed
across the \emph{newmath} and \emph{automath} workspaces, that makes those
claim types explicit.  Generated material is routed through six records:
source object, formal interface, finite evidence row, advisory agent action,
deterministic gate, and human promotion boundary.  The system lets agents
search, repair, and criticize, while certification remains in typed records,
mechanical gates, and human promotion decisions.  The compact theorem spine is
conditional and finite-record only: six-role interface, record-gate soundness,
finite-record exactness, no-free-upgrade, and four methodological case rows.  The
case rows are historical/path-verified gate examples rather than fresh daemon or
artifact reruns, and the finite-script support result is projection-contract
soundness for named scripts rather than semantic soundness of the implementation
stack.  The contribution is a portable audit boundary for AI-assisted formal
mathematics, not a new theorem prover, an end-to-end correctness claim for
automath/newmath, or an automatic venue-acceptance system.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
Proof assistants are excellent at checking obligations they are given.  They do
not decide whether an AI-generated theorem statement is mathematically
substantive, whether it duplicates a submitted sibling, whether an artifact row
is being overread, or whether an intake note has become an active manuscript.
Those are routing failures: the local object may be coherent, but the global
claim status is wrong.

\emph{Newmath} is our structured-source instance.  It separates mathematical
source, Lean-facing interfaces, and finite substrate witnesses.  Lean markers
record one verification axis, but Lean acceptance is not treated as semantic
closure for every BEDC claim.  \emph{Automath} is the research-publication
instance.  It coordinates theoremization, review, overlap checks, novelty
checks, manuscript assembly, and submission packets.  Both workspaces use the
same principle: a generated increment is evidence only after its allowed claim
type has been recorded and gated.

\section{Audit interface}
The pipeline compiles each accepted increment into six roles: the source object
being changed, the formal interface on which it is checked, the finite evidence
row supporting the claim, the advisory agent action that proposed or repaired
it, the deterministic gate that accepted it, and the human promotion boundary
that says what may be claimed to readers.  If one role is missing, the
increment remains a proposal.

\section{Positioning}
The method is complementary to proof-assistant and LLM-proving infrastructure
such as Lean~\cite{deMouraKADR2015Lean}, LeanDojo~\cite{YangEtAl2023LeanDojo},
draft--sketch--prove workflows~\cite{JiangEtAl2023DraftSketchProve}, and
formal-library artifact practice such as AFP mining~\cite{BlanchetteHMN2015AFP}.
Those systems improve the production and checking of formal objects.  Our
interface addresses the surrounding research layer: how a pipeline decides what
kind of claim a generated object is allowed to become.

The public source surface for the BEDC instance is the newmath repository
\cite{bedc-newmath-source}, pinned for this submission to commit
\path{3fb3d6a0641767388a401883062aa522ea0b397b}.  The submitted support surface
may include a technical supplement, source-interface record, theorem inventory,
digest manifest, and review bundle.  These files support finite-record audit
predicates; they are not, by themselves, claims of clean-machine rebuild,
complete source import, dynamic rerun of every artifact, or venue acceptance.

\section{Conclusion}
The useful abstraction is not ``the model proves the theorem.''  It is
``the system routes each generated object to the strongest claim it is entitled
to support.''  Making that routing explicit gives AI agents room to search and
repair without letting polished local output masquerade as accepted
mathematical or publication evidence.
