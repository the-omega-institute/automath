\begin{abstract}
Large formal-mathematics projects increasingly use AI agents for drafting,
review, theorem search, and manuscript preparation.  Ungated agent output,
however, can create theorem-looking but content-poor edits, stale source
references, duplicated submission routes, and premature paper promotion.  We
present an auditable theory-to-paper pipeline used around a Lean-backed BEDC
source workspace and an automath publication workspace.  The central rule is
that AI output is advisory: load-bearing mathematical and submission claims must
pass deterministic gates before they receive publication-facing acceptance
entries.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Safety interface.}
The contribution is a workflow interface rather than a new theorem-proving
method.  The pipeline separates source development, intake packets, active paper
tracks, AI review loops, deterministic gates, and human promotion decisions.  Its
safety rule is: AI-generated text, schedules, theorem candidates, and review
packets are proposals; a load-bearing claim becomes accepted work only when its
acceptance entry records the claim kind, evidence surface, evidence level, and
all deterministic gate passes required for that kind.  The supplemental long
technical supplement \path{main.tex} gives the full publication-safety theorem and
proof interface for this abbreviated statement.

\paragraph{Source interface.}
The imported discovery-gate spine is available in the public newmath repository
\cite{bedc-newmath-source}; the exact revision, source file, line ranges, and
extracted interface record are supplied in the supplemental review bundle
\cite{auditable-pipeline-review-bundle}.  The imported spine records the rules
used here: statement-only and code-only candidates are insufficient, mechanical
expansion and compression are not by themselves discovery, discovery claims need
certificate and ledger witnesses, unsupported claims may be demoted, and
accepted discovery needs lineage and boundary witnesses.

\paragraph{Deterministic gates.}
The gate interfaces include source builds and axiom checks, source-map and
marker audits, anti-hollow theorem-content linting, overlap/submitted blocking,
publication-state checks, artifact-limitation checks, and submission-pack checks.
Passing one gate does not replace another: compilation does not prove theorem
growth, and a human promotion marker does not resolve route overlap.

\paragraph{Evidence boundary and artifacts.}
This two-page CICM presentation artifact uses path-verified source architecture
and historical case evidence.  It does not claim a fresh rebuild of the full
newmath source tree, a fresh publication-daemon run, or a fresh Rule110 dynamic
artifact rerun.  Any later command-run claim must record the source commit,
environment, command, exit code, and log path.  The review interface consists of
the public newmath repository together with supplemental upload of
\path{review_bundle/} and, if permitted by the venue, \path{main.tex}.  The
supplemental bundle records the exact revision, extracted source-interface
manifest, case-evidence manifest, and local publication-pipeline metadata
\cite{auditable-pipeline-review-bundle,automath-publication-pipeline}.
