  263: \section{Operational interface}
  264: 
  265: For this long technical supplement and its two-page CICM presentation artifact,
  266: the following finite interface is sufficient.  A
  267: \emph{claim} is a manuscript assertion that can affect a mathematical,
  268: artifact, route, or submission decision.  A \emph{load-bearing claim} is a
  269: claim whose removal would change a theorem statement, a case-study conclusion,
  270: or the submission-readiness status.  An \emph{advisory output} is any AI-agent
  271: draft, score, suggested theorem, review packet, or repair proposal before a
  272: gate records it.  A \emph{deterministic gate} is a repeatable predicate, script,
  273: or checklist whose inputs and pass/fail decision are recorded independently of
  274: the prose generated by an agent.  A \emph{human promotion} is the explicit
  275: editorial act that moves a candidate into publication state, for example by
  276: creating an active \texttt{2026\_*} paper directory with \texttt{main.tex} and
  277: \texttt{PIPELINE.md}.  An \emph{evidence surface} is the set of source paths,
  278: commits, commands, logs, inventories, and witness records cited for a claim.
  279: An \emph{acceptance entry} is a manuscript or submission-ledger record that marks
  280: a candidate claim as usable in the publication-facing argument.  \emph{Accepted
  281: work} is the subset of candidate claims with such an entry.  This is a status
  282: notion only: the definition records that an editorial acceptance entry exists,
  283: but it does not by itself assert external editorial acceptance or that the entry
  284: is gate-sound.  A pipeline is
  285: \emph{gate-sound for acceptance} when each acceptance entry records the claim
  286: kind, the evidence surface, the evidence level, and a pass record for every gate
  287: in $\Gamma$ assigned to that claim kind, and when a route-boundary entry also
  288: records human promotion.
  289: 
  290: The deterministic dispatch function $\Gamma$ from claim kinds to gates is part
  291: of the operational interface.  Its domain is
  292: \[
  293: \{\text{formal-source},\text{ traceability},\text{ route},
  294: \text{ submission},\text{ artifact},\text{ discovery},\text{ work-selection}\}.
  295: \]
  296: It sends formal-source claims to the build and axiom-purity gates; traceability
  297: claims to inventory and marker-existence gates; route claims to promotion,
  298: overlap, and submission-state gates; submission claims to submission-pack gates;
  299: artifact claims to status and limitation-ledger gates; discovery claims to the
  300: pinned discovery gate above and, for extension claims, to the theorem-content
  301: delta gate; and work-selection claims to the scheduler gate, whose evidence
  302: surface is a critical-path score or backlog window together with the claim kind
  303: that the selected item is meant to advance.  For mixed claims, $\Gamma$ returns
  304: the union of the gate sets for the claim kinds present.
  305: 
  306: The route gates have separate operational meanings.  The \emph{promotion gate}
  307: uses the active-paper evidence surface, namely a human promotion act producing a
  308: \path{2026_*} directory with \path{main.tex} and \path{PIPELINE.md}; passing
  309: means the candidate may be daemon-visible publication work, and failing means it
  310: remains intake or source material.  The \emph{overlap gate} uses route identity,
  311: semantic-overlap ledgers, submitted-sibling paths, and program-board state;
  312: passing means advancement is route-disjoint or has a recorded resolution, and
  313: failing means advancement is blocked until closure, merge, supersession, or
  314: waiting for prior feedback.  The \emph{submission-state gate} uses submission
  315: markers, venue state, board state, and submission-pack metadata; passing means
  316: the route's publication state permits the proposed action, and failing means the
  317: action would duplicate, race, or under-specify an active or submitted route.  The
  318: \emph{theorem-content delta gate} uses theorem inventories, hard-lint output, and
  319: delta records such as \path{ARTIFACT_INVENTORY.md}; passing means that an
  320: extension claim identifies load-bearing mathematical content, and failing means
  321: that compilation, file churn, or surface edits may not be reported as substantive
  322: theorem growth.
  323: 
  324: A \emph{reviewable artifact} for this paper is either a public repository file,
  325: a supplemental file included with the submission, or an explicit
  326: historical-evidence boundary stating that the local path is used only as
  327: path-verified evidence and not as a fresh command-run claim.  A \emph{case
  328: witness record} is path-complete when its evidence surface gives a
  329: concrete source or artifact path for each load-bearing component of the row.  A
  330: route-overlap witness has the fields \emph{route identity}, \emph{submitted
  331: sibling}, \emph{overlap reason}, \emph{gate decision}, and \emph{allowed next
  332: actions}.  An artifact-limitation witness has the fields \emph{artifact status
  333: path}, \emph{limitation ledger path}, \emph{observed limitation}, \emph{gate
  334: decision}, and \emph{allowed manuscript claim}.  A promotion witness has the
  335: fields \emph{candidate surface}, \emph{human promotion evidence},
  336: \emph{active-paper markers}, \emph{gate decision}, and \emph{allowed manuscript
  337: claim}.  A theorem-content witness has the fields \emph{changed surface},
  338: \emph{theorem inventory or hard-lint surface}, \emph{substantive-content delta},
  339: \emph{gate decision}, and \emph{allowed manuscript claim}.  The inventory files
  340: \path{ARTIFACT_INVENTORY.md}, \path{SOURCE_MAP.md}, and
  341: \path{review_bundle/REVIEW_BUNDLE_MANIFEST.json} are part of the evidence surface
  342: for this paper and give the compact path catalogue used by
  343: Table~\ref{tab:case-witnesses}.
  344: 
  345: We distinguish two evidence levels.  A claim is \emph{path-verified} when this
  346: paper records the relevant source path, artifact path, pinned commit or local
  347: publication path, and gate decision, but does not assert that a command was
  348: freshly rerun for this submission.  A claim is \emph{command-run} when the paper
  349: also records the command, source commit, environment, exit code, and log path
  350: for a fresh run.  This long technical supplement uses path-verified architecture
  351: and case evidence unless explicitly marked command-run.
  352: 
  353: A uniform \emph{pass record} is the finite tuple
  354: \[
  355: \rho=(g,I,d,e,\ell),
  356: \]
  357: where $g$ is the gate name, $I$ is the input surface submitted to that gate,
  358: $d$ is the recorded decision, $e$ is the evidence pointer supporting the
  359: decision, and $\ell$ is the evidence level.  A pass record is assigned to a
  360: claim certificate only when its evidence pointer is contained in the claim's
  361: evidence surface, in a submitted manifest or table row, in a bundled snapshot, or
  362: in an explicit historical-evidence boundary.
  363: 
  364: A \emph{publication certificate} for a load-bearing claim $c$ is the finite
  365: record
  366: \[
  367: \mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B).
  368: \]
  369: Here $K$ is the nonempty finite set of claim kinds carried by $c$, $\ell$ is the
  370: evidence level, $E$ is the evidence surface, $H$ is either a human-promotion
  371: record or the marker that $c$ does not cross the intake-to-publication boundary,
  372: $R$ gives one pass record, with the five fields above, for each gate in
  373: $\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)$, $D$ is present exactly when $K$ contains discovery
  374: and then names the pinned discovery-gate source-interface rows used for the
  375: claim, and $B$ records either the command-run fields or the explicit boundary
  376: that no command-run assertion is made.  A certificate is \emph{locally
  377: checkable} when these finite fields are present and every path, manifest row, or
  378: log named by a field lies in the submitted artifact, the supplemental review
  379: bundle, or the explicit path-verified historical boundary.
