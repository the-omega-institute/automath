\begin{abstract}
Generated formal-mathematics artifacts need a publication interface that states
which finite records support which public claims.  We present a reusable
publication-coordinate route schema, then instantiate it as a finite checker
specification for a concrete \emph{newmath}/\emph{automath} theory-to-paper
package.  The reusable part is the separation between public coordinates, typed
evidence surfaces, admissible pointers, deterministic gates, and human promotion
rows.  The instance is a six-coordinate replay-kernel specification.  The
operative PDF-level claim is only the printed finite specification and Horn
calculation.  No reviewer-executable artifact claim is instantiated by this
PDF-only object.  If a later non-PDF archive satisfies the archive contract of
Section~\ref{sec:replay-artifact-boundary}, including ordinary non-symlink
files, byte-equality rows, replay-environment descriptor, stable locator, and
the explicit \(\ScriptOKstageA\) assumption, then the byte-bound replay relation
has the two positive coordinates \(\qlex\) (legacy atom \(\texttt{qinv}\)) and
bounded fixed-replay record-gate soundness.
All stronger readings are collected once in the scope register
Table~\ref{tab:scope-register}.  The paper's technical point is that generated
wrappers, logs, digests, and review records can be retained without letting them
silently promote themselves into proof, source, archive, venue, or semantic
artifact claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Finite checker route}
The object studied here is the publication routing of generated and
machine-maintained formal-mathematics artifacts, not the success rate or
mathematical creativity of the tools that produced them.  Proof assistants check
obligations; artifact and manuscript systems also need to decide which local
objects may support which publication claims.  Our pipeline therefore records
every accepted increment through six roles: source object, formal interface,
finite evidence row, advisory action, deterministic gate, and human promotion
boundary.  A theorem wrapper, review note, log, digest, or support file becomes
load-bearing only at the coordinate named by its finite gate.

The submitted package has one operative layer in this PDF-only reading.  This
article is the route paper: it prints the finite predicates, the coordinate
calculation, and the archive contract.  A separate artifact archive would add a
byte-bound support layer only if it supplied ordinary non-symlink files, the
printed SHA--256 byte equalities, the digest-bound replay-environment
descriptor, a stable locator, and a separate branch-by-branch conformance proof
for any script that is to be treated as executable evidence.  The present
article does not assert that such an archive or conformance proof has been
supplied.  Path names, command lines, generated wrappers, ledgers, digest rows,
and script names are therefore evidence only through the printed finite
relations, not through unproved source-code behavior.

\paragraph{Audit-string typography.}
Every path, command fragment, digest, and code identifier that is part of a
byte-level audit row is typeset as an atomic audit string.  In particular,
\verb|\path| and the paper macros \verb|\auditpath|, \verb|\artifactpath|, and
\verb|\auditcode| are used with discretionary URL/path line breaks disabled.
Thus strings such as \auditpath{certificates/stage_a_manifest.json},
\auditpath{str.splitlines()}, and \auditcode{forward_chain} must be read
literally; a line break in the surrounding prose is not an inserted byte, blank,
underscore, slash, or identifier character.

\paragraph{Terminology.}
The paper uses the following four scope terms only in this finite-record sense.
\emph{Public surface} means the theorem interface a reviewer is asked to read
as the paper's publication claim, here the finite Horn replay theorem and the
two-coordinate replay statement.  \emph{Foreground} means membership in that
public surface; material outside it is ledger or artifact support, even when it
is mathematically useful.  A \emph{support coordinate} is one component of the
finite six-coordinate product
\((\qlex,q_{\mathrm{rgs}},q_{\mathrm{src}},q_{\mathrm{art}},
q_{\mathrm{ext}},q_{\mathrm{ven}})\), together with its required finite input
rows.  \emph{Current-byte} means the exact bytes listed in the manifest and
digest table at the moment the replay script is run; later edits require a new
digest/replay tuple before the term applies again.  These terms do not add
evidence.  They are abbreviations for the displayed finite records, closure
rules, and coordinate projections.  Throughout the public paper, \(\qlex\)
denotes the raw lexical token coordinate; the submitted JSON and Horn
files retain the byte-level legacy atom name \texttt{qinv}.  Thus exact artifact
tables may print \texttt{qinv}, but the coordinate's public mathematical name and
meaning are raw lexical token scanning, not inventory-theoretic.

The unconditional object submitted as this article is a finite checker-boundary

\paragraph{Byte-level raw displayed-token subset scanner for \(\qlex\).}
The \(\qlex\) scanner is the following finite function of the submitted
\path{main.tex} bytes.  On input byte string \(b\), it first decodes \(b\) as
\texttt{utf-8-sig}; undecodable bytes are rejected.  It then applies Python
\path{str.splitlines()} and processes the resulting finite line list in order.
For each line it records at most the first match of the Python regular
expression
\path+\\begin\{(definition|lemma|proposition|theorem|corollary)\}(?:\[([^\]]*)\])?+.
No blank may occur between \path{\textbackslash begin} and the left brace, and
the only accepted environment names are the five displayed alternatives.  For a
recorded begin token with name \(e\), the scanner searches from that line forward
for the first line containing the literal end pattern
\(\texttt{\textbackslash\textbackslash end\textbackslash\{}e\texttt{\textbackslash\}}\).
The environment body is the source slice from the begin line through that first
end line, or through end of file if no matching end line is found.  The label
field is the first match in that slice of
\path+\\label\{([^}]*)\}+,
or is absent if there is no such match.  This gives a deterministic record list
and hence a count vector by environment name.  Formally,
\[
\ScanOK(B,I,J,D)
\]
holds for submitted \path{main.tex} bytes \(B\), inventory JSON bytes \(I\),
inventory Markdown bytes \(J\), and digest table \(D\) exactly when \(B,I,J\)
are ordinary digest-bound rows in \(D\), \(B\) decodes as above, every recorded
environment has a label, no two records have the same label, and the detected
label set is contained in the inventory-label set parsed from \(I\), \(J\), or
the digest-bound static source-label scan row named by \(D\).  The predicate is
finite because all four byte strings are finite and the regular-expression scan,
label extraction, set-uniqueness test, and containment test are bounded by their
lengths.  Thus \(\qlex\) accepts only through this byte-level \(\ScanOK\)
predicate, not merely through the names \texttt{invJson}, \texttt{invMd}, and
\texttt{finalDigest}.  The scanner never strips comments, expands macros,
follows \path{\textbackslash input}, evaluates conditionals, balances nested
environments, or validates proof text.  Its exact guarantee is therefore a raw
displayed-token subset check: every accepted record is generated by a displayed
byte substring matching the grammar above, but matching substrings in comments or
inactive branches are deliberate false positives relative to TeX execution, and
macro-generated, included, nested, or second-same-line begin tokens are deliberate
false negatives relative to TeX execution.  Therefore
\path{\textbackslash begin\{lemma\}\textbackslash label\{lem:x\}\textbackslash end\{lemma\}}
is a positive one-record example; the same byte substring after a percent sign
or inside an inactive conditional branch is still counted.  By contrast
\path{\textbackslash begin \{lemma\}}, \path{\textbackslash begin\{remark\}},
\path{\textbackslash begin\{lemma*\}}, and a second begin token later on the
same source line are not additional accepted begin records under this scanner
grammar.

\paragraph{Reusable route schema.}
The reusable design pattern is not the surrounding project history.  It is the
following finite route from a proposed public assertion to a bounded publication
claim:
\begin{equation}
\tag{RouteSchema}
\begin{array}{c}
\text{proposed public assertion }c\\
\downarrow\\
\text{claim-kind set }K\text{ and declared coordinate vector }Q_c\\
\downarrow\\
\text{finite evidence surface }E=(E_{\mathrm{atom}},E_{\mathrm{path}})\\
\downarrow\\
\text{gate rows }R\text{ with }\PointerOK(u,E,M,S)\text{ evidence pointers}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{human or route boundary }H\text{ and command boundary }B\\
\downarrow\\
\text{accepted finite predicate }\Acc_{\Gamma}(c,C)\text{ and public claim}
\end{array}
\end{equation}
Each arrow is a finite projection: it either records an explicit coordinate or
rejects a stronger reading that lacks the required coordinate.  The schema is
therefore portable across artifact-producing projects, while every instantiated
claim remains relative to its own manifest \(M\), submitted surface \(S\), gate
dispatch table \(\Gamma\), and certificate record \(C=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\).  It
does not say that a generated artifact is mathematically correct merely because a
record exists; it says which public reading the record is allowed to support.

As a non-self-referential instance, consider a separate paper whose appendix is a
program-generated table of \(500\) small graph obstructions.  A conservative
publication-coordinate certificate could assert only
\[
(\text{appendixTableLexicalCoverage},\text{tableDigestReplay})=(\mathsf{true},
\mathsf{true})
\]
and leave
\(\text{graphIsomorphismMinimality}\), \(\text{freshGeneratorRun}\), and
\(\text{archiveUpload}\) false.  Its evidence surface would name the appendix
\TeX{} file, the generator output CSV, a digest table for those two paths, and a
gate row saying that every displayed obstruction row has a matching CSV row under
the recorded SHA--256 values.  That certificate would let the article say
``the displayed table is byte-linked to the submitted generator output''; it
would not let the article say that the obstruction list is complete, minimal, or
freshly regenerated unless new finite rows for those stronger coordinates were
added.  This graph-table example is not an input to the current replay theorem;
it illustrates the schema in Figure~\ref{fig:general-route-schema} outside the
present package.

\begin{proof}
This theorem is the displayed mathematical layer: \(A\) and \(H\) are the finite
set and finite Horn system printed in the statement.  Forward chaining is
monotone, so it is enough to inspect the displayed rules whose conclusions lie
in \(Q\).  Rule \(R1\) applies because its five premises
\(\textsf{mainTex}\), \(\textsf{invJson}\), \(\textsf{invMd}\),
\(\textsf{finalDigest}\), and \(\textsf{scanOK}\) are in \(A\); hence
\(\qlex\in\operatorname{Cl}_{H}(A)\), with artifact spelling
\(\texttt{qinv}\).  Rule \(R3\) applies because its ten premises, namely the
four Stage--A file atoms, \(\textsf{finalDigest}\), the finite predicates
\(\textsf{RecordGateOK}\), \(\textsf{CertDAGOK}\), and
\(\textsf{DigestOKstage\_a}\), the explicit TCB premise
\(\textsf{ScriptOKstage\_a}\), and \(\textsf{qrgsReplayUpgrade}\), are in
\(A\); hence \(q_{\mathrm{rgs}}\in\operatorname{Cl}_{H}(A)\).

For the negative coordinates, the only displayed rules concluding
\(q_{\mathrm{src}}\), \(q_{\mathrm{art}}\), \(q_{\mathrm{ext}}\), and
\(q_{\mathrm{ven}}\) are \(R5\), \(R6\), the two-step \(R7a\)--\(R7b\) path,
and \(R8\).  Their respective missing promotion premises are
\(\textsf{freshFormalSourceUpgrade}\),
\(\textsf{dynamicArtifactSemanticUpgrade}\),
\(\textsf{externalArchiveEquivalence}\) together with the replay-identity atom
\(\textsf{locatorOKstageA}\), where \(\textsf{locatorOKstageA}\) itself requires
\(\textsf{stableLocator}\) and \(\textsf{archiveByteEquality}\), and
\(\textsf{uploadTimeVenueAcceptanceUpgrade}\).  None is a base atom in \(A\),
and no displayed rule derives any of them.  Therefore those four premises are
absent from \(\operatorname{Cl}_{H}(A)\), so the corresponding four support
coordinates are absent as well.  The other rules derive only auxiliary atoms,
not elements of \(Q\).  Thus
\(\operatorname{Cl}_{H}(A)\cap Q=\{\qlex,q_{\mathrm{rgs}}\}\).

This proves the finite coordinate calculation.  The support and upgrade
consequences of each coordinate are those in Table~\ref{tab:scope-register}; the
Richardson obstruction used later is the separate conditional reduction in
Lemma~\ref{lem:richardson-normalizer-reduction}.
\end{proof}

\section{Replay artifact boundary}
\label{sec:replay-artifact-boundary}
This section states the archive preconditions that would be needed to turn the
printed finite specification into an executable replay claim.  The preconditions
are part of the specification; this PDF-only article does not assert that they
have been satisfied by an operative archive.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The PDF article prints predicates, digests, and the archive contract.
\item The certificate files named below are intended replay bytes for a later
archive-instantiated submission.
\item A reviewer-executable claim is instantiated only by a non-PDF artifact
submission that supplies those bytes as ordinary non-symlink files, matches the
byte-equality ledger, pins the replay environment, and adds a stable public or
venue-controlled locator.
\end{enumerate}
The conditional replay claim is instantiated precisely when a review package
satisfies those three bullets using
Tables~\ref{tab:replay-artifact-manifest}--\ref{tab:artifact-submission-checklist},
Table~\ref{tab:printed-sha-ledger}, Table~\ref{tab:artifact-locator-status}, and
Table~\ref{tab:replay-tcb}.  The observed local
\texttt{python -VV}/Windows/NTFS run is provenance for the descriptor, not an
alternative theorem premise.

The conditional review commands are displayed separately so their path tokens do
not carry mathematical content.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{0.93\linewidth}{Y}
\artifactcmd{python}{certificates/replay_stage_a_audit.py}\\
\artifactcmd{python}{certificates/replay_case_rows.py}
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
The first command checks the rows in the inner no-self digest table, rejects an
attempted row for \artifactpath{certificates/stage_a_digest_table.json}, compiles
manifest bytes to typed atoms, forward-chains the fixed Horn schema, validates
certificate DAGs, and compares the recomputed six-coordinate vector with the
replay report.  It does not compute the outer printed digest-table anchor in
Table~\ref{tab:printed-sha-ledger}; that row is an archive-review condition on
the submitted digest-table bytes.  The second command
reconstructs the four printed historical case rows from the review bundle and
the expected-row JSON file in Table~\ref{tab:replay-artifact-manifest}.  The
eligible case universe is closed by the fixed compiler case set in that script:
Newmath intake isolation, upper-fibers overlap blocking, fake-extension
blocking, and Rule110 limitation disclosure.  A manifest row is eligible only if
its case name is in that set, its local provenance list and submitted snapshot
list have the same length, every snapshot path passes the relative-root,
\texttt{resolve(strict=True)}, \texttt{is\_relative\_to}, ordinary-file,

\subsection{Record-gate schema without circular soundness}
The record-gate theorem used here is a finite schema theorem, not a proof that a
pipeline implementation is record-gate-sound.  Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Kind=\{&\textsf{formal\mbox{-}source},\textsf{traceability},\textsf{route},
\textsf{submission},\\
&\textsf{artifact},\textsf{discovery},\textsf{work\mbox{-}selection}\}
\end{aligned}
\]
be the seven-element claim-kind set.  Let \(\Gate\) be a finite gate set and let
\(\Gamma:\Kind\to\mathcal P(\Gate)\) be a deterministic dispatch table.  A
certificate for a ledger claim \(c\) is a tuple
\[
C=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B),
\]
where \(K\subseteq\Kind\) is nonempty, \(\ell\) is an evidence level,
\(E=(E_{\mathrm{atom}},E_{\mathrm{path}})\) is a typed finite evidence surface,
\(H\) is the human-promotion coordinate, \(R\) is the finite list of pass rows,
\(D\) is the discovery-interface coordinate, and \(B\) is the command-boundary
coordinate.  Certificate records use exactly the evidence-level set
\[
\mathsf{Lev}_{\mathrm{cert}}=
\{\textsf{path\mbox{-}verified},\textsf{command\mbox{-}run}\}.
\]
This two-element set is a theorem hypothesis about the certificate-record schema
row, not a convention inferred from the artifact-pattern list.  In particular,
strings used elsewhere as pointer-boundary patterns, including manifest tags,
table tags, snapshot names, and historical-boundary tags, are outside
\(\mathsf{Lev}_{\mathrm{cert}}\).
For this theorem the certificate schema row \(\Sigma\) is part of the submitted
checker input and is itself locked: the parsed value
\(\Sigma[\texttt{evidence\_levels}]\) is exactly
\(\mathsf{Lev}_{\mathrm{cert}}\).  This is separate from the schema's
artifact-pattern vocabulary.  Strings such as
\texttt{explicit-historical-boundary:*} are admissible only as pointer-boundary
tokens after the checks below; they are not evidence levels and are not read by
\(\mathsf{CommandBoundaryOK}\).  Define \(\Acc_{\Gamma}(c,C)\) by the six
finite predicates:
\begin{enumerate}[label={\(\mathrm{P}_{\arabic*}\):}]
\item \(c\) has a ledger row with all required record fields and
\(K\subseteq\Kind,\ K\ne\varnothing\).
\item \(\ell\in\mathsf{Lev}_{\mathrm{cert}}\), and
\(E=(E_{\mathrm{atom}},E_{\mathrm{path}})\) is a submitted typed evidence
surface with finite atom and path components,
\(E_{\mathrm{atom}}\cup E_{\mathrm{path}}\ne\varnothing\), every member of
\(E_{\mathrm{atom}}\) satisfying the non-path evidence-atom grammar, and every
member of \(E_{\mathrm{path}}\) satisfying the relative support-path grammar.
The two grammars are disjoint, and a string with local support-path syntax is
rejected from \(E_{\mathrm{atom}}\).  Thus there is no untyped membership clause
\(u\in E\) in this theorem: evidence membership means membership in one of the
two typed components displayed here.
\item Let \(G_C=\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)\).  The pass rows form an exact
finite gate graph over \(G_C\): \(R\) is a finite list; every row has a
\texttt{gate\_name} field; the map \(r\mapsto r[\texttt{gate\_name}]\) is a
bijection from the rows of \(R\) onto \(G_C\).  Thus every required gate has one
and only one pass row, and no duplicate, stale, irrelevant, or contradictory
extra pass row is accepted.
\item Each row of \(R\) has the required pass fields, the same level \(\ell\), an
allowed decision, and an evidence pointer in the finite admissible-pointer set
declared for the row.  Prefix matches, wildcard expansion, and bare filesystem
existence are not admissible evidence.
\item \(D\) is present exactly when \(\textsf{discovery}\in K\).
\item \(\mathsf{CommandBoundaryOK}(\ell,B)\) holds in the following typed
sense.  In this schema the canonical command-run source field is
\texttt{source\_commit}; there is no separate \texttt{source\_state} key in
\(\mathsf{CommandBoundaryOK}\).
\end{enumerate}
Write \(F_{\mathrm{cmd}}\) for the five required command-run field names
\[
\{\texttt{command},\texttt{source\_commit},\texttt{environment},
\texttt{exit\_code},\texttt{log\_path}\}.
\]
Relative to the same locked schema row \(\Sigma\), the command-boundary
predicate is the conjunction of the following three typed clauses:
\begin{enumerate}[label={\(\mathrm{B}_{\arabic*}\):}]
\item The locked certificate schema has
\(\Sigma[\texttt{evidence\_levels}]=\mathsf{Lev}_{\mathrm{cert}}\), and the
entry level satisfies \(\ell\in\Sigma[\texttt{evidence\_levels}]\).  Thus the
two command-boundary levels are exactly
\(\{\textsf{path\mbox{-}verified},\textsf{command\mbox{-}run}\}\).  Historical
case boundaries, manifest tags, wildcard-looking schema documentation strings,
and table labels are pointer-boundary atoms only; they are never members of this
level set, even if they occur in a schema artifact-pattern field.
\item If \(\ell=\textsf{path\mbox{-}verified}\), then \(B\) contains
\texttt{boundary.no\_command\_run\_assertion}.
\item If \(\ell=\textsf{command\mbox{-}run}\), then
\texttt{boundary.command\_run\_fields} is a finite dictionary and its key set
contains \(F_{\mathrm{cmd}}\).
\end{enumerate}
For mnemonic clause labels retained from the record specification, let
\(\operatorname{NoE9}(e)\) mean that entry \(e\) satisfies clause E9 and let
\(\operatorname{NoE10}(e)\) mean that entry \(e\) satisfies clause E10.
Thus
\[
\Acc_{\Gamma}(c,C)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigwedge_{i=1}^{6}P_i(c,C).
\]
Record-gate soundness is a separate input predicate
\(\RGSS(P,L,\Gamma)\) on a submitted package \(P\), finite acceptance ledger
\(L\), and dispatch table \(\Gamma\).  It is not derived from
\(\Acc_{\Gamma}\).

\begin{lemma}[Finite schema consequence of record-gate soundness]
\label{lem:rgs-schema-consequence}
If \(\RGSS(P,L,\Gamma)\) holds, then for every accepted claim \(c\in L\) there
exists a certificate \(C\) such that \(\Acc_{\Gamma}(c,C)\).  If the submitted
ledger also imposes a total order on candidate certificates, the
lexicographically least such \(C\) defines a function
\(\mathsf{Cert}_{\Gamma}(c)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is only unpacking of the input predicate \(\RGSS(P,L,\Gamma)\): by
definition that predicate supplies, for each accepted ledger claim, one finite
certificate satisfying the six displayed acceptance predicates.  A total order
on the finite candidate-certificate set gives a unique least satisfying
certificate whenever the set is nonempty.  No implementation soundness of a gate
program is inferred.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact pass-row graph has no conflict resolution]
\label{lem:exact-pass-row-graph-no-conflicts}
Fix a certificate \(C=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\) and let
\(G_C=\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)\).  Predicate \(P_3\) in
\(\Acc_{\Gamma}(c,C)\) is equivalent to the conjunction
\[
\begin{aligned}
\{r[\texttt{gate\_name}]:r\in R\}&=G_C,\\
\forall g\in G_C,\quad
|\{r\in R:r[\texttt{gate\_name}]=g\}|&=1,
\end{aligned}
\]
with every row of \(R\) carrying a \texttt{gate\_name} field.  Therefore
acceptance uses no first-row, last-row, priority, or majority conflict
resolution.  Adding any extra row to an already accepted certificate destroys
\(P_3\): if its gate name is in \(G_C\) it creates multiplicity \(2\) for that
required gate, and if its gate name is outside \(G_C\) it creates a row outside
the required gate set.  Thus this lemma is the local uniqueness repair for
\(P_3\), not a coverage-only condition.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The displayed definition of \(P_3\) says that the projection
\(r\mapsto r[\texttt{gate\_name}]\) is a bijection from the finite row list
\(R\) onto \(G_C\).  A finite-map bijection is exactly surjectivity onto
\(G_C\), injectivity on rows, and absence of undefined gate-name values.
Surjectivity gives at least one row for each required gate; injectivity gives at
most one row for each required gate; and the codomain condition excludes every
row whose gate name is not required.  The displayed cardinality condition is
the same statement written gate-by-gate.  If a new row is appended, the new
projection cannot remain bijective: its name either duplicates an existing
required image or lies outside \(G_C\).  Thus irrelevant, stale, duplicate, or
contradictory rows cannot be ignored while preserving acceptance.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[E10 is exactly the command-run boundary clause]
\label{lem:e10-command-boundary-b3}
Let \(e\) be a parsed ledger entry whose evidence level is
\(\ell=\textsf{command\mbox{-}run}\), and let \(B=e[\texttt{boundary}]\).  Use
the canonical field name \texttt{source\_commit}; a clause using
\texttt{source\_state} would not be this lemma's E10 clause.
Then
\[
\operatorname{NoE10}(e)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{B}_3\text{ of }\mathsf{CommandBoundaryOK}(\ell,B).
\]
Equivalently, clause E10 rejects exactly when the command-run boundary clause
fails:
\[
\mathrm{E10}(e)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\neg \mathrm{B}_3.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a command-run entry the displayed E10 clause inspects only the dictionary
path \texttt{boundary.command\_run\_fields}.  It rejects precisely when at least
one member of
\[
F_{\mathrm{cmd}}=
\{\texttt{command},\texttt{source\_commit},\texttt{environment},
\texttt{exit\_code},\texttt{log\_path}\}
\]
is absent from that dictionary.  Clause \(\mathrm{B}_2\) of
\(\mathsf{CommandBoundaryOK}\) handles the different path-verified case and is
not evaluated for a command-run entry.  Clause \(\mathrm{B}_3\) says precisely
that the same dictionary exists as
a finite dictionary and that its key set contains this same finite set
\(F_{\mathrm{cmd}}\).  Therefore satisfaction of E10 is equivalent to
\(\mathrm{B}_3\), and rejection by E10 is its negation.  No other displayed
clause inspects these five command-run keys.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Design-pattern specification for certificate-record acceptance]
\label{thm:certificate-record-checker-spec}
This theorem gives the finite mathematical relation for certificate-record
acceptance.  Use the TCB convention of Tables~\ref{tab:replay-tcb} and
\ref{tab:replay-byte-binding}: JSON inputs are raw byte files decoded as
\texttt{utf-8-sig} and parsed by \texttt{json.loads}; missing files,
inadmissible relative paths, symlinked inputs, and JSON parse failures reject.
Let \(\operatorname{BoundPath}(p)\) mean that \(p\) is an admissible ordinary
relative path under the submitted paper root, is not a symlink, is a regular
file, and is bound by an exact SHA--256 row in the submitted digest ledger.
Executable replay of the current JSON records additionally requires the archive
contract of Section~\ref{sec:replay-artifact-boundary}.  Python-script
conformance is the separate premise of
Lemma~\ref{lem:certificate-record-scriptok-separate}.

The typed string grammars used below are complete and whole-string matched.  Let
\(\alpha\) range over nonempty strings matching
\texttt{[A-Za-z0-9\_:.-]+}, let \(\beta\) range over nonempty strings matching
\texttt{[A-Za-z0-9\_./-]+}, and require every
\(\beta\)-path to contain no empty segment, no \texttt{.} segment, no
\texttt{..} segment, no leading slash, and no backslash.  Then
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
T_{\mathrm{table}}&::=&\texttt{table:}\alpha,\\
T_{\mathrm{formal}}&::=&(\texttt{definition:}|\texttt{lemma:}|\texttt{proposition:}|
\texttt{theorem:}|\texttt{corollary:})\alpha,\\
T_{\mathrm{manifest}}&::=&\texttt{manifest:}\beta,\\
T_{\mathrm{hist}}&::=&\texttt{historical-boundary:}\alpha,\\
T_{\mathrm{snapshot}}&::=&\texttt{review\_bundle/case\_snapshots/}\beta,\\
E_{\mathrm{atom}}&::=&T_{\mathrm{table}}\mid T_{\mathrm{formal}}
\mid T_{\mathrm{manifest}}\mid T_{\mathrm{hist}},\\
E_{\mathrm{path}}&::=&\texttt{certificates/}\beta\mid\texttt{review\_bundle/}\beta
\mid\texttt{submission\_abstract.tex}\mid\texttt{main.tex}
\mid\texttt{appendix\_full\_technical\_ledger.tex}
\mid\texttt{source\_interface\_record.json}
\mid\texttt{PIPELINE.md}\mid\texttt{SOURCE\_MAP.md}
\mid\texttt{ARTIFACT\_INVENTORY.md}
\mid\texttt{theorem\_inventory.json}.
\end{array}
\]
A manifest tag is accepted only when the \(\beta\) key is present in \(M\), a
historical-boundary tag only when an eligible case row names the exact tag, and
a snapshot path only when it is a \(T_{\mathrm{snapshot}}\) string in an eligible
submitted case row.  These productions are pairwise disjoint: every atom token
contains a colon before any slash-bearing path position, while every path token
starts with one of the displayed path prefixes or one of the three exact file
names and contains no colon.  Strings with wildcards, prefixes, absolute-path
syntax, malformed \texttt{..} segments, or unlisted table/manifest/historical
names fail parsing before \(\PointerOK\) is evaluated.

Let \(\Gamma\) be the finite schema dispatch table parsed from the same
digest-bound schema input as \(\Sigma\).  Define
\(\operatorname{AcceptsCertSpec}(\Sigma,L,I,Q,M,C,\Gamma)\) to mean that the
parsed certificate schema, ledger, submission-interface map, primary-claim
inventory, review-bundle manifest, case-row manifest, and dispatch table satisfy
all displayed clauses M1--M3, L1--E10, R1--R4, S1--S2, and P1--P5 below.  The
theorem's logical form is therefore
\[
\operatorname{ParsedCurrentRecordsOK}\Longrightarrow
\bigl(\operatorname{AcceptsCertSpec}(\Sigma,L,I,Q,M,C,\Gamma)\Longleftrightarrow
\text{all displayed clauses pass}\bigr),
\]
where \(\operatorname{ParsedCurrentRecordsOK}\) abbreviates the byte-binding,
JSON-decoding, root-normalization, and grammar-parsing preconditions just stated,
including unique derivation of \(\Gamma\) from the digest-bound schema row.

For a parsed ledger entry with typed evidence surface
\(E=(E_{\mathrm{atom}},E_{\mathrm{path}})\), schema row \(\Sigma\),
review-bundle manifest dictionary \(M\), and submitted case-row manifest \(C\),
write \(S=C.\texttt{case\_evidence}\) for the submitted snapshot list.
\(E_{\mathrm{atom}}\) contains only closed evidence atoms: whole-string table or
formal labels, manifest-row tags whose key is present in \(M\), and exact
historical-boundary tags named by an eligible submitted case row.  It does not
contain wildcard patterns, prefixes, raw absolute paths, or raw relative support
paths.  \(E_{\mathrm{path}}\) contains local support paths and is admissible only
through \(\operatorname{BoundPath}\).  These two parsed lists are typed by
disjoint grammars, so a string with relative support-path syntax cannot be
accepted by first placing it in \(E_{\mathrm{atom}}\).  Define the digest-bound
boundary predicates used by evidence pointers as follows.
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{ManifestBoundary}(M)=
\{\,p :\;& p \text{ is a relative path named in }M,\\
& \operatorname{sha256}(\operatorname{readBytes}(p))
=M[p].\operatorname{sha256}\,\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here ``relative'' and \(\operatorname{readBytes}\) are interpreted by the TCB
above: absolute paths, \texttt{..}, symlinks, directories, and files without an
exact SHA--256 ledger row reject before membership is considered.  Let
\(\operatorname{TableBoundary}\) be the finite set of whole-string table and
formal-label atoms already printed in the submitted paper surface, namely atoms
of the forms \(\texttt{table:}x\), \(\texttt{proposition:}x\),
\(\texttt{lemma:}x\), \(\texttt{theorem:}x\), and \(\texttt{corollary:}x\).  Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{SnapshotBoundary}(S)=
\{\,p :\;& p \text{ occurs in an eligible submitted snapshot list}\\
& \text{and passes the digest checks}\,\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
A submitted snapshot list is eligible only through
Table~\ref{tab:case-row-eligibility}: its case name is in the fixed case set, its
local-path list and reviewable-snapshot list have the same length, every
snapshot path is relative under the submitted root, every snapshot is an ordinary
non-symlink file, its digest equals the submitted digest row, and the row
boundary declares historical or path-verified scope.

The admissible-pointer equivalence used by R4 is exactly the digest-bound finite
promotion predicate
\[
\begin{aligned}
\PointerOK(u,E,M,S)\Longleftrightarrow{}&
u\in E_{\mathrm{atom}}
\vee (u\in E_{\mathrm{path}}\wedge \operatorname{BoundPath}(u))\\
&{}\vee u\in \operatorname{ManifestBoundary}(M)\\
&{}\vee u\in \operatorname{TableBoundary}
\vee u\in \operatorname{SnapshotBoundary}(S).
\end{aligned}
\]
This displayed typed predicate is the entire R4 predicate.  There is no further
well-formedness, reachability, or filesystem-existence disjunct hidden outside
this equivalence.  The untyped
shortcut \(u\in E\) is not available in this theorem: a path-looking string that
is not in \(E_{\mathrm{path}}\) and digest-bound by
\(\operatorname{BoundPath}\) fails R4 even if the same bytes could be written as
an atom string in some malformed record.
The evidence-surface membership clauses are literal membership in the typed
finite certificate evidence lists, not filesystem queries.  A non-path evidence
atom is admitted only through \(E_{\mathrm{atom}}\) after the closed atom grammar
above has rejected wildcard patterns, prefixes, raw paths, and historical tags
not named by an eligible case row.  A local support path is admitted through
\(E_{\mathrm{path}}\) only when the same string also satisfies
\(\operatorname{BoundPath}\).  A file's existence under the submitted root never
promotes it into evidence by itself.

\begin{proof}
After the parsing precondition succeeds, every loop is over a finite JSON array
or over a finite dictionary supplied by the schema.  The displayed relation is
the conjunction of the complements of the listed rejecting clauses.  Each clause
is a Boolean predicate over the parsed records, schema domains, and dispatch
table; no clause mutates those data.  Hence a rejecting clause holds exactly on
violation of its displayed predicate.

The ledger-entry branches are precisely the acceptance predicates.  E1 together
with E2 and E3 is \(P_1\): the row has all required fields and a nonempty
\(K\subseteq\Kind\).  E4 and E5 are \(P_2\): the byte-bound schema row locks the
evidence-level vocabulary to \(\mathsf{Lev}_{\mathrm{cert}}\), the entry level is
in that exact set, and the evidence surface is nonempty, finite, and typed.
The historical-boundary atoms admitted by E5 are pointer tokens, not additional
evidence levels.  E6, R1 restricted to the
\texttt{gate\_name} field, and E7 are \(P_3\): there is a finite pass-row list,
every row has a gate name, and the gate-name projection is a bijection onto the
assigned gate set \(\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)\).  Consequently a missing row,
duplicate row for the same assigned gate, contradictory pair of rows for the
same gate, stale row for an unassigned gate, or irrelevant extra row all violate
the displayed relation and cannot be hidden behind mere coverage.  R1--R4
are \(P_4\): every pass row has the remaining required fields, agrees with the
parent evidence level, has an allowed decision, and has a permitted evidence
pointer according to the displayed finite relation
\(\PointerOK(u,E,M,S)\).  Here \(E\) is always the ordered typed pair
\((E_{\mathrm{atom}},E_{\mathrm{path}})\); the proof never uses an untyped
premise \(u\in E\).  The R4 branch is therefore an exact typed membership test in
the finite union
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{atom}}
&{}\cup \{u\in E_{\mathrm{path}}:\operatorname{BoundPath}(u)\}\\
&{}\cup \operatorname{ManifestBoundary}(M)\cup
\operatorname{TableBoundary}\cup \operatorname{SnapshotBoundary}(S).
\end{aligned}
\]
R4 fails iff the pointer is outside that union.  Both directions of this
statement are by the same five typed disjuncts: membership in
\(E_{\mathrm{atom}}\), digest-bound membership in \(E_{\mathrm{path}}\),
manifest-boundary membership, table-boundary membership, or snapshot-boundary
membership.  No inclusion into a larger evidence set is used.  Consequently the
equivalence
\[
\text{R4 passes}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\PointerOK(u,E,M,S)
\]
is literal after expanding \(E\) as the typed pair above.  If \(u\) is a local
support path, membership through the evidence surface requires exact listing in
\(E_{\mathrm{path}}\) and \(\operatorname{BoundPath}(u)\).  The typed parse
precondition forbids listing that path-looking string in \(E_{\mathrm{atom}}\),
so atom membership cannot bypass byte binding.  Manifest membership requires a
named relative manifest path and equality between the printed SHA--256 field and
the hash of the bytes read at that path.  Snapshot membership requires
occurrence in an eligible submitted snapshot list and the same relative-root and
digest checks.
If \(u\) is a table or formal-label atom, the whole string must be in the
submitted \(\operatorname{TableBoundary}\).  Thus the proof uses no rule of the form
``pointer denotes an existing file under the root'': clause R4 fails
exactly when
\[
\neg\PointerOK(u,E,M,S).
\]
Prefix matching, filesystem existence, schema wildcards, and nonlisted table or
snapshot names all fail R4.
E8 is \(P_5\), the
exact discovery-interface discipline.  Because E4 checks the byte-bound schema
row \(\texttt{schema.evidence\_levels}\) for equality with the certificate set
\[
\mathsf{Lev}_{\mathrm{cert}}=
\{\textsf{path\mbox{-}verified},\textsf{command\mbox{-}run}\},
\]
and then checks \(\ell\) against that same parsed set, E4 is exactly the
\(\mathrm{B}_1\) clause of \(\mathsf{CommandBoundaryOK}\).  It does not consult
the schema's artifact-pattern list and does not treat historical case-boundary
strings as levels.  Those strings are checked only through \(\PointerOK\) as
closed pointer atoms or snapshot boundaries, so they cannot make E4 pass and
cannot create a third command-boundary level.  For
\(P_6\),
clause E9 is exactly the path-verified implication
\(\mathsf{CommandBoundaryOK}.\mathrm{B}_2\): on a path-verified row it fails
precisely when the no-command-run assertion is absent, and on any other level it
is inactive.  Clause E10 is exactly the command-run implication
\(\mathsf{CommandBoundaryOK}.\mathrm{B}_3\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:e10-command-boundary-b3}: on a command-run row it fails precisely
when the five required command-run fields are not all present under
\texttt{boundary.command\_run\_fields}, and on any other level it is inactive.
Thus E9 and E10 together are not an additional predicate; they are
the two level-specific branches of \(P_6\).  L1 and L2 are the current-package
row lock selecting the seven submitted entries; they do not strengthen
\(\Acc_{\Gamma}\) for an individual row, but they fix the finite ledger to which
the theorem is applied.  M1--M3, S1--S2, and P1--P5 are likewise the manifest,
submission-interface, and primary-claim row locks explicitly named in the
statement.

If the displayed relation accepts, no listed rejecting clause holds; therefore
each selected ledger entry satisfies all six predicates defining
\(\Acc_{\Gamma}\), and the three families of row locks also hold.  Conversely,
if all six acceptance predicates and all row locks hold, then every listed
rejecting condition is false, so the displayed relation accepts.  This proves
exact equivalence between the printed finite predicate and the displayed
mathematical relation.  Script conformance and the stronger coordinates are
governed by Lemma~\ref{lem:certificate-record-scriptok-separate} and
Table~\ref{tab:scope-register}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[CurrentLedgerOK, row by row]
\label{lem:current-ledger-ok}
Let \(L_{\mathrm{cur}}\) be the seven parsed entries displayed in
Table~\ref{tab:current-ledger-instantiation}, let \(\Sigma\) be the locked schema
row with the dispatch table printed in
\artifactpath{review_bundle/certificate_schema.json}, and let the finite
manifest, table, and snapshot boundaries be the ones used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate-record-checker-spec}.  Then
\[
\operatorname{CurrentLedgerOK}(L_{\mathrm{cur}},\Sigma)
\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigwedge_{e\in\{\texttt{a1},\ldots,\texttt{a7}\}}
\Acc_{\Gamma}(e,C_e)
\]
holds for the certificates \(C_e=(K_e,\ell_e,E_e,H_e,R_e,D_e,B_e)\) printed in
the table.  In particular, \(L_{\mathrm{cur}}\) satisfies L1--L2 and every row
satisfies E1--E10 and R1--R4 of
Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate-record-checker-spec}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First inspect the row keys: the table has exactly the seven entries
\(\texttt{a1},\ldots,\texttt{a7}\), so L1 and L2 hold.  Every displayed
\(K_e\) is nonempty and is a subset of the seven-kind domain
\(\Kind\), and every displayed level is
\(\textsf{path\mbox{-}verified}\in\mathsf{Lev}_{\mathrm{cert}}\).  The evidence
column separates each surface into \(E_{\mathrm{atom}}\) and \(E_{\mathrm{path}}\).
The atom entries are whole-string table, formal-label, manifest, or historical
boundary tokens of the grammars in Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate-record-checker-spec};
the path entries are relative support paths in the displayed path grammar.  Thus
E1--E6 hold for every row once the byte-bound parse preconditions of the theorem
hold.

It remains only to check the exact gate graph, discovery parity, pointers, and
level boundary for each row.  The schema dispatch assigns traceability to
\texttt{inventory} and \texttt{marker-existence}; route to \texttt{promotion},
\texttt{overlap}, and \texttt{submission-state}; submission to
\texttt{submission-pack}; discovery to \texttt{pinned-discovery} and
\texttt{theorem-content-delta}; and artifact to \texttt{status} and
\texttt{limitation-ledger}.
For \(\texttt{a1}\) the two printed rows are exactly the traceability gates.  For
\(\texttt{a2}\) the four printed rows are exactly the union of the route and
submission gates.  For \(\texttt{a3}\) the three printed rows are exactly the
route gates.  For \(\texttt{a4}\) the four printed rows are exactly the union of
the route and submission gates.  For \(\texttt{a5}\) the two printed rows are
exactly the discovery gates.  For \(\texttt{a6}\) the two printed rows are exactly
the artifact gates.  For \(\texttt{a7}\) the three printed rows are exactly the
union of the traceability and submission gates.  Hence E7 holds row by row, with
no missing, duplicate, or extra gate row.

The discovery-interface column is present only for \(\texttt{a5}\), the unique
row with \(\textsf{discovery}\in K_e\), and absent for the other six rows, so E8
holds.  Each evidence pointer printed in the fourth column is literally either a
member of that row's \(E_{\mathrm{atom}}\), a member of that row's
\(E_{\mathrm{path}}\) subject to the theorem's digest-bound
\(\operatorname{BoundPath}\) precondition, or a manifest/table/snapshot boundary
member already admitted by \(\PointerOK\).  Therefore R1--R4 hold for every
pass row.  Finally, all seven rows have level \(\textsf{path\mbox{-}verified}\)
and their boundary column contains
\texttt{boundary.no\_command\_run\_assertion}; this is exactly
\(\mathsf{CommandBoundaryOK}.\mathrm{B}_2\), while E10 is inactive because no
row has level \(\textsf{command\mbox{-}run}\).  Thus every row satisfies
\(P_1,\ldots,P_6\), proving the displayed
\(\operatorname{CurrentLedgerOK}\) assertion.  This row-by-row lemma is still a
parsed-record theorem: it does not assert that
\artifactpath{review_bundle/verify_certificate_records.py} accepts exactly these
rows unless the separate \(\ScriptOK_{\mathrm{cert}}\) premise is supplied.

including the typed \(\PointerOK\) rule and the symlink-rejecting digest-bound

\(\ScriptOK_{\mathrm{cert}}\) justify
citing that script run as an implementation of the certificate-record

\begin{lemma}[Admissible pointers are exactly finite promoted atoms or bytes]
\label{lem:pointerok-exactness}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate-record-checker-spec}, for fixed parsed
\((E_{\mathrm{atom}},E_{\mathrm{path}},M,S)\), a pointer \(u\) is accepted by R4
if and only if \(u\) belongs to the finite union displayed in the definition of
\(\PointerOK(u,E,M,S)\).  In particular, none of the following facts is
sufficient for R4: \(u\) merely exists as a file under the root, \(u\) shares a
prefix with a manifest key, \(u\) matches a schema wildcard-looking pattern, or
\(u\) is a table, snapshot, or historical-boundary string not named by the
corresponding finite boundary set.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand the definition:
\begin{align*}
\PointerOK(u,E,M,S)\Longleftrightarrow{}&
u\in E_{\mathrm{atom}}\\
&{}\vee (u\in E_{\mathrm{path}}\wedge \operatorname{BoundPath}(u))\\
&{}\vee u\in\operatorname{ManifestBoundary}(M)\\
&{}\vee u\in\operatorname{TableBoundary}\\
&{}\vee u\in\operatorname{SnapshotBoundary}(S).
\end{align*}
Each disjunct is finite and closed.  The atom component is literal membership
after the closed atom grammar; the path component is literal evidence-list
membership plus the digest-bound relative-root predicate; the manifest and
snapshot components require named submitted rows and SHA--256 equality; and the
table component is a finite whole-string label set.  No disjunct mentions bare
filesystem existence, prefix closure, or wildcard expansion.  Therefore R4
passes exactly on that finite promoted set and rejects every pointer outside it.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Richardson-normalizer obstruction}
\label{subsec:richardson-normalizer-obstruction}
The paper uses Richardson undecidability only in the following formal reduction
shape.  This subsection is deliberately separated from the replay theorem above:
it gives a boundary for possible semantic normalizer claims, not another
accepted coordinate of the submitted package.

Fix the Richardson source grammar \(\RE\) in the following imported-theorem
sense.  Its elementary expressions are the single-real-variable expressions
generated from \(x\), rational constants, the constants \(\pi\) and \(\log 2\),
the operations \(+,-,\times\), composition, and the primitives
\(\sin\), \(\exp\), and \(|\cdot|\).  This is the identity-problem fragment
obtained from Richardson's 1968 theorem under its condition that the expression
class contains an absolute-value representative; the later citations are
background, not a different source theorem.  Let
\(\Eq_{\RE}(r,0)\) mean equality to zero on that theorem's own specified domain
convention.  Thus \(\Eq_{\RE}\) is not introduced here as equality on an
unstated or possibly empty domain; it is the identity predicate supplied by the
source theorem itself.

\begin{theorem}[Richardson identity undecidability input]
\label{thm:richardson-source-input}
For the effective elementary-expression grammar \(\RE\) just described, no
algorithm decides \(\Eq_{\RE}(r,0)\) for all \(r\in\RE\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the cited Richardson undecidability theorem for elementary expression
identity~\cite{Richardson1968}, used with the theorem's own grammar and domain
convention.  The later simplification literature records the same obstruction as
the fundamental barrier to canonical elementary simplification
\cite{Caviness1970,Wang1974}.  The present paper imports only this theorem
statement; it does not derive it from the package's finite artifact rows, from
empty-domain equality, or from a target-language normal-form convention.
\end{proof}

For a binary source comparison write
\[
  \Eq_{\RE}(r,s)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Eq_{\RE}(r-s,0).
\]
This derived predicate inherits the same domain convention from
Theorem~\ref{thm:richardson-source-input}.  Let \(\TE\) be any proposed target
fragment whose objects are finite strings in a decidable language, and let
\(\Sem_{\TE}\) be its stated semantics.  Equality
\(\Sem_{\TE}(t)=\Sem_{\TE}(u)\) denotes equality of the semantic objects in that
stated target semantics, including whatever domain information the target
semantics assigns.  No separate filesystem, artifact-row, or empty-domain
convention is hidden in this equality.

A translation is a total computable map \(\tau:\RE\to\TE\).  A finite target
normalizer is a total computable map \(\Norm:\TE\to\TE\) whose outputs have
decidable finite-string equality.  The data needed for a Richardson-normalizer
claim are the following three exactness conditions, for all \(r,s\in\RE\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Eq_{\RE}(r,s)&\Rightarrow
  \Sem_{\TE}(\tau(r))=\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(s)),\\
\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(r))=\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(s))&\Rightarrow
  \Eq_{\RE}(r,s),\\
\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(r))=\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(s))&\Longleftrightarrow
  \Norm(\tau(r))=\Norm(\tau(s)).
\end{aligned}
\]
These requirements are the missing data behind any phrase such as
``Richardson normalizer obstruction'': the exact Richardson source theorem, the
total computable translation, the target semantic equality, and the decidable
finite normal forms must all be named.

\begin{lemma}[Richardson reduction against identity-exact finite normalizers]
\label{lem:richardson-normalizer-reduction}
Suppose there are a target fragment \(\TE\), a total computable translation
\(\tau:\RE\to\TE\), and a total computable finite target normalizer \(\Norm\)
such that, for all \(r,s\in\RE\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Eq_{\RE}(r,s)\Rightarrow
  \Sem_{\TE}(\tau(r))=\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(s)),\\
&\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(r))=\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(s))\Rightarrow
  \Eq_{\RE}(r,s),\\
&\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(r))=\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(s))\Longleftrightarrow
  \Norm(\tau(r))=\Norm(\tau(s)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Then there is a decision procedure for \(\Eq_{\RE}(r,0)\).  Consequently no
paper-local syntax catalogue, finite witness list, or target normal form can be
used as a semantic Richardson identity oracle unless it supplies these
hypotheses and thereby accepts this decidability consequence.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Given \(r\in\RE\), compute the two finite target objects \(\tau(r)\) and
\(\tau(0)\), normalize both, and compare the resulting finite strings.  This is
an algorithm because \(\tau\) and \(\Norm\) are total computable and the target
normal forms have decidable finite equality.

If
\(\Norm(\tau(r))=\Norm(\tau(0))\), the normalizer exactness hypothesis gives
\(\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(r))=\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(0))\), and the semantic reflection
hypothesis gives \(\Eq_{\RE}(r,0)\).  If the normal forms are unequal, the
contrapositive of normalizer exactness gives
\(\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(r))\ne\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(0))\).  The semantic preservation
hypothesis would imply this target equality from \(\Eq_{\RE}(r,0)\), so by
contraposition \(\neg\Eq_{\RE}(r,0)\).  Thus the displayed finite-string
comparison decides \(\Eq_{\RE}(r,0)\) for arbitrary \(r\in\RE\), contradicting
Theorem~\ref{thm:richardson-source-input}.  The reduction never uses equality on
an empty common domain: the only source predicate is Richardson's own
\(\Eq_{\RE}\), and target equality is the explicitly supplied equality of
\(\Sem_{\TE}\)-objects.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Independent execution, packaging, and software identity}
The replay boundary in this paper is deliberately narrower than the major
infrastructure used for computational reproducibility, artifact packaging,
supply-chain integrity, and certificate checking.  RO-Crate packages research
artefacts with machine-readable metadata, identifiers, provenance links, and
relations among data, software, and methods~\cite{SoilandReyesEtAl2022ROCrate}.
In-toto records and verifies signed software-supply-chain layouts and link
metadata across the steps that produce deployable bytes
\cite{TorresAriasEtAl2019InToto}.  DRAT-trim checks a proof-object format for
SAT refutations and can trim or emit dependency information for such
proofs~\cite{WetzlerHeuleHunt2014DRATTrim}.  Guix gives transparent,
verifiable, and long-term reproducible computational environments by recording
the dependency graph and package definitions needed to rebuild software
\cite{ValletMichonneauTournier2022Guix}.  These are stronger frameworks than
the checker specified here: they package a research object, attest a supply
chain, check a proof calculus, or reconstruct an execution environment.  The
present checker does none of those four jobs.

CODECHECK asks an independent executor to run submitted code and issue a
certificate for the observed computation~\cite{EglenEtAl2021CODECHECK}.  Nix and
NixOS make software deployment and system configuration more reproducible by
describing dependencies functionally and isolating build products
\cite{DolstraDeJongeVisser2004Nix,DolstraLohPierron2010NixOS}.  Software
Heritage provides long-term archival source identifiers, including SWHIDs, for
referring to exact source-code objects~\cite{DiCosmoGruenpeterZacchiroli2020SWHID}.
FAIR4RS states findability, accessibility, interoperability, and reusability
principles for research software~\cite{BarkerEtAl2022FAIR4RS}.

This article uses a different evidence unit: a finite row is accepted only for
the predicate printed in the row and only after the raw bytes named by the row
match the submitted digest convention.  That choice is a smaller
publication-coordinate design pattern, not a replacement for the systems above.
Clean-machine reproducibility, archival identity, and full research-software
FAIRness are therefore upgrade coordinates in Table~\ref{tab:scope-register},

external-archive, venue-status, or

\subsection{Zeckendorf normalizer}
Use the Fibonacci convention \(F_1=1,F_2=2,F_{k+2}=F_{k+1}+F_k\).  A finite
word \(c=(c_k)\) is normal when \(c_k\in\{0,1\}\), finitely many digits are
nonzero, and no adjacent digits are both \(1\).  Its value is
\[
\Val(c)=\sum_k c_kF_k .
\]
Let \(X_{\mathrm{fin}}\) be the set of all finite normal words, and let \(X_m\)
be the normal words supported in the window \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\).  The
finite-window lemma below proves that the largest value in \(X_m\) is
\(F_{m+1}-1\), that every value below \(F_{m+1}\) is reached by greedy
normalization inside the window, and hence that \(\Val_m:=\Val|_{X_m}\) is a
bijection
\[
\Val_m:X_m\longrightarrow \{0,\ldots,F_{m+1}-1\}.
\]
For finite-window output we use the following precise rule.  The inverse
\(\Val_m^{-1}(t)\) is defined exactly for \(0\le t<F_{m+1}\) and is computed by
the greedy normalizer below with the additional assertion that no selected index
exceeds \(m\).  If an arithmetic result \(t\) satisfies \(t\ge F_{m+1}\), the
windowed operation returns the distinguished overflow value \(\bot_m\).  The
unbounded protocol instead enlarges the output window to
\[
\omega(t):=\min\{m:t<F_{m+1}\}
\]
and returns the element of \(X_{\omega(t)}\).

The two transport operators below have different codomains and therefore use
different binary operations.  Fix a set \(S\), write \(\operatorname{End}(S)\)
for its self-maps with composition \(\circ_S\), and put
\(\mathcal E_S:=\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{End}(S))\), with composition
\(\circ_{\mathcal E_S}\).  For
\(P,Q:\operatorname{End}(S)\to\operatorname{End}(S)\), define the fixed-output
product
\[
(P\odot_S Q)(f):=P(f)\circ_S Q(f)\qquad(f\in\operatorname{End}(S)).
\]
For \(A,B:\mathcal E_S\to\mathcal E_S\), define the exponentiation-operator
composition
\[
(A\bullet_S B)(T):=A(B(T))\qquad(T\in\mathcal E_S).
\]
Thus \(\odot_S\) composes the two output maps obtained from the same input
\(f\), while \(\bullet_S\) composes the two operators acting on the variable
\(T\).

\begin{quote}\scriptsize
\textbf{Algorithm \(L1\)-Z.}
Input finite normal words \(c,d\), and optionally a requested output window
\(m\).
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\textsc{FibTable}(m): & \text{return }F_1,\ldots,F_{m+1}.\\
\Val(c): & s:=0;\ \text{for each nonzero digit }c_k\text{ set }s:=s+F_k;\ \text{return }s.\\
\NF(n): &
\begin{array}[t]{@{}l@{}}
r:=n;\quad e_j:=0\text{ for all }j;\\
\textbf{while }r>0\textbf{ do}\\
\quad k:=\max\{i:F_i\le r\};\\
\quad e_k:=1;\\
\quad r:=r-F_k;\\
\textbf{end while};\\
\textbf{return }e.
\end{array}\\
\Val_m^{-1}(t): & \text{if }t\ge F_{m+1}\text{ return }\bot_m;\ \text{else return }\NF(t)\in X_m.\\
\Add(c,d): & \text{return }\NF(\Val(c)+\Val(d)).\\
\Mul(c,d): & \text{return }\NF(\Val(c)\Val(d)).\\
\Add_m(c,d): & \text{return }\Val_m^{-1}(\Val(c)+\Val(d)).\\
\Mul_m(c,d): & \text{return }\Val_m^{-1}(\Val(c)\Val(d)).\\
\Phi^S_0(c_n)(f): & \text{return }f^{\circ_S n},\text{ for }f\in\operatorname{End}(S).\\
\Phi^S_1(c_n)(T): & \text{return }T^{\circ_{\mathcal E_S} n},\text{ for }T\in\mathcal E_S.
\end{array}
\]
\end{quote}

\begin{lemma}[Finite-window greedy bound]
\label{lem:finite-window-greedy-bound}
For every \(m\ge0\) and every integer \(t\) with \(0\le t<F_{m+1}\), the
greedy normalizer \(\NF(t)\) never selects an index greater than \(m\).  Hence
\(\NF(t)\in X_m\), \(\Val(\NF(t))=t\), and every value in
\(\{0,\ldots,F_{m+1}-1\}\) has a representing word in \(X_m\).  Moreover every
word in \(X_m\) has value at most \(F_{m+1}-1\); consequently
\[
\Val_m:X_m\longrightarrow \{0,\ldots,F_{m+1}-1\}
\]
is surjective, and it is injective by Zeckendorf uniqueness.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Run the displayed greedy loop on \(t\).  After each completed iteration, let
\(h\) be the last selected index, if one exists.  The loop invariant is the
following state predicate:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{I}(r,e,h):\quad
t&=r+\sum_j e_jF_j,\\
r&\ge0,\\
e_j&\in\{0,1\}\quad\text{for all }j,\\
e_je_{j+1}&=0\quad\text{for all }j,\\
&\text{and if a last index }h\text{ exists, then }(h=1\Rightarrow r=0),\\
&\text{and if a last index }h\ge2\text{ exists, then }r<F_{h-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Initially
\(r=t\), all digits of \(e\) are zero, and no last index exists, so the
invariant holds.
This is a state predicate involving only the current remainder, the current
finite word, and the last selected index; it does not quantify over future
selected indices.

Suppose an iteration chooses \(k=\max\{i:F_i\le r\}\), sets \(e_k:=1\), and
replaces \(r\) by \(r'=r-F_k\).  Maximality gives
\[
F_k\le r<F_{k+1}.
\]
For \(k\ge2\),
\[
r'=r-F_k<F_{k+1}-F_k=F_{k-1},
\]
and for \(k=1\) the equality \(F_1=1\le r<F_2=2\) forces \(r'=0\).  Thus the
new remainder satisfies the branched remainder bound with last selected index
\(h'=k\).

It remains to check that the newly written digit is not adjacent to an older
one.  If this is the first selection, there is no older selected index.  If the
previous last selected index was \(h=1\), the old invariant gave \(r=0\), so the
loop would not run.  If the previous last selected index was \(h\ge2\), the old
invariant gave \(r<F_{h-1}\).  Together with \(F_k\le r\), this implies
\[
F_k<F_{h-1}.
\]
Since the Fibonacci sequence is strictly increasing in the indices used here,
\(k\le h-2\).  Hence the newly selected index is at most \(h-2\), so writing
\(e_k=1\) cannot create adjacent \(1\)'s with any earlier selected digit.  The
value identity and nonnegativity are immediate from replacing \(r\) by
\(r-F_k\).

The nonnegative integer remainder strictly decreases whenever the loop runs,
so the loop terminates.  At termination \(r=0\), and the invariant gives
\(\Val(\NF(t))=t\) with a finite normal word.  If \(t=0\), no index is selected.
If \(t>0\), the first selected index \(k\) satisfies
\[
F_k\le t<F_{m+1}.
\]
Therefore \(k\le m\).  The invariant step just proved shows that after selecting
\(k\), the next selected index, if any, is at most \(k-2\); iterating this
argument shows that every later selected index is also at most \(m\).  Hence
\(\NF(t)\in X_m\).  Thus, if \(0\le t<F_{m+1}\), greedy normalization produces a
normal word in \(X_m\) whose value is \(t\).  This proves finite-window
surjectivity of \(\Val_m\).

It remains only to identify the upper edge of the window.  Let
\[
M_m=\max\{\Val(c):c\in X_m\}.
\]
Then \(M_0=0\), \(M_1=1\), and for \(m\ge2\) a maximizing word either has top
digit \(0\), contributing at most \(M_{m-1}\), or has top digit \(1\), in which
case normality forces digit \(m-1\) to be \(0\) and the remaining contribution
is at most \(M_{m-2}\).  Therefore
\[
M_m=\max(M_{m-1},F_m+M_{m-2})=F_{m+1}-1
\]
by induction.  Thus every word in \(X_m\) has value in
\(\{0,\ldots,F_{m+1}-1\}\), while the greedy construction above gives a word in
\(X_m\) for each value in that set.  Zeckendorf uniqueness on finite normal
words gives injectivity of \(\Val_m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Executable Zeckendorf normalizer]
\label{lem:zeckendorf-totality}
\(\NF\) is the multi-iteration greedy algorithm displayed above: the
\textbf{return} line is outside the \textbf{while} block, and the loop invariant
used in the proof is the invariant stated at the start of the proof below.
All inputs \(c,d\) to \(\Val\), \(\Add\), \(\Mul\), \(\Add_m\), and \(\Mul_m\)
are finite normal Zeckendorf words under the convention
\(F_1=1,F_2=2,F_{k+2}=F_{k+1}+F_k\); the windowed inputs lie in \(X_m\).
\(\NF\), \(\Val\), \(\Add\), and \(\Mul\) are total on these stated unbounded
domains.  For each \(m\), \(\Val_m^{-1}\), \(\Add_m\), and \(\Mul_m\) are total
as maps into \(X_m\cup\{\bot_m\}\), and they return \(\bot_m\) exactly on
overflow.  When no overflow occurs,
\begin{align*}
\Val(\NF(n))&=n,\\
\Val(\Add(c,d))&=\Val(c)+\Val(d),\\
\Val(\Mul(c,d))&=\Val(c)\Val(d),\\
\Val(\Add_m(c,d))&=\Val(c)+\Val(d),\\
\Val(\Mul_m(c,d))&=\Val(c)\Val(d).
\end{align*}
If \(c_n=\NF(n)\), then for every set \(S\) the maps have typed domains
\[
\Phi^S_0:X_{\mathrm{fin}}\to
\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{End}(S)),
\qquad
\Phi^S_1:X_{\mathrm{fin}}\to
\operatorname{End}(\mathcal E_S),
\]
where \(\Phi^S_0(c_n)(f)=f^{\circ_S n}\) and
\(\Phi^S_1(c_n)(T)=T^{\circ_{\mathcal E_S} n}\).  With
\(\odot_S\) and \(\bullet_S\) as defined above,
\[
\Phi^S_0(c_{a+b})=\Phi^S_0(c_a)\odot_S\Phi^S_0(c_b)
\quad\text{as maps }\operatorname{End}(S)\to\operatorname{End}(S),
\]
and
\[
\Phi^S_1(c_{ab})=\Phi^S_1(c_a)\bullet_S\Phi^S_1(c_b)
\quad\text{as maps }\mathcal E_S\to\mathcal E_S.
\]
Given a precomputed table \(F_1,\ldots,F_{m+1}\), \(\Val\) on an \(m\)-digit
input and the greedy inverse on a non-overflow \(m\)-window output use \(O(m)\)
comparisons and additions/subtractions of integers of \(O(m)\) Fibonacci-size
bits.  Consequently \(\Add_m\) and \(\Mul_m\) have this \(O(m)\) greedy
normalization cost plus the ordinary integer-arithmetic cost of computing
\(\Val(c)+\Val(d)\) or \(\Val(c)\Val(d)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(\NF(n)\), after every completed iteration let \(\ell\) be the last
selected index, if a selection has occurred.  The loop invariant is the
state predicate
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{I}(r,e,\ell):\quad
n&=r+\sum_j e_jF_j,\\
r&\ge0,\\
e&\text{ is a finite }0\text{-}1\text{ word with no adjacent }1\text{'s},\\
&\text{and if a last index }\ell\text{ exists, then }
   \bigl(\ell=1\Rightarrow r=0\bigr),\\
&\text{and if a last index }\ell\ge2\text{ exists, then }
   r<F_{\ell-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The invariant is true initially, with \(r=n\) and all \(e_j=0\).  In one
iteration the algorithm chooses the largest \(F_k\le r\), sets \(e_k:=1\), and
replaces \(r\) by \(r-F_k\).  This preserves
\(n=r+\sum_j e_jF_j\) and keeps \(r\ge0\).  Write \(r'\) for the new
remainder.  If \(k\ge2\), maximality gives
\[
r<F_{k+1}=F_k+F_{k-1},
\]
so \(r'=r-F_k<F_{k-1}\); if \(k=1\), then \(r'=0\).  Thus the new state
satisfies the remainder bound with last index \(k\).  If there was a previous
last index \(\ell\), then either \(\ell=1\), where the old state has \(r=0\)
and the loop would not run, or \(\ell\ge2\), where the old state has
\(r<F_{\ell-1}\).  Hence, in the only continuing case,
\(F_k\le r<F_{\ell-1}\), which implies \(k\le\ell-2\).  The new \(1\)-digit is
therefore separated from the previous last selected digit by at least one zero,
and the word remains normal.  The strict decrease of the nonnegative integer
\(r\) proves termination.  At termination \(r=0\), so the invariant gives
\(\Val(\NF(n))=n\).  Zeckendorf uniqueness gives that the returned word is the
unique element of \(X_{\mathrm{fin}}\) with value \(n\).  The explicit
\textbf{return} statement is outside the \textbf{while} block; no partial word
is returned after the first subtraction.

For the finite window, the maximum-value calculation satisfies
\(M_m=\max(M_{m-1},F_m+M_{m-2})=F_{m+1}-1\), with \(M_0=0\) and \(M_1=1\), as
proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-window-greedy-bound}.  The same lemma supplies
the missing surjectivity step: if \(0\le t<F_{m+1}\), then greedy never selects
an index greater than \(m\), so \(\NF(t)\in X_m\) and \(\Val(\NF(t))=t\).  Hence
\(\Val_m^{-1}(t)\) returns \(\bot_m\) precisely when \(t\) is outside this
range; otherwise it returns the unique normal word of value \(t\), supported
inside \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\).  The equations for \(\Add\), \(\Mul\), \(\Add_m\),
and \(\Mul_m\) are then immediate from the definitions through \(\Val\), with
the windowed equations restricted to the non-overflow branch.  For the first
transport law, if \(f\in\operatorname{End}(S)\), then
\[
(\Phi^S_0(c_a)\odot_S\Phi^S_0(c_b))(f)
=f^{\circ_S a}\circ_S f^{\circ_S b}
=f^{\circ_S(a+b)}
=\Phi^S_0(c_{a+b})(f).
\]
For the second transport law, if \(T\in\mathcal E_S\), then
\[
(\Phi^S_1(c_a)\bullet_S\Phi^S_1(c_b))(T)
=\Phi^S_1(c_a)\bigl(T^{\circ_{\mathcal E_S} b}\bigr)
=\bigl(T^{\circ_{\mathcal E_S} b}\bigr)^{\circ_{\mathcal E_S} a}
=T^{\circ_{\mathcal E_S}ab}
=\Phi^S_1(c_{ab})(T).
\]
Thus the addition identity is pointwise output composition, whereas the
multiplication identity is composition of exponentiation operators.

For complexity, the table \(F_1,\ldots,F_{m+1}\) fixes the output window and
the overflow test.  A single pass from \(F_m\) downward finds each greedy digit
and subtracts only when that digit is set, so the normalization pass has \(O(m)\) table
comparisons and at most \(m\) integer subtractions.  The input valuation pass is
linear in the input window.  The only additional work in \(\Add_m\) and
\(\Mul_m\) is ordinary integer addition or multiplication before the inverse
normalization.  This is a computable valuation-transport protocol, not an
efficient online or digit-local Zeckendorf arithmetic algorithm; efficient
Zeckendorf arithmetic is a separate topic, e.g.
\cite{AhlbachUsatineFrougnyPippenger2013,Idziaszek2020}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two proof-audit boundaries}
\begin{lemma}[Fibonacci convention reindexing]
\label{lem:fibonacci-reindexing}
Let \(G_0=G_1=1\) and \(G_{j+2}=G_{j+1}+G_j\).  With the manuscript convention
\(F_1=1,F_2=2\), one has \(G_j=F_j\) for \(j\ge1\).  The boundary cases are
\(G_1=F_1=1\) and \(G_2=F_2=2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two displayed boundary cases give the base of the recurrence comparison.
Both sequences satisfy the same two-term recurrence after that point, so
induction gives \(G_j=F_j\) for all \(j\ge1\).  Any imported theorem written in
the \(G_0=G_1=1\) convention must therefore be shifted only at the unused
initial \(G_0\) endpoint; the local \(s=1,2\) cases are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed transfer exponential bound]
\label{lem:fixed-transfer}
Fix a coefficient field \(K\), a complex embedding \(\iota:K\hookrightarrow
\mathbb C\), a matrix \(A\in K^{r\times r}\), and vectors \(u,v\).  Then every
fixed transfer sequence \(s_m=u^{\top}A^mv\) is exponentially bounded after
applying \(\iota\): there exist \(C,\lambda>0\) such that
\(|\iota(s_m)|\le C\lambda^m\) for all \(m\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose any submultiplicative matrix norm on \(\mathbb C^{r\times r}\) after
embedding the coefficients.  Then
\(|\iota(u)^{\top}\iota(A)^m\iota(v)|\le
\|\iota(u)\|\,\|\iota(v)\|\,\|\iota(A)\|^m\), and increasing \(C,\lambda\) handles
the finite initial cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Admissible auxiliary cover]
\label{def:admissible-cover}
For the \(L2\) route, an admissible cover over fixed normal-form sets \(X_m\) is
a computable finite-fibre family whose fibre sizes are explicitly listed,
generated by a recorded grammar with a bound, produced by a checker with a
transcript, or supplied by a fixed finite-state transfer model satisfying
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-transfer}.  These are the certificate-admissible sources.
An arbitrary one-spike family with no recorded grammar, checker, transcript, or
finite-state generator is not certificate-admissible for the software route.  It
may be used only in the separate stress-test reading below, where the
multiplicities are deliberately declared by hand.  Such a stress-test cover is
not an intrinsic Foldm fibre invariant and cannot prove a separation for
finite-state, local, polynomial-size, grammar-generated, artifact-generated, or
uncovered Foldm behaviour.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Factorial-spike stress-test cover]
\label{thm:declared-factorial-spike-cover}
Let \((X_m)_{m\ge0}\) be finite nonempty normal-form source sets and let
\(x_m^\ast\in X_m\) be a computably selected element for each \(m\).  Define the
explicit auxiliary cover
\[
Y_m=
\{(x_m^\ast,i):1\le i\le (m+1)!\}
\cup
\{(x,1):x\in X_m,\ x\ne x_m^\ast\},
\qquad
\pi_m(x,i)=x .
\]
Its fibre multiplicities are
\[
b_m(x_m^\ast)=(m+1)!,\qquad b_m(x)=1\quad(x\ne x_m^\ast),
\]
and its cover moment is
\[
C_m=\sum_{x\in X_m}b_m(x)^2=((m+1)!)^2+|X_m|-1 .
\]
Assume additionally that \(|X_m|\ge1\) is bounded by \(D\rho^m\) for some
\(D,\rho>0\).  Then the declared moment sequence \((C_m)\) is not represented by
any fixed-field finite-state transfer sequence \(u^\top A^m v\) in the sense of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-transfer}.  This is a stress-test statement about the
hand-declared cover data above.  It is not a certificate-admissible generated
cover claim unless an independent grammar, checker transcript, finite table, or
finite-state generator for the same multiplicities is added as part of the
record surface.  It is not part of the compact article's public positive
coordinate set, and it does not assert an intrinsic Foldm, finite-state, local,
polynomial-size, grammar-generated, artifact-generated, or uncovered-cover
separation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The displayed formula for \(Y_m\) is finite over \(X_m\), and the projection
\(\pi_m(x,i)=x\) gives exactly the listed fibres.  Hence the second moment is
\(((m+1)!)^2\) from the distinguished fibre plus one contribution from each of
the other \(|X_m|-1\) fibres.

Suppose \(C_m=u^\top A^m v\) for fixed transfer data over a fixed coefficient
field and fixed complex embedding.  Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-transfer} gives
\(|C_m|\le C\lambda^m\) for constants \(C,\lambda>0\).  On the other hand
\[
C_m\ge ((m+1)!)^2 .
\]
For any fixed \(\lambda>0\), the ratio
\[
\frac{((m+1)!)^2}{\lambda^m}
=(m+1)^2\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{j^2}{\lambda}
\]
tends to infinity, because all factors with \(j>\sqrt{\lambda}\) exceed \(1\)
and their product is unbounded.  Thus no constants \(C,\lambda\) can
exponentially bound \(C_m\), contradicting the fixed-transfer estimate.  The
extra hypothesis on \(|X_m|\) records the usual finite-source growth regime; it
is not needed for the lower-bound contradiction, but prevents the theorem from
being misread as a statement about arbitrary hidden growth in the source fibres.
\end{proof}

The auxiliary-cover stress test is read only through
Theorem~\ref{thm:declared-factorial-spike-cover} and
Definition~\ref{def:admissible-cover}.  Its moment
\(C_m=\sum_{x\in X_m} b_m(x)^2\) is a property of the displayed cover data
\((Y_m,\pi_m,b_m)\).  The theorem rules out a fixed-field finite-state transfer
representation for that declared moment sequence, by the factorial lower bound
and Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-transfer}.  It is therefore an interface stress test,
not a main contribution theorem: it does not identify an intrinsic obstruction
of the underlying Foldm fibres and does not assert strictness for finite-state,
local, polynomial-size, grammar-generated, artifact-generated, or uncovered cover
classes.
