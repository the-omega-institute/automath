\begin{abstract}
AI-assisted formal-mathematics projects need a publication interface that keeps
model output separate from evidence.  We present a compact finite-audit route
for a concrete \emph{newmath}/\emph{automath} theory-to-paper pipeline.  The
public claim is intentionally narrow: a submitted finite replay package certifies
exactly two artifact-level coordinates.  The theorem-inventory coordinate is only
syntactic label coverage for the artifact appendix's declared five \LaTeX{}
environment names; it is not theorem-dependency analysis, proof checking, scope
adequacy, audit-record completeness, or semantic theorem completeness.  The
second coordinate is bounded fixed-replay record-gate soundness.  The package
does not certify a fresh Lean/BEDC rebuild, artifact semantics, public archive
equivalence, venue status, or whole-program implementation soundness.  The
technical ledger, generated theorem wrappers, and digest tables are supplied as
artifact support, not as the public article body.
\end{abstract}

\section{Finite-audit route}
Proof assistants check obligations; they do not decide which generated objects
may support which publication claims.  Our pipeline therefore records every
accepted increment through six roles: source object, formal interface, finite
evidence row, advisory agent action, deterministic gate, and human promotion
boundary.  A generated theorem, review note, log, digest, or support file becomes
load-bearing only at the coordinate named by its finite gate.

The submitted package has two reader-facing interfaces.  This article is the route paper;
the separate artifact archive is summarized in the manifest tables below.  Long
file names, command lines, generated wrappers, finite ledgers, replay
certificates, and digest tables are treated as manifest entries rather than as
inline theorem text.  They are cited only as support for the finite replay
package and are not inlined into the public paper.  In particular, a statement
mentioning a local inventory, generated theorem wrapper, command log, digest
row, review-bundle path, or archive-equivalence row is an artifact-support
statement unless it is explicitly named in the theorem below.

\paragraph{Terminology.}
The paper uses the following four scope terms only in this finite-record sense.
\emph{Public surface} means the theorem interface a reviewer is asked to read
as the paper's publication claim, here the typed hierarchy theorem and the
two-coordinate replay statement.  \emph{Foreground} means membership in that
public surface; material outside it is ledger or artifact support, even when it
is mathematically useful.  A \emph{support coordinate} is one component of the
finite six-coordinate product
\((q_{\mathrm{inv}},q_{\mathrm{rgs}},q_{\mathrm{src}},q_{\mathrm{art}},
q_{\mathrm{ext}},q_{\mathrm{ven}})\), together with its required finite input
rows.  \emph{Current-byte} means the exact bytes listed in the manifest and
digest table at the moment the replay script is run; later edits require a new
digest/replay tuple before the term applies again.  These terms do not add
evidence.  They are abbreviations for the displayed finite records, closure
rules, and coordinate projections.

\begin{theorem}[Typed verified-core hierarchy]
\label{thm:typed-verified-core-hierarchy}
Let \(\EML\) be the partial principal-branch term algebra used in the support
ledger.  Let \(\BExt\) be the external statement that each B36 primitive has at
least one \(\EML\) representative on its stated source domain.  Let
\(\Trip(P,S,A)\) mean finite pure \(\EML\) terms satisfying \(P=\pi\),
\(S(x)=\sin x\) for all real \(x\), and \(A(x)=|x|\) for all \(x\ne0\).  Let
\(Z\) be the Zeckendorf normal-form protocol of Appendix~\ref{app:compact},
with \(\Val:X_{\mathrm{fin}}\to\mathbb N\), \(\NF\), and transported operations
\(\Add(c,d)=\NF(\Val(c)+\Val(d))\) and
\(\Mul(c,d)=\NF(\Val(c)\Val(d))\).  Let \(\Orbit(g)\) be the category of
one-free-monogenic-orbit ambient-product readouts.  Let \(\Cover(Z)\) be the
category of computable finite-fibre covers over the fixed normal-form sets
\(X_m\).

Then the verified core has the following hierarchy.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\BExt\) gives \(L0\) expression universality and no equality,
normal-form, or compiler data.
\item \(Z\) gives an \(L1\) computable protocol for \((\mathbb N,+,\times)\),
not an efficient online Zeckendorf arithmetic algorithm.
\item For every verified \(\Trip(P,S,A)\), no computable semantic normalizer
into finite decidable objects reflects equality on the translated Richardson
fragment.
\item No faithful same-level ambient-product readout of multiplication on all
\(\mathbb N_{\ge1}\) exists inside \(\Orbit(g)\).
\item There exists a computable object of \(\Cover(Z)\) with fixed-resolution
finite-fibre certificates but no fixed finite-state transfer representation in
the fixed-field sense of Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-transfer}.
\item The finite Stage--A replay surface certifies exactly
\[
q_{\mathrm{inv}}=q_{\mathrm{rgs}}=\mathsf{true},\qquad
q_{\mathrm{src}}=q_{\mathrm{art}}=q_{\mathrm{ext}}=q_{\mathrm{ven}}=
\mathsf{false}.
\]
Here \(q_{\mathrm{inv}}\) means only the artifact-appendix inventory checker's
syntactic label coverage over the five environment names listed in
Table~\ref{tab:inventory-name-manifest}.  It is not a theorem-dependency result,
a proof-checking result, a scope-adequacy result, an audit-record coverage
result beyond the declared finite rows, or a semantic-completeness result.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Items 1--5 are the typed interface projections retained from the support ledger:
each states its hypotheses at the interface boundary rather than inheriting them
from a printed theorem name.  The Richardson item uses only verified triples; a
printed-name branch for \(U47,U111\) is conditional on the separate real-domain
identities \(U47=\pi\) and \(U111(x)=\sin x\).  Without those two identities, no
printed Richardson instance is proved.  No endpoint/central interval-root
certificate for printed roots is part of the submitted proof surface: in
particular no statement of this paper uses an endpoint interval closed at the
same parameter value as a central interval, and \(U126\) is not used to supply a
Richardson absolute-value row.  The one-orbit item repeats the free
monogenic-orbit and same-level readout assumptions, and the cover item is
cover-relative.  Item 6 is the deterministic replay-kernel statement: the
certificate, fixed Horn schema, replay report, and digest table saturate exactly
the finite atoms for syntactic theorem-inventory label coverage and bounded fixed
replay.  The inventory atom is the artifact-appendix checker result over the
declared five environment names only; the proof checker, dependency graph,
semantic adequacy, and audit-completeness fields are absent from its input
alphabet.  Equivalently, if \(H\) is the fixed Horn schema, \(A\) is the atom set
compiled from the submitted manifest bytes, and
\[
Q=\{q_{\mathrm{inv}},q_{\mathrm{rgs}},q_{\mathrm{src}},q_{\mathrm{art}},
q_{\mathrm{ext}},q_{\mathrm{ven}}\},
\]
then the replayed projection is
\[
\operatorname{Cl}_{H}(A)\cap Q=\{q_{\mathrm{inv}},q_{\mathrm{rgs}}\}.
\]
The two included coordinates are derived by the displayed theorem-inventory and
fixed-replay rules.  The four excluded coordinates would require their own
upgrade-row atoms.  Since the finite input alphabet contains no accepted
source-rebuild, artifact-semantic, external-support, venue,
implementation-soundness, proof-checking, theorem-dependency, or
semantic-completeness upgrade row, those coordinates remain negative.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Public scope.}
The theorem is a typed interface theorem, not a global theorem-proving result.
It permits agents to propose and repair while making deterministic gates and
human promotion records responsible for public evidence.  Stronger readings
require their own finite rows: theorem-dependency claims need an explicit
dependency extractor and adequacy predicate; proof-checking claims need a
proof-assistant or equivalent checker trace; fresh proof-assistant rebuilds need
source, environment, command, exit-code, and log rows; artifact semantics need
evaluator source, input bytes, semantic predicate, environment, command, output
manifest, and digest binding; archive or venue claims need upload receipts or
archive-equivalent byte-equality records.  Local files, inventory rows, and
digest rows do not promote themselves into those coordinates.

\subsection{Record-gate schema without circular soundness}
The record-gate theorem used here is a finite schema theorem, not a proof that a
pipeline implementation is record-gate-sound.  Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Kind=\{&\textsf{formal\mbox{-}source},\textsf{traceability},\textsf{route},
\textsf{submission},\\
&\textsf{artifact},\textsf{discovery},\textsf{work\mbox{-}selection}\}
\end{aligned}
\]
be the seven-element claim-kind set.  Let \(\Gate\) be a finite gate set and let
\(\Gamma:\Kind\to\mathcal P(\Gate)\) be a deterministic dispatch table.  A
certificate for a ledger claim \(c\) is a tuple
\[
C=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B),
\]
where \(K\subseteq\Kind\) is nonempty, \(\ell\) is an evidence level, \(E\) is a
finite evidence surface, \(H\) is the human-promotion coordinate, \(R\) is the
finite list of pass rows, \(D\) is the discovery-interface coordinate, and \(B\)
is the command-boundary coordinate.  Define \(\Acc_{\Gamma}(c,C)\) by the six
finite predicates:
\begin{enumerate}[label={\(\mathrm{P}_{\arabic*}\):}]
\item \(c\) has a ledger row with all required record fields and
\(K\subseteq\Kind,\ K\ne\varnothing\).
\item \(\ell\) is an allowed evidence level and \(E\) is a nonempty finite
submitted evidence surface.
\item For every \(g\in\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)\), \(R\) contains a pass row for
\(g\).
\item Each row of \(R\) has the required pass fields, the same level \(\ell\), an
allowed decision, and an evidence pointer denoting an element of \(E\) or an
allowed manifest/table/snapshot boundary.
\item \(D\) is present exactly when \(\textsf{discovery}\in K\).
\item \(B\) contains command, source state, environment, exit code, and log path
at command-run level, and contains an explicit no-command-run assertion at
path-verified level.
\end{enumerate}
Thus
\[
\Acc_{\Gamma}(c,C)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigwedge_{i=1}^{6}P_i(c,C).
\]
Record-gate soundness is a separate input predicate
\(\RGSS(P,L,\Gamma)\) on a submitted package \(P\), finite acceptance ledger
\(L\), and dispatch table \(\Gamma\).  It is not derived from
\(\Acc_{\Gamma}\).

\begin{lemma}[Finite schema consequence of record-gate soundness]
\label{lem:rgs-schema-consequence}
If \(\RGSS(P,L,\Gamma)\) holds, then for every accepted claim \(c\in L\) there
exists a certificate \(C\) such that \(\Acc_{\Gamma}(c,C)\).  If the submitted
ledger also imposes a total order on candidate certificates, the
lexicographically least such \(C\) defines a function
\(\mathsf{Cert}_{\Gamma}(c)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is only unpacking of the input predicate \(\RGSS(P,L,\Gamma)\): by
definition that predicate supplies, for each accepted ledger claim, one finite
certificate satisfying the six displayed acceptance predicates.  A total order
on the finite candidate-certificate set gives a unique least satisfying
certificate whenever the set is nonempty.  No implementation soundness of a gate
program is inferred.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Implementation theorem for the JSON certificate records]
\label{thm:json-certificate-implementation}
Under the following JSON parsing semantics, \artifactpath{review_bundle/verify_certificate_records.py}
computes exactly \(\Acc_{\Gamma}\) for the current JSON certificate records.
Inputs are decoded as UTF-8 with an optional byte-order mark and parsed by
Python's standard \texttt{json.loads}; JSON objects are the resulting finite
string-keyed dictionaries, arrays are finite lists, strings are Unicode strings,
and a duplicate object key has the value retained by that parser.  The root
inputs are
\artifactpath{review_bundle/certificate_schema.json},
\artifactpath{review_bundle/current_package_pass_records.json}, and
\artifactpath{review_bundle/REVIEW_BUNDLE_MANIFEST.json}; the schema-required
auxiliary ledgers
\artifactpath{review_bundle/submission_interface_map.json} and
\artifactpath{review_bundle/primary_claim_inventory.json} are read as row-lock
checks.  On this finite input projection the script exits with code \(0\) iff
every ledger entry satisfies \(P_1,\ldots,P_6\), the manifest contains the
schema-required file bindings, the submission-interface ids are exactly
\(\texttt{sim1},\ldots,\texttt{sim4}\), and the primary-claim inventory contains
certificate and evidence-level fields for every load-bearing row.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The verifier is a straight-line finite checker after parsing.  Lines 73--78
load exactly the schema, ledger, submission map, primary-claim inventory, and
manifest.  Lines 80--87 bind the finite domains used in \(P_1\)--\(P_6\):
claim-kind domain, evidence levels, dispatch table, required record fields,
required pass fields, and accepted artifact-pointer patterns.  Lines 88--93
check the manifest-required file bindings.  Lines 94--113 check the seven
current ledger entries, required record fields, nonempty \(K\subseteq\Kind\),
valid evidence level, nonempty evidence surface, nonempty pass records, and
coverage of every gate in \(\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)\).  Lines 114--121 check
the discovery-coordinate and command-boundary clauses \(P_5\) and \(P_6\).  Lines
122--128 check pass-row fields, level agreement, allowed decisions, and pointer
denotation, which is \(P_4\).  Lines 130--142 perform the two row-lock checks
declared in the theorem statement.  The script appends an error exactly when one
of those finite conditions fails, returns exit code \(1\) when the error list is
nonempty, and otherwise prints the successful finite certificate-record result
and returns exit code \(0\).  Therefore the script implements the displayed
finite predicate for these JSON records; it still does not rerun Lean, validate
artifact semantics, check venue status, or prove \(\RGSS(P,L,\Gamma)\).
\end{proof}

\section{Related work by interface}
The audit route is adjacent to several literatures but imports only bounded
interfaces from them.  The concrete recent CICM comparison is theorem-by-theorem:
library and hosting papers such as Baanen et al.\ on growing
mathlib~\cite{BaanenEtAl2026GrowingMathlib} and Yamamichi et al.\ on Mizar
hosting~\cite{YamamichiEtAl2021MizarPlatform} support the infrastructure
context, but our finite replay theorem instead classifies which submitted
publication coordinates are certified.  Horowitz et al.\ on Herald
autoformalization~\cite{HorowitzEtAl2026Herald} is adjacent to advisory
proposal generation, while our no-free-upgrade theorem makes deterministic
gates and human promotion records the acceptance boundary.  Karatarakis's
Deligne-formalization work~\cite{Karatarakis2024Deligne} is adjacent to typed
formal interfaces, while our Richardson branch remains conditional on an
explicit \(\Trip(P,S,A)\) and, for printed names, the separate \((HW)\)
identities.  Brinich--Johnson's verified QFT generation~\cite{BrinichJohnson2024QFT}
and Bartoletti--Bonzio--Ferrara's certified numerical-semigroup
algorithms~\cite{BartolettiBonzioFerrara2026Semigroups} are adjacent verified
algorithm artifacts; our \(L1\)-Z block is only a computable Zeckendorf
normalizer with totality and size bounds.  Bauer et al.'s Lean-HoG
integration~\cite{BauerEtAl2024LeanHoG} is adjacent to certificate-backed
mathematical data, while our finite-record theorem refuses to upgrade artifact
rows to semantic correctness.  Bentkamp--Avigad's verified optimization
pipeline~\cite{BentkampAvigad2021VerifiedOptimization} is adjacent to verified
software reductions, while our \(L2\) obstruction is cover-relative and only
uses fixed-field transfer growth.
\begin{itemize}
\item Richardson undecidability and symbolic simplification
\cite{Richardson1968,Caviness1970,Wang1974}: used only through a verified
triple and source-domain-preserving translation.
\item EML representation and exact-real/software algebra systems: IRRAM and
RealLib give exact-real context~\cite{Mueller2001IRRAM,Lambov2007RealLib};
Arb, Calcium, FLINT, SageMath, and SymPy give software-algebra
context~\cite{Johansson2017Arb,Johansson2021Calcium,Hart2010FLINT,ZimmermannEtAl2018SageMath,MeurerEtAl2017SymPy}.
Syntax certificates are not semantic identity proofs.
\item Zeckendorf numeration and arithmetic
\cite{Zeckendorf1972,Frougny1999,AhlbachUsatineFrougnyPippenger2013,Idziaszek2020}:
the article uses valuation transport, while efficient digit-local arithmetic is
explicitly out of scope.
\item Finite automata, rational series, and C-finite sequences
\cite{AlloucheShallit2003,KauersPaule2011}: used only as comparison classes for
fixed transfer models, not as a classification theorem.
\item CICM-style formalization, LLM proof tools, and software artifacts:
CICM proceedings and workshops give the venue context
\cite{CICM2021Proceedings,CICM2021Workshops,CICM2025Proceedings}; Lean and
Isabelle/AFP give proof-assistant context
\cite{deMouraKADR2015Lean,BlanchetteHMN2015AFP,Wenzel2019IsabelleAFP};
LeanDojo, Draft-Sketch-Prove, DeepSeek-Prover, and Llemma give LLM-proof-tool
context
\cite{YangEtAl2023LeanDojo,JiangEtAl2023DraftSketchProve,XinEtAl2024DeepSeekProver,AzerbayevEtAl2023Llemma};
artifact badging and repeatability work give artifact context
\cite{ACM2020ArtifactBadging,CollbergProebsting2016Repeatability}.  The
contribution here is a publication-coordinate audit boundary around such tools.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
The useful abstraction is not that a model proves a theorem.  It is that the
system routes each generated object to the strongest claim it is entitled to
support.  For the current package that strongest public route is deliberately
small: a two-coordinate replay certificate, with the large finite ledger and all
generated wrappers moved to the artifact archive.

\subsection{Zeckendorf normalizer}
Use the Fibonacci convention \(F_1=1,F_2=2,F_{k+2}=F_{k+1}+F_k\).  A finite
word \(c=(c_k)\) is normal when \(c_k\in\{0,1\}\), finitely many digits are
nonzero, and no adjacent digits are both \(1\).  Its value is
\[
\Val(c)=\sum_k c_kF_k .
\]
Let \(X_{\mathrm{fin}}\) be the set of all finite normal words, and let \(X_m\)
be the normal words supported in the window \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\).  The largest
value in \(X_m\) is \(F_{m+1}-1\), obtained by the alternating word
\(1010\cdots\) from the top digit down.  Thus \(\Val_m:=\Val|_{X_m}\) is a
bijection
\[
\Val_m:X_m\longrightarrow \{0,\ldots,F_{m+1}-1\}.
\]
For finite-window output we use the following precise rule.  The inverse
\(\Val_m^{-1}(t)\) is defined exactly for \(0\le t<F_{m+1}\) and is computed by
the greedy normalizer below with the additional assertion that no selected index
exceeds \(m\).  If an arithmetic result \(t\) satisfies \(t\ge F_{m+1}\), the
windowed operation returns the distinguished overflow value \(\bot_m\).  The
unbounded protocol instead enlarges the output window to
\[
\omega(t):=\min\{m:t<F_{m+1}\}
\]
and returns the element of \(X_{\omega(t)}\).

\begin{quote}\scriptsize
\textbf{Algorithm \(L1\)-Z.}
Input finite normal words \(c,d\), and optionally a requested output window
\(m\).
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\textsc{FibTable}(m): & \text{return }F_1,\ldots,F_{m+1}.\\
\Val(c): & s:=0;\ \text{for each nonzero digit }c_k\text{ set }s:=s+F_k;\ \text{return }s.\\
\NF(n): & r:=n;\ e:=0;\ \text{while }r>0\text{: choose largest }F_k\le r,\\
        & e_k:=1;\ r:=r-F_k;\ \text{return }e.\\
\Val_m^{-1}(t): & \text{if }t\ge F_{m+1}\text{ return }\bot_m;\ \text{else return }\NF(t)\in X_m.\\
\Add(c,d): & \text{return }\NF(\Val(c)+\Val(d)).\\
\Mul(c,d): & \text{return }\NF(\Val(c)\Val(d)).\\
\Add_m(c,d): & \text{return }\Val_m^{-1}(\Val(c)+\Val(d)).\\
\Mul_m(c,d): & \text{return }\Val_m^{-1}(\Val(c)\Val(d)).\\
\Phi_0(c_n)(f): & \text{return }f^{\circ n}.\\
\Phi_1(c_n)(T): & \text{return }T^{\circ n},\text{ for an endomorphism
operator }T.
\end{array}
\]
\end{quote}

\begin{lemma}[Executable Zeckendorf normalizer]
\label{lem:zeckendorf-totality}
\(\NF\), \(\Val\), \(\Add\), and \(\Mul\) are total on their stated unbounded
domains.  For each \(m\), \(\Val_m^{-1}\), \(\Add_m\), and \(\Mul_m\) are total
as maps into \(X_m\cup\{\bot_m\}\), and they return \(\bot_m\) exactly on
overflow.  When no overflow occurs,
\begin{align*}
\Val(\NF(n))&=n,\\
\Val(\Add(c,d))&=\Val(c)+\Val(d),\\
\Val(\Mul(c,d))&=\Val(c)\Val(d),\\
\Val(\Add_m(c,d))&=\Val(c)+\Val(d),\\
\Val(\Mul_m(c,d))&=\Val(c)\Val(d).
\end{align*}
If \(c_n=\NF(n)\), then
\(\Phi_0(c_{a+b})=\Phi_0(c_a)\circ\Phi_0(c_b)\) and
\(\Phi_1(c_{ab})=\Phi_1(c_a)\circ\Phi_1(c_b)\) at the transported value level.
Given a precomputed table \(F_1,\ldots,F_{m+1}\), \(\Val\) on an \(m\)-digit
input and the greedy inverse on a non-overflow \(m\)-window output use \(O(m)\)
comparisons and additions/subtractions of integers of \(O(m)\) Fibonacci-size
bits.  Consequently \(\Add_m\) and \(\Mul_m\) have this \(O(m)\) greedy
normalization cost plus the ordinary integer-arithmetic cost of computing
\(\Val(c)+\Val(d)\) or \(\Val(c)\Val(d)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The loop for \(\NF(n)\) strictly decreases the nonnegative integer \(r\), so it
terminates.  After choosing the largest \(F_k\le r\), a later choice of
\(F_{k-1}\) would require the old remainder to be at least \(F_{k-1}\), whence
the old \(r\) was at least \(F_k+F_{k-1}=F_{k+1}\), contradicting maximality of
\(F_k\).  Hence the next chosen index is at most \(k-2\), and the returned word
has no adjacent \(1\)'s.  The invariant
\[
n=r+\sum_j e_jF_j
\]
is preserved at each subtraction and gives \(\Val(\NF(n))=n\) at termination.
Zeckendorf uniqueness gives that this returned word is the unique element of
\(X_{\mathrm{fin}}\) with value \(n\).

For the finite window, the maximum-value calculation satisfies
\(M_m=\max(M_{m-1},F_m+M_{m-2})=F_{m+1}-1\), with \(M_0=0\) and \(M_1=1\).
Together with Zeckendorf uniqueness this gives
\(\Val(X_m)=\{0,\ldots,F_{m+1}-1\}\).  Hence \(\Val_m^{-1}(t)\) returns
\(\bot_m\) precisely when \(t\) is outside this range; otherwise the unbounded
normalizer returns the unique normal word of value \(t\), necessarily supported
inside \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\).  The equations for \(\Add\), \(\Mul\), \(\Add_m\),
and \(\Mul_m\) are then immediate from the definitions through \(\Val\), with
the windowed equations restricted to the non-overflow branch.  The
\(\Phi\)-equations are the corresponding composition laws after substituting
\(a+b\) and \(ab\) in the transported exponents.

For complexity, the table \(F_1,\ldots,F_{m+1}\) fixes the output window and
the overflow test.  A single pass from \(F_m\) downward finds each greedy digit
and subtracts only when that digit is set, so the normalization pass has \(O(m)\) table
comparisons and at most \(m\) integer subtractions.  The input valuation pass is
linear in the input window.  The only additional work in \(\Add_m\) and
\(\Mul_m\) is ordinary integer addition or multiplication before the inverse
normalization.  This is a computable valuation-transport protocol, not an
efficient online or digit-local Zeckendorf arithmetic algorithm; efficient
Zeckendorf arithmetic is a separate topic, e.g.
\cite{AhlbachUsatineFrougnyPippenger2013,Idziaszek2020}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two proof-audit boundaries}
\begin{lemma}[Fibonacci convention reindexing]
\label{lem:fibonacci-reindexing}
Let \(G_0=G_1=1\) and \(G_{j+2}=G_{j+1}+G_j\).  With the manuscript convention
\(F_1=1,F_2=2\), one has \(G_j=F_j\) for \(j\ge1\).  The boundary cases are
\(G_1=F_1=1\) and \(G_2=F_2=2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two displayed boundary cases give the base of the recurrence comparison.
Both sequences satisfy the same two-term recurrence after that point, so
induction gives \(G_j=F_j\) for all \(j\ge1\).  Any imported theorem written in
the \(G_0=G_1=1\) convention must therefore be shifted only at the unused
initial \(G_0\) endpoint; the local \(s=1,2\) cases are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed transfer exponential bound]
\label{lem:fixed-transfer}
Fix a coefficient field \(K\), a complex embedding \(\iota:K\hookrightarrow
\mathbb C\), a matrix \(A\in K^{r\times r}\), and vectors \(u,v\).  Then every
fixed transfer sequence \(s_m=u^{\top}A^mv\) is exponentially bounded after
applying \(\iota\): there exist \(C,\lambda>0\) such that
\(|\iota(s_m)|\le C\lambda^m\) for all \(m\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose any submultiplicative matrix norm on \(\mathbb C^{r\times r}\) after
embedding the coefficients.  Then
\(|\iota(u)^{\top}\iota(A)^m\iota(v)|\le
\|\iota(u)\|\,\|\iota(v)\|\,\|\iota(A)\|^m\), and increasing \(C,\lambda\) handles
the finite initial cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Admissible auxiliary cover]
\label{def:admissible-cover}
For the \(L2\) route, an admissible cover over fixed normal-form sets \(X_m\) is
a computable finite-fibre family whose fibre sizes are explicitly listed,
generated by a recorded grammar with a bound, produced by a checker with a
transcript, or supplied by a fixed finite-state transfer model satisfying
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-transfer}.  Arbitrary one-spike covers are allowed only as
stress tests of the cover formalism.  They are not intrinsic Foldm fibre
invariants and do not prove a separation for finite-state, local,
polynomial-size, grammar-generated, artifact-generated, or uncovered Foldm
behaviour.
\end{definition}

The cover obstruction in Theorem~\ref{thm:typed-verified-core-hierarchy} is read
inside Definition~\ref{def:admissible-cover}.  Its moment
\(C_m=\sum_{x\in X_m} b_m(x)^2\) is a property of the declared cover data.  When
the selected cover has factorial-size spikes, the fixed transfer bound above
rules out a fixed-field finite-state transfer representation for that declared
sequence, but it does not identify an intrinsic obstruction of the underlying
Foldm fibres.
