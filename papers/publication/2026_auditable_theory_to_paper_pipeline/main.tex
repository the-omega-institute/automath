\begin{abstract}
Generated formal-mathematics artifacts need a publication interface that states
which finite records support which public claims.  We present such a
publication-coordinate audit route for a concrete \emph{newmath}/\emph{automath}
theory-to-paper package.  The article is not an evaluation of tool performance
or formalization success rates; it is a finite replay and audit-boundary paper.  The
public claim is intentionally narrow: a submitted finite replay package certifies
exactly two artifact-level coordinates.  The first is the legacy atom
\(\texttt{qinv}\), read here only as lexical environment-token coverage for the
artifact appendix's declared five raw \LaTeX{} environment-token names; it is not
theorem-inventory coverage, theorem-dependency analysis, proof checking, scope
adequacy, audit-record completeness, or semantic theorem completeness.  The
second coordinate is bounded fixed-replay record-gate soundness.  The package
does not certify a fresh Lean/BEDC rebuild, artifact semantics, public archive
equivalence, venue status, or whole-program implementation soundness.  The
technical ledger, generated theorem wrappers, and digest tables are supplied as
artifact support, not as the public article body.
\end{abstract}

\section{Finite-audit route}
The object studied here is the publication routing of generated and
machine-maintained formal-mathematics artifacts, not the success rate or
mathematical creativity of the tools that produced them.  Proof assistants check
obligations; artifact and manuscript systems also need to decide which local
objects may support which publication claims.  Our pipeline therefore records
every accepted increment through six roles: source object, formal interface,
finite evidence row, advisory action, deterministic gate, and human promotion
boundary.  A theorem wrapper, review note, log, digest, or support file becomes
load-bearing only at the coordinate named by its finite gate.

The submitted package has two reader-facing interfaces.  This article is the route paper;
the separate artifact archive is summarized in the manifest tables below.  Long
file names, command lines, generated wrappers, finite ledgers, replay
certificates, and digest tables are treated as manifest entries rather than as
inline theorem text.  They are cited only as support for the finite replay
package and are not inlined into the public paper.  In particular, a statement
mentioning a local inventory, generated theorem wrapper, command log, digest
row, review-bundle path, or archive-equivalence row is an artifact-support
statement unless it is explicitly named in the theorem below.

\paragraph{Terminology.}
The paper uses the following four scope terms only in this finite-record sense.
\emph{Public surface} means the theorem interface a reviewer is asked to read
as the paper's publication claim, here the finite Horn replay theorem and the
two-coordinate replay statement.  \emph{Foreground} means membership in that
public surface; material outside it is ledger or artifact support, even when it
is mathematically useful.  A \emph{support coordinate} is one component of the
finite six-coordinate product
\((q_{\mathrm{inv}},q_{\mathrm{rgs}},q_{\mathrm{src}},q_{\mathrm{art}},
q_{\mathrm{ext}},q_{\mathrm{ven}})\), together with its required finite input
rows.  \emph{Current-byte} means the exact bytes listed in the manifest and
digest table at the moment the replay script is run; later edits require a new
digest/replay tuple before the term applies again.  These terms do not add
evidence.  They are abbreviations for the displayed finite records, closure
rules, and coordinate projections.

\begin{theorem}[Self-contained finite Horn replay]
\label{thm:typed-verified-core-hierarchy}
Let
\[
Q=\{q_{\mathrm{inv}},q_{\mathrm{rgs}},q_{\mathrm{src}},q_{\mathrm{art}},
q_{\mathrm{ext}},q_{\mathrm{ven}}\}
\]
be the six-coordinate support alphabet.  The submitted Stage--A manifest rows
are compiled only at the artifact level; the public theorem uses the finite base
atom set \(A\) displayed below.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{0.94\linewidth}{@{}L{0.12\linewidth}Y@{}}
\(A=\) &
\texttt{pubAbs}, \texttt{suppMain}, \texttt{invJson}, \texttt{invMd},
\texttt{finalDigest}, \texttt{stageAHornSchema},
\texttt{stageAManifest}, \texttt{stageAHornCertificate},
\texttt{stageAReplayReport}, \texttt{srcInterface},
\texttt{caseArtifacts}, \texttt{venueReadiness},
\texttt{qrgsReplayUpgrade}.
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
For this theorem, take \(A\) to be exactly the following displayed finite base
atom set; the names are formal atoms and do not import any unprinted support
ledger, source semantics, or artifact semantics.  Let \(H\) be the following
finite Horn system:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{0.94\linewidth}{@{}L{0.10\linewidth}Y@{}}
R0 & \(\texttt{singleRouteSurface}\leftarrow
\texttt{pubAbs},\texttt{suppMain}\).\\
R1 & \(\texttt{qinv}\leftarrow
\texttt{invJson},\texttt{invMd},\texttt{finalDigest}\).\\
R2 & \(\texttt{localInventoryClosed}\leftarrow\texttt{qinv}\).\\
R3 & \(\begin{array}[t]{@{}l@{}}
\texttt{qrgs}\leftarrow
\texttt{stageAHornSchema},\texttt{stageAManifest},\\
\texttt{stageAHornCertificate},\texttt{stageAReplayReport},\\
\texttt{finalDigest},\texttt{qrgsReplayUpgrade}.
\end{array}\)\\
R4 & \(\texttt{boundedReplayRecordGateSoundness}\leftarrow\texttt{qrgs}\).\\
R5 & \(\texttt{qsrc}\leftarrow\texttt{freshFormalSourceUpgrade}\).\\
R6 & \(\texttt{qart}\leftarrow\texttt{dynamicArtifactSemanticUpgrade}\).\\
R7 & \(\texttt{qext}\leftarrow\texttt{externalUploadArchiveUpgrade}\).\\
R8 & \(\texttt{qven}\leftarrow\texttt{uploadTimeVenueAcceptanceUpgrade}\).\\
R9 & \(\texttt{boundedSourceInterface}\leftarrow\texttt{srcInterface}\).\\
R10 & \(\texttt{boundedArtifactRows}\leftarrow\texttt{caseArtifacts}\).\\
R11 & \(\texttt{datedVenueReadiness}\leftarrow\texttt{venueReadiness}\).\\
R12 & \(\begin{array}[t]{@{}l@{}}
\texttt{routeSurfaceQuotient}\leftarrow
\texttt{pubAbs},\texttt{suppMain},\\
\texttt{stageAHornSchema},\texttt{stageAHornCertificate},\\
\texttt{stageAReplayReport},\texttt{invJson},\texttt{invMd},\\
\texttt{finalDigest}.
\end{array}\)
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
No other Horn rule has a conclusion in \(Q\).  Then forward chaining gives
\[
\operatorname{Cl}_{H}(A)\cap Q
=\{q_{\mathrm{inv}},q_{\mathrm{rgs}}\}.
\]
Equivalently, the finite Stage--A replay surface certifies exactly
\[
q_{\mathrm{inv}}=q_{\mathrm{rgs}}=\mathsf{true},\qquad
q_{\mathrm{src}}=q_{\mathrm{art}}=q_{\mathrm{ext}}=q_{\mathrm{ven}}=
\mathsf{false}.
\]
Here \(q_{\mathrm{inv}}\) is a legacy coordinate name for the artifact appendix's
lexical environment-token check over the five raw token names listed in
Table~\ref{tab:inventory-name-manifest}.  The checker scans source bytes and
tokens matching those names; it does not evaluate \TeX{} conditionals, comments,
includes, or macros, and it counts tokens in inactive conditional branches.  Thus
the coordinate is not theorem-inventory coverage, theorem-dependency analysis, a
proof-checking result, a scope-adequacy result, an audit-record coverage result
beyond the declared finite rows, or a semantic-completeness result.  The theorem
has no items 1--5 and makes no public claim about \(\EML\), Richardson
normalizers, orbit readouts, or cover obstructions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Forward chaining is monotone, so it is enough to inspect the displayed rules
whose conclusions lie in \(Q\).  Rule \(R1\) applies because
\(\textsf{invJson}\), \(\textsf{invMd}\), and \(\textsf{finalDigest}\) are in
\(A\); hence \(q_{\mathrm{inv}}\in\operatorname{Cl}_{H}(A)\).  Rule \(R3\)
applies because all six of its premises,
\(\textsf{stageAHornSchema}\), \(\textsf{stageAManifest}\),
\(\textsf{stageAHornCertificate}\), \(\textsf{stageAReplayReport}\),
\(\textsf{finalDigest}\), and \(\textsf{qrgsReplayUpgrade}\), are in \(A\);
hence \(q_{\mathrm{rgs}}\in\operatorname{Cl}_{H}(A)\).

For the negative coordinates, the only displayed rules concluding
\(q_{\mathrm{src}}\), \(q_{\mathrm{art}}\), \(q_{\mathrm{ext}}\), and
\(q_{\mathrm{ven}}\) are \(R5\), \(R6\), \(R7\), and \(R8\).  Their respective
premises are
\(\textsf{freshFormalSourceUpgrade}\),
\(\textsf{dynamicArtifactSemanticUpgrade}\),
\(\textsf{externalUploadArchiveUpgrade}\), and
\(\textsf{uploadTimeVenueAcceptanceUpgrade}\).  None is a base atom in \(A\),
and no displayed rule derives any of them.  Therefore those four premises are
absent from \(\operatorname{Cl}_{H}(A)\), so the corresponding four support
coordinates are absent as well.  The other rules derive only auxiliary atoms,
not elements of \(Q\).  Thus
\(\operatorname{Cl}_{H}(A)\cap Q=\{q_{\mathrm{inv}},q_{\mathrm{rgs}}\}\).

This is the whole public theorem proved here.  In particular the theorem does
not import any \(\EML\), Zeckendorf, orbit-readout, cover, proof-checking,
theorem-dependency, semantic-completeness, external-archive, venue-status, or
Richardson-normalizer claim from unprinted artifact records.  The only
Richardson obstruction used in this article is the explicit conditional
reduction in Lemma~\ref{lem:richardson-normalizer-reduction}.  All such claims
can enter this paper only through separately displayed hypotheses or through a
fresh accepted upgrade atom and a new replay tuple.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Public scope.}
The theorem is a typed interface theorem, not a global theorem-proving result.
It permits proposal and repair artifacts to be retained while making
deterministic gates and human promotion records responsible for public evidence.
Stronger readings
require their own finite rows: theorem-dependency claims need an explicit
dependency extractor and adequacy predicate; proof-checking claims need a
proof-assistant or equivalent checker trace; fresh proof-assistant rebuilds need
source, environment, command, exit-code, and log rows; artifact semantics need
evaluator source, input bytes, semantic predicate, environment, command, output
manifest, and digest binding; archive or venue claims need upload receipts or
archive-equivalent byte-equality records.  Local files, inventory rows, and
digest rows do not promote themselves into those coordinates.

\subsection{Record-gate schema without circular soundness}
The record-gate theorem used here is a finite schema theorem, not a proof that a
pipeline implementation is record-gate-sound.  Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Kind=\{&\textsf{formal\mbox{-}source},\textsf{traceability},\textsf{route},
\textsf{submission},\\
&\textsf{artifact},\textsf{discovery},\textsf{work\mbox{-}selection}\}
\end{aligned}
\]
be the seven-element claim-kind set.  Let \(\Gate\) be a finite gate set and let
\(\Gamma:\Kind\to\mathcal P(\Gate)\) be a deterministic dispatch table.  A
certificate for a ledger claim \(c\) is a tuple
\[
C=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B),
\]
where \(K\subseteq\Kind\) is nonempty, \(\ell\) is an evidence level, \(E\) is a
finite evidence surface, \(H\) is the human-promotion coordinate, \(R\) is the
finite list of pass rows, \(D\) is the discovery-interface coordinate, and \(B\)
is the command-boundary coordinate.  Define \(\Acc_{\Gamma}(c,C)\) by the six
finite predicates:
\begin{enumerate}[label={\(\mathrm{P}_{\arabic*}\):}]
\item \(c\) has a ledger row with all required record fields and
\(K\subseteq\Kind,\ K\ne\varnothing\).
\item \(\ell\) is an allowed evidence level and \(E\) is a nonempty finite
submitted evidence surface.
\item For every \(g\in\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)\), \(R\) contains a pass row for
\(g\).
\item Each row of \(R\) has the required pass fields, the same level \(\ell\), an
allowed decision, and an evidence pointer denoting an element of \(E\) or an
allowed manifest/table/snapshot boundary.
\item \(D\) is present exactly when \(\textsf{discovery}\in K\).
\item \(B\) contains command, source state, environment, exit code, and log path
at command-run level, and contains an explicit no-command-run assertion at
path-verified level.
\end{enumerate}
Thus
\[
\Acc_{\Gamma}(c,C)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigwedge_{i=1}^{6}P_i(c,C).
\]
Record-gate soundness is a separate input predicate
\(\RGSS(P,L,\Gamma)\) on a submitted package \(P\), finite acceptance ledger
\(L\), and dispatch table \(\Gamma\).  It is not derived from
\(\Acc_{\Gamma}\).

\begin{lemma}[Finite schema consequence of record-gate soundness]
\label{lem:rgs-schema-consequence}
If \(\RGSS(P,L,\Gamma)\) holds, then for every accepted claim \(c\in L\) there
exists a certificate \(C\) such that \(\Acc_{\Gamma}(c,C)\).  If the submitted
ledger also imposes a total order on candidate certificates, the
lexicographically least such \(C\) defines a function
\(\mathsf{Cert}_{\Gamma}(c)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is only unpacking of the input predicate \(\RGSS(P,L,\Gamma)\): by
definition that predicate supplies, for each accepted ledger claim, one finite
certificate satisfying the six displayed acceptance predicates.  A total order
on the finite candidate-certificate set gives a unique least satisfying
certificate whenever the set is nonempty.  No implementation soundness of a gate
program is inferred.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Implementation theorem for the JSON certificate records]
\label{thm:json-certificate-implementation}
Use the TCB convention of Tables~\ref{tab:replay-tcb} and
\ref{tab:replay-byte-binding}.  Thus the JSON inputs are raw byte files decoded
as \texttt{utf-8-sig} and parsed by \texttt{json.loads}; missing files,
inadmissible relative paths, symlinked inputs, and JSON parse failures are
rejecting preconditions rather than accepted certificates.  Let
\(\operatorname{exists}(p)\) mean existence under that relative-root policy.  For
a pass-row pointer \(u\) and evidence surface \(E\), define
\[
\operatorname{Allowed}(u,E)
\]
to hold exactly when \(u\in E\), or \(u\) has a prefix obtained from a schema
pattern ending in \(\texttt{*}\), or \(u\) starts with one of
\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\texttt{manifest:} & \texttt{table:} & \texttt{proposition:}\\
\texttt{lemma:} & \texttt{theorem:} & \texttt{corollary:}\\
\multicolumn{3}{@{}l@{}}{\texttt{explicit-historical-}}\\
\multicolumn{3}{@{}l@{}}{\texttt{boundary:}}
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
or \(\operatorname{exists}(u)\).

The following machine-checkable formal pseudocode checker is the operative
finite semantics for the current certificate-record inputs
\artifactpath{review_bundle/certificate_schema.json},
\artifactpath{review_bundle/current_package_pass_records.json},
\artifactpath{review_bundle/submission_interface_map.json},
\artifactpath{review_bundle/primary_claim_inventory.json}, and
\artifactpath{review_bundle/REVIEW_BUNDLE_MANIFEST.json}.  It initializes an
empty error list and appends the displayed error branch exactly when the
displayed predicate is false.  The Python file
\artifactpath{review_bundle/verify_certificate_records.py} is an artifact
implementation of this displayed checker, not an extra unprinted premise for the
iff claim.

\scriptsize

The checker accepts exactly when the final error list is empty.  Consequently,
on the parsed current records, it exits with code \(0\) iff all rows satisfy
\(P_1,\ldots,P_6\) for \(\Acc_{\Gamma}\), the manifest bindings M1--M3 hold,
the submission-interface locks S1--S2 hold, and the primary-claim locks P1--P5
hold.  It exits with code \(1\) iff at least one displayed branch is violated.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After the parsing precondition succeeds, every loop is over a finite JSON array
or over a finite dictionary supplied by the schema.  Each branch in the printed
checker has the form ``append an error if not \(B\)'', with no mutation of the
parsed records, schema domains, or dispatch table.  Hence an error from that
branch is appended exactly on violation of \(B\).

The ledger-entry branches are precisely the acceptance predicates.  E1 together
with E2 and E3 is \(P_1\): the row has all required fields and a nonempty
\(K\subseteq\Kind\).  E4 and E5 are \(P_2\): allowed evidence level and
nonempty finite evidence surface.  E6 and E7 are \(P_3\): there is a finite
pass-row list and it covers every gate assigned by \(\Gamma\).  R1--R4 are
\(P_4\): every pass row has the required fields, agrees with the parent evidence
level, has an allowed decision, and has a permitted evidence pointer according
to the displayed definition of \(\operatorname{Allowed}\).  E8 is \(P_5\), the
exact discovery-interface discipline.  E9 and E10 are \(P_6\), the two cases of
the command-boundary discipline.  L1 and L2 are the current-package row lock
selecting the seven submitted entries; they do not strengthen \(\Acc_{\Gamma}\)
for an individual row, but they fix the finite ledger to which the theorem is
applied.  M1--M3, S1--S2, and P1--P5 are likewise the manifest,
submission-interface, and primary-claim row locks explicitly named in the
statement.

If the checker accepts, the error list is empty, so none of the displayed
violations occurred; therefore each selected ledger entry satisfies all six
predicates defining \(\Acc_{\Gamma}\), and the three families of row locks also
hold.  Conversely, if all six acceptance predicates and all row locks hold, then
every displayed branch condition is false, no append occurs, the final error
list is empty, and the checker accepts.  The program's final return convention
is exactly empty-list \(\mapsto0\), nonempty-list \(\mapsto1\), so the same
equivalence holds for the exit code.  This proves exact computation of the

\subsection{Richardson-normalizer obstruction}
\label{subsec:richardson-normalizer-obstruction}
The paper uses Richardson undecidability only in the following formal reduction
shape.  This subsection is deliberately separated from the replay theorem above:
it gives a boundary for possible semantic normalizer claims, not another
accepted coordinate of the submitted package.

Let \(\RE\) be a fixed effective Richardson source fragment: terms are generated
by a finite grammar over the variable \(x\), rational constants, addition,
subtraction, multiplication, composition, and the elementary functions allowed
by Richardson's identity-undecidability theorem.  A source term \(r\in\RE\)
denotes a partial real function \(\Sem_{\RE}(r)\) on its natural real domain.
Write \(D(r,s)\) for the common defined domain of \(r\) and \(s\), and write
\[
  \Eq_{\RE}(r,s)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \forall x\in D(r,s),\ 
  \Sem_{\RE}(r)(x)=\Sem_{\RE}(s)(x).
\]
Richardson's theorem gives no algorithm deciding \(\Eq_{\RE}(r,0)\) for all
\(r\in\RE\)~\cite{Richardson1968}; the later simplification literature records
the same obstruction for canonical elementary simplification
\cite{Caviness1970,Wang1974}.

Let \(\TE\) be any proposed target fragment whose objects are finite strings in
a decidable set, and let \(\Sem_{\TE}\) be its intended real-function semantics.
A translation is a total computable map
\(\tau:\RE\to\TE\).  It is semantics preserving when
\[
  \Eq_{\RE}(r,s)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \Sem_{\TE}(\tau(r))=\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(s))
\]
and equality reflecting when the reverse implication holds.  A finite target
normalizer is a total computable map \(\Norm:\TE\to\TE\) whose range is a
decidable finite-object language and whose syntactic equality is decidable.
It is equality-complete for \(\tau(\RE)\) when, for all \(r,s\in\RE\),
\[
  \Sem_{\TE}(\tau(r))=\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(s))
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \Norm(\tau(r))=\Norm(\tau(s))
\]
where the equality on the right is ordinary finite-string equality.  These four
requirements are the missing data behind any phrase such as ``Richardson
normalizer obstruction'': the translated source fragment, the computable
translation, the semantic equality predicate, and the decidable finite target
objects must all be named.

\begin{lemma}[Richardson reduction against equality-reflecting finite normalizers]
\label{lem:richardson-normalizer-reduction}
Suppose there are a target fragment \(\TE\), a total computable translation
\(\tau:\RE\to\TE\), and a total computable finite target normalizer \(\Norm\)
such that \(\tau\) is semantics preserving and equality reflecting and
\(\Norm\) is equality-complete for \(\tau(\RE)\).  Then Richardson identity in
\(\RE\) is decidable.  Consequently no paper-local syntax catalogue, finite
witness list, or target normal form can be used as a semantic Richardson
identity oracle unless it supplies one of the hypotheses above and thereby
accepts this decidability consequence.  These are exactly the four formal data
named in the statement: source fragment, computable translation, semantic
equality predicate, and decidable finite target objects.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Given \(r\in\RE\), compute \(\tau(r)\), compute \(\tau(0)\), normalize both
finite target objects, and compare the resulting finite strings.  This is an
algorithm because \(\tau\) and \(\Norm\) are total computable and the target
objects have decidable finite equality.  If the normalized strings are equal,
equality-completeness gives
\(\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(r))=\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(0))\), and equality reflection for
\(\tau\) gives \(\Eq_{\RE}(r,0)\).  If the normalized strings are unequal,
equality-completeness gives
\(\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(r))\ne\Sem_{\TE}(\tau(0))\), and semantics preservation
contrapositively gives \(\neg\Eq_{\RE}(r,0)\).  Thus the displayed procedure
decides whether an arbitrary Richardson source term is identically zero, in
contradiction to Richardson's undecidability theorem for the chosen source
fragment.  Therefore the four hypotheses cannot simultaneously hold.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Zeckendorf normalizer}
Use the Fibonacci convention \(F_1=1,F_2=2,F_{k+2}=F_{k+1}+F_k\).  A finite
word \(c=(c_k)\) is normal when \(c_k\in\{0,1\}\), finitely many digits are
nonzero, and no adjacent digits are both \(1\).  Its value is
\[
\Val(c)=\sum_k c_kF_k .
\]
Let \(X_{\mathrm{fin}}\) be the set of all finite normal words, and let \(X_m\)
be the normal words supported in the window \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\).  The largest
value in \(X_m\) is \(F_{m+1}-1\), obtained by the alternating word
\(1010\cdots\) from the top digit down.  Thus \(\Val_m:=\Val|_{X_m}\) is a
bijection
\[
\Val_m:X_m\longrightarrow \{0,\ldots,F_{m+1}-1\}.
\]
For finite-window output we use the following precise rule.  The inverse
\(\Val_m^{-1}(t)\) is defined exactly for \(0\le t<F_{m+1}\) and is computed by
the greedy normalizer below with the additional assertion that no selected index
exceeds \(m\).  If an arithmetic result \(t\) satisfies \(t\ge F_{m+1}\), the
windowed operation returns the distinguished overflow value \(\bot_m\).  The
unbounded protocol instead enlarges the output window to
\[
\omega(t):=\min\{m:t<F_{m+1}\}
\]
and returns the element of \(X_{\omega(t)}\).

The two transport operators below have different codomains and therefore use
different binary operations.  Fix a set \(S\), write \(\operatorname{End}(S)\)
for its self-maps with composition \(\circ_S\), and put
\(\mathcal E_S:=\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{End}(S))\), with composition
\(\circ_{\mathcal E_S}\).  For
\(P,Q:\operatorname{End}(S)\to\operatorname{End}(S)\), define the fixed-output
product
\[
(P\odot_S Q)(f):=P(f)\circ_S Q(f)\qquad(f\in\operatorname{End}(S)).
\]
For \(A,B:\mathcal E_S\to\mathcal E_S\), define the exponentiation-operator
composition
\[
(A\bullet_S B)(T):=A(B(T))\qquad(T\in\mathcal E_S).
\]
Thus \(\odot_S\) composes the two output maps obtained from the same input
\(f\), while \(\bullet_S\) composes the two operators acting on the variable
\(T\).

\begin{quote}\scriptsize
\textbf{Algorithm \(L1\)-Z.}
Input finite normal words \(c,d\), and optionally a requested output window
\(m\).
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\textsc{FibTable}(m): & \text{return }F_1,\ldots,F_{m+1}.\\
\Val(c): & s:=0;\ \text{for each nonzero digit }c_k\text{ set }s:=s+F_k;\ \text{return }s.\\
\NF(n): & r:=n;\ e:=0;\ \text{while }r>0\text{: choose largest }F_k\le r,\\
        & e_k:=1;\ r:=r-F_k;\ \text{return }e.\\
\Val_m^{-1}(t): & \text{if }t\ge F_{m+1}\text{ return }\bot_m;\ \text{else return }\NF(t)\in X_m.\\
\Add(c,d): & \text{return }\NF(\Val(c)+\Val(d)).\\
\Mul(c,d): & \text{return }\NF(\Val(c)\Val(d)).\\
\Add_m(c,d): & \text{return }\Val_m^{-1}(\Val(c)+\Val(d)).\\
\Mul_m(c,d): & \text{return }\Val_m^{-1}(\Val(c)\Val(d)).\\
\Phi^S_0(c_n)(f): & \text{return }f^{\circ_S n},\text{ for }f\in\operatorname{End}(S).\\
\Phi^S_1(c_n)(T): & \text{return }T^{\circ_{\mathcal E_S} n},\text{ for }T\in\mathcal E_S.
\end{array}
\]
\end{quote}

\begin{lemma}[Executable Zeckendorf normalizer]
\label{lem:zeckendorf-totality}
\(\NF\), \(\Val\), \(\Add\), and \(\Mul\) are total on their stated unbounded
domains.  For each \(m\), \(\Val_m^{-1}\), \(\Add_m\), and \(\Mul_m\) are total
as maps into \(X_m\cup\{\bot_m\}\), and they return \(\bot_m\) exactly on
overflow.  When no overflow occurs,
\begin{align*}
\Val(\NF(n))&=n,\\
\Val(\Add(c,d))&=\Val(c)+\Val(d),\\
\Val(\Mul(c,d))&=\Val(c)\Val(d),\\
\Val(\Add_m(c,d))&=\Val(c)+\Val(d),\\
\Val(\Mul_m(c,d))&=\Val(c)\Val(d).
\end{align*}
If \(c_n=\NF(n)\), then for every set \(S\) the maps have typed domains
\[
\Phi^S_0:X_{\mathrm{fin}}\to
\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{End}(S)),
\qquad
\Phi^S_1:X_{\mathrm{fin}}\to
\operatorname{End}(\mathcal E_S),
\]
where \(\Phi^S_0(c_n)(f)=f^{\circ_S n}\) and
\(\Phi^S_1(c_n)(T)=T^{\circ_{\mathcal E_S} n}\).  With
\(\odot_S\) and \(\bullet_S\) as defined above,
\[
\Phi^S_0(c_{a+b})=\Phi^S_0(c_a)\odot_S\Phi^S_0(c_b)
\quad\text{as maps }\operatorname{End}(S)\to\operatorname{End}(S),
\]
and
\[
\Phi^S_1(c_{ab})=\Phi^S_1(c_a)\bullet_S\Phi^S_1(c_b)
\quad\text{as maps }\mathcal E_S\to\mathcal E_S.
\]
Given a precomputed table \(F_1,\ldots,F_{m+1}\), \(\Val\) on an \(m\)-digit
input and the greedy inverse on a non-overflow \(m\)-window output use \(O(m)\)
comparisons and additions/subtractions of integers of \(O(m)\) Fibonacci-size
bits.  Consequently \(\Add_m\) and \(\Mul_m\) have this \(O(m)\) greedy
normalization cost plus the ordinary integer-arithmetic cost of computing
\(\Val(c)+\Val(d)\) or \(\Val(c)\Val(d)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The loop for \(\NF(n)\) strictly decreases the nonnegative integer \(r\), so it
terminates.  After choosing the largest \(F_k\le r\), a later choice of
\(F_{k-1}\) would require the old remainder to be at least \(F_{k-1}\), whence
the old \(r\) was at least \(F_k+F_{k-1}=F_{k+1}\), contradicting maximality of
\(F_k\).  Hence the next chosen index is at most \(k-2\), and the returned word
has no adjacent \(1\)'s.  The invariant
\[
n=r+\sum_j e_jF_j
\]
is preserved at each subtraction and gives \(\Val(\NF(n))=n\) at termination.
Zeckendorf uniqueness gives that this returned word is the unique element of
\(X_{\mathrm{fin}}\) with value \(n\).

For the finite window, the maximum-value calculation satisfies
\(M_m=\max(M_{m-1},F_m+M_{m-2})=F_{m+1}-1\), with \(M_0=0\) and \(M_1=1\).
Together with Zeckendorf uniqueness this gives
\(\Val(X_m)=\{0,\ldots,F_{m+1}-1\}\).  Hence \(\Val_m^{-1}(t)\) returns
\(\bot_m\) precisely when \(t\) is outside this range; otherwise the unbounded
normalizer returns the unique normal word of value \(t\), necessarily supported
inside \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\).  The equations for \(\Add\), \(\Mul\), \(\Add_m\),
and \(\Mul_m\) are then immediate from the definitions through \(\Val\), with
the windowed equations restricted to the non-overflow branch.  For the first
transport law, if \(f\in\operatorname{End}(S)\), then
\[
(\Phi^S_0(c_a)\odot_S\Phi^S_0(c_b))(f)
=f^{\circ_S a}\circ_S f^{\circ_S b}
=f^{\circ_S(a+b)}
=\Phi^S_0(c_{a+b})(f).
\]
For the second transport law, if \(T\in\mathcal E_S\), then
\[
(\Phi^S_1(c_a)\bullet_S\Phi^S_1(c_b))(T)
=\Phi^S_1(c_a)\bigl(T^{\circ_{\mathcal E_S} b}\bigr)
=\bigl(T^{\circ_{\mathcal E_S} b}\bigr)^{\circ_{\mathcal E_S} a}
=T^{\circ_{\mathcal E_S}ab}
=\Phi^S_1(c_{ab})(T).
\]
Thus the addition identity is pointwise output composition, whereas the
multiplication identity is composition of exponentiation operators.

For complexity, the table \(F_1,\ldots,F_{m+1}\) fixes the output window and
the overflow test.  A single pass from \(F_m\) downward finds each greedy digit
and subtracts only when that digit is set, so the normalization pass has \(O(m)\) table
comparisons and at most \(m\) integer subtractions.  The input valuation pass is
linear in the input window.  The only additional work in \(\Add_m\) and
\(\Mul_m\) is ordinary integer addition or multiplication before the inverse
normalization.  This is a computable valuation-transport protocol, not an
efficient online or digit-local Zeckendorf arithmetic algorithm; efficient
Zeckendorf arithmetic is a separate topic, e.g.
\cite{AhlbachUsatineFrougnyPippenger2013,Idziaszek2020}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two proof-audit boundaries}
\begin{lemma}[Fibonacci convention reindexing]
\label{lem:fibonacci-reindexing}
Let \(G_0=G_1=1\) and \(G_{j+2}=G_{j+1}+G_j\).  With the manuscript convention
\(F_1=1,F_2=2\), one has \(G_j=F_j\) for \(j\ge1\).  The boundary cases are
\(G_1=F_1=1\) and \(G_2=F_2=2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two displayed boundary cases give the base of the recurrence comparison.
Both sequences satisfy the same two-term recurrence after that point, so
induction gives \(G_j=F_j\) for all \(j\ge1\).  Any imported theorem written in
the \(G_0=G_1=1\) convention must therefore be shifted only at the unused
initial \(G_0\) endpoint; the local \(s=1,2\) cases are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed transfer exponential bound]
\label{lem:fixed-transfer}
Fix a coefficient field \(K\), a complex embedding \(\iota:K\hookrightarrow
\mathbb C\), a matrix \(A\in K^{r\times r}\), and vectors \(u,v\).  Then every
fixed transfer sequence \(s_m=u^{\top}A^mv\) is exponentially bounded after
applying \(\iota\): there exist \(C,\lambda>0\) such that
\(|\iota(s_m)|\le C\lambda^m\) for all \(m\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose any submultiplicative matrix norm on \(\mathbb C^{r\times r}\) after
embedding the coefficients.  Then
\(|\iota(u)^{\top}\iota(A)^m\iota(v)|\le
\|\iota(u)\|\,\|\iota(v)\|\,\|\iota(A)\|^m\), and increasing \(C,\lambda\) handles
the finite initial cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Admissible auxiliary cover]
\label{def:admissible-cover}
For the \(L2\) route, an admissible cover over fixed normal-form sets \(X_m\) is
a computable finite-fibre family whose fibre sizes are explicitly listed,
generated by a recorded grammar with a bound, produced by a checker with a
transcript, or supplied by a fixed finite-state transfer model satisfying
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-transfer}.  Arbitrary one-spike covers are allowed only as
stress tests of the cover formalism.  They are not intrinsic Foldm fibre
invariants and do not prove a separation for finite-state, local,
polynomial-size, grammar-generated, artifact-generated, or uncovered Foldm
behaviour.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Declared factorial-spike cover obstruction]
\label{thm:declared-factorial-spike-cover}
Let \((X_m)_{m\ge0}\) be finite nonempty normal-form source sets and let
\(x_m^\ast\in X_m\) be a computably selected element for each \(m\).  Define the
explicit auxiliary cover
\[
Y_m=
\{(x_m^\ast,i):1\le i\le (m+1)!\}
\cup
\{(x,1):x\in X_m,\ x\ne x_m^\ast\},
\qquad
\pi_m(x,i)=x .
\]
Its fibre multiplicities are
\[
b_m(x_m^\ast)=(m+1)!,\qquad b_m(x)=1\quad(x\ne x_m^\ast),
\]
and its cover moment is
\[
C_m=\sum_{x\in X_m}b_m(x)^2=((m+1)!)^2+|X_m|-1 .
\]
Assume additionally that \(|X_m|\ge1\) is bounded by \(D\rho^m\) for some
\(D,\rho>0\).  Then the declared moment sequence \((C_m)\) is not represented by
any fixed-field finite-state transfer sequence \(u^\top A^m v\) in the sense of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-transfer}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The displayed formula for \(Y_m\) is finite over \(X_m\), and the projection
\(\pi_m(x,i)=x\) gives exactly the listed fibres.  Hence the second moment is
\(((m+1)!)^2\) from the distinguished fibre plus one contribution from each of
the other \(|X_m|-1\) fibres.

Suppose \(C_m=u^\top A^m v\) for fixed transfer data over a fixed coefficient
field and fixed complex embedding.  Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-transfer} gives
\(|C_m|\le C\lambda^m\) for constants \(C,\lambda>0\).  On the other hand
\[
C_m\ge ((m+1)!)^2 .
\]
For any fixed \(\lambda>0\), the ratio
\[
\frac{((m+1)!)^2}{\lambda^m}
=(m+1)^2\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{j^2}{\lambda}
\]
tends to infinity, because all factors with \(j>\sqrt{\lambda}\) exceed \(1\)
and their product is unbounded.  Thus no constants \(C,\lambda\) can
exponentially bound \(C_m\), contradicting the fixed-transfer estimate.  The
extra hypothesis on \(|X_m|\) records the usual finite-source growth regime; it
is not needed for the lower-bound contradiction, but prevents the theorem from
being misread as a statement about arbitrary hidden growth in the source fibres.
\end{proof}

The auxiliary-cover obstruction is read only through
Theorem~\ref{thm:declared-factorial-spike-cover} and
Definition~\ref{def:admissible-cover}.  Its moment
\(C_m=\sum_{x\in X_m} b_m(x)^2\) is a property of the displayed cover data
\((Y_m,\pi_m,b_m)\).  The theorem rules out a fixed-field finite-state transfer
representation for that declared moment sequence, by the factorial lower bound
and Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-transfer}.  It does not identify an intrinsic
obstruction of the underlying Foldm fibres and does not assert strictness for
finite-state, local, polynomial-size, grammar-generated, artifact-generated, or
uncovered cover classes.
