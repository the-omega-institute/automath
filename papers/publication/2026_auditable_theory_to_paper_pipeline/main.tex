\begin{abstract}
Large formal-mathematics projects increasingly use AI agents for drafting,
review, theorem search, and manuscript preparation, but ungated agent output can
produce shallow theorem growth, stale source references, duplicated submission
routes, and premature paper promotion.  We report an auditable
theory-to-paper pipeline used around a Lean-backed BEDC/newmath project and its
automath publication workspace.  The architecture separates source
development, intake packets, active paper directories, Oracle review loops,
deterministic gates, and human promotion decisions.  Four case studies show the
role of these gates: an intake boundary preventing seed material from entering
the active manuscript daemon, an overlap/submitted gate preventing random venue
hopping, a fake-extension gate blocking theorem-looking but content-poor edits,
and an artifact gate exposing count drift and an unresolved collision-audit
discrepancy.  The claim is deliberately narrow: AI outputs are advisory, while
load-bearing evidence must remain tied to source paths, command logs,
deterministic checks, and human-approved promotion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and Contribution}

AI assistance can scale review and drafting in a formal-mathematics project,
but file edits and plausible theorem statements are not evidence by
themselves.  The failure modes are structural: a generated theorem may be a
mechanical expansion, a manuscript route may overlap a submitted sibling, an
artifact count may drift from its status file, or an intake note may
accidentally become a daemon-visible paper.  The contribution of this note is
an auditable workflow discipline that treats AI output as advisory and routes
load-bearing claims through deterministic gates.

\section{Architecture}

The workflow separates two repositories and two paper states.  The
BEDC/newmath source workspace contains Lean and artifact sources.  The
automath publication workspace contains intake packets, active paper tracks,
review logs, and submission metadata.  A candidate from the source workspace
first enters an intake seed.  It becomes an active paper only after a human
promotion decision creates a \texttt{2026\_*} directory with a manuscript and
pipeline metadata.  This boundary prevents background source organization from
being mistaken for a submission route.

The pinned source contains a discovery-gate calculus: statement or code
generation is not discovery; mechanical expansion and compression are not
discovery by themselves; certificate ledgers and lineage records are required;
unsupported claims can be demoted; and scheduler selection should favor
classifier-shifting work rather than easy local edits.  These principles are
implemented as source maps, inventories, lints, review gates, and publication
checks rather than as free-form agent judgment.

\section{Scope}

This note does not claim that AI output is proof evidence, that the workflow is
a theorem prover, that all BEDC declarations have been freshly rebuilt, or that
the Rule110 artifact suite has been rerun for this submission.  Its narrower
claim is that a theory-to-paper pipeline can preserve traceability by
separating source evidence, deterministic gates, agent suggestions, and human
promotion decisions.
