\begin{abstract}
Formal mathematics projects increasingly use AI agents for drafting,
review, theorem search, and manuscript preparation.  Ungated agent output,
however, can create theorem-looking but content-poor edits, stale source
references, duplicated submission routes, and premature paper promotion.  We
report an auditable theory-to-paper pipeline used around a Lean-backed
BEDC source workspace and an automath publication workspace.  The
pipeline separates source development, intake packets, active paper
directories, AI review loops, deterministic gates, and human promotion
decisions.  Its central rule is that AI output is advisory: load-bearing
mathematical and submission claims must be tied, at the appropriate evidence
level, to source paths, command logs, deterministic checks, and human-approved
promotion.  Under record-gate soundness, the core interface theorem constructs
the finite publication certificates checked by the package verifier from recorded
gate-passage entries and explicitly submitted review surfaces; its safety claim is
deliberately certificate-level and finite-record only.  It proves neither Lean builds,
daemon behaviour, artifact semantics, nor venue acceptance.  The current package
then proves finite-record certificate exactness for its six-row, path-verified
instantiation, while rejecting stronger readings such as external gate
implementation soundness, proof-assistant correctness, artifact semantic validity,
current execution of every gate, or venue acceptance.  Four case
studies illustrate the gates at the path-verified historical-snapshot level: intake
isolation, overlap/submitted blocking, fake-extension rejection, and
artifact-limitation disclosure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and contribution}

AI assistance is useful in a formal-mathematics project only if the project can
distinguish proposal from evidence.  A generated theorem statement, a legal
piece of code, a mechanical expansion, or a lower score in a review rubric does
not by itself constitute mathematical discovery.  The practical publication
failure modes are equally structural: a route may overlap a submitted sibling,
an artifact status file may drift from generated counts, or an intake note may
accidentally become a daemon-visible manuscript.

coordinates.  By definition, $P^{+}_{6}$ contains the primary article, this

Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table} in both states.  Hence

The added coordinate has a different declared role.  Its accepted claim is not a
new mathematical proof, a proof-assistant rebuild, or an extractor-completeness

\end{proof}

The setting is adjacent to proof-assistant systems such as Lean
\cite{deMouraKADR2015Lean}, machine-learning interfaces to formal proving such
as LeanDojo \cite{YangEtAl2023LeanDojo}, informal-to-formal guidance methods

large-scale formal-proof archives such as the Archive of Formal Proofs
\cite{BlanchetteHMN2015AFP}.  Our focus is different: we study the
publication-facing evidence discipline around such sources.

\section{Architecture}

The workflow separates source work from publication work, and the separation is
the organizing contract for the rest of the supplement.  BEDC source files are
treated as finite-kernel theory together with proof obligations and separate
implementation contracts.  Lean and related markers are formal-verification
records for named obligations, not object-language closure by themselves.
Finite manifests, evaluator rows, bundled snapshots, and command logs are the
substrate witnesses from which publication certificates are built.  AI agents
remain advisory: they may propose, review, and triage, but they do not promote
claims into accepted theory.  The automath layer is therefore a publication
compiler: it packages the source-theory interface, finite witnesses, route state,
and submission boundary without merging those evidence axes.

read evidence from an axis only at the admissible evidence level shown in
the displayed axis map; the negative boundary in the last column
is part of the claim certificate and cannot be discharged by prose from another
axis.

\begin{proposition}[Axis non-escalation]
\label{prop:architecture-axis-non-escalation}
In any record-gate-sound package using Definition~\ref{def:architecture-axis-map},
passage or evidence on one architecture axis cannot certify a load-bearing claim
whose claim kind requires another axis.  More precisely, if a claim certificate
uses an axis $A$ but its stronger reading needs an evidence item or negative-boundary
discharge listed only on a different axis $A'$, then the stronger reading is not
accepted unless the package contains a separate submitted coordinate for $A'$ and
the gates assigned to that coordinate pass.  Consequently
Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface} constructs only finite publication
certificates whose axis coordinates are explicitly present in the submitted support
surface; it does not infer BEDC closure from Lean markers, artifact semantics from
manifest rows, proof evidence from agents, venue upload from automath route state,
or support-surface availability from local bundle paths.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The operational interface records a claim by finite coordinates: claim kind,
evidence surface, evidence level, pass records assigned by $\Gamma$, and the
boundary field.  Definition~\ref{def:architecture-axis-map} makes the axis of each
such coordinate explicit and declares the corresponding negative boundary.  If a
stronger reading needs an item on a different axis $A'$, then either the claim-kind
coordinate does not name the kind requiring $A'$, the evidence surface does not
contain an admissible $A'$ item, a required $A'$ pass record is missing, or the
boundary field still excludes the stronger reading.  In all four cases the finite
record verifier of Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier}
rejects the stronger reading: the dispatch table cannot assign an unmentioned gate,
the surface check cannot read an unsubmitted path or log, the pass-record check
cannot be satisfied without the row, and the boundary check preserves the declared
negative boundary.

Conversely, when a separate coordinate for $A'$ is submitted, it is just another
finite component of the package.  It can be accepted only by the same schema:
its claim kind must dispatch to the appropriate gates, its evidence surface must
list the admissible $A'$ evidence, and its boundary must state exactly what is and
is not certified.  Therefore evidence never moves between axes by paraphrase or by
agent-generated text; it moves only by adding an explicit finite coordinate and
passing the assigned gates.  Applying this to the five rows of
Definition~\ref{def:architecture-axis-map} gives the listed consequences.  They are
also the axis-level instance of the coordinate-determination result later isolated
in Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary}: facts outside the
finite coordinates of the accepted package are not conclusions of the certificate.
\end{proof}
The reader path is therefore architectural before it is logistical.  The rest of
the supplement first fixes the source-interface spine, then defines the finite
record verifier, then proves the certificate construction and non-escalation
boundary, then instantiates that boundary on the current six-row package, and
only then lists the local case snapshots and command-run inventory.  File tables
are included as finite witnesses for this interface, not as a substitute for the
compiler architecture.

\begin{proposition}[Source-interface evidence-level separation]
\label{prop:source-interface-evidence-level-separation}
Let $r$ be any accepted manuscript claim whose discovery component cites one of
the imported labels in Table~\ref{tab:source-interface-record}.  If $r$ uses only
Lemma~\ref{lem:source-interface-extraction-correctness}, then the imported-label
premise of $r$ is accepted only at the manual path-verified bounded-paraphrase
level.  If $r$ additionally cites
Proposition~\ref{prop:source-interface-record-file-consistency}, then the same
premise is upgraded only by the finite record comparisons listed in that
proposition: table--JSON equality, manifest agreement, snapshot digest and line
count agreement, and label containment in the declared ranges.  In neither case
does the premise become a fresh Lean/BEDC rebuild, a proof of the semantic truth
of the imported source theorem, or an unbounded restatement of the source text.
Consequently the finite-record interface theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}, can use the imported
discovery-gate spine only through these two explicitly separated evidence
levels.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The operational evidence level of a cited source-interface premise is determined
by the record fields actually available for that premise.  Lemma~\ref{lem:source-interface-extraction-correctness}
supplies a pinned path, commit, extraction date, label, line range, and bounded
summary for each of the ten imported labels, and its proof states that no later
use may exceed that bounded summary.  Therefore a claim using only the lemma has
exactly the manual path-verified bounded-paraphrase evidence listed there.

Proposition~\ref{prop:source-interface-record-file-consistency} adds a different
finite record: the verifier parses the manuscript table, the JSON mirror, the
manifest's pinned-source-snapshot record, and the bundled source snapshot, and it
checks the six finite conditions enumerated in its proof.  Those checks upgrade
the source-interface premise only with respect to record-file consistency and
range containment.  The same proposition expressly excludes semantic truth of
the imported theorems and preserves the bounded-paraphrase restriction.  No row
in Table~\ref{tab:source-interface-record}, no field of
\path{source_interface_record.json}, and no command-boundary row records a fresh
Lean/BEDC rebuild or a semantic source-theorem verifier.  Hence an imported-label
premise has exactly the two evidence levels stated in the proposition and no
stronger one.

The finite-record interface theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}, requires a discovery-gate source
interface with recorded evidence surface and boundary.  Applying the preceding
paragraphs to each of its imported discovery-gate citations gives precisely the
manual path-verified interface, optionally strengthened by the finite executable
record comparison, and forbids promotion of that interface into an unrecorded
semantic or rebuild claim.\end{proof}

\paragraph{Reader map.}
The supplement now separates the certificate argument into six layers.  The

\subsection{Abstract finite-record interface}

For this long technical supplement and its two-page CICM presentation artifact,
the following finite interface is sufficient.

\begin{definition}[Operational acceptance interface]
\label{def:operational-acceptance-interface}
A \emph{claim} is a manuscript assertion that can affect a mathematical,
artifact, route, or submission decision.  A \emph{load-bearing claim} is a claim
whose removal would change a theorem statement, a case-study conclusion, or the
submission-readiness status.  An \emph{advisory output} is any AI-agent draft,
score, suggested theorem, review packet, or repair proposal before a gate records
it.  A \emph{deterministic gate} is a repeatable predicate, script, or checklist
whose inputs and pass/fail decision are recorded independently of the prose
generated by an agent.  A \emph{human promotion} is the explicit editorial act
that moves a candidate into publication state, for example by creating an active
\texttt{2026\_*} paper directory with \texttt{main.tex} and \texttt{PIPELINE.md}.
An \emph{evidence surface} is the set of source paths, commits, commands, logs,
inventories, and witness records cited for a claim.  An \emph{acceptance entry}
is a manuscript or submission-ledger record that marks a candidate claim as
usable in the publication-facing argument.  \emph{Accepted work} is the subset of
candidate claims with such an entry.  This is a status notion only: the
definition records that an editorial acceptance entry exists, but it does not by
itself assert external editorial acceptance or implementation correctness.  A
pipeline is \emph{record-gate-sound for acceptance} when each acceptance entry records
the claim kind, the evidence surface, the evidence level, and a pass record for
every gate in $\Gamma$ assigned to that claim kind, and when a route-boundary
entry also records human promotion.

The deterministic dispatch function $\Gamma$ from claim kinds to gates is part
of the operational interface.  Its domain is
\[
\{\text{formal-source},\text{ traceability},\text{ route},
\text{ submission},\text{ artifact},\text{ discovery},\text{ work-selection}\}.
\]
It sends formal-source claims to the build and axiom-purity gates; traceability
claims to inventory and marker-existence gates; route claims to promotion,
overlap, and submission-state gates; submission claims to submission-pack gates;
artifact claims to status and limitation-ledger gates; discovery claims to the
pinned discovery gate above and, for extension claims, to the theorem-content
delta gate; and work-selection claims to the scheduler gate, whose evidence
surface is a critical-path score or backlog window together with the claim kind
that the selected item is meant to advance.  For mixed claims, $\Gamma$ returns
the union of the gate sets for the claim kinds present.

The route gates have separate operational meanings.  The \emph{promotion gate}
uses the active-paper evidence surface, namely a human promotion act producing a
\path{2026_*} directory with \path{main.tex} and \path{PIPELINE.md}; passing
means the candidate may be daemon-visible publication work, and failing means it
remains intake or source material.  The \emph{overlap gate} uses route identity,
semantic-overlap ledgers, submitted-sibling paths, and program-board state;
passing means advancement is route-disjoint or has a recorded resolution, and
failing means advancement is blocked until closure, merge, supersession, or
waiting for prior feedback.  The \emph{submission-state gate} uses submission
markers, venue state, board state, and submission-pack metadata; passing means
the route's publication state permits the proposed action, and failing means the
action would duplicate, race, or under-specify an active or submitted route.  The
\emph{theorem-content delta gate} uses theorem inventories, hard-lint output, and
delta records such as \path{ARTIFACT_INVENTORY.md}; passing means that an
extension claim identifies load-bearing mathematical content, and failing means
that compilation, file churn, or surface edits may not be reported as substantive
theorem growth.

A \emph{reviewable artifact} for this paper is either a public repository file,
a supplemental file included with the submission, or an explicit
historical-evidence boundary stating that the local path is used only as
path-verified evidence and not as a fresh command-run claim.  A \emph{case
witness record} is path-complete when its evidence surface gives a
concrete source or artifact path for each load-bearing component of the row.  A
route-overlap witness has the fields \emph{route identity}, \emph{submitted
sibling}, \emph{overlap reason}, \emph{gate decision}, and \emph{allowed next
actions}.  An artifact-limitation witness has the fields \emph{artifact status
path}, \emph{limitation ledger path}, \emph{observed limitation}, \emph{gate
decision}, and \emph{allowed manuscript claim}.  A promotion witness has the
fields \emph{candidate surface}, \emph{human promotion evidence},
\emph{active-paper markers}, \emph{gate decision}, and \emph{allowed manuscript
claim}.  A theorem-content witness has the fields \emph{changed surface},
\emph{theorem inventory or hard-lint surface}, \emph{substantive-content delta},
\emph{gate decision}, and \emph{allowed manuscript claim}.  The inventory files
\path{ARTIFACT_INVENTORY.md}, \path{SOURCE_MAP.md}, and
\path{review_bundle/REVIEW_BUNDLE_MANIFEST.json} are part of the evidence surface
for this paper and give the compact path catalogue used by
Table~\ref{tab:case-witnesses}.

We distinguish two evidence levels.  A claim is \emph{path-verified} when this
paper records the relevant source path, artifact path, pinned commit or local
publication path, and gate decision, but does not assert that a command was
freshly rerun for this submission.  A claim is \emph{command-run} when the paper
also records the command, source commit, environment, exit code, and log path
for a fresh run.  This long technical supplement uses path-verified architecture
and case evidence unless explicitly marked command-run.

A uniform \emph{pass record} is the finite tuple
\[
\rho=(g,I,d,e,\ell),
\]
where $g$ is the gate name, $I$ is the input surface submitted to that gate,
$d$ is the recorded decision, $e$ is the evidence pointer supporting the
decision, and $\ell$ is the evidence level.  A pass record is assigned to a
claim certificate only when its evidence pointer is contained in the claim's
evidence surface, in a submitted manifest or table row, in a bundled snapshot, or
in an explicit historical-evidence boundary.

A \emph{publication certificate} for a load-bearing claim $c$ is the finite
record
\[
\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B).
\]
Here $K$ is the nonempty finite set of claim kinds carried by $c$, $\ell$ is the
evidence level, $E$ is the evidence surface, $H$ is either a human-promotion
record or the marker that $c$ does not cross the intake-to-publication boundary,
$R$ gives one pass record, with the five fields above, for each gate in
$\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)$, $D$ is present exactly when $K$ contains discovery
and then names the pinned discovery-gate source-interface rows used for the
claim, and $B$ records either the command-run fields or the explicit boundary
that no command-run assertion is made.  A certificate is \emph{locally
checkable} when these finite fields are present and every path, manifest row, or
log named by a field lies in the submitted artifact, the supplemental review
bundle, or the explicit path-verified historical boundary.

For later use we make the local check explicit as a finite verifier.  The
concrete package checker supplied with the review bundle is
\path{review_bundle/verify_certificate_records.py}.  Its positive invocation is
\path{python review_bundle/verify_certificate_records.py}; it reads exactly
\path{review_bundle/certificate_schema.json},
\path{review_bundle/current_package_pass_records.json},
\path{review_bundle/submission_interface_map.json},
\path{review_bundle/primary_claim_inventory.json}, and
\path{review_bundle/REVIEW_BUNDLE_MANIFEST.json}, and writes the recorded pass log
\path{review_bundle/certificate_verification_run.log}.  Its negative-example
invocation is
\path{python review_bundle/verify_certificate_records.py --negative-example review_bundle/negative_certificate_record_example.json},
with recorded log \path{review_bundle/negative_certificate_record_example_run.md};
that fixture upgrades a route row to command-run strength while omitting the
command-run tuple and is rejected with the finite bad subrecord
\path{neg1: command-run fields incomplete}.  The mathematical verifier below is
the schema abstraction of this package checker.

Let \(\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)\) be the seven-kind domain and let
\(\mathsf{Lev}=\{\mathsf{path},\mathsf{command}\}\).  For a candidate record
\(\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\), define six finite predicates:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[$P_1$:] $K\ne\emptyset$, $K\subseteq\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)$, and
$\ell\in\mathsf{Lev}$.
\item[$P_2$:] $E$ is finite and each item of $E$ is a submitted path,
review-bundle path, manifest row, log path, pinned commit, or explicit
historical-evidence boundary.
\item[$P_3$:] for every gate $g\in\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)$, $R$ contains a
pass record $(g,I,d,e,\ell')$ whose evidence pointer lies in $E$ or in a named
submitted table, manifest row, bundled snapshot, or historical boundary.
\item[$P_4$:] $H$ records the human promotion exactly when $c$ crosses the
intake-to-publication boundary and otherwise records that no such boundary is
crossed.
\item[$P_5$:] $D$ is present exactly when discovery lies in $K$, and then names
the pinned discovery-gate source-interface rows used by the claim.
\item[$P_6$:] $B$ either records command, source commit, environment, exit code,
and log path for command-run evidence or explicitly records that no command-run
assertion is made.
\end{enumerate}
Define the finite verifier
\[
V(c,\mathcal C)=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{6}P_i(c,\mathcal C,\Gamma).
\]
If this conjunction is false, the rejected subrecord is the finite set of fields
read by the least-indexed failed predicate.  This paragraph defines the verifier
only; no unprinted semantic oracle is part of it.  The next definition packages
the same finite fields as an explicit input type and a finite acceptance table;
the exactness theorem below then proves that the verifier and the independently
named record-acceptance relation coincide on that finite data structure.

For a package $P$ with ledger $L$, dispatch table $\Gamma$, and submitted
support surface $S$, write
\[
\mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)
\]
for the following finite predicate.  For every acceptance entry of a
load-bearing claim $c$ in $L$, there is a publication certificate
$\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$ such that $E\subseteq S$, $V(c,\mathcal C)=1$,
each pass record in $R$ is a submitted row of $L$, the row decision is passing,
and the row's evidence pointer denotes an element of $S$ at the evidence level
recorded in the row.  If $B$ does not contain command, source state,
environment, exit code, and log path, then $\mathsf{RGS}_{S}$ asserts only this
finite row-and-pointer property and asserts no implementation-level correctness
of the gate that produced the row.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Stronger-reading preorder]
\label{def:stronger-reading-preorder}
Fix the dispatch table $\Gamma$ and certificate tuple
$\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface}.  A proposed reading
$c^{+}$ of the same prose claim is \emph{record-stronger} than $c$, written
$c\preceq_{\Gamma} c^{+}$, if at least one of the following finite coordinates is
strictly enlarged while the others are unchanged or enlarged: the claim-kind set
$K$; the evidence level, from $\mathsf{path}$ to $\mathsf{command}$; the evidence
surface $E$; the promotion field $H$; the assigned gate-pass family $R$; the
discovery-interface field $D$; or the boundary field $B$.  It is
\emph{properly stronger} if the enlargement is strict in at least one coordinate.
It is \emph{accepted as a stronger reading} only when the enlarged tuple still
satisfies $\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}$: every newly added kind has its assigned gates,
every newly raised evidence level has the command tuple required by A6, and every
new evidence item is on the submitted support surface or in an explicit
historical boundary.

The preorder is intentionally syntactic and finite-record based.  It is not a
semantic entailment relation between mathematical propositions, and it does not
turn an inventory entry, digest row, or path reference into proof evidence for a
theorem-like environment.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Certificate-schema acceptance table]
\label{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table}
Fix the seven-kind dispatch table $\Gamma$ of
Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface}.  The verifier input for a
load-bearing claim $c$ is the following finite record type:
\[
\mathcal C(c)=
\{K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B\}.
\]
Its typed fields are these and only these: $K$ is a finite set of claim kinds;
$\ell\in\{\mathsf{path},\mathsf{command}\}$ is the declared evidence level; $E$
is a finite evidence-surface list; $H$ is the route-boundary field; $R$ is a
finite family of gate pass records, each with gate name, input surface, decision,
evidence pointer, and evidence level; $D$ is either empty or a finite list of
pinned discovery source-interface rows; and $B$ is the command-boundary field.
This is a data schema, not a theorem: all later exactness claims must be proved
from this displayed record and cannot appeal to unlisted package state.  Define
$\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}(c,\mathcal C)$ to be the conjunction of the six finite
table predicates A1--A6 displayed below; no clause may query generated
prose, a live daemon, an artifact evaluator, a proof assistant, or a venue state
outside these fields and their cited submitted surfaces.

\begin{center}
\centering
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{p{0.08\linewidth}p{0.22\linewidth}X}
\toprule
Clause & Field read & Acceptance condition \\
\midrule
A1 & $K,\ell$ & $K$ is a nonempty subset of the seven-kind domain and $\ell$ is one of the two evidence levels. \\
A2 & $E$ & Every evidence item is a submitted path, review-bundle path, manifest row, log path, pinned commit, or explicit historical-evidence boundary available at level $\ell$. \\
A3 & $K,R,E$ & For every gate in $\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)$, $R$ has a pass record with gate name, input surface, decision, evidence pointer, and evidence level; the pointer lies in $E$ or in a named submitted table, manifest row, bundled snapshot, or historical boundary. \\
A4 & $H$ & $H$ records human promotion exactly when $c$ crosses the intake-to-publication boundary, and otherwise records that no such crossing occurs. \\
A5 & $K,D$ & $D$ is present exactly when discovery lies in $K$, and then names the pinned discovery-gate source-interface rows used by $c$. \\
A6 & $\ell,B$ & $B$ records command, source commit, environment, exit code, and log path for command-run evidence, or records that no command-run assertion is made. \\

\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Finite certificate schema verifier as a record check]
\label{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier}
For every load-bearing manuscript claim $c$ whose component kinds lie in the
seven-kind domain, $V(c,\mathcal C)$ is a finite, prose-insensitive
record-schema verifier for the declared publication certificate.  The submitted
package instantiates $V$ by the script
\path{review_bundle/verify_certificate_records.py}.  On the positive package
input it reads the schema file, pass-record ledger, submission-interface map,
primary-claim inventory, and review-bundle manifest named above, and outputs
acceptance in \path{review_bundle/certificate_verification_run.log}.  On the
negative fixture \path{review_bundle/negative_certificate_record_example.json} it
outputs rejection in \path{review_bundle/negative_certificate_record_example_run.md}
with the bad subrecord \path{neg1: command-run fields incomplete}.  If $V$
accepts, then $\mathcal C(c)$ is locally checkable at exactly its declared
evidence level.  This is certificate-record verification only: it checks finite
record shape, dispatch, evidence pointers, and command-boundary discipline, not
end-to-end external semantic validation of the referenced artifacts or source
theory.  If $V$ rejects, then a reviewer can extract a finite bad subrecord
consisting of the first failed schema check and the fields on which that check
depends.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The verifier inspects only the displayed tuple $\mathcal C(c)$, the finite
seven-kind domain, the finite dispatch table $\Gamma$, and the finite list of
paths, rows, commits, or logs cited by the record.  The package script realizes
these inputs as five finite JSON files: the schema supplies the seven-kind
domain, evidence levels, gate dispatch table, required record fields, required
pass-record fields, accepted decisions, manifest-required files, and accepted
artifact-prefix patterns; the ledger supplies the seven current records $a_1,
\ldots,a_7$; the submission-interface map and primary-claim inventory supply the
finite manuscript-facing row sets; and the manifest supplies the submitted bundle
paths.  The script output is either the pass transcript in
\path{certificate_verification_run.log} or a list of \path{ERROR: ...} lines,
each naming the failed finite condition.  Since $K$ is required to be a subset of
the seven-kind domain, the set \(\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)\) is finite.  The
checks over $E$, $R$, and the command-run fields are therefore finite lookups in
named manuscript or bundle surfaces, not semantic judgments about generated
prose.

If all six checks pass, the tuple has exactly the fields required in the
definition of a publication certificate: a finite kind set $K$, declared evidence
level $\ell$, evidence surface $E$, route-boundary field $H$, gate-record field
$R$, optional discovery field $D$ with the required presence condition, and
evidence-strength boundary $B$.  Since $V$ is the conjunction of the explicit
predicates $P_1,\ldots,P_6$, the six table clauses A1--A6 hold for exactly the
same finite inputs.  Check~(ii) is precisely the local-checkability condition on
cited paths, manifest rows, logs, pinned commits, and historical boundaries.
Checks~(iii)--(vi) prevent record-level escalations of the declared level:
missing gate-passage records, unrecorded route promotion, unsupported discovery
routing, and command-run assertion without command-run fields.  Thus an accepted
record is locally checkable at the level $\ell$ it declares, and no stronger
level is silently inferred by the record checker.

If a check fails, the verifier stops at a finite test.  The bad subrecord is the
finite collection of fields read by that failed test: for example, $K$ and
$\Gamma$ for a gate-domain failure, $E$ for an evidence-surface failure, $K$,
$\Gamma$, $R$, and the cited evidence item for a gate-record failure, $H$ for a
promotion failure, $K$ and $D$ for a discovery-field failure, or $\ell$ and $B$
for an evidence-level failure.  The supplied negative fixture is exactly the last
case: it declares $\ell=\mathsf{command}$ but provides no
\path{boundary.command_run_fields} containing command, source commit,
environment, exit code, and log path, so the bad subrecord is the pair consisting
of the evidence-level field and the boundary field.  This subrecord is
independent of any surrounding paragraph that may have described the claim.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Verifier input--predicate--output contract]
\label{thm:verifier-input-output-contract}
The verifier interface used by the publication-safety theorem is exactly the
following finite mathematical-software contract.  Its input is a pair
$(c,\mathcal C(c))$, where $c$ is a load-bearing manuscript claim and
\(\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\) is the certificate record of
Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table}, together with the fixed
dispatch table $\Gamma$ and the submitted support surface $S$.  Its checked
predicates and outputs are:
\begin{center}
\centering
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{p{0.12\linewidth}p{0.30\linewidth}X}
\toprule
Check & Predicate actually checked & Possible output \\
\midrule
$Q_1$ & $K$ is nonempty, $K\subseteq\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)$, and $\ell\in\{\mathsf{path},\mathsf{command}\}$ & accept this coordinate, or reject with bad fields $K,\ell$ \\
$Q_2$ & each item of $E$ is on $S$ or in an explicitly declared pinned, logged, or historical boundary & accept this coordinate, or reject with bad field $E$ and the offending pointer \\
$Q_3$ & every gate assigned by $\Gamma$ to a kind in $K$ has a passing five-field record in $R$ whose pointer is reviewable through $E$, a submitted table, a manifest row, a bundled snapshot, or an historical boundary & accept this coordinate, or reject with bad fields $K,R,E$ and the missing or non-reviewable gate \\
$Q_4$ & $H$ records exactly the human-promotion crossing asserted by $c$, or records that no such crossing is asserted & accept this coordinate, or reject with bad field $H$ \\
$Q_5$ & $D$ is present exactly for discovery-kind claims and then names only pinned source-interface rows used by $c$ & accept this coordinate, or reject with bad fields $K,D$ \\
$Q_6$ & $B$ either contains command, source state, environment, exit code, and log path, or explicitly marks that no command-run assertion is made & accept this coordinate, or reject with bad fields $\ell,B$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
The positive output is only the finite acceptance bit
$\mathsf{accept}(c,\mathcal C)$ for these six predicates.  The negative output is
only the finite bad subrecord belonging to the first failed predicate.  In
particular, the contract has no output channel for proof-assistant validity,
daemon correctness, artifact semantics, fresh case-study reruns, venue upload, or
venue acceptance.  Therefore any theorem in this paper that invokes the verifier
can certify exactly finite record acceptance for the submitted schema and cannot
certify end-to-end workflow correctness unless an enlarged input schema and an
additional proved verifier contract are supplied.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The input type is displayed in
Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table}: after the fixed table
$\Gamma$ is chosen, the only variable fields are $K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B$.  The
submitted support surface $S$ is used only to decide whether the finite pointers
named in those fields are reviewer-visible at the declared level.  Thus every
listed predicate is a finite Boolean predicate on the displayed input fields and
on the fixed submitted support surface.

For each row, the predicate is identical to one of the six predicates used in the
definition of $V$: $Q_i=P_i$ for $1\leq i\leq 6$, with the same fields read and
with the same failure subrecord, namely the fields read by the least-indexed
failed predicate.  Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier}
proves that the bundled Python checker is an implementation witness for this
finite schema: it reads the schema, pass-record ledger, submission-interface map,
primary-claim inventory, and manifest, and returns either the pass transcript or
finite error records.  The theorem here is the abstract verifier contract behind
that implementation witness, so its output alphabet is precisely
\(\{\mathsf{accept}\}\) together with the finite bad-subrecord reports.

The final boundary follows by inspection of the input and output alphabets.  No
field in $\mathcal C(c)$ contains an object-language proof derivation, a theorem
prover kernel trace, a daemon transition semantics, an artifact evaluator
semantics, a fresh rerun transcript for the historical case studies, or a venue
receipt.  No output of the contract returns such an object either.  A conclusion
depending on one of those objects is therefore not a function of the declared
input record.  It can become checkable only after the schema is enlarged to add
that object as an input coordinate and after a new verifier contract for the
enlarged coordinate is proved.  Hence the present verifier certifies only finite
record acceptance and its finite rejection witnesses.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Schema-relative finite-record acceptance exactness]
\label{thm:record-acceptance-exactness}
Fix the seven-kind dispatch table $\Gamma$, the two evidence levels, and the
typed certificate tuple
$\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table}.  For every
load-bearing claim $c$ and every finite candidate certificate $\mathcal C(c)$,
the finite verifier $V$ of
Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:verifier-input-output-contract} accepts $\mathcal C(c)$ if
and only if the six-clause table predicate
$\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}(c,\mathcal C)$ holds.  This is a schema-relative and
finite-record-relative assertion: both sides read exactly the fields named in the
fixed schema and in the finite submitted record surface, and neither side ranges
over unrecorded semantic facts about the referenced mathematics, artifacts, or
gate implementations.  Moreover, if $V$ accepts a record for $c$, the same record
cannot certify any stronger reading $c^{+}$ that adds a new claim kind, a stronger
evidence level, an omitted route crossing, an omitted discovery interface, an
omitted pass record, or a command-run assertion: such a reading has a finite bad
subrecord of the corresponding clause.  Thus $V$ is exact for this paper's finite
record-acceptance relation over the submitted source, tables, manifests, logs,
and explicitly declared historical boundaries, while remaining silent about
external semantic validity of the referenced theory or artifacts and about the
implementation-level soundness of any deployed gate implementation beyond the
recorded pass rows.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The definition of $V$ and the six clauses above use the same finite inputs.
Predicate $P_1$ is clause~A1, $P_2$ is clause~A2, and $P_3$ is clause~A3,
including both assigned-gate coverage and the requirement that each evidence
pointer be tied to the declared review surface.  Predicates $P_4$, $P_5$, and
$P_6$ are exactly clauses~A4, A5, and A6.  Therefore, if
$\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}(c,\mathcal C)$ holds, every verifier predicate is true
and $V$ accepts.

Conversely, suppose $V$ accepts.  Reading the successful checks in order gives
precisely clauses~1--6: the kind set and evidence level are in the permitted
domains; the evidence surface is reviewer-available at the declared level; every
gate assigned by $\Gamma$ has a pass record with the required five fields and an
allowed pointer; promotion and non-promotion are separated by $H$; discovery-kind
claims and only discovery-kind claims carry $D$; and $B$ fixes the command-run
boundary.  Hence $\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}(c,\mathcal C)$ holds.

For the final assertion, keep the accepted tuple fixed and consider a proposed
stronger reading $c^{+}$.  If $c^{+}$ adds a claim kind, the finite set of gates
assigned by $\Gamma$ grows unless the added kind is vacuous in this interface;
without the corresponding pass records clause~3 fails, and if the kind lies
outside the seven-kind domain clause~1 fails.  If $c^{+}$ raises the evidence
level to command-run, clause~6 fails unless the command, commit, environment,
exit code, and log path are present.  If it adds a route crossing, clause~4 fails
unless $H$ contains the human-promotion record.  If it adds discovery content,
clause~5 fails unless the pinned discovery rows are named.  If it asserts a new
gate-dependent conclusion without a pass record, clause~3 fails.  In each case
the failed clause reads only finitely many fields, so deleting irrelevant unread
fields gives an inclusion-minimal bad subrecord.  If none of the clauses changes,
then $c^{+}$ is not stronger in the record semantics: the certificate still
licenses only the same kind set, same evidence level, same gate records, same
route boundary, same discovery interface, and same command boundary.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-coordinate determination and trust-boundary classifier]
\label{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary}
Fix the dispatch table $\Gamma$ and the verifier $V$.  Let $F_{\Gamma}$ be the
finite schema projection read by $V$ from a certificate record, namely the
claim-kind set, declared evidence level, evidence surface, promotion field,
gate-pass rows, discovery-source rows, command-boundary tuple, and the assigned
gate set determined by $\Gamma$.  If two ambient pipeline or artifact states
induce the same values of all coordinates in $F_{\Gamma}$ for a claim $c$, then
they induce the same record-acceptance decision and the same finite publication
certificate for $c$.  Therefore the exactness of
Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness} is exactness only relative to this
finite schema projection.  No property depending on a coordinate outside
$F_{\Gamma}$---for example the semantic truth of a referenced theorem, truthful
production or implementation soundness of a gate row, a fresh Lean build result,
a daemon trace, an artifact semantic fact, a theorem-environment completeness
claim, or an external venue decision---is a consequence of the accepted
certificate unless that property is first encoded as an additional finite
certificate coordinate and the enlarged record is accepted.  In particular,
record-gate soundness is a load-bearing hypothesis for
Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}; it is not supplied by schema
exactness alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The verifier $V$ is defined as an ordered list of six checks, and each check reads
only one of the coordinates listed in $F_{\Gamma}$ together with the fixed finite
dispatch table $\Gamma$.  If two ambient states induce the same values on
$F_{\Gamma}$, then the input tuple \((K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\), the assigned gate set,
and every submitted pointer queried by $V$ are identical in the two states.  Each
of the six checks therefore has the same truth value in the two states, so $V$
either accepts both records or rejects both records at the same first failed
finite field.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness}, an accepted
decision is equivalent to the same six-clause record-acceptance semantics, hence
the finite certificate determined by those clauses is also the same.  This proves
the schema-relative and finite-record-relative factorisation.

Now let $Q$ be a property whose truth can vary while all coordinates in
$F_{\Gamma}$ are held fixed.  Then there exist two ambient states with the same
certificate tuple and the same verifier decision but different truth values for
$Q$.  A conclusion whose truth changes under such an $F_{\Gamma}$-preserving
substitution is not determined by the accepted certificate.  To make $Q$
load-bearing, the operational interface must add a finite coordinate recording
the required evidence: for a build or daemon trace this is a command-run tuple;
for an artifact semantic fact this is an artifact pass record or fresh evaluator
rerun log; for theorem-environment completeness this is an extractor command,
source state, environment, exit code, and log path; and for a venue decision this
is an external evidence surface and the corresponding route/submission record.
Without that enlargement, Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness} supplies
only the original finite-record conclusion.

Finally, the same argument isolates record-gate soundness.  The predicate
$\mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)$ says that recorded ledger entries actually contain
the assigned passing rows and submitted evidence pointers.  A package can agree
on the schema shape of a candidate certificate while differing on whether such a
record-gate-sound ledger has been supplied.  Thus schema exactness checks the
finite acceptance relation after the row data are present; it does not by itself
make those rows available, truthfully produced, or semantically adequate.
Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface} therefore states record-gate
soundness as an explicit hypothesis, and the trust boundary is exactly the finite
projection $F_{\Gamma}$ plus that hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Deterministic claim-kind dispatch]
\label{prop:claim-kind-dispatch}
For every load-bearing manuscript claim whose kind lies in the seven-kind domain
above, $\Gamma$ assigns a unique finite set of deterministic gates.  In a
pipeline that is record-gate-sound for acceptance, a mixed load-bearing claim may be
used as accepted work only if its acceptance entry records passage through every
gate in the union assigned to its component kinds.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The seven atomic clauses in the definition of $\Gamma$ assign one explicitly named
finite gate set to each atomic claim kind.  Hence an atomic claim has a unique
assigned gate set.  For a mixed claim, the component-kind set is finite because
it is a subset of the seven-kind domain; taking the union of the corresponding
finite gate sets is deterministic and finite.  Record-gate-soundness for acceptance
requires the acceptance entry of a load-bearing claim to record passage through
every gate assigned by $\Gamma$.  If a component gate of a mixed claim were
omitted, then the projection of the claim to that component kind would lack its
assigned pass record.  Such an entry would not be record-gate-sound.  Therefore accepted
mixed claims in a record-gate-sound pipeline must pass every gate in the union.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Mixed-claim projection obstruction]
\label{thm:mixed-claim-projection-obstruction}
Let $c$ be a load-bearing mixed claim with nonempty component-kind set
$K\subseteq\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)$ and submitted candidate certificate
$\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$.  For a nonempty subset $J\subseteq K$, write
\[
G_J=\bigcup_{k\in J}\Gamma(k)
\]
and let $c_J$ denote the projected reading of $c$ that keeps exactly the
claim-kind components in $J$ and keeps the same declared evidence level,
evidence surface, promotion boundary, and command boundary, except that the
discovery field is required precisely when discovery lies in $J$.  Then the
following are equivalent for the projected reading $c_J$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the finite verifier accepts the projected certificate
$\mathcal C_J=(J,\ell,E,H,R|_{G_J},D_J,B)$, where $R|_{G_J}$ is the restriction of
$R$ to rows whose gate name lies in $G_J$ and $D_J$ is $D$ exactly when discovery
lies in $J$ and is absent otherwise;
\item every gate $g\in G_J$ has in $R$ a pass record with the five required
fields and with an allowed evidence pointer, the retained boundary fields $E$,
$H$, $D_J$, and $B$ satisfy the corresponding schema clauses at level $\ell$, and
$J\subseteq\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, if an acceptance entry for $c$ records passage only through a
proper gate subset $Q\subset\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)$, then every component
subset $J$ with $\neg(G_J\subseteq Q)$ has an inclusion-minimal finite bad subrecord,
namely a missing pass record for some gate in $G_J\setminus Q$.  In particular,
no passage through route, submission, scheduler, artifact, traceability,
formal-source, or discovery gates can certify a mixed claim's remaining component
unless the pass rows assigned to that remaining component are also present.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The projected certificate $\mathcal C_J$ is a certificate of the same schema as
Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table}, with claim-kind set
$J$ in place of $K$ and with gate field restricted to the finite set $G_J$.
Because $J\subseteq K\subseteq\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)$, Proposition~\ref{prop:claim-kind-dispatch}
assigns exactly the finite gate set $G_J$ to the projected reading.  Applying the
schema-relative exactness theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness},
to $\mathcal C_J$ gives the equivalence: acceptance is exactly satisfaction of
the six finite schema clauses for the retained fields.  Clause A1 is the domain
condition $J\subseteq\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)$ and $\ell\in\mathsf{Lev}$; clause A2
checks the evidence surface $E$; clause A3 checks that each gate in $G_J$ has a
pass record in $R|_{G_J}$ with an allowed pointer; clause A4 checks the retained
promotion boundary $H$; clause A5 checks precisely the retained discovery field
$D_J$; and clause A6 checks the retained command boundary $B$.  These are exactly
the conditions in item~2, so items~1 and~2 are equivalent.

Now suppose the row for the mixed claim records only pass rows with gate names in
a proper subset $Q$ of the full assigned gate set.  Choose a nonempty
$J\subseteq K$ with $\neg(G_J\subseteq Q)$ and choose $g\in G_J\setminus Q$.  The
projected verifier for $c_J$ must check $g$ by clause A3, because $g$ is assigned
to the retained component-kind set $J$.  No row for $g$ is present among the
recorded gate names in $Q$, so clause A3 fails.  The verifier reads only the
finite fields consisting of $J$, $\Gamma$, the absent gate name $g$, and the gate
row field $R$; deleting fields not read by this failed check leaves an
inclusion-minimal bad subrecord of the second obstruction type in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton}.  Since the argument
uses only membership in the assigned gate set, it applies uniformly to each of
the seven atomic gate interfaces.  Thus one component's gate passage cannot be
substituted for another component's missing assigned pass rows in a mixed
load-bearing claim.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Assigned-gate soundness audit boundary]
\label{thm:assigned-gate-soundness-audit-boundary}
Let $P$ be a finite submitted package with pass-record ledger $L$, dispatch
table $\Gamma$, and submitted support surface $S$.  For a load-bearing claim
$c$, write $G(c)=\bigcup_{k\in K(c)}\Gamma(k)$ for the finite set of gates
assigned to its claim-kind set.  The package may use a ledger row for $c$ as a
sound record-gate witness only through the finite predicate
$\mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)$ of Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface}.
Equivalently, the available finite-record strength is exactly the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every $g\in G(c)$ the submitted ledger contains a pass row for $g$,
including the gate name, input surface, decision, evidence pointer, and evidence
level;
\item every evidence pointer named by those pass rows denotes a submitted path,
manifest row, theorem/proposition/table label, uploaded bundle file, or explicitly
declared historical boundary at the evidence level stated in the row; and
\item if the manuscript asserts any stronger proposition that the underlying gate
implementation was independently sound, freshly executed, or semantically correct
beyond the recorded finite schema check, the submitted package also contains a
gate-soundness audit tuple for that stronger proposition: source path, command,
source state, environment, exit code, and log path for the assigned gate.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, in the absence of the audit tuple in (3), record-gate soundness is
an explicit hypothesis of the publication-safety theorem, not a theorem proved by
the current package.  The finite pass rows prove only the package-level statement
that no accepted ledger claim omits an assigned gate row or its declared
submitted evidence surface.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The dispatch table has finite values by Proposition~\ref{prop:claim-kind-dispatch};
hence $G(c)$ is finite.  A submitted pass-record ledger and support surface are
also finite.  The predicate $\mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)$ therefore expands to a
finite conjunction of row and pointer tests: for each accepted load-bearing claim
it supplies a certificate satisfying $V(c,\mathcal C)=1$, and for each gate name
in $G(c)$ it searches the rows attached to $c$ and compares the finite fields
gate name, input surface, decision, evidence pointer, and evidence level.  If
one of these rows is missing, the certificate contains the second bad subrecord
of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton}, namely a missing
pass record for an assigned gate.  If a row is present, the verifier has only the
finite string and path fields carried by that row.

The second condition is exactly the local-checkability condition for the row's
evidence pointer.  A pointer to a path, manifest row, theorem/proposition/table
label, uploaded bundle file, or explicit historical boundary is a coordinate of
the submitted finite record surface.  If the pointer does not denote one of
those finite surfaces at the stated evidence level, the certificate has the first
bad subrecord of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton}, a
missing evidence surface.  Thus conditions (1) and (2) are precisely the
submitted-row and submitted-pointer clauses of $\mathsf{RGS}_{S}$, and they are
precisely what the package can verify from $L$ and the submitted surfaces.

The third condition concerns a different claim kind.  External implementation
soundness, fresh execution, and semantic correctness assert facts about the
operation of a gate outside the row's finite fields.  By the evidence-level
definitions and by Theorem~\ref{thm:executable-record-non-escalation}, a
command-result claim is not supplied by a path-verified pass row; it requires a
command tuple with source state, environment, exit code, and log path.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary}, two worlds that have
identical submitted pass rows but different unsubmitted gate implementations are
indistinguishable to the finite verifier.  Hence no proof from (1) and (2) can
establish the stronger external-gate proposition.  The stronger proposition is
accepted only after the package is enlarged by the audit tuple in (3).  Without
that enlargement, record-gate soundness remains the explicit finite predicate
$\mathsf{RGS}_{S}$, while the proved package fact is exactly the absence of
missing assigned-gate rows and missing declared evidence surfaces.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite certificate obstruction skeleton]
\label{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton}
Let $c$ be a load-bearing manuscript claim whose component kinds lie in the
seven-kind domain.  The verifier of
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier} gives the following
complete audit classification at the declared evidence level.  A certificate for
$c$ passes the publication-safety interface if and only if it has no bad
subrecord of the following five types:
\begin{enumerate}
\item missing evidence surface for the declared evidence level;
\item missing pass record for a gate in $\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)$;
\item missing human-promotion record when the claim crosses from intake or
source material into active publication work;
\item discovery escalation without the pinned discovery-gate source-interface
rows for certificate, ledger, lineage, and boundary witnesses; or
\item command-run escalation without command, source commit, environment, exit
code, and log path.
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently, every unsafe accepted claim contains an inclusion-minimal finite
bad subrecord of one of these five types, and deleting all other fields preserves
the same rejection by the verifier.  This is an audit-skeleton theorem for the
software-workshop route: it classifies finite certificate failures and makes no
claim about theorem-proving complexity or about fresh execution of the
underlying workflow.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $K$ be the finite set of component kinds of $c$.  Since $K$ is a subset of
the seven-kind domain, Proposition~\ref{prop:claim-kind-dispatch} gives a unique
finite required gate set
\[
G(c)=\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k).
\]
The certificate field $R$ is indexed by exactly this finite set, so checking
gate passage is a finite lookup: each $g\in G(c)$ must have a pass record and the
record must cite an item of the finite evidence surface $E$.  The evidence-level
field $\ell$ and boundary field $B$ make the same lookup finite for evidence
strength: at path-verified level the certificate must name the source, artifact,
pinned commit, local publication path, or gate-decision surface used by the
claim; at command-run level it must additionally name command, source commit,
environment, exit code, and log path.  Neither lookup interprets artifact
semantics, reruns Lean, or predicts editorial outcomes; those are stronger claim
kinds or stronger evidence levels that require their own records.  The human-promotion field $H$ separates
the route boundary from all other gates: it is either a concrete promotion
record or a finite marker that no such boundary is crossed.  Finally, when
discovery lies in $K$, the discovery field $D$ names the pinned source-interface
rows certified by Lemma~\ref{lem:source-interface-extraction-correctness} and
Propositions~\ref{prop:reviewable-source-snapshot-interface} and
\ref{prop:source-interface-record-file-consistency}; these rows are finite and
therefore locally checkable in the same way as the other certificate fields.
Thus the certificate is sufficient for a reviewer to check safety by examining a
finite list of fields and cited artifacts, not by trusting generated prose.

It remains to prove that the displayed obstruction list is complete.  Suppose an
accepted claim fails the publication-safety interface.  Choose an inclusion-minimal
subrecord of its acceptance entry that still witnesses failure.  The certificate
schema has exactly seven coordinates, and the first two of them are not extra
obstruction families.  The claim-kind coordinate $K$ only determines the finite
gate set $G(c)$ through $\Gamma$; once $K$ lies in the seven-kind domain, any
failure caused by that determination is a missing assigned gate and hence type~2.
The evidence-level coordinate $\ell$ only chooses between the path-verified and
command-run interpretations of the boundary field; its failures therefore appear
either as absent evidence at the declared level, type~1, or as absent command-run
coordinates, type~5.  Thus every possible failure must be witnessed in one of
the five substantive schema fields $E,R,H,D,B$.

If the failing field is the evidence surface $E$, the claim is not tied to a
submitted path, bundle path, manifest row, log path, pinned commit, or explicit
historical boundary at the declared level.  The minimal subrecord is therefore
type~1.  If the failing field is the gate-record field $R$, some gate in the
deterministic finite set $G(c)$ has no pass record, no decision, or no evidence
pointer lying in the submitted surface; this is type~2.  If the failing field is
the human-promotion field $H$, then all non-route evidence may be present but the
claim has crossed from intake or source material into active publication work
without the required human promotion marker; this is type~3.  If the failing
field is the discovery field $D$, then a discovery or classifier-shift reading is
being inferred from statement-only, code-only, mechanical, compression-only, or
classifier-scored material without the pinned certificate, ledger, lineage, and
boundary rows; this is type~4.  Finally, if the failing field is the boundary
field $B$, the record either raises path-verified evidence to command-run
strength without command, source commit, environment, exit code, and log path, or
omits the explicit no-command-run boundary; this is type~5.

The preceding paragraph exhausts the schema fields: $K$ and $\ell$ reduce to the
same five substantive checks, and $E,R,H,D,B$ are precisely the five displayed
obstruction types.  Minimality removes all unread fields and leaves exactly one
of the five obstruction skeletons.  Conversely, if no bad subrecord of type~1--5
is present, then each schema check in
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier} succeeds: the evidence
surface is present at the declared level, every required gate has its recorded
pass, the route-boundary field is correct, discovery rows are present exactly
when required, and command-run strength is not asserted without command-run
fields.  Hence the verifier accepts the certificate and the publication-safety
interface holds for $c$ at the declared evidence level.

The final sentence follows from the construction.  The verifier reads only the
finite certificate fields and the finite dispatch table, so the theorem
classifies certificate failures for review.  It does not assert a lower bound,
complexity separation, automated theorem-proving method, or fresh run of any
external command.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite extension-budget normal form]
\label{thm:finite-extension-budget-normal-form}
Fix the dispatch table $\Gamma$, the finite certificate schema, and an accepted
finite certificate
\(\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\) for a load-bearing package claim $c$.
Let $c^{+}$ be a proposed stronger reading of the same row which is not
record-equivalent to $c$ in the sense of
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary}.  Then $c^{+}$ is
accepted by the publication-safety interface if and only if the submitted finite
record surface contains an extension record in the following normal form:
\[
\Delta(c,c^{+})=(\Delta K,\Delta E,\Delta H,\Delta R,\Delta D,\Delta B),
\]
where the nonempty coordinates are exactly the coordinates needed by the stronger
reading:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Delta K$ names every additional claim-kind component asserted by
$c^{+}$;
\item $\Delta E$ supplies the submitted evidence surfaces for those added
components at the declared evidence level;
\item $\Delta R$ supplies pass records for every gate in
\(\bigcup_{k\in K\cup\Delta K}\Gamma(k)\) not already supplied by $R$;
\item $\Delta H$ supplies any newly asserted promotion, route, venue, or external
instantiation boundary;
\item $\Delta D$ supplies the pinned discovery-source rows whenever the stronger
reading adds discovery, classifier-shift, or theorem-content-delta content; and
\item $\Delta B$ supplies the command, source-state, environment, exit-code, and
log-path tuple whenever the stronger reading raises a path-verified row to
command-run strength.
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently, every genuine strengthening has positive finite extension budget:
at least one coordinate of \(\Delta(c,c^{+})\) must be nonempty, and every
nonempty obstruction class from
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton} must be paid by the
corresponding coordinate of \(\Delta(c,c^{+})\).  Without this extension record,
the stronger reading has an inclusion-minimal bad subrecord and remains outside
the certificate constructed in
Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First suppose that the extension record is present.  Enlarge the certificate by
replacing its fields with
\[
(K\cup\Delta K,\ell,E\cup\Delta E,H\cup\Delta H,
R\cup\Delta R,D\cup\Delta D,B\cup\Delta B),
\]
where the unions mean finite row union with the original row identifiers retained.
The six clauses of Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table} are
then exactly the six requirements displayed in the statement.  Clause~A1 reads
the enlarged kind set and evidence level.  Clause~A2 reads the enlarged evidence
surface.  Clause~A3 reads the enlarged gate-pass field and checks every gate
assigned by $\Gamma$ to the enlarged kind set.  Clause~A4 reads the enlarged
promotion and boundary field.  Clause~A5 reads the enlarged discovery-interface
field exactly when discovery is present in the enlarged kind set.  Clause~A6
reads the enlarged evidence-boundary field and accepts command-run strength only
when the command tuple is present.  Therefore
Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness} gives
\(\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}(c^{+},\mathcal C\cup\Delta)\), and the finite interface
accepts the stronger reading at precisely the declared evidence level.

Conversely, suppose $c^{+}$ is accepted.  Since $c^{+}$ is not
record-equivalent to $c$, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary}
implies that some finite coordinate read by the verifier differs from the
coordinate of \(\mathcal C(c)\).  Collect the differences in the six displayed
components: additional kinds in $\Delta K$, additional submitted surfaces in
$\Delta E$, additional gate rows in $\Delta R$, additional promotion or external
boundary fields in $\Delta H$, additional discovery rows in $\Delta D$, and
additional evidence-boundary or command-run data in $\Delta B$.  These are finite
because the verifier reads only finite records and the dispatch table has finite
domain.  Acceptance of $c^{+}$ forces each changed coordinate to satisfy the
corresponding clause A1--A6, so the collected difference record has the normal
form stated above.

It remains only to prove positivity and obstruction completeness.  If every
coordinate of \(\Delta(c,c^{+})\) were empty, then $c$ and $c^{+}$ would induce the
same values on all verifier coordinates and would be record-equivalent, contrary
to hypothesis.  Thus any genuine strengthening has positive finite extension
budget.  If some nonempty obstruction class is not supplied by its corresponding
coordinate, Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton} identifies
the first failed finite field as one of the five bad-subrecord types: missing
evidence surface, missing gate record, missing promotion or external boundary,
missing discovery rows, or unsupported command-run escalation.  Removing all
unread fields leaves an inclusion-minimal bad subrecord.  Hence a stronger
reading is accepted exactly when its finite extension budget is paid in submitted
coordinates, and otherwise remains outside the original certificate.\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Gate-interface separation]
\label{prop:gate-interface-separation}
The promotion, overlap, submission-state, and theorem-content delta gates are
separate deterministic interfaces.  None can be replaced by passage of another
one when a load-bearing claim has the corresponding component.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each named gate has a distinct invariant and evidence surface in the operational
definition.  Promotion asks whether a candidate has crossed the human
publication boundary; its evidence is the active-paper directory and pipeline
metadata.  Overlap asks whether the route duplicates or conflicts with a sibling
route; its evidence is route identity, overlap ledgers, submitted-sibling paths,
and board state.  Submission-state asks whether the proposed publication action
is compatible with submission markers, venue state, and submission-pack metadata.
The theorem-content delta gate asks whether an asserted extension contains
load-bearing theorem growth; its evidence is an inventory, hard-lint surface, or
delta record.  These predicates are logically independent at the interface
level: an active paper can still overlap a submitted sibling, a route with no
overlap can still have an impermissible submission state, and a compiling edit in
a permissible route can still lack theorem-content delta.  Therefore a
load-bearing claim with any one of these components must pass that component's
own gate, and passage through the others cannot supply the missing evidence.\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Human promotion is not daemon-visible active-track equivalence]
\label{cor:human-promotion-daemon-active-track-separation}
In the operational interface of Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface},
a human-promotion record and daemon-visible active-paper markers are not
interchangeable certificate fields.  More precisely, let $c$ be any
route-boundary claim whose accepted certificate is
$\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$.  The certificate may be read as saying that a
candidate is admitted to the publication-facing package only when the promotion
field $H$ records the human editorial act and the corresponding route-gate pass
record in $R$ names the active-paper marker surface required by the promotion
gate.  A record containing only a human-promotion note but no active-paper marker
surface does not certify daemon visibility, and a record containing only a
daemon-visible \path{2026_*} marker surface but no human-promotion field does not
certify editorial promotion.  In particular, the intake-isolation row $a_3$ of
Tables~\ref{tab:current-acceptance-ledger} and~\ref{tab:current-certificate-ledger}
supports exactly the bounded lesson that the intake packet remains outside
daemon-visible publication work until both sides of the promotion interface are
recorded; it does not identify human promotion with daemon enumeration.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Clause A4 of Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table} assigns the
route-boundary information to the certificate coordinate $H$.  The route-gate
part of Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface} is a different
finite coordinate: its pass record belongs to $R$ and its evidence surface is the
active-paper marker surface, namely a human-promotion act producing a
\path{2026_*} directory with \path{main.tex} and \path{PIPELINE.md}.  Thus the
schema checks two typed fields, not one untyped publication-status fact.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness}, the finite verifier accepts a
route-boundary certificate exactly when all required finite schema predicates
hold.  If the active-paper marker surface is missing, then the route-gate pass
record lacks its required evidence pointer in $R$; by
Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-claim-projection-obstruction} this is an
inclusion-minimal bad subrecord for the daemon-visible reading.  If the
human-promotion field is missing, then A4 fails for the route-boundary crossing;
this is a distinct missing-coordinate obstruction in $H$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:gate-interface-separation}
prevents repairing either bad subrecord by passage through overlap,
submission-state, scheduler, discovery, artifact, or theorem-content gates.

For row $a_3$, the acceptance ledger names the intake seed and active-paper
detector paths, while the certificate ledger records the marker that the intake
seed does not cross into publication work before promotion.  The case-witness
table states the same failed active-paper invariant and detector block.  Applying
the preceding paragraph to these finite coordinates gives the advertised bounded
reading: inactive intake is separated from publication work by the promotion
interface, but the certificate does not collapse the editorial act and daemon
marker enumeration into a single equivalent notion.\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Scheduler selection is advisory before discovery gating]
\label{prop:scheduler-selection-advisory}
Let $s$ be a scheduler or critical-path score attached to a candidate claim
$c$.  If $s$ recommends selecting $c$, then the recommendation is a
work-selection claim and by itself can only pass the scheduler gate.  If $c$ is
also asserted to be a discovery or classifier-shifting contribution in a
record-gate-sound pipeline, then the accepted mixed claim must pass both the scheduler
gate and the discovery gate.
Consequently, critical-path scores may prioritize review order, and the imported
classifier-shift selection rule may break ties among otherwise equal discovery
potential coordinates, but neither score nor recommendation accepts a
classifier-shift discovery unless the discovery gate also supplies the required
certificate, ledger, and lineage witnesses.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the definition of $\Gamma$, a scheduler recommendation has claim kind
work-selection, so its atomic gate is the scheduler gate.  The scheduler gate's
evidence surface records a score, backlog window, or analogous critical-path
priority; none of these fields is a semantic naming certificate, a ledger
witness, a DNA-complete theorem genome, a score certificate for a discovery
claim, a lineage witness, or a not-claimed boundary.  Hence scheduler passage
alone records only that $c$ is an eligible or prioritized work item.

Now suppose that the same recorded assertion also says that $c$ is a discovery
or classifier-shifting contribution.  Then the assertion is mixed: it has a
work-selection component and a discovery component.  Proposition~\ref{prop:claim-kind-dispatch}
therefore requires passage through every gate in the union assigned by $\Gamma$,
namely the scheduler gate for the work-selection component and the pinned
discovery gate for the discovery component.  The source-interface record in
Table~\ref{tab:source-interface-record} includes the imported classifier-change
selection theorem only in its bounded form: with positive classifier-change
weight and all other coordinates fixed, such a selection operator favors the
classifier-changing candidate.  The same source-interface record also includes
pipeline safety, which requires certificate, ledger, genome, score, lineage, and
boundary witnesses for accepted discovery.  Combining these two imported
interfaces gives the stated conclusion: scheduling and classifier-shift scoring
can advise selection order, but discovery acceptance still occurs only through
the discovery gate.\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal bad-record witness for command-run escalation]
\label{prop:publication-safety-minimal-bad-record-witness}
In the operational interface of
Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface}, let $r$ be a submitted
claim record whose claim-kind set is \{route\}, whose evidence level is declared
to be command-run, and whose boundary field contains no command-run tuple
$(\text{command},\text{source state},\text{environment},\text{exit code},
\text{log path})$.  Suppose all other route coordinates of $r$ are filled:
there is an evidence pointer, a human-promotion marker at the declared boundary,
and pass rows for the promotion, overlap, and submission-state gates.  Then the
schema verifier rejects $r$, and the inclusion-minimal bad subrecord is exactly
the pair consisting of the evidence-level declaration ``command-run'' and the
boundary field missing the five command-run coordinates.  In particular, adding
more prose, advisory scores, route history, or path-verified evidence surfaces
cannot repair the rejected stronger reading unless the missing command-run tuple
is added as a finite coordinate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a route claim, the dispatch table in
Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface} requires the promotion,
overlap, and submission-state gates.  By hypothesis those pass rows and the
route-boundary marker are already present, so clauses A1--A4 and A6 of
Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table} have no missing route,
evidence-surface, gate-coverage, or promotion coordinate to report.  The only
remaining clause that reads the declared evidence level is the command-run part
of the boundary condition: at command-run strength the boundary field must
contain the command, immutable source state, environment, exit code, and log
path.  These five fields are absent by hypothesis, so the verifier rejects.

The bad subrecord is inclusion-minimal.  If the evidence-level declaration is
removed, the command-run boundary obligation is no longer triggered by the
record.  If the boundary field is replaced by one containing the five command-run
coordinates, the displayed obstruction disappears and the record must be tested
against the remaining clauses, which were assumed filled.  Deleting any unrelated
prose, advisory score, route-history note, or path-verified surface does not add
the missing five fields and therefore cannot change the failed command-run
clause.  Thus the rejected stronger reading is witnessed by a finite bad record,
not by a semantic judgment about the surrounding pipeline.  This is the concrete
negative instance used by Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface},
Proposition~\ref{prop:publication-safety-maximality}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-extension-budget-normal-form} when a path-verified route
or submission row is silently upgraded to command-run strength.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Axis-and-level certificate invariant under record-gate soundness]
\label{thm:publication-safety-interface}
Consider a theory-to-paper pipeline with the operational interface above and a
finite submitted record surface consisting only of the manuscript source, printed
tables, submitted manifests, uploaded review-bundle records, recorded logs, and
explicitly declared historical boundaries.  Assume the following four distinct
finite-record hypotheses.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Acceptance entries}: every load-bearing claim in an active manuscript
is either rejected or has an acceptance entry with an evidence surface inside
this finite submitted record surface.
\item \emph{Record-gate soundness}: writing $S$ for this finite submitted record
surface and $L$ for the acceptance ledger, the package satisfies the finite
predicate $\mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface} as delimited by
Theorem~\ref{thm:assigned-gate-soundness-audit-boundary}: every recorded
acceptance entry has the assigned passing rows and submitted evidence pointers,
every advisory output is demoted unless the deterministic gate set $\Gamma$ for
its claim kind passes, and no independent implementation-soundness,
fresh-execution, or semantic-correctness claim about the gates is inferred unless
the package also contains the corresponding source path, command, source state,
environment, exit code, and log path.
\item \emph{Discovery certificate input}: discovery claims are checked through
the pinned discovery-gate interface certified by
Lemma~\ref{lem:source-interface-extraction-correctness} and
Propositions~\ref{prop:reviewable-source-snapshot-interface},
\ref{prop:source-interface-record-file-consistency}, and
\ref{prop:source-interface-evidence-level-separation}, in particular the
separated manual/path-verified and executable finite-record levels for the
pipeline safety and main pipeline theorem labels.
\item \emph{Promotion and verifier boundary}: no inactive intake seed is treated
as publication work without human promotion, and the constructed tuple below is
submitted to the finite schema verifier rather than being identified with the
acceptance entry itself.
\end{enumerate}
Then, and only under these finite-record hypotheses, the package checker has the
following finite-record invariant.  The checker input is the submitted surface
$S$, the ledger $L$, the dispatch table $\Gamma$, and the schema files and bundle
records named in Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier}.  Its
output is the finite accepted set
\[
\mathsf{Out}_{\Gamma,S}(L)=
\{(c,\mathsf{Cert}_{\Gamma}(c)):\ V(c,\mathsf{Cert}_{\Gamma}(c))=1\}
\]
together with the finite list of rejected bad subrecords.  For every accepted
load-bearing claim $c$ it constructs, from the acceptance entry and the submitted
finite evidence surfaces, a record
\[
\mathsf{Cert}_{\Gamma}(c)=\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)
\]
accepted by the finite schema verifier of
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier} with the input--predicate--output
boundary of Theorem~\ref{thm:verifier-input-output-contract}; equivalently, by the
finite-record exactness of Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness}, the
constructed record satisfies the schema-relative record-acceptance semantics for
the dispatch table $\Gamma$.  Consequently, within the submitted finite records
and under the stated explicit record-gate-soundness hypothesis, $c$ has a recorded evidence
surface, a declared evidence boundary, and a prior pass record for every gate
required for its claim kind.  Accepted discovery claims additionally inherit the
bounded pinned source-interface consequences that statement-only, code-only,
mechanical-expansion, and compression-only outputs are not accepted as discovery
without the required certificate, ledger, and lineage witnesses.  Moreover, the
constructed record is axis-and-level invariant and sharp for the stronger-reading
preorder: if $c\preceq_{\Gamma}c^{+}$ is a properly stronger reading that moves
a load-bearing consequence from one architecture axis of
Definition~\ref{def:architecture-axis-map} to another, raises the evidence level
from path-verified to command-run strength, adds an implementation, artifact,
venue, or semantic-correctness assertion, or adds a theorem-obligation
completeness assertion, then $c^{+}$ is accepted by the same interface if and only
if the submitted record surface contains the corresponding finite extension
coordinates and those records pass the assigned gates.  If those coordinates are
absent, the accepted certificate is maximal below $c^{+}$: it still accepts $c$
and rejects the stronger reading by a finite bad subrecord.

The maximality of this constructed certificate is stated separately in
Proposition~\ref{prop:publication-safety-maximality}, and the exact finite price
of any genuine strengthening is classified by
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-extension-budget-normal-form}; the current-package
instantiation is stated separately in
Corollary~\ref{cor:publication-safety-current-package-instance} and refined by
Theorem~\ref{thm:current-package-certificate-exactness}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We verify the obligations in the order advertised in the statement.

\emph{Package-checker construction.}  The checker first reads the finite record
surface $S$ and ledger $L$; no prose paragraph or live daemon state is queried.
For each ledger row it forms the candidate tuple
$(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$, runs the six predicates $P_1,\ldots,P_6$, appends
$(c,\mathsf{Cert}_{\Gamma}(c))$ to $\mathsf{Out}_{\Gamma,S}(L)$ on acceptance,
and otherwise appends the least failed predicate together with the fields read by
that predicate to the rejected-subrecord list.  Let $c$ be an accepted
load-bearing claim in the manuscript.  The acceptance-entry hypothesis supplies
the claim-kind set $K$, evidence level $\ell$, and evidence surface $E$, so the
decision is attached to a source path, artifact path, command log, inventory, or
witness record rather than to generated prose alone.  Record-gate-soundness
supplies the pass-record field $R$ for every gate assigned by $\Gamma$.  If
accepting $c$ crosses from intake or source material into daemon-visible
publication work, the promotion assumption supplies the human-promotion field
$H$; otherwise $H$ records that no such boundary is crossed.  The evidence-level
definition supplies the boundary field $B$: either the command-run fields are
present or the record explicitly remains path-verified.  If $c$ is a discovery
claim, the discovery-routing assumption supplies $D$ from the pinned
source-interface rows; otherwise $D$ is absent as required by the certificate
schema.  Thus the constructor
\(\mathsf{Cert}_{\Gamma}(c)=\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\) is determined by
finite submitted records and is locally checkable whenever its cited paths and
manifest rows lie in the submitted artifact, review bundle, or explicit
historical boundary.  The output invariant follows from the same case split and
from Theorem~\ref{thm:verifier-input-output-contract}: accepted rows are exactly
those for which all six predicates hold, and rejected rows carry the finite bad
subrecord defined by the first failed predicate, with no output channel for
end-to-end semantic correctness, fresh case reruns, or venue acceptance.  The
same construction verifies $\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}(c,\mathcal C)$, so
\emph{Record exactness.}  The finite-record exactness of
Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness} identifies the constructed
certificate with the paper's independently stated record-acceptance semantics,
not merely with an informal unpacking of the tuple.

\emph{Axis and evidence-level invariant.}  The record just constructed exposes
exactly the axes and levels on which $c$ is allowed to rely.  If a proposed
consequence of $c$ needs evidence on a different architecture axis,
Proposition~\ref{prop:architecture-axis-non-escalation} forces a separate
submitted coordinate for that axis and a pass record for the gates assigned to
that coordinate.  If the proposed consequence instead raises the evidence level
from path-verified to command-run strength, clause~A6 of
Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table} forces the command,
source state, environment, exit code, and log path into $B$.  In both cases,
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-extension-budget-normal-form} identifies the added
finite coordinates as the positive extension budget for the stronger reading.
If those coordinates are absent, Definition~\ref{def:stronger-reading-preorder}
identifies the proposed assertion as a properly stronger reading without the
extension coordinates required for acceptance.  Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness}
then returns the same original certificate and no cross-axis, cross-level,
implementation, artifact-semantic, venue, or theorem-completeness consequence is
certified.  The failed coordinate is a finite bad subrecord because it is either a
missing kind in $K$, a missing gate record in $R$, a missing command tuple in $B$,
or an evidence item outside the submitted surface $E$.

\emph{Gate coverage.}  Propositions~\ref{prop:claim-kind-dispatch} and
\ref{prop:gate-interface-separation} identify the required finite gate set:
formal-source claims use build and axiom gates, traceability claims use inventory
and marker gates, route claims use promotion, overlap, and submission-state
gates, artifact claims use status and limitation-ledger gates, submission claims
use submission-pack gates, extension-style discovery claims use the
theorem-content delta gate together with the pinned discovery gate,
work-selection claims use the scheduler gate, and mixed claims use the union.
Thus $c$ has a prior pass record for the gates required by its kind.  The
advisory-output assumption prevents an agent draft, review packet, score, or
repair proposal from being promoted by prose alone: without the relevant pass
records it remains demoted.  The promotion assumption similarly prevents an
inactive intake seed from becoming publication work merely because a local source
path exists.

\emph{Discovery boundary.}  If $c$ is a discovery claim, the discovery-routing assumption sends it through
the pinned discovery-gate interface.  Proposition~\ref{prop:reviewable-source-snapshot-interface}
supplies the corresponding reviewer-facing snapshot interface, so this routing
does not depend on a reviewer's access to the author's absolute source path.  The
record-file consistency certificate in
Proposition~\ref{prop:source-interface-record-file-consistency} further shows, at
the finite record-comparison level, that the manuscript table, JSON mirror,
review-bundle manifest, digest, line count, and bundled source snapshot name the
same finite imported interface.  The
source-interface table records the imported statements used here:
statement-only, code-only, mechanical-expansion, and compression-only records do
not become accepted discovery claims without the required certificate, ledger,
and lineage witnesses, and classifier-shift selection remains bounded by the
discovery gate as shown in Proposition~\ref{prop:scheduler-selection-advisory}.

Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier}; equivalently,
Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness} identifies it with
$\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}$.  The stronger non-escalation and maximality statement is
Proposition~\ref{prop:publication-safety-maximality}; the finite extension-budget
normal form in Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-extension-budget-normal-form} identifies
exactly which additional finite coordinates a stronger reading would have to add.
The current theorem is therefore the finite certificate-construction component of
the safety interface, with a separately proved budget boundary for extensions.
No current-package conclusion is used in this proof; the package instantiation
appears only later, after its support-surface hypotheses have been stated.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-record maximality]
\label{prop:publication-safety-maximality}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}, the
constructed certificate is maximal with respect to the finite submitted record
surface.  More precisely, let $c$ be an accepted load-bearing claim and let
$\mathsf{Cert}_{\Gamma}(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$ be the certificate constructed in
that theorem.  If a stronger reading $c^{+}$ asserts any external end-to-end
compiler correctness, current execution of unrecorded gates, proof-assistant
correctness, current formal-source currency, dynamic artifact semantics, or venue
acceptance, then either $c^{+}$ is not a distinct finite-record consequence of
$\mathsf{Cert}_{\Gamma}(c)$, or its certificate must strictly enlarge one of
$K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B$ by adding the corresponding claim kind, evidence surface,
gate pass record, promotion or venue field, discovery-interface row, or
command-run tuple.  In the latter case, if the enlargement is absent, a finite
bad subrecord is returned by Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness} or by
one of the obstruction types of
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The schema verifier used in Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface} reads
only the tuple \((K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\), the dispatch table $\Gamma$, and the cited
submitted finite surfaces.  This is exactly the finite-coordinate determination
boundary of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary}.  Hence two
surrounding implementations, formal-source trees, artifact runs, or venue states
that agree on those coordinates obtain the same verifier decision.  A purported
stronger reading which changes none of the coordinates is therefore invisible to
the certificate and cannot become a new accepted finite-record conclusion.

If the stronger reading is made load-bearing, it changes a coordinate.  External
compiler, proof-assistant, formal-source-currency, or artifact-semantics claims
add claim kinds and assigned gate records; fresh executions raise the evidence
boundary to command-run data; route or venue assertions add promotion or venue
surfaces; discovery-strengthening adds source-interface rows.  These are exactly
the finite fields tested by the clauses of
Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table}.  When the required
field is missing, Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness} supplies the
failed acceptance clause, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton} classifies the failure
as a missing evidence surface, gate record, promotion field, discovery-interface
row, or command-run escalation.\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-record boundary for external gate soundness]
\label{cor:finite-record-gate-soundness-boundary}
Under the hypotheses of the finite-record interface theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}, the
publication-safety conclusion is invariant under replacement of the surrounding
implementation by any other implementation with the same submitted acceptance
entries, evidence surfaces, promotion fields, discovery-interface rows, and
evidence-level boundaries.  Therefore the theorem cannot be used, without an
additional submitted record-gate-soundness certificate, to infer external workflow
correctness, current execution of unrecorded gates, Lean correctness, artifact
semantic validity, or venue acceptance.  Conversely, any manuscript assertion
that tries to promote the finite certificate constructor to one of those stronger
implementation claims has a finite overclaim witness: either the required field
is absent from \(\mathcal C(c)\), or the row's declared evidence level exceeds
the cited certificate surface.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The constructor in the finite-record interface theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}, reads only the
finite tuple \(\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\).  Here \(K\) is the claim-kind
set assigned by the dispatch table, \(\ell\) is the declared evidence level,
\(E\) is the submitted evidence surface, \(H\) is the human-promotion field,
\(R\) is the finite list of recorded pass rows, \(D\) is the pinned
discovery-interface field when the claim has a discovery component, and \(B\) is
the evidence boundary.  If another implementation supplies the same values for
these fields, the same finite schema verifier receives the same input record and
therefore gives the same certificate-construction conclusion by
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier} and
the finite-record exactness theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness}.  No step of the proof inspected an
unsubmitted daemon trace, reran a proof assistant, reran a finite-witness
artifact, or queried a venue form after the recorded venue check; those facts are
not coordinates of \(\mathcal C(c)\).

Now suppose the manuscript asserted one of the stronger implementation claims
without adding a new accepted record.  External workflow correctness and current
execution of unrecorded gates require pass data not present in \(R\).  Lean
correctness requires a formal-source certificate rather than the bounded
contextual rows allowed by Proposition~\ref{prop:formal-source-gate-instantiation}.
Artifact semantic validity requires a stronger artifact row than the bounded
manifest records of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-witness-manifest-record-interface}.
Venue acceptance requires a venue decision rather than the submission-pack
readiness row of Proposition~\ref{audit:venue-submission-pack-gate}.  In each
case, either the needed finite field is missing or the row is read at a stronger
evidence level than its certificate permits.  The obstruction is therefore a
finite subrecord of the kind classified by
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:evidence-level-overclaim-obstruction}.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact extension criterion for implementation-soundness readings]
\label{thm:implementation-soundness-extension-criterion}
Fix the dispatch table $\Gamma$ and the finite certificate schema of
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier}.  A reading of a
load-bearing package claim as an implementation-soundness claim is accepted by
the same publication-safety interface if and only if the submitted finite record
surface is enlarged by an implementation-soundness extension record
\[
\mathsf{Impl}(c)=(K^{\mathrm{impl}},E^{\mathrm{impl}},R^{\mathrm{impl}},B^{\mathrm{impl}})
\]
with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $K^{\mathrm{impl}}$ contains the additional implementation claim kind or
claim-kind component being asserted, rather than merely reusing the original
finite-record claim kind of $c$.
\item $E^{\mathrm{impl}}$ is a submitted finite evidence surface naming the
implementation object being certified: for example the relevant source path,
version or commit, configuration, executable verifier, and implementation-log
surface.
\item $R^{\mathrm{impl}}$ contains a pass record, with evidence pointer inside
$E^{\mathrm{impl}}$, for every gate assigned by $\Gamma$ to the enlarged claim
kind $K\cup K^{\mathrm{impl}}$.
\item $B^{\mathrm{impl}}$ declares the evidence level of the implementation
claim and contains the command-run tuple whenever the implementation claim is
asserted at command-run strength.
\end{enumerate}
When such an extension record is present, the finite certificate constructor of
Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface} accepts the enlarged claim exactly
at the evidence level declared by $B^{\mathrm{impl}}$.  When it is absent, every
implementation-soundness reading of the same row has a finite obstruction of one
of the five kinds in Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton}.
The current package supplies no record of the displayed form
$\mathsf{Impl}(c)$ for the package verifiers or gates.  Thus the theorem is a
future-facing exact criterion rather than an implementation-soundness result for
this submission: it says precisely what finite record must be added before a
later package may make that stronger claim.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First suppose the extension record is present.  Replace the original row for
$c$ by the enlarged row whose claim-kind coordinate is $K\cup K^{\mathrm{impl}}$,
whose evidence surface is $E\cup E^{\mathrm{impl}}$, whose pass-record field is
$R\cup R^{\mathrm{impl}}$, and whose boundary field is the original boundary
together with $B^{\mathrm{impl}}$.  The first condition makes the stronger
reading visible to the dispatch table rather than hiding it in prose.  The second
condition supplies a finite submitted surface for the implementation object.  The
third condition supplies every gate record required by $\Gamma$ for the enlarged
claim-kind set, and the fourth condition prevents a path-verified implementation
record from being read as a command-run implementation certificate without the
command, source-state, environment, exit-code, and log-path fields.  Therefore
the tuple has exactly the fields checked by
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier}.  Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface} to the enlarged row constructs an
accepted finite certificate for the implementation-soundness reading, and the
accepted strength is precisely the declared evidence level because the schema
reads no unsubmitted evidence-level coordinate.

Conversely, suppose a manuscript row is read as an implementation-soundness claim
but no extension record with the four displayed properties is submitted.  If the
additional implementation claim kind is not present in the claim-kind coordinate,
then the stronger reading has no assigned implementation gate and is an
unsupported escalation of the original row.  If the kind is present but
$E^{\mathrm{impl}}$ is absent, the claim lacks a submitted evidence surface for
the implementation object, giving obstruction type~1 of
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton}.  If the evidence
surface is present but some gate assigned by $\Gamma$ has no corresponding pass
record in $R^{\mathrm{impl}}$, the bad subrecord is type~2.  If the
implementation reading crosses an intake, source, route, or venue boundary with
no required promotion or decision field, the bad subrecord is type~3.  If the
implementation reading is also a discovery or classifier-shift reading without
the pinned discovery-interface rows, the bad subrecord is type~4.  Finally, if
the row asserts command-run implementation strength without the command-run tuple
inside $B^{\mathrm{impl}}$, the bad subrecord is type~5.  These cases exhaust the
finite schema fields by the obstruction-skeleton theorem, so absence of the
extension record is exactly absence of finite support for the stronger reading.
Inspecting Table~\ref{tab:current-certificate-ledger} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:machine-readable-certificate-records}, the current package
records path-verified certificate rows $a_1,\ldots,a_7$ and command-boundary
inventory rows for bounded verifier invocations, but it does not add a new
implementation claim kind, implementation evidence surface, implementation gate
pass family, and implementation boundary tuple as a single
$\mathsf{Impl}(c)$ extension record.  Therefore this theorem is not instantiated
as an implementation-soundness theorem here; it remains an exact finite-record
extension test for a later package.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Gate-witness criterion]
\label{lem:gate-witness-criterion}
A case record is a historical path-verified witness for the safety interface
when it names the source or artifact snapshot surface, the invariant that failed,
the gate decision recorded for that snapshot, and the safe manuscript lesson.
Under the finite-record interface theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}, such a witness can support a
case-study claim about the recorded historical gate surface, but not a stronger
command-run, benchmark, live-daemon, rebuild, dynamic-artifact, or semantic
validation claim, unless the corresponding command, source state, environment,
exit code, and log path are also recorded.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The four named fields are exactly the data required by the definition of a
path-verified historical evidence level: they identify the snapshot surface on
which the case is checked, which gate predicate was evaluated, what decision was
recorded, and which bounded claim may be carried into the manuscript.  A live
benchmark, daemon execution, full-source rebuild, dynamic artifact run, or
semantic validator would add an event time, command, source state, environment,
exit code, log path, and usually a new artifact-output surface.  Those fields are
not read by a historical witness.  Therefore a witness record with only the four
path-verified fields supports the bounded case-study claim and cannot be used as
evidence for a fresh command-run assertion.\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Path-complete case witnesses]
\label{prop:path-complete-case-witnesses}
Suppose a case-study row is used as load-bearing evidence for
the finite-record interface theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}.  If the row is path-complete in
the sense above, then the row supplies exactly the evidence surface required for
a path-verified historical snapshot case claim and no evidence surface for a
fresh daemon, full-source, theorem-content, artifact-suite, benchmark, or
semantic-substrate rerun.  In fact every accepted path-complete case row factors
through one of four finite witness skeletons---promotion, route/submission
blocking, theorem-content non-growth, or artifact-limitation disclosure---and its
admissible manuscript lesson is the unique bounded lesson attached to that
skeleton.  Replacing that lesson by a fresh experiment, dynamic validation, live
benchmark, or semantic artifact claim changes the skeleton by adding a
command-run or artifact-semantic coordinate, so the original row no longer
certifies the stronger claim.  In
particular, a promotion row is sufficient only
for the bounded claim that an intake packet is not daemon-visible publication
work before human promotion; a route-overlap row is sufficient only for the
bounded claim that the overlap and submission-state gates blocked advancement
until one of the recorded next actions occurs; a theorem-content row is
sufficient only for the bounded claim that file churn or compilation did not
show substantive theorem growth; and an artifact-limitation row is sufficient
only for the bounded claim that the artifact limitation must be disclosed or must
block promotion.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $r$ be a path-complete case row.  By definition, every load-bearing
component of $r$ has a concrete source or artifact snapshot path, directory
manifest, or explicit historical boundary.  The promotion interface names the
candidate surface, human-promotion evidence, active-paper
markers, gate decision, and the bounded manuscript claim; these fields are
precisely the data needed to distinguish inactive intake from publication work.
The route-overlap interface names the active route, the submitted sibling that
creates the chronology constraint, the reason for overlap, the gate decision,
and the finite set of permitted next actions.  These are precisely the data
needed to check whether advancement is allowed without appealing to generated
prose, and the submission-state evidence prevents a route from racing an already
submitted sibling.  The theorem-content interface names the changed surface,
the theorem inventory or hard-lint surface, the substantive-content delta, the
gate decision, and the allowed manuscript claim; these fields are precisely the
data needed to reject extension claims based only on file churn or successful
compilation.  The artifact-limitation interface names the status file, the
limitation ledger, the observed limitation, the gate decision, and the only
manuscript claim that may be carried forward.  These are precisely the data
needed to prevent a failed or bounded artifact check from being promoted into a
stronger dynamic-validation claim.  Lemma~\ref{lem:gate-witness-criterion} then
applies to each such row:
the named paths give the source surface, the interface fields give the failed
invariant and decision, and the last field gives the safe lesson.  The same
lemma also rules out upgrading the row to a command-run claim unless the extra
command-run fields are recorded.  In particular, the proof does not rerun the
publication daemon, overlap scanner, theorem-content detector, artifact suite,
or GroundCompiler evaluator; it proves only that the historical path surfaces are
sufficient for the bounded lessons displayed in the table.

It remains to justify the skeleton and uniqueness assertions.  The operational
interface dispatches each case row by its gate family.  A row whose required gate
is promotion has exactly the promotion skeleton; a row whose required gates are
overlap and submission-state has the route/submission-blocking skeleton; a row
whose required gate is theorem-content has the non-growth skeleton; and a row
whose required gates are status and limitation-ledger has the artifact-limitation
skeleton.  These four skeletons are disjoint because their gate sets differ, and
the row's safe-lesson field is part of the finite record checked by
Lemma~\ref{lem:gate-witness-criterion}.  Hence, once the skeleton is fixed, the
only accepted lesson is the bounded lesson printed in that row.  A fresh
experiment or dynamic validation would require the command-run boundary fields in
Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface}; an artifact-semantic claim
would require an additional artifact pass record or semantic evaluator surface.
Those coordinates are not read by the path-complete row and are not present in
the four skeletons.  The stronger reading is therefore rejected by
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary} together with the
finite obstruction skeleton, while the original bounded historical lesson remains
accepted.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Evidence-level overclaim obstruction]
\label{thm:evidence-level-overclaim-obstruction}
Fix the certificate schema, dispatch function, and evidence-level vocabulary of
this paper.  Let $c$ be a load-bearing claim whose accepted certificate has
level path-verified and whose boundary field $B$ records that no command-run
assertion is made.  Then every stronger reading of $c$ that asserts any of the
following additional conclusions is rejected by the finite schema verifier:
\begin{enumerate}
\item external gate soundness for an arbitrary implementation of the workflow;
\item Lean correctness, full-source rebuild success, or axiom-purity beyond the
recorded formal-source gate fields;
\item end-to-end external semantic validity of artifact data beyond the finite
manifest and evaluator surfaces named in $E$ and $R$;
\item a fresh daemon run, full-source rerun, Rule110 or GroundCompiler rerun, or
journal or workshop acceptance; or
\item bundle-backed reviewability when neither \path{review_bundle/} nor an
archival equivalent carrying the same finite records is in the submitted
artifact boundary.
\end{enumerate}
More precisely, such a stronger reading has an inclusion-minimal bad subrecord of
one of the five types in
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton}.  Hence the accepted
claim is rigid at its declared evidence level: path-verified certificate records
cannot be promoted by prose into command-run evidence, semantic validation,
formal-source correctness, or external acceptance.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $c^+$ be one of the stronger readings.  It has the same displayed manuscript
sentence as $c$ but a larger claim-kind set, a stronger evidence level, or a
larger submitted-artifact boundary requirement.  The verifier does not inspect
rhetorical emphasis; by Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier}
it inspects only the finite tuple
$\mathcal C=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$ and the finite dispatch table.

If $c^+$ asserts external gate soundness for an arbitrary implementation, then
it is not merely the finite certificate-construction claim accepted by
the finite-record interface theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}.  It needs pass records showing
that the relevant implementation ran every gate in
$\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)$.  Those records are not supplied by the
path-verified certificate for $c$, so the minimal bad subrecord is a missing
pass-record obstruction of type~2.  If $c^+$ asserts Lean correctness,
full-source rebuild success, or axiom-purity, then it is a formal-source or
command-run claim.  Proposition~\ref{prop:formal-source-gate-instantiation} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:formal-source-promotion-obstruction} identify the required
build, axiom-purity, and command-run fields; when they are absent, the minimal
bad subrecord is type~2 for missing formal-source gates or type~5 for command-run
escalation.

If $c^+$ asserts end-to-end semantic validity of artifacts beyond the named
manifest and evaluator surfaces, then it has an artifact component not covered
by the finite status and limitation-ledger records in $R$.  The current
acceptance and certificate ledgers display only the bounded artifact row $a_6$:
its evidence surface is the Rule110 status file, limitation ledgers,
paper-data reference, and review-bundle manifest, and its boundary field says
that no fresh artifact rerun is asserted.  A stronger artifact claim therefore
lacks an assigned artifact pass record, giving type~2,
or asserts a fresh evaluator run without command-run fields, giving type~5.  The
same argument covers a fresh daemon run, full-source rerun, and Rule110 or
GroundCompiler rerun: each is a command-run or artifact-strength escalation and
is rejected by the missing gate or command-run boundary fields.

If $c^+$ asserts journal or workshop acceptance, then its acceptance-entry field
would have to contain an editorial or venue acceptance record.  The operational
notion of acceptance entry in this paper is internal and submission-local; the
current ledger contains no external editorial acceptance row.  Thus the stronger
reading either lacks the required route/submission pass record, giving type~2,
or lacks the relevant external evidence surface, giving type~1.  Finally, if a
bundle-backed claim is made while no review bundle or archival equivalent lies
inside the submitted artifact boundary, the evidence item named by the
certificate is absent from $E$ at the declared level, so the minimal bad
subrecord is type~1.  These cases exhaust the displayed stronger readings.
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-certificate-obstruction-skeleton} then gives the stated
classification and proves rigidity at the declared evidence level.\end{proof}

\item \emph{Promoted rerun}: the package is advanced to a new source state by

verifier logs, and digest manifest by one common finite commit--manifest
certificate.
\end{enumerate}
If none of these three normal forms is present, the attempted bundle-backed
reading has a finite obstruction subrecord: either the external exact-inclusion
receipt is absent, the archive-equivalence table has a failed row, or the rerun
branch lacks the command, source-state, environment, exit-code, log-path, and

semantic correctness of the bundled artifacts.

\begin{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:final-support-surface-closure} prove that this exact directory,
with the final digest manifest, is the local byte surface that must be supplied;
the additional receipt is what turns that local selection into submitted support.

Second, the candidate can replace the directory by an archive.  The manuscript
does not accept arbitrary archive availability as a certificate coordinate:
Audit record~\ref{audit:review-bundle-archive-equivalence} gives the finite
hash-and-manifest test, and every failed row of

\begin{theorem}[Manual theorem-inventory boundary]

the theorem chain of

completeness, extractor soundness, or proof-checking evidence.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

bytes.  By the command-result rule in the scope contract, such an executable
completeness claim would require its command, source state, environment, exit

separate coordinates.  Thus \path{theorem_inventory.json} supplies only the
bounded manual traceability coordinate stated in the theorem.  The stronger

the absent extractor command and log tuple.  The weaker manual-ledger reading has
no such bad subrecord because its only asserted coordinates are the finite list,
the manifest path, and the acceptance-ledger pointer.  Hence the finite interface
accepts exactly the non-semantic manual boundary and rejects extractor
completeness.
