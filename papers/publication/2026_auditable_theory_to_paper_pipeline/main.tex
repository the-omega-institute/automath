\begin{abstract}
Large formal-mathematics projects increasingly use AI agents for drafting,
review, theorem search, and manuscript preparation.  Ungated agent output,
however, can create theorem-looking but content-poor edits, stale source
references, duplicated submission routes, and premature paper promotion.  We
report an auditable theory-to-paper pipeline used around a Lean-backed
BEDC source workspace and an automath publication workspace.  The
pipeline separates source development, intake packets, active paper
directories, AI review loops, deterministic gates, and human promotion
decisions.  Its central rule is that AI output is advisory: load-bearing
mathematical and submission claims must be tied, at the appropriate evidence
level, to source paths, command logs, deterministic checks, and human-approved
promotion.  Four case studies
illustrate the gates: intake isolation, overlap/submitted blocking,
fake-extension rejection, and artifact-limitation disclosure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and contribution}

AI assistance is useful in a formal-mathematics project only if the project can
distinguish proposal from evidence.  A generated theorem statement, a legal
piece of code, a mechanical expansion, or a lower score in a review rubric does
not by itself constitute mathematical discovery.  The practical publication
failure modes are equally structural: a route may overlap a submitted sibling,
an artifact status file may drift from generated counts, or an intake note may
accidentally become a daemon-visible manuscript.

This note contributes a compact workflow pattern for avoiding these failures:
an auditable theory-to-paper pipeline in which AI agents propose, review, and
triage, while deterministic gates decide what may be treated as load-bearing
evidence.  The pattern is instantiated in local BEDC and automath workspaces.
The mathematical content of the note is the explicit safety interface for this
workflow: definitions for gate vocabulary, a pinned imported theorem spine, a
proved publication-safety theorem, and witness records for the four required
cases.

The setting is adjacent to proof-assistant systems such as Lean
\cite{deMouraKADR2015Lean}, machine-learning interfaces to formal proving such
as LeanDojo \cite{YangEtAl2023LeanDojo}, informal-to-formal guidance methods
such as Draft--Sketch--Prove \cite{JiangEtAl2023DraftSketchProve}, and
large-scale formal-proof archives such as the Archive of Formal Proofs
\cite{BlanchetteHMN2015AFP}.  Our focus is different: we study the
publication-facing evidence discipline around such sources.

\section{Architecture}

The workflow separates source work from publication work.  The newmath source
workspace contains Lean files, scripts, and artifact sources.  The automath
publication workspace contains intake packets, active paper tracks, review
state, and submission metadata.  A candidate first enters an intake seed.  It
becomes an active paper only after a human promotion decision creates a
\texttt{2026\_*} directory with \texttt{main.tex} and \texttt{PIPELINE.md}.
This prevents source organization from being mistaken for a submission route.

The source side supplies a discovery-gate calculus.  In this calculus,
statement-only and code-only candidates are insufficient; mechanical expansion
and compression are not discovery by themselves; discovery claims require a
semantic certificate and a ledger witness; demotion preserves audit soundness;
accepted claims must form an auditable lineage DAG; and a pipeline is safe only
when discovery claims pass the gate before being recorded as accepted work.

\section{Operational interface}

For this short systems paper, the following finite interface is sufficient.  A
\emph{claim} is a manuscript assertion that can affect a mathematical,
artifact, route, or submission decision.  A \emph{load-bearing claim} is a
claim whose removal would change a theorem statement, a case-study conclusion,
or the submission-readiness status.  An \emph{advisory output} is any AI-agent
draft, score, suggested theorem, review packet, or repair proposal before a
gate records it.  A \emph{deterministic gate} is a repeatable predicate, script,
or checklist whose inputs and pass/fail decision are recorded independently of
the prose generated by an agent.  A \emph{human promotion} is the explicit
editorial act that moves a candidate into publication state, for example by
creating an active \texttt{2026\_*} paper directory with \texttt{main.tex} and
\texttt{PIPELINE.md}.  An \emph{evidence surface} is the set of source paths,
commits, commands, logs, inventories, and witness records cited for a claim.
\emph{Accepted work} is the subset of candidate claims recorded in a manuscript
or submission ledger after the relevant deterministic gate passes and, when a
route boundary is crossed, after human promotion.

We distinguish two evidence levels.  A claim is \emph{path-verified} when this
paper records the relevant source path, artifact path, pinned commit or local
publication path, and gate decision, but does not assert that a command was
freshly rerun for this submission.  A claim is \emph{command-run} when the paper
also records the command, source commit, environment, exit code, and log path
for a fresh run.  The present draft uses path-verified architecture and case
evidence unless explicitly marked command-run.

\begin{theorem}[Publication safety interface]
\label{thm:publication-safety-interface}
Consider a theory-to-paper pipeline with the operational interface above.
Assume that every load-bearing claim in an active manuscript is either rejected
or is recorded together with an evidence surface; that every advisory output is
demoted unless the relevant deterministic gate passes; that discovery claims are
checked through the pinned discovery-gate interface, in particular its pipeline
safety and main pipeline theorem labels; and that no inactive intake seed is
treated as publication work without human promotion.  Then any accepted
load-bearing claim in the manuscript has passed the gate required for its claim
kind before publication recording.  Moreover, accepted discovery claims inherit
the pinned source-interface guarantee that statement-only, code-only,
mechanical-expansion, and compression-only outputs are not accepted as discovery
without the required certificate, ledger, and lineage witnesses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $c$ be an accepted load-bearing claim in the manuscript.  By definition of
accepted work, $c$ is not merely advisory output: it has been recorded only
after the relevant deterministic gate passes, and if recording crosses the
publication boundary it follows a human promotion.  The first assumption gives
an evidence surface for $c$, so the gate decision is attached to a checkable
source path, artifact path, command log, inventory, or witness record rather
than to generated prose alone.  If $c$ is not a discovery claim, this is exactly
the required gate passage for its claim kind: formal-source claims use build and
axiom gates, traceability claims use inventory or marker gates, route claims use
promotion and overlap gates, and submission claims use submission-pack gates.
If $c$ is a discovery claim, the third assumption routes it through the pinned
discovery-gate interface.  The imported insufficiency, ledger, demotion,
lineage, pipeline-safety, and pipeline-theorem labels state precisely that
statement-only, code-only, mechanical-expansion, and compression-only records do
not become accepted discovery claims without the required certificate, ledger,
and lineage witnesses.  Hence every accepted load-bearing claim has gate
passage before publication recording, and discovery claims receive the stronger
source-interface guarantee.\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Gate-witness criterion]
\label{lem:gate-witness-criterion}
A case record is a path-verified witness for the safety interface when it names
the source or artifact surface, the invariant that failed, the gate decision,
and the safe manuscript lesson.  Under Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface},
such a witness can support a case-study claim, but not a stronger command-run
claim, unless the corresponding command log is also recorded.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The four named fields are exactly the data required by the definition of a
path-verified evidence level: they identify where the case is checked, which
gate predicate is being evaluated, what decision was made, and which bounded
claim may be carried into the manuscript.  The definition of command-run
evidence has additional required fields: command, source commit, environment,
exit code, and log path.  Therefore a witness record with only the four
path-verified fields supports the bounded case-study claim and cannot be used
as evidence for a fresh command-run assertion.\qed
\end{proof}

\section{Evidence boundary}

The present draft reports path-verified source architecture and case evidence.
It does not claim a fresh rebuild of the full newmath source tree.  Any later
claim that a command was run for this submission must record the source commit,
environment, command, exit code, and log path.  This boundary is deliberate:
the workflow is meant to make unsupported claims visible rather than to hide
them behind generated prose.

\section{Scope}

This note does not claim that AI output is proof evidence, that the workflow is
a theorem prover, that all BEDC declarations have been freshly rebuilt, or that
the Rule110 artifact suite has been rerun for this submission.  Its narrower
claim is that a theory-to-paper pipeline can preserve traceability by
separating source evidence, deterministic gates, agent suggestions, and human
promotion decisions.
