\begin{abstract}
Formal mathematics projects increasingly use AI agents for drafting,
review, theorem search, and manuscript preparation.  Ungated agent output,
however, can create theorem-looking but content-poor edits, stale source
references, duplicated submission routes, and premature paper promotion.  We
report an auditable theory-to-paper pipeline used around a Lean-backed
BEDC source workspace and an automath publication workspace.  The
pipeline separates source development, intake packets, active paper
directories, AI review loops, deterministic gates, and human promotion
decisions.  Its central rule is that AI output is advisory: load-bearing
mathematical and submission claims must be tied, at the appropriate evidence
level, to source paths, command logs, deterministic checks, and human-approved
promotion.  Four case studies
illustrate the gates: intake isolation, overlap/submitted blocking,
fake-extension rejection, and artifact-limitation disclosure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and contribution}

AI assistance is useful in a formal-mathematics project only if the project can
distinguish proposal from evidence.  A generated theorem statement, a legal
piece of code, a mechanical expansion, or a lower score in a review rubric does
not by itself constitute mathematical discovery.  The practical publication
failure modes are equally structural: a route may overlap a submitted sibling,
an artifact status file may drift from generated counts, or an intake note may
accidentally become a daemon-visible manuscript.

This source is the long technical supplement for a presentation-only route whose
primary artifact is the two-page source \path{submission_abstract.tex}.  It
records a compact workflow pattern for avoiding these failures: an auditable
theory-to-paper pipeline in which AI agents propose, review, and triage, while
deterministic gates decide what may be treated as load-bearing evidence.  The
pattern is instantiated in local BEDC and automath workspaces.
The mathematical content of the note is the explicit safety interface for this
workflow: definitions for gate vocabulary, a pinned imported theorem spine, a
proved publication-safety theorem, and witness records for the four required
cases.  The CICM presentation-only submission uses the short artifact plus
bibliography; this supplement and \path{review_bundle/} are evidence-support
materials rather than competing primary submissions.

The setting is adjacent to proof-assistant systems such as Lean
\cite{deMouraKADR2015Lean}, machine-learning interfaces to formal proving such
as LeanDojo \cite{YangEtAl2023LeanDojo}, informal-to-formal guidance methods
such as Draft--Sketch--Prove \cite{JiangEtAl2023DraftSketchProve}, and
large-scale formal-proof archives such as the Archive of Formal Proofs
\cite{BlanchetteHMN2015AFP}.  Our focus is different: we study the
publication-facing evidence discipline around such sources.

workspace contains Lean files, scripts, and artifact sources.  The automath
publication workspace contains intake packets, active paper tracks, review
state, and submission metadata.  A candidate first enters an intake seed.  It
becomes an active paper only after a human promotion decision creates a

\section{Operational interface}

For this long technical supplement and its two-page CICM presentation artifact,
the following finite interface is sufficient.  A
\emph{claim} is a manuscript assertion that can affect a mathematical,
artifact, route, or submission decision.  A \emph{load-bearing claim} is a
claim whose removal would change a theorem statement, a case-study conclusion,
or the submission-readiness status.  An \emph{advisory output} is any AI-agent
draft, score, suggested theorem, review packet, or repair proposal before a
gate records it.  A \emph{deterministic gate} is a repeatable predicate, script,
or checklist whose inputs and pass/fail decision are recorded independently of
the prose generated by an agent.  A \emph{human promotion} is the explicit
editorial act that moves a candidate into publication state, for example by
creating an active \texttt{2026\_*} paper directory with \texttt{main.tex} and
\texttt{PIPELINE.md}.  An \emph{evidence surface} is the set of source paths,
commits, commands, logs, inventories, and witness records cited for a claim.
An \emph{acceptance entry} is a manuscript or submission-ledger record that marks
a candidate claim as usable in the publication-facing argument.  \emph{Accepted
work} is the subset of candidate claims with such an entry.  This is a status
notion only: the definition records that an editorial acceptance entry exists,
but it does not by itself assert that the entry is gate-sound.  A pipeline is
\emph{gate-sound for acceptance} when each acceptance entry records the claim
kind, the evidence surface, the evidence level, and a pass record for every gate
in $\Gamma$ assigned to that claim kind, and when a route-boundary entry also
records human promotion.

The deterministic dispatch function $\Gamma$ from claim kinds to gates is part
of the operational interface.  Its domain is
\[
\{\text{formal-source},\text{ traceability},\text{ route},
\text{ submission},\text{ artifact},\text{ discovery},\text{ work-selection}\}.
\]
It sends formal-source claims to the build and axiom-purity gates; traceability
claims to inventory and marker-existence gates; route claims to promotion,
overlap, and submission-state gates; submission claims to submission-pack gates;
artifact claims to status and limitation-ledger gates; discovery claims to the
pinned discovery gate above and, for extension claims, to the theorem-content
delta gate; and work-selection claims to the scheduler gate, whose evidence
surface is a critical-path score or backlog window together with the claim kind
that the selected item is meant to advance.  For mixed claims, $\Gamma$ returns
the union of the gate sets for the claim kinds present.

The route gates have separate operational meanings.  The \emph{promotion gate}
uses the active-paper evidence surface, namely a human promotion act producing a
\path{2026_*} directory with \path{main.tex} and \path{PIPELINE.md}; passing
means the candidate may be daemon-visible publication work, and failing means it
remains intake or source material.  The \emph{overlap gate} uses route identity,
semantic-overlap ledgers, submitted-sibling paths, and program-board state;
passing means advancement is route-disjoint or has a recorded resolution, and
failing means advancement is blocked until closure, merge, supersession, or
waiting for prior feedback.  The \emph{submission-state gate} uses submission
markers, venue state, board state, and submission-pack metadata; passing means
the route's publication state permits the proposed action, and failing means the
action would duplicate, race, or under-specify an active or submitted route.  The
\emph{theorem-content delta gate} uses theorem inventories, hard-lint output, and
delta records such as \path{ARTIFACT_INVENTORY.md}; passing means that an
extension claim identifies load-bearing mathematical content, and failing means
that compilation, file churn, or surface edits may not be reported as substantive
theorem growth.

A \emph{reviewable artifact} for this paper is either a public repository file,
a supplemental file included with the submission, or an explicit
historical-evidence boundary stating that the local path is used only as
path-verified evidence and not as a fresh command-run claim.  A \emph{case
witness record} is path-complete when its evidence surface gives a
concrete source or artifact path for each load-bearing component of the row.  A
route-overlap witness has the fields \emph{route identity}, \emph{submitted
sibling}, \emph{overlap reason}, \emph{gate decision}, and \emph{allowed next
actions}.  An artifact-limitation witness has the fields \emph{artifact status
path}, \emph{limitation ledger path}, \emph{observed limitation}, \emph{gate
decision}, and \emph{allowed manuscript claim}.  A promotion witness has the
fields \emph{candidate surface}, \emph{human promotion evidence},
\emph{active-paper markers}, \emph{gate decision}, and \emph{allowed manuscript
claim}.  A theorem-content witness has the fields \emph{changed surface},
\emph{theorem inventory or hard-lint surface}, \emph{substantive-content delta},
\emph{gate decision}, and \emph{allowed manuscript claim}.  The inventory files
\path{ARTIFACT_INVENTORY.md}, \path{SOURCE_MAP.md}, and
\path{review_bundle/REVIEW_BUNDLE_MANIFEST.json} are part of the evidence surface
for this paper and give the compact path catalogue used by
Table~\ref{tab:case-witnesses}.

We distinguish two evidence levels.  A claim is \emph{path-verified} when this
paper records the relevant source path, artifact path, pinned commit or local
publication path, and gate decision, but does not assert that a command was
freshly rerun for this submission.  A claim is \emph{command-run} when the paper
also records the command, source commit, environment, exit code, and log path
for a fresh run.  This long technical supplement uses path-verified architecture
and case evidence unless explicitly marked command-run.

\begin{proposition}[Deterministic claim-kind dispatch]
\label{prop:claim-kind-dispatch}
For every load-bearing manuscript claim whose kind lies in the seven-kind domain
above, $\Gamma$ assigns a unique finite set of deterministic gates.  In a
pipeline that is gate-sound for acceptance, a mixed load-bearing claim may be
used as accepted work only if its acceptance entry records passage through every
gate in the union assigned to its component kinds.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The seven atomic clauses in the definition of $\Gamma$ assign one explicitly named
finite gate set to each atomic claim kind.  Hence an atomic claim has a unique
assigned gate set.  For a mixed claim, the component-kind set is finite because
it is a subset of the seven-kind domain; taking the union of the corresponding
finite gate sets is deterministic and finite.  Gate-soundness for acceptance
requires the acceptance entry of a load-bearing claim to record passage through
every gate assigned by $\Gamma$.  If a component gate of a mixed claim were
omitted, then the projection of the claim to that component kind would lack its
assigned pass record.  Such an entry would not be gate-sound.  Therefore accepted
mixed claims in a gate-sound pipeline must pass every gate in the union.\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Gate-interface separation]
\label{prop:gate-interface-separation}
The promotion, overlap, submission-state, and theorem-content delta gates are
separate deterministic interfaces.  None can be replaced by passage of another
one when a load-bearing claim has the corresponding component.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each named gate has a distinct invariant and evidence surface in the operational
definition.  Promotion asks whether a candidate has crossed the human
publication boundary; its evidence is the active-paper directory and pipeline
metadata.  Overlap asks whether the route duplicates or conflicts with a sibling
route; its evidence is route identity, overlap ledgers, submitted-sibling paths,
and board state.  Submission-state asks whether the proposed publication action
is compatible with submission markers, venue state, and submission-pack metadata.
The theorem-content delta gate asks whether an asserted extension contains
load-bearing theorem growth; its evidence is an inventory, hard-lint surface, or
delta record.  These predicates are logically independent at the interface
level: an active paper can still overlap a submitted sibling, a route with no
overlap can still have an impermissible submission state, and a compiling edit in
a permissible route can still lack theorem-content delta.  Therefore a
load-bearing claim with any one of these components must pass that component's
own gate, and passage through the others cannot supply the missing evidence.\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Scheduler selection is advisory before discovery gating]
\label{prop:scheduler-selection-advisory}
Let $s$ be a scheduler or critical-path score attached to a candidate claim
$c$.  If $s$ recommends selecting $c$, then the recommendation is a
work-selection claim and by itself can only pass the scheduler gate.  If $c$ is
also asserted to be a discovery or classifier-shifting contribution in a
gate-sound pipeline, then the accepted mixed claim must pass both the scheduler
gate and the discovery gate.
Consequently, critical-path scores may prioritize review order, and the imported
classifier-shift selection rule may break ties among otherwise equal discovery
potential coordinates, but neither score nor recommendation accepts a
classifier-shift discovery unless the discovery gate also supplies the required
certificate, ledger, and lineage witnesses.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the definition of $\Gamma$, a scheduler recommendation has claim kind
work-selection, so its atomic gate is the scheduler gate.  The scheduler gate's
evidence surface records a score, backlog window, or analogous critical-path
priority; none of these fields is a semantic naming certificate, a ledger
witness, a DNA-complete theorem genome, a score certificate for a discovery
claim, a lineage witness, or a not-claimed boundary.  Hence scheduler passage
alone records only that $c$ is an eligible or prioritized work item.

Now suppose that the same recorded assertion also says that $c$ is a discovery
or classifier-shifting contribution.  Then the assertion is mixed: it has a
work-selection component and a discovery component.  Proposition~\ref{prop:claim-kind-dispatch}
therefore requires passage through every gate in the union assigned by $\Gamma$,
namely the scheduler gate for the work-selection component and the pinned
discovery gate for the discovery component.  The source-interface record in
Table~\ref{tab:source-interface-record} includes the imported classifier-change
selection theorem only in its bounded form: with positive classifier-change
weight and all other coordinates fixed, such a selection operator favors the
classifier-changing candidate.  The same source-interface record also includes
pipeline safety, which requires certificate, ledger, genome, score, lineage, and
boundary witnesses for accepted discovery.  Combining these two imported
interfaces gives the stated conclusion: scheduling and classifier-shift scoring
can advise selection order, but discovery acceptance still occurs only through
the discovery gate.\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Publication safety interface]
\label{thm:publication-safety-interface}
Consider a theory-to-paper pipeline with the operational interface above.  Assume
that every load-bearing claim in an active manuscript is either rejected or has an
acceptance entry with an evidence surface; that the pipeline is gate-sound for
acceptance; that every advisory output is demoted unless the deterministic gate
set $\Gamma$ for its claim kind passes; that discovery claims are checked through
the pinned discovery-gate interface certified by
Lemma~\ref{lem:source-interface-extraction-correctness} and
Propositions~\ref{prop:reviewable-source-snapshot-interface} and
\ref{prop:source-interface-record-file-consistency}, in particular its pipeline
safety and main pipeline theorem labels; and that no inactive intake seed is
treated as publication work without human promotion.  Then, conditional on those
workflow assumptions, any accepted
load-bearing claim in the manuscript has a recorded evidence surface and a prior
pass record for every gate required for its claim kind.  Moreover, accepted
discovery claims inherit the bounded pinned source-interface consequences that
statement-only, code-only, mechanical-expansion, and compression-only outputs are
not accepted as discovery without the required certificate, ledger, and lineage
witnesses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $c$ be an accepted load-bearing claim in the manuscript.  The conclusion has
three parts.  The first part is definitional: by the acceptance-entry hypothesis,
$c$ has an entry with a claim kind, evidence surface, and evidence level, so the
decision is attached to a source path, artifact path, command log, inventory, or
witness record rather than to generated prose alone.  The second part is the
workflow invariant supplied by gate-soundness for acceptance: the entry must also
record a pass for every gate assigned by $\Gamma$.  This is the non-tautological
condition checked by the pipeline interface, not a standalone assertion about
unrecorded or currently inconsistent pipeline state.  The third part is
the human-promotion boundary: if accepting $c$ crosses from intake or source
material into daemon-visible publication work, the promotion assumption requires
the human promotion evidence before publication-facing acceptance.

Propositions~\ref{prop:claim-kind-dispatch} and
\ref{prop:gate-interface-separation} identify the required finite gate set:
formal-source claims use build and axiom gates, traceability claims use inventory
and marker gates, route claims use promotion, overlap, and submission-state
gates, artifact claims use status and limitation-ledger gates, submission claims
use submission-pack gates, extension-style discovery claims use the
theorem-content delta gate together with the pinned discovery gate,
work-selection claims use the scheduler gate, and mixed claims use the union.
Thus $c$ has a prior pass record for the gates required by its kind.  The
advisory-output assumption prevents an agent draft, review packet, score, or
repair proposal from being promoted by prose alone: without the relevant pass
records it remains demoted.  The promotion assumption similarly prevents an
inactive intake seed from becoming publication work merely because a local source
path exists.

If $c$ is a discovery claim, the discovery-routing assumption sends it through
the pinned discovery-gate interface.  Proposition~\ref{prop:reviewable-source-snapshot-interface}
supplies the corresponding reviewer-facing snapshot interface, so this routing
does not depend on a reviewer's access to the author's absolute source path.  The
record-file consistency certificate in
Proposition~\ref{prop:source-interface-record-file-consistency} further shows, at
the path-verified record level, that the manuscript table, JSON mirror, and
bundled source snapshot name the same finite imported interface.  The
source-interface table records the imported statements used here:
statement-only, code-only, mechanical-expansion, and compression-only records do
not become accepted discovery claims without the required certificate, ledger,
and lineage witnesses, and classifier-shift selection remains bounded by the
discovery gate as shown in Proposition~\ref{prop:scheduler-selection-advisory}.
The stated safety interface follows.\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Gate-witness criterion]
\label{lem:gate-witness-criterion}
A case record is a path-verified witness for the safety interface when it names
the source or artifact surface, the invariant that failed, the gate decision,
and the safe manuscript lesson.  Under Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface},
such a witness can support a case-study claim, but not a stronger command-run
claim, unless the corresponding command log is also recorded.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The four named fields are exactly the data required by the definition of a
path-verified evidence level: they identify where the case is checked, which
gate predicate is being evaluated, what decision was made, and which bounded
claim may be carried into the manuscript.  The definition of command-run
evidence has additional required fields: command, source commit, environment,
exit code, and log path.  Therefore a witness record with only the four
path-verified fields supports the bounded case-study claim and cannot be used
as evidence for a fresh command-run assertion.\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Path-complete case witnesses]
\label{prop:path-complete-case-witnesses}
Suppose a case-study row is used as load-bearing evidence for
Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}.  If the row is path-complete in
the sense above, then the row supplies exactly the evidence surface required for
a path-verified case claim.  In particular, a promotion row is sufficient only
for the bounded claim that an intake packet is not daemon-visible publication
work before human promotion; a route-overlap row is sufficient only for the
bounded claim that the overlap and submission-state gates blocked advancement
until one of the recorded next actions occurs; a theorem-content row is
sufficient only for the bounded claim that file churn or compilation did not
show substantive theorem growth; and an artifact-limitation row is sufficient
only for the bounded claim that the artifact limitation must be disclosed or must
block promotion.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $r$ be a path-complete case row.  By definition, every load-bearing
component of $r$ has a concrete source or artifact path.  The promotion
interface names the candidate surface, human-promotion evidence, active-paper
markers, gate decision, and the bounded manuscript claim; these fields are
precisely the data needed to distinguish inactive intake from publication work.
The route-overlap interface names the active route, the submitted sibling that
creates the chronology constraint, the reason for overlap, the gate decision,
and the finite set of permitted next actions.  These are precisely the data
needed to check whether advancement is allowed without appealing to generated
prose, and the submission-state evidence prevents a route from racing an already
submitted sibling.  The theorem-content interface names the changed surface,
the theorem inventory or hard-lint surface, the substantive-content delta, the
gate decision, and the allowed manuscript claim; these fields are precisely the
data needed to reject extension claims based only on file churn or successful
compilation.  The artifact-limitation interface names the status file, the
limitation ledger, the observed limitation, the gate decision, and the only
manuscript claim that may be carried forward.  These are precisely the data
needed to prevent a failed or bounded artifact check from being promoted into a
stronger dynamic-validation claim.  Lemma~\ref{lem:gate-witness-criterion} then
applies to each such row:
the named paths give the source surface, the interface fields give the failed
invariant and decision, and the last field gives the safe lesson.  The same
lemma also rules out upgrading the row to a command-run claim unless the extra
command-run fields are recorded.\qed
\end{proof}
