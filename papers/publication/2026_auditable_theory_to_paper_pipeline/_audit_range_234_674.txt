234: \section{Architecture}
235: 
236: The workflow separates source work from publication work, and the separation is
237: the organizing contract for the rest of the supplement.  BEDC source files are
238: treated as finite-kernel theory together with proof obligations and separate
239: implementation contracts.  Lean and related markers are formal-verification
240: records for named obligations, not object-language closure by themselves.
241: Finite manifests, evaluator rows, bundled snapshots, and command logs are the
242: substrate witnesses from which publication certificates are built.  AI agents
243: remain advisory: they may propose, review, and triage, but they do not promote
244: claims into accepted theory.  The automath layer is therefore a publication
245: compiler: it packages the source-theory interface, finite witnesses, route state,
246: and submission boundary without merging those evidence axes.

287: \begin{proposition}[Axis non-escalation]
288: \label{prop:architecture-axis-non-escalation}
289: In any record-gate-sound package using Definition~\ref{def:architecture-axis-map},
290: passage or evidence on one architecture axis cannot certify a load-bearing claim
291: whose claim kind requires another axis.  More precisely, if a claim certificate
292: uses an axis $A$ but its stronger reading needs an evidence item or negative-boundary
293: discharge listed only on a different axis $A'$, then the stronger reading is not
294: accepted unless the package contains a separate submitted coordinate for $A'$ and
295: the gates assigned to that coordinate pass.  Consequently
296: Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface} constructs only finite publication
297: certificates whose axis coordinates are explicitly present in the submitted support
298: surface; it does not infer BEDC closure from Lean markers, artifact semantics from
299: manifest rows, proof evidence from agents, venue upload from automath route state,
300: or support-surface availability from local bundle paths.
301: \end{proposition}
302: 
303: \begin{proof}
304: The operational interface records a claim by finite coordinates: claim kind,
305: evidence surface, evidence level, pass records assigned by $\Gamma$, and the
306: boundary field.  Definition~\ref{def:architecture-axis-map} makes the axis of each
307: such coordinate explicit and declares the corresponding negative boundary.  If a
308: stronger reading needs an item on a different axis $A'$, then either the claim-kind
309: coordinate does not name the kind requiring $A'$, the evidence surface does not
310: contain an admissible $A'$ item, a required $A'$ pass record is missing, or the
311: boundary field still excludes the stronger reading.  In all four cases the finite
312: record verifier of Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier}
313: rejects the stronger reading: the dispatch table cannot assign an unmentioned gate,
314: the surface check cannot read an unsubmitted path or log, the pass-record check
315: cannot be satisfied without the row, and the boundary check preserves the declared
316: negative boundary.
317: 
318: Conversely, when a separate coordinate for $A'$ is submitted, it is just another
319: finite component of the package.  It can be accepted only by the same schema:
320: its claim kind must dispatch to the appropriate gates, its evidence surface must
321: list the admissible $A'$ evidence, and its boundary must state exactly what is and
322: is not certified.  Therefore evidence never moves between axes by paraphrase or by
323: agent-generated text; it moves only by adding an explicit finite coordinate and
324: passing the assigned gates.  Applying this to the five rows of
325: Definition~\ref{def:architecture-axis-map} gives the listed consequences.  They are
326: also the axis-level instance of the coordinate-determination result later isolated
327: in Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary}: facts outside the
328: finite coordinates of the accepted package are not conclusions of the certificate.
329: \end{proof}
330: The reader path is therefore architectural before it is logistical.  The rest of
331: the supplement first fixes the source-interface spine, then defines the finite
332: record verifier, then proves the certificate construction and non-escalation
333: boundary, then instantiates that boundary on the current six-row package, and
334: only then lists the local case snapshots and command-run inventory.  File tables
335: are included as finite witnesses for this interface, not as a substitute for the
336: compiler architecture.

497: submitted record surface is enlarged by the corresponding source-statement,
498: formal-build, or command-run coordinate and by the assigned pass records for that

501: bounded interface.
