676: \paragraph{Reader map.}
677: The supplement separates one central theorem chain from support-boundary audit
678: records.  The core finite-record safety argument is:
679: Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface} fixes the certificate tuple,
680: claim kinds, gate dispatch, verifier predicate, evidence levels, and
681: record-gate-soundness hypothesis;
682: Definition~\ref{def:record-gate-soundness-external-interface} isolates that
683: hypothesis as an external finite input/output interface, and
684: Proposition~\ref{prop:record-gate-soundness-external-interface-exactness} proves
685: that it has no hidden semantic or venue-acceptance content;
686: Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table} restates the finite
687: acceptance predicates as the table relation \(\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}\);
688: Theorems~\ref{thm:verifier-input-output-contract},
689: \ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness}, and
690: \ref{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary} prove verifier exactness and
691: non-escalation from those predicates;
692: Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface} constructs finite publication
693: certificates under record-gate soundness; and
694: Theorem~\ref{thm:current-package-certificate-exactness} specializes the calculus
695: to the current six-row package.  The later results about packaging, upload
696: conditions, digests, command runs, inventories, venue records, and case snapshots

702: \subsection{Abstract finite-record interface}
703: 
704: For this long technical supplement and its two-page CICM presentation artifact,
705: the following finite interface is the self-contained formal block used by every
706: later safety theorem.  It fixes, before use, the certificate tuple
707: \(\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\), the dispatch table \(\Gamma\), the finite
708: verifier predicate \(V\), the acceptance relation \(\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}\), the
709: record-gate-soundness predicate \(\mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)\), and the
710: stronger-reading preorder \(\preceq_{\Gamma}\).  All hypotheses in the headline
711: safety theorem are finite-record hypotheses over these objects; none is supplied
712: by a hidden semantic oracle, by the manual theorem inventory, or by generated
713: prose.
714: 
715: \begin{definition}[Operational acceptance interface]
716: \label{def:operational-acceptance-interface}
717: A \emph{claim} is a manuscript assertion that can affect a mathematical,
718: artifact, route, or submission decision.  A \emph{load-bearing claim} is a claim
719: whose removal would change a theorem statement, a case-study conclusion, or the
720: submission-readiness status.  An \emph{advisory output} is any AI-agent draft,
721: score, suggested theorem, review packet, or repair proposal before a gate records
722: it.  A \emph{deterministic gate} is a repeatable predicate, script, or checklist
723: whose inputs and pass/fail decision are recorded independently of the prose
724: generated by an agent.  A \emph{human promotion} is the explicit editorial act
725: that moves a candidate into publication state, for example by creating an active
726: \texttt{2026\_*} paper directory with \texttt{main.tex} and \texttt{PIPELINE.md}.
727: An \emph{evidence surface} is the set of source paths, commits, commands, logs,
728: inventories, and witness records cited for a claim.  An \emph{acceptance entry}
729: is a manuscript or submission-ledger record that marks a candidate claim as
730: usable in the publication-facing argument.  \emph{Accepted work} is the subset of
731: candidate claims with such an entry.  This is a status notion only: the
732: definition records that an editorial acceptance entry exists, but it does not by
733: itself assert external editorial acceptance or implementation correctness.  A
734: pipeline is \emph{record-gate-sound for acceptance} when each acceptance entry records
735: the claim kind, the evidence surface, the evidence level, and a pass record for
736: every gate in $\Gamma$ assigned to that claim kind, and when a route-boundary
737: entry also records human promotion.
738: 
739: The deterministic dispatch function $\Gamma$ from claim kinds to gates is part
740: of the operational interface.  Its domain is
741: \[
742: \{\text{formal-source},\text{ traceability},\text{ route},
743: \text{ submission},\text{ artifact},\text{ discovery},\text{ work-selection}\}.
744: \]
745: It sends formal-source claims to the build and axiom-purity gates; traceability
746: claims to inventory and marker-existence gates; route claims to promotion,
747: overlap, and submission-state gates; submission claims to submission-pack gates;
748: artifact claims to status and limitation-ledger gates; discovery claims to the
749: pinned discovery gate above and, for extension claims, to the theorem-content
750: delta gate; and work-selection claims to the scheduler gate, whose evidence
751: surface is a critical-path score or backlog window together with the claim kind
752: that the selected item is meant to advance.  For mixed claims, $\Gamma$ returns
753: the union of the gate sets for the claim kinds present.
754: 
755: The route gates have separate operational meanings.  The \emph{promotion gate}
756: uses the active-paper evidence surface, namely a human promotion act producing a
757: \path{2026_*} directory with \path{main.tex} and \path{PIPELINE.md}; passing
758: means the candidate may be daemon-visible publication work, and failing means it
759: remains intake or source material.  The \emph{overlap gate} uses route identity,
760: semantic-overlap ledgers, submitted-sibling paths, and program-board state;
761: passing means advancement is route-disjoint or has a recorded resolution, and
762: failing means advancement is blocked until closure, merge, supersession, or
763: waiting for prior feedback.  The \emph{submission-state gate} uses submission
764: markers, venue state, board state, and submission-pack metadata; passing means
765: the route's publication state permits the proposed action, and failing means the
766: action would duplicate, race, or under-specify an active or submitted route.  The
767: \emph{theorem-content delta gate} uses theorem inventories, hard-lint output, and
768: delta records such as \path{ARTIFACT_INVENTORY.md}; passing means that an
769: extension claim identifies load-bearing mathematical content, and failing means
770: that compilation, file churn, or surface edits may not be reported as substantive
771: theorem growth.
772: 
773: A \emph{reviewable artifact} for this paper is either a public repository file,
774: a supplemental file included with the submission, or an explicit
775: historical-evidence boundary stating that the local path is used only as
776: path-verified evidence and not as a fresh command-run claim.  A \emph{case
777: witness record} is path-complete when its evidence surface gives a
778: concrete source or artifact path for each load-bearing component of the row.  A
779: route-overlap witness has the fields \emph{route identity}, \emph{submitted
780: sibling}, \emph{overlap reason}, \emph{gate decision}, and \emph{allowed next
781: actions}.  An artifact-limitation witness has the fields \emph{artifact status
782: path}, \emph{limitation ledger path}, \emph{observed limitation}, \emph{gate
783: decision}, and \emph{allowed manuscript claim}.  A promotion witness has the
784: fields \emph{candidate surface}, \emph{human promotion evidence},
785: \emph{active-paper markers}, \emph{gate decision}, and \emph{allowed manuscript
786: claim}.  A theorem-content witness has the fields \emph{changed surface},
787: \emph{theorem inventory or hard-lint surface}, \emph{substantive-content delta},
788: \emph{gate decision}, and \emph{allowed manuscript claim}.  The inventory files
789: \path{ARTIFACT_INVENTORY.md}, \path{SOURCE_MAP.md}, and
790: \path{review_bundle/REVIEW_BUNDLE_MANIFEST.json} are part of the evidence surface
791: for this paper and give the compact path catalogue used by
792: Table~\ref{tab:case-witnesses}.
793: 
794: We distinguish two evidence levels.  A claim is \emph{path-verified} when this
795: paper records the relevant source path, artifact path, pinned commit or local
796: publication path, and gate decision, but does not assert that a command was
797: freshly rerun for this submission.  A claim is \emph{command-run} when the paper
798: also records the command, source commit, environment, exit code, and log path
799: for a fresh run.  This long technical supplement uses path-verified architecture
800: and case evidence unless explicitly marked command-run.
801: 
802: A uniform \emph{pass record} is the finite tuple
803: \[
804: \rho=(g,I,d,e,\ell),
805: \]
806: where $g$ is the gate name, $I$ is the input surface submitted to that gate,
807: $d$ is the recorded decision, $e$ is the evidence pointer supporting the
808: decision, and $\ell$ is the evidence level.  A pass record is assigned to a
809: claim certificate only when its evidence pointer is contained in the claim's
810: evidence surface, in a submitted manifest or table row, in a bundled snapshot, or
811: in an explicit historical-evidence boundary.
812: 
813: A \emph{publication certificate} for a load-bearing claim $c$ is the finite
814: record
815: \[
816: \mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B).
817: \]
818: Here $K$ is the nonempty finite set of claim kinds carried by $c$, $\ell$ is the
819: evidence level, $E$ is the evidence surface, $H$ is either a human-promotion
820: record or the marker that $c$ does not cross the intake-to-publication boundary,
821: $R$ gives one pass record, with the five fields above, for each gate in
822: $\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)$, $D$ is present exactly when $K$ contains discovery
823: and then names the pinned discovery-gate source-interface rows used for the
824: claim, and $B$ records either the command-run fields or the explicit boundary
825: that no command-run assertion is made.  A certificate is \emph{locally
826: checkable} when these finite fields are present and every path, manifest row, or
827: log named by a field lies in the submitted artifact, the supplemental review
828: bundle, or the explicit path-verified historical boundary.
829: 
830: For later use we make the local check explicit as a finite verifier.  The
831: concrete package checker supplied with the review bundle is
832: \path{review_bundle/verify_certificate_records.py}.  Its positive invocation is
833: \path{python review_bundle/verify_certificate_records.py}; it reads exactly
834: \path{review_bundle/certificate_schema.json},
835: \path{review_bundle/current_package_pass_records.json},
836: \path{review_bundle/submission_interface_map.json},
837: \path{review_bundle/primary_claim_inventory.json}, and
838: \path{review_bundle/REVIEW_BUNDLE_MANIFEST.json}, and writes the recorded pass log
839: \path{review_bundle/certificate_verification_run.log}.  Its negative-example
840: invocation is
841: \path{python review_bundle/verify_certificate_records.py --negative-example review_bundle/negative_certificate_record_example.json},
842: with recorded log \path{review_bundle/negative_certificate_record_example_run.md};
843: that fixture upgrades a route row to command-run strength while omitting the
844: command-run tuple and is rejected with the finite bad subrecord
845: \path{neg1: command-run fields incomplete}.  The mathematical verifier below is
846: the schema abstraction of this package checker.
847: 
848: Let \(\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)\) be the seven-kind domain and let
849: \(\mathsf{Lev}=\{\mathsf{path},\mathsf{command}\}\).  For a candidate record
850: \(\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\), define six finite predicates:
851: \begin{enumerate}
852: \item[$P_1$:] $K\ne\emptyset$, $K\subseteq\mathsf{Dom}(\Gamma)$, and
853: $\ell\in\mathsf{Lev}$.
854: \item[$P_2$:] $E$ is finite and each item of $E$ is a submitted path,
855: review-bundle path, manifest row, log path, pinned commit, or explicit
856: historical-evidence boundary.
857: \item[$P_3$:] for every gate $g\in\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)$, $R$ contains a
858: pass record $(g,I,d,e,\ell')$ whose evidence pointer lies in $E$ or in a named
859: submitted table, manifest row, bundled snapshot, or historical boundary.
860: \item[$P_4$:] $H$ records the human promotion exactly when $c$ crosses the
861: intake-to-publication boundary and otherwise records that no such boundary is
862: crossed.
863: \item[$P_5$:] $D$ is present exactly when discovery lies in $K$, and then names
864: the pinned discovery-gate source-interface rows used by the claim.
865: \item[$P_6$:] $B$ either records command, source commit, environment, exit code,
866: and log path for command-run evidence or explicitly records that no command-run
867: assertion is made.
868: \end{enumerate}
869: Define the finite verifier
870: \[
871: V(c,\mathcal C)=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{6}P_i(c,\mathcal C,\Gamma).
872: \]
873: If this conjunction is false, the rejected subrecord is the finite set of fields
874: read by the least-indexed failed predicate.  This paragraph defines the verifier
875: only; no unprinted semantic oracle is part of it.  The next definition packages
876: the same finite fields as an explicit input type and a finite acceptance table;
877: the exactness theorem below then proves that the verifier and the independently
878: named record-acceptance relation coincide on that finite data structure.
879: 
880: For a package $P$ with ledger $L$, dispatch table $\Gamma$, and submitted
881: support surface $S$, write
882: \[
883: \mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)
884: \]
885: for the following finite predicate.  For every acceptance entry of a
886: load-bearing claim $c$ in $L$, there is a publication certificate
887: $\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$ such that $E\subseteq S$, $V(c,\mathcal C)=1$,
888: each pass record in $R$ is a submitted row of $L$, the row decision is passing,
889: and the row's evidence pointer denotes an element of $S$ at the evidence level
890: recorded in the row.  If $B$ does not contain command, source state,
891: environment, exit code, and log path, then $\mathsf{RGS}_{S}$ asserts only this
892: finite row-and-pointer property and asserts no implementation-level correctness
893: of the gate that produced the row.
894: \end{definition}
895: 
896: \begin{definition}[Record-gate soundness external interface]
897: \label{def:record-gate-soundness-external-interface}
898: The external record-gate interface for a package is the finite predicate
899: \[
900: \mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)
901: \]
902: with the following inputs and outputs.  Its inputs are exactly a package $P$, a
903: submitted support surface $S$, a finite acceptance ledger $L$, and the deterministic
904: claim-kind dispatch table $\Gamma$ of
905: Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface}.  Its positive output is the
906: finite family of certificates
907: \[
908: \{\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B): c\in L
909: \text{ is an accepted load-bearing claim}\}
910: \]
911: such that $E\subseteq S$, $V(c,\mathcal C(c))=1$, every assigned gate in
912: $\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)$ has a submitted passing row in $R$, each evidence
913: pointer in such a row denotes an element of $S$ at the recorded evidence level,
914: the human-promotion coordinate $H$ is present exactly at an intake-to-publication
915: crossing, and the discovery and command-boundary coordinates $D$ and $B$ have the
916: presence conditions required by the verifier predicates $P_5$ and $P_6$.  Its
917: negative output, when the predicate fails for a ledger row, is only the finite bad
918: subrecord read by the first failed verifier predicate among $P_1,\ldots,P_6$.
919: 
920: This interface makes no further claim.  In particular it does not assert that an
921: external venue accepted the work, that a command was freshly rerun, that a Lean
922: development builds, that an artifact has the semantic property named in prose, or
923: that an advisory agent output is proof evidence, unless the corresponding source
924: path, command, source state, environment, exit code, log path, or gate row is one
925: of the finite coordinates read above.
926: \end{definition}

928: \begin{proposition}[Record-gate interface equivalence and non-claim boundary]
929: \label{prop:record-gate-soundness-external-interface-exactness}
930: For the operational acceptance interface of
931: Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface}, the standalone interface in
932: Definition~\ref{def:record-gate-soundness-external-interface} is equivalent to the
933: predicate $\mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)$ used in the publication-safety theorem.
934: Moreover, under this interface every consequence used by
935: Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface} is determined by the finite
936: coordinates $(P,S,L,\Gamma)$ and by the displayed certificates
937: $\mathcal C(c)$; no implementation-level, semantic, venue-acceptance,
938: fresh-execution, or advisory-evidence claim follows from record-gate soundness
939: unless it appears as one of those finite coordinates.
940: \end{proposition}
941: 
942: \begin{proof}
943: The predicate $\mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)$ in
944: Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface} quantifies over exactly the
945: accepted load-bearing ledger entries $c\in L$ and requires exactly the following
946: data: a certificate $\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$, containment $E\subseteq S$,
947: acceptance by the finite verifier $V$, submitted passing rows for the pass records
948: in $R$, and evidence pointers denoting elements of $S$ at the recorded evidence
949: levels.  The verifier definition expands $V(c,\mathcal C(c))=1$ into the six
950: predicates $P_1,\ldots,P_6$, which are precisely the kind, evidence-surface,
951: assigned-gate, human-promotion, discovery, and command-boundary presence
952: conditions listed in
953: Definition~\ref{def:record-gate-soundness-external-interface}.  Hence a positive
954: instance of one definition supplies a positive instance of the other, with the
955: same certificates and the same submitted rows; conversely the standalone
956: interface supplies every finite field required by the original predicate, so the
957: original predicate holds.
958: 
959: For the boundary statement, inspect the fields read by $P_1,\ldots,P_6$.  They
960: contain claim kinds, evidence levels, finite evidence pointers, human-promotion
961: markers, assigned gate rows, discovery rows, and the command-run or no-command-run
962: boundary.  None of these fields is a venue decision, a proof-assistant semantic
963: judgment, an artifact semantic evaluator, a live daemon state, or an advisory
964: agent proof object unless an additional row explicitly records that fact as an
965: evidence coordinate satisfying the same verifier predicates.  Therefore the
966: interface can be used in Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface} only to
967: construct finite publication certificates and finite bad subrecords.  Any stronger
968: reading would require an added coordinate and is outside the predicate just proved
969: equivalent.
970: \end{proof}

972: \begin{definition}[Stronger-reading preorder]
973: \label{def:stronger-reading-preorder}
974: Fix the dispatch table $\Gamma$ and certificate tuple
975: $\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$ of
976: Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface}.  A proposed reading
977: $c^{+}$ of the same prose claim is \emph{record-stronger} than $c$, written
978: $c\preceq_{\Gamma} c^{+}$, if at least one of the following finite coordinates is
979: strictly enlarged while the others are unchanged or enlarged: the claim-kind set
980: $K$; the evidence level, from $\mathsf{path}$ to $\mathsf{command}$; the evidence
981: surface $E$; the promotion field $H$; the assigned gate-pass family $R$; the
982: discovery-interface field $D$; or the boundary field $B$.  It is
983: \emph{properly stronger} if the enlargement is strict in at least one coordinate.
984: It is \emph{accepted as a stronger reading} only when the enlarged tuple still
985: satisfies $\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}$: every newly added kind has its assigned gates,
986: every newly raised evidence level has the command tuple required by A6, and every
987: new evidence item is on the submitted support surface or in an explicit
988: historical boundary.
989: 
990: The preorder is intentionally syntactic and finite-record based.  It is not a
991: semantic entailment relation between mathematical propositions, and it does not
992: turn an inventory entry, digest row, or path reference into proof evidence for a
993: theorem-like environment.
994: \end{definition}

996: \begin{definition}[Certificate-schema acceptance table]
997: \label{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table}
998: Fix the seven-kind dispatch table $\Gamma$ of
999: Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface}.  The verifier input for a
1000: load-bearing claim $c$ is the following finite record type:
1001: \[
1002: \mathcal C(c)=
1003: \{K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B\}.
1004: \]
1005: Its typed fields are these and only these: $K$ is a finite set of claim kinds;
1006: $\ell\in\{\mathsf{path},\mathsf{command}\}$ is the declared evidence level; $E$
1007: is a finite evidence-surface list; $H$ is the route-boundary field; $R$ is a
1008: finite family of gate pass records, each with gate name, input surface, decision,
1009: evidence pointer, and evidence level; $D$ is either empty or a finite list of
1010: pinned discovery source-interface rows; and $B$ is the command-boundary field.
1011: This is a data schema, not a theorem: all later exactness claims must be proved
1012: from this displayed record and cannot appeal to unlisted package state.  Define
1013: $\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}(c,\mathcal C)$ to be the conjunction of the six finite
1014: table predicates A1--A6 displayed below; no clause may query generated
1015: prose, a live daemon, an artifact evaluator, a proof assistant, or a venue state
1016: outside these fields and their cited submitted surfaces.

1036: \begin{proposition}[Finite certificate schema verifier as a record check]
1037: \label{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier}
1038: For every load-bearing manuscript claim $c$ whose component kinds lie in the
1039: seven-kind domain, $V(c,\mathcal C)$ is a finite, prose-insensitive
1040: record-schema verifier for the declared publication certificate.  The submitted
1041: package instantiates $V$ by the script
1042: \path{review_bundle/verify_certificate_records.py}.  On the positive package
1043: input it reads the schema file, pass-record ledger, submission-interface map,
1044: primary-claim inventory, and review-bundle manifest named above, and outputs
1045: acceptance in \path{review_bundle/certificate_verification_run.log}.  On the
1046: negative fixture \path{review_bundle/negative_certificate_record_example.json} it
1047: outputs rejection in \path{review_bundle/negative_certificate_record_example_run.md}
1048: with the bad subrecord \path{neg1: command-run fields incomplete}.  If $V$
1049: accepts, then $\mathcal C(c)$ is locally checkable at exactly its declared
1050: evidence level.  This is certificate-record verification only: it checks finite
1051: record shape, dispatch, evidence pointers, and command-boundary discipline, not
1052: end-to-end external semantic validation of the referenced artifacts or source
1053: theory.  If $V$ rejects, then a reviewer can extract a finite bad subrecord
1054: consisting of the first failed schema check and the fields on which that check
1055: depends.
1056: \end{proposition}
1057: 
1058: \begin{proof}
1059: The verifier inspects only the displayed tuple $\mathcal C(c)$, the finite
1060: seven-kind domain, the finite dispatch table $\Gamma$, and the finite list of
1061: paths, rows, commits, or logs cited by the record.  The package script realizes
1062: these inputs as five finite JSON files: the schema supplies the seven-kind
1063: domain, evidence levels, gate dispatch table, required record fields, required
1064: pass-record fields, accepted decisions, manifest-required files, and accepted
1065: artifact-prefix patterns; the ledger supplies the seven current records $a_1,
1066: \ldots,a_7$; the submission-interface map and primary-claim inventory supply the
1067: finite manuscript-facing row sets; and the manifest supplies the submitted bundle
1068: paths.  The script output is either the pass transcript in
1069: \path{certificate_verification_run.log} or a list of \path{ERROR: ...} lines,
1070: each naming the failed finite condition.  Since $K$ is required to be a subset of
1071: the seven-kind domain, the set \(\bigcup_{k\in K}\Gamma(k)\) is finite.  The
1072: checks over $E$, $R$, and the command-run fields are therefore finite lookups in
1073: named manuscript or bundle surfaces, not semantic judgments about generated
1074: prose.
1075: 
1076: If all six checks pass, the tuple has exactly the fields required in the
1077: definition of a publication certificate: a finite kind set $K$, declared evidence
1078: level $\ell$, evidence surface $E$, route-boundary field $H$, gate-record field
1079: $R$, optional discovery field $D$ with the required presence condition, and
1080: evidence-strength boundary $B$.  Since $V$ is the conjunction of the explicit
1081: predicates $P_1,\ldots,P_6$, the six table clauses A1--A6 hold for exactly the
1082: same finite inputs.  Check~(ii) is precisely the local-checkability condition on
1083: cited paths, manifest rows, logs, pinned commits, and historical boundaries.
1084: Checks~(iii)--(vi) prevent record-level escalations of the declared level:
1085: missing gate-passage records, unrecorded route promotion, unsupported discovery
1086: routing, and command-run assertion without command-run fields.  Thus an accepted
1087: record is locally checkable at the level $\ell$ it declares, and no stronger
1088: level is silently inferred by the record checker.
1089: 
1090: If a check fails, the verifier stops at a finite test.  The bad subrecord is the
1091: finite collection of fields read by that failed test: for example, $K$ and
1092: $\Gamma$ for a gate-domain failure, $E$ for an evidence-surface failure, $K$,
1093: $\Gamma$, $R$, and the cited evidence item for a gate-record failure, $H$ for a
1094: promotion failure, $K$ and $D$ for a discovery-field failure, or $\ell$ and $B$
1095: for an evidence-level failure.  The supplied negative fixture is exactly the last
1096: case: it declares $\ell=\mathsf{command}$ but provides no
1097: \path{boundary.command_run_fields} containing command, source commit,
1098: environment, exit code, and log path, so the bad subrecord is the pair consisting
1099: of the evidence-level field and the boundary field.  This subrecord is

1138: \begin{proof}
1139: The input type is displayed in
1140: Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table}: after the fixed table
1141: $\Gamma$ is chosen, the only variable fields are $K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B$.  The
1142: submitted support surface $S$ is used only to decide whether the finite pointers
1143: named in those fields are reviewer-visible at the declared level.  Thus every
1144: listed predicate is a finite Boolean predicate on the displayed input fields and
1145: on the fixed submitted support surface.
1146: 
1147: For each row, the predicate is identical to one of the six predicates used in the
1148: definition of $V$: $Q_i=P_i$ for $1\leq i\leq 6$, with the same fields read and
1149: with the same failure subrecord, namely the fields read by the least-indexed
1150: failed predicate.  Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-certificate-schema-verifier}
1151: proves that the bundled Python checker is an implementation witness for this
1152: finite schema: it reads the schema, pass-record ledger, submission-interface map,
1153: primary-claim inventory, and manifest, and returns either the pass transcript or
1154: finite error records.  The theorem here is the abstract verifier contract behind
1155: that implementation witness, so its output alphabet is precisely
1156: \(\{\mathsf{accept}\}\) together with the finite bad-subrecord reports.

1160: prover kernel trace, a daemon transition semantics, an artifact evaluator

1163: depending on one of those objects is therefore not a function of the declared

1165: that object as an input coordinate and after a new verifier contract for the
1166: enlarged coordinate is proved.  Hence the present verifier certifies only finite
1167: record acceptance and its finite rejection witnesses.\end{proof}

1169: \begin{theorem}[Schema-relative finite-record acceptance exactness]
1170: \label{thm:record-acceptance-exactness}
1171: Fix the seven-kind dispatch table $\Gamma$, the two evidence levels, and the
1172: typed certificate tuple
1173: $\mathcal C(c)=(K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)$ of
1174: Definition~\ref{def:certificate-schema-acceptance-table}.  For every
1175: load-bearing claim $c$ and every finite candidate certificate $\mathcal C(c)$,
1176: the finite verifier $V$ of
1177: Definition~\ref{def:operational-acceptance-interface} and
1178: Theorem~\ref{thm:verifier-input-output-contract} accepts $\mathcal C(c)$ if
1179: and only if the six-clause table predicate
1180: $\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}(c,\mathcal C)$ holds.  This is a schema-relative and
1181: finite-record-relative assertion: both sides read exactly the fields named in the
1182: fixed schema and in the finite submitted record surface, and neither side ranges
1183: over unrecorded semantic facts about the referenced mathematics, artifacts, or
1184: gate implementations.  Moreover, if $V$ accepts a record for $c$, the same record
1185: cannot certify any stronger reading $c^{+}$ that adds a new claim kind, a stronger
1186: evidence level, an omitted route crossing, an omitted discovery interface, an
1187: omitted pass record, or a command-run assertion: such a reading has a finite bad
1188: subrecord of the corresponding clause.  Thus $V$ is exact for this paper's finite
1189: record-acceptance relation over the submitted source, tables, manifests, logs,
1190: and explicitly declared historical boundaries, while remaining silent about
1191: external semantic validity of the referenced theory or artifacts and about the
1192: implementation-level soundness of any deployed gate implementation beyond the
1193: recorded pass rows.
1194: \end{theorem}
1195: 
1196: \begin{proof}
1197: The definition of $V$ and the six clauses above use the same finite inputs.
1198: Predicate $P_1$ is clause~A1, $P_2$ is clause~A2, and $P_3$ is clause~A3,
1199: including both assigned-gate coverage and the requirement that each evidence
1200: pointer be tied to the declared review surface.  Predicates $P_4$, $P_5$, and
1201: $P_6$ are exactly clauses~A4, A5, and A6.  Therefore, if
1202: $\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}(c,\mathcal C)$ holds, every verifier predicate is true
1203: and $V$ accepts.
1204: 
1205: Conversely, suppose $V$ accepts.  Reading the successful checks in order gives
1206: precisely clauses~1--6: the kind set and evidence level are in the permitted
1207: domains; the evidence surface is reviewer-available at the declared level; every
1208: gate assigned by $\Gamma$ has a pass record with the required five fields and an
1209: allowed pointer; promotion and non-promotion are separated by $H$; discovery-kind
1210: claims and only discovery-kind claims carry $D$; and $B$ fixes the command-run
1211: boundary.  Hence $\mathsf{Acc}_{\Gamma}(c,\mathcal C)$ holds.
1212: 
1213: For the final assertion, keep the accepted tuple fixed and consider a proposed
1214: stronger reading $c^{+}$.  If $c^{+}$ adds a claim kind, the finite set of gates
1215: assigned by $\Gamma$ grows unless the added kind is vacuous in this interface;
1216: without the corresponding pass records clause~3 fails, and if the kind lies
1217: outside the seven-kind domain clause~1 fails.  If $c^{+}$ raises the evidence
1218: level to command-run, clause~6 fails unless the command, commit, environment,
1219: exit code, and log path are present.  If it adds a route crossing, clause~4 fails
1220: unless $H$ contains the human-promotion record.  If it adds discovery content,
1221: clause~5 fails unless the pinned discovery rows are named.  If it asserts a new
1222: gate-dependent conclusion without a pass record, clause~3 fails.  In each case
1223: the failed clause reads only finitely many fields, so deleting irrelevant unread
1224: fields gives an inclusion-minimal bad subrecord.  If none of the clauses changes,
1225: then $c^{+}$ is not stronger in the record semantics: the certificate still
1226: licenses only the same kind set, same evidence level, same gate records, same
1227: route boundary, same discovery interface, and same command boundary.\end{proof}

1268: \begin{theorem}[Finite-coordinate determination and trust-boundary classifier]
1269: \label{thm:finite-coordinate-determination-boundary}
1270: Fix the dispatch table $\Gamma$ and the verifier $V$.  Let $F_{\Gamma}$ be the
1271: finite schema projection read by $V$ from a certificate record, namely the
1272: claim-kind set, declared evidence level, evidence surface, promotion field,
1273: gate-pass rows, discovery-source rows, command-boundary tuple, and the assigned
1274: gate set determined by $\Gamma$.  If two ambient pipeline or artifact states
1275: induce the same values of all coordinates in $F_{\Gamma}$ for a claim $c$, then
1276: they induce the same record-acceptance decision and the same finite publication
1277: certificate for $c$.  Therefore the exactness of
1278: Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness} is exactness only relative to this
1279: finite schema projection.  No property depending on a coordinate outside
1280: $F_{\Gamma}$---for example the semantic truth of a referenced theorem, truthful
1281: production or implementation soundness of a gate row, a fresh Lean build result,
1282: a daemon trace, an artifact semantic fact, a theorem-environment completeness
1283: claim, or an external venue decision---is a consequence of the accepted
1284: certificate unless that property is first encoded as an additional finite
1285: certificate coordinate and the enlarged record is accepted.  In particular,
1286: record-gate soundness is a load-bearing hypothesis for
1287: Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface}; it is not supplied by schema
1288: exactness alone.
1289: \end{theorem}
1290: 
1291: \begin{proof}
1292: The verifier $V$ is defined as an ordered list of six checks, and each check reads
1293: only one of the coordinates listed in $F_{\Gamma}$ together with the fixed finite
1294: dispatch table $\Gamma$.  If two ambient states induce the same values on
1295: $F_{\Gamma}$, then the input tuple \((K,\ell,E,H,R,D,B)\), the assigned gate set,
1296: and every submitted pointer queried by $V$ are identical in the two states.  Each
1297: of the six checks therefore has the same truth value in the two states, so $V$
1298: either accepts both records or rejects both records at the same first failed
1299: finite field.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness}, an accepted
1300: decision is equivalent to the same six-clause record-acceptance semantics, hence
1301: the finite certificate determined by those clauses is also the same.  This proves
1302: the schema-relative and finite-record-relative factorisation.
1303: 
1304: Now let $Q$ be a property whose truth can vary while all coordinates in
1305: $F_{\Gamma}$ are held fixed.  Then there exist two ambient states with the same
1306: certificate tuple and the same verifier decision but different truth values for
1307: $Q$.  A conclusion whose truth changes under such an $F_{\Gamma}$-preserving
1308: substitution is not determined by the accepted certificate.  To make $Q$
1309: load-bearing, the operational interface must add a finite coordinate recording
1310: the required evidence: for a build or daemon trace this is a command-run tuple;
1311: for an artifact semantic fact this is an artifact pass record or fresh evaluator
1312: rerun log; for theorem-environment completeness this is an extractor command,
1313: source state, environment, exit code, and log path; and for a venue decision this
1314: is an external evidence surface and the corresponding route/submission record.
1315: Without that enlargement, Theorem~\ref{thm:record-acceptance-exactness} supplies
1316: only the original finite-record conclusion.
1317: 
1318: Finally, the same argument isolates record-gate soundness.  The predicate
1319: $\mathsf{RGS}_{S}(P,L,\Gamma)$ says that recorded ledger entries actually contain
1320: the assigned passing rows and submitted evidence pointers.  A package can agree
1321: on the schema shape of a candidate certificate while differing on whether such a
1322: record-gate-sound ledger has been supplied.  Thus schema exactness checks the
1323: finite acceptance relation after the row data are present; it does not by itself
1324: make those rows available, truthfully produced, or semantically adequate.
1325: Theorem~\ref{thm:publication-safety-interface} therefore states record-gate
1326: soundness as an explicit hypothesis, and the trust boundary is exactly the finite
1327: projection $F_{\Gamma}$ plus that hypothesis.
1328: \end{proof}
